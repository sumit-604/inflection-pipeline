## SECTION 1: THE BUSINESS MODEL IN PLAIN ENGLISH

### 1A. One-line description
RAMRAT buys copper (and some aluminium) rod, draws and insulates it into enamelled winding wire, or extrudes/draws it into copper tubes, and sells the finished shape to OEMs and distributors who pay for the "conversion" done on the metal, not the metal itself.

### 1E. The chai-stall-uncle version

Think of RAMRAT like a halwai who buys sugar (an input whose price he doesn't control) and turns it into laddoos — the price of sugar moves up and down with the market, but what he actually earns is the little bit extra people pay for his laddoo-making skill, packaging and reputation. RAMRAT does the same thing with copper: it buys copper rod at whatever the London Metal Exchange says that day, and earns a small "making charge" for turning it into wire or tube. Because copper is the input for two different sweets — wire and pipe — the company is now baking a lot more pipe than it used to (22% of sales versus 14% two years ago), because pipe demand (from air-conditioners) is growing faster right now. The company also owns a small stake in a shop next door that makes fan motors, and a majority stake in another one that welds steel tower parts for windmills — neither is core to the copper-wire trade, but both use the same manufacturing muscle. The tricky part for the halwai: he has had to buy a lot more sugar and flour on credit this year to keep up with orders, and customers are also paying him slower — so even though sales and profit are both up sharply, cash in the till has actually gone down.

### 2D. Industry lifecycle stage

- **Winding wires & strips (76% of revenue):** Mature-growth segment. Volume driven by broad Indian electrification/industrial capex, EV adoption, but pricing is largely a copper pass-through (AR p.49, A.3). RAMRAT is an established incumbent here.
- **Copper tubes & pipes (22% of revenue):** Early-growth/import-substitution segment. India's copper-tube market is described as growing at 16-17% CAGR to a global market of USD 70bn by 2032 (Inv. Pres. slide 25), with ~70% of India's copper tube currently imported (Inv. Pres. slide 15). RAMRAT is a newer, scaling entrant here (Bhiwadi commissioned June 2025).
- **Others/Tefabo (2% of revenue):** Early-growth, cyclical to wind-capex; global wind-tower market USD 31-32bn (2025) growing to USD 48-50bn by 2030 at ~8% CAGR (AR p.53).

## SECTION 3: FINANCIAL METRICS THAT MATTER FOR THIS BUSINESS MODEL

### 3A. Ignore-these-track-these

| Commonly tracked ratio | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Headline revenue growth (%) | MISLEADING | Revenue growth is inflated/deflated by copper LME price moves, not just volume; FY26 revenue grew 40.8% consolidated (Inv. Pres. slide 5) partly on a rising copper price cycle described in the AR (p.48) |
| Gross/EBITDA margin in isolation | MISLEADING | Because materials are ~93% of revenue, small absolute swings in the conversion spread move margin % a lot; consolidated EBITDA margin moved from 4.3% (FY25) to 5.1% (FY26), a "big" relative change from a small base (Inv. Pres. slide 29) |
| Net profit margin as a profitability yardstick | MISLEADING | At 1.8-2.2% net margin (AR p.51, Inv. Pres. slide 29) this looks like a commodity trading business on this metric alone; ROE/ROCE are far more telling for a conversion-margin manufacturer |
| Revenue per employee | IRRELEVANT for capital allocation | Business is capital/machine-driven, not headcount-driven (1,288 permanent employees, AR p.57) |
| P/E on trailing consolidated EPS alone | MISLEADING near-term | Business is mid capacity ramp (Bhiwadi); trailing earnings understate normalized earning power once utilization matures — company itself flags ramp-up timing risk (AR p.56) |
| Current ratio as a standalone liquidity signal | MISLEADING | At 1.15x (FY26) vs 1.18x (FY25) (AR p.233, Note 37) it looks stable, but it masks the FLAG-CASH problem: operating cash flow is negative because receivables and inventory are both growing faster than payables |
