### 1A. Precise boundaries

**Product scope (in):**

  FY26 revenue)

  components (28% of FY26 revenue)

  Perspective" section).

**Product scope (out):** passenger-car engine/powertrain components
outside driveline (Divgi does not make these); full e-axle drive units
(motor+inverter+gearbox) — Divgi supplies gear systems into these, not
the complete unit; aftermarket/replacement parts distribution (Divgi is
OEM-supply only, confirmed by its 100% B2B manufacturing revenue
streams per B04, no retail/aftermarket channel disclosed).

**Geographic scope:** India (domestic OEM supply: Mahindra & Mahindra,
Tata Motors, Force Motors — Investor Presentation, Q1FY27, slide on
"Strategic Partnerships") plus named export corridors: Indonesia
(Mahindra Scorpio Pik Up CY2026 program), North America (Project
Mayflower US subsidiary, evaluation-stage), and existing global Tier-1
relationships (BorgWarner, Magna, Toyota Tsusho) that route Divgi
content into non-Indian OEM platforms.

**Customer scope:** OEM (original equipment manufacturer) direct supply
only. No aftermarket, no retail, no distributor channel. Customer
concentration itself is NOT FOUND (B04 flag: no top-customer % disclosed
by the company) — this constrains SAM's customer filter (Section 3A)
to a qualitative judgment, not a sourced percentage.

**Channel scope:** Direct OEM contracts (design-win / platform
qualification model), not open-market or distributor sale.

**Price segment:** Mid-to-value passenger and light-commercial 4x4/AWD
platforms (Scorpio, Thar-class SUVs, Force Gurkha-class LCVs) — not
premium/luxury SUV segment (Divgi's own OEM base is M&M/Tata/Force, not
JLR/Toyota-premium tier for domestic supply).

**Explicit inclusion:** the confirmed Indonesia export program (35,000
units — see verification finding below, materially different from the
70,000 figure carried in this run's injected context).

**Explicit exclusion from core TAM:** the automatic-transmission
tech-transfer opportunity (non-contracted, one customer, no confirmed
timeline) and Other Income / treasury interest (6% of Total Income,
non-operating, per B04).

## SECTION 2: TAM ESTIMATION, MULTIPLE METHODS

### Method 1 — Top-down (global market, apportioned to India)

Global transfer case market size estimates diverge materially across
market-research vendors found via web search:
- $14.0bn (2024 base year)
- $18.82bn (2025 base year, different vendor methodology, likely
  broader AWD-systems scope than pure transfer-case)

Unit-volume estimates also diverge, but on inspection likely measure
different things, not a true conflict:
- ~14-15mn units/yr (annual production/shipment run-rate — consistent
  with the injected CONTEXT figure, unverified independently but
  internally consistent with $14bn ÷ ~$950-1,000 ASP/unit)
- "more than 38 million transfer cases installed globally in 2024"
  (single, less-authoritative source) — this reads as a CUMULATIVE
  INSTALLED FLEET BASE, not an annual shipment figure. Flagged as
  likely reconciled by definition rather than a genuine conflict, but
  not independently confirmed — carried at LOW confidence.

**No India-specific transfer case market value in ₹ Cr was found**
despite extensive search (Mordor, IBEF, ACMA, CRISIL/ICRA style report
titles searched; none returned an India-specific TC market number).
This is a genuine data gap, not an estimate — flagged as `input_gap`.

APAC's share of the global TC market also diverges across sources
(39.34%, ~28%, ~25%). Per the conservative-bias rule, the lowest
credible figure (25%) is used, and the divergence is flagged rather
than averaged.

Because no India-only split exists, India is apportioned from the
global figure using India's share of global light-vehicle production
(~4.5mn units India vs ~94mn units global per SIAM/OICA-style figures,
≈ 4.8%, rounded to a 3-5% range to allow for TC's lower fitment rate in
India's SUV-skewed 4WD segment vs global markets with higher 4WD
truck penetration such as North America):

- Conservative: $14.0bn × 3% = $420mn ≈ ₹3,486cr (at ₹83/$, FY24
  average, matching the data's base year)
- Realistic: $14.0bn × 5% = $700mn ≈ ₹5,810cr

**Confidence: LOW.** This is an apportionment proxy, not a directly
sourced India TC market figure. Staleness: 2024 base year is at the
2-year threshold from the 2026 run date — flagged approaching-STALE.

### Method 2 — Bottom-up (unit x penetration x ASP)

Addressable unit: one 4WD/AWD-capable vehicle sold in India requiring
a transfer case.
- India total PV/UV sales: directionally available (SIAM, ~5mn+ units
  FY25 total PV) but **4WD/AWD penetration % of Indian SUV/UV sales is
  NOT FOUND** in any searched source.
- **Transfer case OEM ASP (₹ per unit) is NOT FOUND** — Divgi discloses
  no per-unit revenue (confirmed independently in B04's
  `unit_economics.revenue_per_unit: "NOT FOUND"`).

**Method 2 output: INCOMPLETE.** Two of three required inputs are
NOT FOUND. Per the hard rule against estimating missing numbers, no
headline ₹ figure is produced from this method. It is retained in the
report to show the gap explicitly, not silently dropped.

### Method 3 — Peer revenue aggregation (oligopoly cross-check)

This category does not behave like a typical fragmented Indian
consumer market. OEM-only supply requires multi-year platform
qualification and tooling investment; there is effectively no
"unorganised sector" in transfer-case/driveline-component OEM supply
(unlike the 30-60% unorganised-sector norm the base method assumes for
consumer categories). This structural point is itself a Section 4
finding, not a gap to paper over.

Divgi's own IPO-period positioning (Investor Presentation) frames it
as the leading India-based independent player, with the principal
alternative suppliers being global majors (BorgWarner, Magna, JTEKT)
who serve Indian OEMs mostly via import or joint-venture routes rather
than local independent manufacture. No named Indian peer's
segment-level transfer-case/component revenue was found via search
(most either don't break out this sub-segment or aren't listed
separately from broader driveline/differential lines, e.g. Sona BLW's
disclosed lines are e-axle/differential, an adjacent but distinct
product).

Using Divgi's own components + synchronizer revenue (₹98.8cr + ₹21.2cr
= ₹120cr, FY26, per B04 revenue_streams) and an unconfirmed assumed
15-25% share of this narrower addressable OEM sub-segment (flagged as
a judgment call, not a disclosed market figure):
₹120cr ÷ 0.20 (midpoint) ≈ **₹600cr** sub-segment TAM.

**Confidence: LOW.** This is the weakest-evidence method component;
retained for triangulation only, not usable as a standalone headline
number.

### Method 4 — Import substitution

Directionally relevant to the automatic-transmission opportunity
(India currently has no domestic Tier-1 for automatic transmissions;
the AT tech-transfer opportunity is explicitly an import-substitution
play) and to broader transmission-component sourcing. However:
- India's transmission/gearbox-specific import bill in $ or ₹ was
  **NOT FOUND** (only broad auto-component import aggregates exist,
  not category-specific).
- An OEM-to-automaker unit price for automatic transmissions was
  **NOT FOUND** — only aftermarket replacement/repair cost data
  ($200-$2,200 range) was located, which is not a clean proxy for OEM
  transfer price and is not used.

**Method 4 output: INCOMPLETE for a headline ₹ figure.** Retained
qualitatively in Section 4 (growth driver) only.

### Method 5 — Global benchmark

Global auto components industry: $2,302.2bn (2025) → $2,818.9bn (2034),
CAGR 2.21%, APAC 38.4% share (AR MD&A, FY2025-26 Annual Report — current,
not stale). Indian auto components industry projected to add $85.85bn
of turnover 2025-2030, implying a much faster India-specific CAGR
(~14.8%, AR MD&A) than the global aggregate. This gap (India growing
~7x faster than the global average) supports a directional "India is
under-penetrated relative to global auto-component intensity" reading,
but is too broad (covers all auto components, not transfer
cases/transmissions specifically) to produce a standalone TAM figure
for this category. **Used as direction only, not as a headline number**
(consistent with the staleness/precision rule: broad benchmarks inform
direction, never the headline).

**Conservative TAM = ₹3,486cr (Method 1) + ₹600cr (Method 3
components add-on, weak evidence) ≈ ₹4,000cr** (core scope: transfer

AT optionality and nascent EV transmission, both too data-poor to
quantify reliably).

**Realistic TAM = ₹5,810cr (Method 1) + ₹800cr (Method 3 upside) +
₹200cr (EV transmission, directional, anchored only to Divgi's own
₹28.2cr E-Gear Drive revenue and India's >199,000-unit EV PV base,

**Management-claim-vs-conservative-estimate ratio:** Using the dated

comparison basis because Claim 2 is itself Divgi's own revenue target,

(>2x band), consistent with Claim 1's lack of a target date and its
inclusion of the non-contracted AT vertical (~₹1,000cr of the ₹2,050cr
total) as if it were a base-case certainty.

## SECTION 3: SAM & SOM

### 3A. SAM (five filters applied to conservative TAM, ₹4,000cr)

| Filter | Rationale | Multiplier |
|---|---|---|
| Product fit | OEM-only; exclude aftermarket/replacement TC demand (India's OEM-warranty-driven market has low aftermarket TC replacement vs global norms) | 0.90 |
| Geography | India + named export corridors (Indonesia, NA-evaluation) already scoped into TAM; no further haircut | 1.00 |
| Channel | Direct OEM contract is the only channel Divgi uses; no incremental restriction | 1.00 |
| Customer | Restricted to OEMs where Divgi has existing or actively-qualifying relationships (M&M, Tata, Force domestically; BorgWarner/Magna/Toyota Tsusho routes globally) rather than the full universe of global OEMs — customer concentration % itself is NOT FOUND (B04), so this is a judgment-call haircut, flagged | 0.70 |
| Capability | 4-plant footprint; capacity utilisation % NOT FOUND (B04 mgmt_question) — moderate haircut applied as a placeholder pending disclosure | 0.85 |

Combined SAM% of TAM = 0.90 x 1.00 x 1.00 x 0.70 x 0.85 = **0.5355
(~54%)**.

**SAM = ₹4,000cr x 54% ≈ ₹2,160cr.** (sam_pct_of_tam computed against
conservative TAM per the conservative-bias rule.)

**Current SAM share:** FY26 Revenue from Operations ₹352.9cr ÷ SAM
₹2,160cr = **16.3%.**

**Revenue headroom = SAM ÷ current revenue = 2,160 / 352.9 = 6.12x.**

### 3B. SOM at 3 and 5 years

Share-gain rule applied: the AGGRESSIVE band (3-5pp gain in 3 years)
is justified, not the 1-2pp normal band, because capacity and
execution evidence exists: FY26 revenue +61.2% YoY, Q1FY27 +85% YoY,
Indonesia program ramping (confirmed 35,000-unit order, see
verification finding below), and active capex deployment (IPO
proceeds + FY26 capex ₹44.32cr, B02/B03). The unorganised-sector
>40%-formalising carve-out for even faster gains does NOT apply here —
this market has no meaningful unorganised layer (Section 4C).

SAM is grown forward at a blended core TAM growth rate of ~8%/yr
(judgment blend between India's broader auto-components CAGR of 14.8%
and the more mature global TC CAGR; no single sourced CAGR exists at
the TC-specific India granularity — flagged).

- SAM Yr3 = ₹2,160cr x 1.08^3 = ₹2,721cr
- SAM Yr5 = ₹2,160cr x 1.08^5 = ₹3,174cr

Share trajectory: 16.3% (current) → 20.3% (Yr3, +4.0pp, aggressive
band) → 22.3% (Yr5, +6.0pp cumulative).

- **SOM_3yr = 20.3% x ₹2,721cr = ₹552cr**
- **SOM_5yr = 22.3% x ₹3,174cr = ₹708cr**

**som_implied_revenue_cagr (formal handoff to stage 11):**
- Yr3: (552 / 352.9)^(1/3) - 1 = **16.1%**
- Yr5: (708 / 352.9)^(1/5) - 1 = **15.0%**

Both figures sit meaningfully below the recent 61-85% YoY spikes,
which were driven by one-off program ramps (Indonesia, post-trough
recovery), not a sustainable run-rate. This is the intended,
conservative-biased output for stage 11's growth-assumption
cross-check.

### 3C. Capacity cross-check

B07 capex_embedded_growth_pct = 13%/yr.
SOM Yr3 implies 16.1% CAGR, i.e. Yr3 revenue of ₹552cr.
At the capex-embedded 13% pace: ₹352.9cr x 1.13^3 = **₹509cr.**

**Gap = ₹552cr - ₹509cr = ~₹43cr by Yr3.** The SOM side is the more
optimistic of the two, by about ₹43cr (roughly 8% of the Yr3 SOM
figure). This gap could close if current capacity utilisation carries
undisclosed headroom — utilisation % is NOT FOUND (B04 mgmt_question:
"What is current capacity utilisation at each of the 4 plants, and how
much incremental capex is needed for the FY27 ₹500cr revenue target?").
Until that figure is disclosed, the capacity_check reads as a
provisional gap, not a confirmed shortfall.

### 4C. Market structure

- Competitor count: narrow. A handful of global majors (BorgWarner,
  Magna, JTEKT) supply via import/JV routes; Divgi is positioned as
  the leading India-based independent manufacturer in this specific
  niche (company's own positioning, Investor Presentation).
- Concentration: high (effectively an oligopoly at the OEM-supply
  level; no meaningful unorganised/fragmented tail exists in
  OEM-qualified transfer-case supply, unlike typical Indian consumer
  categories).
- Organised vs unorganised split: not applicable in the conventional
  sense — this is a qualification-gated B2B category.
- Consolidating or fragmenting: directionally consolidating around a
  small number of qualified suppliers per platform, given multi-year
  tooling/qualification lead times.
- Price vs differentiation competition: differentiation-led
  (design-in, IP, switching costs per B04 moats), not price-led,
  though pricing power is independently rated "weak" in B04 —
  consistent with OEM customers holding negotiating leverage despite
  the narrow supplier base.
- Import share trend: export revenue % of Divgi's own total rose from
  1.35% (FY24) to 6.08% (FY25) to 18% (FY26) (AR CFO overview) —
  the trend runs the other way for Divgi specifically (import
  substitution INTO Divgi from global buyers), reinforcing the
  de-sourcing driver in 4A.

## SECTION 5: SUMMARY & RUNWAY

### 5A. Funnel

```
TAM (conservative, core scope)        ₹4,000 Cr   (Method 1+3 triangulated, L confidence)
  -> SAM (5-filter, 54% of TAM)       ₹2,160 Cr
    -> Current revenue (FY26)         ₹352.9 Cr   (16.3% of SAM)
    -> SOM Yr3                        ₹552 Cr     (20.3% of grown SAM, 16.1% CAGR)
    -> SOM Yr5                        ₹708 Cr     (22.3% of grown SAM, 15.0% CAGR)

Realistic TAM (upside case)           ₹6,800 Cr   (includes EV transmission directional upside)
AT optionality (non-contracted)       ~₹1,000 Cr  (excluded from core TAM/SAM/SOM; binary catalyst)
```

### 5B. Runway assessment

- Revenue headroom: 6.12x (SAM ÷ current revenue) — solid, not
  extreme.
- TAM growth rate: ~8%/yr (core, blended judgment estimate; flagged
  L confidence, no single sourced TC-specific India CAGR exists).
- Company CAGR vs TAM: Divgi's recent actual growth (FY26 +61.2% YoY,
  Q1FY27 +85% YoY) runs far ahead of the ~8% core TAM growth rate — the
  company is gaining share aggressively, not merely riding the market.
  This is the basis for using the aggressive (not normal) share-gain
  band in Section 3B.
- Years to saturate SAM at the SOM trajectory: the SOM path reaches
  only ~22% of SAM by Yr5, so saturation is many years away at this
  pace — SAM concentration (narrow OEM-qualified base) is the binding
  constraint, not near-term market exhaustion.

### 5C. Runway classification: **STRONG**

Headroom (6.12x) is solid and share-gain execution evidence is strong,
but core TAM growth is moderate (~8%, not a >15% secular boom) and SAM
itself is narrow because of OEM-qualification barriers (the 0.70x
customer filter and 0.85x capability filter in 3A). This combination
sits below MASSIVE (which would need both a headroom multiple above
roughly 8-10x AND fast underlying TAM growth) but comfortably above
GOOD/MODERATE given the credible, evidenced share-gain trajectory.

### 5D. SAM expansion levers actually being pursued

| Lever | Potential addition | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Automatic Transmission tech-transfer (one named-but-unnamed OEM) | ~₹1,000cr (Claim 1 segment figure) | Non-contracted; binary; excluded from core SOM per the same discipline as not carrying an unresolved probability into committed figures |
| Export scale-up beyond Indonesia (Project Mayflower, North America) | ~₹200cr (Claim 1 segment figure) | Evaluation-stage; Indonesia program itself is the only confirmed export volume today |
| EV Transmission (E-Gear Drive) scale, contingent on Sigma platform SOP | ~₹250cr (Claim 1 segment figure) | Currently -20% YoY in Q1FY27 on an SOP delay (B04 flag); execution risk before this lever can be counted |

If the AT lever alone were included at an illustrative 20%
contract-probability weight (not a disclosed probability — purely
illustrative), it would add ~₹200cr to the effective opportunity,
lifting revenue headroom to roughly 6.7x. This is NOT incorporated
into the core SOM figures above.

## VERIFICATION FINDINGS (material, surfaced per stage instructions)

**1. Indonesia unit-count correction.** This run's injected CONTEXT
cited "Indonesia 4x4 tender ~70,000 units." Independent web
verification (multiple sources: constructionworld.in, emobilityplus.com,
cartoq.com) confirms the actual Mahindra Scorpio Pik Up Indonesia
CY2026 export order is **35,000 units** — Mahindra's largest-ever
single export order, equal to Mahindra's entire FY2025 export volume.
The injected context figure was roughly double the verified number.
Any SOM or capacity assumption tied to this program should use 35,000
as the ceiling, not 70,000. Flagged in the YAML output.

**2. "40 million installed base" scoping.** The AR MD letter's claim
of "more than 40 million transmissions operating worldwide" is real
(not fabricated) but is narrowly scoped to ONE specific, unnamed North
American OEM's legacy automatic-transmission platform under
technology-transfer evaluation. It is NOT a statement about Divgi's
total addressable automatic-transmission market, and is not treated as
such anywhere in this report's TAM/SAM/SOM math.
