# Capital repaired asset quality still unproven

Dhanlaxmi Bank is a 99 year old private sector scheduled commercial bank based in Thrissur, Kerala, running 261 branches and about 0.2% of all India banking share. Net advances are Rs 13,912 Cr, gold loans are 38% of the book, and the shares trade at Rs 35.4 for a market value near Rs 1,397 Cr. There is no promoter. The public holds 85.74%, foreign investors 14.21%, domestic institutions 0.05%, as of June 2026, with the bank governed by its board under RBI single holding caps.

What is changing is a balance sheet repair. A Rs 297.5 Cr rights issue in February 2025 lifted CRAR from 12.71% to 16.12% and then to 18.92% by FY26, and CET1 from 11.36% to 15.24%, directly plugging the capital shortfall left by a Rs 711 Cr legacy accumulated loss that still blocks any dividend. FY26 net profit rose 54% to Rs 103 Cr, operating profit rose 127% to Rs 216 Cr, and the computed cost to income ratio fell from 85.42% to 73.45%. Foreign investors stepped their stake up from 5% to 15% around the raise and have held it since.

The Gate 0 read is backward looking and lands at AVERAGE, 53 of 160. Median return on assets over ten years is 0.32% and median return on equity 4.85%, both well below thresholds calibrated for operating companies, and both a fair picture of a sub scale, high cost, thin margin franchise. FY26 is materially better than that ten year median, but not yet enough to clear the bar. The moat read is THIN, one confirmed, with the emerging moat scan landing MODEST at the low end because the real contributors, capital headroom and execution delivery, are largely the same gold price driven growth episode counted twice.

The asset quality reality is the central caution. Headline gross NPA improved from 2.98% to 1.89% in FY26, but the improvement is denominator driven by fast gold loan advances growth and flattered by write offs. The cumulative technical write off pool is Rs 692 Cr, roughly 1.9 times the headline gross NPA, and its recovery rate has halved from 6.4% to 3.2%. ICRA's gross NPA including write offs is 7.45% at December 2025 against the 1.89% headline. PCR excluding write offs fell to 53.63% at December 2025, and net NPA rose from 0.86% to 1.11% in the same quarter that headline gross NPA was falling. Liquidity is the clean part of the picture, with LCR at 126.41% and NSFR at 145.48%.

Management says one thing clearly and delivers it, and stays silent on the rest. The credibility grade is B. The stated profitability focus was delivered and is verifiable in the profit, operating profit, cost and return numbers. Against that, the annual report carries zero quantified forward guidance, CASA disclosure stopped after 29.02% in March 2025, gold loan concentration is never discussed as a risk in the bank's own commentary, and the technical write off pool is never referenced in the asset quality narrative. There is no earnings call. The leadership bench was rebuilt through 2024 to 2026 with a new MD and CEO, executive director, CFO and independent director, all career bankers, several from Federal Bank.

Peers confirm the direction and contradict the magnitude. Four listed South India peers ran cost to income between 44% and 65% in FY26, so Dhanlaxmi's 73.45% is still structurally high cost even after the improvement, not yet at peer efficiency. The implied NIM path of 2.88% to 3.07% is credible against peer funding cost trends. Peers also flag that FY26 gold loan growth across the sector was inflated by an RBI April 2025 loan against securities reclassification, which discounts the optics of any gold loan growth figure, and that an expected credit loss transition from April 2027 is being provisioned by every peer and is unaddressed in Dhanlaxmi's own disclosures.

The market opportunity is real but capital gated. The addressable South India gold loan and credit market filtered to the branch footprint runs to roughly Rs 8,40,000 Cr, with a serviceable slice near Rs 2,52,000 Cr and a defensible three to five year capture implying an 8% to 9% revenue CAGR, not the 18% to 26% recent reported rate. Runway is MODERATE despite an 18 times nominal headroom ratio, because weak internal capital generation, a static branch network and an RBI branch expansion restriction cap realistic capture, and ICRA notes further scale up depends on a fresh capital raise that is not committed.

The counterweight to the repair is a live governance and concentration ledger. The bank sat under RBI Prompt Corrective Action from November 2015 to February 2019, has a recurring thread of RBI penalties including Rs 1.20 Cr in FY24 and Rs 1 Cr in January 2026 for KYC and AML lapses, carries two RBI appointed directors on its board, and runs top 20 exposures at 147% of Tier I capital and top 20 depositors at 24.5% of deposits. Growth leans on a gold price cycle against a Kerala and Gulf remittance linked deposit base, the corporate segment ran a widening loss into FY26, and the whole franchise still cannot pay a dividend until the legacy loss is absorbed.
