## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

**2A. Demand environment.** All four peers report resilient-to-strong underlying demand in their core end-markets across the review window, several explicitly framing their own growth as decoupled from (or even inversely related to) industry-level demand swings — CARTRADE repeatedly ("even when [auto sales are] slow, we grow"), RATEGAIN citing the Skift Travel Health Index as evidence travel "continues to be resilient." None speaks to Indian real estate demand specifically, so this does not directly test Aurum's segment, but the pattern of peers uniformly reporting resilience (not softness) in the same broad economic window Aurum operated in is a mild positive-consistency signal for growth-oriented small-caps generally, without confirming Aurum's specific claims.

**2B. Pricing and input costs.** Not a meaningfully comparable dimension — all four peers are asset-light software/platform businesses with no raw-material exposure. CARTRADE discusses ARPU uplift from SUV mix-shift; ZAGGLE discusses declining program-fee take-rate mix and rising interchange economics. Neither maps onto Aurum's real-estate lead-gen pricing-power narrative. No finding either way.

**2C. Capex cycle.** Three of four peers (RATEGAIN via the ~$1bn+ Sojern acquisition; ZAGGLE via a continuous string of bolt-ons — Greenedge, TaxSpanner, Mobileware/86400, Rio.Money/ZAG.money, Dice; NAZARA via Curve, Smaaash, and the large Bluetile/BestPlay acquisition) are aggressive inorganic consolidators, funding growth substantially through M&A rather than pure organic build-out — a pattern structurally similar to Aurum's own PropTiger-driven inorganic growth. CARTRADE is the explicit outlier: sitting on INR1,244cr of cash, declining a live acquisition (CarDekho) after diligence, and stating "we have almost no capex... no need from an investment standpoint" while planning to return cash to shareholders. **This reads as an industry-wide small/mid-cap platform-consolidation race (3 of 4), not a lone-expander situation** — Aurum's PropTiger-consolidation-driven revenue growth sits inside a broader pattern across SaaS/platform-adjacent listed small-caps, which somewhat normalizes (without validating the durability of) Aurum's own reliance on inorganic contribution to headline growth.

**2D. Competitive mentions.** No peer mentions Aurum, Aurum Proptech, PropTiger, NestAway, HelloWorld, Sell.Do, Aurum Analytica, or any unmistakable description of Aurum's business, across all 14 transcripts. This is expected given no direct segment overlap, and is not itself informative about Aurum's competitive standing.

**2E. Risks peers discuss that Aurum's management does not.**
- **Cash-conversion / receivables quality, discussed openly and repeatedly**: ZAGGLE analysts press on operating cash flow vs. PAT divergence on nearly every call (H1 FY26 OCF of -INR19cr, later -INR33-34cr), and management responds each time with specific drivers (festive-season card loading, trade-receivables timing) and a stated glide path to breakeven/positive OCF. This is directly the same category of concern flagged upstream for Aurum at B02/B03 (cash conversion) and confirmed at B05 as never once raised by any analyst on any of Aurum's four calls — the contrast in analyst scrutiny and management transparency on this exact topic is itself a notable gap.
- **AI/LLM disintermediation risk to organic traffic**, discussed explicitly and repeatedly by both CARTRADE (analysts pressing on ChatGPT/Google AI Mode traffic diversion) and RATEGAIN/NAZARA-adjacent commentary — Aurum's AI discussion is framed entirely as opportunity (Unified Brain, proprietary data moat), with no analyst or management commentary on AI-driven disintermediation risk to its own lead-gen/listings traffic funnel.
- **Platform/algorithm dependency risk**: NAZARA's Sportskeeda business lost ~21-38% of revenue to a single Google Core algorithm update and took 3+ quarters to recover — a concrete, quantified example of single-platform dependency risk that is structurally relevant to Aurum's own reliance on organic/SEO-driven lead-gen traffic for Aurum Analytica, but never discussed on any Aurum call.
- **Explicit no-credit-risk governance stance**: ZAGGLE repeatedly and proactively states it takes zero balance-sheet or credit risk on its card/lending-adjacent products, a governance disclosure practice not mirrored in any Aurum commentary on its KuberX loan-origination product.

## PART 4: TRIANGULATION SUMMARY

- **Claims verified: 0 of 9.** No claim reached the two-independent-peer-corroboration bar for full VERIFIED status.
- **Claims partially verified: 2 of 9** (Claim 3, cross-sell; Claim 7, counter-cyclicality) — single-peer or direction-only corroboration.
- **Claims contradicted: 2 of 9** (Claim 4, AI-moat specificity; Claim 9, AI capex disclosure specificity) — these go to synthesis as priority items.
- **Claims unverifiable: 5 of 9** (Claims 1, 2, 5, 6, 8) — no peer evidence either way; the peer set's structural mismatch (no pure PropTech comp) is the main driver of this high unverifiable count.

**Single most consequential contradiction:** Claim 4 (AI-as-a-moat specificity). RATEGAIN and ZAGGLE both back their AI-moat claims with quantified, product-specific evidence (RateGain: "up to 300% increase in ancillary revenue... 75% improvement in NPS"; Zaggle: feature dev cycles cut from 75-80 days to under 30 days), while Aurum's equivalent claim rests on one early-pilot anecdote with a wide, imprecise conversion-rate range on a sample of ~25,000-30,000 interactions. This matters because the "AI-first repositioning" trigger is a priority-7 growth driver in B05 and is precisely the kind of claim now getting a visible, better-evidenced standard set by at least two comparable small/mid-cap platform peers.

**Single strongest independent confirmation:** Claim 7 (counter-cyclicality). CARTRADE independently corroborates the general structural logic behind Aurum's claim — an asset-light classifieds/lead-gen platform asserting resilience to its own underlying industry's demand cycle via a spend-reallocation mechanism, evidenced across multiple quarters of CARTRADE's own results through both an industry downturn and an upturn. It is only one peer (not the two required for full VERIFIED), and it does not confirm Aurum's stronger "developers spend MORE in a downturn" framing, but it is real, mechanism-level support rather than mere plausibility.

**Overall:** the peer set complicates Aurum's narrative more than it either supports or outright undercuts it. It does not touch Aurum's core execution record (that is Stage 5's domain), and on two claims — the general difficulty of cross-sell monetization and the plausibility of lead-gen counter-cyclicality — the peer evidence is mildly supportive or at least non-damaging, showing Aurum is not uniquely weak. But on the two claims where hard comparison was possible (AI-moat evidence, AI-capex disclosure), the better-disclosed half of the checked peer set (RateGain, Zaggle) sets a visibly higher specificity bar than Aurum currently clears, while the other half (CarTrade's TAM style, Nazara's AI vagueness) shows Aurum is not alone in falling short of that bar either. Symmetrically: peers neither vindicate nor demolish Aurum's narrative — they mostly show Aurum sits in the less-disclosed half of a comparable small/mid-cap platform peer set on specificity, while sharing several of its structural challenges (cross-sell monetization lag, aggressive inorganic growth reliance) with at least three of the four.
