## Business Understanding Narrative

**What does it do, and how does it make money?** Namo collects dead
electronics and batteries, takes them apart and sells the recovered copper,
aluminium, iron, gold and silver. It also sells refurbished devices and
compliance credits to producers who must prove recycling under the e-waste
rules (FY26 deck, page 19). The money is the spread between what it pays for
scrap and what the metal and the credits fetch. FY26 revenue was
Rs 194.59 crore and profit after tax Rs 14.35 crore (FY26 deck, page 6).

**What is changing?** The feedstock. Namo started lithium-ion battery
recycling at Nashik in September 2025 (H2 FY26 call, page 5). Management says
batteries earn a 20% to 25% gross margin against 15% to 18% for e-waste
(H2 FY26 call, page 6). Next it plans a hydrometallurgy plant to refine black
mass into lithium, cobalt, nickel and manganese (H2 FY26 call, page 6).

**Why now?** Capacity doubled. E-waste capacity at Palwal went from 16,000 to
32,000 tonnes in the second half of FY26. Hyderabad adds 25,000 tonnes
(H2 FY26 call, page 4). Profit followed: H2 FY26 profit after tax rose 136% to
Rs 7.36 crore (FY26 deck, page 5).

**What must be true?** The battery plant must fill. FY26 battery throughput was
about 1,200 tonnes, "about 10% of the capacity" (H2 FY26 call, pages 5 to 6).
The FY27 plan needs about 10,000 tonnes (H2 FY26 call, page 8).

**What breaks it?** Scrap supply and the price of credits. Management says
"the challenge remains on the sourcing side" at 60% e-waste utilisation
(H2 FY26 call, page 14). Large appliance brands are in court against the
minimum credit price (H2 FY26 call, page 14).

## 2. Business model and archetype

Four revenue streams: e-waste recycling, refurbishment, compliance (EPR)
services and battery recycling (FY26 deck, page 19).

FY26 split, from the call:
- E-waste about Rs 180 crore, including refurbishment Rs 35 crore and EPR
  Rs 25 crore, up from Rs 18 crore (H2 FY26 call, pages 16 and 17).
- Battery Rs 12 crore from recycling plus Rs 6 crore of black-mass trading
  (H2 FY26 call, page 16).

The average credit price realised was Rs 28 (H2 FY26 call, page 17).
Procurement is 80% to 85% direct from businesses on 12 to 18 month contracts
(H2 FY26 call, pages 14 and 15).

**Archetype: commodity converter.** Spread, utilisation, cycle position and
cost-curve rank are the variables. Section 1B v3.7 Amendment 17 binds: no
spot-year ROCE feeds a destination multiple.

## 3. Competitive advantages

Forming, not present.

What is real:
- Scale among formal recyclers. Installed capacity is 82,000 tonnes a year:
  70,000 e-waste and 12,000-plus battery (FY26 deck, page 22).
- Sourcing contracts. Direct B2B supply is the stated differentiator
  (H2 FY26 call, page 14).
- Regional plants cut freight. Logistics costs "have gone considerably down"
  (H2 FY26 call, page 7).
- A regulatory floor on credit prices. "We're not doing any business which is
  below the minimum rates" (H2 FY26 call, page 19).

What is not:
- No process moat yet in hydrometallurgy. "The process is all same, just the
  know-how" (H2 FY26 call, page 17). Patents are planned, not filed.
- Established competitors exist. An analyst named BatX, Lohum and Attero
  (H2 FY26 call, page 17).

## 6. The transition and the quality ladder

FROM R1, a commodity price-taker buying scrap and selling metal. TO R2, a
cost-advantaged converter with scale, regional plants and a regulated credit
floor. The hydrometallurgy plan reaches for R3.

Mechanism: fill the doubled capacity, shift the mix to batteries at a higher
gross margin, then refine black mass in-house.

Management's claim, quoted once: "the target is to achieve 2x PAT from here"
(H2 FY26 call, page 9).

Rung-jump test. R1 to R2 in two years fits the base rate. R3 through
hydrometallurgy does not yet exist: the pilot is due "by December-Jan maximum"
and the full plant after that (H2 FY26 call, page 10). **Ladder reading: R1
leaving for R2, not yet at R2.**

## 8. Proof check

The triggers claim capacity-led growth and margin gain. H2 FY26 shows both.
Total income rose 28% and profit rose 136% against H2 FY25 (FY26 deck,
page 5). Profit margin rose from 5.6% to 7.35% (FY26 deck, page 6).

**Named gap: the FY27 claim has no proof yet.** The plan doubles revenue with
battery revenue rising from Rs 12 to 18 crore to about Rs 200 crore. That is
an 11 to 17 times rise in one year. No half year since the guide has been
reported.

Last year's guide missed. An analyst recalled a 45% to 50% growth guide for
FY26. Revenue grew 29%. The CEO blamed 35 lost days at Palwal and put the
shortfall near Rs 25 crore (H2 FY26 call, page 19).

## 12. Verdict card

**WATCH.** H1 FY27 results decide it, and they fall due within weeks.

A small company doubling capacity, turning cash positive and lifting margin
is a real R1 to R2 climb. The forward multiple on the guide is low for that.
The guide itself is the problem. It asks battery revenue to rise more than
ten times, it disagrees with itself on margin, and last year's guide missed.

**Load-bearing facts to verify first**
1. H1 FY27 revenue and battery tonnes. H1 FY26 revenue was Rs 87.32 crore
   (FY26 deck, page 31). Hyderabad starts mid-year,
   so H1 FY27 of Rs 160 crore or more fits the guide. Near Rs 113 crore
   repeats FY26's 29% growth.
2. Audited FY26 results and cash flow statement.
3. Promoter holding at 31-Mar-2026 and the takeover disclosure of
   21-May-2026.
4. Battery offtake: long-term contracts claimed after December 2025
   (H2 FY26 call, page 20). Names and volumes.
5. Credit-price court case status.

**What would change the view**
- H1 FY27 revenue of Rs 160 crore or more, with a battery line disclosed:
  PROCEED.
- H1 FY27 revenue near Rs 113 crore: the guide fails, and the name trades
  near the tier C multiple of 40x. PASS.

No Role 1 valuation was run and no target price is set.
