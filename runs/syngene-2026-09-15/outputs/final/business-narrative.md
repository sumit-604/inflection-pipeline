## Business understanding

Syngene rents scientists, labs and factory capacity to drug companies that would rather buy the work than build it themselves. Research Services made about 61% of FY26 sales and covers discovery chemistry and biology, dedicated centres run for a single client, and clinical studies. CDMO, which means contract development and manufacturing, made about 39% and produces small molecule drug material at Mangalore and biologics at Unit 3 in Bengaluru and at Bayview in Maryland. A dedicated centre or a validated manufacturing process is slow to move because USFDA, EMA and other approvals attach to the site, while open discovery work moves easily, and some clients moved it on price. About 400 clients buy from Syngene, including 16 of the top 20 global drug makers by the annual report's count and 14 by CRISIL's. Dedicated centres run on long contracts: Bristol-Myers Squibb to 2035, Baxter to March 2028, and Amgen, which only CRISIL names. Two unnamed customers made 37% of FY26 revenue, and the biologics anchor, the animal health company Zoetis, has stopped taking Librela product. Present demand comes from drug companies that want to cut research cost and time. Open discovery work follows Global biotech VC/venture funding levels, and the largest research account follows Bristol-Myers Squibb (BMS) R&D spend and pipeline guidance. Manufacturing demand waits on Zoetis Inc. Librela sales and inventory commentary, and Zoetis says it holds stock for the next couple of years. Growth rests on BIOSECURE Act implementation status, which can shift orders away from Chinese suppliers, and on the Global patent-cliff calendar, which pushes originators to cut manufacturing cost. US FDA facility inspection/approval status for Bayview (Maryland) decides whether the US plant can sell at all. Peer capacity utilisation and new-contract wins (Aragen, Anthem, Sai Life, Piramal) will show whether the industry's capacity race meets real demand. Dedicated centres hold the one working moat, switching costs built on years of co location, and the emerging moat scan credits the BMS partnership once. Open discovery has no moat. Manufacturing holds real regulatory approvals and a Moderate score for rare manufacturing capability, but that evidence is supply side only, with no named new customer and no disclosed utilisation. The run found no evidence of talent asymmetry and no cannibalisation barrier in any line, and the emerging moat scan totals 19, which is MODEST.

Syngene built the capacity before the customers

Syngene is an Indian contract research and manufacturing company, 52% owned by Biocon. FY26 revenue was Rs 3,739 Cr, up 2.6%. About 5,800 scientists work across Bengaluru, Mangalore, Hyderabad and Baltimore. Research work made about 61% of FY26 sales and manufacturing 39%. Two customers made 37% of revenue.

The manufacturing side lost its anchor. Zoetis stopped taking Librela product, and management puts the impact at nearly $50 million a year. That figure looks low against management's own January comments. Manufacturing fell from 41% of sales in Q4 FY26 to 22% in Q1 FY27. Syngene built biologics capacity for that one client first and looked for other clients later. The chairperson said it on the July call: "we were over-dependent on one large customer". Research services then lost clients on price, a problem first disclosed in Q1 FY27. Q1 FY27 revenue fell 16% to Rs 736 Cr. The operating margin was 12% after a Rs 50 Cr hedge loss, and the quarter lost Rs 9 Cr. New management, all from Biocon, calls manufacturing refill the growth engine and aims for double digit growth from FY28. On the same call, the new CEO refused to give any medium term outlook.

The backward scorecard reads AVERAGE at 66 of 160, with a core score of 56 and a moat score of 10. Return on capital fell from 13.3% in FY24 to 8.4% in FY26. Profit fell 36% to Rs 317 Cr. About Rs 77 Cr of pre tax charges came from gratuity and termination costs, and Unit 3 added Rs 25 Cr of depreciation. A third of the fixed asset base still sits in construction work in progress: Rs 1,046 Cr against Rs 3,000 Cr of net block, with Bayview still inside it. The hedge book is a live drag. The FY26 hedge reserve took a Rs 232 Cr loss after tax, and the Rs 50 Cr Q1 FY27 hit is the first part of it to reach profit. Accounting quality scores 6 of 10.

Cash is better than profit, with one catch. Operating cash flow was Rs 915 Cr in FY26, 2.9 times profit. Free cash flow after capex was Rs 547 Cr. Working capital days fell from 44 in FY24 to 30 in FY26. CRISIL reaffirmed AA+ Stable on 11 September 2026 and cites "prudent working capital management". The catch is that operating cash still fell 22%, and a Rs 221 Cr rise in payables, other liabilities and provisions held it up in a year revenue grew 2.6%. Capex also ran below depreciation for the first time in the two years the annual reports cover. Net cash including treasury investments fell from about Rs 1,800 Cr in March to Rs 1,541 Cr in June. A Rs 518 Cr income tax dispute, 11% of net worth, sits outside the balance sheet as a contingent liability.

Management's record grades D. Of eight promises tracked, one was delivered, four partly and three missed. FY27 guidance went from broadly flat in April to a single digit decline in July, and the basis moved from constant currency to rupees without a flag. The April guide already assumed almost no Librela in the first half, so the extra miss sits in the base business. A mid 20s margin for the year needs about 28 to 29% from Q2 to Q4, against 12% in Q1. The Bayview start slipped about 12 months, from H2 FY26 to the end of FY27. Management blamed Bayview for FY26 cost growth, yet Bayview cost stays off the FY27 profit line until the plant is capitalised, and the margin guide leans on that timing. In Q3 FY26 the CFO dismissed an analyst's warning on flat research growth, two quarters before management admitted the attrition. On the other side, the BMS extension to 2035 was delivered. The FY26 margin guide was met, though partly by moving costs into exceptional items. The chairperson also admitted complacency in July.

Biocon's conduct as controller grades CONCERN. 21.6% of institutional votes opposed the chairperson's appointment and pay in June 2026. Five of six senior roles outside finance changed in 12 months, filled mostly from Biocon. The statutory auditor of the subsidiary Syngene Scientific Solutions resigned nine days after signing its FY26 report. That same subsidiary carries an adverse remark in the auditor's statutory checklist, called CARO. Sales to other related parties rose 155% to Rs 209 Cr, including new biotech companies linked to directors, with no pricing breakdown. Against that, Biocon has pledged no shares, no regulator has acted against the company, and the parent's auditor change is a mandatory rotation.

Peers undercut the claim that research pricing is weak for everyone. Sai Life grew its research arm 26% in the quarter Syngene's revenue shrank, with over 90% repeat revenue. Piramal Pharma grew manufacturing 19%. Anthem called its own destocking mostly over by May 2026 and put its soft Q1 FY27 down to delivery timing. The read is direction only, because Syngene never discloses a growth rate for research alone. Peers share one risk with Syngene. Anthem, Sai Life and Piramal are all adding advanced modality capacity on FY27 to FY28 timelines. Against their own guidance, peers slipped one to four months, while Bayview slipped about 12 months on a site Syngene bought already built and validated.

Market size is not the constraint. Management cites a global contract research and manufacturing market of about USD 213 billion. The India slice that fits Syngene is about Rs 31,200 Cr, and Syngene holds about 12% of it, with 8 times revenue headroom. The runway class is STRONG. A top down share path gives about 18% yearly revenue growth, but Syngene grew only 3.5% a year over the last two years. That path also needs more capacity than the current capex builds. The market stage treats it as a ceiling, not a base case, until a named manufacturing customer other than Zoetis appears.
