## Business Understanding Narrative

CSM builds and runs digital platforms for governments. It writes software that lets a
mining department track ore, a state run a single-window investment portal, or a
health agency manage data (Concall Q4FY26 p.5). It wins this work through competitive
government tenders, then delivers and supports it for years (RHP p.31, p.32). Its cost
base is people: 1,300 employees, 180 active projects, 20 Indian states, 14 countries
(Concall Q4FY26 p.4). Profit is what is left after wages, so margin moves with mix and
scale, not a commodity spread.

What is changing: the Company just listed in 2026 and reports its first full year as
public. FY26 EBITDA margin expanded to 21% from 15%, and PAT jumped 70% (Concall
Q4FY26 p.7). Management frames this as a shift from tender execution to a platform
business, investing Rs 10 crore in proprietary intangible assets under development
(Concall Q4FY26 p.6).

Why now: the listing itself, plus an order book of Rs 357.63 crore as of 31 March 2026
giving multi-year revenue visibility (Concall Q4FY26 p.6), plus Q1 FY27 income still
growing 21% to 27% (Press Release Q1FY27 p.2, p.3).

What must be true: the margin gain is a real mix shift, not a one-off; the government
tender pipeline keeps flowing; the profit the business reports turns into cash in hand.

What breaks it: the last one already looks stressed. Operating cash flow went negative
Rs 24.20 crore in the nine months to December 2025, against positive cash flow in each
of the three years before (RHP p.89-90). Return on equity fell for three straight
periods while margin rose. That contradiction is the heart of this card.

## 2. Business model and archetype
Archetype: outsourcing partner, the GovTech / IT-services variant. The KPIs that
matter are client concentration, wallet share, contract stickiness and price per unit,
not a cost spread. CSM sells project-based digital transformation work to government
departments (63% of revenue, nine months to Dec-25), enterprises (24%) and PSUs (12%)
(RHP p.31). Management claims 90% to 95% of revenue is repeat business (Concall Q4FY26
p.5). Projects run 3 to 5 years, giving an order book (Rs 357.63 crore, March 2026)
that converts to revenue over time (Concall Q4FY26 p.6). The economic engine is
billed service hours against a largely fixed people cost; FY25 employee cost was
Rs 93.45 crore against Rs 199.24 crore revenue, well over half of total expense
(RHP p.87).

## 3. Competitive advantages
Domain depth, not price. CSM has run government mining, single-window and agri
platforms since 1998, with named wins across Odisha, Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh, Bihar,
Rajasthan and several African states (Concall Q4FY26 p.4, p.5, p.8). It holds CMMI
Level 5 and ISO certifications and repeat-customer economics above 90% (Concall Q4FY26
p.5). Customer concentration is real but falling, top 3 from 58.40% of revenue (FY23)
to 40.62% (9M Dec-25) (RHP p.238). Against this: the work is still won mostly by
competitive tender, 70.59% to 100.00% of projects across the last four periods (RHP
p.32-33), so pricing power at the bid stage is limited. Moat: forming, on domain
expertise and switching cost, not yet on pricing.

## 4. The promoters
Priyadarshi Pany, Chairman, MD & CEO, holds 93.60% pre-issue, none of it pledged (RHP
p.111-113). His stake grew from 15.51% one year before the RHP to 93.60% almost
entirely through two bonus issues, 20-for-1 in 2023 and 5-for-1 in 2025, both non-cash
(RHP p.111, p.113-114), standard pre-IPO structuring, leaving a weighted average share
cost of Rs 0.50 (RHP p.115). Neeraj Sahni is CFO (Concall Q4FY26 p.2). No employee
stock option scheme, no Group Company as of the RHP date (RHP p.109, p.421).

## 5. Financial trajectory
FY26 (concall): revenue Rs 226 crore, up 12%. EBITDA Rs 48 crore, up 57%, margin 21%
against 15% in FY25. PAT Rs 24 crore, up 70%, margin 10.5% against 7% (Concall Q4FY26
p.7). Q4 FY26 alone: revenue Rs 60 crore, EBITDA margin 26.6%, PAT margin 15.1%
(Concall Q4FY26 p.7), after an exceptional Rs 2.7 crore Labour Code charge.
Q1 FY27: consolidated total income Rs 43.25 crore, up 21.2%, consolidated net loss
Rs 8.28 crore, wider than Q1 FY26's Rs 7.35 crore loss (Press Release Q1FY27 p.2).
Standalone loss narrowed 12.7% on 27.4% income growth (Press Release Q1FY27 p.3).
Management calls Q1 seasonally weak, Q4 strongest; two years of Q1 losses is
consistent with that, not yet disproof of it.
The restated consolidated numbers behind FY26 show a different story underneath the
margin gain: operating cash flow, ROE, debt-equity and receivables turnover all turned
the wrong way over the same three periods (detail in step 9, not repeated here).
Inflection classification: earnings-led on the income statement, but
capital-efficiency-negative underneath it.

## 5A. FY27 forward read
FY26 actual: revenue Rs 226 cr, PAT Rs 24 cr, margin 10.5% (Concall Q4FY26 p.7).
Management gives no FY27 number, only "a strong full-year FY27" (Press Release Q1FY27
p.3); basis (b) applies.
- Primary (b): Q1FY27 total income grew 21.2% (Press Release Q1FY27 p.2). FY27E revenue
  Rs 274 cr. Q1 is a seasonal loss quarter, so PAT takes the FY26 margin: Rs 29 cr,
  +21%. Forward P/E 18x, P/S 1.9x.
- Alternate: order book Rs 357.6 cr at March 2026 (Concall Q4FY26 p.9) is 1.6x FY26
  revenue. If execution matches FY26's Q4 margin structure (PAT 15.1%), FY27E PAT is
  Rs 41 cr, +72%, P/E 12.9x. The corpus does not show that structure holding outside Q4.
- Separating observation: Q2FY27 operating cash flow and receivable days. A second
  negative cash quarter makes the primary the ceiling, not the floor.

## 6. The transition and the quality ladder
Management's claim, quoted once: continued investment "in strengthening technology
infrastructure and proprietary products/platforms" to move from project delivery
toward platform ownership (Concall Q4FY26 p.6).
FROM R3, value-added spec'd supplier: government-tender-driven but with real domain
IP, repeat-customer stickiness above 90%, ROE in the 20-25% band that fits the R3
neighbourhood (FY24 ROE 22.80%, RHP p.375).
TO a claimed R4, franchise-style platform leader: proprietary IP, wallet-share growth
into enterprise and PSU work (PSU order-book share 9.90% FY25 to 24.33% 9M Dec-25,
RHP p.31), and international expansion into two new African geographies (Concall
Q4FY26 p.6).
The ladder test is ROCE durability, not the story. ROE has fallen for three straight
periods while the company was supposedly climbing, the opposite of what an R3-to-R4
climb should show. Either the margin gain is real and returns catch up once
receivables normalise, or the receivables problem is the climb's true cost and the
ladder move has not happened yet.

## 8. Proof check
What fired: revenue growth, EBITDA margin expansion, PAT growth, and order-book scale
all delivered in FY26 and continued into Q1 FY27 (Concall Q4FY26 p.7; Press Release
Q1FY27 p.2). The mix shift toward PSU and enterprise work in the order book is also
visible and delivered, not just promised (RHP p.31).
What has not fired: capital efficiency, detailed in step 9. Named proof point: whether
FY27 operating cash flow returns positive and receivables turnover recovers. Until
then, the margin story and the cash story disagree.

## 9. Flags from the documents
- **Operating cash flow went negative**, Rs -24.20 cr for the nine months to Dec-25,
  against positive Rs 17.42 cr (FY23), Rs 9.59 cr (FY24), Rs 8.72 cr (FY25) (RHP p.89-90).
- **Receivables turnover collapsed** 6.80x (FY24) to 4.09x (FY25) to 2.25x (9M Dec-25),
  company-attributed to "delay in realisation of debtors" (RHP p.375).
- **Return on equity fell three periods running**, 22.80% (FY24) to 20.73% (FY25) to
  17.81% (9M Dec-25), even as EBITDA margin rose (RHP p.375; Concall Q4FY26 p.7).
- **Debt-equity nearly doubled**, 0.46 to 0.86, in nine months, on higher short-term
  bank borrowing (RHP p.375).
- **22 tax proceedings against the Company**, aggregate Rs 11.01 crore (RHP p.63).
- **Credit rating unchanged** across two CARE assessments despite the claimed
  transformation (RHP p.135; see step 10).
- **Geographic concentration still high** though falling: Odisha was 62.56% of revenue
  in the nine months to Dec-25, down from 83.95% in FY23 (RHP p.33).
- **Promoter stake built almost entirely by bonus issues**, not cash, in the year
  before listing (RHP p.111, p.113-114). Standard IPO mechanics, noted for completeness.
Not seen: full related-party schedule, board/KMP detail, director litigation detail
beyond the company-level summary.

## 11. Posture: Transition Decision Matrix
- Proof gate: NOT FIRED, on balance. Revenue and margin growth fired; the
  capital-efficiency proof a quality-ladder climb requires did not (step 9).
- Ugliness: leans STRUCTURAL. A working-capital drag tied to government payment
  cycles is a known feature of this business, and it worsened across three
  consecutive periods, not a one-off.
- Recognition gap: INDETERMINATE. No price analysis was done here; the Company listed
  only in 2026 and no re-rating history exists to read.
Proof not fired plus structural ugliness maps to VALUE-TRAP RISK. Classify DEEP WATCH:
the earnings story is real, but capital efficiency has not proven the climb yet.

## 12. Verdict card
**WATCH.** CSM is a genuine GovTech services business with 28 years of domain history,
a Rs 357.63 crore order book, and real FY26 margin expansion. Not a PASS. But the
capital-efficiency numbers underneath the margin story point the wrong way: cash
conversion negative, ROE down three periods straight, leverage nearly doubled, all in
the window management calls a transformation. That gap needs resolving before a full
`/step1` earns its cost.
Load-bearing facts a deep run must verify first:
1. FY27 operating cash flow and receivables ageing, to see if the Dec-25 reversal
   continues or corrects.
2. The primary Q4 FY26 results filing itself, read directly, not through the concall.
3. The full related-party transaction schedule and board/KMP detail, not yet read.
4. The CARE rating rationale, once reachable, for its own read on receivables and
   leverage.
5. The "Awarding of order(s)/contract(s)" filing held in Bull AI but not yet read, for
   order detail beyond the single Malawi contract named in the press release.
What would change the view: FY27 operating cash flow returning solidly positive, with
receivables turnover recovering toward the FY24 level, would resolve the contradiction
and support a PROCEED. A second negative operating-cash-flow period, or a rating
downgrade, would move this to PASS regardless of the order book. No price here.
