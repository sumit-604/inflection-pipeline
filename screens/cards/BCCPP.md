## Business Understanding Narrative

**What does it do, and how does it make money?** BCC Fuba makes
professional-grade printed circuit boards at Nalagarh in Himachal Pradesh. It
was set up in 1985 as a joint venture with Fuba Hans Kolbe & Co of Germany,
then Europe's largest PCB maker, and DEG, a European development financier.
The plant was equipped with machinery from Germany, Italy, France, the United
Kingdom, the United States and China, and the company combines that with Fuba's
process methods (Annual Report FY2025, page 53). It holds IATF 16949:2016 for
automotive supply and is certified by Underwriters Laboratories (Annual Report
FY2024, page 53).

The money is board area sold at a price set by the customer's specification,
less the cost of imported copper laminate and process chemistry.

**What is changing?** Volume and margin together, and the rate is
accelerating. Revenue went Rs 28.54 crore, Rs 28.65 crore, then to Rs 47.07
crore in FY25 and Rs 72.59 crore in FY26 (Annual Report FY2025, page 54; Q1
FY27 results, page 4). Profit before tax in the first quarter of FY27 was
Rs 5.40 crore on revenue of Rs 25.20 crore, a 21.3% pre-tax margin, against
Rs 1.89 crore on Rs 15.50 crore a year earlier.

**Why now?** The India PCB story finally has policy behind it, and the company
says so: "With the China+1 strategy gaining global traction and India
positioning itself as a trusted manufacturing alternative, the PCB industry in
India is well-placed to evolve from a largely import-dependent market into a
self-reliant and globally competitive hub for PCB and PCBA manufacturing by
2030" (Annual Report FY2025, page 54). The company has also raised money. The
board approved a rights issue of up to Rs 35.00 crore on 13 February 2026, and
the Letter of Offer is dated 5 March 2026 (Bonds / Right issue, page 1).

**What must be true for the thesis to work?** The margin must hold as volume
scales, and the rights money must buy capacity that fills. One quarter at 21.3%
pre-tax margin, against 11.3% for the whole of FY26, is a large claim to
sustain.

**What breaks it?** Technology. The company names the gap itself: "a gap in
advanced PCB technology (like HDI and flexible circuits)" alongside
"dependency on imported raw materials (e.g., copper laminates)" (Annual Report
FY2025, page 54). High-density interconnect and flexible circuits are where
the value is going. A maker of conventional rigid boards can grow with the
Indian market and still be left behind by it.

## 2. Business model and archetype

One plant, one product family, forty years of process. Revenue is boards
multiplied by area multiplied by layer complexity. Certification is the entry
ticket: IATF 16949 for automotive customers and UL for safety-critical
applications are both slow to obtain.

**Archetype: build-to-spec component maker.** Customer capex cycle, design-win
pipeline, content per unit and input-cost pass-through are the variables.

The company has one subsidiary, and it is negligible and loss-making: total
assets Rs 3.30 crore and revenue Rs 6.38 lakh at 31 March 2026 (Outcome of
Board Meeting FY26, page 11); revenue Rs 21.38 lakh and a net loss of Rs 23.95
lakh in Q1 FY27 (Q1 FY27 results, page 7).

## 3. Competitive advantages

Narrow, and real inside its narrowness. A forty-year-old PCB plant with
automotive and UL certification, German process lineage and an established
customer list is not replicated quickly. The annual report calls out "a long
list of eminent clients", which it does not name (Annual Report FY2025,
page 53).

Against that, the company competes in the part of the PCB market where India
has the least technology lead. It does not claim HDI or flexible capability; it
names their absence as an industry challenge. Moat: present in qualification,
absent in technology.

## 6. The transition and the quality ladder

FROM R2 cost-advantaged converter, a domestic board shop competing on price
against imports. TO R3 value-added and spec'd supplier, through automotive
qualification and the import-substitution shift.

The mechanism is external as much as internal: Quality Control Orders, Make in
India purchasing by original equipment makers, and the China plus one shift.
The FY2024 report puts it in the company's own words: "Under the 'Make in
India' campaign of the Government of India, many OEMs have started purchasing
domestically rather than importing their PCBs from other nations. This trend is
expected to increase further in the coming few years" (Annual Report FY2024,
page 54).

One rung, inside the base rate. Note what would stop it at R2: the company
names HDI and flexible circuits as an industry gap and does not claim to be
closing it.

## 8. Proof check

The triggers claim import substitution turning into revenue. The last two
quarters deliver it. Q4 FY26 revenue Rs 22.79 crore with pre-tax profit
Rs 2.59 crore. Q1 FY27 revenue Rs 25.20 crore with pre-tax profit Rs 5.40
crore. Both lines moved and the margin doubled.

Behind them sits a four-year record: revenue from Rs 28.54 crore in FY22 to
Rs 72.59 crore in FY26, and EBIT from Rs 1.42 crore to Rs 5.94 crore by FY25.

**Name the caveat:** one quarter at a 21.3% pre-tax margin against 11.3% for
the prior full year is a step, not a trend, and the largest single contributor
is an unexplained fall in other expenses.

## 9. Flags from the documents

- **Instrument risk, and it is specific to the operator's holding.** BCCPP is
  the partly paid line. A partly paid share carries an obligation to pay the
  outstanding call, on a schedule the holder does not control, with forfeiture
  as the penalty for not paying. It is therefore geared to the fully paid
  share in both directions. **The call amount, the call schedule and the
  forfeiture terms are NOT FOUND** in this corpus, because the Letter of Offer
  of 5 March 2026 was not retrieved. Flag first, and resolve it before
  anything else.
- The objects of the rights issue were varied after the Letter of Offer was
  issued. That is permitted and it is worth reading the reason. Flag.
- Disclosed capex in the variation totals about Rs 1.97 crore against a raise
  of up to Rs 35.00 crore. Flag the use of the remainder.
- Other expenses fell 21% sequentially while revenue rose 11%, and that fall is
  most of the margin step. No note explains it. Flag.
- Imported copper laminate is the main input and the company names the
  dependency itself. Flag input-cost pass-through.
- The company names an industry gap in HDI and flexible circuits, which are the
  high-value segments. Flag the technology position.
- One subsidiary with Rs 3.30 crore of assets generated Rs 6.38 lakh of revenue
  in FY26 and lost Rs 23.95 lakh in Q1 FY27. Small, and it is loss-making
  before it has revenue. Flag.
- Promoter holding, promoter subscription to the rights issue, pledge and
  credit rating NOT FOUND.

## 11. Posture: the Transition Decision Matrix

- **Proof gate: FIRED.** Revenue Rs 28.54 crore to Rs 72.59 crore over four

  for the prior full year, and margin expanding while volume grows.
- **Ugliness: ARTIFACT-OF-CLIMB.** Rising depreciation and a rights issue
  during a growth phase are the cost of adding capacity.
- **Recognition gap: CONTESTED.** Price basis, not a corpus document: Bull AI

  crore. Against the trailing year the market pays a very full multiple.
  Against the latest quarter it does not. The gap turns on whether Q1 FY27 is
  the new run rate.

## 12. Verdict card

**PROCEED to `/step1`.**

The operating record earns the run. Revenue has grown 2.5 times in four years
and the latest quarter more than doubled pre-tax profit year on year while
expanding margin by nine points. A forty-year-old certified board plant sitting
in the middle of India's import-substitution push is a coherent place for that
to happen.

Two conditions travel with the verdict and neither is optional. First, the
instrument. The operator holds the partly paid line, and this corpus does not
contain its call terms. That must be resolved before any position work, because
a partly paid share is a leveraged claim with a payment obligation attached.
Second, the margin. One quarter at 21.3% against a full year at 11.3%, driven
largely by an unexplained fall in other expenses, is a hypothesis.

**Load-bearing facts a `/step1` must verify first**
1. **The Letter of Offer of 5 March 2026**: call money outstanding per partly
   paid share, the call schedule, forfeiture terms, and the ratio to the fully
   paid line.
2. Why other expenses fell 21% sequentially in Q1 FY27.
3. The full use of the Rs 35.00 crore rights issue, and why the objects were
   varied.
4. Whether the promoters subscribed to their rights entitlement in full.
5. Customer concentration, and how much revenue is automotive under the IATF
   certification.
6. Whether the company has any HDI or flexible circuit capability or plan.

**What would change the view**
- A second quarter near a 20% pre-tax margin confirms the step and makes this a
  full position case.
- A reversion to an 11% margin makes the quarter a cost timing artefact.
- Promoters not taking up their rights entitlement.

No Role 1 valuation was run and no target price is set. This verdict decides only whether the
name earns a full `/step1`, and it is not a position.
