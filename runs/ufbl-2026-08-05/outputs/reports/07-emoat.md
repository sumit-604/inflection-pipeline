### 2C Growth embedded in capex — arithmetic

Consolidated PP&E (net block, owned fixed assets, excludes right-of-use lease assets): FY25
₹392.99cr (AR consolidated balance sheet, line "Property, plant and equipment 4,  3,929.91"
in ₹mn). Consolidated revenue from operations FY25: ₹1,233.05cr (AR p.19 financial summary).

Historical fixed-asset turnover (PP&E-only) = 1,233.05 / 392.99 = **3.14x**

FY27 capex under execution (guided): ₹140cr (Section 2A above).

Implied incremental revenue = ₹140cr × 3.14x = **₹439.6cr**, i.e. **~32.8%** above FY26
consolidated revenue of ₹1,339cr (FY26 revenue per Q4 FY26 call: "full year FY'26, consolidated
revenue grew by 8.6% to INR1,339 crores").

**Caveat, applied per pipeline rule 4 (skepticism over promise):** this PP&E-turnover
extrapolation almost certainly overstates near-term realisable growth, because (a) new stores
take 12-24 months to reach mature productivity (management's own words, above), and (b) the
FAT ratio blends mature, fully-ramped stores with a growing tail of immature ones, understating
the true marginal capital intensity of the next cohort. A bottoms-up cross-check using the
store-count math is more defensible for a near-term figure: 40 new stores on a base of 262 is
**15.3% network growth**, broadly consistent with management's own FY27 revenue guidance of
"22-25% overall" (which combines this store growth with double-digit SSSG — Q4 FY26 call,
"if you do double-digit SSSG, then is it reasonable to assume that we grow anywhere between 22%
to 25% revenue this year" / "On a full year basis, yes"). The FAT-methodology figure (32.8%) is
reported in the YAML block per the stage template's literal instruction, but the store-count
figure (~15%) is the operationally grounded number and is flagged as such.

**FAMILY D — Data & Digital**

**D1 Proprietary data asset**: The in-house business intelligence platform, Central Reservation
System (CRS) and Central Feedback System (CFS) (AR p.17) accumulate guest-level data used to
personalise offers by channel (🎙️, Q4 FY26 call: "we exactly understand which guest or which
customer is coming on which platform... the call center or the app would sort of tweak some
offers or campaigns"). This is the same underlying data asset that powers D2 below. Scored under
D2 only, to avoid crediting the same evidence twice. NO EVIDENCE FOUND as a standalone row.

**D2 Digital platform**: The strongest candidate in the scan and the one flagged for rigorous
testing. Evidence, all company-sourced:
- Own-digital-channel share of BBQ India dine-in transactions: 34% (FY25 AR, p.17) → 30.8%
  (Q1 FY26) → 42.3% (Q2 FY26) → 53.3% (Q3 FY26) → 60.9%/60.1% (Q4 FY26) → 65.1% (Q1 FY27) — a
  consistent, accelerating five-quarter trend (📄, Q1 FY27 presentation p.14, cross-checked
  against Q4 FY26 call figure of "60% ... up from 53% in Q3").
- Cumulative app downloads: 7.4mn (FY25 AR) → 7.9mn (Q1 FY26) → 9.7mn (Q1 FY27) (📄, Q1 FY27
  presentation p.14) — up ~31% over five quarters, a materially slower growth rate than the
  digital-channel-share metric, indicating the shift is driven more by deepening usage among an
  existing app base than by rapid new-user acquisition.
- Monthly active users: crossed 1.2mn (Q4 FY26 call, +51% YoY) → ~1.4mn (Q1 FY27 presentation
  p.14, +59.6% YoY).
- Management's explicit framing: "approximately 90% of our dine-in transaction volumes are
  driven from our own captive channels, that are our own app and website, our own in-house
  reservation center and direct walk-ins" and "we believe this is a strong structural moat that
  we compounded over time" (🎙️, Kayum Dhanani, Q4 FY26 call).

**Skeptical read (per pipeline rule 4):** the 90% figure conflates three very different channels
— app/website (genuinely digital and trackable), an in-house call centre (not digital), and
direct walk-ins (not digital, not even a "channel" the company controls — it reflects raw brand
footfall). Only the 65.1% figure is a genuinely digital, trackable, company-owned booking metric.
Whether this constitutes a durable switching-cost moat versus simply an efficiency/CAC metric is
unproven: no peer benchmark exists (B01 note: "PEER DATA NEEDED"), competitors in the same
segment (Sapphire Foods, Westlife, Restaurant Brands Asia) likely operate comparable apps, and
nothing in the record shows this channel mix reduces UFBL's dependence on Zomato/Swiggy-style
discovery for new-customer acquisition — only that existing/repeat customers increasingly book
direct. This is real, growing, well-disclosed evidence of a genuine operating shift, but the
"structural moat" characterisation is management's own claim, not yet demonstrated as a
competitive barrier. **Strength: Moderate** (not Strong), reflecting hard, disclosed KPI growth
downgraded by the moat-characterisation being 🎙️ and unbenchmarked against peers. Evidence type:
📄 for the KPI trend, 🎙️ for the moat framing. Time to materialise: already underway, ongoing.
