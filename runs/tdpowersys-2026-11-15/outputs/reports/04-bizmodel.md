## SECTION 1: THE BUSINESS MODEL IN PLAIN ENGLISH

### 1A. One line

TD Power Systems builds the electricity-making part (the AC generator) that big turbine and engine makers bolt onto their machines, plus industrial and railway motors, in factories near Bengaluru, and sells about two-thirds of it abroad (AR p.4, p.47; BRSR AR p.82: exports 67% of turnover, customers are "manufacturers of prime movers").

### 1E. The chai-stall-uncle version

Think of a firm that makes the steering wheel for a car brand. It does not sell the car. The carmaker cannot sell the car without it, so the carmaker orders in batches and pays on agreed terms. TD Power does that for turbine and engine makers, and the "car" is a power plant. Right now the world wants more power plants, mainly gas ones for data centres, so order books are full. The firm gets paid slowly, about five months after the sale, so cash sits in stock and unpaid bills. Some buyers pay money in advance, and that advance cash is what makes the cash flow look healthy for now. Ten buyers give it 84% of its sales, so if one of them slows down, it hurts.
