## THE CATALYST STORY IN PLAIN WORDS

The whole forward case rests on one thing: the new integrated plant at Avverahalli near Bengaluru. It roughly triples panel capacity and more than doubles relay capacity, it is space driven so it does not need heavy new machinery, and commercial production was targeted for mid May 2026. If it commissions on time and the IPO cash flows into it, three things happen together: revenue keeps scaling into the new capacity, the idle IPO cash stops dragging ROCE down and operating ROCE reasserts near 40%, and the growth machine has room to run for two or three years. That is why the revenue and ROCE verdicts are constructive.

What confirms it: a filing that says commercial production started, followed by a first post listing result that holds the Rs 84 Cr revenue run rate and shows ROCE recovering as cash deploys. What kills it: the KIADB deadline (22 May 2026 for production, October 2026 for the project) slips again, with the stated risk of losing the land, and the cash sits idle or funds a heavier working capital cycle. The KIADB record is the reason for caution here, because the same authority deadline has already been missed by about six years.

Two catalysts are absent and their absence is the finding. There is no documented margin catalyst, so margins hold rather than expand. There is no documented working capital discipline program (no factoring facility, no debtor day target in reported numbers), so the heavy 245 day cycle is not being actively fixed, only funded. Neither absence can be checked against management because there is no concall.

The sector backdrop is supportive but it is not Avana's catalyst. The Danish Power and Aartech Solonics concalls describe a grid modernisation and renewables supercycle running through FY27, and all peers benefit from it equally. Nothing in hand shows Avana capturing differential share, so the tailwind is context, not credit.

## OPERATOR OVERRIDE (review, 2026-07-17) valuation method for a high-growth, cash-bloated post-IPO name

The draft's first pillar build was wrong and the operator corrected it. What the draft said: Pillar 1 anchored on a normalized ROCE of about 30% (Amendment 4.5 blend), no growth premium, destination PE about 18x, framed against a trailing 28x as a de-rating that sat below CMP. The operator's ruling, in the operator's frame: "It is a high-growth company and you are giving it a PE of less than 20. If FTTCP says future ROCE is near 40%, take that. A 40% ROCE company gets a higher base multiple. And you are giving no score for growth visibility, so you must take it to the forward price-to-earnings method, because the growth has to be priced somewhere." Reasoning: normalizing the ROCE down while leaving the idle IPO cash in the denominator double-penalizes, and denying a growth premium while valuing on trailing earnings strips the growth twice.

Adopted for Phase 3:

- **Pillar 1 on operating ROCE, not the normalized blend.** Operating ROCE ex idle IPO cash is about 40% to 44% (EBIT 16.65 over operating capital about 38 Cr, screener Data_Sheet, after stripping about Rs 22 Cr idle IPO cash). Base PE = 24 + 0.3 times (40 minus 33) = about 26x (the ROCE above 33% formula, cap 30x). The AMAGI precedent governs: value the operating business on operating capital and add the surplus cash separately at 1x (about Rs 22 to 27 Cr, about Rs 10 to 12 per share). This supersedes the Amendment 4.5 normalized-30% anchor for this name, because the depression is pure cash bloat, not an operating trough.
- **Growth priced in the earnings, not the multiple.** Pillar 3a stays +0x (the documented gates are genuinely unmet on relayed evidence: order book 0.62x, SOM CAGR 14 to 15%, no delivery grade), so the destination PE is applied to the EXIT-YEAR FORWARD EPS per the operator's forward-PE-at-exit convention. No growth premium AND forward earnings, never trailing earnings with no premium.
