## BUSINESS UNDERSTANDING NARRATIVE

TruAlt Bioenergy Ltd (TRUALT), run 2026-09-18, Phase 1 lite. All figures in ₹ Cr unless stated. No valuation ran, so this file carries no entry zone, no decision and no destination multiple.

TruAlt turns sugarcane juice, syrup, molasses, maize and surplus rice into fuel ethanol at five distilleries in Karnataka. Installed capacity is 2,000 KLPD, and 1,300 KLPD of it can now run on grain as well as on sugar feedstock. Own manufactured ethanol, extra neutral alcohol and by-products such as DDGS, CO2 and dry ice made up 86% of standalone FY26 revenue from operations, traded goods 12%, and a state production incentive about 2%. Oil marketing companies cannot do without the ethanol, because the national blending programme requires it and reached 20% in ESY 2025-26. Compressed biogas from one 10 TPD plant, sold under 10 to 15 year offtake contracts, was 2.4% of consolidated income, and seven company run fuel outlets are too small to show as a separate line.

The buyers are a few public sector oil companies, and four customers took 77% of FY26 standalone revenue. They buy by tender at a price the Government fixes for each feedstock, so the core line has no qualification barrier and no price negotiation. Volume depends on the allocation each tender grants, and peers confirm that FY26 allocations fell short of bids across the sector. On the input side the business depends on the promoter family's own sugar mills, which supplied 72.6% of FY26 purchases.

Present demand comes from the blending mandate, and two public signals track it: the OMC ethanol tender allocation volumes and the national blending percentage in the Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas monthly bulletin. Supply already runs ahead of that demand, because peers put national installed capacity at about 1,995 to 2,300 crore litres against offtake of 1,100 to 1,350 crore litres.

Growth has to come from events a reader can check outside the company: a national blending percentage above 20%, the FCI surplus rice allocation for ethanol, the Karnataka High Court and Supreme Court litigation over a 15 crore litre allocation, and first commercial operation of the Sumitomo and GAIL biogas plants. A national SAF blending mandate notification would open a further line, but the SAF plant is pre revenue and has no signed EPC contract. Maize and FCI rice prices decide how much of any volume growth becomes profit.

The ethanol line has a narrow cost and scale edge from the largest installed capacity in India, 3.6% of the national total, and from running on either feedstock. That edge shrinks to almost nothing when sugar and grain feedstock are dear at the same time, and the line has no pricing power. The emerging moat scan scored the business 17.5, MODEST, with talent asymmetry and cannibalisation barrier both at zero, and a verifier recount puts the score at 14.2 to 15.5 after removing double credit for OMC status. The biogas line has contracted offtake and two credible partners but only one working plant, and the run could not establish a moat for the retail outlets or the SAF project.

# Largest ethanol maker, cash still flowing out

TruAlt listed in October 2025 and owns the largest installed ethanol capacity in India. FY26 revenue was ₹1,728 Cr, down 9% from ₹1,908 Cr, and PAT was ₹96 Cr. Revenue grew 31% a year from FY23 to FY26, off a base of ₹762 Cr. The Nirani family holds 70.55% and runs the business. The same family owns the sugar mills that sell TruAlt most of its feedstock.

The transition is a crush season distillery becoming a year round one. The capex half happened. Three distilleries were converted to dual feed on schedule in 3Q FY26, with 93% of the IPO money set aside for it drawn by March 2026. The operating half has not happened yet. Utilisation was 60.57% in Q1 FY27, against the 80% management promised for that quarter. Grain ethanol margin fell from ₹15 to 16 a litre to ₹6 to 7 a litre as maize rose from ₹17.50 to 22 a kg to ₹25.50. The CFO put core ethanol EBITDA margin, without co-products and subsidies, at 9 to 9.5%, against a 23.5% headline, and the Q1 FY27 margin rode grain stock bought before the price rise. GULPOLY supports the direction of the grain squeeze, while TRIVENI says the maize rise was single digit. A second risk sits on the sugar side, and a verifier found it after the concall and peer stages missed it. About 53% of Q1 FY27 volume was sugar based, two of five units run on sugar only, and BALRAMCHIN and TRIVENI both expect diversion of B-heavy molasses and cane juice to ethanol to be restricted in ESY 2026-27.

Gate 0 scores 61 of 160 and classifies AVOID. The binding reason is FY26 leverage: net debt was 5.20 times EBITDA and interest cover was 1.81 times. The core score is 46 of 100, and a verifier recomputes it at 43 because a three month shareholding window stood in for a three year change. AVOID holds on either number. The strong side is margin. TruAlt runs the highest EBITDA margin in its four company set, 16.8% against a peer median of 8.7%. That comparison flatters TruAlt. It books about ₹22 Cr a quarter of interest subvention and PLI inside EBITDA, while BALRAMCHIN keeps subvention below EBITDA and GULPOLY books incentives only on receipt. These two government schemes were 73.2% of standalone FY26 PBT.

FY26 CFO was minus ₹296 Cr, against plus ₹342 Cr in FY25. Inventory rose from ₹210 Cr to ₹528 Cr while revenue fell. Borrowings rose from ₹1,557 Cr to ₹1,652 Cr in the year the company raised ₹750 Cr of fresh equity, so net debt went up. DSCR fell to 0.99 times from 1.28 times. Short term financial obligations were 8 to 15 times cash on hand. Receivables not yet due fell from 69% to 35% of the book. In May the MD said ₹460 to 500 Cr of ethanol was sitting unsold. A milder reading also fits. Group company payables financed working capital before the IPO, and part of the FY26 swing is that credit being paid down with IPO cash. The half year cash flow statement due with Q2 FY27 results separates the two readings.

Management grades D on credibility. Of 11 promises tracked across four calls, 8 were missed, 2 were partly met and 1 was delivered, the ₹150 Cr SAF viability gap grant. In November the MD called the FY26 allocation a done deal. In May he dated the allocation cut to November. The same plant shutdown was told to investors as intentional and to the court as force majeure. SAF revenue slipped from FY28 to FY29, and the condition for starting capex moved from offtake first to capex first. The counterweight is real. The dual feed capex arrived on schedule, the audit opinion is unmodified, and the MD and executive director took no pay rise in FY26 while the median employee got 11%.

The promoter verdict is CONCERN. A criminal prosecution against the MD under the Factories Act, tied to a 2018 boiler explosion at a group distillery that killed four workers, is live after a Supreme Court restoration. The prospectus gave it as a case citation, and the FY26 annual report leaves it out. The auditor discloses a ₹180 Cr corporate guarantee for the biogas subsidiary's NABARD loan, while the contingent liabilities note says NIL and the related party note says no guarantees were given. 36.85% of promoter shares are pledged. The company secretary was removed within a month of listing, and NSE and BSE each fined the company ₹4.55 lakh for the missing woman independent director. The other side: a professional CFO and company secretary from outside the family are in seat, Sumitomo and GAIL are partners in the biogas business, and the promoter holding company bought shares in the open market in August 2026.

Peers confirm that FY26 allocation friction hit the whole sector, and two of three disclosed the shortfall risk earlier than TruAlt did. They contradict TruAlt's claim that demand and supply are roughly balanced: TRIVENI and BALRAMCHIN both describe national overcapacity. All three peers are cutting core ethanol capex, and TruAlt alone is adding leverage for new capacity in biogas and SAF. TRIVENI warns that standalone distilleries face solvency stress as bank interest moratoriums end, a warning TruAlt never applies to itself. Peer calls could not test TruAlt's related party sourcing or its private OMC pricing story.

The run sizes the Indian fuel ethanol and ENA market at ₹86,100 Cr on the realistic case and ₹58,900 Cr on the conservative case, growing about 18% a year. TruAlt's serviceable market is ₹4,900 Cr, 2.9 times current revenue, and it already holds about 34% of it. The five year obtainable revenue is ₹3,430 Cr, a 15% annual growth rate, and the runway class is MODERATE. The existing plants can make that figure at about 70% utilisation with no new capex, so capacity is not the limit. Allocation is, because national plants clear at 50 to 57% of capacity. Management's ₹50,000 Cr ethanol market claim holds against independent checks. Its USD 30 billion biogas opportunity is 750 to 1,900 times the one independent revenue estimate the run found.
