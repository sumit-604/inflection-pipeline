## BUSINESS UNDERSTANDING NARRATIVE

Permanent Magnets Ltd builds the magnetic parts that sit inside other companies' finished products. Three revenue lines each clear a tenth of sales. Electricity meter components run about 40% of sales: shunts, current transformers, and flux concentrators that measure the current inside a smart meter. A meter maker cannot ship a certified meter without a sensing component that has already passed qualification, so the part matters more than its small unit price. Automotive sensing runs about 23%: magnetic, current, and torque sensing parts for Tier-1 auto suppliers. Alloys run about 10%: specialty nickel, cobalt, and ZAMAK alloys for aerospace, oil and gas, and industrial buyers, a line that grew from roughly 1% of sales in FY24 as the furnace commercialised in Q4 FY26. The customers are named and few. Meter buyers include Itron, where the company holds preferred supplier status, plus Landis and Gyr and Jabil. Auto buyers include Delta, Valeo, Siemens, Pegatron, Mahle, and Mahindra. The top five customers are about 44% of sales and the top ten about 53%, and that concentration is rising, with most work done on purchase orders rather than long term contracts. Present demand traces to three tracked drivers by name: the India RDSS smart-meter rollout progress, where 185 million of a 250 million target are still to install; global light-vehicle and EV production volumes across the Western Tier-1 base the company supplies; and the top-3 global electricity-meter manufacturers that form its largest customer class. Demand should grow if these drivers hold: the RDSS rollout runs to about 2030-31 on the official pace, the China NdFeB rare-earth export-licence regime under MOFCOM gates whether a rare-earth magnet line can ship, REL Developments Ltd (UK) supplies the relay licence behind the guided H2 FY27 ramp, and Genus Power Infrastructures Ltd is the listed AMISP proxy for India meter pull-through. The competitive advantage is uneven across the lines. Meters and current transformers hold a real moat, qualification lock-in, because re-approving a new supplier costs the customer more than the part. Alloys hold a moderate advantage from backward integration, not a lock-in. Auto sensing rides the same qualification-following logic, weaker because the moat circles a shrinking pond: Itron device solutions revenue fell 9% and Landis and Gyr sold its entire EMEA metering business. The latching relay line has a named licence partner but no moat yet, because it is pre commercial with zero orders in four years. The NdFeB magnet line has no moat at all: China holds about 92% of capacity, the product is a commodity converter, and the run did not establish any cost-curve edge.
