## Business Understanding Narrative

**What does it do, and how does it make money?** Optiemus is a contract
electronics manufacturer. It builds phones, hearables, wearables, IoT modules
and accessories for brands that own the customer. The parent is mostly a
holding and trading shell: standalone FY26 revenue was Rs 724.09 crore against
consolidated Rs 1,768.62 crore, and standalone profit before tax was Rs 27.92
crore against consolidated Rs 90.67 crore (Outcome of Board Meeting, FY2027, Q1,
pages 2 and 5). The subsidiaries, chiefly Optiemus Electronics Limited, are the
business. Ten subsidiaries and a joint venture contributed revenue of Rs 833.30
crore in Q1 FY27 alone (Outcome of Board Meeting, FY2027, Q1, page 10).

**What is changing?** The customer list. In September 2025 Nothing, the
London-based brand, and Optiemus announced a strategic manufacturing joint
venture to make India a global production and export hub for Nothing and CMF
products, with over USD 100 million of investment and 1,800+ jobs over three
years (General, FY2026, Q2, page 2). Alongside it the company won an emerging
domestic smartphone brand, a global IoT module leader, Realme power banks, POS
devices and a telecom ODM programme with Accton.

**Why now?** Q1 FY27 is the quarter the wins started billing. Operating revenue
doubled to Rs 882.99 crore from Rs 435.35 crore, EBITDA rose 40% to Rs 41.32
crore, PAT rose 46% to Rs 21.18 crore (Investor Presentation, FY2027, Q1, page
16). Noida Unit 3 was commissioned in the quarter, adding 6 million units of
annual capacity.

**What must be true for the thesis to work?** Volume must arrive without margin
collapsing. EBITDA margin fell to 4.68% from 6.80% because the mix shifted to
high-volume mobile manufacturing (Investor Presentation, FY2027, Q1, page 17).
The company expects margins to improve as Unit 3 fills. If they do not, doubling
revenue at 4.7% barely moves profit.

**What breaks it?** The brand owner. Every rupee here belongs to somebody else's
customer relationship. Optiemus has no brand, no design ownership on most
programmes, and no contracted volume in this corpus. A brand that changes its
EMS partner, or loses share, takes the line with it.

## 2. Business model and archetype

One engine: contract manufacturing capacity sold by the unit. Revenue is units
multiplied by content per unit. Margin is the conversion fee less the cost of
running the line. Working capital is heavy because components are bought before
the brand pays.

The cost structure shows exactly what changed. In Q1 FY27 cost of materials
consumed was Rs 676.01 crore against purchases of traded goods of Rs 177.62
crore. A year earlier those were Rs 149.84 crore and Rs 175.64 crore (Outcome of
Board Meeting, FY2027, Q1, page 5). Manufacturing overtook trading in one year.
That is a genuine change in what the company is, and it is visible in the
accounts rather than the deck.

**Archetype: outsourcing partner (EMS).** Client concentration, wallet share,
capacity fill, contract stickiness and price per unit are the binding variables.

## 3. Competitive advantages

Forming, and narrower than the deck implies. Two assets are real. First, both
Optiemus Electronics and GDN Enterprises are selected under the government's
Production Linked Incentive schemes, for mobile phones and IT products and for
telecom and networking products respectively (General, FY2026, Q2, page 54).
PLI selection is a licence-like advantage: it is capped, time-bound and not
available to a new entrant. Second, customer qualification in phones takes
years, and Optiemus now has it with several brands at once.

Against that, there is no pricing power. Other income of Rs 10.91 crore in Q1
FY27 was "primarily driven by ongoing government policy incentives" (Investor
Presentation, FY2027, Q1, page 17). When over half of quarterly profit before
tax depends on a policy incentive, the underlying conversion economics are
thinner than the headline. Call the moat licence-derived and time-limited, not
structural.

## 6. The transition and the quality ladder

FROM R2 COST-ADVANTAGED CONVERTER. TO R3 VALUE-ADDED / SPEC'D SUPPLIER.

The mechanism is design content. An EMS that assembles to a customer's drawing
at a conversion fee is R2. An ODM that shares design, holds the PLI licence, and
owns tooling and cover-glass capability is spec'd in, which is R3. The Accton
telecom programme, described as entry "as a premier telecom ODM" (Investor
Presentation, FY2026, Q4, page 11), is the clearest R3 evidence in the file.

Management's claim, quoted once: "Expect 40-45% revenue CAGR over the next 3
years driven by: Stable growth from our existing customer base; Incremental
revenue from our new EMS partnership wins" (Investor Presentation, FY2026, Q4,
page 8).

One rung is claimed. That is within the base rate. But note the ladder's own
warning: R3 is defined by ROCE of 20-25% with stickiness, and this corpus
discloses no ROCE at all. The rung claim cannot be tested here.

## 8. Proof check

**The proof gate fired.** Q1 FY27 revenue of Rs 882.99 crore, up 103%, is the
observation. It is not a claim about the future; it is a filed, limited-reviewed
number, and it lands in the same quarter the company said Unit 3 and the AI+
programme would land. Cost of materials consumed rising from Rs 149.84 crore to
Rs 676.01 crore is the corroborating line: the revenue is manufactured, not
traded.

The proof is one quarter, not two. Q4 FY26 revenue of Rs 484.98 crore was only
8.0% above the year-ago quarter. The doubling is a single print.

The named absence: margin. The company says it expects EBITDA margins to improve
in coming quarters. No quarter in this corpus shows that happening. Q1 FY27 is
the lowest margin in the series shown.

## 11. Posture: the Transition Decision Matrix

- **Proof gate: FIRED**, on one quarter. Revenue doubled and the cost structure
  confirms it was manufactured.
- **Ugliness: ARTIFACT-OF-CLIMB.** The ugly optic is the 4.68% EBITDA margin.
  The company's stated cause, a mix shift into high-volume mobile assembly plus
  a newly commissioned Unit 3 running below fill, is the textbook shape of a
  ramp. Incubation losses on drones and cover glass are disclosed separately and
  are investment, not decay.
- **Recognition gap: CLOSED.** Market capitalisation of Rs 5,322.98 crore
  against FY26 profit of Rs 66.01 crore is 80.6 times trailing. Against Q1 FY27
  annualised it is 62.8 times. An EMS earning a 4.7% EBITDA margin on somebody
  else's brand does not belong in the 60x-plus neighbourhood on the quality
  ladder; R3's footprint is nearer 19x. The market has already paid for the
  transition and then some.

**Posture: EARNINGS-ONLY, shading to PRICED NARRATIVE.** The framework's
EARNINGS-ONLY cell is proof fired, artifact, gap closed: the climb is real but
priced, so returns must come from earnings growth alone. Here the multiple is so
far above the rung's neighbourhood that even 40-45% earnings CAGR does not
obviously clear the Hurdle from this price.

## 12. Verdict card

**WATCH.** The business is genuinely inflecting and the evidence is better than
most in this run. The price is the problem, and today's 20% upper circuit made
it worse on news this corpus cannot read.

Load-bearing facts a deep run must verify first:
1. **Today's CMF / Nothing announcement.** Its actual scope, any committed
   volume or investment, and whether Optiemus holds equity in the R&D entity.
   This is the first retrieval a deep run must make, on live web.
2. **The FY26 balance sheet and cash flow.** Net working capital days and net
   debt for a business that doubled revenue in a quarter.
3. **EBITDA margin in Q2 FY27.** Management says margins improve as Unit 3
   fills. Q2 reports in November and is the single observation that separates
   the two readings of this company.
4. **Customer concentration.** What share of revenue is the largest brand, and
   is any volume contracted.
5. **Promoter holding and warrant schedule.** Neither is in this corpus.

What would change the view to PROCEED. A material fall in the price, or Q2 FY27
EBITDA margin back above 7% on revenue above Rs 900 crore, which would make the
40-45% CAGR claim arithmetically capable of clearing the Hurdle.

What would move it to PASS. Q2 margin below 4.5% with revenue flat on Q1. That
would say the doubling bought volume at no profit.

No price. No position.

| Item | Value |

Derivation. Management states "we expect FY27 revenue to double" with a date
(Investor Presentation, FY2027, Q1, page 4), which is tier A on the revenue leg:
FY26 revenue of Rs 1,768.62 crore doubled is Rs 3,537 crore. The margin leg is
mine, not the company's, and is tier B. The low case holds the Q1 FY27 PAT
margin of 2.40% flat, giving Rs 84.9 crore, which I round to Rs 88 crore for the
stated Unit 3 fill. The high case uses 3.0%, between Q1's 2.40% and FY26's
3.73%, giving Rs 106 crore. The company guides no profit number and no margin
number, so no tier A profit estimate exists.

A pure run-rate check, tier B: Q1 FY27 PAT of Rs 21.18 crore annualised is Rs
84.7 crore, a forward PE of 62.8x.

Market capitalisation is the Bull AI company record at 22 September 2026, a
service record, not a filed document. It predates or coincides with today's 20%
upper circuit, so the true forward multiple may be higher than shown.

Longer guidance, recorded but not used: Rs 6,000 crore revenue by FY29 and over
30% annual growth in FY28 and FY29 (Investor Presentation, FY2027, Q1, page 4).

**The warning this step exists to carry.** This is the run's clearest case of
fast growth that does not screen cheap. Revenue may genuinely double, and the
forward multiple is still 50x to 60x, because 2.4% of revenue is what reaches
the bottom line. A thin-margin assembler is levered to margin, not to revenue:
move FY27 PAT margin to 2.0% and FY27 profit is Rs 70.7 crore, barely above FY26
on double the sales. Read this step with steps 5, 9 and 11, never instead of
them.
