## PASS 3 — PATTERN-BASED RE-READ, NEW FINDINGS

### P3-1 (RED) Note 35's own Debt-Equity ratio narrative misstates the direction of borrowings
Note 35 Analytical Ratios (standalone p.105) shows Debt-Equity Ratio improving from 0.70 (FY25) to 0.55 (FY26), with management's own explanation: "Reduced on account of repayment/reduction of borrowings and strengthening of net worth through retained earnings." This is factually wrong on the borrowings side. Note 31 Capital Management, on the SAME page spread (p.98), shows Total Borrowings actually INCREASED 26.6% YoY, from Rs 10,269.93 lakh (FY25) to Rs 13,006.04 lakh (FY26) — confirmed independently by Note 14.1/12.1 (Pass 1 finding). The ratio improved only because the equity denominator grew faster (the Rs 6,125.35 lakh warrant infusion plus retained earnings), not because debt fell. This is a direct contradiction between a note's explanatory text and the primary financial data it is supposed to explain — the kind of mismatch the pattern pass exists to catch. It reads as spin rather than fraud (the underlying numbers are all disclosed correctly elsewhere), but it means the ratio commentary cannot be trusted at face value anywhere else in Note 35 without independent verification.

### P3-2 (WATCH) Trade receivables and payables are explicitly UNCONFIRMED as of the audit sign-off date
Note 38 (standalone p.107; mirrored in the consolidated notes): "Trade Receivables, Trade Payables and Advances from Customers balances are subject to balance confirmation and reconciliation, if any." This means the Rs 22,712.57 lakh gross trade receivable book and the Rs 4,906.24 lakh trade payables balance — the very asset class carrying this report's single largest red flag (the 603% YoY growth in the 6-month-to-1-year overdue bucket, Pass 1 finding #1) — have not been independently confirmed with counterparties as of the 28-May-2026 audit sign-off. This is a standard SME-audit caveat, not unique to this company, but combined with the ECL-reversal pattern it raises the bar on how much confidence to place in the receivable ageing numbers until confirmations are obtained.

### P3-3 (CONFIRMED, upgrades Pass 2 NEW-4 to definitive) Note 30 Financial Risk Management structurally omits foreign currency risk after naming it
Direct re-read of Note 30 (standalone p.98-99, consolidated p.138-139) confirms Pass 2's NEW-4 finding as definitive rather than inferential. The note's Market Risk paragraph states verbatim: "Market risk comprises three types of risk: interest rate risk, foreign currency risk and other price risk." It then provides a full qualitative "Interest rate risk" subsection ("the Company has debt obligations with floating interest rates... the Company is not significantly exposed to interest rate risk") and moves directly into Note 31 Capital Management. No "Foreign currency risk" subsection exists anywhere in the note — not even a placeholder sentence. On a name where 70-75% of raw material is imported (company memory) and a Rs 210.12 lakh net FX gain sits unexplained in Other Income (Note 19), this is a clean, structural disclosure gap: the company's own risk framework names the risk and then skips it entirely.

### P3-4 (WATCH) Prior-year regrouping disclosed only in boilerplate, no specifics
Note 40 (standalone p.107): "Previous year's figures have been regrouped wherever necessary, to conform to the current year's classification." No line items, no amounts, no explanation of what was regrouped. This is standard Indian AR boilerplate and not inherently a red flag, but it is logged here (restatements_found) because it means any YoY comparison drawn from this AR carries an unquantified regrouping risk that cannot be checked from the document alone.

### P3-5 (CHECKED, clean) No going-concern doubt anywhere in the document
Basis of preparation (standalone Note 2.1 p.73; consolidated Note 1.0 p.115) states the financial statements are prepared on a going-concern basis with no qualifying language. The independent auditor's report on the consolidated financial results (p.108-109) contains only standard SA 700 boilerplate on the auditor's RESPONSIBILITY to evaluate going concern ("conclude on the appropriateness of the Board's use of the going concern basis... if a material uncertainty exists, we are required to draw attention...") — this is procedural language present in every clean audit report, not a finding of doubt. The opinion itself is unqualified ("gives a true and fair view"), signed 28 May 2026 by P Chandrasekar LLP. No emphasis-of-matter paragraph, no qualification. Going-concern language: NONE.

### P3-6 (CONTEXT, not a flag) Consolidated results combine audited full-year figures with an unaudited half-year balancing figure
The consolidated auditor's report "Other Matter" paragraph (p.109) explains that the consolidated financial results include the half year ended 31 March 2026 "being the balancing figures between the audited figures in respect of the full financial year... and the published unaudited year-to-date figures up to the First half year... which were subjected to a limited review." This is a structural feature of SME half-yearly SEBI reporting mechanics, not a company-specific concern, and is noted here only so a downstream stage does not mistake the H2 FY26 figures (if ever isolated) for a fully audited standalone period.

No other pattern-level contradictions, mismatches between notes and the primary statements, or unusual restatements were found beyond the above and what Pass 1/Pass 2 already surfaced.

### E. NOTES-BASED RED FLAGS

- ECL allowance reduced 83.6% (two-year pattern) in the same period the >6-month overdue receivables bucket grew 603% YoY, with the reversal booked to Other Income, directly boosting reported PAT.
- Standalone FY26 operating cash flow negative Rs 32.30 Cr against PAT of Rs 30.87 Cr; cash burn (operating + investing, ~Rs 77 Cr) funded almost entirely by warrant proceeds and fresh short-term debt, not organic operations.
- Trade payables turnover accelerated while trade receivables turnover slowed in the same year (double working-capital squeeze), with the payables acceleration substantially bank-financed via Buyer's Credit and bill discounting rather than organic liquidity.
- Note 30's Financial Risk Management framework names foreign currency risk as a market-risk category and then structurally omits any discussion of it, despite heavy imported-input dependence.
- Note 35's own Debt-Equity ratio commentary misstates the direction of borrowings (claims "reduction of borrowings" when total borrowings actually grew 26.6% YoY) — a contradiction between a note's narrative and the primary financial statements.
- Trade receivables and payables balances are explicitly unconfirmed with counterparties as of the audit sign-off date (Note 38), applying directly to the asset class carrying the largest red flag in this report.
- Two related-party/subsidiary interests material enough to feature in company memory and the MD&A (Nexus 26%, UVT 49%, and a second US wholly-owned subsidiary) are entirely absent from the FY26 audited notes.

### F. ONE-LINE NOTES VERDICT

The notes reveal moderate-to-concerning accounting practices. Key concern: provisioning moved the wrong direction on a rapidly deteriorating receivables book, in the same year operating cash flow turned sharply negative against a growing accrual profit, funded by external capital rather than the business itself. Key strength: revenue recognition, tax reconciliation, EPS dilution, and CSR disclosures are clean and transparently presented, and the one large one-off (Labour Codes gratuity/leave step-up) is well isolated and quantified. Overall accounting quality: 4/10.
