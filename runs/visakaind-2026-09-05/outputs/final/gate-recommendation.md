### What a rerun must fix

1. CRITICAL. Segment margin disclosure was requested four times across three
   calls and deferred every time (Q4 FY22 p.8, Deep Gandhi; Q1 FY23 p.8, Ankit,
   and p.11, Deep Gandhi; Q2 FY23 p.16, Rajat Setiya). The stage 5 report,
   Section 2E, states it was requested once and not repeated. That fact is
   wrong and the item never enters the repeated evasions list. Fix the fact and
   add the evasion.
2. V-Next margin was stated as 15% gross and affirmed as 15% EBITDA two lines
   later, uncorrected (Q1 FY24 p.8). Stage 5 carries the figure into guidance
   with no basis note. Flag the basis ambiguity and do not let a margin
   assumption inherit it.
3. Inverted inference on selling points. The claim of doubling and tripling
   every year (Q2 FY23 p.17) is falsified by the company's own next number,
   2,500 to 3,500 in 15 months (Q1 FY24 p.14). Stage 5 Section 3D reads the
   same series as a credibility positive. Reverse the inference.
4. Internal guidance contradiction unreconciled. Rs 450 Cr in FY24 growing to
   Rs 1,000 Cr by 2030 is about 14% a year, against 25% to 30% growth guided in
   the same call (Q1 FY24 p.5, p.8, p.10). Management called the target
   conservative and revised neither number. Both figures are carried forward
   unreconciled.
5. Volunteered negative missed. Management opened with "though rural income is
   down at the moment" beside roofing utilisation above 100% and a segment EBIT
   halved to Rs 27 Cr from Rs 53 Cr (Q1 FY24 p.4 and p.6). This is the corpus's
   clearest price taker evidence and it is thesis critical for the quality
   ladder rung. Capture it.
6. The 30% asbestos input cost figure was re-based to a sequential comparison
   and then partly retracted in the next answer (Q2 FY23 p.6 and p.8). Stage 5
   adopts it as established fact. Downgrade it to a contested number.
7. Vigilance Security Services promoter relationship. The answer contradicts
   itself across three exchanges, with an unnamed adverse article referenced
   and dismissed (Q1 FY23 p.7-8). The exchange is absent from stage 5 entirely.
   Add it; the promoter stage later found the same entity at the centre of an
   unresolved enforcement matter.
8. FX hedging policy question answered with a margin sensitivity rather than a
   policy, with import exposure given as "40 to 45 lakhs" with no unit and
   implausible in rupees (Q2 FY23 p.11-12). Stage 5 lists the sensitivity as
   guidance. Record it as an unanswered question.
9. Pledge severity understated. A pledge of 15.68% of total shares, including
   57% of one promoter's holding, is graded Low-Medium, and it surfaced only
   under a retail investor's question with the aggregate given after closing
   remarks. Re-grade.
10. Stage 6 Claim 5. The CONTRADICTED verdict is overstated. It rests on
    unaudited peer self description of an AAC blocks line, not fibre cement
    boards, which beat its own 4 to 5 month ramp guidance in 3 months, and it
    never reconciles a line level 50% against the CFO's segment level 75% to
    80% in the same call. Re-run the verdict at the evidence it actually
    supports.
11. Stage 6 Claim 1. The net read frames peer evidence as corroborating
    management. Visaka's actual claim was that input cost pressure was "a one
    off phenomenon", which the peer's four year record contradicts (Q2 FY23
    p.5, p.6; BirlaNu Q1 FY26 p.20, Q4 FY26 p.9-10). The correct reading is in
    the stage 6 report's own Section 2B and never reaches the verdict or the
    block.
12. Anchor convention split. Stage 5 anchors on the transcripts' printed footer
    pages and stage 6 anchors on PDF pages, differing by one throughout the
    run. A downstream re check of a stage 5 anchor against a PDF page lands one
    page early and can produce a false ANCHOR NOT FOUND. Pick one convention
    and restate both stages in it.

Seven remaining MINOR items are tabulated in verifier-summary.md and should be
cleared in the same pass.

## 6. Monitorables and triggers

Eight items, deduplicated from the annual report deep read, the emerging moat
catalysts and optionality register, and the market stage's downstream signal
candidates.

1. Watch V-Next boards revenue growth and capacity utilisation. FY26 grew 19%
   at 86% utilisation on 3,55,750 tonnes of installed capacity. Growth falling
   below high single digits, or utilisation falling while capacity rises, kills
   the transition claim. Find it in the next annual report MD&A capacity table
   and in quarterly results, and cross read it against BirlaNu's quarterly
   boards commentary.
2. Watch whether the Rs 450 Cr boards capex plan through 2030 becomes a real
   project. Contracted capital commitments were only Rs 16.87 Cr at
   31-Mar-2026, a 26 times gap against the narrative. A named project with
   location, cost, capacity and a commissioning date converts it. Find it in the
   capital commitments note of the next annual report or in an exchange filing.
   This tests whether the growth engine can physically grow.
3. Watch total debt and the funding source. Consolidated debt fell from
   Rs 534 Cr to Rs 302 Cr, helped by about Rs 70 Cr of asset sale proceeds. Debt
   rising back above Rs 350 Cr, or net debt to EBITDA back above 3 times, says
   the deleveraging did not survive the capex cycle. Find it in the borrowings
   note of the next annual report.
4. Watch the trade receivables tail older than one year against the loss
   allowance. The tail rose from 6.1% to 9.6% of gross while the allowance held
   flat at Rs 8.43 Cr. Any further widening without an allowance increase says
   the expected credit loss calibration is wrong. Find it in the receivables
   ageing schedule in the next annual report or a quarterly filing.
5. Watch the CARE Ratings outlook at the next annual review. It stayed Negative
   through a year management calls a turnaround, and the annual report never
   addresses why. A move to Stable is the cleanest outside confirmation the
   recovery is durable; a downgrade from A+ raises the cost of the debt the
   boards plan needs. Find it in the CARE press release or the Reg 30 filing.
6. Watch the subsidiaries and the related party lending book. Atum Life net
   worth fell to Rs 0.32 Cr against a Rs 7.795 Cr cost with no impairment
   booked, Visaka Green turned cash negative for the first time in FY26, and
   total related party inter corporate deposit exposure grew 19.4% to
   Rs 9.30 Cr. Watch for a further fall, an impairment finally booked, exposure
   above Rs 9.30 Cr, another loan term change, or the first ever disclosed ATUM
   revenue or order book figure. Find it in the investments, loans and related
   party notes and the AOC-1 statement of the next annual report.
7. Watch the promoter register and the enforcement matter. The promoter
   category jumped 4.82 percentage points to 53.24% with both named individual
   promoters unchanged, and Vigilance Security Services was reclassified into
   the promoter group at 4.83%. Confirm that reclassification against the
   primary filing, get the current status of the November 2023 ED and PMLA
   matter, and watch for any further unexplained move. Find it in the quarterly
   SEBI shareholding pattern on BSE and NSE and in enforcement or court records.
8. Watch the five outside demand series that drive roofing and ATUM, none of
   which depend on company disclosure: PMAY-Gramin and PMAY-Urban houses
   sanctioned and completed, IMD monsoon bulletins with MSP farm income data,
   chrysotile asbestos fibre import volumes from Russia, Kazakhstan and Brazil,
   the domestic GI and colour coated steel roofing price index, and PM Surya
   Ghar enrolment and subsidy disbursement. They tell you whether a volume miss
   is the market or the company.
