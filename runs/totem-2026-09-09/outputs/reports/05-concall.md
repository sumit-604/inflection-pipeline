## 1C Trigger evolution across the three annual reports

**Drills: STRENGTHENING, the one trigger with a clean guidance-to-delivery chain, and now a NUMBER attached
to what was being claimed.** FY24 AR: "doubling of our High-Speed Drills portfolio in the last fiscal year"
plus new Centre drill set-up (p.15-16). FY25 AR reuses that paragraph near-verbatim (see 2A/2D quality flag)
AND adds a forward-looking sentence this run traced precisely to the Drills subject, not Taps: "High Speed
Steel Drills are recent introduction to our portfolio... We are confident, this portfolio will grow
substantially over time. Consequent to the above, we have made substantial capacity investments in this
segment and we expect high rate of growth in this segment" (p.19). FY26 AR confirms delivery in narrative
("our actions of the past to increase the capacities in Drill segments... has paid off and we are seeing
substantial growth", p.25) AND in the capacity data (+~350,000 unit CNC addition) AND in the quarterly
numbers this stage can check independently (screener CSV: quarterly sales accelerate from a Rs 52.41cr
trough in Q1 FY26 to Rs 70.96cr in Q4 FY26 to Rs 67.55cr in Q1 FY27, +28.9% YoY).

**Taps: the FY25→FY26 "will grow substantially over time" claim is a TEMPLATE REUSE, not a two-cycle repeat
of the same claim on the same product — correcting run 1.** Run 1 read the FY25 sentence "We are confident,
this portfolio will grow substantially over time" as attaching to Taps. Read in full sentence order, FY25 AR
p.19 attaches TWO separate claims to TWO separate products: Taps gets "the required confidence of our ability
to scale up the business substantially" (a distinct, less specific sentence, earlier in the same paragraph
block); the sentence "We are confident, this portfolio will grow substantially over time. Consequent to the
above, we have made substantial capacity investments in this segment and we expect high rate of growth in
this segment" immediately follows "High Speed Steel Drills are recent introduction to our portfolio" — it is
about DRILLS. FY26 AR then takes that exact template and reassigns it to Taps: "Our Taps segment recorded
steady growth across domestic and export markets... We are confident, this portfolio will further grow
substantially over time. Consequent to the above, we have made substantial investments in this segment and
we expect high rate of growth in this segment" (p.26). So the finding is not "two years, zero numbers,
same claim" (run 1's framing); it is a management team that keeps a stock sentence template and reassigns it
to whichever product it wants to talk up that year, with capex additions actually FALLING (Rs 2,612.20 lakh
FY25 → Rs 1,498.65 lakh FY26) in the very year the template moved to Taps. That is a weaker, more mechanical
signal than a genuine repeated Taps claim — and arguably more concerning as a drafting practice, because it
shows the sentence is not written from Taps-specific evidence at all.

**Export/channel: the honest finding is worse than run 1's "weakening then reversed" framing — one claim was
false when made, and the reversal language is boilerplate, not a fresh FY26 read.** FY24 AR: "overall export
market has not been very encouraging" (p.16). FY25 AR upgrades the language to "an improving trend in the
export business performance" (p.19) — but the SAME annual report's own Annexure IV (p.33) reports export FOB
value FELL, Rs 3,834.10 lakh (FY24, per FY24 AR Annexure III p.29) to Rs 3,791.05 lakh (FY25), a 1.1% decline.
The qualitative claim and the hard number sit in the same document and point opposite ways; this is not a
promise later reversed by an external shock, it is a claim that did not match the company's own filed export
figure in the year it was made. FY26 AR then reverts to "The overall export market has not been very
encouraging" (p.25) — but this exact phrase is verbatim across all THREE annual reports (FY24 p.16, FY25
p.19, FY26 p.25), so it reads as recycled boilerplate that happens to be re-attached to a real, dated,
checkable shock this year (US/Mexico tariffs, named specifically), not a management team freshly diagnosing a
FY26-specific reversal. The tariff attribution itself is independently plausible (US tariff actions on India
in this period are a matter of public record) and the FOB figure did fall further, Rs 3,791.05 lakh (FY25) to
Rs 3,189.46 lakh (FY26), -15.9% (FY26 AR Annexure IV, p.42) — so the DIRECTION of the FY26 claim is
supported by the number, even if the specific sentence used to say so is not written fresh. Net effect: two
of the three years' export commentary either doesn't match the filed number (FY25) or is reused text rather
than a fresh analysis (FY24, FY26); only the underlying FOB series is a reliable read, and it shows a
declining trend across all three years management ever reports it (Rs 3,834.10cr → Rs 3,791.05cr → Rs
3,189.46cr), a story the qualitative language never once states plainly.

**Price pass-through on commodity inflation: RUN 1's CONCLUSION WITHDRAWN.** Run 1 used "materials cost to
revenue rose from 32.0% to 37.2%" as its numeric support for "pass-through quietly weakening." Recomputed on
the P&L exactly as filed (FY26 AR p.70), that ratio (Cost of materials consumed only, against Revenue from
operations) is real: Rs 7,446.42 lakh / Rs 23,266.17 lakh = 32.01% (FY25) → Rs 9,331.99 lakh / Rs 25,101.13
lakh = 37.18% (FY26). But this line by itself is not the cost that was actually matched against what the
company SOLD — Ind AS presentation separates raw-material consumption from the "Changes in inventories of
finished goods, work-in-progress and stock-in-trade" line specifically so a reader can net the two.
Consumption-matched (materials consumed + purchases of stock-in-trade + change in inventories, i.e. the cost
of goods that actually left the P&L against revenue sold): FY25 = (7,446.42 + 31.90 + 503.96) / 23,266.17 =
34.31%; FY26 = (9,331.99 + 0 + (-1,252.37)) / 25,101.13 = 32.19%. That is a ~212bp IMPROVEMENT, not a
deterioration. The raw materials-consumed line rose because the company bought and consumed more raw
material than it sold through in FY26 (see LBF-2 below) — the excess sits in inventory, not in the cost that
hit the P&L. The real FY26 EBITDA squeeze (Operating Profit Margin 18%→16.39%, per the AR's own ratio table)
traces instead to Employee benefits expense (Rs 4,403.22 lakh → Rs 5,044.18 lakh, +14.6%) and Other Expenses
(Rs 5,767.60 lakh → Rs 6,683.38 lakh, +15.9%), both growing roughly twice as fast as the 7.9% revenue growth
(Rs 23,266.17 lakh → Rs 25,101.13 lakh). Run 1's "pass-through quietly weakening" finding is WITHDRAWN and
replaced: the qualitative language did soften ("successfully passed on" FY24 → "trying to pass on as much...
as possible" FY26), which may still be true at the Solid Carbide segment level specifically (the AR's own
FY26 text ties the weaker phrase to Solid Carbide margin pressure, not the whole portfolio), but the
consolidated numbers this run can check do not support treating it as the company-wide margin driver. The
company-wide margin driver is opex growth outpacing revenue, not incomplete material pass-through.

**No trigger disappeared outright, with one exception: the Carbide Taps technology investment.** Every other
trigger named in FY24 AR reappears in some form in FY26 AR. The Carbide Taps technology claim does not: it is
present, word for word, in FY24 and FY25 AR, and is simply absent from FY26 AR's full Technology Absorption
and R&D section, with no delivery statement, no failure statement, no mention at all. This is the clearest
single case in the corpus of a specific forward claim, carried across two consecutive annual cycles, that
gets silently dropped rather than resolved either way.

**New trigger appearing only in FY26 AR, with no prior-year lead-in:** the Austempering furnace (+90 MT) and
the digital transformation/SAP claim both appear for the first time in FY26 AR with zero mention in FY24 or
FY25 AR of either being planned. The furnace investment is capitalised and verifiable (per B03, gross block
additions data); the SAP claim is not (no capex line specific to it, no metric, R&D spend NIL the same year,
as it has been every year).

**Inventory build (LBF-2): HOLDS WITH NUANCE — the single most valuable item this run settles.** The verifier
argued the raw-material-led composition of the build (+170%) resolves LBF-2 in favour of "ramp stock ahead of
demand." My own read of the category split (AR2026 Note 8, p.92) is more qualified. Of the Rs 2,449.08 lakh
total inventory increase FY25→FY26 (Rs 3,193.07 lakh → Rs 5,642.15 lakh, +76.7%):
- Raw materials rose Rs 738.71 lakh → Rs 1,993.71 lakh, +169.9% (+Rs 1,255.00 lakh, 51.2% of the total
  increase). This aligns with CIF raw-material imports rising 60.2% in the same year (Rs 1,823.74 lakh →
  Rs 2,921.18 lakh, FY25 AR p.33 vs FY26 AR p.42) and with the AR's own "abnormal" commodity-price-escalation
  language (p.25). This part of the build has a real, external, dated explanation: buy-ahead against a
  commodity price spike, not unsold product.
- Work-in-progress rose Rs 693.91 lakh → Rs 1,277.50 lakh, +84.1% (+Rs 583.59 lakh); Finished goods rose
  Rs 1,541.74 lakh → Rs 2,210.52 lakh, +43.4% (+Rs 668.78 lakh). Combined, WIP+FG add Rs 1,252.37 lakh — 48.8%
  of the total increase, and this figure reconciles exactly to the P&L's "Changes in inventories" line
  (-Rs 1,252.37 lakh, per the pass-through recomputation above). This is the part of the inventory build that
  is closest to "goods made and not yet sold." Finished goods alone grew 43.4% against 7.9% revenue growth —
  roughly 5.5x faster than sales, and neither the MD&A nor the notes connect this to committed order volume,
  Drills-segment sell-through, or any other demand signal.
My own ruling: LBF-2 is PARTIALLY resolved, not fully resolved as the verifier frames it. The raw-material
half of the build (51% of the increase, the single largest category) has external, evidenced support as
price-driven stockpiling, not demand failure. The finished-goods-plus-WIP half (49% of the increase, growing
far faster than revenue) does not have that support; it is exactly the part of inventory a reader would want
explained before accepting "ramp stock ahead of confirmed demand," and management supplies no such
explanation anywhere in the corpus. The observation that separates the two readings: whether the RM share
(largest single line item, aligned to import data) or the FG+WIP share (roughly equal in rupee terms, growing
faster than sales, and unexplained) is treated as decision-relevant. Both are real; neither alone settles the
question.
