## The engine the thesis would rest on

A capex-and-subsidiary transition: an asset-light RoW exporter with a
registration moat moves into owned plants (injectables, devices, oral solids)
to capture manufacturing margin and triple revenue in three years. The bull
reading: registrations plus owned capacity lift revenue per registration and
margin, and the subsidiaries reach their stated peak revenues. The bear
reading: growth is bought with debt, dilution and stretched distributor
credit (208 debtor days, CFO near zero for four years), and the subsidiary
margins are aspirations. The observation that separates them: receivable days
and CFO in FY27 as TLL Parenterals commissions, alongside subsidiary revenue
against the stated peaks.
