## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

### 2A Demand environment
Split between order books and conversion. ZENTEC consolidated order book Rs 1,239 Cr at 30-Jun-2026 after Rs 141.6 Cr executed and Rs 44.6 Cr booked (Q1 FY27, Chalat, [page 4]); DSSL order book Rs 3,104 Cr (Q1 FY27, Anjaria, [page 4]) but revenue Rs 313 Cr vs Rs 328 Cr a year earlier (Q1 FY27, [page 4]); ADSL Q1 FY27 revenue +19% to Rs 260 Cr but "two quarters... a little bit of silence with respect to larger order wins from the government side" (Q1 FY27, N. Shah, [page 11]). Consensus: demand is claimed strong, conversion is gated by government timing and component supply. This is a more cautious frame than Kwick's 3.5x revenue story. No peer shows growth near Kwick's pace.

### 2B Pricing and input costs
Consensus on cost inflation. DSSL: AI-driven supply tightness raised component prices, budgets "shooting up by 30%, 50%", customers split orders by priority (Q1 FY27, management, [page 8]); lead times deferred revenue (Q1 FY27, Anjaria, [page 4]); back-to-back OEM pricing protects signed orders (Q3 FY26, [page 12]). ADSL: equipment cost "went up by 25%, 30%", Western Railway orders of Rs 165 Cr and Rs 85 Cr went to rebid (Q4 FY26, N. Shah, [page 14]); about Rs 180 to 200 Cr of railway orders exited (Q1 FY27, N. Shah, [page 11]). ZENTEC: own IP holds gross margin 72.9%. Match to Kwick: Kwick's cyber and digital resale line (33.32% of FY26) is exposed to the same hardware inflation, and B05 shows no input-price risk. Whether Kwick has back-to-back OEM pricing is NOT FOUND.

### 2C Capex cycle
Not a capacity race and no lone expander. ZENTEC builds machinery and R&D capacity (Q3 FY26, A. Atluri, [page 4]) but outsources production; DSSL PPE rose Rs 8 Cr to Rs 68 Cr and ROU to Rs 158 Cr total fixed assets for as-a-service (Q4 FY26, Anjaria, [page 4]; Q4 FY26, [page 6]); ADSL deliberately stays out of owning data centres, "game of skills" (Q3 FY26, Nitin Shah, [page 12]). The shared burden is working capital: Zen WC 194 to 196 to 257 days, DSSL net WC 14 to 17 days with supplier credit, ADSL higher finance cost. Kwick's IPO use of Rs 31.42 Cr for working capital fits this pattern.

### 2D Competitive mentions
No peer names Kwick, forensic tools or any forensic competitor. Zen mentions state police only as a user of its smart border suite (Q1 FY27, A. Atluri, [page 14]). Peer mentions of Kwick: none.

### 2E Risks peers raise that Kwick does not
- Hardware price spike between bid and award voids or rebids tenders (ADSL Q4 FY26 [page 14]; Q1 FY27 [page 11]).
- OEM and component lead times defer revenue on orders in hand (DSSL Q1 FY27 [page 4]).
- Auditor qualifications on aged receivables, unbilled revenue, inventory physical verification, then a Rs 36 Cr ECL true-up (ADSL Q3 FY26 [page 16]; Q4 FY26 [page 13]).
- Warranty true-ups: Zen Rs 3.1 Cr (Q4 FY26 [page 8]) and Rs 2.88 Cr (Q1 FY27 [page 4]).
- Working-capital days inflate on supplier advances ahead of delivery (Zen Q1 FY27 [page 4]). Kwick's other current assets rose 91.8% (B05), the same signature.
- Provision release flatters a base quarter, Rs 7.65 Cr (Zen Q1 FY26, cited Q1 FY27 [page 4]).
- Election periods block permissions and billing (ADSL Q3 FY26 [page 10]).
- Government line can shrink while total grows (ADSL FY26 [page 5]).

## PART 4: TRIANGULATION SUMMARY

- Claims verified: 2 of 8 (gross-margin mechanism; named-order disclosure practice).
- Partially verified: 3 of 8 (receivable days, Q4 loading, concentration).
- Unverifiable: 3 of 8 (dealer channel, BNSS and grants, BG and LD). Structural, not adverse.
- Claims contradicted: 0. No peer states the opposite of a Kwick claim. Nothing goes to synthesis as a contradiction.
- Most consequential finding (a complication, not a contradiction): Kwick's 51% Q4 FY25 share against 26% to 34% at peers, and its 74 to 79 day receivables against 75 to 154 days at peers. Both say Kwick's working-capital profile is better than any peer's on paper. Peers earn long receivables from government and BFSI milestone billing. Kwick's low days therefore rest on its private mix, advances and a basis that B05 already flags. LBF-2 and LBF-3 stay open.
- Strongest independent confirmation: three peers confirm that hardware and resale lines carry thin gross margin and that heavy hardware mix compresses it. This supports the B05 reading that Kwick's gross margin fall (37.30% to 27.06%) is mix-driven, and warns that further compression can come from input cost alone.
- Overall: the peer set complicates the Kwick narrative. It supports the margin mechanism and the disclosure benchmark. It cannot speak to forensic demand or the dealer channel, and the silence on a BNSS-driven demand wave is notable but not evidence. It adds three cautions Kwick's prospectus does not: hardware price risk, supplier-credit divergence, and revenue pauses after a big government year. Symmetric note: the peer record is also not hostile to Kwick. No peer shows a forensic competitor, and no peer describes a failure mode specific to Kwick's engine.

cross_peer_hypothesis: "In government and BFSI hardware integration, headline receivable days are not the binding metric. The three peers keep net working capital low by stretching OEM and distributor credit (DSSL net WC 14 to 17 days while receivables doubled over four years, payables about 134 days on an uncontested analyst figure, Q4 FY26 [page 10]; Zen WC 196 to 257 days only because it holds Rs 1,217 Cr cash, Q1 FY27 [page 4]), and the receivable risk shows up later as aged-debtor, unbilled-revenue and ECL items (ADSL Q3 FY26 [page 16], Q4 FY26 [page 13]). No peer states this pattern; each tells only its own story. Kwick plans the opposite: creditor days 44 to 30 and debtor days 74 in FY27 (B05 guidance, prospectus pp.95-96), so its Rs 44.42 Cr gap depends on collections and IPO cash, not supplier credit. Testable: Kwick H1 FY27 creditor days, credit terms with its top supplier (40.86% of purchases), and ageing of receivables beyond 180 days. If creditor days stay near 44 while debtor days hold near 74, the gap is smaller than B05 models. If creditor days fall to 30 and debtor days drift above 80, the working-capital object is under-sized."
