Water treatment EPC scaling faster than cash

Apex Ecotech builds water treatment plants to order. It wins a contract, buys the equipment, assembles the plant, and hands it over. About 96 paise of every rupee comes from these turnkey projects and only 4 paise from running and maintaining plants afterward. Sales were about 149 Cr in FY26, profit about 17 Cr, and the shares trade near 19 times earnings at a market value of about 319 Cr. The company listed on the SME board in December 2024, carries almost no debt, and holds about 34 Cr of net cash.

FY26 was a step change. Sales more than doubled, up about 110 percent, and profit almost doubled. One order from Reliance Consumer Products and its bottler did roughly 70 percent of the second half. The closing order book of about 125 Cr sits below a single year of revenue, and book to bill has slipped to 0.84 times, so new orders are filling the tank slower than old ones ship.

The numbers split cleanly into a strong side and a weak side. Gate 0 scored 86 of 160 and lands at AVERAGE, capped there by cash conversion, not by returns. Cumulative operating cash flow to profit over FY25 and FY26 was 0.41, and even in the FY26 boom year the company converted only about 40 paise of each rupee of profit into cash. The receivables picture is better than the headline once the FY26 filing is read directly. After the Note 7 regrouping of retention money, trade receivables grew about 45 percent against revenue up 110 percent, collection days fell from about 59 to about 41, and retention money fell in absolute terms, so the build reads as growth funding rather than slipping collection. The 61.72 Cr working capital management quotes includes about 34 Cr of cash; operating working capital excluding cash is about 27 Cr, near 65 days, and the largest FY26 cash use was a 9.6 Cr rise in short term loans and advances tied to executing the order book, not uncollected sales. Margins have drifted down three years running, with EBITDA margin easing from about 16.8 percent to about 14.6 percent as material cost climbed toward 76 percent of sales. The forward emerging moat score was 10.1 against a 12 threshold, which reads NONE.

Management does what it says on numbers and slips on governance. It guided to at least 25 percent revenue growth for FY26 and delivered 110 percent, and order to revenue conversion held to the stated gestation window. Against that, the credibility grade is B because the softer promises kept missing: quarterly reporting was requested on all three calls and delivered once as a percentage only circular, the ESOP for core staff was promised for end FY26 and then never mentioned, the international push was abandoned, and on a live call management and its CFO could not reconcile their own order book figures. The governance file adds independent director attendance below 42 percent in the listing year, a remuneration disclosure that contradicts itself across two statutory sections, three company secretaries in 16 months, and a Bank of India cash credit facility of about 665 lakh that appears as cash with no matching entry in the borrowings note.

Peers did not back the company where it needed support most. The claim of 25 to 40 percent raw material inflation in H2 FY26 found no corroboration at that size or timing; two peers show only a 1 to 2 point EBITDA hit, later, and tied to Middle East shipping disruption rather than a broad metals move. The ZLD penetration claim central to the runway story and the top customer concentration both stayed unverifiable across four peers. Three of the four peers also cut guidance through FY26 on execution and payment timing, with one printing revenue down 6.2 percent, which shows how quickly this kind of order book can stall. Our own peer evidence carried citation errors that surfaced in review and widen the error bars on the read.

The runway is real and large. The addressable market runs about 11,250 to 14,500 Cr, the serviceable slice about 6,375 Cr, and the three year serviceable target about 277 Cr against a current share of 2.33 percent, which classes the runway STRONG with roughly 43 times revenue headroom. The binding constraint is not market size. It is order book replenishment, which already lags revenue, so the serviceable path is the optimistic side of the range.

Valuation lands on a wait, not a buy. The four pillar exit multiple is 16.8 times on the conservative required return track that governs, and 20 times on the additive track pinned at the EPC sector cap. That puts base case fair value on the governing track near 394, an entry zone of 161 to 202, and a margin of safety price of 161. At 242 the stock trades about 20 percent above the top of the entry zone. Probability weighted expected return is 16.7 percent against the 25 percent target, the Hurdle Ratio is CONDITIONAL because the base case fails and only the bull case passes, and upside to downside on the binding cash tail is about 1.9 times. The decision is WATCHLIST at a Small size.

The thesis carries one trigger and one kill switch. It works if a working capital unwind lifts cash conversion as growth cools, and it breaks if H1 FY27 operating cash flow to profit prints below 0.7 times while working capital days climb faster than sales, which flips the cash read to structural and drops the exit multiple toward 11 to 16 times. The devil's advocate review left the thesis WEAKENED BUT ALIVE, with moat durability rated destroyed because the marketed Veolia alliance was denied by management, there is no IP or backward integration, and about 70 percent of recent execution rests on two customers.
