**Verdict-discipline note on Q2 (item 8 from both audits, resolved here).** The stage's own Part 1 verdict
rules are asymmetric by design: VERIFIED requires "two or more independent peers," CONTRADICTED
requires only "one or more peers state the opposite; quote them." Both audits asked why a single-peer bar
was accepted for CONTRADICTED but not for VERIFIED. The asymmetry is intentional and this run keeps
the CONTRADICTED verdict on Q2, for a stated reason: affirmatively confirming a claim needs broad
corroboration to guard against one unrepresentative source; a single credible, contemporaneous,
document-verified contradiction is a different kind of evidence — it does not need to be confirmed by a
second source to be worth surfacing as a caution, because its function is to withdraw confidence in TOTEM's
own claim, not to establish an alternative fact about the whole industry. The Wendt quote is verified accurate
at p.3, the fiscal window is identical, and the contradiction is on the empirical DIRECTION of TOTEM's own
specific export-trend claim, not on Wendt's underlying cause (which is company-specific and does not need
to match TOTEM's cause for the direction-level contradiction to hold). Softening this to PARTIALLY VERIFIED
would blur a clean, checkable, single-fact contradiction into the same bucket as genuinely thin one-peer
corroboration, which is not the same evidentiary situation. CONTRADICTED stands.

**The single most consequential contradiction.** TOTEM's FY24-25 AR export claim and Wendt's FY24-25
export result point in opposite directions in the identical twelve months (Q2 above). This does not prove
TOTEM's claim was knowingly false, but it removes the one piece of independent corroboration a reader
might have assumed existed for TOTEM's own export narrative in that year, inside a corpus that already
flags TOTEM's export commentary as reversing hard one cycle later.

**The single strongest independent confirmation.** The industry-wide capacity race (Part 2C): both peers
with transcripts are expanding capacity in overlapping windows, on demand-led (not speculative) terms,
which corroborates that TOTEM's own Drills-capacity growth trigger sits inside a genuine sector tailwind
rather than being a company-specific bet running against the grain.

**Overall: the peer set complicates more than it either supports or undercuts TOTEM's narrative.** It
corroborates the structural growth story (domestic demand growing mid-to-high single digit sector-wide, a

PART 5: CROSS-PEER HYPOTHESIS (rebuilt without the withdrawn "worst quarter" framing)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

**Hypothesis: in this precision-tooling and abrasives sector, the capex/machine-adjacent segment behaves
as a leading but noisy indicator that overshoots in both directions around a capacity cycle, while the
consumable/recurring segment is the true stabiliser — meaning a post-capex margin jump should be treated
as provisional for longer than one quarter before being read as a structural step-change.**

No single peer states this; it emerges only by placing separate peer facts side by side. First, Kennametal's
own machine-tools (MSG) segment swings far harder than its hard-metals consumable segment: domestic
CNC machine growth of 46% (Mar-2024, p.6) against hard-metal consumable growth of 6-11% across the
same set of calls; a ~25% QoQ MSG degrowth from China order weakness in one quarter (May-2023, p.3)
against a stable ~9% hard-metal growth in the same period. Second, Wendt's own capex spike (Rs11cr to
Rs58cr, +422%, FY24-25) landed inside a quarter (Q1 FY26) where sales still grew 6% and the domestic
Super Abrasives (consumable-adjacent) segment grew 11%, while the machine-tools segment fell 18% and
EBITDA fell about 18%, driving PAT down 34% even though it stayed positive — the capex-heavy,
machine-adjacent line item is again the volatile one, and the consumable line is again the stable one. This
is a narrower and more defensible claim than Run 2's withdrawn "worst quarter" framing: it is not that a
capex spike caused a historic collapse, but that in BOTH peers, independently, capex-adjacent segments
show materially more quarter-to-quarter volatility than consumable segments, in both directions.

TOTEM's own Q1 FY27 margin jump (16.1% to 22.9%) follows directly from its own capex delivery (Drills CNC
capacity, confirmed in the FY26 AR). The peer pattern suggests this kind of post-capex margin move is, in
this sector, more often a transient re-basing that takes several quarters to settle than an immediate new
structural plateau — Kennametal's own language for its comparable episode was "sequential improvement"
over FOUR quarters (Mar-2024, p.7), not a one-quarter jump. This is testable: if TOTEM's Q2-Q4 FY27
margin holds inside a narrowing band around the Q1 FY27 level, or moves toward it gradually over several
quarters (consistent with Kennametal's own multi-quarter settling pattern), the hypothesis is supported; a
single-quarter flat-line at exactly 22.9% with no further movement in either direction, sustained for a full
year with no volatility at all, would be the pattern this hypothesis predicts is least likely, given how volatile
the capex-adjacent segments at both peers turned out to be.
