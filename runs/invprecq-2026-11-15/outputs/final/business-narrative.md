## BUSINESS UNDERSTANDING NARRATIVE

Investment & Precision Castings makes metal parts to each customer's drawing, from 8 grams to 330 kilograms, at a single site. It coats a wax copy of each part in ceramic, pours molten steel or superalloy into the shell, and machines and inspects most parts before they ship. Auto castings for engines, transmissions, exhaust gas recirculation and turbos made 64% of FY26 castings sales. Pump, valve, turbine, impeller and tractor parts made 30%, and superalloy aerospace and defence parts made 6%. A buyer cannot switch suppliers easily, because each part is built to print and qualified, and a defence or aerospace approval takes months to years of audits. The named auto buyers are Maruti Suzuki, Mahindra & Mahindra, Tata Motors and Royal Enfield, and the top five customers take about half of income. They order against their own build schedules, reset prices for metal cost from time to time, and hold the bargaining power because they are far larger. The defence buyers in the filings are HAL, the Gas Turbine Research Establishment of DRDO, MKU, and PLR Systems, a joint venture of Adani and Israel Weapon Industries. Present demand rests on Indian vehicle production, ICE and hybrid, which gave about 91% of the FY26 sales increase. Tractor and farm equipment volumes carry 10% of sales, and India casting exports and US tariff changes govern the 12% sold abroad. Growth outside auto depends on HAL purchase orders and supply schedules, on GTRE/DRDO engine programmes, and on MoD capital procurement and defence production data, the one budget behind all three. Defence production reached ₹1.78 lakh crore in FY26, up 15.6%, and the plant runs at about 60% of its 3,060 tonne capacity, so volume can rise without new tonnage. Peer calls show new programmes arrive 6 to 18 months after the first stated date, and the nickel and cobalt price sets the cost of each superalloy part. The auto line has no moat: pricing power is weak and the customer sets the terms. The industrial line has qualification stickiness, but the run did not establish a moat there. The aerospace and defence line has a narrow moat that is still forming: rare vacuum superalloy capability and qualification lock in each score moderate, and the emerging moat score of 7.8 classifies as none. Talent asymmetry scores zero for lack of evidence, and the cannibalization barrier scores zero because Azad and PTC sell the same class of parts. Own solar and wind plants supply about 40% of the energy, which is a cost edge of short to medium life, not a moat.

Auto caster bets on defence, cash lags

FY26 revenue from operations was ₹188 crore, up 14%, and profit after tax was ₹11.8 crore. Castings gave ₹186 crore of that and power sales ₹2.7 crore. The company shows FY26 revenue on four bases across its slides, its results, CARE and screener; this run uses the audited annual report figure of ₹188 crore. From FY27 the company nets a ₹2.7 crore wind power credit against power cost, which lowers reported revenue and lifts the margin percent by a small amount. The promoter family holds 52% and has pledged none of it.

The claimed change is from an auto parts caster to a qualified aerospace and defence supplier. The filed record shows a start, not the step. Aerospace and defence sales doubled to ₹10.9 crore in FY26 from ₹5.4 crore, still 5.9% of revenue. The company's H1 FY27 slide puts the share at 11%, but the results report one segment and no rupee split. Five defence orders reached the exchange between February and October 2026, from GTRE, MKU, HAL, PLR Systems and a carbine programme, and every filing withholds the order value. The HAL order is dated 5 September 2026, 25 days before the half closed, so it explains little of the H1 share. The FY26 operating margin rose from 13.6% to 16.4% on lower power, fuel and material cost, not on a richer mix. On 14 November 2026 the board approved up to ₹100 crore of capex for aerospace, defence and energy, with no project list, timeline or debt split. That sum is 89% of September 2026 equity, and in July 2026 CARE had based its upgrade on the absence of planned major debt funded capex.

The backward score is average. Gate 0 gives 49 of 100 on the core and 52 of 160 overall, with no confirmed moat. Return on capital ran 4 to 5% in FY20 to FY22 and 10 to 13% since; FY26 at 13.3% is the best year since FY18. Cash conversion has a long good record: operating cash flow was 2.6 times profit over ten years and 2.2 times in FY26. Free cash after capex was only ₹1.7 crore in FY26, because ₹24 crore of capex went mostly into the solar plant. Net debt was ₹68 crore at March 2026, 2.2 times EBITDA, and 76% of debt is short term and floating. Two Gate 0 inputs used substitute formulas for ROCE and free cash flow. On the strict written formulas the core score falls to 38, an AVOID class, and that ruling sits with the operator.

The cash flow broke from that record in H1 FY27. Operating cash was ₹3.5 crore against profit of ₹10.8 crore, a ratio of 0.32. Inventory rose ₹11.5 crore in six months, receivables rose ₹7.0 crore, and borrowings rose ₹10.1 crore to ₹80 crore. If ₹3.1 crore in the other assets line is capital advances, as the company said of the same line a year earlier, the ratio is up to about 0.61. The H1 EBITDA margin of 21.6% includes an ₹8.6 crore credit from the change in inventories. Removing the whole credit gives 13.9%, which is a floor: only the fixed overhead parked in stock is a quality risk, and the filings do not split it out. Against this, CARE recorded the operating cycle falling from 150 days to 133 days in FY26, receivables over six months old are 0.15% of the book, and peer Azad describes the same work in progress effect in its own margin. The run classes the cash read as indeterminate until the H2 FY27 cash flow and the Q3 FY27 inventory line arrive.

Management holds no earnings calls, so the credibility grade of C rests on written statements. On soft promises the record is four delivered, two partial and none missed, and no document carries a revenue, margin or order book target that could be missed. The 4 MW solar plant was promised for the second half of FY25 in one annual report and for the second half of calendar 2025 in a May 2025 deck; it was commissioned on 30 March 2026. Five written statements are contradicted by the company's own filings. The annual report speaks of broad based growth across all five segments, yet non auto industrial sales fell about 13% to ₹37.6 crore and auto gave about 91% of the growth. The CFO credits a lower debt to equity ratio to healthy cash generation while debt rose ₹5.6 crore. The family took ₹5.1 crore in pay, commission, guarantee fees and rent, 29% of profit before tax and exceptional items. CMD pay rose 51% on the related party note basis, or 41% on the Board report basis, against 7% for the median employee. The AGM was asked to allow up to ₹18 crore a year of managerial pay in loss years. A ₹3.1 crore capital advance and a ₹1 crore deposit with promoter entities have not moved in a year. On the other side, the audit opinion is unmodified, no pledge exists, and four independent directors joined between 2023 and 2025. The promoter read is CAUTION on structure, not integrity, and its searches of legal databases could not run.

No investment casting peer with earnings calls is in the corpus, and PTC Industries is absent. Azad, Steelcast and Alicon map only in part, so they give base rates, not proof. All three pass metal cost to customers with a lag of one month to one quarter, which supports the company's own pass through claim. All three show new programme revenue arriving 6 to 18 months after the first stated date. Azad took 2.5 years from Rolls Royce engagement to a first qualification batch, Steelcast had no serial defence order a year after prototype approval, and Alicon's JLR programme slipped 18 months. Steelcast calls defence "one of the most riskiest" businesses, with worse pricing than industrial work, against this company's "strategic pillar". The weight is moderate, because Steelcast casts combat vehicle parts in sand, not aerospace parts. All three peers are adding capacity. Steelcast funds its ₹120 crore plant from ₹114 crore of cash; this company pairs a ₹100 crore plan with 0.71 gearing and 60% utilisation.

The India investment casting market is about ₹5,100 crore and grows about 2.8% a year on the IMARC basis. The company holds about 3.6% of it. The run could not filter that market by material or application, so the served market equals the whole and the 27.6 times headroom overstates the room. The runway still grades GOOD. A two point share gain over three years gives sales of ₹313 crore, 19% a year. The five year case of ₹408 crore needs 112% of today's nameplate tonnage, and only 1.9% of growth sits in committed capital work. H1 FY27 sales run 26.5% above the prior year, ahead of that path. Management cites a global market of USD 17 to 20 billion, 30 times the India market, and neither figure is an India claim. The government target of ₹3 lakh crore of defence production by 2029, from ₹1.51 lakh crore in FY25, needs about 19% a year.
