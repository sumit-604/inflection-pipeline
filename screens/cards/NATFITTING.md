## Business Understanding Narrative

**What does it do, and how does it make money?** National Fittings makes ductile iron grooved couplings and fittings, threaded fittings, stainless steel fittings, ball valves and pumps (filings/2025-09-01_letter-of-offer.pdf, p.24). It finishes castings at Kaniyur, Coimbatore, and casts at a foundry in Vedasandur, Dindigul (filings/2026-08-18_annual-report-FY26.pdf, p.46). It sells mostly abroad. FY26 exports were Rs 63.89 crore FOB against revenue of Rs 91.40 crore, so about 70% (AR FY26, p.101; p.19). The export base is the GCC (AR FY26, p.15). Capacity is 600 MT a month (AR FY26, p.18).

**What is changing?** Control. On 22 May 2025 the old promoter, A V Palaniswamy, agreed to sell his 34.04% to Himgiri Castings Pvt Ltd and four Agarwal family members at Rs 130 a share, Rs 40.20 crore in all (filings/2025-05-22_open-offer-PA.pdf, p.3). The open offer at Rs 135 drew 31 shares (AR FY26, p.18). The Agarwals took control on 18 October 2025. Arpit Agarwal became Managing Director (filings/2025-10-19_change-in-management.pdf, p.1). The registered office moved from Tamil Nadu to Vadodara, Gujarat, with a new CIN from 30 April 2026 (AR FY26, p.18).

The new owners now plan three things. They will merge two companies they own outright into the listed company. Banil Casting Pvt Ltd is a Goa foundry that already supplies NFL. Avisa Pvt Ltd holds the Gujarat land (filings/2026-05-22_scheme-2.pdf, p.3). They will build a casting plus finishing plant at Mudhela, Vadodara, of 2,500 MT a month, four times today's capacity (AR FY26, p.18). They will sell more in India (AR FY26, p.15).

**Why now?** The base business is growing. FY26 revenue rose 17.0% to Rs 91.40 crore (AR FY26, p.19). Q1 FY27 revenue rose 31.8% to Rs 27.13 crore and profit after tax rose 50% to Rs 3.94 crore (filings/2026-08-05_results-Q1FY27.pdf, p.2). The share price went from Rs 134 to Rs 356 in a year (market-data-2026-10-18.md, screener.in service record, not a filed document).

**What must be true for the thesis to work?** Three things. The Gujarat plant must come up on time and fill. Captive castings must lift margin, not move profit to the promoter. And the scheme must issue shares at a fair value for Banil and Avisa.

**What breaks it?** The scheme terms. NFL will issue 71,17,184 new shares, 78% of today's 90,83,182, to the Agarwal family. Promoter holding goes from 34.04% to 63.02% (scheme-2, p.5). At Rs 356 those shares are worth about Rs 253 crore. The corpus holds no valuation report and no Banil profit figure. The GCC is the second risk. A Gulf conflict on 28 February 2026 "caused real disruption this year" (AR FY26, p.15).

## 2. Business model and archetype

One reported segment, pipe fittings (Q1 FY27 results, p.3). The engine is buying or casting iron, machining and finishing it into a spec'd fitting, and exporting it through distributors. Raw material was Rs 48.14 crore of Rs 91.40 crore revenue in FY26 (FY26 results, p.2).

The old promoter sold the Thekkalur unit for Rs 28 crore and the Dindigul operation for Rs 13.50 crore in FY25. NFL then bought components from outside (FY26 results, p.3). From 18 October 2025 to 31 March 2026 it bought Rs 10.65 crore of rough castings from Banil and Rs 0.58 crore from Himgiri (AR FY26, p.30). That is 23% of the year's material cost, in under six months.

**Archetype: build-to-spec component maker,** with a converter's cost line. Castings are the input. The scheme rationale calls NFL's move a "transition from a third-party exporter into a fully integrated manufacturer" (scheme-2, p.4).

## 3. Competitive advantages

Thin. Management claims NFL is "the only 100% Made-in-India OEM manufacturing grooved piping products of this kind" (AR FY26, p.15). It has supplied metro, tunnel, Airports Authority and Power Grid projects (AR FY26, p.16). It is now seeking EIL, MES, DRDO, CPWD and NBCC approvals (AR FY26, p.16). Those approvals, once won, are a real switching cost. None is won yet. In exports it competes on price with imports, which the board admits: India's market has "gone to imported products" (AR FY26, p.15). Moat: absent today, forming only if the approvals land.

## 4. The promoters

Himgiri Castings, Kolkata registered, Goa operated, makes and exports iron castings. FY25 revenue Rs 244.76 crore, profit after tax Rs 23.19 crore, net worth Rs 162.17 crore (letter of offer, p.19). Anil Kumar Agarwal (64) and son Arpit Agarwal (32) run it. Declared net worth: Anil Rs 169.65 crore, Babita Rs 75.10 crore, Arpit Rs 91.76 crore (letter of offer, pp.20-22). No wilful defaulter or SEBI bar is declared.

Holding at 31 March 2026 was 34.04% (Himgiri 6.13%, Anil 10.89%, Arpit 9.53%, Babita 6.13%, Arushi 1.36%) (filings/2025-10-24_acquirers-shares.pdf, p.1). The screener.in record shows 34.72% in June 2026 and 36.64% in September 2026 (service record). The filings that cover those buys are not held. No pledge is disclosed (letter of offer, p.17).

Related party dependence is high and rising. Banil supplies castings. Avisa leases NFL the Gujarat land at Rs 1 lakh a month for nine years, then Rs 3.75 lakh (filings/2026-01-07_new-facility-vadodara.pdf, p.2). The AR states that term as 25 years, rising to about Rs 2.5 lakh (AR FY26, p.29). Arpit draws no salary (AR FY26, p.37). The old promoter still draws Rs 1.50 lakh a month as a technical consultant (AR FY26, pp.37-38).

Himgiri itself, the larger casting business, is not in the scheme. Whether it sells fittings that compete with NFL is NOT FOUND.

## 6. The transition and the quality ladder

FROM R1, commodity price-taker: ROCE 9.66% in FY25 (AR FY26, p.108), margins of 10% to 13%, castings bought in. TO R2, cost-advantaged converter: captive castings at two integrated sites and four times the scale. Management's words point at R3: "only 100% Made-in-India OEM" plus PSU approvals.

Management's claim, quoted once: "bring casting supply inside the Company as a fully integrated second manufacturing location in Goa, build a new single-location, fully integrated facility in Gujarat" (AR FY26, p.15).

Two readings. One: a casting family integrates forward into a listed finisher and lifts its margin. Two: a listed shell with Rs 44 crore of cash absorbs the family's private assets for shares. The observation that separates them is the scheme valuation report, with Banil's profit and the implied value per share. Not R0: the listed business earns from operations and is growing. It is being enlarged and re-based, not replaced, for now.

## 8. Proof check

The base business moves the way the story says. Revenue rose in each FY26 quarter against FY25, and Q1 FY27 printed a 19.7% EBITDA margin. Two cautions bind. Q1 is the strong quarter: Q1 FY26 printed 17.4% against 13.2% for the full year. And employee cost jumped to Rs 2.82 crore from Rs 1.97 crore in Q4 (Q1 FY27 results, p.2), the first sign of the new structure's cost.

The transition proof is absent. The Gujarat plant has no commissioning filing. The scheme is not effective. No integrated quarter exists yet.

## 9. Flags from the documents

- **Related party scheme, 78% dilution.** 71,17,184 shares to the promoter family for Banil (FY26 total assets Rs 78.80 crore, turnover Rs 62.60 crore) and Avisa (assets Rs 4.56 crore, nil turnover) (scheme-2, p.3). Valuation report NOT HELD. Avisa's share of the new shares is NOT FOUND.
- **Capacity figures disagree.** January 2026: 10,000 MT a year added. FY26 AR: 30,000 MT a year. The lease term also differs: 9 years against 25 years.
- **Negative operating cash flow** in FY26, with short term debt up despite Rs 44 crore of cash. Rs 10.38 crore of that cash is a capital gains deposit tied to a new undertaking (FY26 results, p.3).
- **Promoter group disclosure.** AR FY25 lists Kumudha Palaniswamy, wife of the old promoter, in the promoter group at 7.52% (AR FY25, p.83). The open offer counted the promoter at 34.04% only. AR FY26 omits Arushi Agarwal from its promoter table (AR FY26, p.90).
- **Ratio note errors.** ROE of 100.26% uses share capital as the denominator (AR FY26, p.108).
- **BSE fine** for late voting results under Reg 44(3) (filings/2026-05-28_secretarial-audit.pdf, p.4).
- **CARO** references whistle blower complaints received in the year (AR FY26, p.63). Count and content NOT FOUND.
- Audit opinion unmodified, no key audit matters (AR FY26, p.56).

## 11. Posture: the Transition Decision Matrix

- **Proof gate: NOT FIRED.** Base growth is real. The integration and the 4x capacity are filings and intent.
- **Ugliness: ARTIFACT-OF-CLIMB on operations.** The inventory build and working capital debt fit a business stocking up for new capacity. The scheme terms are a governance question, held as a flag, not as operating ugliness.
- **Recognition gap: CLOSED.** Price basis: screener.in service record, 2026-10-18, market cap Rs 323 crore, trailing PE 31.0. An R1 to R2 climb sits in a ~15-17x neighbourhood. The price already sits above it.

**Posture: PRICED NARRATIVE (TRAP).** Clean growth optics, transition narrated but unproven, and the price already pays for it. Hard WATCH with no position until the proof gate fires and the price re-opens the gap.

## 12. Verdict card

**WATCH.**

The base business is real and growing. The acquirer is a real iron caster with Rs 162 crore of net worth. But the value case now rests on a related party scheme whose price the corpus cannot see, on a plant whose size the company states two ways, and on a share price that has nearly tripled. That is not a /step1 yet.

**Load-bearing facts to verify first**
1. The scheme valuation report: Banil and Avisa profit, net worth, and the value behind 71,17,184 new shares.
2. Gujarat plant status: first phase commissioned or not, real capacity (10,000 or 30,000 MT a year), and the funding of Rs 60 crore.
3. The April 2026 default disclosure. BSE lists it but serves an error page; get it from the company.

**What would change the view**
- A valuation report that prices Banil near its book value and shows Banil profitable moves this toward /step1.
- A Q2 FY27 EBITDA margin near 19% with the first Gujarat output.
- PASS if the default disclosure is a real default, or if Banil is loss making.

No Role 1 valuation was run and no target price is set. This verdict decides only whether the name earns a full /step1, and it is not a position.
