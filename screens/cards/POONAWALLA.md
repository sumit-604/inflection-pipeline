## Business Understanding Narrative

Poonawalla Fincorp lends and earns the spread between what it charges borrowers and
what it pays lenders, less credit losses and cost. AUM stood at Rs 67,054 crore in
Q1FY27, up 62.5% year on year and 11.1% quarter on quarter (Investor Presentation
Q1FY27 p.6, p.20). The book spans loan against property (28% of AUM), instant consumer
loans (19%), mid-market lending (15%), business loans (11%), and six newer retail
products launched about two years ago (IP p.13, p.14; Concall p.6).

What is changing is mix and margin. Management calls it "6 growth engines mounted on a
single chassis": Prime Personal Loan, Gold Loan, Consumer Durable, Commercial Vehicle,
Education and Shopkeeper Loan (Concall p.6). These contributed 26% of Q1FY27
disbursement, up from 20% two quarters earlier (Concall p.4). ROA moved from 0.68% a
year ago to 1.98% this quarter; PAT grew 391.5% to Rs 308 crore (IP p.6, p.20).

Why now: management dates the plan to "two years ago" (Concall p.6); a Rs 2,500 crore
QIP funded the growth in April 2026 (IP p.7; Concall p.12).

What must be true: the newer products must hold credit quality past two years old, and
NIM/opex gains must be structural for ROA to reach the 3-3.5% guided by a June 2028
exit (Concall p.13, p.17).

What breaks it: the book is young. Six growth engines are inside their first two years,
before most retail books show a true loss curve. Stage 1 and Stage 2 provision coverage
fell every quarter for a year, 1.35% to 0.75% (IP p.26), even as headline GNPA
improved. A book that looks best just before it seasons is the central risk here.

## 2. Business model and archetype
Lender archetype. Profit is interest and fee income less funding cost, opex and
provisions. NII (incl. fees) was Rs 1,415 crore in Q1FY27, up 84.3% YoY (IP p.6, p.20).
Legacy book: LAP (28% of AUM, 9.75-12% ROI), instant consumer loans (19%, 22-29%),
mid-market (15%, 10-12%), business loans (11%, 17-22%), pre-owned car (8%, 14-16%),
machinery/medical and professional loans (1% each) (IP p.14). New layer: Prime PL (9%,
13-16% ROI, salaried top-corporate), gold (3%, 11-28%), education (2%, 10.5-13%), CD
(1%, up to 35%), CV (2%, 10-20%), shopkeeper (near 0%, 18-22%) (IP p.13): smaller,
higher-yielding, the stated growth engine.
NIM held at 9.10%, up from 9.05% (Concall p.4). Cost of borrowing rose slightly, 7.63%
to 7.72% (Concall p.12; IP p.22): the margin gain came from yield, not cheaper funding.

## 3. Competitive advantages
The claimed edge is underwriting talent, not price or brand. Every new business was
staffed "two levels deep" with leaders who "have built these exact loan books"
elsewhere (Concall p.6); the CXO layer is almost entirely ex-HDFC Bank, including MD
Arvind Kapil (IP p.37). Second claimed edge: six products on distinct macro drivers so
"they don't rise and fall together" (Concall p.6-7). AI investment is extensive, 101
projects with 50 delivered (Concall p.9; IP p.32-33), with quantified collection-cost
savings of 26-27% (Concall p.9).
None of this is a moat in the classic sense: pricing power, switching cost, scarcity.
It is operating execution, replicable, concentrated in one talent pool. Moat: forming
at best.

## 5. Financial trajectory
Q1FY27: AUM Rs 67,054 cr (+62.5% YoY, +11.1% QoQ), NII Rs 1,415 cr (+84.3% YoY), PPoP
Rs 785 cr (+141.8% YoY), PAT Rs 308 cr (+391.5% YoY) (IP p.6). Five-quarter trend
(Q1FY26 to Q1FY27): PAT Rs 63/74/150/255/308 cr; RoA 0.68/0.69/1.20/1.81/1.98%; GNPA
1.84/1.59/1.51/1.44/1.37% (IP p.20). Every quarter improved on the prior one.
Debt-to-equity: 3.72/3.64/4.25/4.67/3.82x, last point post the April QIP (IP p.7).
Borrowings Rs 49,866 cr, total assets Rs 64,673 cr, reserves Rs 12,885 cr (IP p.21,
p.30). CAR 19.46%, Tier 1 18.37%, LCR 199.62% (Concall p.12). Write-offs run ~Rs
275-280 cr/quarter, the "new stable level" (Concall p.18), ~1.6-1.7% of AUM annualised.
Inflection: earnings-led and margin-led together. AUM grew 62.5%; PAT grew 391.5%, a
multiple of volume, from yield expansion, credit-cost decline and opex leverage
together.

## 6. The transition and the quality ladder
Management's claim, quoted once: "We did not just launch 6 businesses. We built 6
growth engines mounted on a single chassis" (Concall p.6).
FROM a lender low rung: undifferentiated, ROA near/below 1% a year ago, GNPA above 1.8%
(IP p.20), close to a commodity price-taker where returns sit near cost of capital.
TO a claimed franchise rung: ROA 3-3.5% by June 2028 (Concall p.13, p.17), "5-6x AUM
over next 5 years" (guidance, FY26): durable high ROA, repeat economics, strong cash.
Rung-jump base rate (one rung per 2-3 years) is the live test. The transformation is
about two years old and ROA already moved 0.68% to 1.98% in four quarters: fast,
plausible from a low base, but a further climb to 3%+ ROA on unseasoned products is the
extraordinary claim the framework flags.

## 8. Proof check
What fired: five straight quarters of improving PAT, RoA and GNPA (step 5). NIM
expanded on yield, not cheaper funding. Credit cost fell a fourth straight quarter to
2.4% (Concall p.8). Opex-to-AUM improved despite continued investment. The guided Rs
2,500 crore raise landed on schedule (Concall p.12). A multi-quarter run, not one good
quarter.
What has not fired: the destination claim. ROA of 1.98% is well short of the 3-3.5%
guided exit, and the products carrying that gap are one to two years old, before a full
credit cycle has tested them. Stage 1/2 provision coverage thinned every quarter for a
year (IP p.26), the opposite of confirming the improving-GNPA story is fully reserved.
Named proof point: whether GNPA and early-vintage delinquency (6-MoB 30+, 0.64%,
Concall p.8) keep improving as FY24-25 cohorts cross 24-36 months on book.

## 9. Flags from the documents
- Provision coverage thinning while headline asset quality improves: Stage 1/2 PCR fell
  1.35% to 0.75% over four quarters (IP p.26); Stage 2 PCR alone 23.7% to 17.9%.
- Unseasoned growth engine: six new products are about two years old (Concall p.6); the
  fastest-growing lines (CD up to 35% ROI, shopkeeper 18-22%) are smallest, least
  tested.
- Single-institution pedigree: nearly the whole CXO layer is ex-HDFC Bank (IP p.37),
  undisclosed as a risk in the deck.
- Promoter holding fell ~340 bps QoQ with no stated reason (IP p.43); likely QIP
  dilution, unconfirmed.
- Cost of borrowing rose (7.63% to 7.72%, Concall p.12) despite improving credit,
  attributed only to a general "interest rate environment" (IP p.22).
- Write-offs at a "new normal" Rs 275-280 cr/quarter (Concall p.18) on a book that grew
  62.5% in a year, untested through the newer products' loss curve.
Not seen: promoter pledge, related-party transactions, auditor opinion, litigation,
board committee composition, contingent liabilities.

## 10. Independent check: the credit rating
PARTIAL. CRISIL and CARE both rate the company AAA/Stable long-term and A1+
short-term as of Q1FY27, covering bank facilities, NCDs, sub-debt and commercial paper
(IP p.24), the top rating band. The rationale (leverage tolerance, concentration
limits, an outside read on the newer products' seasoning risk) is NOT FOUND, looked for
only in the held documents this session. A deep run should pull the full rationale.

## 11. Posture: Transition Decision Matrix
- Proof gate: FIRED, on direction. Five consecutive quarters of improving PAT, RoA, NIM
  and GNPA is a real multi-quarter trend.
- Ugliness: none glaring, itself notable for AUM growing 62.5% a year. Closest
  candidate is ARTIFACT-OF-CLIMB: an unseasoned book from rapid growth looks best
  before it ages into a loss curve; thinning Stage 1/2 coverage fits that pattern.
- Recognition gap: INDETERMINATE, no price in this corpus. A large, well-covered NBFC
  is more likely than a small-cap to already carry the story in its price, pointing
  toward EARNINGS-ONLY over RE-RATING LIVE. Stage 11 of a deep run must resolve this.
Proof fired plus artifact ugliness maps to RE-RATING LIVE (gap open) or EARNINGS-ONLY
(gap closed). Both cells are constructive; neither is a value trap. Which applies
changes whether returns need a re-rating or can ride EPS growth alone.

## 12. Verdict card
**PROCEED to `/step1`, with the unseasoned-book risk ranked as the load-bearing
question.** Poonawalla shows a real, multi-quarter earnings and asset-quality
inflection: AUM up 62.5%, PAT up 391.5%, RoA nearly tripled in a year, GNPA improving
every quarter, confirmed independently by AAA/Stable ratings from both agencies.
Not a clean PROCEED, because most of the improvement rides on products one to two years
old, before retail losses typically show up, while provision coverage on the newest
tiers has thinned for a year. Size (Rs 38,155 cr, well above mandate) means price must
be resolved early; this is not an undiscovered name.
Load-bearing facts a deep run must verify first:
1. Early-vintage delinquency and GNPA for the six new products specifically, past 24
   and 36 months on book, not the blended number.
2. Why Stage 1/2 provision coverage fell every quarter for a year while GNPA improved.
3. The full CRISIL/CARE rating rationale, especially how it weighs the unseasoned book.
4. Promoter pledge, and the cause of the ~340 bps quarterly holding drop.
5. Whether price already carries the 3-3.5% ROA target, given size and coverage.
What would change the view: a second year of improving delinquency in the newer
products supports RE-RATING LIVE if the gap is open. A reversal in Stage 3 or 6-MoB 30+
delinquency as cohorts season, or a pledge disclosure showing promoter stress, moves
this to WATCH regardless of the current quarter. No price here.
