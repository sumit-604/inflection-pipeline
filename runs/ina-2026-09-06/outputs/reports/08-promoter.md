## SECTION 3: BUSINESS CONDUCT & ETHICAL TRACK RECORD

### 3A. Related-party transaction history

The single largest and most consequential RPT finding, VERIFIED from AR Note 8
(Investments, p.121) and Note 41 (RPT disclosure, p.135):

**The two LLPs.** The company has invested a fixed capital of Rs 4,00,00,000
plus current capital of Rs 12,25,26,547 in EACH of two LLPs — Happy Buildmart
LLP and Harmony Buildstate LLP — for a combined Rs 32,50,53,094 (~Rs 32.5cr),
unchanged between FY25 and FY26 (AR p.121). The LLP partner tables (AR p.121)
show:
- Happy Buildmart LLP: Insolation Green Energy Pvt Ltd (the company's own wholly
  owned subsidiary) 10%, Vikas Jain 85%, Ekta Jain 5%.
- Harmony Buildstate LLP: Insolation Green Energy Pvt Ltd 10%, Manish Gupta 85%,
  Payal Gupta 5%.
Each LLP is therefore 90% owned by one promoter-director couple, with the
listed group's own subsidiary holding the other 10% — meaning listed-company
money co-funds a vehicle in which the promoter family holds 9x the equity stake
the company itself holds. No stated business purpose, no arm's-length pricing
statement, and no disclosed return on this Rs 32.5cr for either LLP was found
anywhere in the AR. Both LLPs are dated to 26-Feb-2025 (AR p.121), i.e. formed
in the run-up to the Main Board migration. This is the single highest-priority
governance finding in this report.

**Rent, consultancy and staff-training payments to promoter-family individuals.**
FY26 rent expense paid to Manish Gupta and Vikas Jain personally: Rs 184.91 lakh
+ Rs 173.55 lakh = Rs 347.10 lakh combined (~Rs 3.47cr) (AR p.136), consistent
with rent for the personally-mortgaged factory premises (see 3A land-title
finding below). Consultancy fees to Mahendra Kumar Jain (Vikas Jain's father):
Rs 2.87 lakh (AR p.136). Staff Training Expense to Navya Gupta (a relative of
KMP, likely Manish Gupta's family): Rs 62.25 lakh / Rs 61.75 lakh (AR p.136) —
a training-expense line to a named relative is unusual in size and category and
was not further explained in the AR. Electricity charges to Vikas Jain: Rs 8.72
lakh (AR p.136). None of these carry an arm's-length justification statement.

**Land title, mortgage and the AOC-2 contradiction.** Three AR passages
conflict on the ownership of the Jaipur factory land (Khasra No. 766/2, Village
Bagwara):
1. Note 4 (PPE) states: "Title Deeds of all Immovable properties are held in the
   name of Company" (AR p.120/125).
2. The CARO annexure to the Auditor's Report, clause (i)(c), states the same:
   title deeds of all immovable properties are held in the company's name (AR
   p.~124).
3. Note 18A (borrowings security schedule) repeatedly describes the SAME land
   parcel, and a second industrial plot (Village Jatawali, Chomu), as mortgaged
   to lenders (SBI, HDFC) "in the name of Sh. Manish Gupta and Sh. Vikas Jain,
   Director[s] of the Company" (AR p.126-127), alongside standing personal
   guarantees from both promoters on nearly every secured facility, including
   an External Commercial Borrowing from a Netherlands lender (Energy Access
   Relief Fund B.V.) secured purely on the promoters' personal guarantees.
This is a genuine, unresolved three-way conflict on core-asset title: either
the security documentation is imprecisely worded (naming the directors as
mortgagors of company-owned land in their guarantor capacity — a common but
sloppy drafting practice) or the land is not, in fact, on the company's own
title as Note 4/CARO assert. This corpus cannot resolve which; it is a named
finding for live verification (title search), not a conclusion.

**AOC-2 vs. financial-statement RPT notes.** Form AOC-2 (Annexure III, AR p.64)
states, for BOTH arm's-length and non-arm's-length RPT categories: "Not
applicable." This sits directly beside the financial-statement RPT note (Note
41), which discloses real, named, quantified transactions covering sales,
purchases, interest income, rent, consultancy, staff training, remuneration,
director sitting fees, loans given/repaid, investments, and personal
guarantees. AOC-2 exists precisely to summarise what Note 41 already shows; here
it discloses nothing while the financial statements disclose a full page of
RPTs. This is a disclosure-quality failure, not necessarily fraud — the RPTs
ARE in the audited financials — but it defeats the purpose of AOC-2 as a
standalone plain-English RPT summary for shareholders who do not read the notes.

**Fluidcon Engineers as a continuing operating counterparty** (see 1B) —
confirmed live purchase/sale/capital-goods-advance relationship with a
promoter-founded trading firm, still active, not wound down post-IPO.

**Guarantee structure.** Total corporate/counter guarantees outstanding at
FY26 close: Rs 1,65,401.00 lakh (Rs 1,654.01cr) (AR Note 42, p.137), up from
Rs 9,968.00 lakh (Rs 99.68cr) in FY25 — a ~16.6x increase, confirming Stage 3's
figure. Within this, one leg runs in reverse: "Corporate Guarantee given by
Insolation Green Energy Private Limited [the subsidiary] in favour of AU Bank
Limited for Credit Facility of Rs. 24.96 Crores availed by Insolation Energy
Limited [the parent]" (AR p.137) — the subsidiary guaranteeing the parent's own
bank facility, an inversion of normal parent-guarantees-subsidiary structure
that raises questions about which entity is actually bearing credit risk for
which, and about the subsidiary's own board's independent assessment of that
guarantee (Insolation Green Energy is a wholly-owned subsidiary, so the
"independent" assessment is nominal in any case).
