## RECONCILING PASS 1 AND PASS 2

Two places where Pass 2 corrected or refined Pass 1, carried into the
consolidated list below on Pass 2's terms:

1. **Note 41 Level 3 figure**: Pass 1 attributed the unitemised Rs 1,040.93
   lakh Level 3 line to the Mercedes Benz Financial Services preference
   shares. Pass 2 proved this wrong: the consolidated Note 41 shows Level 3
   = nil in both years (the subsidiary investment is eliminated on
   consolidation; the Mercedes Benz stake, held at the parent, would still
   show a Level 3 balance if it were the Level 3 item, and it does not).
   The Rs 1,040.93 lakh Level 3 line is the wholly-owned subsidiary
   investment (cost-carried, not fair-valued), and the Mercedes Benz
   preference shares are instead bundled, unitemised, inside the Level 1
   "Shares" total. This consolidated report uses Pass 2's corrected
   attribution.
2. **RPT asset-sale gain, not cheap sale**: Pass 1 read the Rs 3,397.39
   lakh FY26 related-party disposals as a uniform red flag with "no
   independent valuation." Pass 2's WDV reconciliation (Note 1 PP&E
   rollforward against Note 34 and Note 40) shows the Office Premises sold
   to Mrs. Pranika Jain and Mrs. Sreelekha Jain at Rs 1,400 lakh against a
   net book value of only Rs 465.28 lakh, i.e. a 3.01x-book, Rs 934.72 lakh
   PROFIT, which supplies 92% of the entire exceptional item. Vehicles sold
   near book value (93.2%, a Rs 40.02 lakh loss, routine). This
   consolidated report carries Pass 2's refined framing: the concern is
   not that the company sold cheap to insiders, it is that a large
   related-party gain is being booked with no independent valuation of the
   specific asset to confirm the price reflects true market value, not
   just a large multiple of a decades-old depreciated book value. Layered
   on top, Pass 2 also found that ~66% of the FY26 investment-property
   monetisation (Rs 2,805 lakh of Rs 4,250 lakh) went to a party NOT named
   as related in Note 40 at all, and that the FY25 comparative Rs 2,805
   lakh RPT flat sale has no matching disposal entry in either FY25 asset
   rollforward in this document. Both stand as open items.

## D. FIVE QUESTIONS FOR MANAGEMENT

1. What independent valuation, if any, supports the Rs 1,400 lakh Office Premises sale to Mrs. Pranika Jain and Mrs. Sreelekha Jain, priced at 3.01x net book value (Note 1/34/40)?
2. Who is the counterparty for the approximately Rs 2,805 lakh (66%) of FY26 investment property disposal that is not identified as a related party in Note 40 (Note 4)?
3. What account records the FY25 Rs 2,805 lakh related-party flat sale to Mrs. Sreelekha Jain and Mr. Saumya Jain, given it has no matching entry in the FY25 columns of Note 1 (PPE) or Note 4 (Investment Property) in this Annual Report?
4. Why does Note 43(A) Credit Risk state no significant single-counterparty exposure exists, when the company carries a Rs 509.04 lakh ICD to Radius Estates Projects Pvt Ltd, a counterparty under active IBC insolvency, with no principal provision?
5. What is the correct standalone and consolidated Return on Equity percentage for FY26 and FY25, given Note 45(d) and Note 46(d) currently report figures identical to Basic EPS rather than a computed ROE ratio?

## E. NOTES-BASED RED FLAGS

- Rs 509.04 lakh ICD to insolvent Radius Estates Projects Pvt Ltd carried with no principal ECL provision, and omitted entirely from Note 43(A)'s credit-concentration narrative, despite an auditor Emphasis of Matter (undisclosed risk indicator).
- Rs 3,397.39 lakh FY26 + Rs 2,805 lakh FY25 asset sales to the exiting promoter family, with the FY26 Office Premises leg sold at 3.01x book value and no independent valuation disclosed for the specific asset (RPT fairness / aggressive accounting question, not earnings management per se, since the gain is isolated on the P&L face as an exceptional item).
- FY25 Rs 2,805 lakh related-party flat sale has no matching disposal entry in either FY25 asset-movement note in this document (undisclosed risk indicator / reconciliation gap).
- ~66% of FY26 investment property monetisation (Rs 2,805 lakh) went to a counterparty not named as related in Note 40 (disclosure completeness gap).
- Note 41 fair value hierarchy misclassifies the unquoted Mercedes Benz preference shares as Level 1 and mislabels the cost-carried subsidiary investment as the Level 3 line (aggressive/careless fair-value disclosure).
- Note 45(d)/46(d) Return on Equity Ratio equals Basic EPS, not a computed ROE%, in both standalone and consolidated statements and both years (disclosure-integrity defect).

## F. ONE-LINE NOTES VERDICT

The notes reveal moderate to concerning accounting practices. Key concern:
unresolved related-party asset transfers to the exiting promoter family
sit alongside an unprovided insolvent-counterparty exposure and real
disclosure defects (Note 41 fair value hierarchy, the ROE ratio equalling
EPS, and a credit-risk note silent on the one true concentration). Key
strength: a debt-free balance sheet, clean isolation of exceptional items
on the face of the P&L, and materially improving receivables collection.
Overall accounting quality: 5/10.
