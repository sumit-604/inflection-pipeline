## Business Understanding Narrative

Rappid makes valves, and it has spent three years moving from the easy end of that business to the
hard end. A valve controls the flow of liquid or gas through a pipe. The easy end is a butterfly valve
for a brewery. The hard end is a 550 millimetre nickel aluminium bronze globe valve for a Fleet
Support Ship of the Indian Navy, where "the valve might be the only thing preventing the engine room
from flooding" (IP p.15).

The company designs, machines, casts, assembles, tests and certifies valves from 15mm to 600mm under
the brand "Rapid", in carbon steel, stainless, duplex, aluminium, cast iron, bronze and exotic alloys
(IP p.8, p.13). One plant, 45,000 square feet at Palghar, running at 85% utilisation, 100-plus
employees, 90-plus clients, 60% of them repeat (IP p.7, p.18).

The money comes from certification, not from metal. The promoter states the mechanism plainly: "there
is a body called ISCS [IACS] member. So, there are 7 to 8 surveyors... where you need to take the type
approvals from these surveyors. Once you have these type approvals on board, you are qualified for the
pre-qualification criteria in all the tenders to participate and to win the tender" (Concall p.6). The
approvals are in hand and they took eighteen years to collect: DNV in 2008, the American Petroleum
Institute monogram in 2010, API 607 and IBR in 2012, ISO 9001 in 2021, ISO 14001 and 45001 plus the
Marine Systems Research Institute in 2023, and in 2025 both ClassNK of Japan and UL of the United
States (IP p.23).

What changed is the customer. In FY23 the company sold Rs 13.43 crore of valves into ethanol,
breweries and wastewater, which was 82% of a Rs 16.4 crore business, and Rs 8 lakh into marine
(IP p.29). By FY26 marine and shipbuilding together were Rs 17.49 crore of a Rs 53.2 crore business.
Margins followed: EBITDA went from 13.0% to 19.4% and profit after tax from 3.5% to 12.2% (IP p.31).
Revenue compounded 48% a year across the three years.

Why now: India is building ships. Sixty naval vessels are under construction with seventy to eighty
more planned, a pipeline above Rs 2.3 trillion, with 75% of defence procurement reserved for domestic
suppliers (IP p.27). Rappid supplies the Fleet Support Ship programme through both yards building it:
"the purchase order has been awarded to Hindustan Shipyard, but Hindustan Shipyard has offloaded 2
ships to L&T Shipbuilding... we are supplying to HSL, and we are supplying to L&T Ship Building also"
(Concall p.19).

What must be true: the orders must convert to cash, not just to revenue.

What breaks it: they have not been converting to cash. In FY26 inventories rose Rs 10.51 crore and
receivables Rs 5.60 crore while payables fell Rs 1.35 crore, so working capital absorbed about Rs 17.5
crore against profit after tax of Rs 6.48 crore (IP p.32, p.33). Cash fell from Rs 9.49 crore to
Rs 2.51 crore and short-term borrowings rose from Rs 8.41 crore to Rs 17.84 crore. The company has
never published a cash flow statement in the held corpus, and the FY26 annual report does not yet
appear in Bull AI's inventory.

## 2. Business model and archetype
Buy castings and bar, machine them on CNC and vertical machining centres, assemble, test on a bench
with PMI and X-ray, certify with traceability numbers and third-party inspection reports, and ship
(IP p.17). Revenue is orders times a price set by how few suppliers hold the right approval for that
valve on that vessel. Actuators are bought in from original equipment makers and integrated rather
than manufactured (IP p.12).

Archetype: **Build-to-spec component maker** for the industrial two-thirds, where customer capex
cycle, design-win pipeline and input-cost pass-through are the right dials. The marine third behaves
more like a **Licence/scarcity business**: the type approval is the licence, it expires and must be
maintained, and without it you cannot bid at all. Declare both. The margin bridge runs on which of the
two is growing.

Note the working capital consequence of the archetype, because it decides the card. Made-to-order
certified valves for a shipyard are built against a drawing, inspected, cleared and only then paid
for. The August order sets it out: "All valves shall be delivered within 10 Weeks (F.O.R L&T
Kattupalli) from the date of drawing & QAP approval. Drawing and QAP to be submitted within 2 weeks
from the date receipt of PO" (Orders, 13 Aug 2026). Cash goes out at casting procurement and comes
back after clearance.

## 3. Competitive advantages
Real, specific, and better evidenced than the size of the company would suggest.
- **Type approvals that take years and cannot be shortcut.** ClassNK of Japan is the headline, and the
  deck's claim is checkable: it "Places Rappid among select group of Indian manufacturers with ClassNK
  certification, essential for Hull Valves used in global shipbuilding" (IP p.20). The promoter
  explains why it converts to revenue rather than prestige (Concall p.6).
- **Empanelment with every Indian naval yard that matters**: Cochin Shipyard, Mazagon Dock, GRSE,
  L&T Shipbuilding, plus Hindustan Shipyard through the FSS programme (IP p.9; Concall p.19).
- **A developed product nobody else had.** The 450mm and 550mm nickel aluminium bronze globe valves
  for the Fleet Support Ship required "advanced design simulation, precision machining, and
  specialized foundry coordination" (IP p.21), and type approval arrived in 2026 (IP p.6).
- **Supplying both yards on the same programme** (Concall p.19), which is a position a substitutable
  supplier does not get.
- **60% repeat customers** (IP p.7).
- **Metallurgy as a barrier.** "Fundamentally Different Material Science and Metallurgical Knowledge;
  Limited certified suppliers; Deep customer relationships" (IP p.27).

Against: two-thirds of revenue is still industrial valves sold into ethanol, breweries, wastewater and
chemicals, where the company has no evident advantage; the plant is single-site at 85% utilisation;
and the promoter concedes that in fixed-price environments the company has honoured commitments "even
at a loss" (IP p.15), which is admirable and is also the opposite of pricing power.
**Verdict: a genuine moat on the marine third, none on the industrial two-thirds.**

Building, under the Rule G test, is well evidenced. Dalal started the company in 2002, began

**Three years of climb, then a year of stall.** Revenue tripled from FY23 to FY25 and then grew 2.13%
in FY26. Profit after tax went eleven times from FY23 to FY26, but only 7.3% in the last year. The
margin held, which matters: EBITDA 19.4% and profit after tax 12.2% in FY26 were both the best of the
four years.

**The stall is concentrated in the second half and it has a stated cause.** H2 FY26 revenue of
Rs 24.41 crore was 25% below H2 FY25's Rs 32.51 crore. The company explains it contemporaneously:
"sharp and unpredictable increases in copper and non-ferrous metal prices created significant
uncertainty around future input costs... aggressive bidding at fixed or near-fixed realizations could
have exposed the Company to substantial margin erosion... geopolitical tensions arising from the
Iran-US conflict created disruption in the LPG supply chain. Industrial foundries and casting
manufacturers faced shortages of LPG supply... Accordingly, RVIL consciously adopted a prudent and
selective bidding approach during H2 FY26" (IP p.28). That is a choice, disclosed, with a mechanism.
It is the most candid page in any deck read in this run.

**The returns have collapsed and only half of it is the listing.** Return on equity went 53%, 21%,
13%; return on capital employed 29%, 17%, 14%. The FY24 to FY25 fall is the SME listing putting equity
in. The FY25 to FY26 fall is not: share capital was flat at Rs 519 lakh across both years (IP p.33).
That second fall is flat profit on a larger equity base plus a much larger asset base.

**The working capital is the finding.** Computed from the balance sheet: receivable days went from 135
to 170, inventory days from 113 to 183, payable days from 29 to 19. The cash conversion cycle went
from roughly 219 days to roughly 334 days in one year. Working capital absorbed about Rs 17.5 crore
against profit after tax of Rs 6.48 crore. Note too that the profit and loss account carries a
"Change in inventory" credit of Rs 754 lakh for FY26 against Rs 217 lakh for FY25, up 248% (IP p.32),
and Rs 754 lakh is larger than the year's entire profit after tax.

**And a use of cash nobody explains.** Non-current investments rose from Rs 57 lakh to Rs 476 lakh

short-term borrowing. What was bought is not stated anywhere in the corpus.

**Cash conversion is INDETERMINATE**, and for this company that word is doing more work than on any

**Then the turn.** Q1 FY27 revenue was Rs 14.87 crore, up about 28% year on year, with the executable

## 6. The transition and the quality ladder
FROM **R2 COST-ADVANTAGED CONVERTER.** FY23 was a Rs 16 crore machine shop selling general-purpose
valves into breweries, ethanol and wastewater at a 13.0% EBITDA margin and a 3.5% net margin
(IP p.29, p.31). No pricing power, competing on cost and delivery.
TO **R3 VALUE-ADDED / SPEC'D SUPPLIER.** A certified naval and marine valve maker whose type approvals
are a pre-qualification barrier, selling into contracts the deck describes as "Multi-ship, multi-year,
high-value" (IP p.27).
Mechanism, stated and physical: hold the IACS type approvals, become pre-qualified, win the shipyard
tender. **Management's claim, quoted once:** "Marine valves require specialized metallurgy, stringent
certifications, and zero-failure execution standards, creating high barriers to entry and limiting
qualified suppliers" (IP p.5).
**Ladder ruling: one rung, and on the margin evidence it has largely been climbed already.** EBITDA
went 13.0% to 20.1% between FY23 and FY24 and has held near 19% for three years since. That step is
the rung. What remains unproven is not whether the company reached R3, but whether R3 economics
survive scale, given the working capital that comes with them.

## 8. Proof check
**The proof gate has FIRED on the transition, and the FY26 stall does not unfire it.**

The claimed climb was from general industrial valves to certified marine and naval valves. Test it:
the combined marine and shipbuilding share of revenue went from 0.49% in FY23 to 32.85% in FY26
(IP p.29); EBITDA margin stepped from 13.0% to 20.1% in FY24 and has held near 19% for three years
(IP p.31); eight certification regimes are in hand including ClassNK and UL (IP p.23); and the company
now supplies both yards building the Fleet Support Ship (Concall p.19). None of that is narrative.

**The FY26 pause has a disclosed, dated, external cause** and the company took the revenue hit
deliberately rather than bid badly (IP p.28). **And it is already reversing**: Q1 FY27 revenue up
about 28%, order book up about 60%, and Rs 32.69 crore of new naval orders booked between April and
August 2026 (Update Q1 FY27; Orders 13 Aug 2026).

**The proof point that has NOT fired is cash.** Every number above is an income statement or an order
number. Across FY26 the company earned Rs 6.48 crore of profit, consumed about Rs 17.5 crore in
working capital, spent Rs 4.19 crore on unexplained non-current investments, and funded the gap by
running cash down Rs 6.98 crore and short-term borrowing up Rs 9.43 crore. The cash conversion cycle
lengthened by about 115 days in one year.

**Two readings, and the observation that separates them.** Reading one: the working capital build is
inventory and receivables staged ahead of a Rs 51 crore order book, an artifact of a ramp that unwinds
as the Fleet Support Ship valves ship and clear. Reading two: 334 days is what this business model
costs, because certified made-to-order naval valves are paid for after inspection and clearance, and
the cycle will grow with revenue rather than shrink. **The observation that separates them is cash
flow from operations across FY24, FY25 and FY26, and it is not in the corpus.** If reading one is
right, operating cash flow is negative in FY26 and positive across the three years together. If
reading two is right, it is negative across all three, and every rupee of reported profit to date has
been funded by the lender.

## 11. Posture: the Transition Decision Matrix
- **Proof gate: FIRED.** Step 8. The rung climb is evidenced in margin, mix, certifications and named
  naval programmes, and the FY26 pause is disclosed, externally caused and already reversing.
- **Ugliness: CONTRADICTORY, and that is the honest reading.** The demand pause is plainly
  ARTIFACT-OF-CLIMB: a deliberate bidding halt during a copper spike and an LPG-driven casting
  shortage, explained at the time and reversed within two quarters. The working capital, at 334 days
  and lengthening, reads STRUCTURAL: it is what certified made-to-order naval supply costs, and it
  should grow with revenue rather than shrink.
- **Recognition gap: PARTIALLY OPEN.** About 24.7 times FY26 earnings and 3.1 times book, on a year
  that was the trough. On the Q1 FY27 run rate the multiple is lower. The market is not paying a
  defence-supplier premium, and it is not treating this as distressed either.

**Posture: CONTRADICTION.** The framework is explicit about this cell: "Proof FIRED + STRUCTURAL ->
CONTRADICTION. The gate says climbing, the ugliness says decaying; a flag to resolve, not a posture.
Re-examine the classification before any verdict."

That is exactly the situation. The proof gate says this company completed a rung climb into a
protected niche with a decade-long runway. The balance sheet says it consumed two and a half times its
annual profit in working capital to do it and borrowed the difference. Both readings are supported.
**One document resolves it, and that document is the cash flow statement.**

## 12. Verdict card

**WATCH.** A hard watch on a business that looks genuinely good, blocked on one missing document.

The business case is the second most convincing in this run after Kross, and on niche quality it may
be the best. Eighteen years of certification collected, culminating in ClassNK and UL in 2025 and a
naval type approval in 2026. Empanelment with every Indian yard that builds warships. Supply to both
yards on the Fleet Support Ship programme. A margin that tripled and then held for three years through
a commodity shock the company navigated by declining business rather than bidding badly. Rs 32.69
crore of naval orders won between April and August 2026 against Rs 53.23 crore of FY26 revenue. A
promoter who founded the company, still runs it, answers hard questions himself, and volunteers that
his order book only converts at 65% to 70%.

It does not get a PROCEED because **this company has never published a cash flow statement in anything
the corpus holds, and its balance sheet says that question is not academic.** In FY26 it earned Rs 6.48
crore and working capital took Rs 17.5 crore. Cash fell by three quarters and short-term debt doubled.
The cash cycle lengthened by 115 days. Rs 4.19 crore went into non-current investments nobody
explains. Under the framework, a company whose proof gate has fired while its ugliness reads structural
is a CONTRADICTION to resolve, not a posture to act on, and the resolution is a single statement.

Two further reasons to wait rather than run a `/step1` today. As an NSE Emerge half-yearly reporter,
there is no audited or reviewed number after 31 March 2026, and the Q1 FY27 figure is an unaudited
business update the company revised within a day. And at Rs 160 crore of market value this is a true
micro-cap on the SME platform, where traded liquidity should be checked before research time is spent.

**Load-bearing facts to verify first. The first one unblocks the card.**
1. **Cash flow from operations for FY24, FY25 and FY26.** The FY2026 annual report should carry all
   three. Nothing on this card matters more. If operating cash flow is negative across all three years,
   the verdict becomes PASS regardless of the order book.
2. What the Rs 4.19 crore of non-current investments is, and why it was bought in a year of fresh
   borrowing.
3. Inventory ageing and valuation policy. The FY26 inventory credit of Rs 754 lakh exceeds profit
   after tax of Rs 648 lakh.
4. Receivable ageing by customer, and specifically how long PSU shipyards take to clear and pay.
5. Whether "Mrs. Mansi Gaurav Dalal", listed as an independent director, is related to the promoter
   Gaurav Vijay Dalal, and whether the board composition satisfies Section 149(6).
6. Promoter holding and pledge. Both NOT FOUND.
7. The definitions of the deck's "Marine" and "Shipbuilding & Repair" revenue categories, and whether
   the FY26 rotation between them was a reclassification.
8. Customer concentration: the share of revenue from the top three customers and from the Fleet
   Support Ship programme specifically.
