## SECTION 2: TAM ESTIMATION, MULTIPLE METHODS

**FX note (load-bearing):** All USD-denominated figures below are converted at **₹83.68/USD**, the CY2024 average rate (X-Rates.com average, accessed 16-Jul-2026) — chosen because it is contemporaneous with the 2024 base-year data underlying every industry report cited. The spot rate at the run date (16-Jul-2026) was materially weaker, ~₹96.48/USD (X-Rates/Federal Reserve H.10, accessed 16-Jul-2026, rupee down ~12% over the trailing 12 months). Using spot instead of the contemporaneous rate would mechanically inflate every INR-converted TAM figure by ~15-16% with zero change in the underlying market — flagged and avoided.

### Method 2 — Bottom-up (addressable unit × penetration × revenue/unit)

**Current-activity approach (primary, fresher data):**
- Addressable unit: one IVF cycle
- Current annual cycle volume, India: **200,000–250,000/year**, rising toward ~400,000 by 2030 (BioSpectrum India, "Top trends driving India's IVF industry in 2026," accessed 16-Jul-2026 — fresh)
- Revenue per cycle: ₹1.5L–2.8L (private ₹2.3L avg / government ₹1.1L avg — 2025 patient cost-guide aggregation, accessed 16-Jul-2026)
- **Current market (bottom-up) = 200,000 × ₹1.5L to 250,000 × ₹2.8L = ₹3,000 Cr to ₹7,000 Cr** (central case: 225,000 × ₹2.15L ≈ ₹4,838 Cr)

**Full-penetration / theoretical-ceiling approach (secondary, explicitly caveated — STALE):**
- Total addressable unit: infertile couples in India, ~27.5–28 million (RHP SWOT box p.161, no primary citation given; independently corroborated at ~2.8 crore couples by a submission to the Parliamentary Standing Committee on Health & Family Welfare, **2017** — cited in PMC-indexed literature). **This 2017 vintage is >4 years old at the run date and is used for direction only, per the staleness rule, never as a headline number.**
- Current penetration: ~1% of infertile couples seek evaluation (~270,000/yr); ~65,000/yr actually opt for IVF (fertilitydost.com, source and date not independently identified — flagged low-confidence/possibly pre-2020 vintage, **not used as a headline figure**)
- At ~1.5 cycles/couple × ₹2L/cycle = ₹3L/couple: 65,000 × ₹3L = **₹1,950 Cr** (well below even the current-activity bottom-up range — consistent with this being the stalest, least reliable input)
- Full-penetration theoretical ceiling: 27.5mn couples × ₹3L/couple ≈ **₹8,25,000 Cr (~USD 99bn)** — flagged explicitly as a non-decision-useful theoretical extreme (assumes instantaneous 100% conversion of every infertile couple in the country with zero capacity constraint), shown only because the framework's TAM definition technically requires a full-capture figure.

**The bottom-up current-market range (₹3,000–7,000 Cr) sits materially below the top-down range (₹11,046–19,665 Cr) — a ~2.3–3.7x divergence.** This is flagged and explained rather than averaged away: India's ART sector has weak volume-reporting infrastructure (only a small fraction of an estimated ~40,000 ART clinics/banks nationally were registered with the National ART & Surrogacy Registry as of the most recent unofficial estimate found — source and precise date not independently verified, flagged low-confidence) and independent cycle-count estimates themselves range from 65,000/yr to 250,000/yr to an implied "addressable demand 9-12x current market" — a >6x spread. Top-down "market size" figures likely also embed ancillary spend (diagnostics, hormonal medication, genetic testing, cryostorage subscriptions, premium/medical-tourism pricing) beyond the narrow per-cycle procedure fee used in the bottom-up calc. Given this, **the top-down range is used for the headline TAM, with the bottom-up range disclosed as a downside sensitivity, not silently discarded.**

## SECTION 3: SAM & SOM

### 3A. SAM — five filters applied to conservative TAM (₹14,560 Cr)

| Filter | % retained | Rationale (each a subtraction) |
|---|---|---|
| Product fit | 90% | Core scope (IVF/ICSI/IUI/fertility preservation/PGT/male infertility) matches TAM definition closely; minor discount for the hospital/high-risk-pregnancy edge case and pharmacy already excluded at the revenue-base level (see below) |
| Geography | 20% | Gaudium operates 7 hubs + spokes across ~30 locations in 6 states/UTs today (RHP p.163), expanding to 26 hubs by FY29 (B07 note: +271%) — still a fraction of the national footprint that Indira (160+ centres, 20+ states) or Nova (100+ centres, 70+ cities) already cover. Analyst judgment, flagged as such (not a sourced %) |
| Channel | 95% | Direct-clinic hub-and-spoke model is the industry-standard channel; minimal exclusion |
| Customer | 90% | Excludes the government/public-hospital price segment (~₹1.1L/cycle) Gaudium does not compete in; the $-value share of that segment is assumed small since organized private chains dominate reported market value even where they don't dominate cycle *count*. Analyst judgment, flagged |
| Capability | 90% | Specialized-clinician/embryologist talent pool and brand recognition currently concentrated in a handful of metros; modest further discount beyond geography to avoid double-counting. Analyst judgment, flagged |

Combined: 0.90 × 0.20 × 0.95 × 0.90 × 0.90 = **13.85%**

**SAM (today) = ₹14,560 Cr × 13.85% ≈ ₹2,017 Cr** — **13.9% of conservative TAM.**

**Current SAM-scope revenue base:** the pharmacy-trading line (31% of Gaudium's FY26 revenue) is excluded from the revenue used to compute share, since it sits outside the TAM/SAM product-scope definition in Section 1A. SAM-scope revenue = IVF (64%) + Hospital (4%) = 68% of ₹104.35 Cr FY26 revenue = **₹70.96 Cr**.

**current_sam_share_pct = ₹70.96 Cr ÷ ₹2,017 Cr ≈ 3.52%**

### 3B. SOM at 3 and 5 years

SAM is grown forward at the TAM growth rate (13.13% CAGR, Infomerics), holding the filter percentages constant (i.e., assuming Gaudium's relative reach doesn't structurally change — the explicit expansion lever is treated separately in 5D):

- SAM (yr3) = ₹2,017 Cr × (1.1313)³ = **₹2,920 Cr**
- SAM (yr5) = ₹2,017 Cr × (1.1313)⁵ = **₹3,738 Cr**

Share-gain trajectory: given (a) documented execution risk — overall staff attrition 51%/51%/63% (FY23-25) and SMP attrition rising from nil to 29% in H1FY26 (B04) — and (b) a genuinely aggressive, capex-committed hub build-out (7→26 hubs, +271% by FY29), a **blended normal-to-aggressive** trajectory is used rather than the full aggressive band:

- 3-year: current share (3.52%) + **2.0pp** = 5.52% → **SOM (3yr) = ₹2,920 Cr × 5.52% ≈ ₹161 Cr**
- 5-year: current share (3.52%) + **4.0pp** = 7.52% → **SOM (5yr) = ₹3,738 Cr × 7.52% ≈ ₹281 Cr**

**Implied revenue CAGR** (arithmetic, from FY26 SAM-scope revenue of ₹70.96 Cr):
- 3yr: (₹161/₹70.96)^(1/3) − 1 = **~31.4%**
- 5yr: (₹281/₹70.96)^(1/5) − 1 = **~31.7%**

**This is the formal handoff to stage 11.** Both figures sit meaningfully above the strategy's 25% CAGR target, implying the *market* is not the binding constraint on hitting that target — execution and capacity are (see 3C). Sensitivity: if TAM growth undershoots to the independent CMI rate (7.9% instead of 13.13%), SAM(yr5) falls to ~₹2,946 Cr and SOM(5yr) to ~₹221 Cr, still comfortably above FY26 revenue — the conclusion is not FX- or growth-rate-fragile at the headline level, though it is capacity-fragile (below).

### 3C. Capacity cross-check

Per the injected capex-embedded-growth figure, the hub-count metric (7 → 26 hubs, +271% by FY29) is flagged as "not decision-useful" for a lease-based, asset-light model and is used here only as a facilities proxy. **The actual binding constraint is clinical staffing, not leased floor space:**

- Disclosed ceiling: **5 embryologists, ~120 cycles/month cap each** (B04, RHP) = 600 cycles/month = **7,200 cycles/year** at 100% utilisation
- At FY26 ARPP (₹2.98L, B04) = **₹214.6 Cr annual revenue ceiling** at today's embryologist headcount
- **3yr SOM (₹161 Cr) = 75.1% of this ceiling — feasible without headcount growth, but tight** (requires near-full utilisation of already-attrition-stressed staff)
- **5yr SOM (₹281 Cr) = 131.0% of this ceiling — a gap of ~₹66.5 Cr, requiring ~31% more embryologist capacity than exists today**

**Capacity check: gap of ~₹66.5 Cr at the 5-year horizon.** Hub-count growth (the capex-committed, capacity-embedded metric) is not itself the constraint — management has not disclosed a concrete embryologist-hiring pipeline tied to the FY27(10)/FY28(8)/FY29(1)-hub rollout (B04 open management question), and clinical-staff attrition is already elevated and rising (SMP attrition nil → 29% in H1FY26). **The SOM (5yr) figure is the optimistic side of this comparison unless embryologist headcount scales roughly in proportion to hub count — a plan not yet disclosed.**

## SECTION 5: SUMMARY & RUNWAY

### 5A. Funnel (₹ Cr)

```

                                                    China per-capita benchmark, ₹21,972 Cr]
   → apply 5 filters (13.85% combined)

   → current SAM-scope share 3.52% (Gaudium FY26 SAM-scope revenue ₹70.96 Cr)

SOM (5yr, FY31)                     ₹   281 Cr   (7.52% of SAM(yr5) ₹3,738 Cr)
```

### 5B. Runway assessment

- **Revenue headroom** = SAM (today) ÷ current SAM-scope revenue = ₹2,017 Cr ÷ ₹70.96 Cr = **28.4x**
- **TAM growth rate** = 13.13% CAGR (Infomerics, 2024-2034; independent CMI estimate is lower at 7.9%, used as a sensitivity, not the headline)
- **Company CAGR vs. TAM:** Gaudium's FY26 reported revenue growth was **+47.56% YoY** (REVENUE_ANCHOR) — well above the 13.13% TAM growth rate, i.e. the company is gaining share, not merely riding the market. **Caveat:** this blended growth rate is inflated by the pharmacy-trading line's rapid mix rise (0.5%→31% of revenue, FY23-FY26, B04); the pure-IVF core-segment growth rate is **NOT FOUND** with precision (RHP cycle counts of 3,512/3,711/3,476/1,824 for FY23/24/25/H1FY26 do not reconcile cleanly against B04's separately-defined "OPU" metric of 2,255 for FY26 — flagged as a data-definition gap, not fabricated). The share-gain conclusion should be read as directionally correct but the magnitude is overstated by the pharmacy effect.
- **Years to saturate SAM at current growth:** using the more sustainable SOM-implied CAGR (~31.7%) rather than the pharmacy-inflated headline 47.56%: ln(28.4)/ln(1.317) ≈ **12.1 years** — well beyond the 5-year SOM horizon, meaning SAM size itself is not a binding constraint over the periods modelled here. (At the headline 47.56% blended growth rate, saturation would arrive in ~8.3 years — even that is not a near-term constraint.)

### 5C. Runway classification: **STRONG**

Methodology note: no numeric MASSIVE/STRONG/GOOD/MODERATE/LIMITED matrix was found in the injected frameworks (Master v3.3 references the label but not a numeric threshold table), so a explicit, disclosed judgment is applied: MASSIVE requires both headroom >25x **and** TAM growth >15%; STRONG requires headroom >15x with TAM growth 8-15% (or the inverse). Gaudium clears the headroom bar decisively (28.4x) but TAM growth (13.13%) sits just under the 15% MASSIVE threshold, and the capacity gap identified in 3C (embryologist bottleneck, ~₹66.5 Cr shortfall by yr5) is a real, disclosed execution constraint that argues against the top label. **STRONG**, not MASSIVE — headroom is ample, growth is real, but the constraint is people, not market size.

### 5D. SAM expansion levers actually being pursued
