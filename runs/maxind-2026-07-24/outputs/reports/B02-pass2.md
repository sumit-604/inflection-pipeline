## Note 6 / Note 36C / Note 41(a),(d) / Note 42 — legacy fully-impaired related-party loan (MISSED entirely in Pass 1)
🔴 Standalone "Loans (Current)" (Note 6, p.168) carries "Loans to related parties – considered
doubtful" of ₹1,416.34 lakh (FY25) vs ₹1,466.34 lakh (FY24), 100% offset by an equal impairment
allowance both years. Note 36C (p.198) identifies the debtor as **Max Skill First Limited**. Note
41(a) (p.203) and Note 42 (p.204) trace the origin: erstwhile Max India Limited provided for
diminution of ₹1,916.34 lakh in advances to Max Healthstaff International Limited (now Max Skill
First Limited) between FY2008-09 and FY2014-15; recovery has trickled in over 15+ years (₹450.00
lakh in FY22-23, ₹50.00 lakh in "May 2024" per Note 41(d)). This is a chronically distressed
related-party debtor Pass 1 did not name at all, even though the standalone RPT and investments
sections were covered in detail. Fully provided for, so no incremental P&L/valuation impact, but
it corroborates a separate new finding below (Max Skill First Limited's negative consolidated net
worth) and is a related-party fact Pass 1 should have surfaced.

## Note 3b(v) — CORRECTION to a Pass 1 claim: investment property valuation date
🟡 **Correction.** Pass 1 stated the external valuation underpinning the Max Towers sale-price
comparison was dated "May-2023." The AR text (Note 3b(v), p.170-171, repeated verbatim at
Consolidated Note 4(vi), p.254) states: "the Company has considered the valuation report **dated
May 23, 2024**... fair value of the investment properties was assessed at Rs. 6,550.00 lakhs."
This means the ₹65.50 Cr appraisal predates the Dec-23-2024 Board resolution to sell at ₹105.08 Cr
by roughly **7 months**, not 21 months as Pass 1 characterized it. The revised timeline sharpens
rather than softens the question: a professional DCF-based valuation obtained only months earlier
assessed the asset at 38% below the agreed related-party sale price. Still favorable to Max India
as seller (a positive-direction related-party transaction), but the short gap between valuation and
sale-price agreement is the more precise fact and merits its own direct management question on what
changed operationally/market-wise in that window (the note cites 15%-per-3-years rental growth
assumptions in the valuation; no updated assumption set is disclosed for the sale price itself).

## Note 32A(ii) — explicit parent support commitment language (relevant to going-concern field)
🟡 Standalone Note 32.A(ii) (p.191): "The Company **may provide financial support** to Antara
Senior Living Limited and Antara Assisted Care Services Limited which are wholly owned subsidiaries
of the Company in order to meet their future financial obligations." Pass 1's going-concern check
found no explicit going-concern qualification, which is correct, but this softer letter-of-support
commitment for the two loss-making subsidiaries is going-concern-adjacent disclosure that belongs
in the record — it is the accounting-note evidence behind the parent's continued CCPS funding
pattern (₹171.5 Cr in FY25 alone, per Pass 1) and should inform the going_concern_language field
even though it does not rise to a formal qualification.

## Note 41(b) — historical corporate guarantee / mortgage, now released
🟢 New context, not previously surfaced: a ₹2,225.18 lakh term loan borrowed by Antara Senior
Living Limited was secured by an equitable mortgage over the parent's own investment property (the
same Max Towers L19/L20/20M floors later sold to Max Towers Private Limited) — see Consolidated
Note 4(v), p.254, and Standalone Note 41(b), p.203. The loan was "fully repaid in FY 2023-24 ahead
of its scheduled repayment," releasing the mortgage before the property was reclassified as held-
for-sale in Dec-2024. This clarifies why "no corporate guarantees outstanding" is technically
correct at FY25-end (Pass 1 flagged the operator-context ₹75 Cr guarantee as absent from this AR,
which stands) while showing the parent's real estate has previously been used as subsidiary-loan
collateral — a pattern worth tracking if it recurs.

## Note 3b / Note 36.1 — lease liabilities and ROU assets surged ~138% YoY (MISSED in Pass 1)
🔴 Consolidated Right-of-Use assets grew from ₹4,547.63 lakh to ₹11,184.99 lakh (+146%), with
₹6,807.09 lakh of new additions in FY25 alone (Note 3b, p.252) versus ₹3,422.62 lakh in FY24.
Correspondingly, the lease liability (Note 19 break-up, p.261; Note 36.1, p.274-275) jumped from
₹4,238.06 lakh to ₹10,087.24 lakh (+138%), and total undiscounted future lease commitments more
than doubled from ₹6,344.19 lakh to ₹14,659.48 lakh. Pass 1 did not discuss consolidated lease
liability growth at all (it only touched the much smaller standalone ROU movement). This is a
material new fixed long-term obligation (~₹100.9 Cr on-balance-sheet, ~₹146.6 Cr undiscounted)
taken on in a year when the Group's treasury was being drawn down hard to fund AACSL — almost
certainly reflecting new Care Home/Assisted Care facility leases (consistent with the operator-
context's 8-facility, 485-bed footprint by Q4). Layering rising fixed lease commitments on top of
continuing cash burn deserves explicit tracking; it did not appear anywhere in Pass 1's coverage.

## Note 13 — unexplained ₹31.49 Cr "Tax relating to earlier years" reconciling item
🔴 The consolidated tax-rate reconciliation (Note 13, p.259-260) includes a line "Tax relating to
earlier years" of ₹3,148.93 lakh in FY25 versus ₹(31.06) lakh in FY24 — a swing that dwarfs every
other reconciling item in the table (the next-largest is "Unabsorbed losses" at ₹354.55 lakh). No
entity or matter is identified. The actual consolidated cash/current tax expense stayed modest
(₹181.05 lakh), so this is a reconciling artefact of the "each entity assessed on its own standalone
return" tax methodology the note explains elsewhere — but at ₹31.49 Cr, equivalent to 8.8% of
consolidated net worth (₹358.7 Cr), its size and total absence of explanation is a legitimate gap
Pass 1 did not surface (Pass 1 covered the unrecognised-DTA growth and headline tax-expense figures
but not this reconciliation line).

## Note 39.2 — segment data confirms treasury collapse with a precise number
🟡 Segment assets for "Business Investments" (the parent's own treasury/cash pool) collapsed from
₹25,570.77 lakh to ₹3,199.96 lakh, **-87.5% YoY** (Note 39.2, p.292). Pass 1 described the parent's
liquidity drawdown narratively (current ratio 37.4x→2.4x) but did not cite this precise segment-
level figure, which is the cleanest single number quantifying how much of the Group's own treasury
buffer was consumed funding AACSL/ASLL in FY25.

## Note 43 — Max Skill First Limited carries negative net worth (new)
🟡 The Schedule III "additional information" table (Note 43, p.294-295) shows Max Skill First
Limited with net assets of ₹(1,375.69) lakh at FY25 (₹(1,373.79) lakh FY24) — a small subsidiary
with persistently negative equity. Already fully impaired at the parent-investment level (per Pass
1's standalone Note 5 coverage) and now corroborated by the fully-doubtful related-party loan found
above (Note 6/36C), but the negative-net-worth fact itself was not stated anywhere in Pass 1.

## Note 22 — a discrete revenue line ("MedCare") disappears with no explanation (new)
🟡 Consolidated revenue disaggregation (Note 22, p.263) shows an Assisted Care line item "MedCare"
of ₹1,362.03 lakh in FY24 that goes to **Nil** in FY25, with no note anywhere (including the
segment note, 39.2) explaining discontinuation, rebranding, or reclassification into "Care Home" /
"Care at Home." Pass 1's revenue-recognition-timing narrative (finance lease collapse, AGEasy
scale-up) did not mention MedCare at all. Worth a direct question: was this business line closed,
folded into Care Home, or something else?

## Note 38E — AACSL runs its own separate ESOP scheme; MD personally holds subsidiary options (new)
🟡 Antara Assisted Care Services Limited operates a distinct equity-settled ESOP scheme with
81,14,250 options outstanding at March 2025 (exercise price ₹10, i.e. at par — Note 38, p.283-284),
separate from the parent's Max India ESOP Plan 2020. MD Rajit Mehta personally holds 18,60,000
AACSL options as at FY25 (up from 4,98,000 at FY24) (Note 38E, p.291). This is a direct equity
incentive tied specifically to the loss-making unit, layered on top of his parent-level ESOP
holdings — relevant to understanding management incentive structure and future minority-dilution
mechanics at the subsidiary level if AACSL is ever separately valued, listed, or spun out. Not
mentioned anywhere in Pass 1.

## Two internal inconsistencies worth a management/auditor question (new — pattern-adjacent, flagged now for completeness)
🟡 **Max Estates Noida stake, two different amounts.** The consolidated cash flow statement
("Investment by wholly owned subsidiary in equity shares of consortium," p.223) and RPT Note 38B
both show ₹703.38 lakh for the new 10% equity stake in Max Estates Noida Private Limited, while
Note 6b ("Other Investments," p.256-257) states ₹713.23 lakh for the same 70,33,799-share purchase
"at Rs.10 per share." The ₹9.85 lakh (1.4%) gap between the two sub-notes describing the identical
transaction was not flagged in Pass 1 (which cited both figures separately without cross-checking).
🟡 **Two differently-scaled "marketing fee" lines.** Consolidated revenue Note 22 shows Senior
Living "Marketing fee" of ₹2,230.54 lakh within revenue from operations (the Antara DM/development-
management fee Pass 1 already discussed), while Other Income Note 23 separately shows "Marketing
fees" of ₹239.01 lakh — a similarly-worded but distinct, much smaller line. The near-identical
naming across two different notes creates a real risk of double-counting when a reader reconstructs
total marketing-fee-related income; it compounds the "Lease surrender premium" disclosure-clarity
gap Pass 1 already flagged at Note 29c.

## Note 19 — scale of resident/customer security deposit liability (context, not previously quantified)
🟢 "Security deposit received" totals ₹9,393.07 lakh (₹9,156.67 lakh current + ₹236.40 lakh
non-current, FY25) versus ₹9,086.35 lakh FY24 (Note 19, p.261) — a large, largely stable refundable
liability (~2.6x the size of trade payables) that Pass 1 never mentioned. Likely resident/licensee
deposits from the senior-living communities. Growth is modest (+3.4% YoY) so this is background
context rather than a red flag, but its scale (₹93.9 Cr, ~26% of consolidated net worth) is
material enough that an investor should know it exists as a long-term refundable obligation
alongside the Group's other liquidity claims.
