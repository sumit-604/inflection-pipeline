# FTTCP draft — OBSC Perfection Ltd

OBSC Perfection Ltd (OBSCP). CMP Rs 666. Run date 2026-07-12. First workup, not a refresh. Ran with a live latest concall, so NOT in no concall mode, though two of the seven preferred sources are missing (standalone results filings and the credit rating report).

Precision engineered metal parts maker for automotive Tier 1s and OEMs, listed on NSE Emerge in October 2024. Revenue grew fast, cash did not follow, and the return on capital fell after the listing raise. The forward call turns entirely on whether the cash and return transitions are a temporary growth build or a lasting drain. I have made every call below.

---

## MY RULINGS

Every line here is a decision, not a question. Confidence is tagged `sure` / `fairly sure` / `genuinely uncertain`, and each carries the single fact that would flip it.

1. Forward window. `fairly sure`. The company reports roughly half yearly and broke its own quarterly disclosure promise, so no reliable 3 month print exists. I use 6 months as the primary window and 12 months as the primary window for the return on capital transition. What flips it: a Q1 FY27 quarterly result and concall actually landing on schedule.

2. Business type. `sure`. Standard operating business, an asset heavy precision components manufacturer. I run the four standard transitions, not the lender set. What flips it: nothing in this business; it makes and sells physical parts.

3. Workup intent. `sure`. First workup. No prior companies/OBSCP.md exists and the manifest run_type is full. Every field that would come from a prior valuation (destination PE, prior thesis, prior devil's advocate) is N/A because FTTCP runs before valuation. What flips it: a prior company memory file appearing.

4. Sector cap row (corrected for Phase 3). `fairly sure`. The manifest says "EPC / Civil construction" at 20x. That is wrong. OBSCP is not a civil contractor. It is an asset heavy precision components manufacturer selling products to automotive Tier 1s, with small defense, marine and aerospace tails. The correct Section 1B row is Manufacturing at 25x (the "Recycling / Manufacturing" row; the "Cables / Industrial products" row carries the same 25x ceiling, so the ruling is robust either way). Phase 3 stage 11 must use 25x, not 20x. What flips it: Keerti maintaining a bespoke auto ancillary cap, or the mix shifting so defense (38x cap) becomes the majority of revenue.

5. Cash conversion, structural versus growth induced. `genuinely uncertain`, ruled INDETERMINATE. The drain tracks the growth: in FY25 receivables rose 62%, inventory 79% and payables 118% against 24% revenue growth, and FY26 operating cash flow was negative Rs 1.95 Cr while profit after tax was positive Rs 27.01 Cr. If growth stopped tomorrow, part of that working capital would release, which argues growth induced. But the receivables ageing and expected credit loss schedule that would confirm the receivables are current and collectable sits in annual report pages that were truncated in the source PDF, so I cannot rule out a collection quality problem. Both readings are live. I used INDETERMINATE, which under the project rule caps the pipeline disposition at PROCEED WITH CAVEATS and never resolves to a clean pass. What flips it: the FY27 receivables ageing note. Clean current buckets confirm growth induced; a rising overdue bucket confirms structural.

6. Cash conversion forward verdict. `fairly sure`, ruled DECLINING with catalyst strength NONE. Management named no working capital program, no debtor day target, and no factoring or discounting facility across the two available calls, and FY26 cash flow went negative rather than improving. A missing catalyst on a deteriorating transition is the loudest signal the protocol has, and it engages the Kernex cap. What flips it: a documented receivables factoring facility with a start date, or a stated debtor day target, in the next disclosure.

7. Return on capital, backward state. `fairly sure`, ruled TEMPORARILY DEPRESSED. Return on capital employed fell from 33.4% in FY24 to 19.0% in FY25, a drop of more than 500 basis points, with an identifiable temporary cause: the October 2024 listing raised roughly Rs 57 Cr, so reserves jumped from Rs 12 Cr to Rs 80 Cr and bloated the capital base faster than profit could catch up, on top of capex that had not yet started earning. What flips it: evidence the depression is demand driven rather than balance sheet driven.

8. Return on capital, forward verdict. `genuinely uncertain`, ruled RECOVERING at a low to moderate probability, roughly 45%. The recovery case is real: the listing bloat is a one time step up that profit grows into, earnings before interest and tax rose 53% in FY26, and capex at Sanand, stamping and forging is documented and about to commission. The decline case is also real: the company keeps raising fresh capital (a Rs 43.3 Cr preferential issue in FY26) and keeps starting new capex (the Supa mega factory, quantum undisclosed, plus another Rs 15 Cr to 20 Cr in FY27), free cash flow is negative, and on a screener based proxy FY26 return on capital looks like it fell again rather than recovered. The framework says bias to DECLINING when genuinely torn; I held it at RECOVERING only because the commissioning catalyst is documented and the depressor is identifiably the listing raise. The numbers in this draft used RECOVERING (+1). The operator can flip this to DECLINING (which would drop the composite to 0 and the band to AVOID). What flips it: FY26 return on capital, once anchored at stage 10, printing below 18% with FY27 showing no recovery.

9. Margin forward verdict. `fairly sure`, ruled STAGNANT. The margin is held at a healthy level near 19.5%, but management itself cut the medium term expansion ambition from 4 to 5 points down to about 1 point for FY27, so the expansion transition is not firing. It is not declining either, so STAGNANT is the round down. What flips it: EBITDA margin above 21% for two consecutive prints.

10. Revenue forward verdict. `sure`, ruled FIRING. Revenue grew 54% in FY26, the order book is above Rs 1,200 Cr against roughly Rs 220 Cr of revenue, and new capacity is documented and commissioning. What flips it: two consecutive halves below 30% year on year growth.

11. Composite and position. `fairly sure`. Composite +2 out of 8, which is the DEEP WATCH leaning AVOID band, and the Kernex cap independently holds the verdict at DEEP WATCH because cash conversion is DECLINING with no catalyst. The whole call turns on the FY27 operating cash flow print. What flips it: FY27 cash flow turning clearly positive, which would lift cash from DECLINING and remove the cap.

12. Disposition handoff. `fairly sure`. The FTTCP position band is DEEP WATCH leaning AVOID. Separately, the INDETERMINATE cash determination caps the pipeline disposition at PROCEED WITH CAVEATS with the missing evidence named. These are two different axes and both are stated so Phase 3 inherits both.

## THE CATALYST STORY IN PLAIN WORDS

Revenue has the strongest and most documented catalysts. The order book above Rs 1,200 Cr is more than five times last year's revenue. A dedicated line for Tenneco at Sanand is coming up and management put roughly Rs 40 Cr of revenue on it. Defense grew about 150% last year off a small base. Exports are riding a genuine China plus one shift that the four peers independently confirm. The one caution is that the order book converts slowly, at Rs 100 Cr to 200 Cr a year, so near term growth still leans on the guide and on capacity commissioning rather than on the book alone.

Margin has weak catalysts. Operating leverage on new capacity and in house forging could help, but management itself walked its ambition down to about 1 point of expansion for FY27 and blamed tariffs, the West Asia conflict and shipping costs. Three of the four peers faced the same costs in the same window and reaffirmed their margin guidance rather than cutting it, so the macro excuse does not fully hold, which makes the trim look company specific.

Cash conversion has no catalyst that I could find. Across two calls management named no working capital discipline program, no debtor day target, and no factoring or discounting facility. The receivables and inventory keep building ahead of sales and the payables keep stretching to part fund them. Absence of a plan on a deteriorating transition is itself the finding. What would confirm a turn: a factoring facility with a start date, or a stated debtor day target, plus a positive operating cash flow print. What would kill it: another negative cash year with debtor days above 110.

Return on capital has one real catalyst and one real counter. The catalyst is commissioning: Sanand, stamping and forging entering the revenue phase would earn on capital already deployed, and the listing cash getting put to work would stop dragging. The counter is the treadmill: the Supa mega factory and continued FY27 capex keep adding fresh capital that will not earn for a year or two, and the preferential raise keeps bloating the base. What would confirm recovery: FY26 return on capital anchored above 20% and rising. What would kill it: it prints below 18% and FY27 shows no lift.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | Order book >Rs 1,200 Cr plus Sanand line | Documented, concall and filings | Sanand revenue lands on schedule | Order intake stalls, exports hit by tariffs |
| Margin | Operating leverage, in house forging | Management claim, discounted | EBITDA margin >21% two prints | Cost inflation persists, guide cut again |
| Cash | NONE FOUND | Absence across two calls | Factoring facility or debtor target announced | Second negative cash year |
| ROCE | Capex commissioning earns | Documented capex, unconfirmed return | FY26 ROCE anchored >20% rising | Prints <18%, fresh capex keeps bloating base |

## THE VERDICT

Composite score +2 out of 8: revenue FIRING (+2), margin STAGNANT (0), cash conversion DECLINING (−1), return on capital RECOVERING (+1). That sits in the DEEP WATCH leaning AVOID band. The Kernex cap engages independently, because cash conversion is going backward with no observable catalyst, and it holds the verdict at DEEP WATCH regardless of the score. The TRIM rule does not apply, since not all four transitions are firing backward. The whole call turns on one print: FY27 operating cash flow. A clear positive turn lifts cash off DECLINING, removes the cap, and reopens the case; a second negative year confirms the drain and pushes this toward AVOID.
