## LOAD-BEARING FACTS — FIRST VERIFICATION PRIORITY

### LB1: Cash conversion (receivables, inventory, provisioning)
- Inventories rose Rs 1,870 Lakhs (Rs 18.70 Cr), from Rs 7,221 Lakhs to Rs 9,091 Lakhs (Note 12, p.208 PDF standalone). This EXACTLY matches the briefed Rs 18.70 Cr inventory absorption. The increase is entirely a raw-material build: Raw material Rs 4,881 Lakhs to Rs 6,872 Lakhs (+40.8%), far outpacing revenue growth of 26.5%. Finished goods actually FELL: Rs 950 Lakhs to Rs 863 Lakhs (-9.2%), and finished-goods-in-transit fell too (Rs 557 Lakhs to Rs 474 Lakhs). So the LB1 flag is NOT a finished-goods/channel-stuffing pattern; it is a raw-material stocking decision. Inventory provision (write-down) is Rs 854 Lakhs FY26 vs Rs 805 Lakhs FY25 (Note 12c) — provisioning COVERAGE as a % of gross inventory THINNED, from 11.1% to 9.4%, even as raw-material inventory surged 40.8%. 🟡 Watch: no obsolescence disclosure beyond the aggregate provision; no ageing of raw-material inventory is given.
- Trade receivables (Note 13, p.208-209 PDF) rose from Rs 16,965 Lakhs to Rs 20,646 Lakhs, a Rs 3,681 Lakh (Rs 36.81 Cr) increase — NOT the same as the briefed Rs 35.47 Cr cash-flow-statement absorption figure. This Rs 1.34 Cr gap between the balance-sheet note delta and the cash-flow-statement working-capital line is unexplained within the notes; it likely reflects a forex-translation or reclassification adjustment inside the cash flow statement itself (a primary statement, out of scope for this Notes pass) and should be checked directly against the Cash Flow Statement in a later stage. 🟡 Flag for reconciliation.
- Ageing (Note 13c, both years, p.208-209 PDF) shows real deterioration: the "less than 6 months [overdue]" bucket grew from Rs 1,699 Lakhs to Rs 4,290 Lakhs (+152.4%), nearly 6x the 26.5% revenue growth rate. All buckets beyond 6 months (6mo-1yr + 1-2yr + 2-3yr + >3yr) grew from Rs 186 Lakhs to Rs 323 Lakhs (+73.7%) — still a small absolute share (1.6% of total receivables) but directionally worse for the second consecutive disclosure. Overall, receivables NOT YET DUE fell from 88.9% to 77.7% of the book; overdue-of-any-age rose from 11.1% to 22.3%.
- Despite this, Note 13 carries NO expected-credit-loss allowance line at all (only "Considered good" is shown), and Note 48 (Financial Risk Management, p.232-233 PDF) makes this explicit: "the Company has concluded that there is no significant credit risk or increase in credit risk on the customer profile, and, accordingly, no allowance for expected credit losses have been recognized." This is a direct management judgment call that sits uneasily against the ageing-table facts in the very same note set. 🟡 Watch, arguably 🔴 Red Flag given this is the specific cash-conversion guard the run was told to interrogate: a 152% jump in the near-term-overdue bucket with a zero-ECL conclusion is the kind of gap the Kernex/Tipco pattern warns about.
- Credit-term disclosure is internally inconsistent across three notes describing the SAME receivables population: accounting policy 2.3 says "credit term of 60-90 days" (p.190 PDF); Note 13a says "generally on terms of 45 to 60 days" (p.208 PDF); Note 48 says "typically due within 45 to 90 days" (p.233 PDF). 🟡 Watch — a disclosure-consistency gap, not necessarily a substantive one.
- Cash and bank balances (Note 14, p.209-210 PDF) fell 64.8%, from Rs 1,562 Lakhs to Rs 550 Lakhs in current accounts (total cash+bank balances Rs 1,926 Lakhs to Rs 622 Lakhs). This happened in a year the company paid a larger dividend (Rs 3,506 Lakhs vs Rs 2,828 Lakhs), increased capex commitments (capital advances Rs 312 Lakhs vs Rs 52 Lakhs; capital commitments Rs 2,635 Lakhs vs Rs 367 Lakhs), and grew its investment book by Rs 5,501 Lakhs (Rs 55.01 Cr). Net CFO of Rs 40.47 Cr (per brief) funded all of dividend + capex + incremental investment purchases + working capital absorption, drawing bank balances down. 🟡 Watch — a liquidity-mix shift, not distress (zero borrowings, Note 48), but the cash cushion is now thin relative to the scale of the business.

CONCLUSION FOR LB3: the Lucas TVS gain never touches reported P&L or EPS, now or on eventual sale — it is a permanent equity-reserve build. Separately, Note 29 (Other Income) shows Rs 1,120 Lakhs (FY26) / Rs 860 Lakhs (FY25) "Gain on fair valuation of investment carried at FVTPL" — this DOES run through P&L, but it relates to the FVTPL bucket (listed equities, mutual funds, AIFs, bonds — NOT Lucas TVS), so it is a separate, smaller, and P&L-relevant fair-value stream.
- SCALE CONTEXT: Total investments of Rs 531.53 Cr are 64.7% of total net worth (Rs 821.33 Cr = Rs 1,131 Lakhs share capital + Rs 81,002 Lakhs other equity, Notes 18-19). The Lucas TVS stake alone (Rs 264.12 Cr) is 32.2% of net worth — nearly a third of the company's entire book value sits in a single, related-party, Level-3-valued equity holding whose fair value hinges on a management-chosen multiple unchanged for two years. 🟡 Watch, high investor relevance for valuation and capital-allocation analysis (this is likely to be the single largest driver of the run's Section 1B / SOTP conversation).
- Deferred tax: Note 22 shows the DTL "arising on account of fair valuation of investments" is Rs 4,173 Lakhs FY26 (88% of gross DTL) vs Rs 3,327 Lakhs FY25 — confirming that most of the company's Rs 47.23 Cr net deferred tax liability is an artifact of unrealized investment gains (principally Lucas TVS), not the operating ignition-systems business.
