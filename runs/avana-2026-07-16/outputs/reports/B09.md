## SECTION 2: TAM ESTIMATION — MULTIPLE METHODS

All figures ₹ Crore, India, FY2026 basis unless stated. FX used: ~₹83/USD for FY2024-dated
report bases (RBI historical average), ~₹96/USD for run-date (16-Jul-2026) conversions
(exchangerates.org.uk / Federal Reserve H.10, 13-16 Jul 2026 data).

**Method 1 — Top-down (two anchor reports, power-sector relevant)**

*Anchor A — India Protection Relay market* (marketsandata.com, "India Protection Relay Market
Size, Share and Forecast 2032," cross-confirmed across two independent search hits):
USD 165.75 M (FY2024) → USD 246.76 M (FY2032F), CAGR 5.10%.
- FY2024 in ₹: 165.75M × 83 = ₹1,376 Cr.
- Grown to FY2026 (2 yrs @ 5.10%): 1,376 × 1.051² = **₹1,520 Cr.** STALE flag: base year FY2024,
  ~2.3 years old at run date — data point flagged STALE per staleness rule, informs the estimate
  but confidence downgraded to M.

*Anchor B — India Electric Control Panel market* (marketsandata.com / researchandmarkets.com,
"India Electric Control Panel Market Size, Share & Forecast 2032"): USD 394.80 M (FY2024) →
USD 615.14 M (FY2032F), CAGR 5.70%. Report explicitly states **Low-Tension (LT) panels dominate**
— this is a much broader market than Avana's HT/MV-HV utility-grade niche.
- FY2024 in ₹: 394.80M × 83 = ₹3,277 Cr. (One source also quotes "₹4,028.2 Cr" directly for what
  appears to be the same metric at a different base year/FX assumption — the two do not
  reconcile cleanly; **conservative bias applied, ₹3,277 Cr used**, divergence flagged.)
- Grown to FY2026 (2 yrs @ 5.70%): 3,277 × 1.057² = ₹3,660 Cr — **total control panel market,
  LT+HT, all end-uses.**
- Carve-out for Avana's HT/MV-HV utility-protection niche: since LT "dominates," the HT segment
  is a minority. **No published split % found** — analyst assumption applied (flagged, not
  sourced): HT/utility-grade share = 20% (conservative) to 30% (realistic) of the total.
  → ₹3,660 Cr × 20–30% = **₹732–1,098 Cr.**

Because protection relays are largely deployed *inside* control & relay panels, Anchors A and B
overlap substantially rather than stack additively (summing would double-count the relay content
embedded in panels). **Method 1 conclusion: ₹1,100–1,700 Cr**, taking the overlap-adjusted band
between the relay-market anchor and the HT-panel-carve anchor rather than a naive sum.
Confidence: **M** (sourced anchors, but the HT/LT split and the overlap-adjustment are analyst
judgement, not a directly published figure).

## SECTION 3: SAM & SOM

**3A SAM — five filters applied to conservative TAM (₹1,100 Cr)**

| Filter | Cut | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Product fit | −5% | TAM already carved to HT/MV-HV protection & control; residual cut for adjacent SCADA-software-only value not physically supplied by Avana |
| Geography | 0% | TAM is already India-only; Avana is India-only — no further cut |
| Channel | 0% | Avana's direct/EPC-tender channel matches the bulk of this TAM's channel structure |
| Customer | −15% | Excludes largest-scale PSU/EHV (>220kV, e.g. 400kV+ transmission-class) tenders reserved for top-tier qualified majors (BHEL, Siemens, CG, L&T) where Avana lacks disclosed qualification/track record beyond 220kV for panels |
| Capability | −10% | Current installed capacity (70,000 relays/yr, 600 panels/yr) and scale versus large diversified competitors constrain near-term serviceable share regardless of tender eligibility |

Cumulative: 1,100 × 0.95 × 1.00 × 1.00 × 0.85 × 0.90 = **₹800 Cr (SAM).**
SAM as % of TAM (conservative) = 800 / 1,100 = **73%.**

**3B SOM at 3 and 5 years**

Current SAM share = Avana FY26 revenue ÷ SAM = 83.86 / 800 = **10.5%.**

Share-gain trajectory: Avana does not fit the ">40% unorganised, fast-formalising" exception
(this niche is organised-conglomerate-dominated, not fragmented-unorganised, per Method 3
discussion). It also is not a case of competitor exit/acquisition (no evidence found), so the
">5pp" band is not invoked. Recent execution (FY24→FY26 revenue ₹52.99→₹83.86 Cr, ~26% 2-yr CAGR)
and an IPO-funded capacity expansion in progress support the **aggressive 3–5pp band** rather than
the normal 1–2pp band.

- 3-year share gain: **+3pp** → 13.5% of SAM at year 3.
- 5-year share gain: **+5pp** (top of the aggressive band, held at the rule's ceiling since no
  competitor-exit/M&A evidence exists to justify going higher) → 15.5% of SAM at year 5.

SAM itself grows with the underlying market. Blended TAM/SAM growth rate used: average of the two
Method-1 anchor CAGRs (5.10% relay, 5.70% panel) = **~5.5%/year.**

- SAM at year 3 (FY2029): 800 × 1.055³ = ₹939 Cr. SOM_3yr = 13.5% × 939 = **₹127 Cr.**
- SAM at year 5 (FY2031): 800 × 1.055⁵ = ₹1,046 Cr. SOM_5yr = 15.5% × 1,046 = **₹162 Cr.**

Implied revenue CAGR (arithmetic, from FY26 base ₹83.86 Cr):
- To SOM_3yr ₹127 Cr over 3 years: (127/83.86)^(1/3) − 1 = **14.8%.**
- To SOM_5yr ₹162 Cr over 5 years: (162/83.86)^(1/5) − 1 = **14.1%.**

**Flag:** both SOM-implied CAGRs (14–15%) sit well below Avana's realised historical revenue CAGR
(24.5% FY20-26 six-year CAGR; ~26% FY24-26 two-year CAGR, both from screener-Data_Sheet.csv).
This is a material tension for stage 11: either (a) recent hyper-growth partly reflects
order-book lumpiness, a low base, and one-off IPO-linked capacity additions that will not repeat
at the same rate, or (b) the standard 1–2pp/3–5pp share-gain rules understate what Avana can
realistically capture given its current momentum, or (c) the TAM/SAM base triangulated here
(built entirely from third-party aggregator search snippets, no Avana-specific segment report)
is itself conservative. Stage 11 should treat the SOM-implied CAGR as a **floor cross-check**,
not a ceiling, given this tension.

**5B Runway assessment**

- Revenue headroom = SAM ÷ current revenue = 800 / 83.86 = **9.5x.**
- TAM growth rate (blended) ≈ **5.5%/year** (5.10% relay-market CAGR, 5.70% control-panel-market
  CAGR, both third-party sourced, both STALE-flagged base years).
- Company CAGR (24.5%, FY20-26, screener-Data_Sheet.csv) vs TAM growth (5.5%): **company is
  gaining share dramatically**, not merely riding the market — consistent with the tension flagged
  in 3B.
- Years to saturate SAM at current (historical) growth: illustrative only — if Avana's revenue
  continued to compound at ~24.5% against a SAM compounding at ~5.5%, the effective share-gain
  rate of ~18%/year would close the gap from 10.5% to 100% of SAM in roughly **13-14 years**
  (illustrative arithmetic, not a forecast — sustaining 24.5% CAGR for over a decade is not
  itself underwritten by anything in this stage).

**5C Runway classification: GOOD.**
Rationale: revenue headroom (9.5x) sits in a solid-but-not-explosive band, while TAM growth itself
is only mid-single-digit (5.5%) rather than double-digit — the bulk of Avana's growth optionality
here is share-gain-driven (supported by the very large gap between 24.5% historical company CAGR
and 5.5% market growth) rather than market-expansion-driven. This combination — meaningful
headroom, modest market growth, large share-gain runway — reads as GOOD rather than STRONG or
MASSIVE (which would require faster underlying market growth) and clearly above MODERATE/LIMITED
(headroom is not the binding constraint here).

**5D SAM expansion levers**

- **Capacity expansion (evidenced, in progress):** ₹11.55 Cr of IPO proceeds committed to a new
  integrated manufacturing unit (RHP, per IPO-aggregator sources). If this closes the -10%
  capability filter applied in 3A, SAM would rise from 73% to ~81% of TAM (₹1,100 Cr conservative
  base), i.e. from ₹800 Cr to **~₹884 Cr**, adding ~₹84 Cr of revised headroom. This is the one
  lever with direct evidentiary support this run.
- **Export expansion (not yet evidenced for Avana):** peers Aartech (targeting continued
  overseas wins in Indonesia/Qatar/Oman/China per its May-2026 investor meet) and Danish Power
  (targeting 20-30% export mix within two years, per its Nov-2025 and May-2026 concalls) are
  both actively pursuing export share gains in adjacent product categories. **No evidence found
  that Avana itself is pursuing exports** — this is a plausible but currently un-evidenced SAM
  expansion lever for Avana specifically, not counted in the figures above.
- **Higher-voltage-tier qualification (>220kV):** would relax the -15% customer-tier filter if
  achieved, but no evidence found that Avana has begun this qualification process (contrast with
  Danish Power's explicit 220kV/245kV validation programme) — not counted.
