## Part 1: Claim by claim

**Q1 Peer working-capital days and basis.** Verdict: PARTIALLY VERIFIED.
- Peer evidence: MTAR reports 260 days (MT-Q3 [page 6]), 172 days at Mar 2026 (MT-Q4 [page 5]), 59 days (MT-Q1 [page 7]), 50 days at Sep 2026 (MT-Q2 [page 7]). Its Q1 inventory days fell 208 to 145 and receivable days 140 to 82 (MT-Q1 [page 9]), so 59 days is net of a large payables or advances offset. Unimech reports 120-125 days (UN-Q4 [page 7]), 130 (UN-Q1 [page 7]), 110 (UN-Q2 [page 8]), and expects 150-160. PTCIL gives no days figure (PT-23 [page 20] gives debt ratios only).
- Basis (revenue or COGS, average or closing): NOT STATED by any peer.
- Peers silent: PTCIL.
- Net read: peers confirm WC days are reported, but never on a stated basis, so Azad's 200-day target and CARE's 344 cannot be mapped to them. MTAR's analyst puts inventory at about Rs 680 Cr, near 5 months of guided revenue (MT-Q2 [page 10]). That sits beside a 50-day headline. [INFERENCE] MTAR's days are net of advances and creditors.

**Q2 Commissioning to full-rate invoicing, including delta qualification.** Verdict: PARTIALLY VERIFIED.
- Peer evidence: MTAR Phase 3 hot box capacity went from "early CY27" (MT-Q3 [page 8]) to Mar 2027 (MT-Q1 [page 11]) to Jun 2028 in a new facility (MT-Q2 [page 4]). The O&G plant went from Sep end (MT-Q4 [page 3]) to Oct (MT-Q1 [page 6]) to end Nov (MT-Q2 [page 6]). Aerospace qualification "would take about 1.5 years" incl. NADCAP (MT-Q2 [page 17]). Unimech: FTWZ "customs end of May" (UN-ACQ [page 4]) became "fully operational" by Aug (UN-Q1 [page 5]); aerospace qualification takes "2-3 years or more" (UN-Q4 [page 12]); Q2 is "a qualification year", harvest next year (UN-Q2 [page 17]). PTCIL: rotating engine parts need the longest approval (PT-23 [page 31]).
- Peers silent: none; but no peer gives a plant-commissioning-to-full-rate number for a delta-qualified dedicated plant.
- Net read: slips and multi-quarter lags are the norm. Azad's one-quarter slip (Q3 to Q4 FY27) is mild against MTAR's 15-month Phase 3 move. No like-for-like magnitude exists.

**Q3 Bill discounting or factoring on export receivables.** Verdict: PARTIALLY VERIFIED.
- Peer evidence: MTAR CFO: "We have not discounted. Whatever we have done is organically. Only around USD1 million is discounted" (MT-Q2 [page 13]). Recourse and cash flow presentation NOT STATED.
- Peers silent: Unimech, PTCIL.
- Net read: the one peer that fixed WC did so without material discounting. Azad's plan leans on discounting (recourse NOT STATED in B05). A positive Azad OCF from discounting would be financing, not conversion. This peer gives no support for that route.

**Q4 OEM advances (percent of PO, discount).** Verdict: PARTIALLY VERIFIED.
- Peer evidence: MTAR sought advances "in a big way" (MT-Q3 [page 5]). In May the MD said "It's not about advances. It's about better payment terms in terms of credit period" (MT-Q4 [page 13]). In Oct the CFO said advances came "from all the sectors", nuclear tenders carry "30%, 40%", others "20%" or future-deliverable advances (MT-Q2 [page 11], [page 18]). Discount given: NOT STATED.
- Peers silent: Unimech, PTCIL.
- Net read: advances of 20% to 40% are the peer range. Azad's 50% of PO at about 1% discount (one OEM) is above it. MTAR's May and Oct statements differ.

**Q5 Dedicated facilities with take-or-pay or minimum volume.** Verdict: UNVERIFIABLE.
- Peer evidence: MTAR builds hot box capacity "based on a clear visibility given by the customer" (MT-Q3 [page 9]) and withholds numbers under NDA (MT-Q1 [page 11]). Unimech: "We do have some capacity dedicated for each customer, but typically all these machines are fungible" (UN-Q2 [page 13]). MTAR: 15 of 16 units fungible (MT-Q2 [page 19]). No peer states take-or-pay terms.
- Peers silent: PTCIL.
- Net read: peers also disclose no volume-commitment terms. Silence is informative: committed-capacity builds in this cohort run on customer forecasts, not disclosed contracts.

**Q6 Margins, gross margin, consumption-to-sales, WIP.** Verdict: PARTIALLY VERIFIED.
- Peer evidence: Unimech EBITDA 36.5% in Q1 and 40% in Q2 on gross margin 65-69% (UN-Q1 [page 6]; UN-Q2 [page 8]). MTAR EBITDA 19.5% FY26 (MT-Q4 [page 6]), 24.4% Q2 FY27 (MT-Q2 [page 3]) on gross margin 46.8% (MT-Q2 [page 7]). MTAR is "building the WIP for various nuclear programs" (MT-Q4 [page 16]). Unimech says qualification programs "carry higher initial cost" (UN-Q1 [page 6]).
- Net read: a 36-40% EBITDA band has a peer precedent (Unimech, tooling with Hobel). Azad's consumption at 4.2% of revenue has no peer match: peer gross margins imply input cost of 31% to 54% of revenue. [INFERENCE] Definitions differ, so this is not a contradiction. No peer discloses how much cost sits in WIP.

**Q7 Customer concentration.** Verdict: PARTIALLY VERIFIED.
- Peer evidence: Hobel: two OEM groups "close to around 93% of their revenue" (UN-ACQ [page 10]); an analyst puts Cummins at 70-80% and management confirms the QSK95 platform (UN-ACQ [page 11], [page 12]). MTAR: clean energy about 70% of FY27 revenue (MT-Q4 [page 6]); management says "we hold a majority of the share" at its key customer without a number (MT-Q1 [page 19]); only two hot box suppliers exist (MT-Q2 [page 16]). PTCIL: "nobody wants a single customer" (PT-23 [page 22]).
- Net read: Azad's top-1 of 18.6% and top-5 of about 47.0% (B05) are less concentrated than both active peers. No peer states a customer share of dedicated capacity. The MTAR pattern (capacity built around one OEM) is the closest analogue to GE Vernova holding 50.9% of Azad's dedicated area.

**Q8 Hot-section and engine component economics in India.** Verdict: PARTIALLY VERIFIED.
- Peer evidence: PTCIL (2023): controlled microstructure and single-crystal castings are held by "very few companies and countries"; aerospace casting capacity 30-40 t/yr now, 600 t planned; aerospace castings priced Rs 10,000 per kg up to far higher (PT-23 [page 24], [page 30]); Safran approval is "an extremely difficult audit" taking years (PT-23 [page 22]); PCC named as the comparator (PT-23 [page 28]). Howmet is not named. Unimech: no hot-section work (tooling, Dheya micro turbines).
- Net read: one stale peer. No margin by component and no 2026 data. Weak support only.

**Q9 Industry growth rates (aero engine airfoils, gas turbine, nuclear; 25-26% dollar growth).** Verdict: PARTIALLY VERIFIED.
- Peer evidence: MTAR customer "projected to grow at an average rate of approximately 30% through 2030" (MT-Q3 [page 4]); India nuclear target 100 GW by 2047 (MT-Q1 [page 4]); nuclear order book Rs 650 Cr+ (MT-Q4 [page 4]) rising to Rs 820 Cr (MT-Q2 [page 4]). Unimech: bellows market USD 2.6 bn growing 6% (UN-ACQ [page 6]); Honeywell 10-year engine LTA (UN-Q2 [page 5]). PTCIL: 42,000 aircraft over 20 years, defence budget about 12% CAGR (PT-23 [page 5], [page 9]).
- Peers silent on gas turbine growth: all. No peer cites a growth rate for airfoils or gas turbines, and none confirms the 25-26% analyst figure.
- Net read: nuclear and aero engine demand is corroborated by two peers; gas turbine is not. Peer growth in H1 FY27 (MTAR +200% YoY Q2, Unimech +85% H1, MT-Q2 [page 7]; UN-Q2 [page 3]) is far above Azad's 25% plus, but from different mixes.

**Q10 Domestic steel suppliers (Sunflag, Star Wire) and saving.** Verdict: UNVERIFIABLE.
- Peers checked: MTAR, Unimech, PTCIL. No peer names a domestic steel supplier or reports a saving. MTAR says most bought-outs are now domestic (MT-Q2 [page 15]). That is not steel.

**Q11 Capex cycle: equity or debt beyond FY29, utilisation at commissioning.** Verdict: VERIFIED.
- Peer evidence: MTAR capex about Rs 500 Cr (Rs 100 Cr FY26 plus Rs 400 Cr FY27), "75% planned through the debt and 25% through internal accruals" (MT-Q2 [page 8], [page 11]); utilisation "up to 100%" at 8,000 boxes (MT-Q3 [page 17]); Rs 500-700 Cr for FY30 revenue of Rs 5,000 Cr (MT-Q4 [page 15]). Unimech: board resolution for up to Rs 750 Cr (UN-Q1 [page 18]); "equity fundraise in early calendar year 2027" (UN-Q2 [page 9]); utilisation 50% to 65% (UN-Q4 [page 8]; UN-Q2 [page 8]). PTCIL raised rights, warrants and preferential equity to build capacity (PT-23 [page 20]).
- Net read: two independent peers fund expansion with external capital, one by debt and one by equity. Azad's QIP and acquisition hint fits the cohort. Revenue per capex differs: MTAR says 3x (MT-Q2 [page 12]), 4-5x and 6x (MT-Q1 [page 10]); Unimech 2-2.5x (UN-Q2 [page 12]). No peer supports a Rs 150-180 Cr revenue per plant figure directly.

**Q12 Wallet share with OEMs.** Verdict: PARTIALLY VERIFIED.
- Peer evidence: Hobel is "a very small contributor to their wallet share" at Cummins (UN-ACQ [page 16]). MTAR claims 60% to 70% of orders for nuclear reactors (MT-Q1 [page 17]) and a majority at its hot box customer (MT-Q1 [page 19]).
- Net read: small share with runway is plausible for a niche supplier, but no peer quotes a figure under 2%. The under-2% claim stays unchecked.

**Q13 Currency lift.** Verdict: PARTIALLY VERIFIED.
- Peer evidence: MTAR books FX gain in other income, Rs 25 Cr for FY26 (MT-Q4 [page 11]), Rs 2.3 Cr of Rs 8.5 Cr in Q2 FY27 (MT-Q2 [page 17]); exports above 70% of revenue, imports 30-35% (MT-Q4 [page 11]). Unimech booked a Rs 9.6 Cr FX loss on foreign currency borrowing in finance cost (UN-Q4 [page 7]).
- Net read: peers place currency in other income or finance cost, not revenue. No peer quotes a revenue lift percent. Azad's 5-6% revenue lift is not tested.

**Q14 Domestic defence engine programmes (GTRE and others).** Verdict: PARTIALLY VERIFIED.
- Peer evidence: MTAR AMCA structural order is Rs 4 Cr (MT-Q4 [page 12]); "volume production are going to come much later" (MT-Q2 [page 8]); Tejas actuator order of Rs 130-150 Cr awaited since Q4 FY26, "we don't have a specific time line" (MT-Q4 [page 18]; MT-Q2 [page 14]). PTCIL 2023: Kaveri engine parts, Su-30 engine MOU with HAL (PT-23 [page 19]). Unimech: Dheya micro turbine, two test engines ordered (UN-Q4 [page 9]).
- Net read: GTRE ATGG is named by no peer. Peer defence orders convert slowly and in small amounts, which matches Azad's ATGG date slips.

## Load-bearing facts tested against peers
1. Cash conversion: MTAR shows a path (260 to 50 days, H1 OCF Rs +350 Cr) driven by customer terms and advances, and basis unstated. No gas turbine OEM customer evidence. Azad's fix rests on discounting and one advance. Not cleared.
2. Growth above 25% and Q4 FY27 ramp: nuclear and aero engine demand and long qualification lead times are corroborated. Gas turbine demand is not testable from this set.
3. Concentration: peers are more concentrated than Azad. Dedicated capacity around one OEM is a peer pattern.
4. Capex and commissioning pace: peers slip by 2 to 15 months; Azad's one-quarter slip is within the peer range.

## Part 4: Triangulation summary
- Claims verified: 1 of 14 (Q11). Partially verified: 11. Unverifiable: 2 (Q5, Q10). Contradicted: 0.
- Most consequential point: no claim is contradicted, but the peer set shows what cash conversion looks like when it improves (MTAR customer terms and advances, minimal discounting). Azad's route differs and its WC days are still unreported on a defined basis.
- Strongest independent confirmation: capacity beyond plan is funded externally by peers (Q11), and nuclear and aero engine qualification takes 1.5 to 3 years (Q2, Q9).
- Overall: the peer set complicates the Azad narrative. It supports demand (nuclear, aero engine) and capex funding. It does not support the cash conversion fix, gas turbine demand, the domestic steel saving, or margin structure. PTCIL adds almost nothing.

## Part 5: Cross-peer hypothesis
Both active peers moved from sample to volume in 15 to 24 months, and both now say the binding constraint is capacity and qualification time, not orders. Their reported working capital falls when a dominant OEM grants advances or short credit terms to secure capacity (MTAR 260 to 50 days), and rises when the mix shifts to long-cycle first-article programs (Unimech 110 to 150-160 days). So working-capital outcome tracks customer bargaining position and program stage, not management discipline. Testable: Azad's days should fall only if its dedicated-plant OEMs (GE Vernova holds 3 of 6 plants) pay advances or shorten terms, and should rise during delta qualification. [INFERENCE]
