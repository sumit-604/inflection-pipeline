## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

**2A Demand environment.** Broadly positive to mixed. Ion Exchange describes strong, supply-constrained demand in its chemicals segment and a large (~Rs 9,011 crore) engineering bid pipeline (IONEXCHANG, Feb 2026/Q3 FY26 call). Anup Engineering's order intake and pipeline remain active through the Middle East (Dubai presence, ADNOC orders). HLE Glascoat frames the Middle East as "the next opportunity to come" for its heat-transfer business (HLEGLAS, Jun 2026/Q4 FY26 call). Praj Industries is the outlier: its **domestic** 1G ethanol Greenfield demand is explicitly slowing ("continuing to experience a slowdown in the Greenfield fuel ethanol products," PRAJIND, Jun 2026/Q4 FY26 call), though its international bioenergy pipeline (USA, Latin America) is building. Net: no peer describes an industry-wide demand boom of the scale FABTECH's own narrative implies for MENA/Africa pharma infrastructure specifically; the positive demand commentary that does exist is in adjacent segments (water, heat exchangers, biofuels), not pharma-cleanroom EPC.

**2B Pricing and input costs.** Consistent with Q2 above: all four peers report Middle East-conflict-linked cost or logistics pressure in the same window, supporting FABTECH's margin-compression narrative as directionally real and sector-wide. The one hard number available for comparison (Praj's 3% consolidated RM increase) is far below FABTECH's claimed 33%/43%, which should temper confidence that the full magnitude of FABTECH's margin compression is external and macro-driven rather than partly company-specific.

**2C Capex cycle.** Two of four peers are actively expanding with committed capital into new geography/capacity: Ion Exchange (Rs 450 crore Roha chemical plant, new Dammam Saudi manufacturing plant, Rs 80-100 crore incremental CAPEX ex-Roha) and HLE Glascoat (Omeras acquisition, Aug 2025). The other two are in a holding/optimization posture: Anup shows no major new-capacity announcements in the three calls read, and Praj states plainly it is not adding facilities beyond its existing five ("we are not looking for building up any facility per se," PRAJIND, Jun 2026/Q4 FY26 call), instead focused on absorbing GenX fixed costs. This is a **split, not a uniform capacity race**: two names are underwriting capital-intensive regional localization, two are staying capital-light. FABTECH's SACE JV places it in the capital-committed camp alongside Ion Exchange and HLE Glascoat, not as a lone expander against a cautious peer set.

**2D Competitive mentions.** No peer mentioned FABTECH by name or unmistakable description in any of the 12 transcripts read.

**2E Risks peers discuss that FABTECH does not.** (1) Ion Exchange quantifies and discloses specific shipment deferrals (~Rs 60 crore) tied to the West Asia crisis — FABTECH's calls do not quantify an equivalent shipment/logistics impact despite claiming the same macro driver for cost inflation (IONEXCHANG, Jun 2026/Q4 FY26 call). (2) Praj Industries discloses a specific deferred-order figure (~Rs 300 crore inquiry basket not converted to firm orders) explicitly due to raw-material price uncertainty, i.e., named order attrition tied to the cost environment (PRAJIND, Jun 2026/Q4 FY26 call) — FABTECH's calls describe order-book growth without flagging any comparable deferred/lost-bid impact from the same macro conditions it cites. (3) Praj separately discloses a one-time Rs 3,344 million labor-code-driven gratuity/leave liability impact (PRAJIND, Feb 2026/Q3 FY26 call) — a structural cost-base risk category not raised on any FABTECH call. (4) Ion Exchange openly discusses project-level margin drag from specific legacy/onerous contracts (UP and Sri Lanka projects) with analysts pressing for and receiving granular detail quarter after quarter (IONEXCHANG, Nov 2025/Q2 FY26 call) — a level of project-specific transparency that stands in contrast to FABTECH's total silence on its own auditor-flagged Rs 58.9 Cr >365-day overdue-receivables KAM.

**Single strongest independent confirmation:** All four peers (Anup, HLE Glascoat, Ion Exchange, Praj Industries) independently reference Middle East conflict-driven raw material, freight, or logistics disruption during FY26, corroborating the direction of FABTECH's RMC/execution-cost inflation narrative as a genuine sector-wide phenomenon in this window, even though the specific magnitude FABTECH claims (33%/43%) is not matched by any peer's disclosed figures.

**Overall: the peer set complicates FABTECH's narrative.** It supports the macro cost-pressure explanation directionally (all four peers), and it supports the local-entity/JV response to GCC market access as a legitimate, capital-committed sector adaptation rather than a discretionary one-off (Ion Exchange's Dammam plant and Oman JV run in parallel to FABTECH's SACE structure). But it undercuts two of FABTECH's more self-serving claims: the $30bn/$70bn opportunity figure and the "no listed Indian peer" differentiation claim remain entirely unattributed and untriangulated by any of the four checked names, and the unresolved revenue-recognition status looks more like an outlier than a genuinely hard industry problem once set against peer practice.

## PART 5: CROSS-PEER HYPOTHESIS

Across the four peers, a pattern emerges that none of them states explicitly: **the Indian process-engineering/EPC export sector serving MENA and Africa is bifurcating into two distinct strategic responses to the same regional volatility (Middle East conflict, freight disruption, localization pressure) — capital-intensive regional localization versus capital-light selective bidding.** Ion Exchange (Dammam Saudi manufacturing plant, 51%-owned Oman DBOOT JV) and HLE Glascoat (Omeras acquisition, sustained Saudi tank-delivery track record) are underwriting committed local capital and equity JV structures to secure regional market access and de-risk supply chains. Anup Engineering and Praj Industries, facing the same disruption, are instead managing it through contract-term flexibility (Anup's shift away from 100% fixed-price terms, selective project acceptance) and deferring orders rather than committing new regional capital (Praj's ~Rs 300 crore deferred inquiry basket, no new facility additions). FABTECH's SACE joint venture places it in the capital-intensive localization camp alongside Ion Exchange and HLE Glascoat — a higher-conviction, higher-capital-commitment strategic bet relative to the capital-light path chosen by two of its four comparators. This is testable: if the localization camp begins reporting disproportionately stronger MENA order conversion and margin resilience over the next several quarters relative to the capital-light camp, it would validate the JV-investment thesis; if the capital-light peers match or beat the localization camp's MENA performance without the balance-sheet commitment, it would suggest FABTECH's SACE capital commitment (Rs 24 Cr+) is unnecessary risk rather than necessary adaptation.
