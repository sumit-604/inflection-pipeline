# Poonawalla Fincorp, Q2 FY27: the story in plain words

Sources: the committed review (work/review_poonawalla_q2fy27.md), the two A5 audits, and work/notion-thesis.md. No agent was re-run.

1. **First look, no earlier analysis on record.** There is no Notion page, no Decision Status, no entry zone and no tripwires for this company. So we cannot compare this quarter with any earlier view. This is a first read, not a thesis check.

2. **What the company does.** Poonawalla Fincorp is a lender. It lends to people and small businesses. Its main loans are against property, small fast consumer loans, loans to mid-sized firms, business loans, personal loans, used cars and gold. About half the book has security behind it. The other half does not.

3. **What happened this quarter.** Profit after tax was about 375 crore. That is 22% more than last quarter and about five times the same quarter last year. Last quarter had a one-off gain from selling loans. This quarter had none, so the profit is cleaner than it looks.

4. **Loan growth.** The loan book is about 74,000 crore. It grew 10% in three months and 55% in one year. That is very fast. Management's own target is 35 to 40% a year, and it is running ahead of that.

5. **Asset quality.** The share of bad loans fell to 1.2%. The cost of bad loans fell to 2.19% of the book. But in rupees, the losses did not fall. They stayed flat at about 375 crore. The ratios look better mainly because the book grew bigger. Also, the money set aside against weak loans fell for four quarters in a row. And in this same quarter the company sold about 160 crore of stressed loans to recovery firms. The filing does not say if those loans were already counted as bad.

6. **Margins.** Income grew well, and costs per rupee of income are far lower than a year ago. But this quarter, costs grew a little faster than income. The deck claims "strong operating leverage". This quarter does not show it. Borrowing costs also started to rise. Fees are an important part of profit, and a proposed insurance rule could cut some of them.

7. **Capital.** The company raised 2,500 crore of new equity in April. In one quarter, it used about half the room that raise created. Debt is now 4.3 times equity. Profit alone cannot fund this speed of growth. Expect another raise, or slower growth, within about a year.

8. **What management said, and how believable it is.** Management said low credit cost is "structural" and that profit will not suffer as rates rise. They gave no number for write-offs. They gave no level for their June 2028 return target. They gave no number for the insurance fee risk, after three questions on it. The CEO misstated the credit cost drop on the call. The tone is confident, but the claims about the future are mostly adjectives, not numbers. Treat them as unproven.

9. **Forensic red flags.** First, no document gives write-offs. Second, the money received for written-off loans has no clear line in the profit statement. A separate loss on loan sales also does not tie to its own note. Third, one of the two audit firms left after July, with no reason given. Fourth, an employee trust that the auditors do not review sold about 228 crore of company shares in six months. Fifth, two filings give different dates for the April share sale. Sixth, one table in the debt security annexure does not add up.

10. **Flagged open items.** The final check found four points still open. Two concern the stressed loans sold to recovery firms. The evidence suggests they were already bad loans. If so, bad loans before the sale rose a little in rupees, though the ratio still fell. The other two are wording fixes in the review. None of them changes the verdict.

11. **What this means for the thesis.** There is no thesis yet. The verdict is PROCEED WITH FLAGS. Profits are real and growing fast. The open question is whether low losses are real or only look low because the book is young and growing. The operator decides whether to start a full first study (Step 1) on this company.

12. **What to watch next quarter.** The results in mid January 2027 will settle most of this. Watch losses in rupees, not only the ratio. Watch for a write-off number. Watch whether borrowing costs rise faster than loan rates. Watch for any sign of a new equity raise. If rupee losses grow slower than the book, the bull case gets stronger. If the loss ratio goes back to 2.4% or higher, the bear case wins.
