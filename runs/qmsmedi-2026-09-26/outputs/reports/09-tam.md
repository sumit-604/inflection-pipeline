# Stage 9: TAM / SAM / SOM Market Sizing — QMS Medical Allied Services Ltd (QMSMEDI)
Run date: 2026-09-26 | Model: Sonnet 5 + web search

## SECTION 1: MARKET DEFINITION

### 1A Precise boundaries

QMS runs two structurally different addressable markets. Each needs its own boundary; a single
blended "healthcare" TAM would hide both (consistent with B04's SOTP finding).

**Market A — Products (third-party + own-brand device distribution).**
- Product scope: primary-care diagnostic instruments and point-of-care devices distributed under
  exclusive tie-ups (3M, HEINE Optotechnik, Rossmax), point-of-care consumables, own-brand
  Q-Devices (BP monitors, glucometers), e-commerce (QMSMEDS / Q-Devices online).
  EXCLUDES: imaging equipment, implants, surgical instruments, IVD reagents, hospital capital
  equipment — categories QMS does not touch (B04 revenue-stream list; no line item covers them).
- Geographic scope: India only. No export revenue found in the corpus.
- Customer scope: pharma companies (doctor-engagement/B2B promotion channel) and hospitals/trade
  distribution; retail via e-commerce is a rounding error (0.6% of FY26 revenue, B04).
  EXCLUDES: government tenders, direct-to-consumer retail pharmacy chains (no evidence QMS sells
  through this channel).
- Channel scope: exclusive brand-distribution agreements (structural feature, not incidental — see
  Section 2 Method 2). This is the single most important boundary: QMS is not a generalist device
  wholesaler competing for open-market share, it is a *named-brand exclusive agent*, so its
  addressable market is the sum of the brands it holds or could plausibly sign, not the whole
  Indian device market.
- Price segment: mid-market primary-care instruments, not premium capital equipment.

**Market B — Services (pharma-funded patient engagement).**
- Product scope: B2B health-screening camps (32,380 camps FY26, B04) and Patient Support
  Programmes (PSP) including Saarathi Healthcare (100% owned from Sep-2026) and the proposed
  HCAH-PSP combination (25-Sep-2026 scheme; press release,
  R\inputs\announcements\20260925-Press-Release-QMS_25092026193735_SignedPressReleaseIntimation.txt).
  EXCLUDES: hospital-funded or government-funded patient programmes — every source describes this
  as a pharma-funded, promotional-regulation-driven market.
- Geographic scope: India only. Management's own investor-deck slide cites a "$70B market
  worldwide" for PSPs (Investor_Presentation_1.txt line 808) purely as *context*; QMS discloses no
  revenue from outside India anywhere in the corpus. The India-specific figure from the 25-Sep-2026
  HCAH press release ("India market growing at a CAGR of 15-20% to reach c.US$1 billion by 2030")
  is the only geographically relevant management TAM claim for this segment.
- Customer scope: pharmaceutical companies as payer (patients are the end beneficiary but not the
  revenue source); QMS states relationships with "over 50 leading pharmaceutical and healthcare
  companies" (HEINE press release, 27-Aug-2026).
- Channel scope: chronic-disease-focused programmes (Ophthalmology, Orthopedic, Diabetes,
  Hypertension, Cardiology, Obesity — Investor_Presentation_1.txt line 813-814).

Wrong-definition risk flagged and avoided: management's slide juxtaposes the $70bn *global* PSP
figure with QMS's India-only P&L on the same page (Investor_Presentation_1.txt lines 808-815).
Read uncritically, that pairing implies TAM = SAM for QMS, which the framework calls dishonest.
Section 2 treats the global figure as context only and anchors TAM to the India-specific claim.

## SECTION 2: TAM ESTIMATION, MULTIPLE METHODS

### Method 1 — Top-down

**India medical device market (context/ceiling for Products).** Mordor Intelligence: India medical
devices market USD 18.30bn in 2026, rising to USD 20.23bn per IMARC (WebSearch, 2026-09-26,
current-year estimate, not stale). Take the conservative $18.3bn. At an approximate ₹88/$ FY26/27
rate: Rs 18.3bn × 88 ≈ **Rs 1,61,000 Cr**. This is the entire device market (manufacturing +
import, all channels — hospital direct, OEM tender, retail, distributor). No source found splits
out the third-party-distributor channel share specifically (searched; not found — see Searches
Skipped). Because QMS's model is a *named-exclusive-brand* distributor, not a generalist device
wholesaler, this Rs 1,61,000 Cr figure is held as an unreachable ceiling/context number only — it
is NOT used as the Products TAM headline, per the 1A boundary finding above. Confidence: L (scope
mismatch acknowledged, not resolved).

**India PSP market (used as the Services TAM headline).** Back-solving management's own claim
(India PSP to reach ~US$1bn by 2030 at 15-20% CAGR, 25-Sep-2026 press release) to a 2026 "today"
figure, discounting 4 years (2026→2030) at each end of the stated CAGR band:
- At 20% CAGR (higher rate → lower implied "today" size, the CONSERVATIVE read per the stage's
  bias rule): $1bn ÷ 1.20^4 = $1bn ÷ 2.0736 ≈ **$482mn ≈ Rs 4,240 Cr** (at ₹88/$).
- At 15% CAGR (lower rate → higher implied "today" size, the REALISTIC read): $1bn ÷ 1.15^4 =
  $1bn ÷ 1.749 ≈ **$572mn ≈ Rs 5,030 Cr**.
India PSP TAM (2026): conservative Rs 4,240 Cr, realistic Rs 5,030 Cr. Confidence: M (single
management-sourced claim, not cross-verified by an independent report; dated 25-Sep-2026, fresh).

### Method 2 — Bottom-up

**Products (brand-tie-up model).** QMS's addressable Products market is structurally the sum of
the brands it holds exclusivity for, or could plausibly sign, not the open device market (1A). The
only hard, dated data point disclosed is HEINE: India brand revenue ~Rs 30 Cr (CY2026), management
guides this to ~Rs 70 Cr by 2031 purely from the QMS exclusivity effect (27-Aug-2026 press
release) — an incremental Rs 40 Cr of TAM added by ONE new tie-up over five years. QMS holds two
other named brand relationships (3M, Rossmax) whose India-specific revenue is NOT FOUND anywhere
in the corpus (B04 input gap, carried forward). [INFERENCE, labelled]: if the existing 3M and
Rossmax relationships are of broadly similar scale to HEINE (Rs 20-70 Cr India brand revenue
each — the only anchor available), the *current* named-brand portfolio addressable market sits
roughly in the **Rs 100-250 Cr** band. QMS's own disclosed FY26 Products revenue is already
Rs 119 Cr (68.9% of Rs 172.9 Cr, B00 LBF1/B04). That means QMS may already be capturing a large
share — plausibly most — of the India revenue of the specific brands it holds exclusivity for. This
is the single most important structural finding of this stage: for an exclusive-tie-up business,
TAM and SOM converge for each individual brand by design; further Products growth is a function of
(a) organic growth of existing tied-brand India sales (~10-12%, per B05's reaffirmed guide) and
(b) SIGNING NEW brands (the actual expansion lever, see Section 5D), not classic market-share
gain within an open market. Conservative Products bottom-up TAM: Rs 250 Cr (near-term, current +
one HEINE-scale addition). Realistic: Rs 400 Cr (current portfolio plus 1-2 further HEINE-scale
signings over the SOM horizon). Confidence: M (anchored to one hard number, extrapolated to an
unverified portfolio).

**Services (unit-economics check).** QMS ran 32,380 camps in FY26 for Rs 21-23 Cr of camps revenue
(figures diverge Rs 21.2 Cr per B04's derived per-camp math vs Rs 22.9 Cr per B00 LBF1 — the same
unreconciled camps-revenue issue B05 already flagged as a HIGH red flag; this stage does not
attempt to resolve it, only notes it propagates here too). PSP revenue (incl. Saarathi) was
Rs 30.0 Cr FY26 (B00 LBF1). Combined Services revenue Rs 53.9 Cr sits against the Rs 1,613 Cr
"Patient Programs, Screening Camps" 2024 pharma-promotional-spend slice (Section 1B) — implying a
~3.3% share of that pool already, BEFORE the Saarathi/HCAH scale-up. This bottom-up cross-check is
used only as a sense-check on Method 1's Services TAM, not as an independent headline figure,
because the Rs 1,613 Cr slice and the India-PSP-market figure are differently scoped (see
Triangulation table divergence note below).

### Method 3 — Peer revenue aggregation (context only; scope mismatch flagged, not averaged)

Three peer FY26 revenues (WebSearch, 2026-09-26): Entero Healthcare Solutions (pharma wholesale
distribution, much broader scope than device distribution) Rs 6,591 Cr; Poly Medicure (device
*manufacturer*, largely direct/export) Rs 1,875 Cr; Indegene (digital commercialization/CRO
services for global pharma, not physical camps or device distribution) Rs 3,510 Cr. None of the
three peers mirrors QMS's specific hybrid (exclusive-brand device distribution + pharma-funded
patient camps/PSP). Summing them (Rs 11,976 Cr) would materially overstate QMS's addressable
market because each peer's revenue includes large categories QMS does not touch (full-line pharma
wholesale, device manufacturing, digital/CRO services). This method is retained only as a scale
sanity check: it confirms organized players in *adjacent-but-different* segments already operate
at 10-40x QMS's current revenue, so headroom is not implausible on its face, but it cannot be
averaged into the TAM number without misrepresenting scope. Confidence: L, context only.

### Method 4 — Import substitution: NOT APPLICABLE

QMS is a distributor/agent, not a domestic manufacturer competing against imports; import
substitution dynamics (consumption vs domestic production vs imports, government targets) do not
map onto its exclusive-distribution-agent business model. Marked not applicable rather than forced.

not usable.

## SECTION 3: SAM & SOM

### 3A SAM

Because the TAM in Section 2 was already scoped narrowly to QMS's actual business model (India-
only, pharma-funded, chronic-disease-focused, exclusive-brand distribution — see 1A), most of the
five standard filters were already applied during TAM definition rather than left for this step.
Stating that plainly rather than forcing an artificial second round of cuts that would misrepresent
a genuinely narrow-niche business:

- Product/service fit: Products TAM is already brand-portfolio-scoped (Method 2); no further cut.
  Services TAM (India PSP, all disease areas) is filtered to the chronic-disease categories QMS
  already targets (Ophthalmology, Orthopedic, Diabetes, Hypertension, Cardiology, Obesity —
  Investor_Presentation_1.txt lines 813-814). [INFERENCE, no source splits India PSP spend by
  disease category]: chronic-disease programmes dominate pharma PSP spend generally (the entire
  premise of both QMS's and its named peers' PSP commentary, B06), estimated at ~70-75% of the
  India PSP pool. Applying 70% (conservative) to Rs 4,240 Cr and 75% (realistic) to Rs 5,030 Cr:
  Services SAM = Rs 2,968 Cr conservative, Rs 3,773 Cr realistic.
- Geography: already India-only in both TAM figures; no further cut.
- Channel fit: Products already scoped to the exclusive-tie-up channel; no further cut. Services
  already scoped to pharma-funded programmes (not hospital/government-funded); no further cut.
- Customer fit: QMS discloses "over 50 leading pharmaceutical and healthcare companies" as existing
  partners (HEINE press release). Total addressable pharma companies actively running India PSP or
  device-promotion budgets (top-20 MNC plus a meaningful slice of the top-100 domestic majors) is
  NOT FOUND as a precise count anywhere in the corpus or in web search; treated as already broadly
  captured within the disease-category filter above rather than double-counted.
- Capability: QMS's current infrastructure (150-seat call centre per B04's unit-economics note;
  1,800+ field officers, 1,000+ dieticians, 135 certified DMLTs per Investor_Presentation_1.txt
  line 801) sets a real near-term execution ceiling. No further TAM cut applied here; capacity is
  addressed as a check on SOM feasibility in 3C instead.

**SAM (combined) = Products Rs 250-400 Cr + Services Rs 2,968-3,773 Cr = Rs 3,218 Cr conservative,
Rs 4,173 Cr realistic.** SAM as % of TAM ≈ Rs 3,218 / Rs 4,490 ≈ **72%** (conservative basis) — a
high ratio versus the "SAM should be materially smaller than TAM" norm, explained by the 1A finding
that TAM itself was already scoped tightly to a genuinely narrow, structurally exclusive business.

### 3B SOM at 3 and 5 years

Current SAM share = FY26 total revenue (Rs 172.9 Cr) ÷ SAM (Rs 3,218 Cr, conservative basis) =
**5.4%**.

Share-gain trajectory: base case uses the framework's "1-2pp in 3 years, normal" band; QMS's own
documented execution issues (B05 credibility grade C: unacknowledged Q-Devices miss, three
straight quarters of margin-guidance slippage, twice-restated camps revenue) argue against the
aggressive 3-5pp band on the ORGANIC side. But the 25-Sep-2026 HCAH scheme is an explicit
competitor-consolidation/acquisition event, which the framework's own rule allows to justify
>5pp gains "on competitor exit or acquisition" — that upside is treated qualitatively as a scenario
modifier below the base-case arithmetic, not folded into the headline number, because the scheme's
own completion date is not yet stated (B07 input gap; only the linked BeamOptics indicative date,
30-Nov-2027, exists).

**Base case (organic, 2pp/3yr and 4pp/5yr):**
- SAM growth: blended TAM growth ~13% (Section 5B calculation) applied to the Rs 3,218 Cr SAM base.
- 3-year SAM = Rs 3,218 Cr × 1.13³ ≈ Rs 4,646 Cr. Share = 5.4% + 2pp = 7.4%.
  **SOM (3yr) = 7.4% × Rs 4,646 Cr ≈ Rs 344 Cr.**
  Implied 3-year revenue CAGR from FY26's Rs 172.9 Cr: (344/172.9)^(1/3) - 1 ≈ **25.3%**.
- 5-year SAM = Rs 3,218 Cr × 1.13⁵ ≈ Rs 5,927 Cr. Share = 5.4% + 4pp = 9.4%.
  **SOM (5yr) = 9.4% × Rs 5,927 Cr ≈ Rs 557 Cr.**
  Implied 5-year revenue CAGR: (557/172.9)^(1/5) - 1 ≈ **26.2%**.

Both horizons cluster around 25-26% CAGR — internally consistent, and it lands almost exactly on
the strategy's 25% CAGR hurdle. This SOM path sits BELOW management's own most aggressive public
target (Rs 500 Cr by FY29 implies a 3-year CAGR of ~42.6% from the FY26 base — a materially faster
climb than a normal 1-2pp/3yr share-gain path can support without either (a) the HCAH-scheme
acquisition delivering an outsized step-change in Services share in year one or two, or (b) the
India PSP market itself growing meaningfully faster than the 15-20% management guides). This
divergence — SOM-implied ~25-26% vs management's implied ~43% — is the single most load-bearing
number this stage hands to Stage 11 and to the FTTCP deliberation: it names the gap between an
evidenced, share-gain-arithmetic growth path and management's own stated ambition, without
resolving which is right.

### 3C Capacity cross-check

B07's capex-embedded-growth figure is 0% / NOT FOUND — there is no capex programme at all; QMS is
asset-light (B07 confirms this is a genuine finding, not an extraction gap). A ₹-Cr capacity gap
against a capex plan therefore cannot be computed, because there is no capex plan to compare
against. The relevant capacity constraint instead runs through headcount, and the evidence already
in hand shows headcount spend is ALREADY running ahead of revenue: consolidated employee cost rose
3.5x YoY (Rs 3.2 Cr to Rs 11.2 Cr) in Q1 FY27 while revenue grew only 1.22x (B04's FLAG-COST). Read
against the SOM buildout, this is not a hard capacity ceiling (asset-light labour models can scale
via further hiring) but it is the visible mechanism translating SOM ambition into margin pressure —
the same mechanism B04 and B05 already flag as a live monitorable. Capacity verdict: **sufficient
in principle (no physical/asset ceiling), but the cost side of that capacity build is running ahead
of realized revenue right now, which is the optimistic side of the plan, not the SOM side.**

### 4C Market structure

- Competitor count: no named public competitor exists in any QMS filed document (B04 input gap,
  confirmed absent). Peers used for cross-read (INDGN, ENTERO, POLYMED) are adjacent, not direct,
  competitors (Method 3 above). HCAH is the one named direct competitor/partner in the PSP space,
  now being absorbed via the Sep-2026 scheme rather than competed against.

  Consolidating: the HCAH-QMS scheme is itself direct evidence of active consolidation.

  CRM/technology plus scale (Services), not on price — consistent with weak pricing power flagged

- Entries/exits: HCAH's absorption into Saarathi (an exit-by-merger from HCAH's independent PSP

- Import share trend: not directly disclosed for the specific device categories QMS distributes; the
  broader Indian medical-device market imports a majority of high-value devices (general industry
  knowledge, not independently re-sourced here given time constraints — treat as directional only).

### 5B Runway assessment

- Revenue headroom = SAM ÷ current revenue = Rs 3,218 Cr ÷ Rs 172.9 Cr ≈ **18.6x**.
- TAM growth rate (blended, revenue-mix-weighted: 69% Products at management's reaffirmed 10-12%
  guide + 31% Services at management's own 15-20% India-PSP CAGR claim) ≈ 0.69×11% + 0.31×17.5% ≈
  **13.0%**.
- Company CAGR vs TAM: QMS's FY23-FY26 disclosed revenue CAGR is 18% (Investor_Presentation_1.txt
  line 1024-1030), already running ahead of the 13% blended TAM growth rate — i.e. QMS has been
  GAINING SHARE, not merely riding the market, over the last three years. That lends some credibility
  to a continued share-gain path, tempered by B05's credibility grade C on forward guidance
  specifically (past delivery on revenue has been more reliable than past delivery on margin).
- Years to saturate SAM at the current ~18% growth rate: ln(3,218/172.9) / ln(1.18) ≈ ln(18.6) /
  ln(1.18) ≈ 2.92 / 0.166 ≈ **~18 years** — a long runway on the conservative SAM basis.

### 5C Runway classification

**STRONG.** Not classified MASSIVE because the Products side of this business is structurally
capped by the exclusive-brand-tie-up model (Section 2 Method 2 finding) rather than open-ended, and
because the Services TAM rests on a single management-sourced CAGR claim (M confidence, not
independently cross-verified) rather than a published third-party market report. But an 18.6x SAM
headroom, a market growing faster than most Indian small/micro-cap end-markets (~13% blended), and
a company that has out-grown that blended rate over three years together clear the STRONG bar
comfortably above GOOD/MODERATE.

## Analyst note

The most important finding this stage adds is structural, not numerical: QMS's Products business
is an exclusive-brand-agency model where TAM and SOM converge brand-by-brand, so its real growth
lever is signing new exclusivities (HEINE being the one disclosed, dated example), not gaining share
in an open device market. Layered onto that, the Services SAM/SOM arithmetic lands almost exactly on
the strategy's 25% CAGR hurdle using a normal, non-aggressive share-gain path — comfortably below
management's own Rs 500 Cr/FY29 target (~43% implied CAGR). That gap is not a red flag on its own
(the HCAH scheme is a genuine, named acquisition-driven consolidation event that the framework's own
share-gain rules would allow to justify a faster path), but it does mean Stage 11 and FTTCP should
treat the Rs 500 Cr/FY29 figure as management's upper-bound ambition, not as the base case, when
setting revenue-growth assumptions. The Rs 1,613 Cr (2024, stale) vs implied Rs 4,240-5,030 Cr
(2026) divergence in Services TAM readings is scope, not error, but it means the Services TAM number
carries real uncertainty band width that Stage 11 should treat as M, not H, confidence.
