# Business Understanding Narrative

Modison makes electrical contacts and contact materials from silver, copper and tungsten. The contact is the small metal part inside a switch that touches to complete a circuit and separates to break it. Every low, medium and high voltage switchgear unit, circuit breaker and vacuum interrupter needs these parts to switch power and to protect the line. A switchgear maker cannot ship a breaker without them. Modison reports one segment, split about 88% domestic and about 12% export. Both sell business to business on contracts that pass metal prices through to the buyer.

The customers are original equipment makers of switchgear. Named anchor buyers are GE, Siemens, ABB, L&T, GM Modular and BHEL. A buyer designs a contact into a product and type approves it, which raises the cost of moving to another supplier once a part is qualified. The company names customer concentration in a limited set of OEMs as a formal risk. It does not disclose top five or top ten customer share.

Present demand comes from those OEMs buying to fill their own order books, so their capex and order intake drive Modison's orders. Reported revenue also moves with silver, copper and tungsten prices, since metal is about 87% of the cost passed through. India transmission and distribution capex under the CEA National Electricity Plan is the largest structural pull for high and medium voltage switchgear parts.

Demand should grow with several external programmes. The CEA transmission plan carries a Rs 7.93 lakh crore programme. The MNRE 500 GW renewable target needs grid switchgear for integration. India data centre buildout, tracked by JLL and CBRE, pulls low voltage switchgear and contacts. An ISRO import substitution recognition points at a possible government and defence pipeline. Each links to a signal a reader can check outside the company.

Competitive advantage is thin and unproven on filed evidence. The high voltage niche carries a self claimed sole India manufacturer position, but the company never discloses segment revenue, so the claim cannot be checked. Switching costs from OEM design in are moderate and real. The cost advantage claim from backward integration does not hold: the refining affiliate MCPL is a related party, not a captive plant on Modison's books, and it depends on Modison for about 49% of its own sales. R&D spend fell about 24% in the record year. The emerging moat scan scored 8 of 92 and found no forming moat. No business line shows a proven moat on the filed record.

# Record margin meets negative cash flow

FY26 revenue was Rs 710 cr, up 44.9%. PAT was Rs 72.55 cr and EBITDA was Rs 115 cr, a 16.1% margin. Metal is about 87% of revenue, so gross margin moves with silver, copper and tungsten prices, not with company value add. Domestic sales are about 88% of the total, exports about 12%. The Modi family founded the business and runs it; the shares have been listed since 1983.

The transition claim is a margin recovery. EBITDA margin climbed from a FY22-23 trough of 6.95% to 8.33% back to a record 16.1% in FY26. That record sits at the top of a 10 year band of 6.95% to 16.29%, so it reads as cycle peak, not new normal. Management frames a climb from commodity converter toward a value added supplier. Its own FY27 guidance is "at least 10-12%," a step down from 16%.

Gate 0 scored the company AVERAGE, 66 of 160. The cash block scored 0 of 20, a deal breaker. Growth and near term balance sheet ratios score well in isolation. That split is the signature of growth funded by debt before the strain shows up in coverage or gearing.

Operating cash flow tells the cash story. It ran Rs +1.24 cr in FY24, then Rs -16.10 cr in FY25, then Rs -64.18 cr in FY26, on the filed annual report basis. On the screener basis FY24 reads Rs -3.89 cr, so the negative run is two years on the filed accounts and three on screener. Receivables rose 83.8% and inventory rose 72.4%, both faster than revenue at 44.9%. Borrowings roughly doubled to Rs 174 cr. A Rs 14.60 cr dividend was paid in the year operating cash flow was negative. The working capital swing consumed all of operating profit and more.

Management credibility grades C. The dependency of the affiliate MCPL was stated at about 25% at the AGM but reads about 48.76% in the annual report, and the two are unreconciled. The Vapi fire was downplayed at the AGM against a Rs 10.63 cr booked loss and a Rs 16.24 cr claim that insurers have not admitted. The low voltage market share claim doubled from 16-17% to 30-35% with no explanation. The MD&A claims "robust operational cash flows" in the same table that discloses the Rs -64.18 cr figure. Against that, delivery on headline revenue and margin targets was real: FY25 revenue landed at about 98% of the 2022 target, and the 2030 revenue target was beaten and then raised.

Peers cut both ways, and one earlier read needs correcting. SBCL, the closer silver contacts peer, expanded EBITDA margin about 250bps to 22.9% and calls silver margin neutral, which supports Modison's record margin being plausible. Salzer alone compressed, from about 12% to 7.5-8%, and called the pass through lag "an industry-wide challenge," so the peer contradiction is Salzer only, not both peers. Two quality of earnings caveats sit under the record: the FY26 margin is substantially a Q4 catch up from retroactive tungsten price approvals, and there was a Rs 9.51 cr silver hedging loss in Q4. Both peers also showed working capital deteriorating in the same silver cycle, the opposite of Modison's claimed turnover gain.

The market runway grades GOOD. The conservative TAM is about Rs 2,100 cr, SAM about Rs 2,920 cr, and revenue headroom about 4 times. That runway rests on low quality report mill data; same year global estimates for the category span 1.7 times. The SOM implied revenue CAGR of about 9.2% to 9.5% is roughly half management's implied 17.6% needed to reach the Rs 1,360 cr FY30 target. FY26 capex was only Rs 15.10 cr, so no disclosed capacity stands behind that target.
