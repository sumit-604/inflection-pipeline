## Business Understanding Narrative

**What does it do, and how does it make money?** Rolex Rings hot-forges steel
into rings and gear blanks, then machines them. It sells bearing rings to the
big bearing makers and transmission and chassis parts to global auto Tier 1s
(Q1 deck, page 10). Three plants in Rajkot hold 26 forging lines, 1,65,000 MTPA
of capacity and 625 CNC spindles (Q1 deck, page 11). About 85% of revenue is
machined parts (Call, page 8). The money is conversion margin on steel, with
quarterly steel pass-through to most overseas customers (Call, page 7).

**What is changing?** Three things at once. The legacy debt is closed: a
Rs 101 crore Right of Recompense payment to the old CDR lenders was made on
31-Mar-2026 (Call, page 3). Cash is going back to shareholders: a Rs 180 crore
buyback closed in Q1 FY27 without promoter participation (Q1 deck, page 4).
And the customer base is shifting toward European auto programmes (Q4 deck,
page 15).

**Why now?** US tariffs knocked about 30% off US exports in FY26 (Call, page
4). Management says US orders came back from February 2026 and guides FY27
revenue growth of 15% to 17% (Call, pages 9 and 18).

**What must be true?** Revenue must finally grow. It was Rs 1,017 crore in
FY22 and Rs 1,143 crore in FY26 (Q1 deck, page 20). Four years of near-flat
sales must turn into mid-teens growth.

**What breaks it?** The agency does not believe the guide. Ind-Ra expects 7%
to 9% growth in FY27 and EBITDA margin of 19% to 20% (Ind-Ra, page 3).
Management guides 15% to 17% and at least 20.5% to 21%. The gap between those
two views is the whole forward case.

## 2. Business model and archetype

FY26 components revenue was Rs 1,060 crore: domestic bearing rings 386,
domestic auto 170, export bearing 154, export auto 350. Scrap added 71 and
export incentives 13, for Rs 1,143 crore (Call, page 7, reader summary; it
reconciles). End-markets in FY26: passenger vehicles 52.1%, CV and HCV 21.9%,
industrial 18.3%, BEV and hybrid 7.7% (Q1 deck, page 21).

The engine is steady conversion margin on a large, under-used asset base.
Capacity use is 62% to 63% (Call, page 8). Programmes run five to seven years
once awarded, and new customer approval takes 15 to 18 months (Call, page 10).

**Archetype: build-to-spec component maker.** Customer capex and programme
wins, content per unit, and steel pass-through are the variables.

## 3. Competitive advantages

Present. Seven of the top ten customers have bought from Rolex for more than
15 years (Q1 deck, page 14). The CFO puts its share at 45% to 50% of the
wallet of the three big bearing makers and 35% to 40% with some auto Tier 1s
(Call, page 19). He claims about 30% of a Rs 1,600 to 1,800 crore domestic
hot-forged bearing-ring market (Call, page 8). He also claims no peer has more
than 10% to 20% of its forging and machining capability (Call, page 17). These
are issuer claims.

The moat is qualification and scale: long approval cycles and 26 forging
lines. It is not pricing power. Gross margin sits in a 49% to 53% band by
management's own account (Call, page 16).

## 4. The promoters

The Madeka family. Promoter and promoter group held 52.24% before the buyback.
On full acceptance it "may increase to 54.23%" (Letter of Offer, page 25).
Directors Manesh Madeka (9.54%), Bhautik Madeka (8.70%) and Mihir Madeka (1.64%)
hold 19.88% between them (Postal ballot notice, page 21). Ind-Ra names five
Madeka brothers as promoters (Ind-Ra, page 5). Manesh Madeka founded the
business in 1987 (Q1 deck, page 16).

Conduct signal: the promoters stayed out of the Rs 180 crore buyback (Letter
of Offer, page 20). They also carried the company through CDR from 2013 and
paid the lenders' recompense in full (Call, page 3). Pledge NOT FOUND.

## 6. The transition and the quality ladder

FROM R3, a spec'd supplier of bearing rings with long qualification and
sticky wallet share. TO a higher R3 with more machined auto content and
European programmes. The claim is a mix shift within R3, not a rung climb.

Management's claim, quoted once: "this shift in business mix will be a driver
of margin expansion going forward" (Q4 deck, page 15).

The evidence cuts against a climb. ROCE fell from 30.7% in FY23 to 18.0% in
FY26 (Q1 deck, page 20). Part of that is idle cash, part is flat revenue on a
fixed asset base. EBITDA margin has slipped every year from 22.7% in FY22 to
20.1% in FY26. **Ladder reading: R3, holding, not climbing.** The case is a
recovery and capital-return case, not a transition case.

## 8. Proof check

The triggers claim a US recovery and new programmes from Q1 FY27. Q1 delivered
revenue up 4% (Q1 deck, page 6). Bearing rings fell 6%. Auto components rose
13% (Q1 deck, page 5). Management blames a labour shortage, not demand (Q1
deck, page 4).

The margin proof fired: gross margin 55.6% and EBITDA margin 22.6%. The
revenue proof did not. To reach the low end of the guide, revenue in Q2 to Q4
must rise 18.6% over the same nine months of FY26. **Name the proof point:**
Q2 FY27 revenue growth in the mid-teens or better, due by November 2026.

## 9. Flags from the documents

- **Management's own numbers contradict each other.** The CMD letter says
  "We delivered 15% revenue growth YoY" for FY26 (Q4 deck, page 15). Revenue
  fell 1.0% (Q4 deck, page 18). The CFO says domestic revenue "grew by 50%"
  (Call, page 4). The segment split gives +9.9%. Treat management's
  growth language with care. This matters because the FY27 guide is also
  management's.
- **Other deck contradictions:** FY26 exports 56% (Q4 deck, page 17) against
  44% (Q1 deck, page 21; Ind-Ra, page 4). FY26 ROCE 20.1% against 18.0%.
  Working-capital days 195, 158 and 188 in three documents. A net-debt table
  showing Rs 367 crore gross debt in a company that says it is debt-free (Q1
  deck, page 20).
- **Customer concentration.** Top five groups 61% of FY26 revenue (Ind-Ra,
  page 4). The CFO says 65% to 70% (Call, page 19). One US customer's plant
  shutdown caused 75% of the US loss (Call, page 18).
- **US tariff exposure.** US is 21% of revenue. Section 232 duty stays at 25%
  (Call, page 12; Ind-Ra, page 4).
- **Other income dependence.** Rs 50.3 crore in FY26, 20.8% of profit before
  exceptional items (Q1 deck, page 22). The buyback cuts the cash base behind it.
- One-off items in FY26: Rs 22 crore of US customs duty, half still to be
  recovered, and Rs 6 crore of RoR legal cost (Call, page 5).

## 10. Independent check: the credit rating

**COMPLETE.** India Ratings upgraded the bank facilities to **IND A/Stable/
IND A1** from IND A-/Positive, rated limits cut to Rs 104 crore from Rs 189.4
crore (Ind-Ra, pages 1 and 2). Reasons: stable FY26 revenue and EBITDA despite
tariffs, full RoR settlement, net cash, interest cover of 160x (Ind-Ra, pages 2
and 3). Constraints: "subdued revenue growth in FY25-FY26" and an elongated
working-capital cycle of 188 days (Ind-Ra, pages 2 and 3). Cash and
equivalents including marketable investments were Rs 357.6 crore at FYE26
(Ind-Ra, page 4).

The agency's forward view is the useful part. It expects **7% to 9% revenue
growth in FY27 and EBITDA margin of 19% to 20%** (Ind-Ra, page 3). That is
roughly half the management guide.

## 11. Posture: the Transition Decision Matrix

- **Proof gate: NOT FIRED.** Revenue grew 4% in the quarter that was meant to
  start the recovery. Margin fired; the top line did not.
- **Ugliness: ARTIFACT, contested.** US tariffs and a labour shortage are
  dated and external. But flat revenue since FY23 predates both, and Ind-Ra
  names it as a constraint.
- **Recognition gap: CLOSED.** Price basis, not a corpus document: Bull AI
  company record, market capitalisation Rs 5,124.68 crore, read 2026-09-28.
  That is 26.6x FY26 adjusted PAT and 21.3x to 23.1x the FY27 estimate below.
  The R3 neighbourhood is about 19x.

**Posture: PRICED NARRATIVE.** The recovery is priced before it prints.

## 12. Verdict card

**WATCH.** Hard watch, no `/step1` yet.

This is a sound business with a clean balance sheet, a fresh rating upgrade,
steady cash and promoters who stood aside in a buyback. It is not a
transition. Revenue has not grown in four years. The market already pays
above the R3 neighbourhood for a recovery that one quarter did not show.

**Load-bearing facts a `/step1` must verify first**
1. Q2 FY27 revenue growth against Q2 FY26. Management needs about 18.6% in
   Q2 to Q4 to hit the low end of its guide.
2. Other income after the buyback. Q1 carried Rs 19.8 crore.
3. The rupee value of the US tariff refund and when it hits revenue.
4. Ramp of the Mexico and US plant programmes, in rupees.
5. Final post-buyback promoter holding, and any pledge.

**What would change the view**
- Q2 and Q3 FY27 revenue growth of 15% or more with EBITDA margin above 21%
  moves the proof gate to FIRED and the posture to EARNINGS-ONLY.
- Q2 growth under 9%, inside Ind-Ra's view, moves this to PASS.

No Role 1 valuation was run and no target price is set. This verdict decides
only whether the name earns a full `/step1`. It is not a position.

**Read the adjusted growth, not the reported.** Reported FY26 PAT carries
Rs 51.6 crore of RoR exceptionals. The +57% to +71% line compares a clean
year with a one-off year.

**The agency case.** Ind-Ra's 7% to 9% growth gives revenue of Rs 1,223.5 to
1,246.4 crore. At the same 16.9% margin, PAT is Rs 206.8 to 210.6 crore and
the forward PE is 24.3x to 24.8x.

**Temper the run-rate.** Q1 PAT carried Rs 19.8 crore of other income on a
cash base the buyback then cut by Rs 180 crore. Q1 was also labour-hit on
revenue. The two effects pull in opposite directions. No seasonality warning
was filed. The high case of Rs 264.9 crore (guided top end at Q1's 19.8% PAT
margin, 19.3x) needs both Q1's margin and the full guide, and is not the
central row.
