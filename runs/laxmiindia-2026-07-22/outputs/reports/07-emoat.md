## SECTION 2: CAPACITY & CAPEX PIPELINE

A conventional plant-capex table does not apply to an NBFC — growth is
balance-sheet-funded (loan book), not fixed-asset-funded. The capacity analogue is
branch network and capital-adequacy headroom, both addressed under Family B/E/G
below rather than force-fit into a capex-turnover framework (per Section 3 rule 5:
never force-fit).

**2A (branch-network proxy):**
| Metric | FY23 | FY24 | FY25 | FY26 | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Branches | 119 | 135 | 158 | 176 | DRHP p.185/198; Inv. Pres. slide 7 |
| States | 5 | 5 | 5 | 6 (added Maharashtra) | Inv. Pres. slide 22 |
| AUM per branch (₹mn) | 57.7 | 71.2 | 80.8 | ~92.4 (implied, ₹1,626.26cr/176) | DRHP p.187 (FY23-25 audited); FY26 own calc |

**2B utilisation trajectory**: AUM per branch rose every year (57.7→71.2→80.8→~92.4,
₹mn), i.e., branch productivity is compounding faster than branch count — branches
added in FY22-24 are still ramping. This is the honest positive reading of the
"execution moat" question (see F2), even though headline branch-addition guidance
was missed in FY26 (see Section 6B).

**2C growth-embedded-in-capex arithmetic**: not computable in the standard
fixed-asset-turnover sense (NOT APPLICABLE for a lender — no capex program funds
future revenue the way plant capex does). The closest analogue, CRAR headroom
(26.12% FY26 vs 15% regulatory minimum, Inv. Pres. slide 10), signals capital
capacity for roughly another leverage turn of balance-sheet growth before requiring
fresh equity, but translating that into a %-above-current-revenue figure would
require assumptions not disclosed anywhere in the injected documents — left as
NOT FOUND rather than estimated.

**2D new geography/market entries**: covered in 1B/E1 below (Maharashtra FY26; one
state per year cadence).

## SECTION 3: THE 20-CATEGORY SCAN

### FAMILY A — Product & Technology (adapted: loan-product & tech-stack for a lender)

**A1 (rare "manufacturing" capability)**: NOT APPLICABLE — no manufacturing base. NO EVIDENCE FOUND.

**A2 (patent/IP pipeline)**: only a registered trademark/logo under Class 36 and the
lifc.co.in domain (DRHP p.209). No product-IP pipeline. NO EVIDENCE FOUND.

**A3 (process innovation)**: 📄 Documented. Digitised LOS/LMS/CRM, cash-flow
underwriting for non-income-proof customers, geo-tagging, TAT of 24-48 hours (CV
loans) and 7-10 days (MSME) (Inv. Pres. slide 24); collection efficiency 98.92% /
96.69% / 96.76% in FY23/24/25 — a KPI independently verified per the DRHP's KPI
certification process (DRHP p.186, p.187 KPI verification note). Strength:
MODERATE-STRONG. Time to materialise: already active, compounding over 12-24m.

**A4 (product platform/modular architecture)**: 📄 Documented. Product count grew
from the founding MSME/vehicle/construction core to 9 named products (Inv. Pres.
slide 19), including EV loans (FY24) and wholesale lending (FY25) added without a
step-change in headcount (sales headcount 678 of 1,470 total, DRHP p.209).
Strength: MODERATE. Time to materialise: 12-24m for wholesale/EV to become
AUM-meaningful.

### FAMILY B — Supply chain (adapted: liability-side / funding-source structure)

**B1 (backward integration / RM security → funding-source diversification and
cost)**: 📄 Documented, STRONG. Lenders grew from 47 (8 PSU banks, 10 private
banks, 7 SFBs, 22 NBFC/FIs — DRHP p.189) to 50+ (Inv. Pres. slide 42-44, 5 new
lenders onboarded FY26: South Indian Bank, Karur Vysya, IndusInd, Credit Saison,
PFS, WINT). Average tenure 4+ years with top-5 lenders, 80% repeat-funding rate,
+7.20% YoY credit-limit increase with top-5 lenders (DRHP p.189). Cost of borrowing
fell every year: 12.42%→11.98%→11.48%→10.80% FY23-26 (Inv. Pres. slide 8; DRHP's
own KPI computation shows a related but not identical series — 12.24%/12.06%/12.02%
FY23-25, DRHP p.190 — the two differ by methodology/averaging convention, both
directionally consistent). This is the single most credible emerging moat in the
scan: for an NBFC, funding-cost advantage is the closest analogue to a durable
structural edge.

**B2 (qualification lock-in)**: the closest analogue (lender-relationship tenure)
is already captured in B1 to avoid double-crediting the same fact through two
mechanisms. NO EVIDENCE FOUND as a distinct category.

**B3 (supply-chain/two-sided network effect)**: 🎙️ Management claim, WEAK. The
PSL-eligible book (>80% of MSME loans qualify as Priority Sector Lending under RBI
guidelines, DRHP p.184-185) lets the company sell/assign portfolios to banks that
need PSL compliance — a genuine two-sided value flow (banks get PSL credit, LIFL
gets cheap, capital-light funding), discussed in the Q3 FY26 and Q4 FY26 calls, but
not separately quantified as a stand-alone disclosed metric. See R1, which captures
the regulatory dimension of the same mechanism — flagged as the same lever, not
double-counted.

### FAMILY C — Customer

**C1 (customer ecosystem / cross-sell)**: 🎙️ Management claim, WEAK. Cross-sell is
explicit Strategy #2 in the DRHP (p.194: "leveraging our existing branch and
network of customers... data-driven insights... advanced analytics"), but the
disclosed repeat-customer metric is thin and non-monotonic: repeat-customer share
of MSME disbursements was 9.31% (FY23) → 8.24% (FY24, a dip) → 10.44% (FY25) (DRHP
p.196). The "advanced analytics" capability itself is asserted, not evidenced by
any disclosed model, tool, or lift metric.

**C2 (customer-concentration improving)**: 📄 Documented, MODERATE. Top-10 DSA

customer base grew 16,851→23,906→35,568→42,809 (FY23-26, Inv. Pres. slide 7) with
new-geography diversification (5→6 states). Distribution-channel concentration is
genuinely declining.

### FAMILY D — Data & Digital

**D1 (proprietary data asset)**: 🔍 Analyst inference, WEAK. ~30 years of
cash-flow-underwriting history (legacy to 1993 DFL), CIBIL/CRIF/bureau
integrations (Inv. Pres. slide 26), but the LOS/LMS is third-party (Synofin,
"partnered with a third-party vendor," DRHP p.209) and no disclosed proprietary ML
model, in-house scorecard performance, or replicability assessment exists. The data
asset is asserted ("advanced analytics," DRHP p.195) but not evidenced.

**D2 (digital platform)**: 📄 Documented, but the trend argues against crediting
this as a strengthening moat. Digital-channel share of disbursement by value
*declined* from 1.70% (FY24) to 0.94% (FY25) (DRHP p.192, "Digital channels" row),
even as management markets a "digital-first" identity (Inv. Pres. slide 19: "Why
Us? — Digital-First Operations"). Branch/DSA sourcing remains >99% of disbursement
volume. The Laxmi Mitra referral app exists and reduces acquisition cost per
management (DRHP p.186), but it sits inside an overwhelmingly branch-led
distribution model, not a scaling digital platform. Strength: WEAK, flagged as a
countervailing data point against the "digital-first" framing repeated in investor
materials.

## SECTION 4: REGULATORY & POLICY TAILWINDS (R1)

**4A regulatory approvals in pipeline**: none disclosed. LIFL operates as an
existing RBI-registered NBFC ("NBFC – Middle Layer" under the Scale Based
Regulation framework, DRHP p.206); no new licence, PLI-type scheme, or fresh
regulatory approval is pending. NO EVIDENCE FOUND for a new regulatory unlock.

**4B government policy tailwinds**: the operative tailwind is >80% of MSME loans
qualifying as Priority Sector Lending under RBI guidelines (DRHP p.184-185). This
is not new (it has applied since the company's MSME book existed) but management's
active *use* of it — selling/assigning PSL-eligible pools to banks via direct
assignment/co-lending, discussed at length in the Q3 FY26 and Q4 FY26 calls, and
evidenced by ₹27.93cr sold to ARC/assignment structures in FY26 per the stage-wise
loan-movement table (Inv. Pres. slide 12) — is scaling. No PLI, tax incentive, or
import-substitution scheme applies to an NBFC. Competitors (SBFC Finance, Akme
Fintrade, Ugro Capital, Moneyboxx Finance, Five Star Business Finance, MAS
Financial, all named as primary competitors, DRHP p.210) share the same PSL
eligibility if their MSME books qualify — this is not an exclusive advantage, just
a sector-wide lever LIFL appears to be using somewhat more actively.

**4C regulatory moat assessment**: emerging in *degree of use*, not in *existence*.
Time to kick in: already active (FY26 DA/assignment sales). Sustainability:
moderate — depends on continued availability of bank counterparties wanting PSL
credit and on LIFL's own asset quality (the Up Money episode shows the same
DA-sale mechanism can also transmit counterparty risk back onto LIFL's book).
