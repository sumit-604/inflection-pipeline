### NEW FINDING 1 (🔴 Red Flag, elevates Pass 1 Finding #3) — Three independent auditor-authored
documents call Cybercons Infosec a "subsidiary," directly contradicting the Board's Report, the
RPT note, and AOC-1, all of which call it a 50% "associate"

Pass 1 found the *consolidation mechanics* (NCI, capital reserve) inconsistent with "associate"
treatment. Missed in Pass 1: the inconsistency is not confined to consolidation mechanics — it runs
through the auditor's own written language in three separate places in this Annual Report:

1. **CARO Annexure (standalone audit report), clause (xxi), p.94**: "The CARO report of the
   Company's **Indian Subsidiary Company, Cybercons Infosec Private Limited**, had not been issued
   by the subsidiary's auditor till the date of signing of this audit report." — the statutory
   auditor explicitly and unambiguously calls Cybercons a "subsidiary company" in a formal
   compliance clause, not a stray adjective.
2. **Consolidated Auditor's Report, "Other Matters," p.147-148**: states the consolidated
   financial statements "include the unaudited financial statements of **2 subsidiaries**...
   total assets of Rs 311.38 Lakhs... total revenues... Rs 39.19 Lakhs and total net profit after
   tax of Rs 7.62 Lakhs," with "1 subsidiary located in India" (unnamed, unaudited, furnished by
   management) and "1 subsidiary...located outside India" (matching the Singapore PTE Ltd,
   consistent with Pass 1). Since the Group's only other Indian entity is Cybercons, this "1
   subsidiary located in India" can only be Cybercons.
3. **Same Consolidated Auditor's Report, Regulatory Requirements section (g)(i), p.147**: two pages
   away, the auditor instead refers to "the Group, **its associate** – Refer Note No.31.6" —
   using the opposite term for what appears to be the same relationship, within the same report.

By contrast, the **Board's Report (p.35)** and the **AOC-1 Annexure (p.46)** are both internally
consistent and unambiguous: "The Company has **a subsidiary** Dynacons Systems & Solutions Pte.
Ltd.... an **Associate Company**...in the name of 'Cybercons Infosec Private Limited.'" The
inconsistency therefore sits specifically in the statutory auditor's own paperwork (CARO clause,
Other Matters paragraph) versus the Board's own disclosures and the Ind AS 28 classification used
everywhere else. 🔴 This is a disclosure-integrity red flag in its own right (the auditor's report
is not internally consistent on the legal/accounting status of a related entity with real P&L and
balance sheet transactions this year — see Pass 1's RPT step-up finding), and it independently
corroborates, from a completely different source (auditor language, not consolidation mechanics),
Pass 1's conclusion that Cybercons's true accounting status is unresolved in this Annual Report.
(CARO Annexure B cl.xxi, p.94; Auditor's Report on Consolidated FS, "Other Matters" p.147-148 and
cl.(g)(i) p.147; Board's Report p.35; AOC-1 Annexure I p.46)

### NEW FINDING 2 (🔴 Red Flag) — The auditor's "2 subsidiaries" combined profit figure (Rs 7.62
lakh) reconciles EXACTLY to summing Schedule III's Singapore + Cybercons profit shares, using the
Schedule III sign/magnitude, NOT the AOC-1 sign/magnitude — direct evidence the audited financials
run on the Schedule III figures rather than the AOC-1 figures Pass 1 flagged as contradictory

Schedule III Note (C-31.10, p.196-197) profit shares for FY25: Singapore PTE Ltd +Rs 29.64 lakh;
Cybercons Infosec (Rs 22.02) lakh. Sum = **Rs 7.62 lakh**. This is the *exact* figure the Auditor's
Report "Other Matters" paragraph (p.147-148) cites as the "total net profit after tax" of the "2
subsidiaries" for FY25. This is new, precise corroborating evidence for Pass 1's Finding #2 (the
Schedule III vs AOC-1 profit contradiction): it shows the audited consolidated numbers are actually
built from the Schedule III figures (which is standard, since Schedule III is drawn directly from
the consolidation working papers), while the AOC-1 Annexure — a separate statutory filing — was
evidently populated from a different, unreconciled source (the Singapore entity's local-GAAP
figures in USD, and a different Cybercons profit number entirely). It confirms AOC-1 is the outlier
of the two disclosures, not an ambiguous "which one is right" situation. 🔴 (Auditor's Report
"Other Matters" p.147-148; Note C-31.10, p.196-197; AOC-1 Annexure I, p.46-47)

### NEW FINDING 3 (🔴 Red Flag) — The Schedule III table (Note C-31.10) does not arithmetically
reconcile against itself year-over-year for either group entity, with two almost exactly offsetting
unexplained gaps

Missed in Pass 1: the Schedule III table (p.196-197) discloses net assets AND profit/OCI share for
**both FY25 and FY24**, which allows a roll-forward check the Pass 1 analysis did not perform.

- **Singapore PTE Ltd**: FY24 closing net assets Rs 3.51 lakh; FY25 profit share +Rs 29.64 lakh;
  FY25 OCI share Rs 0 (shown as "-"). Expected FY25 closing net assets ≈ Rs 3.51 + Rs 29.64 = **Rs
  33.15 lakh**. Actual FY25 closing net assets disclosed = **Rs (13.40) lakh**. Unexplained gap =
  **Rs (46.55) lakh** — net assets are Rs 46.55 lakh lower than the disclosed profit and OCI
  movements can account for, with no dividend, impairment, or FX-translation-reserve line disclosed
  to explain the difference (the OCI column is explicitly nil for this entity in both years, despite
  it being a foreign operation that should ordinarily carry an FCTR movement).
- **Cybercons Infosec Pvt Ltd**: FY24 closing net assets Rs 7.34 lakh; FY25 profit share (Rs 22.02)
  lakh; FY25 OCI share Rs 0. Expected FY25 closing net assets ≈ Rs 7.34 − Rs 22.02 = **Rs (14.68)
  lakh**. Actual FY25 closing net assets disclosed = **Rs 32.02 lakh** (this figure does tie to
  AOC-1's net-worth figure, as Pass 1 noted). Unexplained gap = **+Rs 46.70 lakh** — net assets are
  Rs 46.70 lakh higher than the disclosed profit movement can account for.
- The two gaps, Rs (46.55) lakh for Singapore and +Rs 46.70 lakh for Cybercons, are **near-identical
  in magnitude and opposite in sign** (difference of only Rs 0.15 lakh). No related-party, cash
  flow, or investment-note disclosure anywhere in the Annual Report shows an intercompany capital
  transfer, additional investment, or reclassification of this size (~Rs 0.47 Cr) between the two
  entities during the year (investment carrying values for both are unchanged at cost, Rs 5.05 lakh
  and Rs 5.00 lakh respectively, per Note S-3/C fixed at cost). This pattern is consistent with a
  value having been misattributed or swapped between the two entities' net-asset rows when the
  Schedule III table was compiled — a data-integrity issue in the disclosure itself, independent of
  and additional to the AOC-1 cross-reference contradiction Pass 1 already flagged. 🔴 (Note
  C-31.10, p.196-197 — both FY25 and FY24 columns)

### NEW FINDING 4 (🟡 Watch, new supporting evidence for Pass 1 Finding #3) — Line-by-line
comparison of standalone vs consolidated P&L and balance sheet items shows Cybercons's own asset
and expense balances are being added into the Group totals, which is not how an equity-method
associate is consolidated

Missed in Pass 1: a systematic standalone-vs-consolidated comparison across multiple line items
(beyond the receivables/CWIP deltas Pass 1 already flagged) shows small but consistent additions
that can only arise if Cybercons's full balance sheet and P&L are being combined line-by-line
(Ind AS 110 style), not equity-accounted (Ind AS 28 style, under which only a single "Investment in
associate" line and a single "share of profit of associate" line would appear, with no effect on any
other consolidated line item):

| Line item | Standalone FY25 (Rs Cr) | Consolidated FY25 (Rs Cr) | Delta (Rs Cr) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Capital work-in-progress | 21.95 | 24.07 | +2.12 |
| Trade payables | 392.86 | 395.60 | +2.74 |
| Employee benefits expense | 43.57 | 43.66 | +0.09 |
| Other expenses | 12.69 | 12.95 | +0.26 |
| Auditors' remuneration | 0.090 | 0.095 | +0.005 |
| Legal & professional charges | 1.53 | 1.75 | +0.22 |
| Purchases of stock-in-trade | 1,090.26 | 1,090.17 | (0.09) |

Every line except purchases is HIGHER on a consolidated basis than standalone — the direction and
pattern (small additions across CWIP, payables, employee costs, audit fees, legal fees) is exactly
what would result from adding a small entity's full financials line-by-line, not from equity
accounting. The one line that falls (purchases, down by Rs 0.09 Cr) is far smaller than the Rs 2.55
Cr Cybercons "Purchase" RPT transaction disclosed in Note S-32.4 — meaning even if some intercompany
purchase elimination is occurring, it is far short of full elimination of the disclosed RPT, which
is itself inconsistent with either a clean subsidiary consolidation (full elimination expected) or a
clean associate treatment (no consolidation elimination expected at all, since RPT with an associate
is not eliminated, only disclosed). 🟡 This is new corroborating detail, not previously quantified,
that strengthens Pass 1's Finding #3 with concrete P&L/balance sheet evidence rather than relying
solely on the NCI/capital-reserve mechanics. (Note S-2(d)/C-2(d) CWIP p.114/170; Note S-23/C-22
Trade Payables p.126-127/181-182; Note S-29/C-28 Employee Benefits p.128/183; Note S-31/C-30 Other
Expenses p.128-129/183-184; Note S-32.4 RPT p.133-134)

### NEW FINDING 5 (🟡 Watch) — Standalone and consolidated Note 32.9/31.8 (financial instruments
risk management) contain two internal, self-contradicting boilerplate statements not tailored to
DSSL's actual disclosed facts

Missed in Pass 1 (which cited the underlying numbers accurately but did not flag the surrounding
narrative text as self-contradictory):

1. **Interest rate risk paragraph** (Note S-32.9(c)/C-31.8(c), p.139/194): "The Company's exposure
   to the risk of changes in market interest rates **are managed by borrowing at fixed interest
   rates. During the year Company did not have any floating rate borrowings**." Two lines below, in
   the SAME note, the Interest Rate Exposure table the company itself discloses shows **Variable
   Rate Borrowings of Rs 49.64 Cr against Fixed Rate Borrowings of Rs 2.25 Cr (95.7% of total debt
   floating)**, plus a floating-rate sensitivity table showing a Rs 0.124 Cr P&L impact per 0.25%
   rate move. The qualitative sentence is the direct opposite of the company's own quantitative
   disclosure two lines later, in both the standalone and consolidated versions of the note. This
   reads as unedited boilerplate/template language, not a data error (the numbers Pass 1 used are
   correct), but it is a disclosure-quality flag: an investor reading only the narrative risk
   commentary would be told the opposite of the company's actual floating-rate exposure.
2. **Derivatives/hedging opening paragraph** (Note S-32.9/C-31.8 intro, p.136-137/192-193): "The
   Company **also enters into derivative transactions to hedge**...All derivative activities for
   risk management purposes are carried out by Group Treasury Team." Two pages later, the same
   note's FX-specific sub-section states plainly: "the company **has not entered into any forward
   contract for hedging or otherwise** in respect of foreign currencies during the year, and there
   are no such contracts outstanding at the end of the year" (Note S-32.9(c)/C-31.8(c)). Again, both
   statements appear verbatim in both the standalone and consolidated notes, indicating templated
   risk-disclosure language carried over without being reconciled to the company's actual (and
   correctly disclosed) zero-derivatives position. 🟡 Neither point changes any number in Pass 1,
   but both are new findings about the reliability of the *qualitative* risk narrative as a source,
   which matters because Note 32.9/31.8 is the primary Notes-based source for assessing DSSL's
   interest-rate and FX risk management framework. (Note S-32.9, p.135-139; Note C-31.8, p.191-194)

### NEW FINDING 6 (🟡 Watch, corroborates and elevates Pass 1 Finding #7) — The statutory auditor
identified "Revenue recognition (cut-off)" as the sole Key Audit Matter on both the standalone and
consolidated audit, explicitly citing period-end cut-off risk

Missed in Pass 1 (which analysed the revenue-recognition *policy* text but did not read the
Auditor's Report's Key Audit Matters section): both audit opinions (standalone p.90-91 area,
consolidated pp.144-145) designate "Revenue for the Company" / cut-off as the single Key Audit
Matter, stating: "Considering the **large volume of revenue transactions near period end**, there
may be a risk of revenue recognition occurring before the satisfaction of the performance
obligations by the company... there is a presumed risk that revenue may be misstated owing to the
improper recognition of revenue... revenue recognition (cut-off) was identified as a key audit
matter for the current year audit." The auditor's response procedures were limited to sample
testing of "customer acknowledgements to evidence proof of delivery... at or near period end" — i.e.
the auditor's own designated area of highest judgment for this audit is precisely the mechanism Pass
1 identified as structurally linked to the receivables ageing deterioration (Pass 1 Finding #4):
point-in-time revenue recognition at dispatch/delivery, no contract-asset buffer, and a
disproportionately growing near-period-end revenue/receivables profile. This is new, independent,
auditor-sourced evidence — not previously cited — that elevates Pass 1's inferential finding to one
directly corroborated by the statutory audit opinion itself. 🟡 (Independent Auditor's Report, Key
Audit Matters section, standalone p.90-91 and consolidated p.144-145)

### NEW FINDING 7 (🟢/🟡 minor, new) — Consolidated Note 31.1 (EPS) cites the STANDALONE profit
figure as "Profit after Tax," and the consolidated Basic/Diluted EPS shown in the P&L appears to be
computed on total Group profit before deducting NCI's share, not on profit attributable to owners

Note C-31.1 (p.185) tabulates, for FY25: "Profit after Tax (Rs/lakhs) **7,241.44**" — this is the
exact standalone PAT figure (matches the Standalone P&L line item precisely), not any consolidated

resulting overstatement is immaterial (≈ Rs 0.07/share, EPS 57.01 vs a technically-correct ~56.94),
but the Note 31.1 table itself uses a third, different (standalone) figure that matches neither
computation — a drafting/copy-paste inconsistency within the note. 🟢/🟡 (Note C-31.1, p.185;
Consolidated P&L, p.152-153)
