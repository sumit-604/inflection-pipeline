## SECTION 1: THE BUSINESS MODEL IN PLAIN ENGLISH

### 1A. One-line description
Smruthi Organics makes the active chemical ingredient inside generic medicines (APIs and drug intermediates) at two Solapur factories, sells almost all of it in bulk to Indian and overseas drug formulators, and is trying to move up from commodity, price-taking API supply into regulated-market (EU/Brazil/Korea) supply where filings and inspections — not price — decide who gets to sell (AR p.14, p.51).

### 1B. Money flow chain, by revenue stream

**1. Bulk Drugs & Drug Intermediates — domestic sale (largest stream)**
[Raw material inputs + power/utilities + in-house process know-how] → [Company synthesises the API/bulk drug at Unit I (Akkalkot Road) or Unit II (Chincholi MIDC), Solapur] → [ships bulk API in drums to Indian generic-drug formulators] → [formulator buys on standard commercial terms] → [pays cash on ~60-90 day credit from dispatch] (AR Note 3.5(a) revenue recognition, p.53; Note 1, p.51).

**2. Bulk Drugs & Drug Intermediates — export sale**
[Raw material + Drug Master File (DMF) dossiers filed with foreign regulators + Hyderabad/Solapur R&D] → [Company manufactures to the specification behind an approved/pending DMF] → [ships bulk API abroad — China and Russia named as growth markets in FY25; EU/Brazil/Korea targeted next via EDQM/ANVISA] → [overseas buyer (generic manufacturer/distributor) purchases] → [pays in USD/EUR, ~60-90 days, with no currency hedge in place] (AR MD&A p.14; Note 26B, p.77; Note 47, p.93-94).

**3. Finished Dosage Forms (FDF) — formulations marketing (smallest, shrinking stream)**
[Finished tablets/branded generics] → [Company's field force markets and distributes across Maharashtra, Andhra Pradesh and Telangana] → [sells to pharmacies/distributors] → [buyer pays on delivery/short credit] (AR Note 1, p.51). Secondary sales fell 36.4% in FY25 to Rs.37.27 lakh from Rs.58.57 lakh, attributed to reduced geographic coverage and field-force attrition (AR MD&A p.14).

**4. Export incentives (RoDTEP/duty drawback) — other operating revenue**
[Qualifying export shipment] → [Government issues export-incentive scrip/credit under the applicable scheme] → [Company recognises the credit once entitlement is established] → [credit monetised/offset against duties] (AR Note 3.5(c), p.53; Note 26A, p.77).

Within the API stream, product concentration is material: the top three molecules by value — Diloxanide Furoate (Rs.3,110.89 lakh), Metformin HCL (Rs.3,059.71 lakh) and Phthaloyl Amlodipine (Rs.1,928.53 lakh) — together account for Rs.8,099.13 lakh, or 65.1% of the Rs.12,438.03 lakh API revenue (AR Note 26D, p.77).

### 1E. The chai-stall-uncle version
Think of Smruthi as a wholesale spice grinder, but for medicine ingredients instead of masala. It buys raw chemicals, grinds and refines them in its own two factories in Solapur into the exact powder a drug-maker needs (say, the Metformin that goes into a diabetes tablet), and sells that powder in bulk — not the finished tablet, and mostly not to you or me directly. Because lots of other grinders around the world (especially in China) can make the same powder, the price it can charge keeps getting squeezed, like a vegetable vendor competing with five other stalls selling the same tomatoes. Its way out is to get "certified" to sell into stricter markets — Europe, Brazil — where fewer grinders are allowed to compete, which is a slow, paperwork-heavy process it is only now completing. Until that certification pays off, it is running hard (more debt, more factory spending) just to stand still on margin.

### 2D. Industry lifecycle stage and this company's position
The core business — commodity generic APIs such as Metformin — sits in a mature, price-competitive segment of the global API industry where China is the dominant low-cost supplier (implicit from management's repeated references to price pressure and to China itself as an important customer, AR p.14). Smruthi's stated strategy is a transition play: use its DMF pipeline and pending ANVISA/EDQM clearances to migrate a portion of volume into the earlier-lifecycle, less-penetrated regulated-market segment, where competition is narrower and pricing should be less commoditised. As of FY25 that transition is not yet reflected in margins (EBITDA margin improved only modestly, from 9.42% to 9.73% — see Section 3).

### 3D. Unit economics — the physics of the business

**Define one unit:** 1 kilogram of API sold (the company discloses volume and value by product, AR Note 26D, p.77).

| Product | FY25 volume (kg) | FY25 revenue (Rs. lakh) | Revenue per kg (approx., derived) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Diloxanide Furoate | 122,214 | 3,110.89 | ~Rs.2,545/kg |
| Metformin HCL | 1,333,763 | 3,059.71 | ~Rs.229/kg |
| Phthaloyl Amlodipine | 99,050 | 1,928.53 | ~Rs.1,947/kg |

(All derived from AR Note 26D, p.77; per-kg figures are Stage-4 arithmetic on disclosed volume/value, not company-reported unit prices.)

This spread is the key physics point: Smruthi is not one uniform per-kg business. Metformin is a high-volume, low-unit-price commodity (~Rs.229/kg) where the company is essentially a price-taker competing on cost; Diloxanide Furoate and Phthaloyl Amlodipine are lower-volume, much higher unit-value products (~Rs.1,900-2,550/kg) where differentiation/regulatory filing status matters more. A cost-per-kg breakdown by product is not disclosed — NOT FOUND, check investor presentation or concall — so blended gross margin (raw material 53.8% of total revenue, AR Note 28, p.78) is the best available proxy.

- **Volume drivers:** new DMF/regulatory approvals opening fresh geographies; customer requalification cycles; backward-integration-enabled capacity.
- **Price drivers:** global (especially Chinese) API supply/pricing; customer negotiating leverage given rising concentration; USD/EUR realisation on unhedged export sales (AR Note 47, p.93-94).
- **Cost drivers:** raw materials (53.8% of revenue), power & fuel (9.2%, +15.2% YoY), employee costs (13.8%), R&D (3.6%) (AR Note 28/30/33, Annexure II).
- **Incremental margin / operating leverage:** Fixed-ish costs (employee costs + depreciation + R&D) total roughly Rs.2,737 lakh, ~21.7% of revenue — high enough that volume growth should normally flow through to margin. In FY25, however, revenue was flat-to-down while power/fuel costs rose 15.2% and finance costs rose 32.8% (AR Note 31, p.79) — the year showed *negative* operating leverage, with EBITDA margin improving only 31 basis points (9.42% to 9.73%) despite a 38% jump in export volumes, because rising input and financing costs absorbed the gain.
