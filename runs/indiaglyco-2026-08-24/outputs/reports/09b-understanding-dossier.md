**1. Archetype, per business line.** India Glycols is not one archetype. It is four revenue lines inside one pre-demerger shell, splitting on 1 Sep 2026 into three separately listed entities (B00, B04):
- **BSPC — bio-based glycols, glycol ethers, industrial gases, staying in residual India Glycols (29% of revenue):** Commodity converter. Section 1B v3.7 Amendment 17 binds this line by name (B04 valuation_methods, tertiary method).
- **Potable Spirits — IMFL, country liquor, ENA, moving to IGL Spirits (32% of revenue):** Licence/scarcity business (state excise licence is the gate), carrying brand/franchise consumer characteristics on its IMFL premium sub-mix (Bacardi tie-up, Amrut distribution, CSD/paramilitary institutional approvals) (B04, B07).
- **Bio-Fuel — fuel-grade ethanol under the EBP, moving to IGL Spirits (35% of revenue):** Licence/scarcity business. This line is a government-quota supply contract; the company does not set its own volume or, largely, its own price (B04).
- **Ennature Bio Pharma — plant APIs, nutraceuticals, nicotine, moving to Ennature Bio Pharma Ltd (5% of revenue):** Outsourcing partner (specialty B2B pharma-ingredient manufacturing), with a licence/scarcity streak from its Thiocolchicoside API global-leadership and CEP/patent position, itself a single-product concentration (B04).

**2. Dominant variables (four; everything else is declared noise for this model):**
- **BSPC price spread and Chinese competitive pressure.** Bio-glycol realisation fell from roughly Rs 3.19 lakh/MT (FY25) to roughly Rs 1.93 lakh/MT (FY26); BSPC revenue fell about 26% cumulative over FY24-FY26 while its own India TAM grew about 6.7% a year — a market-share loss, not just a cyclical dip, on the evidence held (B04, B09).
- **EBP ethanol allocation volume.** Down about 22% year on year (19.82cr to 15.43cr litres) even as the national blending mandate held at E20; direction confirmed by two peers, exact magnitude unconfirmed by any peer (B06, B07, B09).
- **Spirits premiumization and institutional-channel execution.** B2 qualification lock-in (Bacardi/Amrut/CSD) scores Strong; management guides IGL Spirits FY27 EBITDA above Rs 500 cr with Q1 FY27 already at Rs 120 cr (B05, B07).

**3. The simple analogy.** Picture one shop today with four counters under a single roof: a chemicals counter selling industrial ingredients, a liquor counter selling whisky and country spirits, a government-contracted fuel-ethanol counter, and a small pharma-ingredients counter. ## SECTION 3: BUSINESS UNDERSTANDING NARRATIVE

India Glycols runs four businesses under one pre-demerger roof. BSPC makes bio-based glycols (MEG, DEG, TEG, Heavy Glycols), ethylene-based glycol ethers, and industrial gases; these are inputs other manufacturers use to make polyester and PET, cosmetics, and brake fluid, and switching a qualified supplier is not a quick decision for a buyer (B04). Potable Spirits makes IMFL, country liquor, and ENA, physical products a consumer or an institutional buyer drinks or a formulator uses as base alcohol (B04). Bio-Fuel supplies fuel-grade ethanol into the government's Ethanol Blending Programme, blended into petrol under a national mandate (B04, B09). Ennature Bio Pharma, at 5% of revenue, makes plant-derived APIs including Thiocolchicoside, plus nutraceuticals and nicotine derivatives, where the company holds a global leadership and CEP/patent position on a single product (B04).

BSPC's customers are industrial formulators who run qualification cycles before they switch a supplier; the emerging NSU/bio-amine pipeline names BASF, Unilever, and Mibelle as prospective qualification-stage customers (B07 catalysts). Spirits customers split between retail IMFL drinkers concentrated in Uttar Pradesh and Uttarakhand and institutional buyers in the CSD/paramilitary canteen channel, where a brand needs a first approval that is hard for a rival to get quickly (B04, B07). Bio-Fuel's only customers are Oil Marketing Companies buying under a government-set allocation and price, not an open market (B04, B09).

Demand for BSPC's products is tied externally to crude oil levels, which the AR ties to EO and bio-MEG competitiveness, and to Chinese glycol-ether feedstock and export pricing, both named as downstream signals to track (B09). Demand for Spirits rests on state excise volume growth in the company's home states and on continued institutional-channel approvals (B04, B07). Demand for Bio-Fuel rests entirely on India's ethanol-blending policy; the current E20 mandate sets the floor, and any move to E25/E100 sets the ceiling on this line's growth (B09).

Forward growth differs sharply by line. BSPC sits inside a realistic India TAM of roughly Rs 63,252 cr growing about 6.7% a year, with the company holding only about 6.0% of its own served-addressable market and 16.6x revenue headroom on paper (B09); but BSPC's own revenue fell about 26% cumulative over three years against that growing market, a share loss the run did not resolve as cyclical or structural (B09). Spirits carries the strongest near-term guidance: FY27 segment EBITDA above Rs 500 cr, with an aspiration above Rs 1,000 cr in four to five years and a doubling of volume guided for FY27 (B05). Bio-Fuel's growth driver, the EBP allocation, moved backward 22% year on year even as the national blending mandate held steady (B07, B09). Ennature carries a four-to-five-year aspiration of Rs 130-150 cr EBITDA, with no nearer-dated guidance found in the transcripts read (B05).

Competitive advantage does not sit evenly across the four lines. Spirits carries the clearest documented moat: Strong-rated qualification lock-in through the Bacardi, Amrut, and CSD/paramilitary relationships, plus a Moderate backward-integration position in captive ENA (B07). BSPC carries a moat that the run's own evidence calls "moderate but eroding": it is the sole Indian producer of ethylene-based glycol ethers via Sulzer Chemtech technology, but that position is under pressure from Chinese imports, and its process-innovation and NSU-platform categories score only Moderate (B04, B07). Bio-Fuel carries no company-built moat at all in this scan; its only related category is a Moderate regulatory-tailwind rating, and that tailwind is the one currently shrinking (B07). Ennature's moat rests on a single-product API leadership position and a Moderate strategic-partnership rating (Clariant JV, Algatechnologies), a concentrated rather than a broad advantage (B04, B07). This run's B07 scan carries no scored entries under numbered Categories 21 or 22; nothing in the committed blocks speaks to those categories for this company.

## SECTION 5: PLAIN-LANGUAGE SUMMARY

1. India Glycols today is one company running four different businesses under one roof: chemicals, spirits, ethanol fuel, and pharma ingredients (B04).
2. The chemicals business makes bio-based glycols, glycol ethers, and industrial gases, used to make things like polyester, cosmetics, and brake fluid (B04).
3. The spirits business makes whisky, vodka, country liquor, and industrial alcohol, sold mostly in Uttar Pradesh and Uttarakhand plus government canteens (B04, B07).
4. Chemicals customers are industrial buyers who must approve a supplier before switching, which builds some loyalty (B04).
5. Spirits customers include home-state retail drinkers and paramilitary canteen buyers who value a long-trusted, already-approved brand (B07).
6. The ethanol-fuel business sells only to government oil companies, at a volume and price the government sets each year (B04, B09).
7. The chemicals market is growing about 6.7% a year, but the company's own chemicals revenue fell about 26% over three years, so it is losing ground in a growing market (B09).
8. Management guides more than Rs 500 crore of spirits profit for the coming year, but one quarter's own numbers showed volume growing faster than revenue, the opposite of the premium-mix story told on calls (B05, B12b).
9. Ethanol-fuel volume allocated to the company fell 22% in one year, even though the national blending target held steady (B07, B09).
10. The spirits business has the clearest advantage: long, hard-to-copy exclusive ties to Bacardi, Amrut, and government canteens (B07).
11. The ethanol-fuel business has no company-built advantage; it depends entirely on a government quota that is currently shrinking (B07, B09).
12. The right way to see this company is not as one business, but as three separate businesses about to get three separate share certificates on 1 September 2026, each standing on its own numbers (B00, B04).
13. The overall picture is fragile. Several different things must go right at once, and a stumble in the three-way split alone would upend how the whole company should be read (B00, B04, B07).
14. The company's own annual report never explains why it let 92% of its foreign-currency hedges lapse in the same year its unhedged exposure roughly doubled (B02).
15. Two open questions stand out: what will each of the three new companies' balance sheets look like on their own after 1 September 2026, and is the chemicals price fall a temporary dip or a lasting loss of market share (B03, B09)?
