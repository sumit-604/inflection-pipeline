### PART A: THE FROM STATE

**A1. ARCHETYPE** (from the ARCHETYPE LIBRARY).
- Fabric lines (A&D Fabrics 31.67%, Industrial and Automotive 24.43%, Outdoor and Lifestyle 18.57% of FY26 revenue from contracts, B04): Build-to-spec component maker. Customer qualification, design-in, content per unit and input-cost pass-through drive it (B04; B07 B2). The outdoor line sits nearer an Outsourcing partner, since brands nominate the fabricator (B04). The draft keeps one archetype for the three fabric lines.
- A&D Solutions (22.97% of FY26 revenue from contracts, B04): Order-book business. Order inflow, execution pace, working capital and margin on the order drive it. Orders arrive as single tenders with no order book disclosed (B04; B05).

**A2. THE SIMPLE ANALOGY.** Kusumgar is a tailor-and-mill for very demanding customers. It makes the cloth in its own plants (weave, dye, coat, laminate). Customers such as armed forces, car-part makers and sports brands tell it the exact strength, weight and finish. It then spends years proving the cloth to each customer. Once the customer approves it, the customer rarely changes supplier (B04; B07). Since FY25 it also sews some cloth into finished parachutes, camouflage nets and shelters. For the parachutes it uses a design licensed from a US partner, who keeps the design rights (B07 FLAG-PARTNER-IP). It gets paid one order at a time, with no long contracts (B04). The plants are about half full, after capacity grew from 46.86 to 127.80 million metres (B03; B04).

## SECTION 3: BUSINESS UNDERSTANDING NARRATIVE

Drafted from B01 to B09 against the five-question spec in prompts/13-synthesis-pipeline.md. Stage 13 holds the final copy.

Kusumgar weaves, dyes, coats and laminates nylon and polyester cloth to customer specification. Since FY25 it also cuts and sews some of that cloth into finished defence systems (B04). Aerospace and defence fabrics were 31.67% of FY26 contract revenue. They go into parachutes, tactical gear and stealth systems, made to exact strength and weight limits (B04; Prospectus p.190 via B04). Aerospace and defence solutions were 22.97%. They are parachute systems, camouflage nets, decoys and shelters, plus maintenance work, and the parachute designs run under a licence from a US partner who keeps the design rights (B04; B07). Industrial and automotive fabrics were 24.43%, cloth for tapes, rubber goods and inflatables. Outdoor and lifestyle fabrics were 18.57%, cloth made to a global brand's specification for fabricators who supply it (B04). A customer keeps a qualified supplier because approval takes years and a change would restart it. One automotive tape customer ran a two-year type qualification (B04; B07 B2).

Customers are Indian government-owned entities (15.09% of FY26 revenue), private defence integrators, export customers (39.99%), automotive tape makers and the fabricators behind Decathlon (699.77 INR mn in FY26) (B03; B04). The company names Gliders India Limited and Troop Comforts Limited as entities that purchase its fabric directly and make finished goods for the military (B09 downstream candidates; Call p.13 via B09). Every sale is a purchase order with no long-term agreement. The top customer fell from 47.26% of contract revenue in FY24 to 11.13% in FY26 after two single orders did not repeat (B07 C2; B02). Present demand rests on the Ministry of Defence capital acquisition budget, which reserves 75% for domestic industry (B09 candidate, MoD capital acquisition budget), and on Defence Acquisition Council approvals and the parachute and camouflage tenders that trigger each Solutions order (B09 candidate, DAC approvals and tenders). Exports tie demand to monthly technical-textile export data and to US tariff actions on Indian engineered fabrics. The tape and roof-liner business ties to SIAM monthly vehicle production (B09 candidates).

Growth should come from defence indigenisation, from brand-by-brand approvals in outdoor fabric, and from export customers adding a supplier outside China (B05 triggers 4 and 5; B07 E2). Outside-India revenue rose from 1,167.43 to 2,698.44 INR mn from FY24 to FY26 (B07). The company-commissioned report puts growth in the domestic pools at 13.1% a year, but the company paid for it, and the run found no independent size for the Indian parachute market (B09). Outside checks on the defence leg are DAC releases, GeM bids and Gliders India filings (B09). The moat sits unevenly. A&D fabrics and industrial fabrics carry a moderate qualification barrier, shown by AS9100D and IATF 16949 certificates and the tape qualification (B07 B2). The Solutions line carries a moderate partnership position that the company does not own (B07 H2). Exports carry a moderate China+1 position (B07 E2). The run established no separate moat for the outdoor line, where the brand nominates the fabricator (B04). The company holds no registered IP, and the emerging moat score reads 15.5 to 17.5, MODEST, with no category scored strong (B07; B12c). The run did not establish the line mix of H1 FY27, because the company reports one segment after listing, or any order book (B04; B05).

## SECTION 5: PLAIN-LANGUAGE SUMMARY

1. Kusumgar is a 36 year old Gujarat textile maker. It weaves, dyes, coats and laminates nylon and polyester cloth in six plants (B01; B04).
2. FY26 revenue was Rs 692 Cr, down 11.2% from FY25. H1 FY27 revenue was Rs 498 Cr, up 90.7% on H1 FY26 (B03; B01).
3. Since FY25 it also sews some of that cloth into finished parachutes, camouflage nets, decoys and shelters. The parachute designs run under a licence from a US partner, who keeps the rights (B04; B07).
4. Armed forces and government entities purchase fabric and systems. Export customers took 39.99% of FY26 revenue. Car-part makers and sports brands take the rest (B04; B09).
5. Government-owned entities took 15.09% of FY26 revenue. The company says defence and indigenisation are the main reasons for demand (B04; B05).
6. Every sale is one purchase order. There are no long-term agreements. The top customer fell from 47.26% of revenue in FY24 to 11.13% in FY26 as two big orders did not repeat (B07; B02).
7. Demand should grow with defence orders, brand approvals in outdoor fabric, and export customers who add a supplier outside China. The company paid for the report that sizes the market (B05; B07; B09).
8. The moat is moderate in the defence and industrial cloth, because approvals take years. The Solutions line leans on a partner's design. The outdoor line has no moat the run could establish. The company has no registered IP (B07; B04).
9. The draft mental model: a maker of qualified cloth is trying to climb from a specified-cloth supplier (R3) to a finished-systems franchise (R4). The climb is proven only if cash is collected, orders are executed and the empty plant fills. The model is a DRAFT and is not signed (Section 2).
10. The fragility reading is FRAGILE. Five outside variables must go right. One failure, slow collection of the receivables, can break the thesis alone (Section 4c).
11. Cash is the weak point. Receivables rose from Rs 56 Cr to Rs 233 Cr in FY26 while revenue fell. Cash from operations was 15.0% of EBITDA. By Sep 2026 receivables had fallen to Rs 220 Cr (B02; B05).
12. The corpus could not establish the line mix after listing, any order book, any collections after 31 Mar 2026, or covenant headroom. The company now reports one segment and held no Q2 call (B04; B05; B02).
13. The corpus also lacks an annual report, the scanned FY2025-26 standalone statements as text, the exchange shareholding filing, and any research note (Section 1).
14. The biggest open question is whether the FY26 receivable build is a timing effect or a lasting feature. Mar 2027 receivable days and FY27 cash conversion decide it (B13).
15. The next open question is whether the A&D order executes by Mar 2027 without a second deferral. A second question is how the market reads the finished-systems claim (Section 2 Part B4, resolved at Stage 11).
