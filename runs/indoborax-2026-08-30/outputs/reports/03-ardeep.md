- **ECL matrix:** Trade receivables ECL is provision-matrix based (AR

### 2B. RPT map — extended

Beyond the office premises and residential property sales already covered
in Phase 1/B02, this pass surfaces two RPT items **not previously
isolated by B02**:

**NEW FINDING — Sale-and-leaseback to the exiting family.** AOC-2 (Annexure
III, item 2, AR p.42) discloses that the SAME office premises sold to Mrs.
Sreelekha Jain and Mrs. Pranika Jain for Rs 1,400.00 lakh was
simultaneously licensed BACK to Indo Borax & Chemicals Ltd "on leave and
license basis... for the period of 3 years," at a license fee of **Rs
55.00 lakh per annum, escalating 5% per year**. This is confirmed
independently in Note 40 (RPT note, "Rent Paid," AR p.108: Rs 13.75 lakh
each to Mrs. Pranika Jain and Mrs. Sreelekha Jain in FY26, totalling Rs
27.50 lakh for a partial year) and in the Cash Flow Statement, Financing
Activities, "Rent paid (27.50)" (AR p.86) — the half-year run rate of the
new 3-year lease. **This means the company booked a Rs 934.72 lakh
one-time gain on selling its own office to the exiting promoter family,
and simultaneously locked in a recurring rent obligation to the same
family for at least 3 years.** This is a materially different economic
picture from a simple asset monetisation: cash and a one-time accounting
gain now, in exchange for a rent stream to the departing family later.

**NEW FINDING — Commission payable to the outgoing MD still on the books
at year-end.** Note 22 (Other current financial liabilities, AR p.103)
shows "Salaries due to director: Rs 330.00 lakh" as at 31-Mar-2026 (vs Rs
448.14 lakh, FY25). This exactly matches the Rs 330.00 lakh performance
commission owed to Mr. Sajal Jain (outgoing MD & CFO, resigned 23-Jan-2026)
per the Directors' Remuneration table (Board's Report Annexure IV, AR
p.50). By construction, MD commission is payable only after AGM approval
of the year's financial statements — this is standard practice, not a
timing irregularity — but it means an additional ~Rs 3.3 cr is still due
to flow to the departed promoter-family MD after the balance sheet date,
on top of the Rs 62.02 cr gross RPT asset disposals (Phase 2 verification,
Rank 7) already booked in FY26.

**RPT concentration check:** the FY26 gross RPT asset-sale proceeds alone
(Rs 6,202.39 lakh) equal **28.8% of FY26 standalone revenue** (Rs
21,545.11 lakh) and **16.1% of FY26 total assets** (Rs 40,283.91 lakh) —
both well above any conventional materiality threshold for a single
counterparty group, concentrated entirely in the exiting promoter family,
inside the same year the change of control occurred.

### 6E. Quiet Abandonment Check (mandatory)

Three candidates were tested; two qualify as quiet abandonments, one does

1. **R&D investment priority — SILENT DROP, material.** Opening claim

   activity. That section instead states: "The Company has no specific
   Research & Development Department. However, in-house quality control
   facilities are utilized for product and process improvement and
   updation." This is a SILENT DROP: the opening claims a forward R&D
   priority; the operational (and legally mandatory) section addresses R&D
   directly and says none exists as a distinct function, with no
   acknowledgement of the tension between the two statements in the same
   document. **Materiality: significant** — R&D is one of the four named
   growth pillars, and the mandatory disclosure it should be tested against
   flatly denies the department exists.

2. **Boron Oxide facility capex — SILENT DROP, material.** Opening claim:

   present-continuous tense ("we are investing") has no matching capex,
   CWIP, or capital commitment disclosure anywhere in the FY26 financial
   statements. **Materiality: significant but narrower than the R&D
   finding** — the underlying product claim is true; only the specific
   Rs 20 cr facility investment claim is unevidenced.

3. **International expansion (South America/SE Asia) — NOT classified as
