## Business Understanding Narrative

Ugar Sugar crushes sugarcane. Farmers within about 80 villages deliver cane, the mill
extracts sugar at a recovery rate of 10.8% to 11.7%, and what is left over becomes three
other products: molasses, power from burning bagasse, and ethanol (CARE p.3, p.4). The
company has run this since 1939.

The economics are set by other people. The state fixes the cane price, and in FY26 the
Government of Karnataka told mills to pay above the fair and remunerative price, which
added about Rs 50 crore to manufacturing expenses (CARE p.3). The central government fixes
the ethanol price and the sugar release quota. The company's decisions are limited to
recovery, utilisation and mix.

What is changing is mix. A 800 kilolitre per day cane juice distillery commissioned in
December 2022, and it can be switched to 400 kilolitres per day of grain based ethanol in
the off season (CARE p.3). In the first half of FY27 the company is running that plant on
maize and FCI rice "to ensure better margins" (CARE p.3). Total capacity is 24,200 tonnes
of cane a day, 59.5 MW of co-generation and 845 kilolitres per day of multi-feed distillery.

Why now: FY26 was a genuine recovery. Operating profit before interest, depreciation and
tax went from Rs 45 crore to Rs 106 crore on revenue up 11%, and a loss of Rs 16 crore
became a profit of Rs 14 crore (CARE p.5). CareEdge upgraded the rating two notches in
August 2026.

What must be true: the distillery must run hard enough, on grain in the off season, to make
the company less of a sugar mill and more of an energy converter.

What breaks it: the balance sheet. Gearing is 3.13 times and total outside liabilities are
4.98 times net worth (CARE p.3). A weak cane year does not dent this company. It threatens
it.

## 2. Business model and archetype
Integrated sugar mill. Two units: Ugar Khurd with 20,000 TCD crushing, 44 MW co-generation
and the distillery; Jewargi with 4,200 TCD and 15.5 MW, and no distillery (CARE p.3).
Revenue streams are sugar, industrial alcohol and ethanol, and power.

Archetype: commodity converter. Section 1B v3.7 Amendment 17 binds, which matters at
valuation time because spot year ROCE and rupee working capital trends must not be fed into
Section 1B or FTTCP for this classification. The one economic engine is the gap between the
regulated cane price and the realised value of sugar plus ethanol plus power, multiplied by
recovery and by crushing volume.

Cash timing is part of the model and it is unusual. Oil marketing companies pay for ethanol
within 20 days and the power distribution companies within a month, while farmers must be
paid within 14 to 15 days (CARE p.4). The working capital cycle runs 135 to 145 days
because inventory sits at 145 to 160 days.

## 3. Competitive advantages
Location and relationships, not product. The command area has roughly 76,000 acres of cane,
the Krishna river is about a kilometre away, and recovery runs at the high end of the
Indian range at 10.8% to 11.7% (CARE p.3). Promoters have "wide acceptance among local
farmers and enjoy cordial relationships", which in this industry decides whether cane comes
to your gate or a neighbour's. Average crushing has been about 20 lakh tonnes over three
seasons.

Against that, sugar is a price taker in a regulated market, the nearby mills are named
competitors for the same cane, and ethanol pricing is a government decision. Pricing power
is absent. Moat verdict: a cost and catchment position, not a moat.

## 4. The promoters
The Shirgaokar family, who set the mill up in 1939 and still run it. Neeraj Shirgaokar and
Chandan Shirgaokar are the managing directors (CARE p.3). The promoter and promoter group
list runs to 44 entries, 25 individuals or HUFs and 19 entities including S B Reshellers
Private Limited, Tara Tiles Pvt Ltd and several family LLPs (Takeover file p.2, p.3).

Two positives are documented. The family declared no encumbrance of any kind on promoter
shares during FY26 (Takeover file p.1). And every recent insider record held is a family
purchase from the open market, including 2.26 lakh shares by Sohan Sanjeev Shirgaokar at
Rs 36.56 in March 2026 (market file). No insider sale appears.

Promoter holding percentage is NOT FOUND. So is the related party schedule, which matters
here because S B Reshellers is a promoter group company and reshelling is a service sugar
mills buy every season. That is the first document a deeper read must open.

## 6. The transition and the quality ladder
FROM R1, a commodity price taker with cyclical returns. TO an R2 claim, a cost advantaged
converter whose distillery flexibility smooths the cycle. The mechanism is feedstock
optionality: cane juice ethanol in season, maize or FCI rice ethanol out of season.

The agency's words, quoted once because no management text exists in this corpus: "CareEdge
Ratings continues to positively factor in the company's diversified revenue streams from
sugar, ethanol, and co-generation, leading to mitigation of the risk related to the cyclical
and seasonal sugar industry to a certain extent" (CARE p.2). Note the last five words.

The rung climb is partly real and entirely unfinished. Nothing in the corpus quantifies how
much of the Rs 106 crore of FY26 PBILDT came from ethanol rather than sugar.

## 8. Proof check
FY26 proves the recovery. PBILDT more than doubled while the company absorbed about Rs 50
crore of extra cane cost from the Karnataka directive (CARE p.3). Interest cover moved from
0.92, which means operating profit did not cover interest at all in FY25, to 1.74. The
rating went from BB+ Negative to BBB- Stable. That is a real, agency verified improvement.

The June quarter is where the proof thins. Revenue of Rs 481 crore produced Rs 26.7 crore
of PBILDT and Rs 1.49 crore of profit after tax (CARE p.5). Annualise nothing from a sugar
first quarter, since the season is November to April. But note the margin: 5.6%, against
7.1% for FY26, and the agency's downgrade trigger is a sustained margin below 5%. The buffer
is one bad season wide.

Proof of a cycle recovery: delivered. Proof of a structural rung climb from ethanol mix: not
delivered, and not measurable from this corpus.

## 10. Independent check: the credit rating
FULL, and it is the best document in this corpus. CareEdge upgraded long term bank
facilities of Rs 700 crore from CARE BB+ Negative to CARE BBB- Stable on 13 August 2026
(CARE p.2). The agency credits improved profitability, sugar realisation and co-generation,
and expects it to hold through FY27. It offsets that against a leveraged capital structure,
working capital intensity, and a seasonal, cyclical, regulated industry exposed to
agro-climatic risk.

Read the history line too. This is a recovery to BBB-, not a new high: the same facilities
were BBB- Stable in November 2023 and BBB- Positive in July 2023, then fell to BB+ Negative
in May 2025 (CARE p.6). The company has been here before and round tripped.

## 11. Posture: Transition Decision Matrix
- Proof gate: FIRED on profitability. PBILDT Rs 45 crore to Rs 106 crore, verified by an
  agency, is not a narrative.
- Ugliness: STRUCTURAL FEATURE. Gearing of 3.13 times has not moved in two years, the
  working capital cycle of 135 to 145 days is inherent to cane procurement, and the cane
  price is set by a state government.
- Recognition gap: CLOSED on earnings, and that is the sharpest fact on this card. Rs 562
  crore of market value against Rs 13.6 crore of FY26 profit is about 41 times. Against
  roughly Rs 232 crore of implied net worth it is about 2.4 times book (derived from total
  debt of Rs 726 crore at 3.13 times gearing, CARE p.3 and p.5; treat as an estimate).
Proof fired plus structural ugliness is the matrix CONTRADICTION row. That is a flag to
resolve, not a posture to act on. Resolve it by splitting the FY26 PBILDT between sugar and
ethanol. If ethanol carries most of it, the ugliness is being climbed out of. If sugar
price carries it, this is a cycle and the leverage is exactly what it looks like.
