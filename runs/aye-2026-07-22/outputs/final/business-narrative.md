Micro enterprise lender growth outpacing earnings quality

Aye Finance lends to India's informal micro enterprises. FY26 AUM was about Rs 7,044 Cr, FY26 PAT was Rs 194 Cr, and the shares listed on BSE and NSE in February 2026. The book runs on 70 plus business clusters and AI/ML underwriting, spread across 571 branches in 18 states and 3 union territories, with average tickets of Rs 1.5 to 4.9 lakh at yields near 22%. Interest income on loans is 84% of revenue. The company has no identifiable promoter; it is private equity and venture backed, with Elevation Capital at 12.6% and LGT at 10.1%.

The company is shifting three things at once. It raised Rs 710 Cr of fresh primary capital at the IPO, lifting CRAR to about 42% from roughly 31% before listing. It is building the secured mortgage and LAP book from 1.86% of the portfolio in FY23 toward a 30% target, currently near 22%. It is guiding credit cost down toward 3.5 to 4.0% for FY27 and a 3.25 to 3.75% comfort range over three years, with two rating agencies moving the same way: ICRA at A stable and India Ratings upgraded to A plus in June 2026 on the post IPO capitalisation.

Gate 0 scored AVOID at 59 out of 160. That posture is driven by three mechanical things: a backward disclosed history of four fiscal years, listing recency, and NBFC ratios that score near zero against bands calibrated to manufacturing returns. It is not driven by demonstrated fundamental deterioration, so it is carried as a flag for human synthesis rather than a stop. Cutting the other way, historical ROE reached 16.1% in FY24 and disclosed AUM compounded around 25%, both of which argue against reading the AVOID literally.

Cash flow from operations is negative every year, and for this business that is structural, not a warning. Loan disbursements are an operating outflow under Ind AS 7, so a lender funding book growth through borrowings will show negative CFO by construction. The real quality questions sit elsewhere: asset quality, and a growing gain on derecognition line. Income from securitisation and direct assignment rose from 1.94% of total income in FY23 to 3.65% in FY26, a day one gain that inflates reported profit and must be stripped before valuing the core spread business.

Credibility grades C on a maiden call with one transcript. Management missed its most confident guidance, 29 to 30% AUM growth for FY26, delivering about 26.6% on anchored on book loans. It delivered on what it said about asset quality, with Gross Stage III improving from 4.77% at March 2026 to 4.49% at June 2026, and on profit, with Q4 FY26 PAT of Rs 86 Cr, up 102% over the prior quarter. The excuse pattern is balanced, with no repeated evasions logged.

The earnings picture has already turned in the numbers. RoE fell from 17.28% in FY24 to 7.63% annualised in H1 FY26, RoA from 4.29% to 1.92%, and credit cost roughly doubled from 2.70% in FY23 to 5.15% in FY25. DuPont attribution puts the fall on profitability, not leverage. Set against that: CRAR near 42%, high liquidity coverage, an ECL cushion 3.4 times the RBI floor, unmodified audit opinions across all five presented periods, and covenant breaches on 23.6% of borrowings that are mostly unwaived and correlated with the same credit stress.

Peers corroborate the funding cost tailwind and contradict two AYE framings. MASFIN, Northern Arc and SBFC all report incremental cost of borrowing declining over the same window, which supports the NIM story. But SBFC's Karnataka ordinance experience cuts against the Bihar insulation framing: it brought collections down sharply, and management said it was "not about geography so much" as "about ticket sizes." SBFC also cautions the sub 2% mortgage aspiration, describing sub Rs 7 lakh secured MSME as carrying enhanced credit cost with SARFAESI recovery timelines almost twice the book average, against a mortgage book only about three years old.

The market runway is genuinely large. TAM classes as STRONG, with a serviceable market of about Rs 1,20,400 Cr where AYE holds 6.1%. The framework disciplined three year SOM of Rs 12,140 Cr implies an 18.3% revenue CAGR. Management's own aspiration of roughly 28 to 33% runs 1.5 to 1.8 times faster than that, and its headline TAM claim of about Rs 34 lakh crore is 8.3 times the framework figure, read as inflated. The 5 year path also runs about Rs 1,064 Cr past the current capital ceiling absent further net worth accretion or a raise.
