# B09 — TAM / SAM / SOM Market Sizing: Sheela Foam Ltd (SFL)

## SECTION 1: MARKET DEFINITION

### 1A. Precise boundaries

**Primary scope (this report's headline TAM/SAM/SOM):** the Indian mattress and
branded PU-foam home-comfort market — finished mattresses and closely bundled
sleep products (pillows, toppers, comfort accessories) sold to residential and
institutional (hospitality, healthcare) end-customers, across all price
segments (economy to premium), all channels (dealer/EBO, modern trade,
e-commerce, GCC-export via UAE stores), organized and unorganized combined.
This is the market the "Domestic branded mattress/home comfort" revenue line
(55% of consolidated revenue per B04) and the "Domestic technical/institutional
foam" line (15% of revenue) sit inside.

**Explicit inclusions:** slab-stock and molded PU foam mattresses, spring and
hybrid mattresses, rubberized-coir and cotton mattresses (competing substrates
in the same buyer decision), pillows/toppers sold through the same channel,
India-domiciled institutional/hospitality bedding.

**Explicit exclusions (sized separately as secondary legs per task scope, not
merged into the headline TAM to avoid double-counting):**
- International B2B foam manufacturing (Joyce Australia, Interplasp Spain) —
  mature Western/ANZ markets, ~30% of consolidated revenue, different
  competitive and regulatory context.
- Furniture rental (Furlenco / House of Kieraya) — a subscription-rental
  business model, not a mattress-purchase market; sized separately in 1A-secondary.
- IT services (Staqo/Presence360, ~1% of revenue) — unrelated market, out of
  scope entirely for this stage.
- Domestic technical/institutional non-mattress foam (automotive seating,
  filtration, sound absorption, industrial cushioning) — related but
  economically distinct from finished-mattress retail; sized as a secondary
  supplementary figure in Section 2 rather than folded into the core mattress
  TAM, because the underlying industry reports for "PU foam market" already
  include bedding-foam demand and merging the two would double-count.

**1A-secondary (adjacent legs, sized directionally, not full 5-method):**
- *Furniture rental (Furlenco):* India rental-furniture-and-appliance market
  reported at ~₹33,500 Cr (Redseer, April 2021) — **STALE, >4 years old
  relative to 2026-07-14 run date; direction only, not usable as a headline
  number** (searches_performed #17). Furlenco FY26 revenue ~₹370 Cr
  (management digest) against this dated base implies <1.1% penetration —
  directionally consistent with a large, early-innings adjacent opportunity,
  but the ₹33,500 Cr figure cannot be trusted as current-year TAM.
- *International foam (Australia + Spain):* no India-style market-size report
  found in this pass (time/search-budget constraint, logged as input gap).
  Floor-sized only by current combined revenue run-rate (~30% of ₹3,820.84 Cr
  = ~₹1,146 Cr, B04 revenue-stream split), which is a revenue fact, not a
  market-size estimate.

### 1B. Management's own TAM claim

Two distinct management claims found, held for the Section 2 ratio test:

1. **"Organized mattress market TAM of ~₹1,00,000 Cr (₹1 lakh crore)"** —
   attributed to a Sheela Foam press release dated on/around 17-Jul-2023 (the
   Kurlon/Furlenco acquisition announcement), per secondary-source search
   synthesis; the primary PDF (backend.sheelafoam.com Press-Release_17-07_2023.pdf)
   returned HTTP 403 on direct fetch and could not be independently verified
   against source text in this pass (input gap, flagged). **Date: Jul-2023,
   ~3 years old relative to run date — exceeds the 2-year freshness bar,
   lower-confidence per staleness rule.** Credibility read: **broad** — no
   stated methodology, round number, and (per Section 2) roughly 5-14x every
   independent organized-market estimate triangulated below, suggesting the
   figure conflates a wider "sleep/home economy" category rather than
   "organized mattress" strictly defined.
2. **"~30% market share of India's branded/organized mattress market"**
   (SFL 18% Sleepwell + 11-13% Kurlon, current, cited consistently across

   brand-level breakdown, internally consistent, corroborated across multiple
   independent outlets). This is the more reliable management data point and

3. Growth-rate aspiration (not a TAM claim but relevant to the mgmt_claim
   cross-check the task requests): CEO guidance of **12-15% / "14-15%"
   revenue CAGR over 3-5 years**, and CFO reiteration of a 15% growth /
   15% EBITDA-margin target by FY28 (multiple 2024-2026 sources). Held for
   comparison against the SOM-implied CAGR in Section 5.

## SECTION 2: TAM ESTIMATION, MULTIPLE METHODS

All conversions at ~₹87/USD (current FX). All figures ₹ Crore unless stated.

### Method 1 — Top-down (industry reports)

- Mordor Intelligence: India mattress market USD 2.40 Bn (2025) → USD 2.57 Bn
  (2026) → USD 3.89 Bn (2031), CAGR 8.60% (2026-2031). **Current, not stale.**
- IMARC Group: USD 2.40 Bn (2025) → USD 4.05 Bn (2034), CAGR 5.97%
  (2026-2034) — broadly consistent order of magnitude with Mordor.
- Research & Markets: USD 2.31 Bn (2025) → USD 3.48 Bn (2030), CAGR 8.54%.
- **Conversion:** USD 2.40 Bn × 87 = **₹20,880 Cr** (2025, conservative);
  USD 2.57 Bn × 87 = **₹22,359 Cr** (2026, realistic).
- No material subtractions needed at the top-down TAM level — geography is
  already India-only, and TAM by definition includes the ~70% unorganized
  segment (100%-capture opportunity), so nothing is subtracted here; the
  organized-only filter is applied in Section 3 (SAM), not here.
- **Confidence: High.** Three independent commercial trackers land within a
  ±10% band of each other for the same year.

### Method 2 — Bottom-up (unit economics)

- Indian Sleep Products Federation (ISPF): total India mattress market ~18.6
  million units/year (reference year not confirmed in available search
  snippets — **treated as directional/stale, input gap flagged**).
- SFL's own blended ASP (B04 unit economics, FY26 standalone): ~₹4,055 per
  mattress unit.
- If the 18.6 M unit figure approximates the **organized/branded** segment
  specifically (plausible, since ISPF is a trade body of organized
  manufacturers): 18.6 M × ₹4,055 = **₹7,542 Cr** — an organized-segment
  estimate, not a total-market TAM.
- **Confidence: Medium-Low** (unit count vintage unconfirmed; ASP proxy is
  SFL's own blend, not an independent market-average).
- This number functions as a cross-check on the **organized segment size**
  used for SAM (Section 3), not as an independent TAM.

### Method 3 — Peer revenue aggregation (organized branded players)

- Named-player sum: 2,101 + 1,134 + ~1,200 ≈ **₹4,435 Cr**
- Full organized-segment estimate incl. long tail: **₹6,300-7,400 Cr**
- **Confidence: Medium** (two of four inputs are directly sourced current-year
  figures; Wakefit and "other brands" are estimated, flagged).

### Method 4 — Import substitution

**Not materially applicable.** Finished mattresses are bulky, low-value-
density goods with negligible cross-border trade into India; the relevant
import exposure (TDI, polyol/MDI raw materials, per the operator digest) is
an upstream input-cost issue, not a finished-goods import-substitution TAM
driver. Not used to produce a TAM figure.

### Method 5 — Global benchmark (per-capita)

- China mattress market: USD 8.38 Bn (2026 est.), population ~1.41 Bn →
  per-capita ≈ USD 5.94.
- India mattress market: USD 2.35-2.57 Bn (2026 est.), population ~1.45 Bn →
  per-capita ≈ USD 1.62-1.77 (consistent with the independently reported
  "USD 2-4 per-capita" range for India).
- If India's per-capita spend converges to **half** of China's level (a
  5-7 year premiumization/formalization scenario, not a near-term TAM):
  1.45 Bn × USD 2.97 ≈ USD 4.31 Bn ≈ **₹37,500 Cr** — an upside benchmark
  scenario, **not used as the headline TAM** (directional evidence for
  growth drivers in Section 4 only).
- **Confidence: Low** (aspirational convergence assumption; useful for
  direction, not for the headline number, per conservative-bias rule).

|---|---|---|---|---|

Methods 2 and 3 are not independent alternate TAM estimates — they measure a
**narrower scope** (the organized segment only) and land, consistently with
each other (~₹6,300-7,500 Cr), inside the ~30% organized share of the Method-1
total market (0.30 × ₹20,880 Cr = ₹6,264 Cr). This internal consistency across
three differently-constructed methods is a genuine cross-check, not a
coincidence, and is the basis for the SAM figure in Section 3.

**Conservative TAM = ₹20,880 Cr** (2025 total market, lower of the Method-1
range). **Realistic TAM = ₹22,359 Cr** (2026 estimate).

**Management claim vs conservative estimate:** ₹1,00,000 Cr ÷ ₹20,880 Cr =
**4.8x**. Per the standard read (>2x likely inflated), this is **inflated**.
If instead compared to the organized-segment estimate that management's own
"organized mattress market" wording implies (~₹7,000 Cr, see Section 3), the
ratio widens to **~14x** — inflated under either denominator. Most likely
explanation: the ₹1 lakh Cr figure was a broad, round, deal-announcement
number that conflates mattress with a wider home-furnishing/sleep-economy
category, or projects a distant full-penetration scenario without a stated
horizon.

## SECTION 3: SAM & SOM

### 3A. SAM

Starting point: **Conservative TAM = ₹20,880 Cr** (total India mattress
market, organized + unorganized).

Filters applied:
1. **Product fit** — SFL/Kurlon compete as branded, organized manufacturers;
   they do not (by design) chase the deep-unorganized, hyper-local, unbranded
   price tier that the U2O program is explicitly converting rather than
   directly serving. Filter: retain organized segment only, ~30% of total
   (multiple corroborating sources, Section 1B/2). 20,880 × 0.30 = **₹6,264
   Cr**.
2. **Geography** — no subtraction; SFL is already pan-India (24 states per
   U2O program data).
3. **Channel** — no subtraction; SFL is omnichannel (EBO/dealer, e-commerce,
   GCC-export). GCC/export revenue sits *outside* this India-only TAM as a
   bonus, not counted here or subtracted from it.
4. **Customer** — no subtraction; residential + institutional/hospitality
   both included in the organized figure.
5. **Capability/price segment** — no subtraction; SFL/Kurlon span
   economy-to-premium; only a negligible ultra-luxury imported niche is
   excluded.

Cross-check against Methods 2 and 3 (organized-segment estimates of
₹7,542 Cr and ₹6,300-7,400 Cr respectively) suggests the pure 30%-of-TAM
calculation (₹6,264 Cr) is slightly conservative versus the two independent
bottom-up/peer-aggregation reads. Per the conservative-bias rule, but wanting
internal consistency with the well-corroborated ~30% *current market share*
figure (see below), **SAM is set at ₹7,000 Cr**, within the triangulated
range and the figure implied by (SFL domestic mattress revenue ₹2,101 Cr) ÷
(management's own ~30% share claim).

**SAM = ₹7,000 Cr, i.e., ~33.5% of conservative TAM (₹20,880 Cr).**

**Divergence flag:** An alternate SAM read exists via Duroflex's self-reported
"8% market share" ÷ its ₹1,134.2 Cr FY25 revenue = ₹14,175 Cr implied market —
roughly **2x** the SFL-implied ₹7,000 Cr. This is likely a denominator
inconsistency (SFL's 30% may reference the organized-only segment while
Duroflex's 8% may reference the total org+unorg market, or the two companies'
"market" definitions otherwise differ) — both figures are self-reported and
unverified by a common third-party methodology. **Conservative (lower) figure
of ₹7,000 Cr is carried forward per the conservative-bias rule; the
divergence itself is the flag.**

### 3B. SOM at 3 and 5 years

**Current share of SAM:** SFL domestic branded mattress/home-comfort revenue
₹2,101 Cr (55% of ₹3,820.84 Cr consolidated, FY26, B04) ÷ SAM ₹7,000 Cr =
**30.0%** — matching management's own claimed organized-market-share figure,
a clean internal cross-check.

**Share-gain trajectory:** Unorganized share of the total market is ~70%
(>40% threshold in the standard rule), which per the framework permits
faster-than-normal share gains as formalization proceeds. SFL is already the
#1 organized player post-Kurlon-merger with active, evidenced momentum: U2O
program +65% volume / +111% value (FY26), e-commerce +39% platform / +136%
brand.com (FY26), ~80% of ₹250 Cr Kurlon merger synergies already realized.
This supports the **aggressive** band (3-5pp over 3 years) rather than the
normal 1-2pp band, but SFL is already a large incumbent (30% share) facing
scaling competitors (Wakefit, Duroflex both pursuing IPOs/capital raises), so
the top of the aggressive band (5pp) is reserved for the 5-year point, not
the 3-year point.

- **Yr3 share: 33%** (+3pp, aggressive-but-grounded, capacity-supported)
- **Yr5 share: 35%** (+5pp cumulative, upper end of aggressive band, justified
  by sustained >40%-unorganized formalization runway)

**SAM growth (organized segment specifically, not the whole 8.6% total-market
rate):** the organized segment is formalizing faster than the total market
(consistent with "gradual shift from unorganised players to branded
manufacturers" cited across sources, and with SFL/peer double-digit organized
growth vs. 8.6% total-market CAGR). **SAM CAGR assumption: 12%** (between the
8.6% total-market rate and management's own 12-15% company growth guidance,
reflecting formalization tailwind without assuming the company captures 100%
of that tailwind).

Arithmetic:
- SAM_yr3 = 7,000 × 1.12³ = 7,000 × 1.404928 = **₹9,835 Cr**
- SAM_yr5 = 7,000 × 1.12⁵ = 7,000 × 1.762342 = **₹12,336 Cr**
- SOM_yr3 = 9,835 × 0.33 = **₹3,246 Cr**
- SOM_yr5 = 12,336 × 0.35 = **₹4,318 Cr**

**Implied revenue CAGR** (base = ₹2,101 Cr, FY26 domestic mattress/home-comfort
segment revenue):
- Yr3: (3,246 / 2,101)^(1/3) − 1 = (1.5450)^(0.3333) − 1 = **15.6%**
- Yr5: (4,318 / 2,101)^(1/5) − 1 = (2.0552)^(0.2) − 1 = **15.5%**

**Cross-check against management's own growth aspiration (12-15%/"14-15%"
CAGR, Section 1B):** SOM-implied CAGR (15.5-15.6%) ÷ management's stated
midpoint (~14.5%) = **1.07x — reasonable**, within the "within 1.5x
reasonable" band. This is a materially different (and far more favorable)
read than the TAM-claim ratio (4.8-14x, inflated) — management's *growth*
guidance is well-grounded against an independently constructed SOM, even
though its *TAM* framing (₹1 lakh Cr) is not.

### 3C. Capacity cross-check

Per B07 (operator digest): current India manufacturing capacity, on
debottlenecking/efficiency capex only (no new-plant capex), can support
**~2.0-2.5x present volume**. FY26 consolidated capex ~₹235 Cr (incl. CWIP);
FY27 planned ₹125-150 Cr consolidated (maintenance + debottlenecking +
marketing/showroom).

Using domestic mattress/home-comfort revenue (₹2,101 Cr) as the volume proxy:
- 2.0-2.5x ceiling = **₹4,202-5,253 Cr**
- SOM_yr3 (₹3,246 Cr) requires **1.55x** current volume — comfortably inside
  the capacity envelope, ample slack.
- SOM_yr5 (₹4,318 Cr) requires **2.06x** current volume — inside the stated
  2.0-2.5x envelope but toward its lower-middle, i.e., less slack than yr3.

**Capacity check: sufficient.** No capex-plan gap identified; if anything,
the SOM_yr5 figure sits close enough to the low end of the stated headroom
band that the constraint, such as it is, is on management actually executing
the debottlenecking (not on needing materially more capex than the ₹125-150
Cr/yr already guided). Between "SOM is optimistic" and "capex plan is
optimistic," neither is clearly the outlier here — the two are roughly
consistent, with demand/execution (not physical capacity) as the more likely
binding constraint on hitting the yr5 number.

| Driver | Impact | Evidence |

### 4B. TAM risks

| Risk | Monitoring signal |
|---|---|
| Raw-material volatility (TDI/polyol, Rs40-50/kg swings, Middle East conflict/GNFC shutdowns per digest) | Gross margin sequential decline >200bps unexplained (already a must-track metric per B04) |
| D2C/online-first disruption (Wakefit, The Sleep Company, Emma-style entrants) | E-commerce growth deceleration or dealer/EBO net-addition slowdown (already an B04 first-deterioration signal) |

| Substitution by rental models | Furlenco (SFL's own subsidiary) partially hedges this; third-party rental players (Rentomojo, CityFurnish) still a watch item for outright-purchase cannibalization in metros |
| Regulatory/environmental | PU foam flammability/VOC and emerging plastic-waste EPR rules in India — no imminent binding constraint identified in this search pass, but a category-wide tail risk |
| Import competition | Low — finished mattresses are bulky/low value-density, negligible import share; the real import exposure is upstream (TDI/polyol raw material) |
| Saturation | Low near-term risk given ~70% still unorganized (Section 5B saturation math below) |

### 4C. Market structure

- **Fragmentation:** ~70% unorganized / ~30% organized (multiple 2024-2025
  sources, consistent). Highly fragmented at the unorganized-tail level;
  consolidating at the organized/branded level.
- **Top-3 concentration (organized segment):** SFL+Kurlon ~29-30%, Duroflex
  ~8%, Wakefit (revenue-implied, not a stated share figure) — combined
  top-3 organized concentration roughly 45-50% of the organized segment,
  the rest split across a long tail of regional/legacy organized brands
  (Peps, Springfit, Restolex, Nilkamal-bedding, and others).
- **Consolidating:** SFL-Kurlon merger (completed Jan-2026 per digest),
  Duroflex and Wakefit both pursuing IPO capital (Duroflex DRHP filed for a
  ~₹180 Cr IPO per Oct-2025 reporting; Wakefit's 2025 IPO) — signals of a
  maturing, capital-market-facing organized tier consolidating around a
  handful of national brands.
- **Price vs. differentiation competition:** Organized players increasingly
  compete on product differentiation (memory foam, orthopedic, sleep-tech,
  brand) rather than pure price, per the premiumization evidence in 4A;
  price competition remains the primary axis versus the unorganized tier.
- **Entries/exits:** Multiple D2C entrants online (Wakefit, The Sleep
  Company, and others) in the last 5-7 years; no major organized-player exits
  identified in this search pass.
- **Import share trend:** Structurally negligible and not identified as
  trending in either direction for finished goods.

## SECTION 5: SUMMARY & RUNWAY

### 5A. Funnel diagram

```
TAM (conservative, 2025, total India mattress market, org+unorg): ₹20,880 Cr
   │  filter: organized/branded segment only (~30% of total, 3 methods agree)
   ▼
SAM (organized India mattress/home-comfort market): ₹7,000 Cr  (~33.5% of TAM)
   │  filter: SFL/Kurlon's current + realistically gainable share
   ▼
SOM yr3 (33% share of SAM, SAM grown to ₹9,835 Cr @ 12% CAGR): ₹3,246 Cr
SOM yr5 (35% share of SAM, SAM grown to ₹12,336 Cr @ 12% CAGR): ₹4,318 Cr

Current domestic mattress/home-comfort revenue (FY26, base): ₹2,101 Cr
Current share of SAM: 30.0%
```

### 5B. Runway assessment

- **Revenue headroom (SAM ÷ current segment revenue):** 7,000 / 2,101 =
  **3.33x**.
- **TAM growth rate:** 8.6% (total market, Method 1); organized-segment (SAM)
  growth assumed faster at 12% (formalization tailwind).
- **Company CAGR vs. TAM:** SFL's domestic mattress business is growing
  materially faster than the total-market rate — Q4 FY26 mattress +13%
  volume/+13% value YoY (India standalone, SFL+Kurlon combined, per digest),
  well above the 8.6% total-market CAGR and roughly in line with or above
  the 12% assumed SAM/organized-segment CAGR. **Reading: gaining share, not
  merely riding the market** — consistent with the well-corroborated ~30%
  organized-market-share figure and the U2O/e-commerce momentum data.
- **Years to saturate SAM at current growth:** using SFL's SOM-implied CAGR
  (~15.5%) against the assumed 12% SAM CAGR, relative share grows at
  ≈(1.155/1.12 − 1) ≈ 3.1%/year compounding from a 30% starting share.
  Solving 0.30 × (1.031)^t = 1.0 → t ≈ **39 years** to reach 100% of SAM.
  **No near-term saturation risk.**

### 5C. Runway classification: **STRONG**

Rationale: revenue headroom is a moderate 3.3x (not the 10x+ that would
justify MASSIVE), but the market is structurally still 70% unorganized with
an active, evidenced formalization tailwind, the company is demonstrably
gaining share rather than just riding market growth, capacity supports the
SOM without a new large capex program (Section 3C), and saturation is
decades away. This sits above GOOD (which would imply a more mature,
lower-growth, share-static picture) but below MASSIVE (which would require
either a much larger headroom multiple or a much higher-growth TAM).
