## Business understanding narrative

QMS sells two things to drug companies: branded medical devices and patient services. About 57% of FY26 revenue came from reselling devices such as BP monitors, stethoscopes, orthotics and surgical kits made by 3M, Heine and Rossmax to pharma companies and hospitals, and another 11% came from point of care test consumables used in clinic camps. Services made up 31% of revenue. That line runs about 32,380 health camps a year with field teams of physiotherapists, phlebotomists and nurses, plus 20 patient support programmes (PSPs), where counsellors and a 150 seat call centre keep patients on their prescribed therapy for months at a time. A pharma client finds these services hard to drop because tighter limits on direct promotion to doctors push drug companies toward patient programmes, and a running PSP ties the vendor's CRM dashboards into the client's own tracking. The customers are more than 130 institutional buyers, including more than 50 pharma companies with the top 10 among them, hospitals since FY25, and a small online retail slice under 1% of revenue. PSP contracts run for several months, and management puts rollover at 60 to 70%, which also means 30 to 40% of programmes do not renew. No filing discloses the top client's share of revenue, so the run did not establish customer concentration. Present demand rests on pharma promotion budgets: the Indian pharma market grew 9% in CY2025 after 6.4% the year before, and the slice spent on patient programmes and screening camps was Rs 1,613 Cr in 2024, which the "IQVIA Indian Pharmaceutical Market size and promotional-spend %" signal tracks. The live driver that QMS and two peers name is the wave of GLP-1 obesity and diabetes drugs, tracked by "GLP-1/anti-obesity drug India launches and generic entries", and the peers confirm the tailwind is real but broader than the QMS framing. Forward demand has two documented legs. HEINE made QMS its sole India distributor for five years from 1 January 2027, on an India revenue base of about Rs 30 Cr that HEINE expects to reach Rs 70 Cr by 2031, tracked by "HEINE Optotechnik India revenue delivery vs Rs 30 Cr (2026) to Rs 70 Cr (2031) path". The second leg is the composite scheme that folds the QMS and HCAH patient programme books into Saarathi, promising 160 plus programmes and 1.2 million patients, tracked by "HCAH/Saarathi composite scheme NCLT and shareholder approval progress" and still short of court and shareholder approval. Management sizes the India PSP market at about US$1 bn by 2030, growing 15 to 20% a year, while the US$70 bn figure in its deck is a global number with no QMS revenue in it; the "CDSCO import/distribution licence status" and "Named pharma-client relationship count and renewal" signals gate the product and client sides. The products line has no moat of its own: its edge is supplier granted exclusivity plus a CDSCO licence, smaller traders still compete, and pricing power is weak. The services line carries moderate advantages: the emerging moat scan scored qualification lock in through the SOC 2 Type II certificate, customer relationships and execution as Moderate, and strategic partnerships (HEINE and HCAH) as Strong. Talent asymmetry (Category 21) and the cannibalization barrier (Category 22) both scored zero, because a funded rival such as Indegene could build a comparable PSP platform without destroying anything of its own. The own brand, Q-Devices, is small at Rs 14 Cr in FY26 and three years old, so its brand advantage is low to moderate, and the scan's total of 17 sits in the MODEST band.

## Device distributor chasing a services margin

QMS Medical Allied Services is a Mumbai device distributor and pharma services vendor run by the Makhija family, who hold 68.11% with nothing pledged. It listed on the NSE SME board in 2022 and moved to the main board in June 2026. FY26 consolidated revenue was Rs 173 Cr, EBITDA Rs 26 Cr at a 15.0% margin, and PAT Rs 12 Cr. Products were 69% of revenue and services 31%. Q1 FY27 revenue was Rs 57 Cr, up 22%, with services at 41%.

The transition is a shift from device trading to patient services. QMS bought 51% of Saarathi Healthcare for Rs 45 Cr in July 2024, 25% more for Rs 14.5 Cr in October 2025, and the last 24% for Rs 14.2 Cr in September 2026. The implied value of the whole company fell from about Rs 88 Cr to Rs 56 to 59 Cr across those tranches. Saarathi earned a 20.7% net margin on Rs 25 Cr of income in FY26, against 4.4% for the parent, and supplied about 44% of group profit. Then on 25 September 2026 the board approved a scheme that moves QMS's own healthcare services business, Rs 37 Cr or 24% of FY26 standalone turnover, into Saarathi alongside HCAH's programme book, with Saarathi to list separately. The promoter review reads the filing as giving QMS public shareholders Saarathi shares one for one and 59.5% of the combined company. The moat review reads QMS as keeping a stake in a larger Saarathi. Either way, the services engine this thesis rests on may sit outside the listed QMS once the scheme closes. The same filing signed an MOU to buy BeamOptics, an optics instrument maker with Rs 1.1 Cr of revenue.

The backward scorecard grades AVERAGE, 53 of 100 on core and 61 of 160 overall, and the verifier's recount of 48 to 58 leaves the grade unchanged. Standalone revenue grew 16.6% a year from FY19 to FY26 with one down year. The eight year ROCE median of 25% is a relic of a tiny early capital base; FY26 ROCE was 9.6% and ROE 6.4%. Standalone PAT fell 36% to Rs 6.7 Cr. Net debt is 2.83 times EBITDA and interest cover is 3.51 times, each one band from a deal breaker line. EBITDA margin fell from 16.3% to 15.0% in FY26 and printed 14.6% in Q1 FY27, when employee cost jumped from Rs 3.2 Cr to Rs 11.2 Cr against flat material cost.

Cash is the weakest part of the record. Reported operating cash flow was Rs 19.4 Cr, 1.6 times profit. Both cash flow statements book the Rs 16.7 Cr rise in short term borrowings inside operating activities, and no policy note explains it. Take that out and operating cash was Rs 2.8 Cr, 0.23 times profit, after 0.36 times in FY25. Receivables older than six months rose from 14.5% to 30.6% of the due book, with no credit loss provision at all. The audited ratio note shows debt service cover of 0.79 times, while the MD&A in the same report prints 1.99 times. A new Rs 19.7 Cr bill discounting line appeared as the company paid suppliers faster and collected from customers slower. No credit rating exists to explain any of it, so the cash reading stays open between growth funding and a structural drag.

Management delivers what it controls and misses what it must convert. Revenue grew 11% and the Saarathi tranches landed on schedule. The Q-Devices target of at least Rs 25 Cr for FY26 came in at Rs 14 Cr, was never acknowledged, and became a revenue share goal. The services target of Rs 60 Cr landed near Rs 54 Cr. Margin guidance slipped every quarter: about 17% for FY26 became 15%, and 18 to 19% for FY27 sits against a 14.6% first quarter. The camps revenue figure was restated on two separate calls, and a Lupin programme share of 6 to 7% of turnover disclosed in 2025 was withheld in 2026. The Rs 500 Cr FY29 target appears only in a press release and was never defended on a call. Credibility grades C, and the verifier puts it at the C/D boundary.

Governance reads CAUTION. The statutory auditor resigned in FY24-25 and neither the firm nor the reason is disclosed. In the year standalone PAT fell 36%, the MD's pay rose 19% to Rs 2 Cr, his wife's salary rose 107% and his daughter's 40%. Promoter group firms with similar objects operate with no agreement barring competition. Through FY26 the parent borrowed Rs 16.4 Cr from Saarathi at an undisclosed rate while outsiders still owned 24% of it. The annual report has no accounting policies note, no segment result, no lease accounting and no financial risk note. On the clean side, the audit opinion is unqualified two years running, board attendance was 100%, and no legal or regulatory action was found against the promoter.

The peers undercut two company claims. QMS blamed shipping for its weak second half product sales, yet Poly Medicure said in November 2025 that Suez transit had fallen from two and a half months to one, and Entero's domestic business grew 42.6% in the final quarter. QMS calls 60 to 70% programme rollover sticky, while Indegene reports net retention above 100%. Entero runs about 61 days of net working capital and Poly Medicure about 140 days of cash cycle, against about 181 inventory days at QMS. Entero does confirm that commercial service roles earn EBITDA margins of 10% or more against 4 to 5% for pure distribution. Poly Medicure raises Chinese device dumping on every call, and QMS has never mentioned it.

The market runway is STRONG on paper. The reachable India market is about Rs 3,218 Cr and QMS holds 5.4% of it, so revenue could grow about 18.6 times at full reach. A steady share gain path reaches Rs 344 Cr in three years and Rs 557 Cr in five, a 25 to 26% growth rate. The Rs 500 Cr FY29 target needs about 43% a year. Product growth comes brand by brand through exclusive tie ups, so HEINE is the test. Services growth past the base path depends on the HCAH scheme, which has no stated completion date.
