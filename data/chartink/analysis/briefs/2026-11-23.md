## The picture in one paragraph

This is the picture of Monday 23 November, the latest session on record. On Friday the banks carried the index while the average stock fell. On Monday the banks fell too. The Nifty lost about one and three quarter percent and closed near 25,350. The three bank indices each lost more than three percent, and metals lost more. About seven stocks in ten closed lower. The count of stocks above their long trend line fell to 965, the first close under 1,000 since the first week of November. The Friday brief named that level as the line where the hopeful view starts to fail. The short and middle counts fell further and now sit near 900 each. Yet the day was a slide, not a rout. Only 75 stocks fell hard, turnover was lower than Friday, and fear rose only a little. The bounce off the October low has now given back about a third of its climb. The question for the coming sessions has changed. Last week it asked whether the members would follow the banks up. Monday answered the other way, and the open question now is how far.

## Where the market stands

Picture three clocks on every stock. The 20 day line is the short clock. The 50 day line is the middle clock. The 200 day line is the long clock. A stock above a line is in an uptrend on that clock. On Monday 916 stocks stood above the short line, 901 above the middle line and 965 above the long line. All three counts sit about 200 or more below half the market. All three fell on Monday, and the short count fell the most, by 134 stocks in one session.

The autumn gives these counts their meaning. 23 September was the last day when more stocks stood above the long line than below it. October brought two waves of selling. On 21 October, 408 stocks fell hard in a single session. Two sessions later 387 more fell hard, and only 607 stocks held the long line. That is still the low of the year on this count.

The climb out came in two bursts. On 4 November, 318 stocks rose hard, and 242 more did the next day. Those were real thrust days. By 12 November the long count reached 1,148 and the short count reached 1,711. That was the high water mark of the bounce. Since then the long count has lost 183 stocks. That is about a third of the whole climb from the October low. The short count has lost almost 800. More than four in ten stocks that held the short line eleven days ago have lost it.

Monday itself had a clear shape. About seven stocks in ten closed lower and fewer than three in ten closed higher. That is as wide as last Wednesday and wider than Friday. But it was a shallow kind of wide. Only 75 stocks fell four and a half percent or more, and 47 rose that much. The day of 16 November had 218 hard fallers. The October days had about 400. So most stocks lost a little, and few lost a lot. The damage on Monday was in the big names, not in the tail.

The turnover fits that reading. Money traded came to about 488 crore, against about 568 crore on Friday. That is level with the same session a year ago. So Monday was an ordinary volume day.

Two smaller readings say who controls the day. About one stock in eight traded four percent above the prior close at some point. Only one in four of those kept the gain into the close. That is the lowest share in twenty sessions. About one stock in six traded four percent below the prior close, and more than a third of those closed near the low. Buyers show up in the morning and sellers own the close.

One reading improved. The share of stocks down ten percent or more over five sessions had risen for five straight days. On Monday it fell, from about one in sixteen to about one in twenty three. Part of that is arithmetic, because the hard day of 16 November has now dropped out of the five day window. It is still the first easing in the tail in over a week. Fewer than one stock in a hundred is up fifteen percent over five sessions, close to the lowest in the record's last twenty sessions.

Fear rose, but mildly. The India VIX measures what traders pay for protection against large swings in the Nifty. It rose about two percent to 17.7 on a day the Nifty fell almost two percent. Last week it rose faster on days the Nifty climbed. It is up almost sixty percent over three months. One cross asset gap stays wide. The dashboard's gold tile shows gold funds down about sixteen percent over the month, while its Nifty reading is up about thirteen.

## Thirty points

### For the trader

**1. The bounce is hurt, not dead.** The long count sits at 965, against 607 at the October low and 1,148 at the peak. About a third of the climb is gone. The bounce is proven only when the long count holds above half the market, about 1,165.

**2. The line the Friday brief named has broken.** The Friday brief said a long count under about 1,000 would mark a new leg down. Monday closed at 965. One close does not confirm a leg, but the level no longer holds the market up.

**3. Monday was wide but shallow.** Seven stocks in ten closed lower, yet only 75 fell hard. The October sell days had about 400 hard fallers each. A wide day with few hard fallers is a slide. It turns into something worse when the hard faller count climbs past 200.

**4. The index and the members now move the same way.** Last week the Nifty rose while most stocks fell. On Monday both fell. The gap between them closed from the top, which is the cautious way for it to close.

**5. Banks gave back height, not trend.** The three bank indices lost a little over three percent each. Eight bank stocks in ten still hold the four week line. The setup is damaged only if that share starts to fall.

**6. Bank selling came without a volume rush.** Market turnover fell from about 568 crore to about 488 crore. The sheet has no volume figure for banks alone. On the market level reading, Monday was not a panic. A heavy volume down day in banks would be a different event.

**7. The short clock belongs to the sellers.** 916 stocks hold the 20 day line, about 250 short of half. Seven sessions ago 1,711 did. The short count has fallen on five of the last six sessions.

**8. The middle clock is the swing vote, and it keeps slipping.** 901 stocks hold the 50 day line, against 1,342 at the peak. Rallies run into stocks that are underwater on this clock. A turn up here usually comes before a turn on the long clock.

**9. Gains fade by the close more than at any time this month.** Only one in four stocks that traded four percent up kept the gain. That is the lowest share in twenty sessions. Half or more holding their gains would mark a change in who controls the close.

**10. Breakdowns outnumber breakouts.** About one stock in six traded four percent down during the day, against one in eight that traded four percent up. More than a third of the fallers closed near the low. Chasing strength within the day is a penalised setup in this tape.

**11. The tail eased for the first time in over a week.** The share of stocks down ten percent in five sessions fell to about one in twenty three. Part of that is the hard day of 16 November leaving the window. A second lower reading would make it a real sign.

**12. Metals are the penalised side.** The metal index fell almost four percent and is the worst index over one day and three months. One metal stock in five holds the four week line. The few small metal names at highs do not change the group.

**13. IT rose alone, and the members have not confirmed it.** The IT index gained almost two percent while three members in ten hold their weekly lines. An index move without members is a penalised setup to chase. A rise in the member share would turn it into a real rotation.

**14. Media and realty remain index traps.** Both indices sit about ten percent above their thirty week lines. Only about one member in four holds the forty week line in each. A few large names carry both.

**15. Large company strength halved in two sessions.** 45 large companies trade above their day's average price with rising long averages, against 95 on Thursday. Large company setups were the favoured side last week. They are less favoured now, with healthcare and autos the parts still producing fresh momentum.

**16. Fear rose less than the fall deserved.** The VIX rose about two percent to 17.7 on a Nifty fall of almost two percent. Protection is not being bid in a hurry. A close above about 18 would say fear has taken charge.

**17. Shorting has more to work with, but not a clean field.** More stocks sit under every trend line than above it, and breakdowns hold better than breakouts. Yet only three stocks are down twenty percent in five days, the same as the number up that much. Short setups are no longer penalised, and no longer rare.

**18. Froth is at its floor.** Fewer than one stock in a hundred is up fifteen percent in five sessions. Only three stocks are up twenty percent in five days. There is little excess left to unwind, which limits the fuel for a fast fall in the leaders.

## What would change this read

The read is a bounce off the October low that has lost a third of its climb and, on Monday, lost the help of its leader. Every "from" figure below is Monday's, and the next download will test them. The constructive view has already taken its first failure, the long count under 1,000. It fails fully if the slide deepens. A day of 200 or more hard fallers would turn the slide into selling with force, from 75 on Monday. A long count under about 900 would mean more than half the bounce is gone, from 965. A VIX close above about 18, from 17.7, would say fear has taken charge. A fall in the share of bank stocks above the four week line to about half, from eight in ten, would say the leader has broken. A long count under 607, the October low, would erase the whole bounce.

The cautious view fails if Monday proves to be a one day shakeout. The first sign is the long count back above 1,000 within a few sessions, from 965. The second is the short count turning up and heading back toward half the market, about 1,165, from 916. The third is a wide up day with 200 or more hard risers, from 47, on turnover above about 600 crore. The fourth is gains holding into the close, with half the day's four percent risers keeping the gain, from one in four. The fifth is the share of stocks down ten percent in a week falling for a second and third session. The first two signs together, with any one of the others in the same week, would put the repair back on track.
