- **TMT direct-charging delay (items 1-2)**: no specific excuse given, simply reframed as an ongoing initiative each quarter. Classified as **deflection** (no explanation, no revised date offered).

## SECTION 3: COMPETITIVE INTELLIGENCE FROM CONCALLS

### 3A. What management says about competitors (with credibility check)

Management never names specific competitors. The recurring competitive narrative across Q2 FY26 and Q4 FY26 is that "two large acquisitions of [stainless steel] plants operating in similar space under IBC" by "fairly large companies" caused a supply-side push-up that pressured pricing and margins across the industry as those plants ramped up capacity. This is repeated almost verbatim in both calls (Q2 FY26: Udit Rathi to Arth Jain and Prashant Shah; Q4 FY26: management to Vidhi Purohit on utilization-ramp constraints), which gives it internal consistency, but it is **unverifiable from the transcripts alone** since no company names, capacity figures, or timing are given. This is flagged as peer_question #3 for stage 6 to test.

### 3B. Industry and market intelligence dropped in the calls

- **Pricing/demand (Q2 FY26)**: industry pricing "a little soft" on excess domestic supply and rising imports; domestic consumption/demand "continues to grow at a decent pace"; imports via free-trade route (Indonesia, Vietnam) undercutting stainless steel; 12% import duty in place but pressure persists at industry level.
- **Policy/trade (Q3 FY26)**: Union Budget 2026 allocated INR12.2 lakh crore to capex (railways, infra, energy, urban development); Specialty Steel PLI scheme — 85 MOUs worth INR11,887 crore signed, incentives 4-15% for high-carbon steel production; INR20,000 crore earmarked for carbon capture and green steel transition; India-EU FTA moving tariffs on most iron/steel products toward zero, over 70% of tariff lines seeing immediate duty elimination; India-US reciprocal tariffs reduced from ~50% to 18% across a broad industrial basket.
- **Demand-supply/pricing recovery (Q4 FY26)**: domestic steel demand described as strong (infra, railways, roads, transmission, urban development); global steel volatility, elevated fuel costs (Iran-war-driven), and geopolitical uncertainty flagged; India "temporarily transitioned into a net importer in certain steel categories"; domestic TMT/rebar pricing shows "improving demand conditions and recovery... after a prolonged period of softness"; green steel becoming an industry priority, with the Ministry of Steel "intensifying its focus" on green certification.

### 4B. QUESTIONS FOR PEER VERIFICATION (formal handoff to stage 6)

1. **Question**: Is domestic steel/TMT demand growth genuinely tracking the government infra spending and tariff-relief narrative management cites (Union Budget 2026 INR12.2 lakh crore capex; India-EU FTA >70% tariff lines to zero; India-US reciprocal tariffs cut from ~50% to 18%)? **Why it matters**: this macro tailwind is management's core justification for the 20-25% CAGR guidance; if peers don't corroborate similar demand momentum or tariff impact, Rathi's framing may be overstated relative to company-specific execution. **Check peers**: other small/micro-cap North India TMT and stainless steel producers with comparable geographic footprint.

2. **Question**: Do peers report similarly comfortable scrap/ferro-alloy sourcing (no shortages, India a net ferro-alloy exporter, raw material cost ~75-80% of sales, imports curtailed to 7-10% of purchases)? **Why it matters**: tests whether Rathi's claimed sourcing advantage is sector-wide or genuinely company-specific (NCR proximity to auto/engineering scrap generators). **Check peers**: other scrap-based EAF/IF steelmakers in North India.

3. **Question**: Do peers corroborate an industry-wide stainless steel oversupply from two large IBC-acquired plants ramping up over the last 2-3 years, pressuring pricing and margins across the sector? **Why it matters**: this is management's primary external excuse for Rathi's stagnant ~4% EBITDA margin; if peers don't report the same dynamic, the miss looks more company-specific than management's framing suggests. **Check peers**: other stainless steel wire rod/billet producers in the NCR/North India market.

4. **Question**: Is the quantum of savings claimed from direct billet/hot-charging technology (Rs 3,000-4,000/ton on selling price, ~6-7% including yield benefits) plausible and comparable to what peers using similar hot-charging/EAF-route processes report? Does Rathi's "India's first and only stainless steel wire rod manufacturer using direct charging" claim hold? **Why it matters**: this is one of the four headline claims explicitly flagged for independent testing and underpins the forward margin-expansion thesis for the TMT division. **Check peers**: other stainless steel wire rod manufacturers and TMT/EAF-route peers using hot-charging technology.

5. **Question**: Is the green-steel/GreenPro-certified demand premium from institutional buyers (developers preferring certified green construction materials, higher FAR benefits cited) a real, monetizable industry trend, or an aspirational narrative specific to Rathi's positioning? **Why it matters**: management could never quantify this despite being asked in Q2 FY26 and twice in Q4 FY26 (see 2E); external corroboration would materially change the credibility of this trigger. **Check peers**: other green-steel/EAF-route TMT and stainless steel producers claiming similar certifications or renewable-power sourcing.

6. **Question**: Do peer disclosures on capacity utilization show similar quarter-to-quarter volatility/inconsistency to the pattern seen at Rathi (55-60% in Q2 FY26, 60-65% in Q3 FY26, ~51-53% FY26 average per the Q4 FY26 call)? **Why it matters**: the unreconciled utilization figures directly affect confidence in the margin-expansion thesis; if peers report more stable, better-reconciled utilization trends, this becomes a Rathi-specific disclosure-quality flag rather than an industry norm. **Check peers**: other regional steel melting shop operators of comparable scale.
