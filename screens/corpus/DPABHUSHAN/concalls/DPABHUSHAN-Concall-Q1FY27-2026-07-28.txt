===== PAGE 2 =====
"Q1 FY27 Earnings Conference Call", 22 July 2026.
Management: Anil Kataria (Whole-Time Director), Santosh Kataria (Chairman and Managing
Director), Vikas Kataria (Promoter), Manish Laddha (Chief Financial Officer).
Moderator: Ajit Mishra, Ernst & Young Investor Relations.

===== PAGE 3 =====
Ajit Mishra: introduces call, notes press release, investor presentation and financial
results already circulated.
Anil Kataria (Whole-Time Director): "The Indian jewellery industry entered FY27 against
the backdrop of elevated gold prices, policy-related developments, and evolving consumer
behaviour. During the early part of the quarter, high gold prices continued to influence
purchase decisions... the sharp correction in gold prices during June, followed by
relative stability in July, helped improve buying sentiment... While overall consumer
demand remained somewhat subdued in June, industry feedback indicated that jewellery
buying gained traction after the price correction, supported by exchange offers,
promotional campaigns, and flexible payment options."

===== PAGE 4 =====
Anil Kataria continues: seasonal demand (Akshaya Tritiya, summer weddings), broad-based
across plain gold and studded categories, growth in customer additions and ticket sizes.
Rising interest in lightweight, daily-wear, minimalist designs. Increasing relevance of
old-gold exchange given elevated gold prices and the May 2026 import duty increase from
6% to 15%. "While the recent government announcement and increase in gold import duty
from 6% to 15% in May 2026 has introduced near-term cost and demand sensitivity, the
long-term fundamentals of the sector remain intact."
Santosh Kataria: "I'm happy to share that the company has had an impressive start to
FY27. We delivered healthy performance in the first quarter, with revenue growing 58%
year on year, supported by festive occasions such as Akshaya Tritiya and the onset of
the summer wedding season." Footfall-to-conversion ratio 81%. Average ticket size
approximately Rs 1,57,000. Old gold exchange contributed approximately 25% of total
sales in Q1FY27, "which improves affordability for customers and reduces our dependence
on fresh gold procurement."

===== PAGE 5 =====
Santosh Kataria continues: Expansion "a very important pillar of our long-term growth
strategy." Locations finalised for Jabalpur (Madhya Pradesh) and Dahod (Gujarat). "All
key agreements, approvals, and necessary formalities have been completed. Fit-out and
showroom development activities are underway at both locations, and the stores will be
operational soon." Dahod (~3,200 sq ft) marks strategic entry into Gujarat under the
Company-Owned Company-Operated (COCO) model. Jabalpur (~3,750 sq ft) will be the first
Franchisee-Owned Company-Operated (FOCO) model showroom. E-commerce website and mobile
app launched during the quarter, live on Play Store and App Store, with expansion onto
online marketplaces underway. Brand sponsorship of the Madhya Pradesh League (Scindia
Cup 2026). Launched the Rani Rupmati Collection.
Manish Laddha (CFO): "During Quarter 1 '27, our total revenue stood at INR853.63 crores
as compared to INR541 crores in Quarter 1 FY26, registering a strong growth of 58%
year-on-year... At operating level, EBITDA increased by 70% year-on-year to INR93.99
crores in Q1 '27 as compared to INR55 crores in Q1 '26. EBITDA margin expanded by 80
basis points year-on-year to 11.01% as compared to 10.21% in the corresponding quarter
of the previous year."

===== PAGE 6 =====
Manish Laddha continues: "PAT increased by 77% year-on-year to INR64 crores in Q1 FY27
as compared to INR36 crores in Q1 FY26. PAT margin expanded to 7.55%, up 82 basis points
year-on-year." Revenue mix: gold revenue Rs 781 crore (Q1FY26 Rs 491 crore), up 59%.
Silver Rs 40 crore (Q1FY26 Rs 16 crore), up 150%. Diamond Rs 29 crore (Q1FY26 Rs 31
crore), down about 7%. Wedding-related purchases 62% of quarter sales, festive/light-
weight 18%, other/gifting 20%. Same-store sales growth 52% overall. Company continues
gold procurement hedging via exchange-led procurement, gold metal loan (GML) and MCX
hedging. Q&A opens: Praveen Jayaraman (Avendus Spark) asks about the D.P. Swarn Plus
scheme.
Manish Laddha: Swarn Plus is a structured monthly gold-accumulation SIP scheme
introduced in April 2026, minimum ticket Rs 5,000/month, 10-11 month tenor, redemption
into bullion or jewellery with price protection. "Even above INR 1,00,000/- almost 50
customers have been added which are contributing like more than INR 1,00,000 per month
per scheme."

===== PAGE 7 =====
Praveen Jayaraman asks about SIP traction timing. Manish Laddha confirms the scheme
started in April, runs 10 months, so redemptions will show from around Jan-Feb 2027.
On the Jabalpur FOCO model: "this is the first pilot model which we have started after
long period... a revenue sharing model which we are doing with that franchisee, whereby
franchisee will get good amount of return and as well as this gold gain will also be
received by them... we will continue for next four-five years also with idea of that
every year we may look for two to three franchisee by this model only."

===== PAGE 8 =====
On store maturity economics: "whenever we have opened, almost after two to three years
they reach to the three turnover of our inventory and after four to five years they
reach to the optimum level... so far as break-even is concerned, we generally receive
between six to nine months only... the last showroom of 8,000 to 10,000 square feet will
provide a turnover of INR350 crores to INR400 crores once it reaches to three to four
years and the smaller between INR150 crores to INR200 crores."
Kanishk Gupta (SS Family Office) asks: last call management targeted 51 stores by FY30,
but the implied annual pace (three to four stores) only reaches 28 to 32 stores by FY30
from a base of 12. Asks how the gap to 51 will be bridged and whether FOCO comes in later.

===== PAGE 9 =====
Manish Laddha: "for FY26, FY27, on an overall basis we are looking for six stores...
And in FY27, FY28, six to eight more stores will open and the same persistently going on
up to FY30. So, by this way we will achieve this 51 which is our vision also." Mix: "I
guess between six to seven will be COCO and we will target between one to two will be
FOCO" (revised later in the same answer to "Almost five to seven will be FOCO and
remaining will be COCO only" — internally inconsistent within the same answer). "the
current year was something different so far because you know in the month of May lot of
things were happening. But however, that traction has already gone over and things are
now almost near to settlement... we will continue with our vision of 51 stores, there is
no doubt. It may be that in a year one it may be instead of eight it will be six, but in
the year two and year three it will be compensated accordingly."
Vikas Kataria: "our overall plan remains broadly unchanged we aim to reach a total of 51
stores, and the majority of these will continue to be under the Company-Owned,
Company-Operated (COCO) model."

===== PAGE 10 =====
Vikas Kataria continues: "For this year as well, we are targeting the addition of
approximately five to six new stores... following certain developments in May, we
decided to temporarily moderate the pace of our expansion during the first quarter.
However, the situation is now well under control... we have already finalized two new
locations, while discussions and development activities are progressing for two
additional locations currently under planning. Furthermore, there is a good possibility
that by the end of this month, we will be able to finalize another two to three
locations."
Kanishk Gupta asks why multi-state expansion instead of deeper Tier 2/3 penetration in
MP/Rajasthan. Vikas Kataria: expansion follows brand-awareness adjacency (e.g. Dahod is
~100 km from Ratlam with an existing customer base); Tier II/III focus continues, no
plans for metro entry.

===== PAGE 11 =====
Vikas Kataria continues on competitive positioning: "in Indore, if we consider all
formats of a leading national jewellery brand, there are approximately 14-15 stores in
the city. However, the combined revenue of those stores is still lower than the revenue
generated by a single D.P. store." Anchal Maheshwari asks about hedging strategy for
gold and silver going forward.

===== PAGE 12 =====
Manish Laddha describes the four-layer hedging approach: real-time replenishment booking
with vendors, natural hedge via old-gold exchange, Gold Metal Loan (GML)-backed
inventory, and MCX-based hedging. On studded ratio: "As of now our business is doing
like between 6% to 7% of the studded mix. And we have targeted by March '28, we would
like to increase up to 12% to 15% on an overall basis." Diamond segment target: "for
March '28, our target is to take it to at least 2x to 3x of what we have today."

===== PAGE 13 =====
Discussion of the Amoura sub-brand (lightweight daily-wear studded jewellery, launched
about two years earlier) and continued dominance of 22-karat/18-karat in the company's
Tier II/III markets versus lower-karat fashion jewellery. Management not currently
pursuing lab-grown diamonds: "Lab-grown in the Tier 2, Tier 3 may not get good response
because it is not for the purpose of investment but for the purpose of fashion
accessory."

===== PAGE 14 =====
Nitin Dhanawat (Aurum Edge) asks about volume growth trend. Manish Laddha: "When we
compare Q1 FY27 with Q1 FY26, we have delivered volume growth of approximately 1% to 2%.
The balance growth has largely been driven by higher gold prices... only around 10% to
15% of the growth can be attributed to inventory gains. The majority of the improvement
has come from higher making charges." On last financial year (FY26): "the jewellery
industry did not see any meaningful volume growth during the last financial year. This
was largely an industry-wide phenomenon."

===== PAGE 15 =====
On gold inventory valuation: "we are following weighted average cost concept... As of
today, if we look at the prevailing gold price, it is trading at around INR1,51,000 -
INR1,52,000 whereas the inventory value reflected in our books is approximately
INR1,20,000. This creates a sizeable gap of nearly 20% between the carrying value of
inventory and the current market price." Fresh procurement at Rs 1,45,000-1,50,000.
On operating cash flow, asked directly why it has been negative for three years running:
"almost 95% to 98% of our assets consist of inventory. Therefore, whenever an
organization in this industry maintains healthy inventory levels, it is very difficult
to see significantly positive operating cash flows... This is simply the nature of the
business and a matter of financial mathematics."

===== PAGE 16 =====
Manish Laddha continues: inventory turnover ratio around 4.7x to 5.0x annually. Analyst
Nitin Dhanawat: "Got it, though I don't agree, but I would not like to argue right now."
Madhav Agarwal (SKP) asks about karat-wise revenue mix. Manish Laddha: valuation is done
on pure-gold-content basis, not per-karat SKU count. 22-karat is about 80% of gold
jewellery inventory, 18-karat about 15%, balance lower karatage.

===== PAGE 17 =====
Continued discussion on karat mix and lightweight 22-karat product engineering. Question
on diamond recoverable/exchange value versus competitors: "whenever certified diamond
jewellery is sold in the market, there is generally no issue when it comes to exchange,
resale, or remelting... value realization for certified jewellery typically remains
within a similar range, with only a marginal variation of around 5% to 10%."

===== PAGE 18 =====
Lokesh (individual investor) asks about inventory gains math: at a Rs 1,20,000 weighted
average cost versus a roughly Rs 1,50,000 selling price, is the company booking a ~20%
inventory-gain-driven gross margin on the quarter's gold sales. Manish Laddha clarifies
the pure-gold price (Rs 1,45,000-1,50,000) translates to about Rs 1,35,000-1,38,000 for
22-karat jewellery, and margin outcomes depend on category mix and making charges, not
solely on the pure-gold spread.

===== PAGE 19 =====
Manish Laddha continues clarifying SKU-level margin math; normal gross margin range 10%
to 11%, varying by product category (gold, silver, diamond each have distinct margin
profiles). Lokesh requests more frequent festive/quarter-specific sales updates; Manish
Laddha agrees to continue sharing festive sales data from August onward.
Sonu Nebhwani (Sonu Investments) asks: with revenue growth of 57-58% built almost
entirely on gold-price appreciation (volume growth only 1-2%), and with six to eight new
stores a year typically running at a lower 3-4x asset turnover in their early years, how
does the company sustain growth once the gold-price benefit rolls off the base.

===== PAGE 20 =====
Vikas Kataria responds: demand for gold jewellery pauses when prices rise sharply and
recovers once prices stabilise; deferred demand is now returning as prices corrected
from around Rs 1,70,000-1,75,000 to Rs 1,45,000-1,50,000. On guidance: "Broadly, we are
targeting around 10% volume growth going forward... value growth... will naturally
depend on the prevailing gold prices... For us, what matters most is how many kilograms
or tonnes of jewellery we have sold, as that is the true indicator of business growth."
