## 1C Trigger evolution across the three annual reports

**Drills: STRENGTHENING, and the only trigger with a clean guidance-to-delivery chain.** FY24 AR: "doubling
of our High-Speed Drills portfolio in the last fiscal year" (FY23-24) plus new Centre drill set-up (PDF
p.15-16). FY25 AR: near-identical paragraph reused almost word for word (see credibility finding, Section 2),
plus explicit forward statement — "we have made substantial capacity investments in this segment and we
expect high rate of growth in this segment" (PDF p.20). FY26 AR: delivery confirmed in narrative ("our
actions of the past to increase the capacities in Drill segments... has paid off and we are seeing
substantial growth... We expect the trend to continue in the next few years too", PDF p.24) AND in the
capacity data (+~350,000 unit CNC addition, Annexure IV) AND in the quarterly numbers this stage can check
independently (screener-Data_Sheet.csv: quarterly sales accelerate from a Rs 52.41cr trough in Q1 FY26 to
Rs 70.96cr in Q4 FY26 to Rs 67.55cr in Q1 FY27, +28.9% YoY). This is the one place a three-year documentary
paper trail and the numbers agree.

**Taps: UNCHANGED, still purely aspirational after two full annual-report cycles.** FY25 AR: "We believed
that the success seen in Indian and companies abroad in these area give us the required confidence of our
ability to scale up the business substantially... We are confident, this portfolio will grow substantially
over time" (PDF p.20). FY26 AR: "Our Taps segment recorded steady growth... We are confident, this portfolio
will further grow substantially over time. Consequent to the above, we have made substantial investments in
this segment and we expect high rate of growth in this segment" (PDF p.24). The language is stronger in tone
in FY26 ("steady growth... recorded") but still carries NO revenue figure, NO unit figure, and NO segment
disclosure anywhere in Note 21/Note 38 (single reportable segment, confirmed by B02/B03) to check it against.
Two annual reports of "confident... will grow substantially" with zero quantification either time is a
timeline-without-a-timeline, not a slipping deadline — there was never a deadline to slip.

**Export/channel: WEAKENING then explicitly REVERSED, tracks a real external shock.** FY24 AR: "overall
export market has not been very encouraging" but names offsetting double-digit growth in the CS Taps &
Carbide Rotary Burrs line (PDF p.16). FY25 AR upgrades the language: "improving trend in the export business
performance" (PDF p.20) — the only year this trigger reads positively. FY26 AR reverses hard: "overall export
market has not been very encouraging... Tariffs imposed by USA and Mexico on India led to a drop in sales...
competition and Government protectionist policies make inroads difficult" (PDF p.25), while also claiming
partial recovery ("regained some business in Far East and GCC countries"). This is a real, externally-caused
reversal (US/Mexico tariff action is independently plausible and dated to the FY26 period), not obvious
spin — but it means the ONE quarter (FY24→FY25) where export commentary improved was immediately followed by
the worst export language of the three years. A reader following export guidance alone would have been wrong
twice in three years.

**Price pass-through on commodity inflation: QUIETLY WEAKENING.** FY24 AR: "we implemented price increases
across our portfolio, which we were able to successfully pass on to our customers" — an unqualified success
claim (PDF p.16). FY26 AR: "the business faced margin pressure due to global raw material price escalation
to which your company responded by trying to pass on as much cost increase as possible to the consumers /
customers" (PDF p.25) — the same maneuver described with hedged, weaker language ("trying to... as much as
possible" versus "successfully... pass on"), and the qualitative retreat is corroborated by the hard numbers:
materials cost/revenue rose from 32.0% to 37.2% FY25→FY26 (B03 Phase 3C, +517bp), i.e. pass-through was
materially incomplete in FY26 even though the FY24 AR's earlier claim was an unqualified win. This is a
genuine, evidenced softening of an earlier claim, dropped from "successfully" to "trying... as much as
possible" in the same recurring sentence pattern — a small but real tell.

**No trigger disappeared outright.** Every trigger named in FY24 AR reappears in some form in FY26 AR; none
was quietly abandoned. The abandonment pattern in this corpus runs the other way (Section 2, "quiet
abandonment" on operational CLAIMS, not growth triggers): the front-matter claims are what get contradicted
by the same document's own body, not the underlying growth story itself.

**New trigger appearing only in FY26 AR, with no prior-year lead-in:** the Austempering furnace (+90 MT) and
the digital transformation/SAP claim both appear for the first time in FY26 AR with zero mention in FY24 or
FY25 AR of either being planned. The furnace investment is capitalised and verifiable (gross block additions
Rs 14.99cr per B03); the SAP claim is not (no capex line, no metric, R&D spend NIL the same year).

**Pattern check.** Do they blame externals while crediting themselves? Partially: domestic growth (7.8-7.9%
FY26) is credited to internal capacity investment and sales execution, while export weakness is blamed on
tariffs — a real asymmetry, but each half is independently well evidenced (capacity numbers are real; the
tariff shock is real), so this reads as accurate attribution more than self-serving spin. Do they ever say
"we made a mistake"? Yes, once and plainly: FY25 AR's "growth was however not in line with the expectations"
is as close to an unforced admission as this corpus contains. Do they raise hard topics proactively or only
under pressure? There is no external pressure mechanism in this corpus (no analyst, no Q&A) to test this
against, so the honest reading is: attrition and the FY25 growth miss were raised proactively (nobody was
asking); the pledge and the FY26 inventory-vs-claim contradiction were NOT raised, proactively or otherwise,
across three full annual cycles.

**Overall excuse pattern: BALANCED, leaning toward honest on operating misses (growth shortfall, attrition)
but silent on the two items with the most information value (pledge, inventory).** This is the excuse-pattern
finding, not the same as the credibility grade below, which also weighs the contradicted inventory claim.
