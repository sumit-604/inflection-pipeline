## SECTION 2: LEGAL & REGULATORY RECORD (searched hardest; the load-bearing
section for this stage)

**Critical sourcing note:** none of the four regulatory matters below
(2A i-iv) are disclosed anywhere in the FY26 Annual Report. Board's Report
Item 37 states "there are no significant and material orders passed by
the Regulators or Courts or tribunal impacting the going concern status
and Company's operations in future" (AR, Item 37). The BRSR legal
proceedings table reports "NIL" penalties/settlements under its NGRBC
Principle format, with a blanket carve-out ("except as disclosed in the
Annual Report or as otherwise communicated to the stock exchanges from
time to time" — AR p.94). All four items below were disclosed to the
stock exchanges (Reg 30), just not inside the audited Annual Report a
shareholder would normally read for a legal-proceedings summary; three of
the four surfaced in this run only because the company's own NCLT
scheme-of-amalgamation filing (a Reg 30 disclosure filed for an unrelated
purpose — the Silverleaf Capital Services merger) contains a mandatory
"pending litigations" annexure. This is a genuine disclosure-placement
pattern, consistent with B02/B03's separate findings on where the NCD
early-redemption and Enshrine-acquisition items were (and were not)
disclosed.

### 2A SEBI actions

**(i) SEBI Settlement Order, "Settlement Scheme for Association with
Certain Algo Platforms, 2025," dated 17-Mar-2026 (order text PSD/SD/
SettScheme/2/2025-26).** ✅ VERIFIED (SEBI order text in corpus;
company's own Reg 30 disclosure; corroborated by web media). Share India
Securities Ltd (PAN AAACF6462E) is item 99 of 111 stock brokers who
settled SEBI adjudication proceedings arising from their APIs remaining
integrated with an algo platform (identified in the company's own later
filing as Tradetron) whose displayed strategies claimed guaranteed or
consistent returns, in alleged violation of SEBI Circular 2022/117 and
the stock-broker Code of Conduct. Settlement amount: Rs 1,00,000, no
admission of guilt, matter closed. The peer list is close to the entire
Indian broking industry (Zerodha, Kotak Securities, ICICI Securities,
HDFC Securities, Motilal Oswal, Angel One, IIFL, Nuvama, SMC Global,
Choice Equity Broking and 101 others) — this materially dilutes the
severity read: it is an industry-wide compliance gap, not company-specific
misconduct.

**(ii) SEBI Adjudication, NSE co-location facility, final order
30-May-2022, Rs 3,00,000 penalty.** ✅ VERIFIED (business media
contemporaneous coverage; company's own NCLT filing quoting the order).
The Adjudicating Officer found Share India connected to NSE's secondary
Tick-By-Tick (TBT) server against NSE's advice (conduct dated 2012-2014),
violating PFUTP Regulations and the stock-broker code of conduct, but
explicitly found **no collusion with NSE, no fraudulent intent, and no
evidence of undue advantage** — a materially narrower finding than the
main NSE co-location scandal (which involved different parties and much
larger sums).

**This did not end there.** In August 2023, on a fresh April-2023 report,
SEBI issued a **new show-cause notice alleging abnormal profits of Rs
1.91 Cr (Rs 191 Lakh)** tied to the same co-location matter. The company
contested this and appealed to the Securities Appellate Tribunal (SAT);
SAT has since disposed of that appeal, and **"the matter presently
stands before SEBI"** as of the company's own August 2026 filing — i.e.
this is **live and unresolved** as of this run, with a rupee quantum
roughly 64x the original 2022 penalty. This is the single most material
regulatory item found in this stage. ✅ VERIFIED (company's own sworn
litigation-status disclosure, filed 2026, in the corpus).

**(iii) NSE adjudication / SAT appeal, order-placement/market-disruption
allegation.** ✅ VERIFIED (company's own filing). NSE issued a
show-cause notice 25-Nov-2022 alleging Share India placed orders
"significantly below the intrinsic value of the NIFTY 14500 CE contract,"
depressing derivative prices and disrupting orderly market functioning,
in alleged violation of F&O Regulations and exchange circulars. NSE found
inadequate risk controls and market disruption and imposed a penalty.
Share India's SAT appeal (Appeal No. 248/2024) was pending — rejoinder
filed, no resolution — as of the company's own August 2026 filing.

**(iv) MCX adjudication, Natural Gas options open-position-limit breach,
penalty Rs 1,09,43,750 (~Rs 1.09 Cr), imposed 23-Aug-2023.** ✅ VERIFIED
(company's own filing). MCX alleged a 21.46% breach of intraday
open-position limits, **calculated by combining Share India's
proprietary account positions with those of two entities — R S Futures
LLP and R S Securities — that appear on the AR's own Note 52 list of
"entities in which KMP or their relatives have control or significant
influence."** Share India disputes the computation (states actual
utilisation was only 68.58% of the limit, that the strategies were
standard conversion-reversal/butterfly/calendar-spread positions, and
that the related parties acted independently with no company control
over them), and has an appeal pending before SAT (Appeal No. 109/2025)
as of the company's own August 2026 filing. **This is a regulator's own
working finding that positions in a promoter/relative-linked vehicle were
aggregated with the company's book in the company's core proprietary
derivatives-trading activity** — the same activity that generates ~58%
of consolidated income (per B02/B03's BRSR-anchored finding). Not proven
(appeal pending, company denies control), but a genuinely new,
load-bearing finding for this run.
