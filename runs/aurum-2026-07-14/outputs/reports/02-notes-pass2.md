## PART A — STANDALONE, NEW FINDINGS

### A1. CARO Annexure B (standalone), Section 186 disclosure table (p.90) — Investment
balance does NOT reconcile with the audited Note 4.a.1
The auditor's own Section 185/186 table states: "Balance outstanding as at balance
sheet date — Subsidiaries: Investments ₹15,242 Lakhs; Loans ₹8,223 Lakhs" (Annexure B,
para iii(A), p.90). The Loans figure reconciles exactly with Note 4.b.1 + 4.b.2
(₹581 + ₹7,642 = ₹8,223 Lakhs). The Investments figure does **not** reconcile with
Note 4.a.1 (total investments ₹26,120 Lakhs, p.102) or with the RPT table's subsidiary
investment balance of ₹25,420 Lakhs (Note 21(D)(i), p.116) — a gap of roughly
₹10,000-10,900 Lakhs (~₹100-109 Cr), around 28-30% of standalone net worth
(₹36,248 Lakhs, Note 24.d, p.119). The "Aggregate Amount granted/provided during the
year — Investments ₹2,496 Lakhs" (same CARO table) also does not cleanly reconcile
with the ₹1,022 Lakhs of new equity investment reported in Note 21(A)(i)(a) (p.115)
even after adding the ₹700 Lakh Pranami Neev NCD and ESOP-linked additions. 🔴 Red
Flag — an unreconciled, material (>₹100 Cr) discrepancy between the statutory
auditor's own CARO annexure and the same auditor's audited financial statement notes,
on the identical subject (subsidiary investment balances), with no explanation
anywhere in the AR. This sits directly alongside Pass 1's #1 finding (CARO
qualifications) and should be treated with similar weight; it also bears on the
credibility of the ₹254.2 Cr "Investments in subsidiaries" balance underpinning the
parent's related-party financing structure (Pass 1 #5).

### A2. Legacy Mastek demerger stamp-duty dispute, ₹249 Lakhs paid under protest
Note 7 (Other current assets, p.106): "Share of stamp duty ₹249 Lakhs against demand
on Mastek Ltd. by the office of the Superintendent of Stamps, Gandhinagar, for
implementation of the demerger scheme, paid under protest." This is an asset carried
on the books whose recoverability depends on an unresolved legacy stamp-duty dispute
tracing back to the Company's original demerger from Mastek. Not disclosed in the
Contingent Liabilities note (Note 22, p.117, which states nil commitments and
contingencies for the standalone entity) despite being an active dispute. 🟡 Watch —
recoverability risk on a ₹2.49 Cr asset, disclosure sits in an "Other current assets"
footnote rather than the contingent liabilities note.

### A3. Aurum Platz (Goregaon) Pvt Ltd — ₹1,500 Lakh related-party deposit round-trip
Note 21(C)II.i (p.114): "During the year the Company has invested in inter-company
deposit of Aurum Platz (Goregaon) Private Limited amounting to ₹1,500 Lakhs which were
encashed during the year and there was no deposit as on March 31, 2025," earning
₹23 Lakhs interest income. Aurum Platz (Goregaon) is a related party by virtue of
common directorship, not a subsidiary — it sits outside the consolidation boundary.
A ₹15 Cr deposit placed and fully withdrawn with a director-linked entity within the
same year is a new item not surfaced in Pass 1. 🟡 Watch — large in-and-out related-
party fund movement with an entity outside the Group, no further disclosure on
purpose or terms beyond the interest rate implied (~1.5% for partial year, unstated
tenor).

### A4. Standalone liquidity-risk boilerplate contradicts the Company's own DSCR
Note 24.c (p.118): "The Company consistently generated sufficient cash flows from
operations to meet its financial obligations as and when they fall due." This
standard liquidity-risk paragraph sits one page away from Note 26 (p.119), which
discloses DSCR of 0.11x (down from 0.39x) with management's own explanation
attributing the decline to "repayment of borrowings" — i.e., debt service this year
was not covered by operating cash generation. Pass 1 flagged the DSCR number itself;
this is the specific textual claim in Note 24.c that conflicts with it. 🟡 Watch —
boilerplate risk-management language not reconciled with the entity's own ratio
disclosure two pages later.

### A5. Standalone "Return on Investment" ratio (0.83x) is a new metric this year,
driven by an unrealised Level-3 fair value gain
Note 26 (p.119-120): a new ratio row, "Return on investment," shows 0.83x (Numerator
₹639 Lakhs / Denominator ₹766 Lakhs average invested treasury funds; no prior-year
comparative, "-"). The ₹639 Lakhs numerator is dominated by "Income on investments
measured at fair value" of ₹541 Lakhs (Note 14, p.106) — the unrealised fair value
gain on the Level 3 Integrow Real Estate Special Situation Fund, which grew from
₹1,500 Lakhs to ₹3,217 Lakhs (standalone) / ₹4,267 Lakhs (consolidated) during the
year (Note 4.a.2/24.b, p.103, p.118). An 83% "return on investment" headline is
therefore substantially an unrealised, management/independent-valuer-driven Level 3
mark, not cash yield. 🟡 Watch — new ratio disclosure whose composition is not
transparent from the ratio table itself.

### A6. ESOP costs for subsidiary employees systematically capitalised into
"Investment in subsidiary," not expensed
Note 4.a.1 notes (iii), (v), (vi), (ix) (p.103): the fair value of stock options
granted to employees of Monk Tech Labs (₹3 Lakhs), Helloworld (₹4 Lakhs), Aurum
Analytica (₹38 Lakhs) and NestAway (₹2 Lakhs) — ₹56 Lakhs combined in FY25 (also
visible in Note 21(A)(i)(b), p.115) — under the disclosed accounting policy
(Note 2.(7)(c), p.97), the fair value of options granted to subsidiary employees is
recorded as "Investment in subsidiaries" rather than as an expense. This is policy-
compliant and disclosed, but it means the parent's subsidiary investment carrying
values are inflated every year by a non-cash, ESOP-related addition that layers on
top of cash investments and debt-to-equity conversions, compounding the opacity
around what the ₹254.2 Cr standalone "Investments in subsidiaries" balance actually
represents. 🟡 Watch — mechanical/structural, not a misstatement.

### A7. FY24 standalone cash loss quantified for the first time
CARO Annexure B, clause xvii (p.91): "the Company has not incurred cash losses in the
current financial year, however there were cash losses amounting to ₹488 Lakhs in the
immediately preceding financial year." A specific new figure (₹4.88 Cr FY24 cash
loss, cash-positive FY25) not carried in Pass 1's standalone coverage. 🟢 Clean/
informational — supports the standalone cash-generation improvement narrative already
partly visible in the cash flow statement.

## PART B — CONSOLIDATED, NEW FINDINGS

### B1. Principal auditor did not audit ~44% of Group assets; relies on 9 other auditors
Auditors' Report, "Other Matters" (a) (p.204): the consolidated financial statements
include audited figures from 9 subsidiaries with total assets of ₹29,457 Lakhs
(≈44% of consolidated total assets of ₹67,451 Lakhs), total income ₹21,980 Lakhs,
total net loss after tax ₹2,633 Lakhs, and net cash outflow ₹109 Lakhs — all audited
by other (unnamed) independent auditors, not Kirtane & Pandit LLP, the principal
auditor. Kirtane & Pandit's opinion "in so far as it relates to the amounts and
disclosures included in respect of these subsidiaries is based solely on the report
of such auditor." 🔴 Red Flag — the principal auditor did not directly audit close to
half the Group's balance sheet. This materially compounds the CARO-exceptions finding
from Pass 1 (#1): several of the entities carrying CARO qualifications (Integrow,
NestAway, K2V2, Monk Tech Ventures, Bonds Brain) are very likely among these
component-audited entities, meaning the qualifications surfaced through a
multi-auditor reporting chain the principal auditor does not directly control.

### B2. Auditor change between FY24 and FY25 — comparatives not audited by the
current auditor
Auditors' Report, "Other Matters" (b) (p.204): "We have not audited the comparative
financial information included in the consolidated financial statements for the year
ended March 31, 2024. The consolidated financial statements for the year ended
March 31, 2024 were audited by the predecessor auditor, whose report dated
April 29, 2024 expressed an unmodified opinion." This confirms a statutory auditor
change occurred between FY24 and FY25 (Kirtane & Pandit LLP is a new appointment for
FY25); the FY24 comparatives relied upon throughout this AR were audited by a
different, predecessor firm. Not surfaced anywhere in Pass 1. 🟡 Watch — auditor
rotation itself is routine and not inherently adverse, but combined with B1 (heavy
reliance on other auditors) and the CARO exceptions, it means a first-year auditor
is opining on a Group with multiple subsidiary-level compliance exceptions and
limited direct audit coverage of comparatives.

### B3. IEPF transfer delay (auditor) contradicts "no amount due" (notes) — direct
contradiction on the same matter
Auditors' Report, Rule 11 disclosures (p.204): "There has been a delay in transferring
amounts, required to be transferred, to the Investor Education and Protection Fund by
the Company." Yet both the standalone (Note 10.b.2, p.108) and consolidated
(Note 10.b.2, p.143) notes state, on the identical unclaimed-dividend/IEPF item:
"During the year, the Company has transferred ₹2 Lakhs to Investor's Education and
Protection Fund (IEPF) and there is no amount due and outstanding as at Balance Sheet
date to be credited to the fund." 🔴 Red Flag — a direct, verifiable contradiction
between the statutory auditor's own compliance finding and management's note
disclosure on the same statutory-compliance matter within the same AR. Feeds
directly into Pass 3's "contradictions between notes and other sections" test.

### B4. Goodwill: Helloworld is a SECOND acquisition with negative net identifiable
assets, not just NestAway
Note 3.c(A)(iii), consolidated (p.136), "Calculation of goodwill" table: Helloworld's
net identifiable assets acquired were ₹(576) Lakhs — NEGATIVE — generating
₹4,387 Lakhs of goodwill (25.2% of total consolidated goodwill of ₹17,425 Lakhs).
Pass 1 identified only NestAway (₹9,635 Lakhs goodwill, 55.3%, negative net assets
₹(1,317) Lakhs at acquisition) as bought with negative net identifiable assets. With
Helloworld added, **two of six** acquisitions — together 80.5% of total goodwill —
were purchased with liabilities exceeding identifiable assets at acquisition, with
the entire premium parked in goodwill. 🔴 Red Flag — materially deepens Pass 1's #3
finding; the goodwill impairment-risk concentration is broader than Pass 1 captured.

### B5. Direct charges to Retained Earnings bypassing the Consolidated P&L
Consolidated Statement of Changes in Equity (p.127): two FY25 line items adjust
Retained Earnings directly, outside the Consolidated Statement of Profit and Loss —
"Investment in subsidiary (65)" and "Subsidiaries no longer consolidated (93)"
(₹158 Lakhs combined). The "Subsidiaries no longer consolidated" charge appears tied
to the Imogentechno/Wisetechno SPV deconsolidation (Note 28, p.157) but no
corresponding loss-on-deconsolidation is visible in the Exceptional Items note
(Note 19, p.150, which shows nil FY25). 🟡 Watch — exactly the "direct debits/credits
to reserves bypassing P&L" category the extraction brief calls out; immaterial in
size (₹1.58 Cr) but a genuine disclosure gap on the deconsolidation accounting.

### B6. Non-controlling interest grew despite Group losses — undisclosed subsidiary
equity issuance to outside investors
Total NCI grew from ₹759 Lakhs to ₹1,012 Lakhs (Consolidated Balance Sheet, p.126)
even though NCI's share of total comprehensive loss was ₹(790) Lakhs this year
(Consolidated P&L, p.126). The explanation sits in the Consolidated Cash Flow
Statement (financing activities, p.128): "Proceeds from issue of equity shares by
subsidiary ₹1,507 Lakhs" (nil PY). No note anywhere identifies which subsidiary
issued this equity, to whom, or at what valuation — a ₹15.07 Cr capital raise at the
subsidiary level larger than the entire opening NCI balance, undisclosed. 🟡 Watch —
question for management; plausibly linked to the SM REIT external-investor onboarding
under YieldWiseX (Note 28) but not stated.

### B7. Lease-liability "written back" gain: FY24 comparative does not reconcile
between the note and the cash flow statement
Note 3.b(iv) / Note 14 (p.135, p.145), "Rent concession and liabilities no longer
required written back": ₹(1,028) Lakhs FY25 / ₹(1,068) Lakhs FY24. The Consolidated
Cash Flow Statement (p.128) shows the equivalent adjustment as "Gain on lease
terminated and lease liability no longer required written back ₹(1,028) / ₹(699)."
FY25 figures match exactly (₹1,028 = ₹1,028); the FY24 comparative does not
(₹1,068 vs ₹699, an unreconciled ₹369 Lakh gap) for what is described as the same
line item in both places. 🟡 Watch — Pass 3 cross-check item; may reflect a
combined/netted cash-flow line versus an isolated note figure, but the AR gives no
reconciliation.

### B8. Note 5.a (Deferred tax) contains a verified internal arithmetic error —
confirmed via page-image render of the source PDF
Note 5.a, consolidated (rendered PDF p.140, printed page 238-239): the sub-table
"Deferred tax assets to be recognised in Consolidated Balance Sheet" shows, for FY24:
Deferred tax assets ₹1,243 Lakhs, Deferred tax liabilities ₹(6) Lakhs, Total
"(1,237)" — printed with a negative sign and a value that is arithmetically consistent
internally (1,243 − 6 = 1,237) but **contradicts** the "Deferred tax assets /
(liabilities), net" figure of ₹2,349 Lakhs stated twice earlier in the identical note
(as the net DTA figure and again as the reconciliation's FY24 closing balance), and
also contradicts the Consolidated Balance Sheet's own reported FY24 "Deferred tax
assets (net) = ₹2,349 Lakhs" (p.126). This was verified against a rendered image of
the actual PDF page (not just the text extract) to rule out an OCR artifact — the
error is genuinely printed in the audited financial statements. 🔴 Red Flag — a bona
fide, verified clerical/reconciliation error within an audited note that neither
preparer nor auditor caught; low quantitative materiality on its own but a direct,
verifiable data point for the accounting-quality and disclosure-transparency scoring
dimensions.

### B9. Two external real-estate NCD holdings alongside the Integrow fund — an
undiscussed ~₹60 Cr treasury book
Beyond the Integrow Real Estate Special Situation Fund (₹4,267 Lakhs, already flagged
in Pass 1), the Group also holds: 15% unquoted NCDs of Pranami Neev Realty Limited,
₹760 Lakhs total (₹700 Lakhs non-current + ₹60 Lakhs current, Note 4.a.1/4.a.2,
p.137-138), and 15% unquoted NCDs of Peer Realty Pvt Ltd, ₹990 Lakhs (down from
₹1,000 Lakhs PY, Note 4.a.2, p.138). Neither counterparty appears in the related-
party disclosures (Note 21, p.151), so both are external. Combined with the Integrow
fund, the Group runs an approximately ₹60.2 Cr Level-3, real-estate-lending-style
treasury book carrying attractive 15% coupons — sizeable relative to the Group's
₹284.47 Cr total equity — that is not discussed as a business activity, segment, or
capital-allocation strategy anywhere in the notes reviewed. 🟡 Watch — question for
management on rationale, counterparty due diligence, and whether this constitutes an
undisclosed related or quasi-related lending activity.

### B10. Subsidiary ESOP fair values are difficult to reconcile with negative net worth
NestAway ESOP Plan V (Note 15.b, p.150): grant-date fair value/share price of
₹1,427.84 per option for 29,054 options granted in FY25, exercise price ₹1. NestAway's
own statutory net worth is disclosed elsewhere in this same AR as NEGATIVE
₹(3,608) Lakhs and worsening (from ₹(3,478) Lakhs PY) (Note 25, p.147), and NestAway
contributed the single largest share of Group comprehensive loss this year
(₹(1,165) Lakhs, 28%). Helloworld's Plan IV (Note 15.b, p.148-149) shows an even
higher weighted-average grant-date fair value of ₹6,012 per option, for a subsidiary
that is also loss-making (₹(1,015) Lakhs comprehensive loss, 24% of Group total) with
net worth of ₹(1,742) Lakhs (Note 25, p.147). 🟡 Watch — the independent valuers are
evidently pricing these subsidiaries on a forward/platform basis wholly disconnected
from book net worth; plausible for early-stage assets but the AR gives no
reconciliation, and this bears directly on whether the goodwill carried against these
same two entities (Pass 1 #3, B4 above) is realistically supportable. Question for
management.

### B11. K2V2 ESOP (Plan II) disclosure contains an implausible input and a future date
Note 15.b, Plan II (p.148): the Black-Scholes input table lists a "Risk free interest
rate" of 43.97% used across grant dates spanning "Oct-21" through "Oct-25" — the
latter a date in the future relative to the FY25 balance sheet date, and 43.97% is not
a plausible risk-free rate for any real-world instrument in this period. Financially
immaterial (Plan II impact nil FY25, ₹1 Lakh FY24) but a disclosure-quality/data-entry
flag. 🟡 Watch, low materiality.

### B12. Unexplained ₹447 Lakh asset write-off
Note 18 (Other expenses, consolidated, p.150-151): a new line, "Assets written off
₹447 Lakhs" (nil PY), with no further note detail on which assets, which subsidiary,
or the circumstances. Occurs in a year with multiple corporate restructuring events
(K2V2's Beyond Walls/Kylas divestiture, SPV deconsolidation). 🟡 Watch — question for
management on composition.

### B13. New recurring finance-cost line: interest on delayed statutory dues
Note 16 (Finance costs, consolidated, p.150): "Interest on delayed payment of
statutory dues ₹4 Lakhs (₹2 Lakhs PY)" — small but present in both years, indicating
recurring late payment of GST/TDS/PF-class statutory dues somewhere in the Group.
🟡 Watch, low materiality, recurrence noted.

### B14. K2V2 stake percentage discrepancy — Note 29's "81.27%" directly read and
confirmed as the outlier
Pass 1 flagged a numeric discrepancy between the standalone Note 4.a.1(i)/Note 21(D)(i)
figure (81.94%) and consolidated Note 29 (which Pass 1 read as "81.27%"). Having now
read consolidated Note 29 directly in full (p.157): "the Company increased its stake
in K2V2 to 81.27% from 44.44% for additional investment of ₹112.30 Lakhs" — confirmed
verbatim. This is corroborated as a genuine AR-internal inconsistency: three other
tables (standalone Note 4.a.1(i) p.103, standalone Note 21(D)(i) — implicitly via the
consolidated Note 1 table p.129, and the Ind AS 27 disclosure table p.103) all
consistently state 81.94%, leaving Note 29's 81.27% as the clear outlier, not a
transcription error in Pass 1. 🟡 Watch, confirmed.

### B15. Nominal-value disposal of three step-down subsidiaries
Note 27, consolidated (p.157): NestAway sold its entire shareholding in City Synapse
Information Pvt Ltd, Eukleia Technologies Pvt Ltd, and Nestassist Services Pvt Ltd
for a combined sale consideration of ₹1.31 Lakhs (~₹131,000) in March 2024 — a token/
nominal-value disposal of three step-down subsidiaries. Consistent with these being
loss-making shells, but the specific figure (three companies sold for ~₹1.31 Lakhs
combined) is new context on how the Group prunes its cap table. 🟢 Clean/
informational.

### B16. "Kylas Technologies Private Limited" name collision, unclarified in the AR
The Group carries a nil-value (₹0 Lakhs, both years) FVTOCI equity stake in "Kylas
Technologies Private Limited" (Note 4.a.1/4.a.2, standalone p.102 and consolidated
p.137) — an entity distinct from, but confusingly similarly named to, the "Kylas"
business unit that K2V2 divested during FY25 as part of the "Beyond Walls and Kylas"
business-unit sale referenced in Note 29 (p.157). The AR does not clarify whether
these are related. 🟡 Watch — disclosure ambiguity, question for management.

### B17. Group-level liquidity-risk boilerplate not obviously reconciled with the
scale of lease cash obligations
Consolidated Note 24.c (p.156) repeats the same "the Group consistently generated
sufficient cash flows from operations to meet its financial obligations as and when
they fall due" language used at standalone level (A4 above). Total cash outflow for
leases alone was ₹7,050 Lakhs in FY25 (Note 3.b(v), p.135) against net cash generated
from operating activities of ₹2,768 Lakhs (Consolidated Cash Flow Statement, p.128) —
lease payments alone exceeded operating cash generation by roughly 2.5x, with the
shortfall funded through financing activities (₹1,846 Lakhs net) rather than
operations. 🟡 Watch — the "sufficient cash flow from operations" claim is not
self-evidently supported by the cash flow statement once lease obligations (the
Group's dominant fixed-obligation category per Pass 1 #6) are isolated.
