## SECTION 1: MARKET DEFINITION

### 1A Precise boundaries

**Product scope (IN):** LT power cables, LT aerial-bunched (AB) cables, HT
cables up to 33 kV, MVCC (medium-voltage covered conductor), aluminium
conductors (AAC/AAAC/ACSR), aluminium and copper winding wires/strips
(transformer-OEM intermediate). Susan's HT capability is explicitly capped
at 33 kV (Infomerics report p.83, printed p.82: "HT cables up to 33 kV").

**Product scope (OUT):** telecom/fibre-optic cables, residential/domestic
building wire, industrial control/instrumentation cables, solar DC and
EV-specific cables, HV/EHV cables above 33 kV, transformers/switchgear and
other electrical equipment, and the aluminium rod/wire/ingot TRADING line
(commodity resale of unprocessed metal — RHP p.24 calls this segment
low-margin with "relatively low" entry barriers; it has no product-fit or
capability moat and is sized separately below, not folded into TAM/SAM/SOM).

**Geographic scope:** India only. Susan has zero forex earnings/outgo (AR
Board's Report, per B03/B04) — no export revenue to net out. Within India,
Susan is vendor-registered with state utilities in Uttar Pradesh (PVVNL),
Haryana, Telangana, Jharkhand, Bihar, Madhya Pradesh and Rajasthan
(Infomerics p.83), operating from an NCR (Ghaziabad/Delhi) manufacturing
base. Infomerics' own SWOT flags "Geographical Concentration... limits
depth of penetration in western and southern markets" (Infomerics p.84,
printed p.83).

**Customer scope:** State DISCOMs/utilities (tender-based), EPC contractors
(named in the Q1 FY27 call: Monte Carlo, RVNL), and transformer OEMs (for
winding wire). Order book at 30-Jun-2026 is ~50:50 government/private
(Q1 FY27 call, p.4). Excluded: telecom operators, retail/residential buyers,
export customers.

**Channel scope:** Direct tender bidding to utilities plus EPC subcontract
supply (B2B, institutional). Excluded: the dealer/distributor retail channel
that Infomerics describes serving "MSME converters, retail housing, and
small contractors" (Infomerics p.45, printed p.44) — Susan's disclosed order
book shows no participation in this channel.

**Price/spec segment:** Mid-market, BIS-certified, standard utility
specification. Not the ultra-low-cost uncertified segment (Infomerics:
"widespread unorganized market supplying non-certified or low-specification
products" — RHP p.124-125 area), and not a premium/branded consumer segment
(Susan has no B2C brand).

### 1B Management's own TAM claim

Susan management states no standalone numeric TAM figure anywhere in the
corpus. The investor decks are image-heavy with no market-size text (B00
note; grep of all four presentation .txt files returns no TAM/market-size
sentence). The Q1 FY27 call gives no addressable-market number, only a
company-specific capacity-linked figure ("peak capex reference potential
of ~₹700 to 800 Cr in revenue" — transcript p.4 — carried into Section 3C,
not treated as a TAM claim).

The closest thing to a management TAM claim is structural: the RHP's own
"Our Industry" section (Section III, pp.115-140) reproduces the
company-commissioned Infomerics figures verbatim and unqualified — the
**Indian Wires and Cables Market, USD 10.32 billion (₹98,498 Cr) in 2025E
growing to USD 22.35 billion (₹2,13,317 Cr) by 2035P, CAGR 8.02%** (RHP
p.119; Infomerics p.43). Date: RHP filed 08-Jun-2026; Infomerics report
labelled "2026 (IPO)". This figure is presented as the industry Susan
operates in, with no disaggregation to the voltage class, product type, or
geography Susan can actually serve.

Credibility read: **BROAD**. It is company-commissioned (NON-ANCHORED per
B00 input_gaps), it is the WHOLE India wires-and-cables market (including
telecom/fibre, residential wire and EHV cables Susan cannot supply), and it
is presented adjacent to the company's own growth narrative without ever
stating the serviceable fraction. Held for the Section 2 triangulation
below (mgmt_claim_ratio).

## SECTION 2: TAM ESTIMATION, MULTIPLE METHODS

### Method 1 — Top-down

Start: India Wires & Cables market, USD 10.32bn 2025E = ₹98,498 Cr
(10.32 × 95.4437 = 984.98bn INR = ₹98,498 Cr); USD 22.35bn 2035P =
₹2,13,317 Cr; CAGR 8.02% (RHP p.119, Infomerics p.43).

Subtraction 1 — non-power segments (telecom/fibre, residential/real-estate,
industrial control/instrumentation, consumer appliance, renewables/EV
niche cables): Infomerics' own end-consumer breakdown states "Power T&D
Utilities: Approximately 60% of cable demand through RDSS/SAUBHAGYA
projects. MV and HT cables form the core..." (Infomerics p.45). Applying
this 60% filter:
- 2025E: 60% × ₹98,498 Cr = **₹59,099 Cr**
- 2035P: 60% × ₹2,13,317 Cr = **₹1,27,990 Cr**

Caveat: this 60% split is sourced from the SAME company-commissioned
Infomerics document as the parent number — Method 1 is single-vendor at
its core, cross-checked independently in Methods 2/3/5 below.

Subtraction 2 (attempted, NOT FOUND) — within the 60% T&D-relevant slice,
netting out HV/EHV cables above 33 kV that Susan structurally cannot supply
(its HT line caps at 33 kV, Infomerics p.83). No voltage-class value split
of the India cable market exists in the RHP, the Infomerics report, or in a
targeted web check. This is flagged as a genuine gap: the ₹59,099 Cr figure
is best read as an UPPER-END top-down anchor for the cable/conductor slice,
not a precision estimate net of EHV.

Winding wire is a SEPARATE market in Infomerics' own taxonomy (not counted
inside "wires and cables") — see Method 5 for its India-specific derivation,
added back at the end of this section.

### Method 2 — Bottom-up (partial: aluminium conductor sub-segment only)

Addressable unit: 1 tonne of aluminium conductor (AAC/AAAC/ACSR) produced
in India. Volume: 170.39 thousand tonnes, FY2024-25 (RHP p.120, CMIE
Outlook — independent of Infomerics). Price: ₹128-142/kg quoted for
mid-tier ACSR (web search, generic trade-listing pages, undated — LOW
confidence, no verified OEM/wholesale contract price found).

Value = 170,390 tonnes × ₹1,30,000-1,42,000/tonne = **₹2,215-2,420 Cr**.

This is a genuine independent-source bottom-up figure but covers ONE
product line only (bare aluminium conductor), not the insulated LT/HT/MVCC
cable lines that carry most of Susan's manufacturing revenue. Read as a
sanity floor, not a competing TAM estimate: a market this small for
conductors alone is consistent with Method 1's much larger cable-inclusive
figure, since insulated cable carries far higher value-add per km than bare
conductor. No material divergence — the two methods are measuring different
slices of the same industry.

consistent, not divergent — Method 3's raw sum is low only because the
enumeration is structurally incomplete, not because the true market is
smaller than Method 1 implies.

### Method 4 — Import substitution

RHP trade data (CMIE Outlook, RHP p.120-121): wires & cables imports rose
from USD 1.01bn (FY17-18) to USD 1.49bn (FY24-25) = **₹14,220 Cr** at
FY24-25 (1.49 × 95.4437). Exports rose faster, USD 0.87bn → USD 2.34bn over
the same period. The RHP itself states imports "largely comprise copper
and aluminium insulated conductors, fibre-optic cables, specialised MV/HV
power cables, and project-specific configurations that require advanced
technologies or are not yet produced domestically at scale" (RHP p.120) —
i.e., the import basket skews toward EHV/fibre categories Susan cannot
supply given its 33 kV HT ceiling. Import substitution is therefore a real
but largely INDIRECT tailwind for Susan (via PLI/Make-in-India localisation
policy generally) rather than a direct, quantifiable TAM addition to
Susan's own serviceable slice. No split of the ₹14,220 Cr import basket by
product/voltage class exists in the corpus → NOT FOUND for a Susan-specific
number.

### Method 5 — Global benchmark

Global Wires & Cables market: USD 242.68bn (₹23,16,230 Cr) 2025E → USD
402.54bn (₹38,41,990 Cr) 2035P, CAGR 5.18% (Infomerics p.42). India's
2025E share of the global market = 10.32/242.68 = **4.25%**, rising to
22.35/402.54 = **5.55%** by 2035P (consistent with India's forecast CAGR
8.02% running ~2.8x the global 5.18% rate — a genuine underpenetration
story, not merely a base-effect artifact, since the share itself is
projected to rise).

Global winding wire market: USD 11.91bn (2025E) → USD 21.51bn (2035P),
CAGR 6.09% (RHP p.122; Infomerics p.51). No India-specific winding-wire
figure is disclosed anywhere in the corpus. DERIVED (benchmark-implied,
LOW confidence, explicitly not a disclosed number): ratio of global winding
wire to global W&C market = 11.91/242.68 = 4.909% (2025E); applying this
ratio to the India W&C figure: 10.32bn × 4.909% = USD 0.5066bn = **₹4,835
Cr** (2025E implied India winding wire market). At 2035P the same ratio
method (21.51/402.54 = 5.344%) gives 22.35bn × 5.344% = USD 1.194bn =
**₹11,402 Cr**.

Aggressive benchmark (dream scenario, clearly speculative): if India's
share of the global W&C market converged to its population share of the
world (~1.45bn/8.1bn ≈ 17.9%), the 2035P Indian market would be 17.9% ×
USD 402.54bn = USD 72.05bn ≈ **₹6,87,700 Cr** — roughly 3.2x the Infomerics
base case. Shown only as an upper aspirational bound, not used in the
triangulation table below.

## SECTION 3: SAM & SOM

### 3A SAM

Base: Realistic TAM 2025E = ₹63,934 Cr.

**Filter 1 — Product fit**: already built into the TAM base (Method 1's
60% T&D filter + Method 5's winding-wire add are both product-scoped to
what Susan maks). No further quantitative cut applied here; the unresolved
EHV/HV gap (Section 2, Method 1) means the base may still be modestly
generous — carried as a caveat, not a further haircut, since no source
supports a specific percentage.

**Filter 2 — Geography**: Susan's vendor registrations cover 7 states (UP,
Haryana, Telangana, Jharkhand, Bihar, MP, Rajasthan). Using each state's
approximate share of India's population as a proxy for regional cable/T&D
demand (population data, general knowledge, NOT a disclosed cable-spend
split — flagged LOW-confidence approximation): UP ~17%, Bihar ~8.7%, MP
~6%, Rajasthan ~5.6%, Telangana ~2.9%, Jharkhand ~2.7%, Haryana ~2.1% ≈
**45%** combined.
SAM = 45% × ₹63,934 Cr = **₹28,770 Cr**.

**Filter 3 — Channel**: the TAM base (Power T&D Utilities-relevant slice)
is already institutional/tender-channel demand, matching Susan's direct
B2B tender model. No further cut (Susan doesn't serve the dealer/retail
channel Infomerics separately describes, but that channel is not counted
in the 60% T&D slice to begin with).

**Filter 4 — Customer**: DISCOM/EPC/transformer-OEM customer types already
match the TAM base's customer composition (order book ~50:50 govt/private,
Q1 FY27 call, consistent with the T&D-utility skew). No further
quantitative cut; qualitatively satisfied.

**Filter 5 — Capability**: BIS certification + the 7-state vendor
registration are already reflected in the geography filter. The true
binding capability constraint is manufacturing CAPACITY, which is a
volume/output ceiling, not a market-access ceiling — tested in 3C below
rather than cut here.

**SAM = ₹28,770 Cr (2025E), = 45.0% of realistic TAM.**

### 3B SOM at 3 and 5 years

Current SAM share = FY26 manufacturing revenue ÷ SAM = ₹160.83 Cr ÷ ₹28,770
Cr = **0.56%**.

Share-gain rules (per stage instruction): normal 1-2pp in 3 years;
aggressive 3-5pp with capacity+execution; >5pp only on competitor exit/
acquisition. Applying the LOWER bound of each band (conservative bias):

- Normal (+1pp over 3yr): share → 1.56%. SAM at year 3 (SAM grows with TAM
  at ~8%/yr, filter held constant): ₹28,770 × 1.08³ = ₹36,242 Cr.
  SOM_3yr(normal) = 1.56% × ₹36,242 Cr = **₹566 Cr**.
- Aggressive (+3pp over 3yr, capacity+execution): share → 3.56%.
  SOM_3yr(aggressive) = 3.56% × ₹36,242 Cr = **₹1,290 Cr**.

Cross-checked against Section 3C's capacity ceiling below, the aggressive
scenario is NOT capacity-feasible within 3 years. The reported SOM_3yr uses
management's own capacity-linked reference instead (see 3C): **SOM_3yr =
₹700 Cr** — a figure that sits between the "normal" share-gain outcome
(₹566 Cr) and the "aggressive" one (₹1,290 Cr), consistent with a
capacity-led ramp (+60% nameplate) that structurally supports more than a
normal 1-2pp gain but is capped well short of the mechanical aggressive
share-gain math.

**SOM_5yr**: no further capacity expansion beyond the Feb-2027 12,000 km/yr
round is disclosed anywhere in the corpus. Allowing for modest
de-bottlenecking/efficiency gains (5-10%) beyond nameplate over years 4-5,
without a fresh capex round: **SOM_5yr = ₹750 Cr**.

Implied SOM revenue CAGR (manufacturing-segment base only, FY26 = ₹160.83
Cr — the FORMAL handoff to Stage 11):
- 3yr: (700/160.83)^(1/3) − 1 = **~63%**
- 5yr: (750/160.83)^(1/5) − 1 = **~36%**

The CAGR tapering from year 3 to year 5 (63% → 36% annualised) reflects a
front-loaded ramp as the new capacity comes online (Feb-2027) followed by a
plateau at the capacity ceiling absent a further announced expansion — not
a demand slowdown. Stage 11 should treat the yr3 figure as this run's
central near-term growth cross-check and the yr5 figure as capacity-bound,
NOT market-bound.

### 3C Capacity cross-check

Installed capacity: 7,500 km/yr (current) → 12,000 km/yr (+60%) from
Feb-2027, funded primarily by the IPO capex object (₹10.30 Cr, ₹1,029.49
Lakh from net proceeds) plus FY27 company-wide capex guidance of ₹15-20 Cr
(Q1 FY27 call, Q5 answer). Utilisation as of 30-Jun-2026: only ₹1.20 Cr of
₹10.30 Cr (11.7%) spent, per the Monitoring Agency Report (Brickwork
Ratings, 12-Aug-2026) — "No deviation" flagged, but the pace is thin with
roughly 8 months left to the Feb-2027 target.

Management's own reference point: "Peak capex reference potential of ~₹700
to 800 Cr in revenue" tied to this capacity round (Q1 FY27 call, p.4),
framed against a "2-3 years" horizon to "reach a much larger scale"
(transcript p.3).

**Cross-check result**: the mechanical aggressive share-gain SOM_3yr
(₹1,290 Cr, Section 3B) exceeds management's own peak-capacity reference
(₹700-800 Cr) by **₹490-590 Cr**. The market itself is not the constraint —
SAM (₹28,770 Cr) is roughly 179x Susan's current manufacturing revenue, so
demand absorption headroom is not remotely scarce. The constraint is
Susan's own capacity and its execution: only 11.7% of the Unit-III capex
object spent 8 months before the commissioning target, and the HT/MVCC
mix-shift guidance underpinning the higher-value ramp is internally
inconsistent within the SAME call (~15% vs ~50% of FY27-end revenue — B05
flag, HIGH severity). **The capex/execution plan is the side to doubt in
this cross-check, not the market-absorption assumption.**

### 5B Runway assessment

Revenue headroom = SAM ÷ current revenue. On a manufacturing-only basis
(consistent with the SAM definition): ₹28,770 Cr ÷ ₹160.83 Cr = **178.9x**.
(On a whole-company basis including the trading line: ₹28,770 Cr ÷ ₹269.36
Cr = 106.8x — shown as an alternative, not the headline figure, since SAM
excludes trading by construction.)

TAM growth rate: **~8.0%** (Method 1/blended realistic CAGR, Section 2).

Company CAGR vs TAM: Susan's own manufacturing-plus-trading revenue grew
from ₹103.48 Cr (FY24) to ₹269.36 Cr (FY26), a ~61% 2-year CAGR, and Q1
