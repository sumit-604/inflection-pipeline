## 1. NARRATIVE

1. Indosolar runs one solar module plant of 1.3 GW at Noida. Waaree Energies, India's largest module maker, owns the controlling stake.
2. The plant earns money two ways. In tolling, it converts a customer's material for a fee. In own manufacturing, it buys material and sells its own modules.
3. Tolling gives a high margin and needs little cash. Own manufacturing gives more revenue per module but a thinner margin and a large stock.
4. This quarter the company moved most volume to own manufacturing. Revenue was 155.6 crore, down 23% from a year ago.
5. Profit fell much harder. Operating profit (EBITDA: profit before interest, tax and depreciation) was 14.8 crore against 70.7 crore. Net profit was 5.0 crore against 46.3 crore.
6. Two causes share the blame. Thinner spreads on module sales explain about three-fifths of the margin fall. The lost tolling fees explain about two-fifths.
7. Management blames regulation, geopolitics, high input costs and low use of plants across the industry. It gave no numbers and no guidance. No concall was held.
8. The good part: the company has no debt and holds 85 crore of cash. Cash flow for the half year was strong, at 1.9 times profit.
9. The worrying part: that cash flow came from one-off sources. Old customer dues were collected, and a liability of 90 crore appeared that no note explains. That liability is larger than the cash.
10. Stock rose ten times in six months, to 222 crore. The plant made 148 MW but sold only 98 MW. A fall in module prices can force a write-down.
11. The biggest fact is the merger. On 23-Sep-2026 the board approved a plan to merge Indosolar into Waaree Energies. Indosolar shares will then convert into Waaree shares.
12. The exchange ratio is not in the documents this run read. Watch two things next: the ratio and the profit year behind it, and Q3 operating profit per MW sold.
