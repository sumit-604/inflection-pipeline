### 2C. Growth embedded in capex — the arithmetic

- Forward (not-yet-spent) capex under execution as of this run date: AR's 3-yr core plan (Rs 300
  Cr, FY25-27) less Rs 73.27 Cr spent FY25 (AR p.35) less ~Rs 100 Cr spent FY26 (Q4 FY26 call) =
  ~Rs 127 Cr core capex remaining for FY27, plus BESS Phase-1 Rs 150-200 Cr (Q3 FY26 call) of which
  ~Rs 70 Cr is allocated within FY27 (Q4 FY26 call) and the balance (~Rs 80-130 Cr) falls into
  FY28. Total forward capex under execution ≈ **Rs 277-327 Cr** (midpoint ≈ Rs 300 Cr).
- Historical fixed asset turnover (Revenue ÷ Net PPE): FY25 net PPE = Rs 270.63 Cr (Rs 27,062.65
  lakh, AR p.116, "Property, Plant and Equipment"); FY25 gross turnover = Rs 2,603.32 Cr (AR p.83).
  FAT = 2,603.32 ÷ 270.63 ≈ **9.6x**.
- Mechanical implied incremental revenue = Rs 300 Cr (forward capex) × 9.6x (historical FAT) ≈
  **Rs 2,880 Cr**, i.e. ≈ **110% above FY26 revenue** (Rs 2,948 Cr, Inv. Pres. p.23).
- **This figure is almost certainly overstated and is flagged as such, not adopted at face
  value.** The FAT multiplier is a blended ratio across legacy, fully-ramped assets (Sioplas/XLPE
  running at 87-89% utilisation) and reflects the business's genuinely asset-light compounding
  economics — it is not the marginal productivity of freshly-commissioned capacity. Management's
  own words on Bhiwadi confirm the gap: "we put up a capacity of 48,000 tons, knowing fully well
  that 48,000 tons will not be consumed... to achieve that scale... we have gone ahead... which
  should be fully utilized maybe [FY27, FY28]" (Rajesh Kothari, Q4 FY26 call). A more grounded
  cross-check is management's own multi-year target: FY30 core revenue Rs 5,000 Cr + BESS Rs
  2,000-2,500 Cr = Rs 7,000-7,500 Cr vs FY26's Rs 2,948 Cr, i.e. **+138% to +154% over ~4 years**
  (Inv. Pres. p.32) — a similar order of magnitude to the mechanical FAT estimate but spread over
  four years of ramp rather than realised immediately. **capex_embedded_growth_pct is reported as
  ~110% (mechanical, single-year FAT method) with this overstatement explicitly flagged.**

### 4C. Regulatory moat assessment

The HFFR public-infrastructure mandate and the BESS VGF allocation are both **active or
near-active, not merely emerging** — the mandate already shapes demand for an existing DDEV
product line, and the VGF is a budget-year mechanism the whole BESS supply chain can draw on. Time
to kick in: HFFR mandate already active; BESS VGF disbursement timing not specified in these

### 6A. Moat evolution timeline

  utilisation (📄, commissioned Apr-2026); BESS Phase-1 commissioning targeted H2 FY27 (🎙️, guided

  June 2026 (🎙️); FY27 core guidance (231,000 MT volume, ~13% revenue growth, ~11% EBITDA margin)
  as the next test of guidance credibility given the FY26 volume miss.
- **12-24 months**: BESS ramp toward the first 1 GWh milestone and Rs 800-900 Cr revenue run-rate
  (targeted FY28, 🎙️); 132 kV market stabilisation completing, clearing the path to formally begin
  the 220 kV certification clock (🎙️/📄).
- **24-36 months**: 220 kV certification cycle in progress (targeted completion ~FY2029-30, 🎙️);
  BESS scaling past 1 GWh with (if converted) signed Tier-1 developer contracts.
- **3-5 years**: FY30 core revenue target Rs 5,000 Cr plus BESS incremental Rs 2,000-2,500 Cr
  (🎙️, Inv. Pres. p.32); 220 kV commercial availability (🎙️); BESS at or approaching 5 GWh design
  capacity (🎙️).

### 6B. Risks to the top-scoring emerging moats

- **B2 (qualification lock-in, 4.0)**: risk is that the barrier is real but not exclusive — new
  PVC entrants (Adani, UltraTech) show that well-capitalised majors can enter adjacent segments;
  if any of them pursue backward integration into compounding, or a global compounder licenses
  technology to a competitor, the lock-in erodes faster than the 3-4 year certification cycle
  would suggest. Early warning: any disclosed new domestic XLPE/HFFR compounding capacity from a
  materially-capitalised entrant.
- **G1 (war chest, 4.0)**: risk is that the same capex cycle funding this war chest is already
  straining working capital (Block B deal-breaker, WC days +18.63 FY24-FY26, per B01). If
  inventory/receivables continue to build faster than CFO generation, the net-debt-free buffer
  could compress even without new debt being drawn. Early warning: further WC-day deterioration in
  FY27 results, or a rise in fund-based facility utilisation beyond the ~31% average cited in CRISIL's
  rating rationale (per B01 data notes).
- **R1 (regulatory tailwinds, 2.8)**: both the HFFR mandate and the BESS VGF are policy-dependent
  and shared sector-wide, not DDEV-specific; a change in scheme design, disbursement delay, or
  dilution of the HFFR mandate's enforcement would remove the tailwind without DDEV having done
  anything wrong. Early warning: BESS VGF disbursement delays reported industry-wide, or absence of
  signed customer MOUs for BESS beyond the "discussions" stage disclosed as of Q4 FY26.
- **F2 (execution moat, 1.4, the weakest of the active rows)**: the FY26 volume miss and the
  twice-revised BESS guidance are the clearest evidence that management's forward numbers should be
  treated with a discount, which directly undercuts confidence in the BESS and 220 kV timelines
  cited throughout this scan.
