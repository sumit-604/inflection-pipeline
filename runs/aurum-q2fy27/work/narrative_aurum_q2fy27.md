# Aurum Proptech: Q2 FY27 in plain words

Source: the committed review, the A5 audit and work/notion-thesis.md (run aurum-q2fy27, commit 7918b8fa). No agent was re-run.

1. **What the company does.** Aurum sells software and leads to property developers. It also runs co-living homes and rental platforms. A small third arm wants to launch a small REIT. In September it bought Housing.com, a large property portal, and paid in its own shares.

2. **How this compares with our earlier view.** We first looked at Aurum on 14 July 2026. We said AVOID, because the price was far above our value, and set an entry zone of Rs 71 to 89. Our main tripwire was simple: two quarters in a row of losses in the core business, once one-off income is taken out. Q1 was the first strike. This quarter was the second strike. So that tripwire has now fired.

3. **What happened this quarter.** Sales rose about 64% on a year ago. Most of the rise came from the developer business, and most of that likely came from PropTiger, which Aurum bought last year. The company does not split out how much is real organic growth.

4. **What improved.** The core loss is much smaller than a year ago. It went from about Rs 11 Cr to about half a crore. The rental business is now near breakeven before lease interest. Reported profit was positive for the second quarter running.

5. **What got worse, or stayed bad.** The core business still lost money once other income is removed. The company also moved Rs 3.7 Cr of its own staff cost onto the balance sheet. If that cost were counted as an expense, the loss would be about Rs 4 Cr. The developer arm looks profitable, but its margin falls below our 12% line once head office cost is shared out. Co-living beds fell 14%.

6. **Cash and balance sheet.** Cash from operations looks better, but part of it was a tax refund. Most of the rise in cash came from selling the old building and from cash that came with Housing.com. Goodwill and other intangibles now equal almost all of shareholders' equity. That is a lot of value resting on accounting judgement.

7. **What management said.** The press release calls this a fourth straight quarter of profit. We can confirm only two of those quarters, and both are losses on the core test. Management talks about an AI operating system and about joining Housing.com to the group. It gives no dates and no numbers for either. Housing.com will appear in the results from next quarter.

8. **The red flags in plain words.** No document tells us whether Housing.com makes money, so we cannot tell if the price was fair. One loan of about Rs 9.5 Cr earns about 42% a year, and the borrower is not named. This matters, because our record has a standing worry about loans to the promoter company. The promoter's warrants are priced near the market, but the promoter has paid only a quarter of the money. It can walk away from the rest. The REIT still has no first scheme, more than three years after its licence.

9. **Open items.** The audit passed after two rounds of fixes. Only small wording notes remain. Three of your rulings are still open: whether the warrant price passes your tracker test, whether to rebase the house case to the larger share count, and what monitoring item 9 covers. Our goodwill baseline also needs fixing. Notion says Rs 174 Cr, but the audited March figure is Rs 207 Cr. Both auditor ID numbers were unreadable in the scan, so they need a check.

10. **What this means for the thesis.** The AVOID view holds and is firmer. The core business has now lost money for two quarters in a row. The balance sheet now rests on a large purchase whose profit we cannot see. Nothing here moves Aurum toward a buy. Your decision status stays yours to set.

11. **What to watch next quarter.** The Q3 result is due by about mid-February 2027. Look at one number: core profit with other income removed, shown with and without Housing.com. It should also be shown with the capitalised staff cost counted as an expense. A third loss in a row confirms the bear case. Before that, the half-year related-party filing should show whether any money went to the promoter company. Zero is good. Anything above zero confirms the worry.
