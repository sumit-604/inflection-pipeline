Re-reading the notes a third time, laterally rather than sequentially, against
the six pattern prompts:

**1. Notes that contradict each other.** The clearest contradiction sits
between Pass 1's own initial characterisation of the standalone auditor note
("Deloitte Haskins & Sells (unchanged)," Standalone Note 21A, p.156) and the
Consolidated Auditor's Report ("consolidated financial statements... for the
year ended March 31, 2025, were audited by predecessor auditor," p.169) read
together with the Board's Report (27th AGM, 26 June 2025, appointed Deloitte
"for a consecutive term of five years," p.63). On direct re-check, the
apparent contradiction resolves to a Pass 1 drafting error, already corrected
in Pass 2: Deloitte is genuinely new for FY26 (first-year group audit engaged
in the same year as three acquisitions, a new hedge program, the TCPA
provision and the escalated TP dispute). Retained here as CONFIRMED, not a
residual document-level inconsistency — the "five-year consecutive term"
language is standard Companies Act rotation-appointment wording and does not
itself imply prior continuity. Second contradiction, genuinely internal to
the filing: the SAME fixed-price-contract revenue-recognition judgment that
is escalated to Key Audit Matter status at the consolidated level (p.168) is
explicitly NOT a KAM at the standalone level ("no key audit matters to
communicate," p.132) even though standalone revenue is 86.8% intercompany
(Standalone Note 27) and carries the identical judgment plus the added
transfer-pricing exposure. This is a document-internal divergence in
auditor risk assessment across the two audit opinions of the SAME year, by
the SAME firm, not explained anywhere in the filing. 🟡

**2. Numbers not matching the main statements.** The Note 12 consolidated
trade-receivables growth (+30.7% YoY, ₹9,818mn vs ₹7,514mn) appears at first
read to conflict with the Consolidated Cash Flow Statement's working-capital
line, which shows a small POSITIVE ₹35mn (inflow) against "(Increase)/
decrease in trade receivables" (p.173). This is not a data error: the CFS
classifies most of the receivables growth under "loans and advances and
other assets" (₹(231)mn outflow) rather than the narrower CFS "trade
receivables" caption, reflecting the unbilled/contract-asset component
(+77.5% YoY) sitting in a different CFS line than Note 12's combined "total
trade receivables" figure. Confirmed as a classification nuance on this
third pass, not a genuine inconsistency, but the two disclosures cannot be
read interchangeably. 🟡

**3. Deliberately minimal disclosure vs detail elsewhere.** Two items stand
out on the pattern re-read precisely because of the CONTRAST with how well
other notes are disclosed: (a) Note 26 segment reporting states in as many
words that segment assets/liabilities are "not currently practicable" to
disclose because they are "used interchangeably between segments" — yet the
SAME note discloses segment revenue and segment result to the rupee, and
Note 6 separately discloses goodwill BY CGU (including ₹330mn/₹33cr
allocated to the loss-making "Others" CGU). The asymmetry — P&L-level detail
paired with a flat refusal on balance-sheet-level detail for the one segment
that has lost money two years running — reads as a disclosure choice, not a
genuine data-availability constraint, given goodwill-by-CGU is already
tracked. (b) Note 33/28 quantifies the transfer-pricing dispute to the rupee
(₹1,114mn) but disposes of "various" other income-tax assessment matters
under appeal with no aggregate quantum at all — the same note that is
maximally precise on one contingent item is silent on the size of another
category in the same paragraph. Both patterns were individually flagged in
Passes 1–2; grouping them here shows a consistent house style of granular
disclosure where required by a specific standard (segment revenue, one
material TP matter) and minimal disclosure where a general standard leaves
room for judgment (segment balance sheet, the tax-appeal basket). 🟡

**4. Prior-year restatements/reclassifications.** Confirmed: Brand Activation
was merged into Enterprise Commercial Solutions (ECS) during FY26, reducing
reportable segments from three to two, with FY25 comparatives restated on
that basis (Note 20, p.194). Consistently, the Note 6 goodwill impairment
table uses "EMS" as the FY26 CGU label against "Omnichannel" as the FY25
label in the same comparative column — a reclassification, not a data
error, and internally consistent with the Note 20 segment merger. No
restatement of prior-year P&L or balance-sheet figures (as opposed to
segment/CGU relabelling) was found anywhere else in the notes. 🟢 (mechanical,
transparently cross-referenced).

**5. Events after balance sheet date that materially change the picture.**
Three post-year-end items, none individually altering the FY26 numbers but
each relevant to FY27 tracking: (a) final dividend ₹2.25/share proposed
29-Apr-2026, ≈₹542mn, subject to AGM approval (Note 42); (b) Cake
Kommunikations PPA remains provisional with a measurement-period deadline of
30-Jan-2027 — the goodwill/intangible split for the third FY26 acquisition
could still move (Note 7d); (c) the ADT-1 filing-defect remediation (GNL-1
filed 7-Jan-2026, Registrar action still pending "as on the reporting date")
and the RSU-allotment-deviation board resolution (29-Apr-2026) are both
open, unresolved compliance matters as of the report signing date (Board's
Report, pp.69–70). 🟡 for (b) and (c); 🟢 for (a) (routine, second
consecutive payout — see Pass 2 dividend-history resolution).

**6. Going concern language.** NOT FOUND anywhere in the Auditor's Report
(standalone or consolidated) or in the notes. No going-concern qualification,
emphasis of matter, or material uncertainty language of any kind. Consistent
with the net-cash, unlevered balance sheet (Note 28: Nil borrowings, Nil
gearing, both years). 🟢

## E. NOTES-BASED RED FLAGS

- TCPA class-action provision (₹203mn against a ₹417mn maximum), a genuine US conduct/litigation risk with residual uncertainty on final settlement quantum and whether the underlying unsolicited-marketing practice has been discontinued. (Note 37)
- Transfer-pricing dispute of ₹1,114mn (21.2% of FY26 PBT), unprovided, tied directly to the intercompany pricing structure underlying 86.8% of standalone revenue. (Note 33, Standalone Note 27)
- TriloDocs GmbH ₹135mn related-party convertible loan, 100% written off within roughly a year, with the counterparty's relationship to the Group NOT FOUND in the notes. (Note 8)
- Consolidated PAT declined 1.4% YoY while standalone PAT grew 16.9% — earnings drag concentrated entirely in international/acquired subsidiaries, a pattern that masks weakening consolidated-level quality behind a strong India-entity narrative. (Consol/Standalone P&L pp.172, 137)
- Contingent consideration liability grew roughly 19x to ₹2,914mn, entirely Level 3 (unobservable-input) fair value. (Note 16, 27a)
- Goodwill impairment-test terminal growth rate raised to a uniform 5.0% in the exact year goodwill tripled to ₹16,176mn — a less-conservative assumption change concurrent with the largest goodwill addition in the company's history. (Note 6)

No evidence found of revenue manipulation, improper capitalisation, or off-balance-sheet structuring. The red flags above are litigation/conduct, contingent-liability, and assumption-judgment risks, not accounting fraud indicators.

## F. ONE-LINE NOTES VERDICT

The notes reveal moderate accounting practices, clean on core mechanics but concentrated in judgment-heavy M&A-year risk. Key concern: three unprovided-to-fully-quantified contingent exposures (TP dispute ₹1,114mn, TCPA max ₹417mn, contingent consideration ₹2,914mn) landing in the same first-year-auditor cycle as a tripled goodwill base tested with a raised growth assumption. Key strength: net-cash, unlevered balance sheet with strongly improving cash conversion (OCF/PAT 1.62x consolidated, up from 1.09x) and no going-concern language anywhere. Overall accounting quality: 7/10.
