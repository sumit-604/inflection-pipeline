Mattress leader recovering from leveraged Kurlon integration

Sheela Foam makes Sleepwell and Kurlon mattresses and polyurethane foam, and the shares trade at Rs 807 for a market cap of Rs 8,812 Cr. Revenue splits roughly 55% domestic branded mattress and home comfort, 15% domestic technical and institutional foam, 30% international foam through Joyce Foam in Australia and Interplasp in Spain, and about 1% IT services through Staqo. FY26 consolidated PAT was Rs 161 Cr, up 78% on FY25, on operating cash flow of Rs 409 Cr. A mattress sells for about Rs 4,055 and a tonne of foam for about Rs 2.5 lakh.

The company is midway through absorbing two acquisitions, Kurlon in 2024 and the Furlenco furniture rental business, both funded with debt and a September 2023 QIP. Borrowings fell from Rs 1,493 Cr at the FY24 peak to Rs 908 Cr by FY26, a 39% reduction, and net debt fell Rs 156 Cr in FY26 alone. The Kurlon merger completed legally in January 2026. Management declared the first ever dividend in FY26 at 20% of face value, ten years after the 2016 listing. Alongside the deleveraging it is pushing premiumisation, an unorganized to organized dealer programme, and e-commerce.

Gate 0 scored 39 of 160 and classified AVOID, but that score is backward looking and mechanical. The low reading traces to the FY24 acquisition inflating capital employed and an FY25 ROCE trough of 5.0%, not to structural decline. PAT troughed at Rs 89 Cr in FY25 and recovered to Rs 161 Cr in FY26 as borrowings were repaid. The transition read, not the Gate 0 number, is the live question here.

Cash conversion is strong and improving. FY26 operating cash flow of Rs 409 Cr covered PAT of Rs 161 Cr 2.6 times, and free cash flow swung positive to Rs 212 Cr from negative Rs 22 Cr in FY23. Two items temper the reported profit quality. Receivables grew 27.7% to Rs 442 Cr against revenue growth near 11%, partly Kurlon consolidation. And Rs 144 Cr of the Rs 161 Cr FY26 PAT landed in the second half, lifted by a January 2026 depreciation policy change worth about Rs 14 Cr of PBT, a Furlenco fourth quarter step up, and raw material inventory timing.

Management delivers on financial headlines and slips on operational ones. It hit FY26 margin expansion, 78% PAT growth, the first dividend, and Furlenco revenue of exactly Rs 370 Cr. It also cut its FY26 showroom target three times, from 1,000 to 800 to 700, then added about 600. The Rs 40 to 60 Cr Kurlon synergy tail has slipped four consecutive quarters on the same overseas machine delay. Credibility grades B.

Peers confirm the demand shift and contradict the margin thesis. Wakefit, Centuryply and Greenlam all describe an unorganized to organized shift, which supports the U2O programme. But Wakefit's May 2026 call states competitive intensity and price matching have increased over the last six to nine months, against SFL's assumption that discipline improves as funded peers list. An analyst on the same call cited ten years of mattress industry EBITDA pool erosion. SFL's claimed raw material pass through of 25% to 35% also runs above the roughly 15% peers report.

The organized mattress and branded foam market SFL targets sizes at Rs 20,880 to 22,359 Cr, with an organized served market near Rs 7,000 Cr and SFL already at about 30% of it. Runway grades STRONG on a three times revenue headroom and a modelled 15.5% five year revenue CAGR. Management's Rs 1,00,000 Cr market claim runs 4.8 times the conservative estimate and reads as inflated.

The promoter record is clean on legal, regulatory and criminal checks, which is the decisive positive. Against it sit a decade of zero dividend to FY25 alongside family pay linked to up to 3.75% of profit, CFO and external CEO turnover during the Kurlon integration, and four long serving independent directors exiting on term limits in 2026 with replacements seated concurrently. Institutions did not enter on the turnaround, they declined, FII plus DII from about 31% in late 2023 to 24.7% by March 2026, and FII alone from 6.6% to 4.15%.
