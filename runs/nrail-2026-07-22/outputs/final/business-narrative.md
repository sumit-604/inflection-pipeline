Scaled paperboard bet delivered profit not cash

N R Agarwal Industries makes packaging board and writing and printing paper from recovered fibre, run by the Agarwal family out of Gujarat, selling to converters, printers and institutional buyers, mostly inside India. FY26 revenue was Rs 2,145 Cr, up 29% on the year, split roughly 62% packaging board and 38% writing and printing paper. Installed capacity is around 500,000 MT a year. It is a single reportable segment with no named competitors disclosed and no product level margin split.

The company doubled its paperboard capacity in the FY24 cycle, spending close to Rs 1,000 Cr, most of it borrowed, and commissioned a new 900 TPD plant that ran at about 95% utilisation in its first year. It built that capacity at roughly Rs 40,000 a tonne against a greenfield benchmark near Rs 1,00,000 a tonne. FY26 was the payoff year. Revenue rose 29%, PBT rose 274% to Rs 60 Cr, PAT rose 148% to Rs 44 Cr, EPS more than doubled to Rs 25.68, and the March quarter swung to a Rs 14 Cr profit against a loss a year earlier.

The backward screen still fails. Net debt to EBITDA sits near 4x and interest coverage near 2x, both breached in FY25 and again in FY26. FY25 was the trough, and once Rs 19 Cr of investment fair value gains and asset sale profits are stripped out, the core paper business likely ran a small pre tax loss that year. Finance costs stepped up sharply as construction interest stopped being capitalised on commissioning, and interest coverage fell from 17.6x to 2.3x.

Cash conversion is the open question and it did not follow the profit. Trade receivables more than doubled in FY25 while revenue grew 28%, draining about Rs 100 Cr of operating cash against Rs 18 Cr of net profit. Operating cash flow then fell 46% in FY26 to Rs 95 Cr even as revenue grew 29%, and Rs 294 Cr of capex swung free cash flow to about minus Rs 199 Cr. A payables spike financed through bank acceptances offsets the receivables build on paper, so reported working capital actually shrank, which makes the true picture harder to read rather than easier.

Management said Rs 2,200 Cr and delivered Rs 2,145 Cr, which is 97.5% of the target and a narrow miss. It said the new plant would drive a profit recovery and it did, and it paid the exact dividend it promised, confirmed in the cash flow statement. That earns a credibility grade of B, held there by a no concall ceiling and by a Rs 294 Cr capex program that carries zero explanation in the filed results. The FY26 profit was also concentrated in the March quarter, which alone was a third of the year, so the durability of the recovery is not yet proven.

Governance is the sharp caveat. The Income Tax Department ran a search and seizure at company premises, plant sites and directors' residences on 2 September 2025, and the outcome is undisclosed. Promoters' entire shareholding was pledged for the PNB term loan at FY25 year end, and secondary sources put total multi lender encumbrance near 99% through mid 2026, though that figure is not confirmed at a primary filing. An all promoter finance committee with no independent directors oversees a treasury book near Rs 100 Cr, and the annual report carries three inconsistent figures for promoter director pay. Against that, there is no promoter selling, the ICRA rating held at A minus with adequate liquidity, a credentialed independent director was added in September 2025, and prior meeting cadence compliance gaps were remediated in FY26.

Peers confirm some of the frame and contradict two parts of it. The peer set is imperfect, since JK Paper runs virgin fibre and Kuantum runs wood and agro fibre, and neither sources recovered paper the way NRAIL does. Peers do confirm an industry wide capex race running through the same downturn, and demand tied to India consumption growth. Peers contradict two of NRAIL's own framings. JK Paper management rejects the idea that smaller mills are closing because of import dumping, attributing closures to their own inefficiencies. JK Paper also frames coated board as structurally import dependent at 50% to 60% imported and expected to stay that way, not an import substitution gap for domestic players to capture.

The runway is real but the headline market number is inflated. On a conservative bottom up build the realistic addressable market is about Rs 61,500 Cr, the served market about Rs 33,300 Cr, and current share sits near 4%, leaving roughly 25x revenue headroom and an implied revenue CAGR near 20% to reach the served share. Management's own cited market of Rs 1,17,245 Cr reads 2.1x that estimate and likely bundles kraft and corrugated segments NRAIL does not make. The optimistic share case at the five year mark also exceeds current installed capacity by Rs 2,400 Cr to Rs 2,600 Cr, so it depends on a second unit that is not yet confirmed in any primary filing.
