## SECTION 1: THE BUSINESS MODEL IN PLAIN ENGLISH

### 1A. One-line description

Visaka makes four things in factories and sells them through dealers: cement
roofing sheets for rural roofs, fibre-cement boards for modern interiors and
walls, synthetic yarn for fabric mills, and a newer solar roof panel. Three
are mature manufacturing lines that behave like commodity converters; one
(boards) is a genuine growth engine; one (solar roof, ATUM) is still an
unproven side bet.

### 1E. The chai-stall-uncle version

Imagine a shop that sells four different things under one roof. Roof
sheets for far-off village homes, sold cheap and in bulk through a huge
dealer network, decades old and reliable but not exciting. Fancy cement
boards for city apartments and offices, the shop's new star product,
selling out as fast as it is made. Yarn for cloth mills, a side business
tied to global fashion trends that goes up and down with the world
economy. And a brand-new solar roof panel that the owner is proud of but
that customers are still slow to buy. The shop makes good money from the
roof sheets, great money from the boards when they can make enough, okay
money from yarn when times are good, and almost no disclosed money yet
from the solar roof.

### 3A. Ignore-these, track-these

| Commonly tracked ratio | Why it MISLEADS or is IRRELEVANT here |
|---|---|
| Consolidated blended net profit margin (5.29% FY26) | Blends a declining commodity line (roofing), a growth line (boards) and a cyclical line (yarn) with very different structural margins; masks which engine is actually improving (AR FY26 p.30) |

## SECTION 4: RISKS, VALUATION APPROACH & MONITORING

### 4A. Business-model-specific risks

| Category | Risk | First financial line item that would deteriorate |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue model | Rural income cycle weakens further, roofing volumes fall | Cement roofing segment sales/volume growth turning negative (AR FY26 p.51 table) |
| Margin | Imported fibre or PET cost spikes and cannot be passed through | Cost of materials consumed rising above the FY26 run-rate of ~50% of standalone revenue (AR FY26 Note 26, p.156) |
| Balance sheet | Rs450cr boards capex (2030 plan) is debt-funded faster than demand materialises | Net debt/EBITDA rising back above the FY26 level of 2.19x, or debt-equity above 0.42 (AR FY26 p.31) |
| Execution | New boards capacity (plant "nearing commissioning") comes online but utilisation does not hold | Boards capacity utilisation falling below the FY26 level of 86% even as installed capacity rises (AR FY26 p.55) |
| Structural | Asbestos regulatory action tightens faster than the company's "gradual" transition assumption; separately, ATUM/subsidiary losses continue indefinitely | A sudden, non-gradual drop in cement roofing segment revenue; or further impairment of the Rs7.795cr cost basis in Atum Life Private Limited, already carrying only Rs0.32cr net worth (AR FY26 Note 44, p.272-273) |

### 4B. Valuation method applicability

| Method | Applicable? | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Segment-wise (SOTP) EV/EBITDA or P/E, keyed to Section 1B destination PE per line's archetype | **PRIMARY** | The company is not one business. Roofing behaves like a commodity converter (Amendment 17 territory), boards behaves like a value-added/spec'd supplier, yarn behaves like a commodity converter in a different (textile) sector, and ATUM is an undisclosed-revenue optionality. A single blended multiple hides that the growth engine (boards, 30% of revenue) and the legacy engine (roofing, 53% of revenue) deserve different multiples. Section 1B governs each destination multiple; this stage only flags that the blend needs decomposing, it does not compute the multiple |
| Consolidated EV/EBITDA (blended) | **SECONDARY cross-check** | Useful sanity check against peers on a whole-company basis, and captures the balance-sheet deleveraging story (net debt/EBITDA 2.19x FY26 vs 4.66x FY25) that a pure P/E view would miss (AR FY26 p.31) |
| Replacement cost / asset-based, on the boards capex programme | **TERTIARY** | The Rs450cr capex-to-2030 plan (AR FY26 p.33) gives a concrete floor/anchor for what new boards capacity costs to build, useful as a cross-check on whether the market is pricing the expansion at all |
| DCF | Not applicable as primary | Multi-segment cash flows with materially different growth/margin trajectories and a live capex programme make a single blended discount-rate DCF fragile without first separating segments; better handled inside the SOTP framework at Stage 11 |
| Dividend Discount Model | Not applicable | Payout ratio swung from 31.5% (FY23) to 3,085.71% (FY25, an EPS-denominator artefact) to 11.81% (FY26); no stable payout policy to anchor a DDM (AR FY26 p.45) |
| PEG | Not applicable | FY26 PAT growth is a meaningless "62,636%" off a near-zero FY25 base, and includes a one-off Rs59.70cr exceptional gain; earnings growth is not usable as a PEG denominator without heavy normalisation first (AR FY26 p.26, Note 59) |
| Blended (unsegmented) trailing P/E | Not applicable as primary | Masks the fact that roofing (mature, ~R1/R2 neighbourhood) and boards (growing, ~R3 neighbourhood) sit on different rungs of the CLAUDE.md quality ladder; a single P/E either overpays for roofing or underpays for boards |

Cycle stage that matters for valuation: the company is mid-transition, a
mature/declining core (roofing) still funding a growing, capacity-constrained
line (boards), with the yarn segment exiting a cyclical trough and ATUM
still pre-scale. Valuation should be anchored to where each line sits in
its own cycle, not to the consolidated number.

### 4C. Quarterly monitoring checklist

1. Segment revenue split (roofing / boards / yarn), disclosed quarterly. Good: boards growth holds double digits. Trouble: boards growth decelerates toward roofing's low-single-digit pace.
2. Boards capacity utilisation. Good: stays near 85%+ even as new capacity lands. Trouble: utilisation drops as capacity rises (demand did not show up).
3. Non-asbestos revenue share. Good: continues rising past FY26's 47%. Trouble: stalls or reverses.
4. Dealer count and dealer-channel revenue share. Good: decline stabilises or is explained by a deliberate shift to institutional accounts. Trouble: continued unexplained decline.
5. Net debt / EBITDA. Good: stays at or below FY26's 2.19x through the capex ramp. Trouble: climbs back above 3x.
6. CARE (or successor agency) rating outlook. Good: moves from Negative to Stable. Trouble: outlook stays Negative for another full cycle, or the rating itself is cut.
7. Cost of materials consumed as % of revenue. Good: stays near or below FY26's ~50%. Trouble: rises without a matching price increase.
8. Exceptional items disclosure. Good: none, or clearly one-off and immaterial. Trouble: recurring "exceptional" gains used to flatter reported PBT (watch the pattern after FY26's Rs59.70cr land-sale gain).
