Strength: **Strong** — this functions as a referral/trust gate with hospitals and insurers analogous to a qualification lock-in, though no TPA/insurer-reimbursement contract explicitly tied to the accreditation has been disclosed.

### 6B. Risks to each top-scoring emerging moat

- **A2 (patents)**: Filed ≠ granted ≠ commercially defensible; management's own framing ("the biggest moat we will have") is aspirational until a competitor product is actually blocked or licensing revenue appears. Early warning sign: patents lapsing/rejected, or a competitor launching a functionally identical product without challenge.
- **B2 (accreditation)**: Accreditation is open to any qualifying operator — Athulya, Sukino, KITES and new entrants (DLF, J Estates) can pursue the same NABH/HSSC standards. Early warning sign: a competitor achieving the same or a higher-tier accreditation, eroding the "only"/"first" framing.
- **C1 (cross-sell)**: Currently small in absolute ₹ terms (~₹7 Cr/year); depends on residence/care-home occupancy actually maturing to generate a larger addressable base. Early warning sign: cross-sell ₹ figure stagnating even as occupancy grows.
- **D2 (digital platform / AGEasy)**: Marketing-efficiency-dependent (RoAS improvement from ~1 to ~2-2.9 over the period) and platform-dependent (the Flipkart/Meta disruptions in Q1 and Q3 FY26 show real fragility to third-party platform policy changes). Early warning sign: RoAS regression or a repeat of a marketplace-specific outage.
- **E1 (first-mover accreditation/brand)**: Erodes automatically as competitors catch up; the CEO himself noted "infrastructure, anybody can copy" (Q3 FY26 call) and pinned the real differentiator on service consistency, which is harder to verify from documents.
- **H2 (partnerships)**: Several partnerships (boAt watch) have already underperformed relative to expectation; partnership-linked revenue is not separately audited/disclosed, so the "big revenue contributor" claim for the IIT Delhi wheelchair cannot be independently sized.
