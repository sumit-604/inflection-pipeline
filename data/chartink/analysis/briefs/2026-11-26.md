## The picture in one paragraph

Monday's slide did not turn into a rout. Wednesday was a second soft day, and it pushed the short and middle trend counts to the lowest of this pullback. Thursday then bounced, led by IT and realty. About 100 stocks rose hard and fewer than 30 fell hard, the best balance in more than two weeks. Fear fell, and the VIX closed near 17. Yet the bounce was thin under the surface. Only a little over half of all stocks closed higher, and turnover was below a year ago. The long count rose to 993, still just under the 1,000 line the last two briefs watched. The IT index jumped on its largest names while only three IT stocks in ten hold their weekly trend lines. The banks kept their long trend, but the share of bank stocks above the four week line fell from eight in ten to under six. The smallest companies slipped under their own long line. So the market is a strong top held up by a few large groups, and a wide base that has stopped falling but has not started to climb.

## Where the market stands

Picture three clocks on every stock. The 20 day line is the short clock. The 50 day line is the middle clock. The 200 day line is the long clock. A stock above a line is in an uptrend on that clock. On Thursday 945 stocks stood above the short line, 917 above the middle line and 993 above the long line. All three counts sit about 175 to 250 below half the market.

The autumn gives these counts their meaning. 23 September was the last day when more stocks stood above the long line than below it. October brought two waves of selling. On 23 October, 388 stocks fell hard in a single session, and only 607 held the long line. That is still the low of the year on this count. The climb out came in two thrust days, on 4 and 5 November, when 317 and then 241 stocks rose hard. By 12 November the long count reached 1,149, and the short count reached 1,715. That was the high of the bounce.

Since that high the long count has given back about 155 stocks. That is a little under a third of the whole climb from the October low. The short count has given back almost 800. Wednesday marked the low of this pullback on the short and middle clocks, at 887 and 868. The long count held flat at 967 on Wednesday, the same as Monday. On Thursday all three counts turned up. The short count rose by about 60 and the long count by 26. That is the first up day on all three clocks since 19 November.

Wednesday deserves its own paragraph, because no brief covered it. It was a soft day with a weak finish. A little over half of all stocks closed lower. 89 stocks fell hard and 70 rose hard. The count tile showed 100 new yearly lows against 44 new highs, the widest gap of the pullback. The share of stocks down ten percent or more over five sessions rose again, to about one in twenty. Turnover came to about 520 crore against about 434 a year earlier, a little above ordinary. So Wednesday was a second slide day, not a capitulation.

Thursday had a different shape. 102 stocks rose four and a half percent or more, and only 29 fell that much. That ratio of about three and a half to one is the best since 12 November. Yet the plain count of risers and fallers was close. About 55 stocks in a hundred closed higher and 44 closed lower. So the strength sat in a group of strong movers, not across the whole market. Turnover was about 451 crore against about 465 a year earlier. That is an ordinary volume day, and lower than Wednesday. Thursday was a good day, not a thrust day. The November thrust days had three hundred hard risers on rising turnover.

Two smaller readings say who controlled the day. About one stock in six traded four percent above the prior close at some point, and a little more than a third of those kept the gain into the close. On Monday it was one in four. About one stock in eleven traded four percent below the prior close, and three in ten of those closed near the low. The close improved, but buyers still lose most of their intraday gains.

Fear eased. The India VIX measures what traders pay for protection against large swings in the Nifty. It fell almost five percent on Thursday to about 16.8. That is below Monday's 17.7 and below the level of 18 that the last brief set as the sign that fear had taken charge. The VIX is still up about half over three months, so protection is not cheap. ## What would change this read

The read is a bounce off the October low that has lost a little under a third of its climb, held a floor for three sessions, and turned up on Thursday on a strong but thin day. Every "from" figure below is Thursday's, and the next download will test them. The constructive view fails if the floor gives way. A long count under about 900 would mean more than half the bounce is gone, from 993. A day of 200 or more hard fallers would turn the slide into selling with force, from 29. A fall in the share of bank stocks above the four week line below half, from about 56 in a hundred, would say the leader has broken. A VIX close above about 18, from about 16.8, would say fear has taken charge. A long count under 607, the October low, would erase the whole bounce.

The cautious view fails if Thursday proves to be the start of a repair. The first sign is the long count back above 1,000 and holding there, from 993. The second is the middle count climbing toward half the market, about 1,170, from 917. The third is a wide up day with 200 or more hard risers, from 102, on turnover above about 600 crore, from about 451. The fourth is gains holding into the close, with half the day's four percent risers keeping the gain, from a little over a third. The fifth is the IT member share rising toward half, from three in ten, so the index move gains members behind it. The first two signs together, with any one of the others in the same week, would put the repair back on track.
