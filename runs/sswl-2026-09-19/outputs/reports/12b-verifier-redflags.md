### Material items (CRITICAL + MAJOR)

**R1. Bhuj timeline slips across three calls while management says "as planned". MAJOR.**
- Q3 FY26, Dheeraj Garg, p.10: "All these projects will start before Diwali this year."
  Q3 FY26, p.16: "the next facility will come in by October. And, hopefully, by December,
  we can start commercially supplying wheels."
- Q4 FY26, Garg, p.5: "trial runs will happen starting from October and culminating in
  maybe, let's say, January." Q4 FY26, p.19: "This expansion is coming, in fact, in my
  opinion, delayed by four, five months."
- Q1 FY27, Garg, p.4: "trial production is expected to commence in the fourth quarter of
  this current financial year". The same speech, p.4: "Capacity expansion at our Bhuj
  facility is progressing as planned."
- The date moved from commercial supply by Dec-2026 to trial production in Jan-Mar 2027.
  That is about one quarter of slip per call. The management wording stayed "as planned".

**R2. Management gives two different figures for Bhuj knuckle capacity. MAJOR.**
- Q3 FY26, Garg, p.10: "1.2 million in the aluminum wheels and 0.6 million in the
  knuckles". Q3 FY26, p.12: "Mehsana facility is 5 lakhs and the Bhuj facility is 6 lakhs.
  So, 11 lakhs total."
- Q4 FY26, Garg, p.5: "knuckles are going to be about 1.1 million". Q4 FY26, p.6: "two
  phases... 600 thousand and... another 500 thousand".
- Q1 FY27, Garg, p.4: "aluminium knuckles facility with an annual capacity of 1.1 million
  units". Q1 FY27, Pranav Jain (VP Finance), p.7: "knuckles capacity 0.6 million. And the
  total capex in both these are around INR420 crores."
- In the Q1 FY27 call the MD and the finance head gave different numbers. The Q2 FY26
  plan (Mohan Joshi, p.13) was a second 0.5mn line costing Rs130cr, live from Sep-Oct
  2026. That line appears to have been folded into Bhuj.

**R3. Management admits alloy-wheel pricing and margin pressure, then denies it to another analyst. MAJOR.**
- Q3 FY26, Garg, p.19: "as we grow in volume in the aluminum segment, the margins are
  coming under pressure because there is competition."
- Q1 FY27, Garg to Yash Jhurani, p.12: "yes, prices have gone down, but... we get a
  premium. In some cases, not in all cases, in a few cases". Then: "Yes, the domestic
  margins are crashing... our competitors are taking businesses that are below cost
  margins."
- Q1 FY27, Garg to Hitaindra Pradhan, p.12, minutes earlier: "margin in every segment of
  the business is improving... It is expanding at every segment."
- The admission comes in 2 quarters. In Q1 FY27 two analysts got opposite answers. The
  item goes to the heart of LBF2, the alloy mix-shift margin thesis.

**R4. The alloy mix shift has stalled. Management said it would rise. MAJOR.**
- Alloy share of revenue by call: 36% (Q2 FY26, Pranav Jain, p.3), ~37% (Q3 FY26, Jain,
  p.4), ~36% (Q4 FY26, Garg, p.4), 35% (Q1 FY27, Garg, p.3).
- Promise: "share in overall revenue likely to rise further in the coming quarters" (Q2
  FY26, p.3). "alloy wheels increasing their share of overall revenue going forward" (Q3
  FY26, p.4).
- Q1 FY27, Garg, p.12: per-wheel EBITDA "has not gone up just because of aluminum wheels...
  we have got input increases". Q1 FY27, p.3: margin expansion was "driven by input
  increases received from OEMs other than raw materials". So the Rs262 to Rs314 jump came
  from price renegotiation, not mix. Hitaindra Pradhan (Q1 FY27, p.11) says the plateau
  out loud.

**R5. Capex keeps rising, a no-steel-capex statement is reversed, and the debt guide does not move. MAJOR.**
- FY27 capex by call: Rs420cr Bhuj + ~Rs40cr maintenance (Q3 FY26, Garg, p.8). Rs500cr +
  ~Rs50cr brownfield (Q4 FY26, Garg, p.16). Rs420cr + Rs80cr + Rs150cr new agri/PV steel
  line, "INR600 crores plus/minus" (Q1 FY27, Jain and Garg, p.7). The parts add to
  Rs650cr.
- Q3 FY26, Garg, p.17: "I do not think next year there will be anything. Maybe there is a
  small paint shop... Rs. 10 crores-15 crores". Q3 FY26, p.16: "we do not have intention to
  add capacity here". Two quarters later a Rs150cr steel line appears.
- Debt guide: "+Rs200cr" in Q3 FY26 (p.10) and Q4 FY26 (p.16-17). It is unchanged even
  though capex rose by Rs140-190cr. The Q4 FY26 debt projection was "based on a volume and
  EBITDA of about last year's level" (p.16).
- Management adds (Q1 FY27, p.8): "many things on drawing board" for more aluminium and
  knuckle capacity.

**R6. Management's own words contradict its "absolute pass-through" claim. MAJOR.**
- Q3 FY26, Garg, p.5: "there is an absolute pass-through. Maybe sometimes there is a lag".
  Q4 FY26, Garg, p.20: "Whether it rains or shines, the situation remains the same for us."
- Same exchange, Q4 FY26, p.21: "We are impacted. We have to use more working capital for
  sure." Also: "we have made sure that the OEMs realized that we are not here to die because
  prices were a killer". Also: "our marketing department will be constantly recovering...
  the company's cost increases."
- Q1 FY27, Garg, p.13: "had we worked under the previous regime, we would have lost
  millions of crores". Q1 FY27, p.9: "excess pressure of INR150 crores... it takes time for
  the money to arrive from the customer".
- The claim held under a quarterly lag only because the lag was renegotiated in 2026. The
  cost of the lag lands in working capital.

**R7. Data is pushed to offline channels in 3 quarters, a selective-disclosure pattern. MAJOR.**
- Q2 FY26, Mohan Joshi, p.6: segment split "we can take it offline with SGA". Q2 FY26, p.7:
  "we will give you a perspective of per wheel kind of a concept if we take it offline".

  revenue run rate, "We will give that number to you through SGA."
- Q1 FY27, Garg, p.10-11: "next time I will prepare this statement". Also: "We'll talk
  about it in the next call."
- Operating data is offered to single analysts through the IR agency (SGA). None of it
  appears on the record in a later call.

**R8. The FY26 export target was missed by ~Rs150-200cr. The same Rs600cr figure is re-issued for FY27. MAJOR.**
- Q2 FY26, Joshi, p.11: "at the beginning of the year that we are going to be hitting
  INR600 crores to INR650 crores".
- Q4 FY26, Aditya Dixit, p.15: FY26 actual "about INR 454 crores". FY27 target: "in the
  range of INR 600 crores" (Q4 FY26, p.7), with the MD's words "very, very easily
  workable".
- Q1 FY27, Aditya Khetan, p.5: exports Rs127cr, down YoY from Rs160cr.

**R9. The knuckle ramp under-delivers and the customer count is stuck at 2. MAJOR.**
- Q2 FY26, Joshi, p.12: FY26 knuckle revenue "INR75 crores to INR80 crores". Q2 FY26,
  Jain, p.3: goal to fully commercialise "by FY '26, bringing major OEMs into our customer
  base".
- Q3 FY26, Garg, p.5: "knuckles, although it has not done 100% of what our expectations
  were". Q4 FY26, Garg, p.4: "good progress, but not great, great progress". Q4 FY26,
  Jain, p.22: knuckles are "1%" of revenue.
- Q1 FY27, Khetan, p.6: Q1 revenue of Rs23cr "looks similar when we compare it with your
  Q3 numbers... we are working with 2 customers for over the last one year". Garg, p.6:
  "you've seen the ups and down in the EV market".
- FY27 guide is Rs110-130cr, "almost doubling from last year" (Q1 FY27, p.13). That
  implies FY26 was ~Rs55-65cr. This is derived from the "doubling" remark. The exact FY26
  knuckle revenue is NOT FOUND in the transcripts.
- Q4 FY26, Garg, p.19: "We are the sole supplier for knuckles right now in the market...
  hoping to win two major businesses". By Q1 FY27 the new wins came from the same 2
  customers.

**R10. Bhuj is re-described as an export-aftermarket plant with a Chinese technical partner. MAJOR.**
- Q1 FY27, Dixit, p.14: "this plant in Bhuj is predominantly coming in for market outside
  India to be catered and more so on the aftermarket side. Some capacity of that plant will
  also be utilized for the OEM markets... that is where Arays is going to support us."
- Earlier framing: OEM approvals and OEM demand (Q4 FY26, p.5-6, p.13). "sold out" (Q1
  FY27, p.7). Q3 FY26, Garg, p.16: "I paid Rs. 140 crores for this... to strategically
  stop the Chinese from taking over this asset in India."
- Aftermarket export volume has a different demand quality from OEM nominations. This bears
  on trigger 2 (Bhuj) and on how far the "sold out" claim can be relied on.

**R11. Management describes receivables factoring. The pipeline equates it with the payables-side supplier finance arrangement. MAJOR.**
- Q3 FY26, Jain, p.9: "there are some of the factoring lines and that factoring is adjusted
  in the debtor. There are debtor factoring limits... same is adjusted against the debtor."
- Q1 FY27, Jain, p.9: "factoring limits... in the range of INR400 crores to INR500 crores".
- Analysts pressed the interest-cost gap in 2 quarters (Vikas Sharda, Q3 FY26, p.8-9;
  Aditya Jhavar, Q1 FY27, p.9). Both times the answer was debtor factoring.
- The transcripts never mention the supplier finance or reverse-factoring arrangement on the
  payables side.

**R12. The steel-vs-alloy per-wheel EBITDA split is refused or deferred, a repeated evasion. CRITICAL (item grade).**
- Q2 FY26, Joshi, p.6: "we don't do it separately... take it offline".
- Q3 FY26, Garg, p.12: "I do not want to reveal my trade secrets".
- Q1 FY27, Garg, p.10-11: to Madhur Rathi and then to Hitaindra Pradhan, deferred to the
  "next call".
- Precision note. Q2 FY26 (Joshi, p.7) did give EBITDA-margin % ranges: domestic steel
  8-9%, export steel 13-15%, alloy 11-13%. Q3 FY26 (Garg, p.13) implicitly confirmed alloy
  above Rs450/wheel: "we are assuming a higher margin than Rs. 450... it will be much more
  than Rs. 450".

**R13. A peer contradicts SSWL's premiumisation claim. MAJOR.**
- UNOMINDA Q4 FY26 (18-May-2026, Sunil Bohra, p.7): "near-term moderation in alloy wheel
  penetration... stronger growth in entry level of the models". It also says "steel wheels
  were adopted in place of previously planned alloy wheels" in some 2W programmes.
- SSWL Q4 FY26 (02-Jun-2026, Garg, p.4): alloy growth "supported by a premiumization of
  vehicles demanding more aluminum wheels". SSWL's own Q4 FY26 p.18 adds: "customers are
  asking us for steel wheels".

**R14. A peer finds alloy wheels to be its lowest-return segment. MAJOR.**
- WHEELS Q2 FY26 (31-Oct-2025, Srivats Ram, p.16): "the one where the return on investment
  is lowest is cast aluminium because we haven't reached the scale yet". The same call puts
  the double-digit-margin threshold at 60,000 wheels a month (p.10-11).
- This cuts against SSWL's alloy-premium framing. The peer effect depends on scale.
