Rationale and key rating drivers 
Ratings assigned to bank facilities and fixed deposits of K.C.P. Sugar and Industries Corporation Limited (KCP) derive strength 
from its established track record of eight decades in the sugar industry and integrated operations comprising distillery and 
cogeneration. Ratings also derive stre ngth from a comfortable capital structure and liquidity profile, marked by sizeable 
investments in mutual funds and equities. 
 
Ratings are constrained by lower cane availability and lower recovery rate of sugarcane in its command area, affecting profitability 
margins. Ratings also factor in susceptibility of revenues and profitability to demand -supply dynamics and the cyclical and  
regulated nature of the sugar industry in terms of command area and plant location. 
 
CARE Ratings Limited (CareEdge Ratings) has withdrawn ratings assigned to the company’s term loan facility, as it has repaid 
this loan in full and there is no amount outstanding under the loan as on date per the no due confirmation from the lender. 
 
Rating sensitivities: Factors likely to lead to rating actions 
Positive factors: Factors that could individually or collectively lead to positive rating action/upgrade: 
• Increase in registered cane area and effective utilisation of capacity, leading to improved operational performance on a 
sustained basis 
• Generating profit from operations on a sustained basis 
Negative factors: Factors that could individually or collectively lead to negative rating action/downgrade: 
• Debt funded capex, resulting in net term debt/net worth >0.3x on a sustained basis. 
• Continued decline in cane crushed/cane availability on a sustained basis, leading to lower capacity utilisation 
 
Analytical approach: Standalone 
 
Outlook: Stable 
CareEdge Ratings believes that the company will maintain its risk profile, considering its comfortable capital structure and liquidity. 
 
Detailed description of key rating drivers 
Key strengths 
Established track record in sugar industry and integrated nature of operations 
KCP has an established track record of over eight decades in the sugar industry. It currently owns and operates an integrated  
sugar complex in Vuyyuru, Andhra Pradesh (Unit I), in the fertile Krishna River delta region. The unit has a crushing capacit y of 
7,500 tonnes of cane crushed per day (TCD), distillery capacity of 50 kilo litres per day (KLPD), and cogeneration facility o f 15 
megawatt (MW). The unit also includes a chemical division with facilities to manufacture pharmaceutical -grade calcium lactate, 
carbon dioxide (a by-product of the distillery division), and a biotech division with facilities to manufacture bio -fertiliser and bio-
compost.

In FY20 (FY refers to April 01 to March 31), the company closed operations at Unit II in Lakshmipuram (4,000 TCD), ~40 km 
from Unit I, due to a steady decline in cane availability. Cane from this unit is now diverted and crushed in Unit I. In FY23 , the 
company sold the plant and machinery of Unit II and established a black gram (urad dal) processing unit using the existing 
infrastructure. 
 
Strong relationship with farmers 
The company’s sugar command area is situated in Krishna district, Andhra Pradesh, which is highly fertile and irrigated by Krishna 
River canals. In recent years, farmers in the district have preferred cultivating alternate cash crops such as paddy, black g ram, 
vegetables, and pulses. The fertile Krishna Delta region and developed irrigation systems support the growth of these crops. 
Despite a decline in cane cultivation, black gram cultivation has remained consistent and is increasing, with the region contributing 
~30% of Andhra Pradesh’s total black gram production. KCP maintains strong relationships with farmers due to its long-standing 
presence in the region, facilitating procurement of cane and black gram. 
 
Diversification into other business segments 
The company has diversified its operations by establishing a black gram (urad dal) processing unit in February 2023 , with a 
capacity of 22,000 metric tonne. This unit contributed 20.2% of total operating income (TOI) in FY25 compared to 3.9% in FY24. 
Apart from this, the company also operates engineering and chemical manufacturing units. 
 
Comfortable capital structure 
The company’s capital structure has shown continuous improvement, marked by an overall gearing of 0.30x as on March 31, 
2025 (PY: 0.41x). Overall debt stood at ₹109.82 crore as on March 31, 2025, comprising ₹45.28 crore of working capital 
borrowings and ₹64.55 crore of fixed deposits. The company has nil term debt as on date. 
 
As on March 31, 2025, the company had total liquid investments of ₹269.67 crore, of which ₹65.59 crore is lien marked towards 
working capital borrowings. The company is net debt negative as on March 31, 2025.  
 
With no major debt-funded capital expenditure planned for the near term, the capital structure is expected to remain comfortable 
in the medium term. 
 
Key weaknesses 
Lower cane recovery rate affecting operating margins 
The sugar business has not been profitable in recent years due to lower cane availability and reduced recovery rates in the Krishna 
belt. In FY25, unfavourable weather conditions in Krishna district led farmers to shift to other cash crops, reducing cane crushing 
to 2.6 lakh metric tonne (PY: 4.4 lakh metric tonne). Recovery rates also declined to 8.05% in FY25 from 8.50% in FY24. TOI 
declined 22% in FY25 to ₹227 crore (PY: ₹292.54 crore), and profit before interest, lease rentals, depreciation, and taxatio n 
(PBILDT) margin dropped to -1.89% in FY25 (PY: 3.98%).  
 
In FY25, non-operating income was ₹19.82 crore, compared to ₹62.52 crore in the previous year. This mainly comprised dividend 
income from investments of ₹3.99 crore (PY: ₹3.64 crore), interest on fixed deposits of ₹4.03 crore (PY: ₹5.07 crore), and gains 
from fair value adjustments of equity instruments amounting to ₹4.37 crore (PY: ₹48.19 crore). 
 
Cyclical and regulated nature of sugar business 
The sugar industry is cyclical and vulnerable to government policies due to its classification as an essential commodity and its 
impact on the wholesale price index (WPI). The government regulates raw material prices through mechanisms such as state 
advised prices (SAP) and fair and remunerative prices (FRP). These factors influence sugarcane cultivation patterns and affect 
profitability. FRP increased 8% to ₹340/quintal in 2024 -25 from ₹315/quintal in 2023 -24. It is expected to increase further to 
₹355/quintal in 2025-26. In 2025, domestic sugar prices are in the range of ₹4,170 -₹4,270/quintal, reflecting a ~2% year -on-
year increase due to the anticipated shortage of sugar in the country. For the 2025 -26 ethanol supply year (ESY), India lifted 
restrictions on the diversion of sugarcane juice, syrup, and molasses to ethanol production, allowing for unlimited production from 
these feedstocks. The nationwide rollout of E20 fuel (20% ethanol blend) began in April 2025, five years ahead of the original 
2030 target is expected to increase diversion towards ethanol. Sugar prices are anticipated to stay firm through sugar season 
2026 (SS26), bolstered by reduced production and stable demand. 
 
Liquidity: Strong 
Liquidity remains strong, marked by free cash and investments of ₹204.08 crore as on March 31, 2025. Average working capital 
utilisation stood at 37.65% for 12 months ended June 2025, providing headroom for additional working capital requirements in

KCP is part of the KCP group. K.C.P. Limited (KCPL), the group's flagship company was promoted by late V Ramakrishna in 1941. 
Over the years, KCPL diversified into different industries including cement, and engineering, apart from sugar. In 1995, KCP was 
formed under the leadership of late V M Rao to take over sugar mills and KCPL’s workshop. KCP presently owns and operates 
sugar mill at Vuyyuru, AP with an aggregate capacity of 7,500 TCD, distillery capacity of 50 KLPD and incidental co -generation 
capacity of 15 MW. It also has facility to manufacture 500 Tonnes Per Annum (TPA) pharma grade calcium lactate, 60 TPA carbon 
dioxide, 1,200 TPA Bio -fertiliser and 1400 TPA Mycorrizha. In February 2023, it added to cart another new vertical black gram 
(urad dal) processing plant with capacity to process 22,000 metric tonne per annum (MTPA).
