## SECTION 1: MARKET DEFINITION

### 1A. Precise boundaries

- **Product scope:** CNC metal-cutting machine tools — vertical machining centres (VMC), horizontal machining centres (HMC), turning/turn-mill centres, double-column machines (DCM), vertical turret lathes (VTL), 5-axis machines, drill-tap centres, plus the premium NEXA range (₹29-32L ASP) and early automation/FMS attachments. **Excluded:** metal-forming machine tools (presses, forging), non-CNC/manual/conventional machine tools, grinding/EDM specialty machines, and machine-tool accessories/controllers as a standalone market (Macpower buys ~99% of its CNC controllers from FANUC/Siemens/Mitsubishi rather than selling controllers — Q4 FY26 call, Concall_Jun_2026_Transcript.pdf p.6).
- **Geographic scope:** India, domestic. Exports were <1% of FY25 revenue (₹2.52 lakh of ₹261.82cr — AR Segment Note, per B07) and remain a stated aspiration, not a current reality; global TAM is shown separately in Method 5 for benchmarking only.
- **Customer scope:** Automobile/auto-ancillary, general engineering/capital goods, die & mould, agriculture, defence/aerospace (DRDO, ordnance factories, HAL, ISRO), government PSUs/education — historically weighted to tier-3/tier-4 MSME customers, expanding to tier-1/tier-2 and institutional/defence buyers (Investor Presentation p.26; concalls).
- **Channel scope:** Direct sales via 9-10 branch offices, 8-9 tech centres, 9 business associates across 39 cities (Investor Presentation p.17); government tender/GeM route for defence/PSU business; no material export-dealer channel yet (EMO Germany 2025 attendance is early-stage — AR "Exhibition Highlight" pp.15-16).
- **Price segment:** Mid-market entry/standard machines (₹15-16L ASP) through premium NEXA (₹29-32L, up to ₹1.5cr for large-format) — explicitly **excludes** the ultra-high-end segment (>₹1cr import machines, ~55-60% of India's machine-tool imports by value) that management itself says India "does not make" (Q4 FY26 call, p.15) — this is a genuine capability gap, not just a pricing choice, and is filtered out in the SAM calculation below (Section 3A).

**A wrong definition would make every later number useless**, so the operative scope for this analysis is: **India, domestic, CNC metal-cutting machine tools, excluding the ultra-high-end import-only technology tier.**

### 1B. Management's own TAM claim(s) — held for comparison

Management cites market-size claims across three separate documents, and they do not fully reconcile with each other:

1. **Q4 FY26 concall (28-May-2026), citing IMTMA data "released at the Goa Summit":** Indian machine tools industry domestic consumption ≈ **₹35,000 cr** (2026), rising to **₹54,000 cr by 2030** and **₹2,30,000 cr by 2049**; India production ≈ **₹16,000 cr** (2026); India ranked 9th globally in production, 4th in consumption; global machine tools market **"$82 billion,"** India's share ~5% (Concall_Jun_2026_Transcript.pdf, p.4). No underlying IMTMA publication was independently retrieved this run — this is management's second-hand restatement, not a primary-source read.
2. **Investor Presentation (Q1FY27, dated 29-Jul-2026), "Global Machine Tools Market Overview" slide, sourced grandviewresearch.com:** global machine tools market **"$117.2 billion in 2025,"** projected to **$183.5bn by 2033** (CAGR 5.8%, 2026-2033); Asia-Pacific 56.2% revenue share (2025); India ranked 9th in production, **7th** in consumption (source cited: "IMTEX 2025") (Investor_Presentation_1.pdf, p.28).
3. **FY25 Annual Report MD&A (Aug-2025):** current machine tool **manufacturing** industry ≈ **₹12,000 cr**, which "will have to move... to ~₹45,000-50,000 cr over the next decade" if manufacturing's GDP share rises from 17% to 25%; separately, "India machine tools market is expected to reach **US$3.2 Billion by 2032**, exhibiting CAGR of 8.2% during 2024-2032"; consumption demand growing ~15%/yr; domestic production meets <50% of domestic demand (Annual_Report_2025.pdf, p.79).

**Credibility read: BROAD, not specific.** These three claims use different scopes (total machine tools vs CNC vs global), different sources (IMTMA/Goa Summit vs grandviewresearch.com vs an unnamed 2024-vintage report), and give **inconsistent global market size ($82bn vs $117.2bn, ~43% apart)** and **inconsistent India consumption rank (4th vs 7th)** within the company's own document set, roughly two months apart. Management's own current-share claim about *itself* is similarly unstable: **4%** and **4.5%** (both used in the same Q2 FY26 call and again in the same Q3 FY26 call), later explicitly corrected under direct analyst questioning to **1% of consumption / 2% of production** (Q4 FY26 call, p.15 — Kushal Mondal: "you said in your opening remarks that our market share in the consumption is 1%. But... in the last few concalls, you said that our market share is around 4.5%. Has the market share decreased?" Rupesh Mehta: "No... Let me correct you. 1% of consumption... 2% of manufacturing"). This is a 2-4x self-correction, not rounding, and is treated as a live flag throughout this report — held for comparison against Section 2's independent estimates.

## SECTION 2: TAM ESTIMATION, MULTIPLE METHODS

### Method 1 — Top-down (IMTMA total × CNC-share overlay)

Start: India total machine tools domestic consumption ≈ ₹35,000 cr (2026, IMTMA per Q4 FY26 concall, Section 1B #1).
Subtract non-CNC machine tools (conventional/manual lathes, non-CNC forming, grinding not on CNC platforms). The task's operator context frames CNC as "~60-70% of that" consumption — **this specific split was NOT independently found stated in any anchored document read this run** (not in the AR, not in any of the three concalls, not in the Investor Presentation); it is carried here as a labeled, unverified scoping assumption, using the **midpoint 65%** for the "realistic" case and the **low end 60%** for the "conservative" case.
- Realistic: ₹35,000 cr × 65% = **₹22,750 cr**
- Conservative: (see reconciliation below, which also revises the base) 

Growth: ₹35,000 cr (2026) → ₹54,000 cr (2030) is a **CAGR of ~11.5%/yr**, holding at the same CNC ratio.

**Confidence: Medium.** Single-sourced (management's second-hand IMTMA citation), but internally cross-validated against the global-benchmark calculation in Method 5 (₹35,000cr ≈ 5% of "$82bn" global figure — consistent).

### Method 2 — Bottom-up (unit economics)

Addressable unit: one CNC machine (turning, VMC, HMC, double-column — Macpower's core categories). Per B04 (anchored): FY25 revenue/unit ≈ ₹18.8 lakh (₹26,011.78 lakh product sales ÷ 1,382 machines). Current blended realisation (FY26, per concalls) ≈ ₹20 lakh/machine.

Total units (India, these categories only): management states (Q2 FY26 call, Nov-2025, Concall_Nov_2025_Transcript.pdf p.6) "India is producing 30,000 machines and import is just 4,000" — i.e. ≈34,000 units/yr for turning + VMC + HMC + double-column specifically (Rupesh Mehta: "Turning machine almost 90% India's production compared to consumption. VMC is almost 80%. HMC is 60%"). This is a rough, unsourced management verbal estimate — **low confidence, single quarter, no external corroboration** — and narrower in product scope than Method 1 (excludes 5-axis, VTL, drill-tap and other CNC categories).

Value: domestic production (30,000 units × ~₹15-20L, using a broader-than-Macpower average since the bulk of these are entry/standard-tier, not NEXA-mix-skewed) ≈ ₹5,250-6,000 cr, **plus** imports (4,000 units × >₹1cr average, per Q3 FY26 call: "the average price of import machine is more than INR1 crore") ≈ ₹4,000 cr. **Total ≈ ₹9,250-10,000 cr** for this narrower four-category basket.

This sits well below Method 1's full-CNC figure — expected, since it excludes several CNC categories and may understate the true average price of Indian-made machines. **Confidence: Low** (single management estimate, not a full independent method); used here only as a partial cross-check, not a headline number.

### Method 3 — Peer revenue aggregation (+ unorganised estimate)

JYOTICNC (closest direct CNC peer) discloses, independently of Macpower: "the Indian CNC machine tool market is estimated around close to **US$3.5 billion** in annual demand... nearly **60%** of this requirement is still met through imports" (JYOTICNC Q2 FY26 call, per B06-peers, verified in this run's peer-verification stage). At ~₹85/US$, that is **≈₹29,750 cr**.

Independent reverse-engineering cross-check: web search finds JYOTICNC "is India's third-largest CNC manufacturer, with a domestic market share of approximately 10% in FY2023." JYOTICNC's FY26 consolidated revenue is ≈₹2,093 cr (web search). If ~10% share holds, implied total CNC TAM ≈ ₹2,093cr ÷ 10% = **≈₹20,930 cr** — independently derived, and notably close to Method 1's ₹22,750cr.

Unorganised-sector estimate: the framework's standard 30-60% unorganised uplift (typical for Indian consumer/light-manufacturing sectors) is **explicitly NOT applied at that scale here** — management repeatedly and consistently states the unorganised/Chinese-CKD-assembly threat is small: "the Chinese market share is in total terms of the value is not more than 5% also" (Q3 FY26 call, p.17-18). Precision CNC capital goods have real qualification/reliability barriers (5-10 year machine life, service networks) that limit informal-sector participation more than typical consumer categories. I therefore apply a much smaller **~5-8% unorganised uplift** to the organised-peer-implied TAM rather than the 30-60% default, flagged explicitly as a deviation from the standard framework assumption with reasoning shown.

Adjusted Method 3 range: ₹20,930-29,750 cr organised-peer-implied, +5-8% unorganised ≈ **₹22,000-32,100 cr**.

**Confidence: Medium-High** for the ₹20,930cr low end (derived from an independently-disclosed peer share, not from Macpower or a trade body); **Medium** for the $3.5bn/₹29,750cr JYOTICNC figure (peer's own market claim, undated vintage, not independently verified beyond the peer-verification stage's own checks).

### Method 4 — Import substitution

Consumption ₹35,000 cr (2026) − domestic production ₹16,000 cr (2026) = **₹19,000 cr import gap (≈54% of consumption)**. Management's own five-year trajectory: "45% to 50% is import and India is... 50% to 55%... India is producing" (Q3 FY26 call, p.15) — i.e. import share falling only modestly, from ~54% today toward ~45-50% over five years, a **slow-moving structural shift, not a fast one.** Government targets referenced in the FY25 AR (manufacturing/GDP share 17%→25%, machine tool manufacturing industry ₹12,000cr→₹45,000-50,000cr "over the next decade") point the same direction but on a longer, more uncertain horizon, and use an AR-vintage (Aug-2025) production baseline (₹12,000cr) that itself does not reconcile with the fresher Q4 FY26 concall's ₹16,000cr "2026" production figure — flagged as an internal AR-vs-concall inconsistency, not resolved by management.

If import share falls to the low end of management's own 45-50% band by 2030 against the ₹54,000cr 2030 consumption figure, domestic production would need to reach ≈₹27,000-29,700 cr — **roughly 1.7-1.9x today's ₹16,000cr domestic production**, i.e. the import-substitution pool is the single largest structural growth driver available to Indian CNC makers, Macpower included. **Confidence: Medium** (arithmetic is sound given the inputs; the inputs themselves carry Method 1's citation-quality caveats).

### Method 5 — Global benchmark

Global machine tools market: management cites **two different figures** two months apart — "$82 billion" (undated, Q4 FY26 concall) and "$117.2 billion in 2025" (Investor Presentation, Jul-2026, sourced grandviewresearch.com; growing to $183.5bn by 2033, CAGR 5.8%). At 5% India share (management's own stated share of global, both times): $82bn × 5% = **$4.1bn ≈ ₹34,850 cr** (closely matches the ₹35,000cr IMTMA domestic figure — internally consistent); $117.2bn × 5% = **$5.86bn ≈ ₹49,810 cr** (43% higher, and inconsistent with the ₹35,000cr domestic figure). **This ~43% divergence between two management-cited "global machine tools market" figures is flagged explicitly and not silently averaged** — the $82bn/₹35,000cr pairing is used as the more internally-consistent case.

**Independent (non-Macpower) third-party figures found via web search this run:**
- IBEF (citing IMARC Group): "India's machine tools market size stood at ₹164.70 billion (US$1.83 billion) in 2025... projected to reach ₹315.90 billion (US$3.51 billion) by 2034, CAGR 7.49% (2026-2034)" — **≈₹16,470 cr (2025)**.
- Grand View Research: India machine tools market "$3,873.2 million by 2030," CAGR 7.4% (2025-2030) → implied 2025 base ≈$2,712mn ≈ **₹23,050 cr**.
- Grand View Research (narrower CNC-specific sub-segment): "India CNC machining centers and turning centers... $1,536.0 million by 2030," CAGR 8.5% (2025-2030).
- Another aggregator figure: "machine tool market in India to grow by USD 3.46 billion during 2025-2030, CAGR 11.7%."
- An unattributed "provisional estimate" (vintage unclear, flagged STALE/low-confidence): production ≈₹12,328cr, consumption ≈₹24,536cr.

**This is a material, well-documented divergence: IMTMA's own ₹35,000cr (2026) figure is ≈2.1x IBEF/IMARC's independent ₹16,470cr (2025) figure for what is nominally the same "India machine tools market."** Only ~1 year apart, so vintage alone does not explain a 2x gap; scope-definition differences (IMTMA likely captures a broader universe including EDM/grinders/forming/accessories/spares plus more of the informal segment; IMARC's methodology may be narrower or miss India-specific informal activity) are the more likely explanation, but this is **not resolved in this run** and is flagged rather than averaged away.

This is the headline finding of Section 2 and is carried into the YAML as `mgmt_claim_ratio`.

## SECTION 3: SAM & SOM

### 3A. SAM — five filters applied to TAM (realistic, ₹24,000 cr)

1. **Product/capability fit:** subtract the ultra-high-end, import-only technology tier that management itself says India "does not make" (~55-60% of imports by value, Q4 FY26 call p.15). Using the ~54% import share (Method 4) and a 57.5% midpoint of the "unaddressable" slice: ₹24,000cr × 54% import share × 57.5% unaddressable ≈ ₹7,452 cr filtered out.
2. **Geography:** no subtraction — TAM is already India-only (exports are additive optionality, not part of this SAM, per 1A).
3. **Channel:** Macpower's dealer/branch network (39 cities, 9-10 branches) is broad but not universal; apply a modest 10% discount for tier-4/rural micro-cluster reach gaps on the remaining pool.
4. **Customer:** folded into product/capability above (tier-1/tier-2 and defence expansion is a share-capture question for SOM, not a market-definition exclusion for SAM).
5. **Capability (current capacity):** **not** filtered here — capacity constraints are a 3-5 year execution question, handled in 3C below, not a structural SAM exclusion (SAM asks what could be served with the right investment, not what's servable today).

SAM = (₹24,000cr − ₹7,452cr) × (1 − 10%) = ₹16,548cr × 0.90 ≈ **₹14,900 cr**.
**SAM as % of TAM (realistic) ≈ 62%.**

(Applying the same 62% filter to the conservative TAM of ₹20,000cr gives a conservative SAM of ≈₹12,400cr — shown for sensitivity, not carried as the headline.)

### 3B. SOM at 3 and 5 years

**Current SAM share:** FY26 revenue ₹333.18 cr ÷ SAM ₹14,900 cr = **2.24%**. (Cross-check: FY26 revenue ÷ realistic TAM ₹24,000cr = 1.39%, and ÷ Method-3-implied TAM ₹20,930cr = 1.59% — both land close to management's own corrected "1-2%" current-share statement, which is a meaningful independent corroboration of the *corrected* figure and a further reason to distrust the earlier 4-4.5% claims.)

**Share-gain assumption (conservative/"normal" case, per the standard rules):** 1-2pp gain in 3 years is "normal"; 3-5pp requires demonstrated capacity **and** execution. Given Gate 0's mechanical **AVOID** classification (thin Block B, cumulative CFO/PAT 0.4538 <0.50 threshold) and B07's flagged 3x land-catalyst slippage, the **low end of the normal band (+1pp over 3 years, +1.7pp over 5 years)** is used — the "aggressive" 3-5pp case is not supported by the execution evidence on file.

- **Year 3:** SAM share 2.24% + 1pp = 3.24% of a SAM grown at 11% TAM growth for 3 years (₹14,900cr × 1.11³ ≈ ₹20,380cr) → **SOM(3yr) = 3.24% × ₹20,380cr ≈ ₹660 cr**.
  Implied revenue CAGR: (660/333.18)^(1/3) − 1 ≈ **25.6%**.
- **Year 5:** SAM share 2.24% + 1.7pp = 3.94% of a SAM grown for 5 years (₹14,900cr × 1.11⁵ ≈ ₹25,110cr) → **SOM(5yr) = 3.94% × ₹25,110cr ≈ ₹989 cr**.
  Implied revenue CAGR: (989/333.18)^(1/5) − 1 ≈ **24.3%**.

Both SOM-implied CAGRs (25.6% at yr3, 24.3% at yr5) land **inside or just below** management's own repeatedly-guided 25-30% revenue growth band (Q2/Q3/Q4 FY26 calls) — a useful, independently-derived cross-check that management's near-term guidance is directionally plausible from a bottom-up market-share lens, without requiring the "aggressive" share-gain case.

### 3C. Capacity cross-check

Installed capacity: 2,500 machines/yr (reached Q4 FY26). Committed 13-acre plant (Phase 1 debottleneck + Phase 2): capacity to 3,500-4,000 machines/yr within ~24-36 months (per B07, credibility-adjusted range, vs. the mechanical 70% capex-embedded-growth figure flagged as an overstatement in B07). The 60-acre greenfield (5,000→10,000 machines, long-dated, already slipped 3x per B05/B07) is **not** assumed here.

At **flat** realisation (₹20 lakh/machine, FY26 level): SOM(5yr) ₹989cr would require ₹989cr ÷ ₹0.20cr ≈ **4,945 machines/yr** — this **exceeds** even the top of the committed 13-acre Phase 1+2 range (3,500-4,000 machines). **Gap ≈ 945-1,445 machines, or roughly ₹190-290 Cr of revenue** at flat pricing.

This gap closes only if realisation/mix rises toward ~₹25 lakh/machine by year 5 (a continuation of the NEXA/defence-mix trend already visible: ₹18.28L→₹20L→NEXA at ~40% of order book), which would bring the required unit count down to ≈3,956 machines — just inside the top of the committed capacity range. **Given B07's explicit flag that the 60-acre land catalyst has already slipped three consecutive quarters, and that the 13-acre plant itself is still pre-construction as of this run, the SOM/growth trajectory is the more optimistic side of this pairing, not the capex plan** — SOM(5yr) is achievable only if both (a) the 13-acre capacity delivers on the stated ~24-36 month timeline **and** (b) ASP/mix continues its multi-quarter upward trend without further disruption.

### 4C. Market structure

- **Competitor count:** 6-7 organised domestic players by management's own repeated framing ("all the 6, 7 players are neck to neck," Q3 FY26 call, p.6; "six to seven players... 90% of their market share," Q4 FY26 call, p.15).
- **Top-3 concentration:** not independently quantified; management self-assesses Macpower as "top 3 in India" by **product-basket breadth**, not by revenue share (Q3 FY26 call, p.6) — this is a self-assessed, unverified claim, distinct from the revenue-share figures used elsewhere in this report.
- **Organised vs unorganised:** heavily organised; unorganised/Chinese-CKD-assembly participation is small and management-dismissed (<5% of value, entry-level only, "reliability of Chinese machine is zero" — Q3 FY26 call, p.17-18).
- **Consolidating or fragmenting:** directionally consolidating at the top — JYOTICNC is executing a ~₹450cr capex programme nearly tripling capacity (6,000→16,000 machines/yr, per B06-peers) while Macpower and others expand more modestly; no evidence of competitor exits.
- **Price vs differentiation competition:** management explicitly frames selling as feature/reference-based ("speed... RPM... accuracy level... service support... reference list," Q4 FY26 call, p.16), not price-led.
- **Import share trend:** ~54% today, management's own 5-year glide path only to ~45-50% — a slow structural shift.

### 5B. Runway assessment

- **Revenue headroom (SAM ÷ current revenue):** ₹14,900cr ÷ ₹333.18cr ≈ **44.7x**.
- **TAM growth rate:** ~11% (IMTMA-implied CAGR 2026-2030), vs. a lower independent-research CAGR of ~7.5% (IBEF/IMARC 2026-2034) — flagged range, not averaged.
- **Company CAGR vs. TAM:** Macpower's FY22-26 revenue CAGR is 15% (Investor Presentation p.34), and recent realised growth is materially faster (FY26 +27.3% YoY; Q1FY27 +56.1% YoY) — the company is clearly **gaining share**, growing several multiples faster than the ~11% (or 7.5%) TAM growth rate.
- **Years to saturate SAM at current [guided] growth:** using management's own guided 25-30% (midpoint 27.5%) revenue growth against ~11% SAM growth, the ~44.7x headroom closes in **≈25 years** on a simple differential-growth basis. This is a mechanical, illustrative figure — no one should expect 27.5% growth to persist for 25 years — but it correctly signals that penetration-driven runway, not TAM growth, is the dominant lever, and that runway is not a near-term constraint.

### 5C. Runway classification: **MASSIVE**

Basis: revenue headroom >40x, current penetration only ~1-2% of the relevant market (corroborated three independent ways — management's own corrected statement, the SAM-share calc, and the TAM-share calc), double-digit TAM growth, and a company growing several multiples faster than its market. This classification describes market **size**, not investment merit — it sits alongside, and does not override, Gate 0's mechanical **AVOID** verdict (B01) and the Emerging Moat scan's **MODEST** classification (B07), both driven by execution and balance-sheet-quality issues, not by market size.

### 5D. SAM expansion levers actually being pursued

- **JV/technology-transfer with a shortlisted global OEM** (NDA-stage with 3 of 5 shortlisted, per B07 H2 — "Weak" evidence, stalled) would, if it converts, unlock some of the ~₹7,452cr ultra-high-end import-only tier filtered out of SAM in 3A. Illustrative potential addition: capturing even 5-10% of that filtered pool ≈ **₹370-745 cr of revised SAM** — meaningful but speculative and not yet evidenced beyond NDA stage.
- **Export markets** (EMO Germany 2025, stated ambition for Europe/Gulf distribution) would extend geographic scope beyond the India-only boundary set in 1A, but off a <1% revenue base today — a longer-dated, currently unquantifiable optionality, not a near-term SAM addition.
- **Automation/FMS attachment revenue** is an adjacent product category (not core machine-tool TAM) that could add incremental SAM if it scales, but is pre-revenue-scale today (Investor Presentation p.25).

Revised SAM inclusive of the JV-unlock illustrative addition: **≈₹15,270-15,645 cr**, a modest 3-5% uplift over the base ₹14,900cr — reinforcing that the **already-filtered ₹14,900cr SAM is the dominant, realistic addressable pool for the 3-5 year horizon this analysis targets**, not these longer-dated levers.

### 5E. Final output card

- **TAM:** ₹20,000 cr (conservative) / ₹24,000 cr (realistic), India, CNC metal-cutting machine tools, growing ~11%/yr (management-implied) to ~7.5%/yr (independent-research-implied) — flagged divergence.
- **SAM:** ₹14,900 cr (62% of realistic TAM).
- **SOM:** ₹660 cr (3yr, ~25.6% implied revenue CAGR) / ₹989 cr (5yr, ~24.3% implied revenue CAGR).
- **Current SAM share:** 2.24% (independently corroborated against management's own corrected 1-2% claim).
- **Runway:** MASSIVE (44.7x headroom), but capacity/execution — not market size — is the binding constraint (Section 3C: ₹190-290 Cr gap at flat realisation, closing only if both the 13-acre plant and NEXA/defence mix deliver on schedule).
- **Management's own TAM claim (₹35,000cr) vs. the conservative estimate (₹20,000cr, total-machine-tools-scope-matched to ₹16,470cr independent) is ~2.13x — read as "likely inflated."**

**Valuation implication line:** At **24%** revenue CAGR implied by SOM (5yr), with a margin trajectory of PAT margin rising from **10.2%** (FY26) toward an estimated **~13%** (partial progress on management's own 5-6 year, repeatedly-flagged-as-optimistic 25% EBITDA aspiration — B07/B06), the earnings growth embedded here is **~31% CAGR** (PAT ₹33.87cr FY26 → an illustrative ₹128.6cr at 5yr SOM revenue and assumed margin), which **does not clearly support** the current valuation of **~43.7x P/E** (CMP ₹1,481 ÷ FY26 EPS ₹33.86 — manifest.yaml, Investor Presentation p.34). A PEG of ~1.4x at this P/E, combined with Gate 0's mechanical **AVOID** verdict and the repeatedly-flagged execution risks (3x-slipped land catalyst, deteriorating working capital, R&D/IP credibility gap per B07), means the SOM-implied growth is consistent with — but does not conservatively underwrite — the current price, even though the underlying market-size runway itself is genuinely MASSIVE.
