Aimtron grows fast, cash conversion unproven

Aimtron makes electronics for other companies on contract, assembling printed circuit boards, building finished boxes, and doing some design work, for medical, automotive, aerospace, defence, industrial and telecom customers from a plant in Waghodia, Vadodara. The Vasani family controls it, Mukesh Vasani chairs it, and it listed in June 2024. Revenue was Rs158 Cr in FY25 standalone, Rs257 Cr in FY26 audited standalone, and Rs301 Cr on a consolidated basis in FY26. Revenue and profit have both compounded above 65% a year across three years. The balance sheet holds net cash with debt to equity at 0.00.

The mix is shifting and the footprint is widening. Box Build rose from 27% to 69% of standalone revenue in one year while board assembly fell from 69% to 29%. The company bought a US operation in FY26, is building a greenfield Vadodara plant aimed at Rs500 Cr of added capacity, and holds a certification stack (AS9100D, IATF 16949, ISO 13485, CDSCO) that qualifies it into regulated programs.

Gate 0 scores 72 of 160 and reads AVOID, though the label is order sensitive and could read AVERAGE pending an operator ruling. The backward score is pulled down by cash conversion, not by profitability, because the core plus moat reading on its own is GOOD. The forward Emerging Moat scan is 23 of 80, MODEST.

Cash generation lags the profit. Standalone operating cash flow was negative Rs17.69 Cr in FY25 even as profit grew 89% to a record, and FY26 consolidated operating cash flow was negative Rs40 Cr, a two year pattern. Trade receivables rose 417% in FY25 against 70% revenue growth, and 46.3% of receivables were owed by related parties, including one counterparty balance that flipped from a payable to a receivable while sales to it fell.

Management delivered on the numbers and missed on disclosure. It beat its FY26 revenue target, held PAT margin in band, closed the AS9100D certification, and executed the named aerospace order. It also broke a no fundraising promise, never issued the reclassification press release or moved to the quarterly reporting it committed to, and stated related party revenue was under 20% on a call against 27.68% to 31.53% documented. The credibility grade is C.

Related party exposure is the governance question. About 31.5% of FY25 standalone revenue flowed to non consolidated promoter controlled foreign entities, and Chairman Mukesh Vasani is externally disclosed as CEO of the largest of them, Aimtron Corporation USA. The FY26 AGM sought a Rs120 Cr related party ceiling, about 76% of FY25 turnover, with no external valuation. Against that sit a 0% promoter pledge, promoter open market buying, the January 2026 sale of one related party vehicle, and a new FY26 shareholder vote regime on material related party deals.

Four listed EMS peers confirm the demand, not the specifics. Avalon, Centum, Cyient DLM and Vinyas all grew order books through FY26 on a broad industry upcycle, while Aimtron's own pipeline stayed static across three calls. Two Aimtron claims break against peer disclosure: its roughly Rs100 Cr of revenue per SMT line against Vinyas at Rs500 to 600 Cr per line, and its framing of defence as low margin against Centum showing defence at 20% to 25% EBITDA versus 9% to 11% in general EMS.

The runway is real and the headline claim is not. The addressable Indian EMS market runs to roughly Rs2 lakh Cr and grows around 20% a year, giving a STRONG runway class. Management's cited TAM is about 21 times a conservative estimate because it quotes India's entire national electronics output target, and its five year revenue ambition of Rs1,300 Cr sits exactly at the disclosed capacity ceiling with no margin.
