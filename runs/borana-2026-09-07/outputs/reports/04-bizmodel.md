## SECTION 1: THE BUSINESS MODEL IN PLAIN ENGLISH

### 1A. One-line description
Borana buys polyester filament yarn, texturises part of it in-house, and weaves it on water-jet looms into unbleached "greige" fabric, which it sells as a raw input to dyers and processors in the Surat cluster (RHP p.185).

### 1B. Money flow chain, by revenue stream

**Stream 1 — Grey (greige) fabric, ~91% of FY26 revenue** (AR FY2026 p.112, Note 21: Grey Sale Rs 353.41 cr of Rs 388.59 cr total)
[POY yarn purchased] → [texturise in-house into PTY yarn → warp → weave on water-jet looms → fold, quality-check, QR-tag] → [unbleached grey fabric, delivered ex-factory, freight paid by buyer] → [traders and processing houses in Surat, who dye/print/finish it] → [payment on trade terms, no direct exports (AR FY2026 Note 21(2): export revenue nil)]

**Stream 2 — PTY (texturised) yarn, ~4.6% of FY26 revenue** (AR FY2026 p.112, Note 21: Yarn Sale Rs 17.73 cr)
[POY yarn purchased] → [texturised in-house] → [surplus PTY yarn not consumed internally] → [sold to external yarn buyers as a by-product of maintaining spare texturising capacity — "we have added a little extra capacity... whatever extra yarn is being manufactured is being sold, but it is not part and parcel of the business" (Concall Q3FY26, p.13)] → [payment on trade terms]

**Stream 3 — Beam, downgrade, wastage sales, ~4.4% of FY26 revenue** (AR FY2026 p.112: Beam Sale Rs 15.87 cr, Downgrade Rs 0.42 cr, Wastage Rs 1.16 cr) — residual/scrap-value streams, not core.

### 1C. Revenue model classification

| Stream | Type | % of FY26 revenue (anchor) | Predictability |
|---|---|---|---|
| Grey fabric | Manufactured commodity input, sold to processors | 90.95% (Rs 353.41cr / Rs 388.59cr, AR FY2026 p.112) | Medium — volume-driven, price follows input yarn cost + mix |
| PTY yarn | By-product of spare texturising capacity | 4.56% (Rs 17.73cr, AR FY2026 p.112) | Low — explicitly not a core focus (Concall Q3FY26 p.13), share has fallen from 50.78% (FY22, RHP p.179) to 4.56% (FY26) as fabric scaled faster |
| Beam/downgrade/wastage | Residual/scrap value | ~4.5% (AR FY2026 p.112) | Low, mechanical by-product of production mix |

### 1E. The chai-stall-uncle version
Think of Borana as a very large, very fast cloth-weaving workshop. It buys plastic thread (polyester yarn), twists some of it to make it stronger, then feeds it into machines that shoot it through with jets of water instead of a mechanical shuttle — like a power-washer weaving cloth instead of a hand loom. Out comes plain grey cloth, not dyed, not printed, nothing fancy. Borana does not sell this cloth to a shop or a customer wearing it; it sells it to another business in the same city (Surat) that dyes and finishes it. Borana never touches the customer who wears the shirt. It is one link — the weaving link — in a longer chain, and it is paid for speed, volume and consistency, not for a brand.

### 2B. Competitive positioning
Independent verification of named competitors (SAAM Textiles, Paramount, Madhav Fashion, Jaincotex) was not possible from the provided corpus — these come from a single third-party industry report cited throughout the RHP (D&B Report), not from Borana's own filings or a screener cross-check. Treat as low-tier, directional evidence only. On the size claim ("scale that is currently unmatched among companies with a similar product focus," Concall Q3FY26 p.3), no named comparator or market-share figure is provided — flag as an unverified management assertion.

### 2C. Moat assessment

| Moat type | Evidence | Durability |
|---|---|---|
| Network effect | None identifiable | N/A |
| Switching costs | Low — grey fabric is a specification-light commodity; relationships (e.g., the multi-year top-customer list) suggest some stickiness but no contractual lock-in disclosed | Low |

| Regulatory / licensing | None protecting Borana's output; the one regulatory event found (BIS-linked import restriction removal, Dec-2025) went the other way — it cheapened a competitor input too, so it likely helps the whole industry's importers, not Borana specifically, unless Borana was earlier/faster to use it | Low, and a two-edged policy tailwind, not a moat |
| Data / network | Not applicable to this business model | N/A |
| Scarce resource / location | Surat cluster proximity to customers and suppliers (RHP p.186) — real but shared with essentially every competitor in the cluster, so not scarce to Borana specifically | Low |

**Bottom line on moat**: thin. The clearest advantage found in the documents is modern-vintage, higher-speed machinery relative to a still partly outdated competitor base (RHP p.176), plus two time-limited state subsidies. Neither is durable in the sense the QUALITY LADDER requires for an R3+ rung; this fits closer to R2 (cost-advantaged converter) with the Amendment 17 converter multiple cap in view for Section 1B, not higher.

### 2D. Industry lifecycle stage
Growth-stage within a mature overall textile industry: India's polyester filament yarn demand and man-made-fibre share are structurally rising (RHP pp.181-182 cites 4mt to a projected 6.7mt polyester demand), and Borana itself is mid-expansion (loom count 700 at Dec-2024 to 1,212 by Q1FY27, Investor Presentation Q1FY27 p.4), i.e., a company-specific capacity growth phase riding a structurally growing but still cyclical and commodity-priced end market.

### 2E. Key industry drivers

| Driver | Direction | Impact on Borana |
|---|---|---|
| India polyester demand growth (4mt → 6.7mt projected) | Up | Positive, tailwind for volume |
| POY/PFY import policy (BIS restriction removed Dec-2025) | Input cost down | Positive near-term margin, but two-edged (industry-wide, not Borana-specific) |
| Gujarat Textile Policy 2024 subsidies | Time-limited, expiring per-unit 2026-2028 | Currently positive, reversing over FY27-FY29 unless renewables/scale offset it |

| Capacity race (Borana's own 700→2,000 loom plan by Mar-2028) | Up | Execution risk (twice-delayed renewable project, B03) more than demand risk |

### 3A. Ignore-these, track-these (converter-specific)

### 3D. Unit economics — the physics of the business

| Field | Finding |
|---|---|
| Unit | 1 metre of grey (greige) fabric |
| Revenue per unit | Rs 11.27/metre, clean grey-fabric-only realisation, FY24 (Rs 14,366.13 lakh grey-fabric revenue ÷ 12,75,15,434 metres actual production, both RHP p.179-183; arithmetic shown). A second, blended metric — total revenue (all products) ÷ fabric-metres-produced — is what management itself quotes: ~Rs 15.80/m FY25, ~Rs 16.90/m Q3FY26, ~Rs 17.50/m 9M FY26 (Concall Q3FY26 pp.8-9). | Cost per unit | POY yarn cost NOT FOUND on a clean per-metre-of-fabric basis — the input is shared between fabric and yarn output and the documents do not allocate it. | Volume drivers | Loom count and capacity utilisation, not demand pull in the sense of price signalling — production has scaled almost exactly with loom additions (700 → 1,212 looms alongside revenue near-doubling FY24-FY26) |
| Price drivers | Product/GSM mix (winter-weight vs. summer fabric, Concall Q3FY26 p.8), and the December-2025 input-cost tailwind, not evidenced pricing power |
| Cost drivers | POY yarn price and import mix (71-87% of total expenses historically; import share 0%→32.7% in FY26); power and labour are secondary and partly subsidised (time-limited) |
| Incremental margin / operating leverage | Company-guided incremental unit economics for the 160-164 Unit 4B looms: ~Rs 35cr project cost → ~5cr metres/year incremental capacity → ~Rs 60-75cr incremental annual revenue (Concall Q3FY26 pp.6-7), implying an incremental realisation of roughly Rs 12-15/metre on that specific tranche and a capex-to-incremental-revenue ratio near 1:1.7-2.1. Management also states depreciation and interest are currently running below their eventual run-rate because new capacity is not yet fully commissioned/capitalised, so near-term margin gains partly reflect operating leverage that will normalise once new assets are fully depreciated (Concall Q3FY26 p.11) |

**Loom capital-cost anchor** (core converter arithmetic, shown per the run brief's instruction):
- 348 new water-jet looms bought for the original IPO-funded Unit 4, at US$9,500/loom, exchange rate Rs 86/US$ as stated in the RHP = **Rs 8.17 lakh/loom**, ex-installation, ex-duty (RHP pp.114-115)
- Full Unit 4 plant & machinery cost (348 looms + texturiser, warping/beaming machine, transformer, compressor, water treatment, etc.): Rs 45.97cr for the whole package (RHP p.114-115) — not a per-loom figure, since it bundles ancillary equipment
- Unit 4 installed capacity: 11,27,52,000 metres/year (RHP p.181), implying **~3.24 lakh metres/loom/year** for that tranche (11,27,52,000m ÷ 348 looms)
- Separately, the ~160-164-loom "Unit 4B" tranche cost ~Rs 35cr all-in (looms + building + ancillary), implying a **fully-loaded ~Rs 21.3-21.9 lakh/loom** project cost — roughly 2.6-2.7x the bare imported-loom cost, i.e. land, civil works, ancillary machinery and installation are the larger share of the all-in capex, not the loom itself (Concall Q3FY26 pp.6-7)
- That tranche's incremental capacity, ~5cr metres/year ÷ 160-164 looms = **~3.05-3.13 lakh metres/loom/year**, consistent with the Unit 4 RHP-derived figure above — a genuine cross-check that holds up

### 4B. Valuation method applicability

| Method | Applicable? | Notes |
|---|---|---|

Cycle stage that matters for valuation: mid-expansion, capacity-led growth phase, with a live near-term policy/input-cost tailwind (Dec-2025 BIS-restriction removal) layered on top of a structurally elevated but historically volatile 38-41% gross-margin band. Section 1B (framework) governs the actual destination/exit multiple for this CONVERTER; this stage's role is confined to identifying which lens and which earnings base feed that downstream calculation cleanly.

### 4C. Quarterly monitoring checklist

1. Blended capacity utilisation % (good: 80%+ and rising; trouble: falling while capacity grows)
2. Loom/capacity additions vs. guided schedule (good: on-time; trouble: further slippage after two prior delays on the renewable project, B03)
3. Gross margin % (good: holding 38%+; trouble: reverting toward the Q2FY25 trough of 31.7%)
4. EBITDA margin ex-other-income (good: 22%+; trouble: below 20% for two consecutive quarters)
5. Cost of materials consumed as % of revenue (good: stable or falling; trouble: rising, especially alongside rupee weakness)
6. Imported raw material % of total raw material, and hedging disclosure (good: hedging policy disclosed; trouble: rising unhedged imported share)
7. Cash & cash equivalents vs. gross debt (good: cash rebuilding post-capex; trouble: cash near zero while gross debt rises, as at FY26 close)
8. Net working capital days (good: stable near FY24's 39.4; trouble: continuing to rise past FY26's 66.5, B01)
9. Related-party revenue/purchase disclosure, especially Borana Industries LLP's share and any stated pricing basis (good: an arm's-length benchmark disclosed; trouble: continued silence on pricing basis)
10. PTY yarn revenue share and absolute trend (good: stable low single-digit share; trouble: an unexplained spike suggesting fabric demand softness pushing product back to yarn)
11. Renewable project (19.79MW hybrid) commissioning date vs. Unit 1's subsidy lapse date (good: renewable live before 2026 year-end; trouble: further delay past the subsidy lapse)
12. Other Income composition, specifically interest subsidy and one-off items (good: disclosed and modest; trouble: growing reliance on Other Income to support PAT)
13. CWIP-to-PPE capitalisation pace (good: capitalisation tracking commissioning announcements; trouble: CWIP balance growing faster than capitalisation, as in FY26 where only Rs 8.24 lakh of Rs 80.94cr was capitalised, B03)

### 4D. Highest-value questions for management

1. What is the arm's-length pricing policy for grey-fabric sales to Borana Industries LLP, the single largest customer in every disclosed period since FY22? *Reassures*: an independently benchmarked pricing policy. *Worries*: board-approved with no external reference point (consistent with B02's finding of no disclosed price basis for any RPT).
2. What exactly drove the Q2FY25 EBITDA margin trough to 16.49% (gross margin 31.7%), and could it recur? *Reassures*: a clearly identified, now-resolved one-off. *Worries*: a recurring seasonal or raw-material-timing pattern.
3. With Unit 1's power subsidy window closing in 2026 and the hybrid renewable project now on its third commissioning date, what is the contingency if renewable power is not live before the subsidy lapses? *Reassures*: a costed bridge plan. *Worries*: no contingency beyond "renewables will cover it."
4. Of the Rs 60-75cr incremental annual revenue guided for the Unit 4B looms, how much has materialised in the quarters since commissioning? *Reassures*: on or ahead of the guided run-rate. *Worries*: an unflagged shortfall.
5. How much of the FY26 gross-margin gain (38.80%→41.37%, and 44.82% in Q4FY26 alone) is durable once competitors also access the cheaper post-restriction Chinese POY, versus a temporary window? *Reassures*: an edge beyond the import-cost window (scale, contracts, timing). *Worries*: margin drifting back toward the 38-41% band as the window closes for everyone.
6. Is the ~32.7%-of-raw-material FY26 import book now hedged, and to what extent? *Reassures*: a formal FX/price hedging policy. *Worries*: continued full unhedged exposure.
7. What is the realistic funding plan for the Rs 350-400cr, 700→2,000-loom expansion by March-2028, given equity dilution has been ruled out and cash fell to Rs 1.68cr at FY26 close? *Reassures*: a phased, self-funded plan with headroom. *Worries*: a funding gap forcing dilution or a re-levered balance sheet.
