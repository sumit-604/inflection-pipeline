Good polymer business priced above the entry

Jubilant Agri and Consumer Products is two businesses inside one listed company that is splitting in two. One half makes adhesives, latex and food grade polymers and is retained, becoming Jubilant Industries. The other half makes fertiliser and agri nutrients and leaves as Jubilant Agri Solutions. The shares trade at Rs 2,342, a market cap of Rs 3,549 Cr. FY26 revenue was Rs 1,895 Cr and PAT Rs 128 Cr.

The demerger is the whole story, so you value the two halves apart. FY26 profit growth came almost entirely from the fertiliser segment that is being demerged out, while the retained polymer segment grew profit under 1 percent. On a sum of the parts, fair value is near Rs 4,625 Cr and about 88 percent of it sits in the polymer half. The agri half is worth roughly Rs 250 Cr.

The polymer business earns 67.5 percent on capital at the segment level, against a group figure of 36 percent, and it is nearly debt free. Adhesives are 28 percent of the mix and compounding in the high teens, with Q1 FY27 up 19 percent on revenue and 40 percent on EBIT. The other 72 percent is VP latex and food grade PVAc, where the company is number one in India, growing about 10 percent at high margin. Net debt to equity is 0.06 and interest cover is 24 times.

Gate 0 scores 86 of 160, GOOD+, moat STRONG, no deal breakers. Cash conversion fell in FY26: operating cash flow was 59 percent of PAT against 149 percent the year before, driven by a fertiliser subsidy receivable up 92 percent. That drag sits in the agri division that is leaving, and pre working capital cash flow still grew 34 percent, so it reads as structural and located, not a decline in profit quality. No standalone polymer cash statement exists yet.

Credibility grades C. Samlaya was commissioned in the guided quarter with independent confirmation, the agri inventory sell down target was missed and blamed on a weak monsoon, and the demerger has cleared every regulatory gate on time. The Chairman's letter praised the agri business and diversification without once naming the Board approved demerger of that entire division. Governance is concentrated: a Finance Committee with Rs 1,250 Cr borrowing authority and zero independent directors, key management pay up 64 percent against 10 percent for staff, and CSR routed fully through the family foundation.

Peers back the near term excuses and undercut the longer story. KRISHANA confirms the weak monsoon, and four peers confirm the West Asia input cost spike. Apcotex printed record latex volumes and EBITDA in the same year JACPL called latex demand subdued, and the market share gain claim cannot be verified because the nearest peers were themselves growing fast.

Runway is STRONG. The addressable adhesives market is near Rs 50,000 Cr against a current served share of 2.5 percent, so this is a share gain catch up, not a mature market: JACPL runs 80,000 MTPA of polymer capacity against Pidilite's 600,000 plus. Capacity, not demand, is the near term ceiling, and the 24 percent revenue growth the runway implies sits well above the audited 7.5 percent, so treat it as an upper bound until a full year confirms the Q1 acceleration.

Destination PE is 35 times for the polymer business on both valuation tracks, where the specialty chemicals cap binds, about 13 to 14 times for the cyclical agri stub, and 29.5 times blended for the entity as it trades. The entry zone is Rs 1,766 to Rs 2,208 per share and the margin of safety price is Rs 1,766. CMP Rs 2,342 sits about 6 percent above the entry and above every valuation floor. The Hurdle Ratio is 1.87, a conditional pass that clears 25 percent only on forward earnings and only in the bull case; on trailing FY26 polymer earnings near 33 times the hurdle is a stop. The call is WATCHLIST and buy on dips, with a 12 to 15 percent pullback or the first standalone polymer accounts turning it into a buy. Agri is AVOID.
