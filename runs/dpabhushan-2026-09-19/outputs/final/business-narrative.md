# D. P. Abhushan Ltd (DPABHUSHAN): business narrative

Run 2026-09-19. PHASE 1 LITE: evidence stages 0 to 9 and verifiers A, B, C (Gate 0 and Emerging Moat half) and D. No valuation has run. This file carries no entry zone, no decision and no destination multiple.

## BUSINESS UNDERSTANDING NARRATIVE

D. P. Abhushan sells hallmarked gold, diamond, silver and platinum jewellery through 12 showrooms in Madhya Pradesh and Rajasthan under the D. P. Jewellers name. Showroom sales made up 87% of FY26 revenue, and gold carries about nine tenths of that value. Families buy these pieces for weddings and festivals and keep them as savings, and the showroom sells assurance of purity and weight behind an 86 year local name. A second line, reported only as non retail operations, made up 12% of revenue and fell 6% in FY26; the run did not establish what it sells or to whom. The buyers are households in Tier 2 and Tier 3 towns who pay at the counter, so no single customer matters and receivables are close to zero. Switching costs are low, because one jewellery purchase does not bind the next; the DP Swarn Plus ten month savings plan and old gold exchange, about a quarter of Q1 FY27 sales, are the only ties that bring a family back. Present demand rides on weddings, festivals and the gold price itself: the MCX/LBMA domestic gold price sets roughly 91% of revenue value, and the IMD monsoon/kharif season forecast and rural income indicators set what a rural family can spend. That price link cuts both ways, because in FY26 gold grams sold fell 13% while gold revenue rose 21%. Demand should grow because the organised share of Indian gold jewellery, about 37% in CY25, is projected to reach the mid forties by CY29 to CY30 inside a market growing about 12% a year. The outside signals for that shift are the BIS hallmarking district-coverage expansion, at 385 districts by May 2026, and the ICRA/CRISIL/IBEF notes on organised-vs-unorganised jewellery retail share, while the World Gold Council India Gold Demand Trends (quarterly) tests the volume side. The CBIC/Ministry of Finance gold and silver import duty notifications cut the other way, since duty rose from 6% to 15% in May 2026 and lifts landed cost for every seller. Company growth also depends on new stores, and Dahod in Gujarat, a first franchise site at Jabalpur and Jodhpur are its first steps beyond the home base. The advantage in the showroom line is regional trust: an 86 year name, first mover presence in towns such as Ratlam, and central buying from Ratlam within about 300 km, each of medium durability and untested outside two states. On the gold value itself the company has no pricing power, because bullion passes straight through, and its two levers are making charges and diamond mix, where diamond revenue fell 5% in FY26. The emerging moat scan found no meaningful emerging moat: only the Gujarat first mover step and the hallmarking tailwind scored, the tailwind is shared with every organised chain, and the two structural asymmetry categories, 21 and 22, scored zero. Competitor store-opening announcements in MP/Rajasthan/Gujarat/Chhattisgarh/Maharashtra are the watch item for that regional moat, because PNG Jewellers now trades in Indore and Senco has built a Central region. The non retail line and the hedging income booked inside revenue show no moat, and the run did not establish what protects either.

Gold price carried growth, cash went backwards

D. P. Abhushan is an 86 year old Ratlam jeweller run by the Kataria family. It buys bullion, has outside goldsmiths make the jewellery, and sells it from 12 showrooms, 11 of them rented. FY26 revenue was Rs 4,070 Cr and PAT Rs 212 Cr. The audited accounts treat it as one segment, so the gold, diamond and silver split exists only in the investor decks.

The company says it is climbing. EBITDA margin rose from about 5.3% in FY25 to 7.6% in FY26 and printed 11.0% in Q1 FY27. Management targets 51 stores by FY30, a diamond share of 12 to 15% by March 2028, a first franchise store and an online channel. The evidence so far says price did most of the lifting. Gold grams sold fell 13% in FY26 while revenue per gram rose about 39%. Diamond revenue fell in FY26 and again in Q1 FY27. The network added one net store in FY26. Management put the inventory gain share of the margin story anywhere from 10% to 30%, on a different basis on each of four calls. Two readings stay open: a real step in making charges and mix, or holding gains on a record gold run. The observation that separates them is the margin in a quarter when gold is flat or falling.

The backward scorecard shows the same split. ROCE was 46% and ROE 41% in FY26. Revenue compounded 28% a year over nine years and profit 52%. Operating cash flow went the other way: Rs 0.3 Cr negative in FY24, Rs 19 Cr negative in FY25 and Rs 97 Cr negative in FY26. Cumulative operating cash over ten years is below zero against Rs 541 Cr of profit, which caps Gate 0 at AVERAGE whatever the returns say. The moat profile scores STRONG, five of twelve, after the framework verifier corrected an overscored network effects test. Accounting quality rates 6 of 10. An Rs 18.6 Cr MCX hedging gain, nil a year earlier, sits inside revenue. Two audited notes disagree on the FY26 raw material closing stock by Rs 50 Cr, and the company does not explain it. The audit opinion is clean, contingent liabilities are 0.1% of net worth, and no restatement was found.

The cash reality is a stock build paid for with borrowed money. In FY26 inventory rose 39% to Rs 1,004 Cr while trade payables fell 36% to Rs 114 Cr. Borrowings rose 77% to about Rs 290 Cr, including Rs 60 Cr of new gold metal loans and leased gold, while promoter loans fell. Seven family members guarantee every bank facility and six properties, several family owned, are pledged. Gold grams in stock fell 6% in the same year, so the rupee rise came from price, not from filling new shelves. One reading calls this growth: Dhar opened at year end with Rs 100 to 125 Cr of stock and more stores follow. The other calls it structural: a gold seller's stock reprices with gold, and the gain shows in profit but stays on the shelf. The pipeline could not settle which, so the cash determination is INDETERMINATE. The half year cash flow filed with Q2 FY27 results is the next test. CARE reaffirmed A minus, Stable, in January 2026 on FY25 figures, when the operating cycle was 54 days. Its downgrade trigger is an operating cycle beyond 75 days.

Management's words and its delivery part ways more often than they meet. FY26 revenue growth of 23% and EBITDA margin of 7.6% landed inside the ranges given in November 2025. Most later promises slipped. A 25 to 30% growth "minimum" was missed. An FY27 margin guide of 6 to 6.5% was followed two months later by 11.0% with no revision. The store plan changed three times, and a Rs 600 Cr QIP went from "soon" to silence. Operating cash was asked about twice and deflected twice. The concall stage grades credibility C, and Verifier B puts it at the C to D boundary. Verifier B also found that the Q1 FY27 margin exceeds the CFO's own stated normal gross margin, and that the company never mentions the customs duty stock gain its peers quantified. On governance the promoter verdict is CAUTION. Two independent directors left mid term in FY25 with no replacement, and related party loan churn is large. On the other side, an outside CFO joined in June 2025, the promoter pledge is zero, and the first employee stock options went out.

Peers confirm the setting and undercut the story told about it. Senco, PNG Jewellers and Kalyan all describe the same festive peaks, the May 2026 duty hike and the soft May and June demand. None supports an industry volume fall of 20 to 29%: Senco reported minus 10% for nine months and PNG Jewellers reported plus 25 to 27%. All three give a hedge ratio and a numeric bridge for every guidance gap on every call. DPABHUSHAN gives neither. The 42 kg of unfixed gold in the FY26 notes is disputed. Three stages read it as open exposure, while Verifier B reads it as the gold loan hedge itself, and Halt 1 must re-read the note. The first franchise also runs in reverse of Kalyan's model, because the company bears the costs and the franchisee keeps the gold gain.

The market is not the constraint. Organised gold led jewellery in West and Central India is about Rs 53,000 Cr, the company holds about 7.7% of it, and headroom is about 13 times current revenue, a STRONG runway. The base case share path implies revenue growth of 25 to 26% a year. The Rs 15,000 Cr FY30 target appears only in a press report and implies 39% a year, above management's own guidance on the calls. The binding limit is money for stock. The market stage estimated a Rs 1,150 to 1,750 Cr working capital need for the FY30 path, against borrowings of about Rs 290 Cr and a QIP on hold.
