## 1. A SECOND MAJOR RELATED-PARTY LENDER PASS 1 DID NOT NAME: JITF COMMODITY TRADEX LIMITED

- 🔴 Pass 1's Section 2 attributed the Group's entire ₹1,85,715.12 lakh related-party borrowings
  figure (Note 20, p.179) to Siddheshwari Tradex Private Limited alone. Re-reading Note 20's
  entity-level loan breakdown (p.185) and Note 42.14 Related Party Balances (p.229) shows the
  total is actually split between **two separate promoter-group lenders**: Siddheshwari Tradex
  Private Limited (₹1,64,148.20 lakh) **and JITF Commodity Tradex Limited (₹21,575.36 lakh,
  down from ₹38,970.55 lakh, -44.6% YoY)** — a second, previously unidentified promoter-group
  entity (confirmed on the "entities falling under same promoter group" list, Note 42.14, p.225)
  extending sizeable secured-style intercompany debt to JUISL and JITF Urban Infrastructure Ltd
  at 9.95%-10.70% p.a. monthly compounding (Note 20, p.185).
- 🔴 JITF Commodity Tradex Limited also charged the Group interest expense of **₹3,178.11 lakh**
  (FY26) vs ₹3,884.84 lakh (FY25) (Note 42.14, p.227) — on top of Siddheshwari Tradex's
  ₹14,895.32 lakh already flagged in Pass 1. **Combined related-party interest expense =
  ₹18,073.43 lakh, which is ~45.0% of the Group's total Finance Cost of ₹40,128.90 lakh**
  (Note 39, p.189) — nearly half of all Group interest expense flows to two promoter-family
  lending vehicles. Pass 1 flagged promoter-entity funding dependency qualitatively; this
  quantifies it and should sharpen the finding materially.

## 2. NON-CONTROLLING INTEREST IN JWIL INFRA IS ITSELF A PROMOTER-GROUP ENTITY

- 🔴 New finding, not identified in Pass 1: the 45.40% non-controlling shareholder of JWIL Infra
  Limited (the subsidiary that carries the ₹4,592.17 lakh ESOP charge) is **Samarpan Infra
  Holdings Private Limited**, which is listed as an "entit[y] falling under same promoter group"
  in the RPT related-party list (Note 42.14, p.225) and received a dividend of **₹373.73 lakh in
  FY26, up from ₹93.43 lakh in FY25 (+300% YoY)** (Note 42.14 Related Party Transactions,
  p.229) — an amount that reconciles almost exactly to 45.4% of JWIL Infra's ₹823.37 lakh
  FY26 dividend payout (Note 42.37, p.256). This means the "non-controlling interest" in the
  Group's key subsidiary is not independent third-party capital; the minority stake — and the
  cash dividend flowing to it — sits with another entity in the same promoter family. This
  materially changes how NCI at JWIL Infra should be read and was entirely absent from Pass 1.
- 🟡 More broadly, subsidiaries **paid** (not merely proposed) dividends totalling ~₹5,126.32
  lakh during FY26 — JWIL Infra ₹823.37 lakh, TOWMCL ₹1,499.88 lakh, TWEPL ₹425.00 lakh,
  JUWM (Vishakhapatnam) ₹965.94 lakh, JUWM (Guntur) ₹1,412.13 lakh (Note 42.37, pp.256-257)
  — Pass 1 characterised this note only as "routine post-year-end dividend proposals"; in fact
  substantial cash was actually distributed during the year at the subsidiary level, at a time
  the consolidated Group carries negative net worth of ₹(51,327.81) lakh and 115.63% gearing.
  Worth a management question on capital-allocation priority given the stressed consolidated
  balance sheet.

## 3. SHAREHOLDING STRUCTURE: SIDDHESHWARI TRADEX IS ALSO A TOP-5 SHAREHOLDER, AND THE NAMED "PROMOTER" HOLDS NEAR-ZERO EQUITY

- 🟡 Pass 1's RPT section extensively covered Siddheshwari Tradex Private Limited's roles as
  guarantor and lender but never connected this to the shareholding table (Note 11, standalone
  p.122-123; Note 19(A), consolidated p.176-177): Siddheshwari Tradex holds **11.68% of
  JITFINFRA's equity directly** (30,02,350 shares), making it simultaneously the Group's
  largest related-party lender, principal loan-security/guarantee provider, AND a top-4
  shareholder of the listed parent — a concentration of financial roles in one promoter-group
  counterparty worth flagging explicitly.
  All four >5% shareholders (Nalwa Sons Investments 16.75%, Four Seasons Investments 13.61%,
  Sigma Tech Inc 9.42%, Siddheshwari Tradex 11.68% = 51.46% combined) are themselves classified
  as "Promoter Group" entities (Note 11(d), p.122), not the named "Promoter."
- 🟡 The individually-named "Promoter," Prithavi Raj Jindal, holds only **7,934 shares = 0.03%**
  of the company (Note 11(d), p.122; Note 19(A)(d), p.177) — effectively all economic control
  (63.02% combined) sits in a web of investment-company and family-trust vehicles classified as
  "Promoter Group" rather than with the named promoter individual. Not unlawful or unusual for
  Indian family-controlled listed companies, but a governance-opacity point an investor should
  register alongside the guarantee/lending concentration above.

## 4. A DIRECT CONTRADICTION BETWEEN TWO STANDALONE NOTES: LOAN TO A KMP

- 🔴 Standalone Note 24.11.7 (Related Party Transactions, p.147) discloses **"Loan given during

  ₹13.50 lakh"** in FY25-26, with ₹1.69 lakh repaid and **₹10.13 lakh outstanding as loan

  standalone financial statements — a disclosure-accuracy red flag warranting a management
  question, independent of the loan's small size.

## 5. RETENTION MONEY: A LARGER, FASTER-GROWING WORKING-CAPITAL COMPONENT THAN PASS 1'S RECEIVABLES ANALYSIS CAPTURED

- 🔴 Pass 1's DSO/receivables-growth analysis (Section 4) used only the ageing-note trade
  receivables figure (₹1,41,932.47 lakh, Note 42.24). Re-reading the balance-sheet notes shows
  **two additional retention-money components not folded into that analysis**:
  (a) Non-current trade receivables of ₹14,656.79 lakh (up from ₹13,718.71 lakh, +6.8% YoY),
  explicitly footnoted as **"retention money"** (Note 7, p.174);
  (b) Current trade receivables **already include** retention money of **₹17,912.99 lakh, up
  from ₹10,598.61 lakh, +69.0% YoY** (Note 12, p.175, footnote "@").
  **Combined retention money = ₹32,569.78 lakh (FY26) vs ₹24,317.32 lakh (FY25), +33.9% YoY**
  — cash the Group has earned on EPC contracts but cannot collect until defect-liability/
  retention periods lapse, growing faster than revenue. This materially reinforces (and adds a
  cleaner, more specific anchor to) Pass 1's FLAG-CASH working-capital deterioration finding.
- 🟡 Mirror-image item on the liability side: **retention money payable to the Group's own
  subcontractors** rose from ₹4,911.66 lakh to ₹5,875.13 lakh, **+19.6% YoY** (Note 22, p.185,
  "Other non-current financial liabilities — Retention Money") — the Group both suffers
  customer retention and imposes its own retention on subcontractors; useful balancing context,
  not previously noted.

## 6. TWO CUSTOMER-ADVANCE FIGURES IN DIFFERENT NOTES DO NOT RECONCILE

- 🟡 Note 29, "Other current liabilities" (p.187): **Advance from customer ₹7,450.99 lakh (FY26)
  vs ₹15,255.45 lakh (FY25), -51.2% YoY**.
  Note 42.29, "Contract liabilities (Advance from customers)" (p.243): **Closing balance
  ₹15,927.16 lakh (FY26) vs ₹19,566.35 lakh (FY25), -18.6% YoY** — the figure Pass 1 cited.
  These are two differently-scoped "advance from customer" balances reported in different notes
  with no cross-reference or reconciliation disclosed between them. Both point the same
  direction (advances falling as the Group increasingly pre-finances EPC customers, consistent
  with Pass 1's finding), but the lack of reconciliation between two notes purporting to
  describe overlapping balances is itself a disclosure-consistency question worth raising with
  management.

## 7. CAPEX-IN-PROGRESS BALANCES ARE GROWING FAR FASTER THAN THE CAPITAL-COMMITMENTS FIGURE ALONE SUGGESTED

- 🔴 Capital work-in-progress (PPE) rose from ₹206.72 lakh to **₹1,210.03 lakh, +485.4% YoY**
  (Note 42.23, p.239, ageing table) — of which ₹91.75 lakh has sat in the 1-2 year bucket.
- 🔴 Intangible assets under development (concession-asset construction, likely the
  not-yet-commissioned Jodhpur, Kakinada and Nellore WtE plants) rose from ₹4,065.75 lakh to
  **₹19,406.80 lakh, +377.3% YoY** (Note 42.27, p.241-242) — with **₹404.07 lakh sitting in
  the "more than 3 years" ageing bucket**, i.e. a small pocket of long-stalled project spend not
  yet capitalised or written off, worth a management question on impairment risk for that
  aged tranche. Together these figures give a much sharper, quantified picture of the capex
  acceleration behind Pass 1's "capital commitments more than doubled" finding (Note 42.12).
- 🟡 Pre-operative expenses pending allocation within intangibles-under-development (Note 42.28,
  p.242) show **salaries and wages of ₹604.25 lakh (FY26) / ₹1,133.98 lakh (FY25)** and interest
  and other operating-type costs being capitalised into project cost rather than expensed —
  standard under Ind AS for assets under construction, but a legitimate area to probe under
  "expense capitalisation honesty" given the scale (₹28.53 crore of costs, including payroll,
  parked on the balance sheet rather than in the P&L this year).

## 8. LIQUIDITY ACTUALLY IMPROVED — A COUNTERPOINT TO THE WORKING-CAPITAL DETERIORATION NARRATIVE

- 🟢 Cash and cash equivalents rose from ₹13,385.42 lakh to **₹20,042.23 lakh, +49.7% YoY**
  (Note 13, p.175), and other bank balances (FDs >3 months, largely pledged as guarantee margin
  or DSRA) rose from ₹20,550.05 lakh to ₹23,635.41 lakh, +15.0% (Note 14, p.175). Combined
  cash-like balances grew **+28.7% YoY** (₹33,935.47 lakh → ₹43,677.64 lakh). This was not
  mentioned in Pass 1's working-capital discussion and is a genuine, if partial, offset to the
  receivables/payables deterioration finding — though it should be read alongside the +13% YoY
  rise in total borrowings (i.e., liquidity growth appears partly debt-funded, not purely
  organic cash generation).

## 9. AUDIT TRAIL / SYSTEM-ADMINISTRATOR LOGGING GAP (STANDALONE AND CONSOLIDATED)

- 🟡 Both standalone (Note 24.19, p.154) and consolidated (Note 42.38(b), p.257) disclose: "The
  Company enabled audit trail in all the tables throughout the year except that as per the ERP

  discussed in Pass 1's accounting-policy section.

## 11. POSSIBLE DRAFTING ERROR IN THE NAYA RAIPUR GOING-CONCERN NOTE

- 🟡 Note 42.15(1) / cross-referenced Note 42.26(a) on JITF Water Infra (Naya Raipur) Ltd states
  the concession lapse "indicates that a material uncertainty exist that may cast significant
  doubt on **NRDA's** ability to continue as a going concern" (p.231/240). NRDA is introduced in
  the same note as "the grantor" (the local authority counterparty), not the reporting entity.
  Read literally, the note describes the *grantor's* going-concern risk, which does not track
  with the substance of the disclosure (a municipal/local authority does not typically carry
  corporate going-concern risk) or with the auditors' actual Material Uncertainty paragraph on
  the Company (per Pass 1, Section 12/Top Findings #2). This reads as a drafting/proofreading
  error (likely should reference "the Company's" going concern) rather than a substantive new
  risk, but is a disclosure-quality point worth flagging.

## 12. COVID MORATORIUM-INTEREST-CONVERTED-TO-TERM-LOAN APPEARS IN MORE SUBSIDIARIES THAN THE TWO PASS 1 IDENTIFIED

- 🟡 Pass 1 (Section 7) identified this legacy stress indicator at only TWEPL (₹716.41 lakh) and

  interest-capitalisation feature also appears under the Jindal Urban Waste Management (Guntur)

  legacy Covid-era interest-capitalisation feature is present in more than two subsidiaries
  across the WtE portfolio, all amortising out to FY36 — was not captured in Pass 1's summary.

# PASS 2 NEW FINDINGS SUMMARY

Twelve genuinely new items surfaced on re-read, the most consequential being: (1) a second,
previously unnamed promoter-group lender, JITF Commodity Tradex Limited (~₹216 crore
outstanding), which combined with Siddheshwari Tradex means related-party interest is ~45% of
total Group finance cost; (2) JWIL Infra's 45.4% non-controlling interest is held by another
promoter-group entity, Samarpan Infra Holdings Private Limited, which received a dividend up
300% YoY — meaning the Group's "minority" capital and cash leakage are not truly external; (3) a
direct contradiction between two standalone notes on whether a loan was made to a KMP (it was —
to the Whole-Time Director/CEO — while a separate regulatory note asserts no such loan exists);
(4) retention money (a receivables sub-component) growing 33.9% YoY on a combined non-current +
embedded-current basis, sharpening the FLAG-CASH working-capital finding with a cleaner anchor;
and (5) capital/intangible work-in-progress balances up 377-485% YoY, quantifying the capex
acceleration behind Pass 1's capital-commitments finding far more precisely. A genuine
counterpoint was also found: cash and bank balances grew ~29% YoY, partly offsetting (though
likely debt-funded rather than organically generated) the working-capital deterioration
narrative. These findings should be carried into Pass 3 for pattern-testing against the
main financial statements and the auditors' report, and into the consolidated Top 15 and Key
Risks tables.
