## Where the market stands

Each stock in the record has three trend lines: 20 days, 50 days and 200 days. A stock above a line is in an uptrend on that clock. On Thursday 1,714 stocks stood above the short line, nearly three in four. The record last showed more on 8 May. 1,335 stood above the middle line, nearly six in ten. 1,143 stood above the long line, a little under half. The short trend belongs to the buyers. The middle trend belongs to the buyers by a wider margin than Wednesday. The long trend is almost even, with the sellers ahead by 60 stocks.

The year gives these counts their scale. On 23 March the long count hit a low of 286, on a day when 960 stocks fell hard. On 1 April came a great thrust day, when 1,128 stocks rose hard. The long count then climbed through the summer and peaked at 1,367 on 26 August. It slid through September. The last day with more stocks above the long line than below it was 23 September. The count fell to 879 on 8 October. A short repair followed, and then the floor broke. On 21 October 414 stocks fell hard in one day. On 23 October only 605 stocks held the long line. Thursday's 1,143 sits more than 500 stocks above that low.

The bounce is now about three weeks old. On 23 October only 188 stocks held the 20 day line. By 5 November the count had reached 1,494. Friday 6 November brought the first test, with 151 hard fallers. Monday was quiet and thin. Wednesday was the wide day, with 186 hard risers on the best turnover of the week. Thursday did not match that width. It did not give any of it back either.

Thursday itself was a day of rest with a positive lean. The hard risers outnumbered the hard fallers by about six to one, against about nine to one on Wednesday. About one stock in five traded four percent above its prior close at some point, down from about one in three. Four stocks in ten stayed inside a narrow three percent range, up from about three in ten. Turnover came to about 490 crore. A year ago the same session saw about 430. About six stocks traded on heavy volume for every ten on light volume. On Wednesday the two were about equal. The crowd stepped back a little, and prices held.

The top of the market held its width while the bottom stayed empty. New yearly highs came to 62, close to Wednesday's 66. New yearly lows came to seven for a second day. The stocks within fifteen percent of a yearly high now equal the stocks within fifteen percent of a yearly low. Each group is a little under one stock in five. A month ago the low group was far larger. The record last showed the high group ahead on 25 September.

Fear eased on Thursday, for the first time in a way worth noting. The India VIX measures what traders pay for protection against big Nifty swings. It fell about five percent on the day and closed near 16.6. It is down almost ten percent on the week. It is still up almost half over three months and sits about fifteen percent above its thirty week line. Gold funds fell about seven percent over the month. The Nifty rose about twelve percent over the same month. Money has left the safe asset for stocks, and the price of insurance has started to follow it down.

## Thirty points

### For the trader

**1. Thursday was a rest day that leaned up.** 123 stocks rose hard and 21 fell hard, about six to one. Wednesday showed 186 against 20. The buyers did less, and the sellers did no more. A rest after a wide day is normal, and this one cost nothing.

**2. The volume stepped back.** Turnover fell to about 490 crore from about 570. About six stocks traded on heavy volume for every ten on light volume. A quiet day that holds its gains is a good sign. It is not proof, because proof needs a wide day on heavy turnover.

**3. The thrust test from the previous brief stays open.** It asked for 200 or more hard risers on turnover above about 610 crore. Wednesday fell just short with 186 on about 570. Thursday brought 123 on about 490. The bounce has not yet had a thrust day to confirm it.

**4. The swing vote added to its lead.** 1,335 stocks hold the 50 day line, against 1,011 below it. That is 76 more than Wednesday and the best count since 11 August. The middle clock is the one that moved most on a quiet day. A fall back under half the market, about 1,170, would reverse it.

**5. The short count is stretched.** 1,714 stocks stand above the 20 day line, nearly three in four. The record last showed more on 8 May. A count this high leaves few stocks left to join. A stall or a dip here for a few sessions would be ordinary.

**6. Gains still fade by the close.** About four in ten stocks that traded four percent up kept that gain. That share has stayed in a narrow band all week. Sellers still use strength to leave. Half or more holding their gains would mark a real change in who controls the close.

**7. Small companies led large ones on the day.** The small and micro company indices rose about one percent each. The Nifty rose less than a fifth of one percent. ## What would change this read

The read is an early uptrend that widened on Wednesday and held on Thursday, led by banks, parts makers and healthcare. It turns worse if a day of 300 or more hard fallers returns and goes unanswered. A 50 day count back under half the market, about 1,170, would undo the week's main gain. A short count back under about 1,270, the 6 November level, would say the bounce is losing its grip. A long count back under about 1,050, Monday's level, would erase this week's progress. A long count under about 605, the 23 October low, would erase the whole bounce. New yearly lows above about 100 would say sellers have returned. A VIX close back above about 18 would say fear has taken charge again. A sharp fall in banks on heavy volume would remove the group now carrying the market.

The cautious read fails if the long clock clears half and the fear gauge keeps falling. The first test is the long count above about 1,173, half the market. Thursday reached 1,143. The second test is a wide up day, with 200 or more hard risers, on turnover above about 610 crore. Thursday brought 123 on about 490. The third test is a VIX close under 15. Thursday closed near 16.6. The fourth test is gains held into the close. About half of the stocks that trade four percent up should keep it. Thursday held about four in ten. Two of these in the same week, with the 50 day count still above half, would confirm the uptrend on every clock.
