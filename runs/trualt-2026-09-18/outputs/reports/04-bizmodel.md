# STAGE 4 — BUSINESS MODEL DECODER
## TruAlt Bioenergy Ltd (TRUALT) | Run date 2026-09-18

Archetype (CLAUDE.md ARCHETYPE LIBRARY): **Commodity converter.** TruAlt buys
farm feedstock (sugarcane juice/syrup, B-heavy and C-heavy molasses, maize,
surplus rice) and converts it into fuel ethanol sold to Oil Marketing
Companies (OMCs) at a price the Government of India fixes by feedstock type
each Ethanol Supply Year (prospectus p.233-234). The company earns the
spread between that fixed output price and its feedstock cost, not pricing
power over its buyer. Utilisation (currently 60.57%, Inv. Pres. Q1FY27
slide, p.6), feedstock-cost cycle position (maize at Rs17-22/kg versus
Rs25.50/kg, concall Aug-2026 p.8-9) and cost-curve rank (largest installed
capacity in a fragmented field, prospectus p.232) are exactly the four
variables the archetype names. This classification binds Section 1B
Amendment 17: spot-year ROCE and rupee-denominated working-capital trends
must not feed Section 1B or FTTCP for TRUALT.

## SECTION 1: THE BUSINESS MODEL IN PLAIN ENGLISH

### 1A. One-line description
TruAlt turns sugarcane by-products and grain into fuel ethanol, sells most
of it to government oil companies at a government-fixed price, and is
trying to add gas and jet fuel to the same five factories.

### 1B. Money flow chain, by revenue stream

**Ethanol (1G) to OMCs.** Sugarcane juice/syrup/molasses (own distilleries,
~50% of Q1FY27 volume, concall Aug-2026 p.8) or maize/rice (dual-feed
distilleries, ~50% of Q1FY27 volume) -> fermented and distilled at five
Karnataka units, 2,000 KLPD installed, 1,300 KLPD dual-feed (AR p.21/41) ->
delivered to OMC depots -> OMC (Indian Oil, BPCL, HPCL type buyers,
prospectus p.300 names Customer 1-4 as "(OMC)") pays a price the
Government fixes per feedstock type for the Ethanol Supply Year
(prospectus p.234) -> paid on delivery, point-in-time recognition (AR
Note 26/A(i), p.340).

**Extra Neutral Alcohol (ENA) to spirits makers.** Same distillation
process, diverted to a higher-purity cut -> sold to John Distilleries,
InBrew Beverages, Amrut Distilleries, Elite Vintage Winery (prospectus
p.300) -> private commercial buyers pay a market price, typically higher
realisation and shorter payment cycle than ethanol (prospectus p.300).

**Compressed Biogas (CBG) and Fermented Organic Manure (FOM).**
Agricultural/organic waste -> anaerobic digestion at one operational
10.20 TPD plant, Leafiniti Bioenergy (51% TruAlt) (AR p.22/43) -> gas sold
to OMCs and GAIL under 10-year and 15-year offtake agreements (prospectus
p.300); solid/liquid residue sold as FOM to fertiliser companies and
farmers, one contract at Rs5,565/MT including GST with a PSU chemicals and
fertiliser buyer (prospectus p.300).

**By-products (DDGS, CO2, dry ice).** Grain distillation residue -> Dried
Distillers Grains with Solubles, commenced Q3 FY26 (Inv. Pres. Q1FY27
p.5) -> animal feed buyers. CO2 from fermentation -> liquid CO2 and dry
ice -> industrial gas buyers.

**Retail fuel outlets.** TruAlt holds OMC status itself (AR p.19/39, "the
first biofuels company... to attain OMC status") -> 7 outlets operational,
4 under construction of a planned 100 (AR p.24/44) -> motor spirit and
high-speed diesel sold direct to retail consumers, a nascent downstream
leg.

**Government scheme income.** Not a customer-paid revenue stream but a
policy-contingent add-on: Performance Linked Incentive (1.75% of eligible
turnover, booked in revenue, Rs2,571.73 lakh FY26, AR Note 26, p.340) and
interest subvention (booked in other income, Rs5,446.46 lakh FY26, AR
Note 27, p.341). Together these were 73.2% of standalone PBT in FY26
(B02/B03 finding, Notes 26-28).

### 1E. The chai-stall-uncle version
Picture a chai stall that also sells petrol, except the government tells
the stall exactly what price it will pay for the milk that goes into the
chai, and that price changes depending on whether the milk came from a
cow, a buffalo, or was bought in bulk from the market. TruAlt is that
stall, scaled to industrial size: it buys sugarcane juice or maize,
ferments it into ethanol, and sells it to government-owned petrol
companies at a price Delhi sets each year by feedstock type. The stall
used to only be open during cane-crushing season, four to five months a
year. It just spent two years and a lot of borrowed money teaching itself
to also run on maize and rice, so it can stay open all year. The catch:
maize got expensive right when the new taps opened, so the extra litres
it can now sell earn a much thinner margin than the old sugarcane litres
did.

## SECTION 2: INDUSTRY DYNAMICS & COMPETITIVE POSITION

### 2A. Five forces, plainly

| Force | Read | Helps / Hurts / Neutral |
|---|---|---|
| Competition count | Fragmented market: TruAlt leads at 3.6% of national installed ethanol capacity, ahead of Shree Renuka Sugars and Balrampur Chini Mills (prospectus p.232). | Entry barriers | Distillery construction is capital-heavy and needs environmental/state consents, but the underlying feedstock-to-ethanol process is not proprietary; many sugar mills already run captive distilleries (AR risk section names "feedstock availability" as the top listed risk, AR p.91/112) | Neutral — capital and consents raise the bar moderately, but do not block a well-funded new entrant |
| Supplier power | Feedstock (sugarcane derivatives, maize, rice) is a farm commodity with seasonal price swings; 72.6% of FY26 total purchases were from Nirani-group (promoter-family) sugar mills, exceeding 100% of disclosed FY25 total purchases in an unreconciled note (B02 rank 2, Notes 28/38) | Hurts — concentrated, related-party feedstock dependency with an unreconciled disclosure gap is a genuine supplier-power and governance risk, not just a commodity-cycle risk |
| Customer power and concentration | Top 3 customers (all OMCs) were 72.11% of FY25 revenue from operations (35.93% + 23.14% + 13.04%, prospectus p.301); 4 customers were more than 10% each of FY26 standalone revenue, together Rs1,30,921.30 lakh of Rs1,70,465.34 lakh = 76.8% (AR Note 26/A(ii), p.340) | Hurts — a handful of government-owned buyers, allocating by tender, hold effective control of volume |
| Substitutes | Petrol/diesel directly (if blending mandates were relaxed) and, longer-term, EVs reducing petrol demand itself; within biofuels, compressed biogas and future SAF are complements more than substitutes for TruAlt specifically since it is building both | Neutral to Hurts — the EBP mandate (20% blending achieved ESY2025-26 Nov-Jun average, AR p.73/3449) is the demand floor; mandate durability is a policy risk, not a market one |

### 2D. Industry lifecycle stage
The Indian ethanol-blending programme is in **late growth, approaching
maturity** for the core 1G ethanol line: blending reached ~20% average in
ESY2025-26 (Nov-Jun), the original 2030 target already achieved five years
early (AR p.73/3449; prospectus p.233). TruAlt sits within this mature
core business while simultaneously building three **early-stage growth**
legs beside it: CBG (1 plant operational, 4 under construction, AR
p.22/43), SAF (FEED stage, Rs150cr PM JI-VAN grant sanctioned, B03
guidance_table), and retail fuel (7 of a planned 100 outlets open, AR
p.24/44). The company's own positioning: "one of India's largest biofuels
players" moving from a single-product ethanol maker toward an "integrated
platform" (AR p.19/39, p.84/105).

## SECTION 3: FINANCIAL METRICS THAT MATTER FOR THIS BUSINESS MODEL

### 3A. Ignore-these-track-these

| Commonly tracked ratio | Why it misleads for TRUALT |
|---|---|
| Gross margin % | Price is fixed by feedstock type, not by the company; the informative number is the absolute spread per litre against that period's feedstock cost, not a percentage margin that mixes traded goods, own manufacture and government scheme income |
| Trailing P/E | FY26 PAT was propped by government-scheme income at 73.2% of standalone PBT (B02/B03); a P/E on unadjusted trailing EPS overstates the earnings quality behind the multiple |
| Debt/Equity ratio (as improvement signal) | FY26 improvement to 0.61x standalone (AR p.107) is a dilution effect from the Rs750cr gross IPO, not deleveraging — net debt itself rose (B02 rank 3, AR Note 53) |
| Revenue growth YoY (single year) | The FY26 decline of 9.33% standalone (AR p.105/6019) was driven by lower ethanol tender allocation and a deliberate three-plant shutdown for multi-feed conversion, not demand loss; a single-year growth read would misclassify a planned transition as a demand problem |
| Return on Equity (FY26 print) | ROE fell to 5.29% standalone (AR p.107) mechanically because the equity base roughly doubled on IPO proceeds mid-year; the fall is a denominator effect, not a profitability collapse |
| Inventory turnover (FY26 print) | Fell 56.60% standalone (AR p.107) because of a deliberate feedstock stock-build ahead of the multi-feed ramp, not a demand or obsolescence problem; needs a composition check (B02 rank 6 flags no management narrative on the 155.6% inventory jump) |

### 3D. Unit economics — the physics of the business

- **Unit**: one litre of ethanol delivered to a customer (OMC, private buyer, or ENA-equivalent).
- **Revenue per unit**: blended ~Rs74/litre in Q1FY27 (Rs630-odd crore on 8.5 crore litres, concall Aug-2026 p.8); split by feedstock — sugar-based ~Rs63.4/litre (4.37 crore litres = Rs277cr) and grain-based ~Rs80.1/litre (3.92 crore litres = Rs314cr) that quarter (concall Aug-2026 p.8, lines 284-287). Management's own full-utilisation math: 55 crore litres/year at "current prices" implies ~Rs4,000cr revenue, or ~Rs72.7/litre blended (concall Aug-2026, lines 810-811).
- **Cost per unit**: feedstock dominates — standalone cost of material consumed was Rs1,08,031.39 lakh against Rs1,70,465.34 lakh standalone revenue from operations FY26 (AR Note 28, p.340), roughly 63% of revenue. Feedstock cost itself is government floor-priced for cane-derived inputs (linked to the Fair and Remunerative Price, prospectus p.234) but market-set and volatile for maize (Rs17.50-22/kg Oct-2025 to mid-Feb-2026, rising to Rs25.50/kg by Jul-2026, concall Aug-2026 p.8-9).
- **Volume drivers**: OMC tender allocation, private-OMC offtake, plant utilisation (60.57% currently, Inv. Pres. Q1FY27 p.6), and dual-feed KLPD share (1,300 of 2,000 KLPD, AR p.21/41).
- **Price drivers**: the government-set ethanol procurement price by feedstock type for the Ethanol Supply Year (prospectus p.233-234); private-OMC and ENA realisations move separately and are somewhat more negotiable (private-OMC margin target Rs10-12/litre, concall Aug-2026 lines 824-826).
- **Cost drivers**: feedstock commodity price (maize/rice/cane derivatives), 72.6% of FY26 total purchases sourced from the promoter-family Nirani group (B02/B03), plus power and fuel (Rs9,511.36 lakh standalone FY26, AR Note 33, p.341) and finance cost on the capex used to build dual-feed capability.
- **Incremental margin and operating leverage**: high. The multi-feed conversion capex is substantially spent (standalone capital commitments collapsed 96.4% per B03), so each additional litre sold mostly adds variable feedstock and power cost against largely fixed plant and finance cost. Grain margin compression (Rs15-16/litre to Rs6-7/litre as maize rose, concall Aug-2026 p.9) shows the same leverage cuts both ways: it amplifies gains when feedstock is cheap and losses when it is not. This operating leverage, not a pricing edge, is the entire mechanism behind the multi-feed transition thesis.

## SECTION 4: RISKS, VALUATION APPROACH & MONITORING

### 4A. Business-model-specific risks

| Category | Risk | First financial line item to watch |

| Revenue model | OMC tender allocation shrinks or a contracted volume goes unfulfilled (a live example: ~15 crore litres subject to a Karnataka HC judicial-relief claim, B03 guidance_table) | Quarterly ethanol sales volume (crore litres) and "revenue from operations" line |

| Execution | CBG plants (4 under construction) or the SAF FEED-stage project slip past their own guided quarters (Q2/Q3/Q4 FY27 for CBG, AR p.22/43; 24-30 months for SAF, B03) | Capital work-in-progress and capacity-commissioning disclosures in board meeting outcomes |
| Structural | Government withdraws or delays PLI/interest-subvention claims that were 73.2% of FY26 standalone PBT (B02/B03), or the administered ethanol price schedule is revised unfavourably | Other income and "Performance linked incentive" lines (AR Notes 26-27) |

### 4B. Valuation method applicability

| Method | Applicability | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Cycle-normalized P/E, anchored to Section 1B destination PE | **PRIMARY** | The framework's sole exit-multiple authority is Section 1B; a converter-classified name needs its EPS built off a normalized, not spot-year, spread — spot FY26/Q1FY27 grain margin (Rs6-7/litre) is depressed and should not itself define the multiple base (Amendment 17) |
| EV/EBITDA | **SECONDARY** | Standard cross-check for a capital-intensive, leveraged commodity converter; captures the finance-cost burden that a pure P/E can understate given FY26's elevated Debt/EBITDA (3.29x standalone, AR p.107) |
| Sum-of-the-parts (SOTP) | **TERTIARY** | CBG (Leafiniti/TruAlt Gas), the SAF project, and retail fuel are option-like, pre-earnings legs sitting beside a mature core ethanol business; a single blended multiple risks double-counting or missing this optionality |
| DCF | Not applicable as primary | Feedstock-spread volatility (maize Rs17.50 to Rs25.50/kg within one year, concall Aug-2026 p.8-9) makes multi-year cash-flow forecasting unusually input-sensitive without a separately underwritten spread assumption; usable only as a bounded cross-check, not the anchor |
| Dividend discount model | Not applicable | No established dividend policy disclosed in this corpus; company is capex-heavy and growth-stage |
| Replacement cost / asset-based (NAV) | Not applicable as primary | Relevant only as an R0/floor sanity check (per the CLAUDE.md Quality Ladder), not as an operating valuation for a business with a demonstrated (if administered-price) earnings stream |
| Precedent transactions | Not applicable | No disclosed comparable pure-play ethanol-distillery transaction in this corpus |

Cycle stage that matters for valuation: the grain-margin compression from
Rs15-16/litre to Rs6-7/litre (concall Aug-2026 p.9) marks TRUALT as
**mid-cycle on the feedstock-cost axis, early-cycle on the utilisation
axis** — Section 1B and FTTCP must normalize the former and can credit the
thesis on the latter, per Amendment 17's bar on spot-year inputs.
