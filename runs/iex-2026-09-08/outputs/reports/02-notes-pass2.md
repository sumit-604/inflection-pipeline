**1. Board's Report describes the whistleblower investigation as CONCLUDED, with
findings and remedial action already taken — the Notes, approved by the same
Board on the same date, describe it as still OPEN.** 🔴 (outside Notes mandate,
but directly material to interpreting the Notes disclosure Pass 1 flagged)
Board's Report, "Whistle Blower & Anti-Fraud Policy" section (Annual Report FY26,
p.80): "During the year under review, the Company received a complaint under the
said mechanism, which was reviewed by the Audit Committee and subjected to an
independent investigation in accordance with the established procedures. **Based
on the findings of the investigation, appropriate actions were implemented as
directed by the Audit Committee.**" Past tense throughout: findings exist, actions
were implemented.
Compare Note 46 standalone (p.224) / Note 49 consolidated (p.290), both approved by
the Board on the SAME date, 23-Apr-2026: "The Audit Committee has initiated an
independent investigation, which **remains ongoing as at the date of approval** of
the financial statements by the Board... which do not have a material impact."
The two documents cannot both be read literally: one says the investigation
concluded with findings and action taken, the other says it was still running with
no conclusion reached, as of the identical sign-off date. Either the Board's Report
language is loose (describing an earlier, different, already-closed vigil-mechanism
matter, conflated with the still-open one the Notes describe), or the Notes
understate how far the investigation had actually progressed by 23-Apr-2026. The
AR gives no way to tell which. This is the single most important finding of this
pass — it goes to whether the "no material impact" conclusion in the Notes was
reached before or after the investigation's substantive findings were known.

**2. BRSR discloses ZERO conflict-of-interest complaints against Directors or KMPs,
both years — narrowing (not resolving) who "certain officials" means.** 🟡 (outside
Notes mandate, but directly narrows a Notes-flagged ambiguity)
BRSR Principle 1, Leadership Indicators (Annual Report FY26, p.139-140): "Number of
complaints received in relation to issues of Conflict of Interest of the Directors:
Nil / Nil (FY26/FY25)" and "...of the KMPs: Nil / Nil (FY26/FY25)." The BRSR
question is scoped narrowly to Directors and KMPs. Since it reports Nil for both,
and since the whistleblower complaint under Note 46/49 undisputedly exists and was
received in FY26, the complaint must — on the company's own formal BRSR
categorisation — concern officials who are NEITHER Directors NOR KMPs. This
narrows "certain officials" in the Notes language toward operational/mid-level
staff, away from the CMD, JMD, CFO, or Board. It does not resolve what "diversion
of business" means or its scale.

**8. ESOP: full reconciliation shows the P&L charge nearly HALVED year on year
despite a fresh grant, and total options outstanding fell despite the new grant.**
🟡 Standalone Note 51(c)/(d), consolidated Note 47(c)/(d) (Annual Report FY26,
p.224-225/p.290, identical figures both statements): employee stock option expense
was ₹80.59L (FY26) vs ₹164.65L (FY25) — down 51%. This sits alongside a NEW grant
of 1,00,000 options on 29-Jul-2025 at ₹143 exercise price (Black-Scholes fair value
₹45.43, volatility 41.49%, already flagged by Pass 1). The most likely explanation
is that the largest historical tranche (11,44,000 options granted 24-Jan-2024) is
approaching the end of its four-year graded-vesting amortisation schedule, so its
per-year charge is declining faster than the small new grant adds back — a
plausible, non-alarming mechanical explanation, but the Notes do not state it
explicitly, so it remains an inference. Total options outstanding at year-end:
40,000 (₹140) + 7,53,760 (₹141) + 1,00,000 (₹143) = 8,93,760, DOWN from 9,98,175
at FY25-end (50,000 + 9,48,175) — forfeitures (1,53,875) plus exercises (50,540)
outpaced the new grant. Exercisable-but-unexercised at year end: 3,66,510 options
(10,000 + 3,56,510). Weighted average share price at exercise was ₹191.18 (FY26)
vs ₹194.83 (FY25) — both well above the ₹140-143 exercise price band, so the
options carry real intrinsic value; the diluted-EPS impact stays small (24,855
incremental shares per Note 35, already found by Pass 1) purely because the
treasury-stock method nets most of the exercise proceeds against assumed
buybacks, not because the options are economically immaterial to option-holders.
MINOR CORRECTION to Pass 1's phrasing: Pass 1 wrote "~889.3cr shares outstanding"
for the EPS denominator; the Note 35(c) figure (standalone p.213-214) is
