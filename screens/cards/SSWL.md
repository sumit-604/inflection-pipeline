## Business Understanding Narrative

Steel Strips Wheels makes steel and alloy wheels, plus a new aluminium knuckle
product, for cars, trucks, tractors and two-wheelers, selling to named OEMs under
long-running agreements, each with a disclosed share of business per model
(Presentation Q1FY27 p.12, p.17). Steel wheels made Rs 953.8 crore of Q1FY27 revenue,
63% of the total. Alloy wheels made Rs 532.9 crore, 35%. Knuckles made Rs 23 crore, 2%
(Presentation Q1FY27 p.8).

What is changing is the mix and the input-cost fight. Alloy wheels earn a premium
management links to engineering, not price. The company spent two years pushing OEMs
to reset input-cost formulas rather than absorb raw-material swings (Concall p.5,
p.10-11). EBITDA per wheel jumped from Rs 262 in Q1FY26 to Rs 314 in Q1FY27, the
figure the CFO calls the cleanest lens on the business (Concall p.3, p.13).

Why now: capacity is largely sold out, above 95% across units (Concall p.8). The
knuckle line runs at 100%, order book "almost completely sold out" (Concall p.7). A
Bhuj, Gujarat expansion adds 1.2 million alloy wheels and 1.1 million knuckles, trial
production due Q4FY27 (Concall p.4). US tariff disruption is fading, a 10% baseline
now applied evenly across India, Vietnam and Thailand (Concall p.6). What must be
true: the pass-through stays durable, Bhuj commissions on schedule and finds export
demand, and alloy margins survive rising import competition without commoditising the
way steel wheels have (Concall p.12).

What breaks it: the full-year numbers just posted move opposite to the one-quarter
story. FY26 revenue grew 17% to Rs 5,182.8 crore, but EBITDA margin fell from 11.0% to
9.9%, and PAT fell from Rs 210.0 crore to Rs 202.1 crore (Presentation Q1FY27 p.27).
One good quarter follows one bad year. Whether Q1FY27 starts a re-rating or is a
rebound off a low base is the load-bearing question.

## 2. Business model and archetype
One factory floor, three products, one input-pass-through model. Steel wheels (63% of
revenue) sell against a 34-53% domestic market share depending on segment (Presentation
Q1FY27 p.12). Alloy wheels (35%) sell against named model programmes: Hyundai 74%,
Mahindra 64%, Tata Motors 62% share of business (Presentation Q1FY27 p.17). Knuckles
(2%, growing) target EV and ICE lightweighting with two OEM customers so far (Concall
p.6). Archetype: build-to-spec component maker; customer capex cycle, design-win
pipeline, and pass-through are all explicit. Strategic investors Tata Steel (6.9%) and
Nippon Steel (5.4%) supply raw material and technical linkage (Presentation p.7).

## 3. Competitive advantages
Two advantages, one company. Steel wheels compete on cost: the MD claims SSWL is "the
lowest cost producer" ex-China, with the caveat "I don't know what the costs in China
are because a lot of Chinese companies have subsidies" (Concall p.10), an honest,
partial claim, not a proven moat. Alloy wheels compete on technical capability and
switching cost: the MD states "there are only 2 serious players" domestically and
earns "a premium... in a few cases," while some competitors sell below cost (Concall
p.12). India Ratings independently credits "strong customer relationships" (Credit
Rating p.3). Moat: forming in alloy and knuckles, cost-only in steel.

## 5. Financial trajectory
FY26 (standalone): revenue Rs 5,182.8 crore, up 17%. EBITDA Rs 511.1 crore, up 5%, but
margin fell to 9.9% from 11.0%. PAT fell to Rs 202.1 crore from Rs 210.0 crore
(Presentation Q1FY27 p.27). Net debt Rs 826 crore, flat versus FY25 (guidance p.6).
Q1FY27 (standalone): revenue Rs 1,509.8 crore, up 27.2%. EBITDA Rs 162.3 crore, up
33.0%, margin 10.7% versus 10.3%. PAT Rs 71.5 crore, up 43.3%, margin 4.7% versus 4.2%
(Presentation Q1FY27 p.22). Volume grew 7.9% to 52 lakh units.
RoCE and RoE both declined every year, FY22 to H1FY26: RoCE 26% to 18%, RoE 23% to 11%
(IP FY26 Q3 p.24). Inflection classification: mixed. Q1FY27 alone reads earnings-led
and margin-led; the full FY26 year before it reads the opposite. Only one clean
quarter of the newer pattern is held.

## 6. The transition and the quality ladder
Management's claim, quoted once: "profitability growing significantly faster than
revenue, reflecting better product mix, capacity utilization and operational
efficiencies" (Concall p.4).
FROM R2, cost-advantaged converter: steel wheels earn on cost position against a
commodity input; the MD says "the domestic margins are crashing" in places (Concall
p.12). TO R3, value-added/spec'd supplier: alloy wheels and knuckles carry named
design wins, partial pricing power, and OEM-programme switching costs. The stated 70%
"value added" share supports this directionally (Concall p.10), a company-declared
figure, not an audited segment number.
The direction is plausible, but RoCE and RoE across four years run the wrong way for a
business claiming to climb; a genuine R2-to-R3 move should show rising, not falling,
returns on capital. That gap is what a deep run must resolve.

## 8. Proof check
What fired: Q1FY27 EBITDA per wheel, margin, and PAT all moved as the narrative
predicts, with volume up 7.9% and revenue up 27.2% (Presentation Q1FY27 p.21).
What has not fired: FY26, the full year immediately before, shows the opposite. Only
one quarter of the newer pattern is held; no discrete Q4FY26 data exists to show when
the turn began.
Named proof point: whether Q2FY27 repeats the gain, and whether the pass-through is a
durable reset or a one-quarter catch-up. Management itself warned not to "count EBITDA
percentages because raw material prices are so volatile" (Concall p.14).

## 10. Independent check: the credit rating
FOUND. India Ratings affirmed and assigned SSWL's bank facilities IND AA-/Stable and
IND A1+ on 18 February 2026 (Credit Rating p.1-2). Strengths: strong business profile,
sustained revenue growth, stable credit metrics, rangebound margins, locational
advantage, diversified revenue. Sole named weakness: "capex-heavy business" (Credit
Rating p.2). Ind-Ra flags net adjusted leverage above 2.5x since FY24, likely through
FY27, and free cash flow turning negative again amid the current expansion (Credit
Rating p.4): solid investment-grade, but tolerant of capital intensity rather than
confident on returns.

## 11. Posture: Transition Decision Matrix
- Proof gate: NOT CLEANLY FIRED. Q1FY27 shows the story working alone, but it reverses
  a full FY26 decline, and only one quarter of the favourable pattern is held. The
  framework's two-quarter standard cannot be tested from this corpus.
- Ugliness: STRUCTURAL-FEATURE, on balance. Falling RoCE and RoE run four years deep,
  matching a capex-heavy model India Ratings itself names as the core weakness, not a
  one-off dip (Credit Rating p.2, p.4). The closer call: segment-level returns data
  could move this to ARTIFACT-OF-CLIMB if the decline concentrates in newest,
  still-ramping capacity.
- Recognition gap: INDETERMINATE. No price or PE data in this corpus.
Proof not cleanly fired plus structural ugliness maps to VALUE-TRAP RISK. Classify DEEP
WATCH: one strong quarter against a four-year decline, a flag to test, not a verdict.

## 12. Verdict card
**WATCH.** The alloy-wheel and knuckle design-win business is real: named OEM
programmes, a strategic-investor supply chain, and a capacity crunch that supports
pricing. Q1FY27's EBITDA-per-wheel jump to Rs 314 is a real number, not a guided
estimate. But the transition thesis needs rising returns on capital, and this company
shows four straight years of falling RoCE and RoE, rising leverage, and a full FY26
year where margin and profit both fell despite double-digit revenue growth. One good
quarter does not yet outweigh that pattern.
Load-bearing facts a deep run must verify first:
1. Whether Q2FY27 repeats the EBITDA-per-wheel and margin gain, confirming a durable
   pass-through reset, not a one-quarter catch-up.
2. Segment-level (steel versus alloy versus knuckle) EBITDA and RoCE, to test whether
   falling consolidated RoCE hides an improving alloy/knuckle return profile.
3. The CFO change's date and reason, and related-party amounts with the fifteen named
   promoter entities.
4. Promoter pledge status, confirmed rather than inferred as nil.
What would change the view: a second quarter of margin expansion with a stabilising
leverage ratio moves this toward RE-RATING LIVE. A third straight year of falling
RoCE, or a Bhuj delay on top of the existing capex load, moves it to PASS. No price
here.
