This is NOT a no-findings pass. The pattern re-read, cross-checked against the
Independent Auditor's Report and its CARO Annexures (Standalone p.99-102, Consolidated
p.172-176 — outside the Notes-to-financial-statements scope, but read here specifically
to corroborate or contradict Notes-based findings, following the precedent Pass 2 set
for the whistleblower/audit-trail ancillary items), surfaces one materially important
new item and confirms two clean findings:

1. GOING CONCERN — NONE. Both the Standalone (p.99-102) and Consolidated (p.172-176)
   Independent Auditor's Reports carry unmodified, unqualified opinions. Annexure A
   describes the auditor's going-concern evaluation procedure in standard boilerplate
   but there is no material-uncertainty-related-to-going-concern paragraph in either
   report, and no going-concern language anywhere in the Notes reviewed (Pass 1/2). This
   closes out the going-concern check cleanly.

2. CARO ANNEXURE CROSS-CHECK MATERIALLY AMPLIFIES A NOTES FINDING. The Consolidated
   Auditor's Report Annexure (p.175-176) lists a table of 40 subsidiaries (out of the
   group's roughly 65 CGUs/subsidiaries) carrying an "unfavourable or qualified or
   adverse" CARO clause for FY26. The overwhelming majority carry Clause xvii (cash
   losses incurred in the financial year and the immediately preceding financial year)
   and/or Clause ii(b) (discrepancies between quarterly stock/turnover statements filed
   with banks for working-capital limits and the books of account). At least 25 of the
   40 carry Clause xvii alone or in combination; several also carry Clause vii(a)/(b)
   (statutory dues / disputed statutory dues). This is a DIFFERENT, larger population
   than Note 54's ~15 subsidiaries with negative net assets/losses (Pass 1 Finding 2):
   the CARO list is 40 entities, roughly 2.5x the Note 54 count, because CARO clause
   xvii captures any subsidiary with a cash loss in either of the last two years
   (a lower bar than sustained negative net worth). Read together, Note 54 and the CARO
   Annexure triangulate on the same underlying pattern — this is not a contradiction,
   it is corroboration at a larger scale, and it changes how the Note 54 finding should
   be sized: the acquisition-integration quality question is broader than the Notes
   alone show. (Note 54, p.240-244; CARO Annexure to Consolidated Auditor's Report,
   p.175-176 — flagged for awareness per this stage's Notes-only mandate, consistent
   with how Pass 2 treated the whistleblower and audit-trail items, but retained in the
   Top 15 below given its direct, corroborating relevance to a Notes-based finding.)

3. NO CONTRADICTIONS FOUND between note figures and the primary statements on the
   items re-checked (PBT/tax reconciliation in Note 41 vs Statement of P&L; goodwill
   closing balance in Note 6 vs Balance Sheet; borrowings in Note 25 vs Balance Sheet).

4. NO ERROR-CORRECTION RESTATEMENT of prior-year figures found. The only direct-to-
   equity, prior-period-adjacent item is the recurring "Impact due to common control

## D. FIVE QUESTIONS FOR MANAGEMENT

1. What is the basis for CEO and COO remuneration each rising 116% in a year when consolidated PAT to owners grew 21.3% and the standalone entity's own ROCE and debt-service coverage stayed negative (Note 52 p.235; Note 57 p.168)?
2. Note 54 shows ~15 subsidiaries with negative net worth or a loss; the CARO Annexure to the Consolidated Auditor's Report shows 40 subsidiaries with an adverse or qualified clause, mostly cash losses (p.175-176). What is the plan and timeline to bring these entities to sustainable profitability, and why do some of the older acquisitions (FY24/FY25 vintage) remain in this population?
3. What are the coupon, maturity, and conversion terms of the ₹1,160.84M Optionally Convertible Debentures issued to six subsidiaries in FY26 (Note 50B), and how will they be treated in the consolidated capital structure going forward?
4. What was the commercial rationale for extending, and the reason for fully waiving, the ₹364.69M related-party loan (plus ₹95.82M interest) to Curever Pharma Private Limited (Note 55A p.164), and what controls have been added since to prevent a recurrence?
5. What drove the CFO and Company Secretary departures within roughly two years of the February 2024 IPO (Note 52(D); signing blocks p.110-114/180-184)?

## E. NOTES-BASED RED FLAGS

- Goodwill concentration: 43.8% of consolidated net worth, built on deals priced 87-100%+ goodwill for unrecognisable customer/vendor relationships (Note 6, Note 46).
- Subsidiary-level financial distress broader than the Notes' own headline figure suggests once cross-checked against the CARO Annexure (15 vs 40 entities) — an earnings-management-adjacent quality signal (not fraud) on the pace vs. quality of the roll-up.
- Trade receivables outrunning revenue by 1.7x with ECL up 166% (Note 15).
- A related-party loan waiver of ₹364.69M plus ₹95.82M interest with zero disclosed rationale (Note 55A).
- Over ₹1.5bn of NCI put/call option fair-value movement routed directly through equity, bypassing the P&L an investor would normally read (Note 23, Note 45(A)).

No evidence of revenue-recognition manipulation, undisclosed related-party fraud, or going-concern impairment was found; the audit opinion is unmodified in both the standalone and consolidated reports.

## F. ONE-LINE NOTES VERDICT

The notes reveal moderate, trending-toward-concerning accounting practices. Key concern: a fast, debt- and intercompany-funded acquisition roll-up is inflating goodwill (43.8% of net worth) while a meaningful, larger-than-first-appears slice of the acquired base (15 to 40 entities depending on the disclosure cross-checked) still runs at a loss or fails a CARO test, alongside receivables growing 1.7x faster than revenue. Key strength: disclosure is dense but complete, the audit opinion is clean with no going-concern qualification, contingent liabilities are genuinely low, and MSME payables carry zero overdue interest. Overall accounting quality: 5/10.
