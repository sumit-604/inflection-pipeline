## THE CATALYST STORY IN PLAIN WORDS

**What could make margin fire.** The investment spend is now fully in the cost base, so
the drag stops even if nothing improves. BioPharm was bought at a lower margin than the
group and management says it normalises toward 27% to 28% on net revenue over about six
quarters from October 2025, which lands around Q1 FY28. Scale itself helps: revenue up
about 27% this year on a cost base that does not need to grow proportionally. What would
confirm it: two consecutive quarters of sequential operating margin expansion, and a
restated numeric target on a call. What would kill it: another quarter flat or down, or
management dropping the recovery language entirely. What is missing: any dated,
documented, margin specific action. Everything here is management intent, and intent
alone does not move a verdict.

**What could make capital efficiency fire.** This one has a clearer mechanism. Revenue
compounds on a capital base that only grows if management buys something else, so
arithmetic does most of the work. Amortisation steps down by Rs 50mn a quarter from
Q3 FY27 and again by Rs 25mn from Q3 FY28, which lifts operating profit directly and is
dated rather than aspirational. And roughly Rs 1,100 Cr of treasury is still waiting to
be deployed, so how it gets spent is the swing factor. What would confirm it: return on
capital rising for two consecutive years while no new large acquisition lands. What
would kill it: another sizeable acquisition, which would rebloat the base before the
last one has earned. Note that Rs 291 Cr of earnout payments are contractually due
across FY27 and FY28, so some of the treasury is already spoken for.

**What sustains revenue.** Named mandates won in Q3 and Q4 FY26 recognise revenue
mainly in FY27, including an omnichannel programme above USD 10mn of annual value, a
USD 20mn total contract value biotech engagement, and a USD 7.5mn pharmacovigilance
mandate that went live in January 2026. The Tectonic win in Germany with the largest
client is meant to be a beachhead. What would confirm it: those named deals showing up
as revenue on the stated schedule. What would kill it: further deferral, or the named
deals quietly disappearing from the calls.

**What sustains cash.** Nothing needs to happen. The model has no inventory and no
project billing, and collection is already improving. The risk is not operational, it
is that acquisitions consume the cash the operations generate.

**What is genuinely absent.** There is no documented, dated action anywhere on margin.
Management has said the right things across three calls and delivered the integration
work ahead of schedule, which is real, but no filed or contractual step exists that
mechanically improves operating margin. Under the intent and action test that confirms
the conservative reading rather than loosening it.

## THE VERDICT

Composite +5 of 8 puts Indegene in the BUY-candidate band, standard conviction, small
to medium sizing if the remaining gates clear, and Tier B caps that at 4% to 6% unless
you override it. Two transitions are firing outright, revenue and cash, one is
recovering on a mechanism that is mostly arithmetic, and one is not working. Neither the
Kernex cap nor the trim rule engages, so the score stands as calculated.

The call turns on one print: whether operating margin expands sequentially for two
consecutive quarters, starting with Q2 FY27. Margin is the only transition with no
documented action behind it, it is the one where management softened its own promise,
and it is the one where the peer set says Indegene is slow rather than proven. If margin
turns, the capital efficiency recovery follows almost mechanically and this becomes a
four transition story. If it does not, the composite is carrying a revenue line that is
flattered by acquisitions and a cash line that was always strong.

### THE EARNINGS BASIS QUESTION, YOUR CALL

I am not choosing this one. The multiple has to be applied to something and the two
options give materially different answers.

**One year forward earnings.** Applies the multiple to next year's earnings per share.
The argument for it here: FY26 earnings are held down by items that do not repeat, a
Rs 20.3 Cr litigation provision, a hedge accounting charge, and acquisition amortisation
that is contractually scheduled to step down from Q3 FY27. Trailing earnings therefore
understate the earning power the multiple is meant to price.

**Trailing earnings.** Applies the multiple to the last reported figure. The argument
for it here: the margin recovery has not printed, Q1 FY27 profit was flat against the
same quarter last year, and forward earnings for this company require assuming exactly
the recovery that ruling 8 says has not started. Trailing keeps the valuation honest
about what has actually happened.

The tension in one line: forward credits a recovery the margin transition says is not
yet visible, and trailing prices a business whose reported profit is depressed by items
that are genuinely one off. Tell me which basis, and why, and I will record it.
