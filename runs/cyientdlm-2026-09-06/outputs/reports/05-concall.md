### 2B Excuse pattern analysis

Every miss above is explained with an EXTERNAL-BLAME frame: West Asia war (materials, approvals), US tariffs (customer hesitancy), memory-component lead times, NPA/customer-approval delays, "one-off" wage-code and M&A-evaluation costs. Across all four calls, the closest thing found to an HONEST-ADMISSION is Krishna Bodanapu's Q4 FY26 remark: "we did have a problem in FY25 in order intake, and that's why we had to make some changes in the organization" (Q4 FY26 call, p.20) — but this is offered to explain FY25, not the FY26 misses under discussion, and is immediately redirected into a bullish argument ("that... will translate into a very strong FY27"). No instance of management saying "we got the FY26 Q4 call wrong" was found for the two most material misses (revenue timing, NWC). Hard topics (tariffs, West Asia, Altek margin drag) are raised proactively in opening remarks rather than only surfacing under analyst pressure, which is a point in management's favour — but the QUANTIFICATION of those hard topics is consistently declined when analysts press for numbers (Q3 FY26 call p.14, Q4 FY26 call p.12).

Classification: EXTERNAL-BLAME-HEAVY, with one partial, self-serving exception.

## SECTION 3: COMPETITIVE INTELLIGENCE FROM CONCALLS

### 3A Competitor commentary and credibility check

Management rarely names competitors directly. The sharpest competitive claim came under analyst pressure: in the Q4 FY26 call (Kiran, p.19-20), the analyst cited peer order-book growth of "35%, 40%" across "defence, aerospace, industrial, medical" and asked whether Cyient DLM has a structural growth problem. Krishna Bodanapu's response (p.20) claims Cyient DLM's own FY25-to-FY26 order-intake growth is "at that percentage or even... higher than competition," but gives no supporting figures. This claim is UNVERIFIED within the concall record and is the single most checkable competitive claim for stage 6 (see peer_questions below). Separately, RMS frames aerospace as a structural moat ("very, very difficult to reach for a new entrant," Q1 FY27 call, p.18) — a positioning claim, not benchmarked against any named peer.

### 3B Industry/market intelligence

- Global EMS market: ~$650bn (2025) growing to ~$1.1 trillion by 2033, ~6% CAGR, cited in both Q4 FY26 (p.5) and Q1 FY27 (p.4) calls, with slightly different 2025 base ($650bn vs "$625-650bn").
- Geographic EMS demand split: APAC 40%, North America 35%, Europe 25% (Q4 FY26 call, p.5).
- Rising defence spending across multiple regions cited as a tailwind (Q3 FY26 call, p.4).
- Geopolitical supply-chain realignment / "China plus one" cited as a structural tailwind for India-based EMS (Q3 FY26 call p.4; Q1 FY27 call p.4).
- AI/AI-infrastructure buildout (data centres, edge devices) cited as an emerging demand driver from Q3 FY26 onward (p.4), becoming a named strategic lane by Q4 FY26/Q1 FY27.
- A 50% US tariff rate on India-origin electronics exports is discussed from Q2 FY26 onward (p.10-11), with management describing four unspecified customer-level mitigation "options."
- Memory-component shortage/extended lead times cited as an industry-wide stress starting Q4 FY26 (p.4).
