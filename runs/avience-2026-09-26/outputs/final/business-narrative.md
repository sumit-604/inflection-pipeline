# Business narrative: Avience Biomedicals (AVIENCE)

Phase 1 lite. Evidence stages only. This file carries no valuation, no entry zone, no decision and no destination multiple. Those resolve after Stage 11.

## Business understanding narrative

Avience sells the chemicals, test kits and machines that hospitals and labs use to test blood and other samples, a field called in vitro diagnostics. Most of what it sells is not its own. In FY26, 60% of turnover came from reagents and consumables bought from Mindray, a Chinese diagnostics maker, and resold as Mindray's regional channel partner for Delhi NCR and eastern UP. Another 13% came from Mindray instruments. Its own Noida factory makes reagents and consumables (15% of turnover), rapid test cards for pregnancy, dengue and HIV (9%), and a few analysers and oxygen concentrators (3%), under 88 CDSCO product licences. A lab cannot run a blood analyser without the matching reagents, and for 80 to 90% of the reagent business the machine takes only the reagents it was placed with. So Avience gives away 80 to 85% of its instruments free on five year contracts and earns the money back on the reagents each machine consumes.

The buyers are hospitals, private labs and diagnostic centres (45% of 10 month FY26 standalone sales), other businesses and distributors (45%), government (8%) and export (2%). Named accounts include Max Healthcare, Sarvodaya Hospital and Dr Lal PathLabs. The ten largest customers take more than half of revenue and are not named. A lab that signs a placement contract buys that machine's reagents at agreed prices for five years, so switching is costly inside the term and open again at renewal. Government buyers pay slowly, 90 to 120 days on large projects by management's own account.

Present demand comes from a rising load of tests for diabetes, TB, heart disease and cancer, wider health insurance, and free diagnostics under the National Health Mission. Two signals govern it for Avience. Mindray territory and exclusivity decisions for North India decide the traded 72% of sales. State government diagnostic reagent tender awards in UP, Uttarakhand and new states decide the order led growth, and the unfiled Rs 47 Cr Uttarakhand order is the current test.

Forward growth has four external signals. CDSCO product licence grants toward the 175 product FY27 target gate how many products the factory can sell, and they show on the CDSCO SUGAM portal. YEIDA plant commissioning and medical device park incentive notifications gate the new capacity from October 2026. The PLI Scheme for Pharmaceuticals covers IVD devices, but Avience is not among the five selected applicants. GST Council and CBIC notifications on diagnostic kit rates set a live price variable for the whole range. The conservative market sizing grows the Indian IVD market about 6.5% a year.

The advantage differs by line. The placed instrument base with its locked reagent stream carries a moderate switching cost, bounded by the five year contract term. The 88 licences are a moderate barrier to a new entrant but do not stop Roche, Abbott or Transasia, who clear the same process at far larger scale. The traded Mindray line has no moat of its own: the arrangement is non exclusive and Mindray can reallocate the territory. Rapid cards have no moat, since many licensed makers sell the same tests. The emerging moat scan finds no new moat forming, with a score of about 9 to 10 against a threshold of 12 for modest development. Only three categories reach moderate strength: backward integration, where China's share of purchases fell from 31% to 3%; the incremental reagent rental lock in from the new plant; and a regulatory tailwind that other medical device parks share equally. The Family I items scored zero.

## Mindray reseller bets on own reagents

Avience listed on NSE Emerge in June 2026 at Rs 208 a share and raised Rs 30 Cr in fresh money. FY26 revenue was Rs 52.5 Cr, up 16%. PAT was Rs 8.75 Cr, up 23%. EBITDA margin was about 28 to 29%. Promoters hold 64.6% with nothing pledged, and the fall from 87.9% before the IPO is dilution from new shares, not selling.

The company wants to become a maker, not a reseller. Management guides the manufactured share of turnover from 27% to about 50%, a new YEIDA plant in production from October 2026, FY27 revenue up at least 60% to above Rs 100 Cr, and Rs 160 to 165 Cr in FY28. A Rs 47 Cr Uttarakhand order "in hand" carries about half of the FY27 target. Three facts cut against the guidance. No exchange filing, customer name or tender number confirms the order in 18 filings since listing. The second half of FY26 grew revenue only 5% on the year and fell 4% against the first half, while second half PAT fell 1%. A 50% manufactured share at Rs 100 Cr needs about Rs 50 Cr of own product against Rs 14 Cr in FY26, from a plant that starts at 15 to 20% use, while the order is heavy on equipment and the high end machines are Mindray's. On that arithmetic the manufactured share more likely lands near 33 to 40% unless the order is for Avience's own goods, which is an inference, not a filed fact.

The quality screen scores Avience GOOD, 87 of 160. Returns are high: ROCE was 31.5% in FY26 and 34.1% in FY25. Revenue compounded 48% a year and PAT 101% a year from FY24 to FY26 on a small base. The cap comes from cash. The cash generation block scores 2 of 20, and that deal breaker alone holds the grade at GOOD. The screen also counts four moats, which the business model read finds thinner and bounded by contract term.

Profit has not turned into cash. Operating cash flow covered 48% of PAT in FY25 and 68% in FY26. Free cash flow was negative all three years, about minus Rs 3 Cr, minus Rs 6 Cr and minus Rs 7.5 Cr, as the YEIDA plant took Rs 12.9 Cr of work in progress with nothing yet capitalised. The working capital side is the harder part. Receivables older than six months rose from 10% to 39% of the standalone book in one year, while the provision against them fell from 15.8% to 8.3% of that aged book. Finished goods stock rose 81% while revenue rose 16 to 22%. Debt stands at Rs 28.9 Cr, 1.7 times EBITDA. The IPO plan assumed receivables would fall to 80 days in FY27; on the call management cited 90 to 120 days on government work and conceded the full working capital need is not yet funded. No credit rating exists, so no outside view of working capital is available.

Management grades C on credibility, the default for a company nine weeks past its first ever call. No guidance has reached a test date. The first one is the H1 FY27 result due by November 2026. One promise is in progress: the plant building slipped about three months against the IPO schedule, while the October 2026 production date holds. The FY27 export target of Rs 5 to 7 Cr sits on a FY26 base of Rs 0.53 Cr, after exports fell 90% from Rs 5.24 Cr, and nobody on the call raised it. The first annual report as a listed company carries three reporting slips: profit split labels swapped between parent and subsidiary, a Board's Report line saying the audit carries no qualification when the auditor's own table lists one, and consolidated results revised in August 2026 for an elimination error. The non audit fee exceeds the audit fee in both years. The promoter review lands at CAUTION: clean regulatory and criminal record, zero pledge, an outside finance chief and company secretary, but a group company in the same business outside the non compete, a new Rs 5 Cr guarantee for the subsidiary's bank line, and an incoming auditor with no findable track record.

Peers complicate the story. Tarsons guided a 15 to 20% first year and 30 to 35% second year ramp on its new plant, the same shape Avience guides, and then slipped its own commissioning date twice in nine months. Tarsons also reports that government buying on GeM at the lowest bid has eroded its government lab revenue over five to seven years. Q-Line, the nearest mirror, earns 15 to 20% gross margin on instruments it makes and 10 to 15% on instruments it trades, which undercuts the higher margin management claims for the equipment heavy order. Molbio collects in about 87 days and confirms the closed system reagent model. All three peers guide FY27 growth of 15 to 35%, the same direction as Avience but well short of 60%. No peer confirms or contradicts the Uttarakhand order.

The market sizing puts the Indian IVD market at about Rs 12,100 to 12,800 Cr and the part Avience can serve at about Rs 1,900 Cr. Avience holds 2.8% of that served market, so headroom is about 36 times current revenue and the runway grades STRONG. An evidence based three year path reaches Rs 155 Cr, about 43% growth a year, and Rs 270 Cr in five years. Management's guidance to FY28 implies 76% a year, well ahead of that path. The gap most likely reflects a lumpy, one off government order rather than repeatable share gain, so FY27 growth should be read as order driven until a second order of similar size appears. Plant capacity of Rs 250 to 265 Cr of revenue does not bind; the constraint is the mix shift and unfunded working capital.
