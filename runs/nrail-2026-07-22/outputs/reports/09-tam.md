# Stage 9 — TAM / SAM / SOM Market Sizing: N R Agarwal Industries Ltd (NRAIL)

Run date: 2026-07-22 | Model: claude-sonnet-5 | Pipeline mode

## Revenue anchor (confirmed from primary source)

FY26 (year ended 31-Mar-2026) Revenue from Operations = **₹2,14,544.53 lakhs = ₹2,145.45 Cr**, audited (results PDF, p.4, "Year ended March 31, 2026" column). FY25 comparative = ₹1,65,903.17 lakhs = ₹1,659.03 Cr (same source, and matches Annual Report p.4/p.44). YoY growth = (2,145.45−1,659.03)/1,659.03 = **29.32%**.

Management's own FY26 target, set in the FY25 Chairman's letter, was ₹2,200 Cr (AR p.8, "The Company expects to achieve ₹2200 cr in revenues during the current financial year... an impressive 36% higher than the ₹1617 cr achieved... in FY 2023-24"). Actual FY26 revenue of ₹2,145.45 Cr came in at **97.5% of that guided target** — a modest miss, flagged for stage 11 as a data point on management guidance reliability, not a going-concern issue.

All figures below in ₹ Crore unless stated. FX conversions use ₹85.47/US$, the FY25 year-end rate disclosed in the AR MD&A (p.13).

## SECTION 1: MARKET DEFINITION

**1A — Precise boundaries.**
- **Product scope**: recycled-fibre based packaging paperboard — coated/uncoated duplex board, Folding Box Board (FBB), Solid Bleached Sulphate (SBS) grey-back/white-back varieties — plus writing, printing and copier paper (W&P). Excludes: kraft paper/linerboard and corrugated medium (used for corrugated boxes — a distinct manufacturing process NRAIL does not run), virgin-fibre premium imported coated board, tissue, and newsprint.
- **Geographic scope**: India (82.90% of FY25 revenue, AR p.6) as the core market; exports to 33 countries (17.10% of FY25 revenue, ₹280.01 Cr FY25 per AR p.39 Schedule V disclosure) as a secondary, smaller market.
- **Customer scope**: converters/printers serving FMCG packaging, pharma, cosmetics, food & beverage and e-commerce brand-owners (board); publishers, printers, stationers and institutions (W&P) (AR p.15).
- **Channel scope**: direct sales to converters/printers plus a dealer network (dealer deposits carried on balance sheet, Note 20).
- **Price segment**: value/mid-market recycled-fibre board — NRAIL competes on cost (AR p.7: "probably the lowest cost paperboard manufacturer in India... due to our scale and technology benefit"), not at the premium virgin-fibre import-grade end.

**1B — Management's own TAM claim.** The FY25 AR's MD&A (company-prepared, citing Mordor Intelligence/IBEF/Data Insights Markets/Research & Markets) states: "The Indian paperboard packaging market is valued at US$ 13.72 billion in 2025 and is projected to grow at a CAGR of 6.63%, reaching US$ 18.92 billion by 2030" (AR p.19). Converted at ₹85.47/US$: **≈₹1,17,245 Cr (2025)**. Dated 2025 (fresh, not stale). Separately, MD&A also states "Packaging is the fifth-largest sector of the Indian economy... expanding at an annual rate of 22-25%" (AR p.15) — this is the *entire* Indian packaging industry (plastic, metal, glass, paper combined), not paperboard specifically, and is not used as the claim tested below.
**Credibility read: broad.** The US$13.72bn figure is a "paperboard packaging market" value that on its face bundles kraft/corrugated-linerboard-based packaging paperboard (which NRAIL does not manufacture) together with the duplex/FBB/SBS niche NRAIL actually serves, and likely includes downstream converting value rather than pure mill-gate revenue. See Section 2 triangulation for the numeric read (ratio to conservative estimate).

## SECTION 2: TAM ESTIMATION — MULTIPLE METHODS

**Method 1 — Top-down.**
Start: India paperboard packaging market = US$13.72bn (2025) → **₹1,17,245 Cr** (AR p.19, sourced to Mordor Intelligence/IBEF/Data Insights Markets/Research & Markets; cross-checked independently via web search — Mordor Intelligence and IMARC corroborate the US$13.72bn 2025 / ~US$14.54bn 2026 figures). Subtraction of non-relevant segments (kraft/corrugated linerboard, premium virgin-fibre imports) could not be cleanly quantified — no primary source gives a tonnage or value split between duplex/FBB/SBS and kraft/corrugated within this headline figure (**NOT FOUND**). Retained as an unadjusted **outer-bound ceiling**, confidence **Low** given the scope-mismatch risk flagged in 1B. A second, materially larger web-sourced figure (India "packaging board market" ≈ US$38.30bn / ₹3,31,595 Cr in 2025, ~6.5% CAGR to 2030) surfaced in search but uses an evidently broader/different market definition (likely full packaging value chain, not paperboard manufacturing) — flagged as a divergence, not used, per the requirement to explain rather than average conflicting scopes.

**Method 2 — Bottom-up** (primary method; most defensible for NRAIL's actual product scope).
Addressable unit = 1 tonne of packaging paperboard consumed in India.
Total current units = **15 million tonnes (2024)**, stated as 65% of India's total 23 million tonne paper/paperboard/newsprint market (AR p.19, citing Data Insights Markets/IBEF/Mordor Intelligence/Research & Markets). Dated 2024 — approaching the 2-year staleness boundary at this run date; flagged (not yet STALE).
Revenue per unit = NRAIL's own realized paperboard price band: **₹37,000/tonne (FY25 close, trough) to ₹41,000/tonne (recent peak)** (AR p.7, Chairman's letter: "paperboard realized ₹41 per kg... by close of FY25, realizations had declined to ₹37 per kg").
- Current market value = 15,000,000 t × ₹37,000/t = **₹55,500 Cr** (conservative, trough pricing)
- Current market value = 15,000,000 t × ₹41,000/t = **₹61,500 Cr** (realistic, peak/normalized pricing)
Forward (5-yr) at IPMA-cited packaging paper/paperboard volume growth of **8.2% CAGR** (search-sourced; corroborates the segment's stated 22-25% broader-packaging growth rate direction while being paperboard-specific): 15MT × (1.082)^5 = 22.25MT (2030) → ₹82,325 Cr at ₹37,000/t (holding price flat, conservative) to ₹91,225 Cr at ₹41,000/t.
Confidence: **Medium** — tonnage figure sourced but 2024-vintage; realization anchored to the company's own disclosed price band (a reasonable but company-specific, not industry-average, proxy).

**Method 3 — Peer revenue aggregation.**
NRAIL FY25 revenue ₹1,659 Cr (packaging board portion ≈₹1,037 Cr at 62.49% mix). Named peers with FY25 revenue found via search: West Coast Paper Mills ₹2,501 Cr (FY25, includes a non-paper optical-fibre-cable segment — paper-only split **NOT FOUND**); Kuantum Papers ₹1,113 Cr (FY25, primarily W&P, limited packaging-board relevance). FY25 revenue for JK Paper, Emami Paper Mills, Century Pulp & Paper, ITC-PSPD and TNPL — **NOT FOUND** in this search pass (ITC-PSPD and TNPL revenue is not separately disclosed/attributable at the segment level from public search). Given this incompleteness, a reliable organized-sector aggregate cannot be built; the standard India unorganised-sector uplift (30-60% per pipeline convention, *stated as an estimate*, not sourced) is noted but not applied to a headline number. **Confidence: Low, directional only** — confirms mid-size organized players operate in the ₹1,000-2,500 Cr revenue range, consistent with a fragmented but sizeable organized segment, but does not independently corroborate a TAM figure.

**Method 4 — Import substitution.**
Waste paper (recovered fibre) is NRAIL's core input, not its output market, so classic import-substitution logic (domestic production replacing imports) runs in the *opposite* direction here: AR p.7 attributes FY25's realization decline explicitly to "global paperboard oversupply and cheaper imports," and the Director's Report risk section flags "product dumping risk" with anti-dumping duty as the stated mitigant (AR p.16). Search-sourced waste-paper import tonnage into India ranges widely (4.0MT to 7MT/year cited by different sources, against a projected future need of ~15MT/year) — **NOT FOUND** as a single reliable figure; not used quantitatively. This method does not yield a standalone TAM number for NRAIL; it is retained only as a **Section 4B risk** (import competition pressuring realizations), not a growth lever.

**Method 5 — Global benchmark (per-capita).**
India per-capita paper & paperboard consumption ≈16 kg vs global average 57 kg vs China ≈93.4 kg (2023) (search-sourced, Statista-derived for China). At India's ~1.4bn population, closing the gap to the global average would imply ~82 million tonnes of total paper/paperboard demand versus ~23 million tonnes today — a multi-decade convergence story, not decision-grade for a 3-5 year SOM window, and not decomposable by paper-type from available sources (**NOT FOUND**: packaging-board-specific per-capita split). Used only directionally in Section 4A (growth driver), not fed into the headline TAM.

## SECTION 3: SAM & SOM

**3A — SAM.** Five filters applied to conservative TAM (₹55,500 Cr):

1. **Product fit**: NRAIL's addressable range is duplex/FBB/SBS + W&P, not kraft linerboard/corrugated medium or premium virgin-fibre imports. No sourced tonnage/value split exists to quantify this precisely (**NOT FOUND**). An explicit, clearly-flagged **analyst estimate of a 40% cut** is applied (i.e., SAM retains 60% of TAM) — this is *not* a sourced figure; it reflects the qualitative, widely-noted industry pattern that kraft/corrugated packaging (serving bulk e-commerce/FMCG shipping) represents the majority of Indian "packaging paperboard" tonnage while folding-carton-grade duplex/FBB/SBS is the minority. Flagged Low confidence.
   → ₹55,500 Cr × 60% = **₹33,300 Cr**
2. **Geography**: TAM is already India-only; NRAIL states pan-India distribution ("our paperboard output was marketed pan-India," AR p.7) alongside a 60% western-India concentration reflecting plant location — no further cut applied (0%).
3. **Channel**: NRAIL sells direct + via dealer network; no channel-based exclusion identified in filings (0% cut, **NOT FOUND** any channel-restriction data).
4. **Customer**: NRAIL serves FMCG, pharma, cosmetics, food & beverage, e-commerce and W&P institutional buyers (AR p.15) — broad-based, no material segment exclusion found (0% cut).
5. **Capability**: NRAIL's board is CFTRI-certified for food-contact applications (AR p.3), supporting broad qualification rather than restricting it; capability is not a demand-side constraint (0% cut here — physical capacity is handled separately in 3C, not as a demand-filter).

**SAM = ₹33,300 Cr (60% of conservative TAM).**

**3B — SOM at 3 and 5 years.**
Current share: FY26 packaging-board revenue (FY25 mix of 62.49% applied to FY26 total as a proxy — **NOT FOUND**: FY26 segment split not disclosed in the Q4/FY26 results filing) = ₹2,145.45 Cr × 62.49% = **₹1,340.8 Cr**.
Current SAM share = 1,340.8 ÷ 33,300 = **4.03%**.

Share-gain trajectory: NRAIL just ramped a 900 TPD (240,000 TPA) duplex-board plant to 95% utilization (AR p.7-8) — a capacity-and-execution-backed case. The AR also describes active industry consolidation ("small paperboard manufacturing units are exiting the business... manufacturing capacities are moving into stronger hands," AR p.7-8), supporting an **aggressive (3-5pp)** share-gain case rather than the normal 1-2pp case, but not the >5pp tier (which is reserved for competitor exit/acquisition, and NRAIL is not disclosed as acquiring capacity).
- 3-yr: +3pp → 4.03% + 3.00% = **7.03%** of SAM
- 5-yr: +5pp → 4.03% + 5.00% = **9.03%** of SAM (upper bound of the aggressive band)

SAM growth (applying the 8.2% volume CAGR from Method 2 to the fixed 60%-of-TAM SAM base):
SAM₍yr3₎ = 33,300 × (1.082)^3 = **₹42,183 Cr**; SAM₍yr5₎ = 33,300 × (1.082)^5 = **₹49,386 Cr**

Packaging-board SOM:
- SOM₍3yr₎ = 42,183 × 7.03% = **₹2,965.5 Cr**
- SOM₍5yr₎ = 49,386 × 9.03% = **₹4,459.6 Cr**

W&P paper (separate, mature/declining segment; growth proxied at IPMA's cited +3.05% FY24-25 W&P volume growth, a single-year data point extrapolated forward — flagged): FY26 base ₹804.65 Cr (37.51% of ₹2,145.45 Cr) → yr3 ₹880.7 Cr, yr5 ₹935.3 Cr.

**Total SOM (blended board + W&P):**
- **SOM₍3yr₎ = ₹3,846 Cr** (implied revenue CAGR from FY26 base of ₹2,145.45 Cr: (3,846/2,145.45)^(1/3) − 1 = **21.5%**)
- **SOM₍5yr₎ = ₹5,395 Cr** (implied CAGR: (5,395/2,145.45)^(1/5) − 1 = **20.3%**)

**3C — Capacity cross-check.** NRAIL states it is "equipped to produce half a million tons of paperboard starting the current financial year" (500,000 TPA, AR p.8) — the hard ceiling for the packaging-board segment absent further capex. At NRAIL's own realization band (₹37,000-41,000/tonne), maximum physically deliverable packaging-board revenue at 100% utilization is **₹1,850-2,050 Cr**; at the plant's demonstrated 95% utilization, **≈₹1,758-1,948 Cr**.
Against this ceiling:
- 3-yr packaging-board SOM (₹2,965.5 Cr) implies ≈801,500 tonnes sold — a **gap of ≈301,500 tonnes (≈₹1,115 Cr of revenue)** above the 500,000 TPA installed base.
- 5-yr packaging-board SOM implies far more tonnage still, for a **gap of roughly ₹2,410-2,610 Cr** versus the current capacity ceiling.
**The B07_CAPEX_FIGURE input names an unconfirmed ~₹1,500cr Unit VI Dahej project (1,020→1,500 TPD) per a 12-May-2026 operator-relayed announcement — this is explicitly NOT corroborated by any primary document read in this run and is excluded from the capacity math above, per instruction.** The AR's own (confirmed) forward signal is softer: management states it is "exploring the possibility of commissioning a second large greenfield paperboard manufacturing unit" (AR p.7-8) without disclosing capacity, cost or timeline (**NOT FOUND**).
**Conclusion: the SOM is the optimistic side.** Absent a confirmed, capitalized expansion beyond the current 500,000 TPA base, NRAIL's installed-plus-committed capacity cannot physically produce the 5-year share-gain-implied SOM — a gap of roughly **₹2,400-2,600 Cr** in the packaging-board segment alone. Stage 11 should treat the capacity-bound ceiling (≈₹1,850-2,050 Cr packaging-board revenue at current capacity) as the realistic near-term constraint, and the share-gain SOM as an *upper-bound, capex-contingent* scenario.

**4C — Market structure.** Organized-vs-unorganised: more than 300 small/medium mills hold roughly half of India's installed paper capacity, alongside ~43 large integrated mills (search-sourced); the top-3 organized players' (TNPL, JK Paper, ITC) combined share reportedly rose from ~8-12% (FY20) to ~15-20% (FY25) (search-sourced) — **consolidating**, not fragmenting further. NRAIL positions itself as "the largest paperboard manufacturer in India (greyback and white back paperboard varieties) and the largest recycled paper manufacturer" (AR p.8) — a company self-claim, not independently verified with a market-share percentage (**NOT FOUND**). Competition is price-led at the commodity end (duplex board) with signs of a differentiation push (FBB launch). Import share trend: rising/adverse per AR's own commentary on FY25 realizations (cheaper imports cited as a driver of price decline).

**5B — Runway assessment.**
- Revenue headroom (SAM ÷ current segment revenue) = 33,300 ÷ 1,340.8 = **24.8x** (packaging-board segment basis; total-company basis not computed given W&P sits in a separate, unsized market)
- TAM growth rate = **8.2%** (volume, IPMA-cited; cross-checked against Mordor's 6.63% value-CAGR and the global 5.9% value-CAGR — broadly consistent, value CAGR modestly below volume CAGR implying continued price softness, consistent with FY25's realization decline)
- Company CAGR vs TAM: NRAIL's FY26 revenue grew 29.3% YoY — well above the 8.2% market growth rate, but this is a **one-off capacity-ramp effect** (new 240,000 TPA plant reaching 95% utilization), not sustained market-share capture at that pace; SOM-implied *forward* CAGR (20.3-21.5%) is more representative of a share-gaining-but-capacity-bound trajectory.
- Years to saturate SAM at current growth: at NRAIL's FY26 growth rate (29.3%) against a ₹33,300 Cr SAM growing at 8.2%, NRAIL would not physically saturate SAM within any realistic horizon (SAM itself is >20x current segment revenue) — capacity, not market size, is the binding constraint (see 3C).

**5C — Runway classification: STRONG.** (Scale used, disclosed: MASSIVE >50x headroom & >15% TAM growth; STRONG 20-50x & 8-15%; GOOD 10-20x & 5-8%; MODERATE 5-10x & 2-5%; LIMITED <5x & <2%.) NRAIL's 24.8x headroom and 8.2% TAM growth place it in the STRONG band on a pure market-size basis, but this is **tempered by the capacity gap identified in 3C** — the addressable opportunity is large, but NRAIL's own physical plant, not the market, currently caps near-term capture.

**5D — SAM expansion levers actually being pursued** (all AR-sourced, none fabricated):
- Value-added FBB launch (post-FY25 balance-sheet date) — moves NRAIL toward higher-value, import-substitution grades within the same SAM (AR p.8); no incremental revenue figure disclosed (**NOT FOUND**).
- ₹150 Cr pulp-mill/converting balancing program, targeted completion September 2025 (Director's Report, AR p.44) — incremental capacity/quality within the existing 500,000 TPA envelope, not a SAM-expanding move per se.
- Management is "exploring the possibility of commissioning a second large greenfield paperboard manufacturing unit" (AR p.7-8) — directionally would expand capacity (and hence SOM capture potential) if committed, but carries no disclosed capacity, cost or date (**NOT FOUND**); the ₹1,500cr Unit VI Dahej figure supplied as operator context is explicitly unconfirmed and excluded from this analysis.
- 7.5MW captive power plant using plastic extracted from waste paper — a cost lever supporting competitive pricing/margin, indirectly supporting SAM capture rather than expanding SAM itself.
Revised headroom if the (unquantified) second unit proceeds: **NOT FOUND** — cannot be computed without a disclosed capacity/cost figure.

**5E — Final output card**

- Market definition: Indian recycled-fibre packaging paperboard (duplex/FBB/SBS) and writing & printing paper, sold domestically (~83%) and exported (~17%) to converters, printers and institutional buyers.
- TAM (conservative / realistic): ₹55,500 Cr / ₹61,500 Cr
- SAM: ₹33,300 Cr (60% of conservative TAM, product-fit-driven, analyst-estimated cut flagged)
- SOM 3-yr / 5-yr: ₹3,846 Cr / ₹5,395 Cr (demand-side, share-gain method; **exceeds current physical capacity from roughly year 2-3 onward** — see 3C)
- SOM-implied revenue CAGR: 3-yr 21.5%, 5-yr 20.3%
- Runway class: STRONG (market-size basis), capacity-constrained in practice
- Management TAM claim: ₹1,17,245 Cr (2.11x conservative estimate) — **inflated**
