## The picture in one paragraph

The break on Wednesday did not hold as a single bad day. It became a slide. On Friday 360 stocks fell four and a half percent or more, and only 25 rose that much. That is the second day this heavy in three sessions. Only 579 stocks now trade above their 200 day line, about one in four. The count has not been this low since 9 April. 453 stocks made new yearly lows, the most since the March bottom. Turnover was the heaviest since early September, so holders are being pushed out, not drifting away. The Nifty fell less than half a percent, because IT and a few large names still hold it up. The small company indices fell about three percent. Every test the 21 October brief set for a worse market fired except one, and every test for a better market failed. The breadth downtrend is now confirmed, and it holds until a broad day of buying says otherwise.

## Where the market stands

Picture the market as about 2,250 stocks. A stock above its 20, 50 or 200 day average is in an uptrend on that clock. On Friday 579 stocks sat above the long line, about one in four. Only 253 sat above the 50 day line, about one in nine. Only 178 sat above the 20 day line, about one in thirteen. The short count has not been this low since 23 March, the worst week of the spring bottom.

The year's story shows why the number matters. The market made its low in late March. On 23 March only 288 stocks held the long line, and 952 stocks fell hard that day. A week later, on 30 March, 728 fell hard and new yearly lows topped 900. Then broad buying arrived. On 1 April more than 1,100 stocks rose four and a half percent in one session. On 8 April 878 did. Traders call these thrust days. The long count climbed through spring and summer. It peaked at 1,367 on 7 August, the highest in this record.

Autumn has been a staircase down. The count fell through August into a shock on 15 September. A repair took it back to about 1,180. A second shock came on 8 October and took it to 837. A third repair reached 937 on 16 October. Since then the count has fallen every session: 852 on Monday, 720 on Wednesday, 673 on Thursday and 579 on Friday. That is a loss of more than 350 stocks in five sessions.

The pattern of the last week matters as much as the level. Wednesday brought 383 hard fallers. Thursday was calmer, with 127, and it looked like a pause. Friday brought 360 again. A shock followed by a quiet day and then a second shock is how a slide looks, not a washout. The last time two days this heavy came so close together was the last week of March. Back then a thrust followed within days. No thrust day has come since 12 June.

Inside Friday's session, sellers owned the day again. About two stocks in three traded at least four percent below their prior close at some point. About four in ten of those finished near their low. About one stock in eleven traded four percent above its prior close. Only one in four of those kept the gain. That is better than Wednesday's one in seven, and still poor. Almost everything moved, and most of it moved down.

Volume confirms the damage. Turnover reached about 611 crore, against about 431 crore on the same day a year ago. That is the heaviest day since 7 September. The share of stocks on heavy volume, against those on light volume, tripled from Thursday. It now sits near the top of its own history. The market has now had three days with 300 or more hard fallers since 8 October.

The damage has spread from the middle into the far end. More than one stock in four is down ten percent or more over five days. That is the highest share in this whole history. 26 stocks are down twenty percent or more in five days, against seven up that much. On Wednesday only six stocks had fallen that far. About one stock in fourteen now trades more than ten percent below its 10 day average. That stretch is also a record for this data.

New highs have almost vanished. Only eight stocks made a yearly high on Friday, the same as Thursday. No day since 24 March had fewer. New yearly lows rose to 453, the most since 30 March. About four stocks in ten now sit within fifteen percent of a yearly low. Only about one in fourteen sits that close to a yearly high.

Fear keeps rising at the index level. The India VIX measures what traders pay to protect against big swings in the Nifty. It rose about seven percent on Friday to about 16.9. It is up about a fifth on the week and more than forty percent over three months. Gold funds gave no shelter either. They fell about eight percent over the month, while the Nifty fell about four. The usual safe asset keeps falling harder than the stocks.

## What would change this read

The read is a confirmed slide in breadth, on heavy volume, hidden by a steady Nifty. It turns worse if the long count falls under about 450. The count last sat that low on 7 April, a week after the spring bottom. New yearly lows above about 700 would say the same. Another day of 300 or more hard fallers within the next week would turn a slide into a rout. A VIX close above about 17, with the Nifty falling as hard as the small names, would say the large company shelter has failed. If IT turns down while breadth stays this weak, the index loses its last support.

The cautious read fails if buying returns broad and fast. The first test is a thrust day, with about 250 or more stocks up four and a half percent on heavy turnover. The last one came on 12 June. The second test is the long count back above about 840, the old autumn floor, within a few sessions. The third test is new lows back under about 100 while new highs climb past about 50. A VIX close back under about 15 would support all three. The record stretch below short averages makes a snap back likely soon. A snap back that fails every one of these tests is a rally inside a downtrend. Tonight the auto parts makers, the jewellers and a few large drug makers are the only groups rising against the tide, and three groups do not make the case.
