## Candidate Pattern A — DISCLOSURE QUALITY AS A SYSTEM

The evidence assembled across all three passes: two confirmed uncorrected comparative errors (receivables,
jobwork); no capital-commitments disclosure against an active Rs 80.94 cr CWIP programme; hedging stated
Nil/Not Applicable twice against a first-year ~Rs 120 cr unhedged import book; an EPS weighted-average share
count that conflates a FY25 capital-increase authorisation with the FY26 IPO cash issuance; no price-basis
methodology for any related-party transaction anywhere in the RHP or the AR; a credit-rating section naming
only Brickwork, silent on CARE's ISSUER NOT COOPERATING status; the April-2026 SEBI search absent from notes
approved 14-May-2026; and at least seven secretarial/exchange compliance lapses in the first listed AGM
cycle.

Reading the evidence supports: MIXED, not a single pattern, and the two sub-groups point different ways.

Sub-group 1 — numeric/procedural, reads as FIRST-YEAR IMMATURITY. The receivables and jobwork comparative
errors are literal transcription slips, confirmed against an independent prior-year filing, with no
consistent direction (one inflates a number, the other understates one, neither by much rupee value). The
EPS weighting issue, on the Pass 2 illustrative recompute, UNDERSTATES reported EPS by roughly 3-4% — the
opposite of what an earnings-inflation motive would produce. The seven secretarial/exchange lapses (SMS
e-voting, Smart ODR Portal, Structured Digital Database, MGT-14, NSE/BSE observation letters) are classic
small-company-goes-public administrative gaps, each individually explained and remediated per the Board's own
account, not financial-statement manipulation.

Sub-group 2 — qualitative omissions, reads closer to SELECTIVE DISCLOSURE. The CARE ISSUER NOT COOPERATING
omission is a choice to name the favourable rating and stay silent on the unfavourable one, in a disclosure
section whose only purpose is to inform shareholders of rating status. The SEBI 11C(9) search omission is a
complete silence across three notes sections that each independently could have carried it, for a related
party with a live Rs 300 lakh loan, historical yarn-purchase dependency, and a jobwork-linked affiliate
sitting inside the FY26 cost stack. The missing RPT price-basis and the boilerplate FX/hedging language both
sit in exactly the two areas (related-party pricing, new import-driven currency exposure) that a transition-
thesis investor would most want tested.

What would distinguish the two readings, going forward: (1) whether CARE's status appears in ANY other
exchange filing or investor communication around this period — appearing elsewhere but omitted only from the
AR argues oversight; appearing nowhere argues selective; (2) whether the next annual report discloses the
SEBI search and price-basis methodology promptly and completely, or repeats the same silences; (3) whether the
secretarial lapse count falls in the next AGM cycle. None of these can be resolved from the FY26 AR alone;
they are the correct claude.ai live-verification and next-cycle tracking targets.

The series shows ROTATION, not decline. FY23-FY24: related-party dependency is FINANCING-side (revolving
loans taken from RB Industries and Ricon Industries, cleared out before the IPO). FY25: dependency shifts to
the PURCHASE side (yarn, peaking at 11.85% of POY yarn) plus a first appearance of jobwork, a deposit, a loan
given, and a contingent guarantee — the broadest related-party footprint of any year, coinciding with the IPO
process and the 14-May-2025 self-certification. FY26: purchase-side dependency collapses to near-nil, but
jobwork more than doubles and a new, wider unrelated-party loan book appears. No year in the five-year window
is genuinely free of related-party dependency; only the FORM changes each year, and the form that is largest
in FY26 (jobwork) is the one form with the least disclosed pricing detail of any category in Note 34.

Does the FY26 margin survive withdrawal of related-party input buying? YES on the input-purchase side: yarn
purchases from related parties fell to 0.04% of total purchases while the EBITDA margin held near 23.6% —
the FY26 margin is demonstrably not propped up by related-party yarn pricing in the current year. NOT
resolved on the jobwork side: the jobwork line more than doubled in the same margin year, sits inside the
same cost stack, and has no disclosed price basis in either direction — it cannot be ruled out that the
arrangement moves margin the OPPOSITE way a purchase subsidy would (i.e., if Borana Weaves pays an
above-market jobwork rate to Borana Industries LLP, that would UNDERSTATE Borana Weaves' own margin, not
inflate it; if below-market, it would inflate it). The notes do not permit a determination either way.

What this implies for FY24 and FY25 margins: both carry a materially larger unresolved related-party pricing
question than FY26 does, because the purchase-side dependency (5.02% FY24, 8.83% FY25, up to 11.85% at the
9-month mark) was larger in rupee and percentage terms than FY26's near-nil level, and rests on nothing more
than a bare arm's-length attestation from the Company's own statutory auditor, with no independent valuer, no
comparable-transaction benchmark, and no rate-card disclosed anywhere in the RHP or either AR. An investor
using FY24-FY25 margins as the base case for a transition thesis is relying on a track record whose input
pricing has never been independently tested.

## Candidate Pattern C — CAPITAL DISCIPLINE

Read in full: 2025-12-31_IPO-monitoring-agency-report.pdf (Acuité Ratings and Research, quarter ended
31-Dec-2025, report dated 09-Feb-2026, filed to exchanges 11-Feb-2026).

Key finding: by the end of Q3 FY26 (31-Dec-2025), the Company had utilised 100% of the Rs 144.89 cr net IPO
proceeds across all four disclosed objects — new manufacturing unit Rs 71.42 cr (revised from Rs 71.35 cr,
a Rs 0.07 cr overrun funded by reallocating Issue Expenses savings), incremental working capital Rs 26.50 cr,
General Corporate Purposes Rs 32.89 cr, issue expenses Rs 14.09 cr — with Nil unutilised balance, "No
Deviation... observed," and the Board's Directors marking every line "AGREED." (2025-12-31_IPO-monitoring-
agency-report.pdf, pp.4-8)

This directly answers the orchestrator's question in one respect: the ~Rs 10.7 cr third-party loan book
(Khwaish Exim LLP, Nirmit Dalmia, R&B Denims Ltd, Whitelotus Industries Ltd, Nakoda Developers — CARO
Annexure A(iii), AR p.87) and the ~Rs 1 cr mutual-fund/equity trading activity (Note 5, AR p.106), both
observed at FY26 year-end (31-Mar-2026), are NOT a diversion of unutilised IPO proceeds — there were none left
to divert after Dec-2025 per the monitoring agency's own accounting. They must be funded from OTHER
post-Dec-2025 cash: operating cash flow generated in Q4 FY26, incremental borrowings, or working-capital
release. Given FY26 full-year CFO of Rs 36.96 cr (run context) against FY26 capex of Rs 178.98 cr, the Company
was net cash-consuming for the year as a whole; deploying scarce non-IPO cash into a side loan book and a
trading book, in the same period an Rs 80.94 cr capex programme sits substantially incomplete, is a capital-
discipline question that stands independent of, and in addition to, the IPO-proceeds-utilisation question —
it is not a SEBI proceeds-monitoring violation (that mechanism reports clean), but it is a broader use-of-cash
question the monitoring report's clean bill does not address.

A second, more specific finding inside the GCP breakdown: of the Rs 32.89 cr General Corporate Purposes
allocation, Rs 7.90 cr was applied to raw-material imports (nine tranches, 30-Aug-2025 to 16-Sep-2025,
consistent with the first-time import-sourcing shift documented under Note 39(c)/(d)) and Rs 24.99 cr — 76%
of the entire GCP bucket — was applied on 16-Dec-2025 to a single line item, "Renewable Hybrid Project - Solar
+ Wind" (2025-12-31_IPO-monitoring-agency-report.pdf, p.8). This is a named, directed capital project, not a
broad-purpose/working-capital use of the kind "General Corporate Purposes" ordinarily implies; the monitoring
agency and Board record it as within-object and non-deviant (GCP is discretionary by design), but it means the
Company's actual flexible-use IPO cash was, in substance, largely a second capex allocation. This does NOT
cleanly reconcile against the Rs 868.43 lakh (Rs 8.68 cr) Solar Roof Top System addition seen in the PPE note
(Note 3) — the Rs 24.99 cr GCP "Renewable Hybrid" spend is roughly triple that PPE addition, suggesting either
a wind-power component not yet separately identified/capitalised (potentially sitting inside the Rs 80.94 cr
CWIP balance) or a reconciliation gap between the monitoring report and the AR's own asset notes. NOT FOUND
IN DOCUMENT: no note in the FY26 AR cross-references or explains the Rs 24.99 cr Renewable Hybrid Project by
that name. Worth a direct management question and a claude.ai extraction target (subsequent monitoring-agency
reports, which should show the Q4 FY26 position).

No diversion is found against the stated OBJECTS of the issue as monitored by Acuité; the capital-discipline
concern is a level up from that — it is about what the Company does with cash the IPO-proceeds monitoring
mechanism does not track (post-utilisation operating and debt-funded cash), set against a still-incomplete
capex programme and materially negative cumulative free cash flow (run context: minus Rs 194.8 cr over five
years).

## Candidate Pattern D — THE RETURNS DECAY

Figures used here are the PASS 2 corrected ratios (see governing rule above): true Inventory Turnover 6.26x
(FY26) vs 7.23x (FY25); true Trade Payables Turnover 36.49x (FY26) vs 21.00x (FY25). ROE 35.01% (FY26) vs
59.45% (FY25); ROCE 24.17% (FY26) vs 42.82% (FY25) (Note 40 items 4, 10, AR pp.128-129).

ROE/ROCE decay: WHOLLY capital-base-driven, on the note's own arithmetic and its own stated reason ("due to
increase in Average Shareholder's Equity/Capital Employed due to IPO Proceeds," Note 40 items 4 and 10).
Average shareholder's equity nearly tripled (Rs 67.62 cr to Rs 184.56 cr); capital employed rose from Rs
126.17 cr to Rs 338.24 cr (+168%) while EBIT grew only 51.3% (Rs 54.03 cr to Rs 81.74 cr) — mechanically
consistent with near-zero CWIP-to-revenue conversion in the same year (Pass 1, Spear Fact 5: only Rs 8.24
lakh capitalised out of Rs 80.94 cr CWIP). No operating-margin deterioration accompanies this — EBITDA margin
held at 23.6%, revenue grew 33.9% — so this component of the decay is explained wholly by the capital base,
not by an operating problem.

Inventory turnover decay: HAS a genuine operating component, distinct from capital-base dilution. Yarn
(raw-material) inventory grew 92.4% against consumption growth of only 27.9% — a deliberate stocking build,
not a capital-base artefact. This is consistent with either the longer lead times of the new import channel
(32.7% of raw material, up from 0%) or advance buying ahead of the capacity ramp; both are operating choices,
not passive effects of a bigger balance sheet.

Trade payables turnover (the "odd one," now confirmed genuine by the Pass 2 correction rather than an
extraction artefact): the raw balance collapsed from Rs 376.88 lakh to Rs 23.19 lakh (-93.8%) while purchases
rose 39.2% (Rs 17,938.69 lakh to Rs 24,976.20 lakh) — the Company is paying suppliers dramatically faster in
the same year it is running its largest-ever capex programme. Testing the three hypotheses the orchestrator
named against the evidence in the notes:
- Loss of supplier credit: NOT supported. No default disclosed on any borrowing facility (Note 14, Note 38);
  no MSME delayed-payment interest in either year; no adverse credit-rating commentary tied to trade credit.
  Nothing in the notes suggests suppliers pulled terms.
- Change in supplier mix (imports): BEST SUPPORTED by the notes, though not proven beyond doubt. Raw-material
  imports went from 0% to 32.7% of consumption in exactly this year (Note 39(c)/(e)), and Rs 7,726.59 lakh of
  that was CIF-valued import purchase (Note 39(d)) — a purchase channel typically settled via letter of
  credit or advance payment ahead of or at shipment, i.e., structurally short- or no-credit, unlike the
  domestic yarn suppliers (including the former related-party suppliers RB Industries/R&B Denims Ltd) that the
  Company previously bought from. A supplier-mix shift toward LC-settled imports is a plausible, evidence-
  consistent driver of a payables collapse of this scale, but the notes do not provide a payment-terms-by-
  supplier breakdown that would confirm it directly.
- Discount-capture choice: NOT FOUND IN DOCUMENT. No early-payment discount policy, no supplier-negotiation
  narrative, is disclosed anywhere in the notes or Board's Report.
Conclusion: the payables collapse most likely has a genuine operating driver (the import-mix shift), meaning
the working-capital deterioration (39.4 to 66.5 days per run context) is not wholly a capital-base/IPO
artefact the way ROE/ROCE decay is — part of it is a real change in how the Company finances its purchases,
worth confirming directly with management or via next-quarter payment-terms disclosure, since it feeds
directly into the Block B cash-generation concern already on record from Stage 1.
