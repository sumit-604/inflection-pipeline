### 2B. Excuse pattern analysis

Every volume/revenue miss is explained by an external actor: OMC
allocation methodology, government policy "dilly-dally," a parallel
court case, the Middle East crude crisis (for retail capex pause). Some
of this is independently verifiable (the Karnataka HC order, the 04-Feb-2026
writ of mandamus, are named with dates and disclosed to exchanges) and is
not fabricated. But the pattern is consistent: management credits itself
for the multi-feed capex delivery and CBG plant economics (self-praise on
delivered items) while attributing every miss to OMCs, courts, or
geopolitics (external-blame on missed items) — the asymmetric-credit
pattern named in Master v3.7 as a credibility flag. One clear
counter-example of honest admission exists: the Q1 FY27 call, on the Ind
AS 8 GST-reversal restatement, MD volunteers "this was an oversight from
our tax consultants... we could have increased our PAT margin by ₹4
crores," a direct, unprompted admission of an internal error. Hard topics
are usually raised only under analyst pressure (retail stagnation,
de-leveraging plan, private-OMC price reneging) rather than proactively
in the opening remarks; the opening remarks in all four calls lead with
optimistic framing ("tremendous transformation," "fairly good quarter")
before the miss is unpacked in Q&A.

classification: external-blame-heavy (see YAML `excuse_pattern`).

### 2C. Tone ratings (1-5, evidence)

- Transparency: 3/5. Court order, allocation shock, and the GST
  restatement are all disclosed with dates and figures. But the 72 cr L
  bid vs 26 cr L award ratio — the single most important number explaining
  the FY26 volume miss — is disclosed only in the Q4 FY26 call, after the
  miss had already printed, not flagged as a risk in the Q3 FY26 call when
  the 37 cr L guidance was reaffirmed.
- Specificity: 3/5. Feedstock-level pricing, plant-level capex, and TPD
  figures are given in granular detail every call. Set against this,
  volume and revenue reconciliation numbers required repeated mid-call
  correction (H1 FY26 volume stated as "9 cr L" then corrected to "5.7 cr
  L," Q1 FY26 production stated as "3.3" then "3.03 cr L," in the Q2 FY26
  baseline call).
- Consistency: 2/5. Capacity-utilisation denominator changes between
  "operating days," "gross quarter," and "run-rate" across calls without
  reconciliation. CBG plant counts under the Sumitomo and GAIL JVs also
  vary call to call (16 total → 24 → "3 of 4" Sumitomo/"5 or 6" GAIL)
  without a single call ever reconciling the prior count against the new
  one.
- Accountability: 3/5. The GST-reclass admission is a genuine
  accountability moment. The 15 cr L court order and the retail rollout
  are never owned as company-side execution misses; they remain framed as
  OMC/regulatory failures throughout.
- Defensiveness: 2/5 (2 = more defensive). On the Q3 FY26 stock-in-trade
  question (Pratham Modi), MD responds "I think I should have to ask the
  investor community what's happened," a deflection rather than an answer
  to a direct balance-sheet question.
- Over-promotion: 2/5 (2 = more over-promoted). Superlatives recur every
  call ("fastest rollout," "one of the largest plants in the world,"
  "amongst the largest global producers of ethanol derived SAF") attached
  to targets that have not yet delivered revenue.

### 2D. What they are NOT saying

None of the four calls mentions cash flow from operations, DSCR, or the
related-party feedstock concentration — the three findings B02/B03 rate
as the highest-severity items in the audited FY26 financials (standalone
CFO -₹296.15cr, DSCR 0.99x, RPT purchases 72.6% of total FY26 purchases).
Every opening statement instead asserts balance-sheet strength in general
terms ("robust solvency," "disciplined capital allocation," "sound
liquidity position," Q4 FY26 call) using debt-equity and current-ratio
metrics that do not address the cash-conversion or RPT-concentration
findings. The corporate guarantee for TGPL's NABARD facility (contradicted
by the AR's own NIL contingent-liability note, per B02/B03) is never
raised. The two Company Secretary vacancies spanning the IPO listing
window are never raised. The likely reason: none of these topics were
asked by any analyst on any of the four calls either — the analyst base
skews retail/individual-investor and volume-focused, and no institutional
analyst pressed on cash conversion, RPT, or governance in this evidence
set.

## SECTION 3: COMPETITIVE INTELLIGENCE FROM CONCALLS

### 3A. Competitor commentary

Direct competitor naming is rare. Green ammonia peer "AM Green or Greenko"
named once (Q3 FY26 call) as already committing capex to bunker-fuel green
ammonia — a factual, checkable industry reference, not disparaging.
Sugar-company entrants into ethanol are addressed defensively (Q1 FY27
call): management claims "we don't see any further investments in the
ethanol space," estimating at most 3-4 more plants nearing commissioning
industry-wide, adding 8-10 cr L/annum of capacity. This is a specific,
quantified, checkable claim — flagged to peer verification below. No
credibility check possible from the transcripts alone (no named source).

### 3B. Industry/market intelligence

- India ethanol installed capacity ≈1,800-1,900 cr L on a 365-day basis;
  operating capacity ≈1,500-1,550 cr L on a realistic 300-day, 90%
  utilisation basis (Q4 FY26 call).
- E20 blending demand ≈1,200-1,300 cr L; AlcoBev demand ≈200 cr L; chemical
  industry demand ≈300 cr L (Q1 FY27 call) — a broadly balanced
  demand-supply picture at current blending, tightening only if blending
  rises above 20%.
- Each 1 percentage point of blending increase ≈55-56 cr L of incremental
  national demand, on a base of ~5,500 cr L of petrol consumption (Q4 FY26
  call).
- OMC private-sector (Jio-bp, Nayara/Reliance) reneging on already-issued
  purchase orders and pressuring for price cuts after an industry-wide
  ethanol oversupply developed (Q4 FY26 call) — a disclosed, dated,
  checkable industry pattern, not unique to TruAlt.
- CBG gas price fixed at ₹84-85/kg under the current SATAT scheme; a new
  "Sampoorn" policy is repeatedly referenced as a rumour across three
  calls (Q3, Q4, Q1 FY27) without ever being confirmed — management is
  careful each time to flag it explicitly as unconfirmed rumour, a
  positive specificity signal.
- Karnataka HC and a parallel LTOA-holders-vs-OMC Supreme Court matter are
  both actively shaping national ethanol allocation policy as of the Q1
  FY27 call — an industry-wide legal overhang, not company-specific.

### 3C. Toughest analyst questions

- Deepak Poddar (Q3, Q4 FY26 calls): repeatedly forced volume-math
  reconciliation (guided vs actual litres, inventory offsets). Management
  answers were internally consistent once corrected, but required several
  rounds each time — a real, not fully resolved, disclosure-quality
  concern.
- Vedant Sarda (Q4 FY26 call): directly challenged the Q3 FY26 call's
  ₹350-400cr/month "stabilised" ethanol run-rate promise against the
  actual ₹595cr half-quarter print. Management's answer (inventory
  build-up plus the unimplemented court order) is coherent and
  cross-checks against the disclosed ₹500cr inventory figure, but confirms
  the original run-rate guidance did not hold.
- Nitin Awasthi (Q4 FY26 call): pressed on private OMCs reneging on
  already-issued purchase orders at a price TruAlt had already won. MD's
  candid answer ("it's almost become like an oligarchy... we have to play
  nice with them") is a real, disclosed commercial-power risk, well
  answered, and a genuine concern for the investment case (pricing power
  sits with OMCs, not producers).
- Shubh Gala (Q1 FY27 call): pressed on the Ind AS 8 restatement. Answered
  directly and with a self-admitted PAT understatement of ₹4cr — the
  clearest instance of unprompted accountability in the corpus.
- Pratham Modi (Q3 FY26 call): asked about the stock-in-trade increase.
  MD deflected ("I should have to ask the investor community"). Not
  satisfactorily answered; a real balance-sheet-quality question left
  open, matching B02's inventory-growth flag.

### 3D. Customer/order book signals

- Public-sector OMC allocation for ESY 25-26: 26 cr L (down from a 72 cr L
  bid, 34% hit rate, Q4 FY26 call).
- Private OMCs (Jio-bp, Nayara): 8 cr L contracted at ~₹60.5-62/litre;
  only 1.6-3 cr L actually lifted by the Q1 FY27 call, the balance still
  pending and subject to a price-renegotiation demand from the OMCs.
- ENA (extra neutral alcohol, potable alcohol industry): ~3 cr L at
  ~₹62/litre realised in FY26.
- FY27 order book in hand, as of the Q1 FY27 call: 44 cr L base case
  (26 public OMC + 8 private OMC + 6 ENA + 15 court-ordered, netted

- CBG offtake: GAIL contractually guarantees "more than 80-90% of sales on
  a daily basis" for the GAIL JV plants (Q1 FY27 call) — a concentration

- Fertiliser byproduct (FOM) offtake: named counterparties Rashtriya

  Coromandel for ~₹6,000/ton (Q1 FY27 call) vs ~₹2,500-2,800/ton open
  market — a real, quantified upside if it converts.

## Analyst note

The single most load-bearing pattern this stage found is that TruAlt's
concall guidance consistently outruns its delivery on every VOLUME-linked
metric (ethanol litres, DDGS revenue, CBG commissioning dates, retail
outlets), while COST/MARGIN-side execution (multi-feed capex, CBG unit
economics, EBITDA margin) has broadly delivered. The company also changes
its own utilisation denominator quarter to quarter in a way that always
flatters the current quarter, and it never once raises, across four
calls and roughly 20 analyst questions, the cash-conversion and
related-party concentration issues that B02/B03 rate as its most severe
audited-financial-statement problems. None of the four calls contains a
going-concern-adjacent admission, and one real unprompted accountability
moment exists (the GST-restatement PAT admission), which keeps this from
a D grade. But the combination of a genuine, repeated, ~35% volume miss,
a stalled retail rollout, and total silence on the AR's own red flags
argues for treating every FY27 volume and margin projection this
company gives on a call as the ceiling of a range, not the base case,
until the next 1-2 quarters show the 15 cr L court order and the CBG
commissioning dates actually resolve.
