NEW FINDING 1 — Rs 243.87 lakh cross-AR reclassification inside current liabilities,
fully traced; downstream ratio consequences not disclosed anywhere
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
🔴 RED FLAG (disclosure quality, not fraud) — the most significant Pass 2 finding.

**The reclassification, traced line by line.**
AR_FY2025's own standalone Note 20 "Other Financial Liabilities" for FY25 (AR_FY2025,
p.124/Note 20, "20 OThER FINANcIAL LIABILITIES"):
  Expenses Payable            Rs 277.61 lakh
  Security deposits received  Rs 8.94 lakh
  Creditors for exp.          Rs 294.48 lakh
  Unpaid dividend account     Rs 43.04 lakh
  TOTAL                       Rs 624.07 lakh

The SAME FY25 year, as shown in the comparative column of AR_FY2026's Note 21 "Other
Financial Liabilities" (standalone p.135):
  Expenses payable             Rs 19.04 lakh
  Employee payables            Rs 258.57 lakh   <- new line, did not exist in FY25's own note
  Security deposits received   Rs 8.94 lakh     <- unchanged
  Creditors for capital goods  Rs 50.61 lakh    <- renamed/shrunk from "Creditors for exp."
  Unpaid dividend account      Rs 43.04 lakh    <- unchanged
  TOTAL                        Rs 380.20 lakh

Fall in this note's FY25 total across the two ARs: Rs 624.07 lakh -> Rs 380.20 lakh =
Rs 243.87 lakh moved OUT.

Rise in Trade Payables (Note 20, AR_FY2026, standalone p.135) "Others" (non-MSME) line
for the identical FY25 year: AR_FY2025's own balance sheet (p.113, Note 19(B))
Rs 466.62 lakh -> AR_FY2026's comparative (Note 20(B)) Rs 710.49 lakh = Rs 243.87 lakh
moved IN. The MSME sub-line (Rs 55.03 lakh) is identical in both ARs and untouched.

The two deltas are the exact same figure to the rupee (Rs 243.87 lakh), confirming a
genuine reclassification of Rs 243.87 lakh of FY25 liabilities out of "Other Financial
Liabilities" (Expenses Payable + Creditors for exp.) and into Trade Payables, with a
new "Employee payables" sub-line (Rs 258.57 lakh) carved out along the way. The
Balance Sheet grand total is unaffected: TOTAL EQUITY AND LIABILITIES as at 31-Mar-2025
is Rs 16,643.79 lakh in BOTH AR_FY2025's own balance sheet and AR_FY2026's comparative
column (AR_FY2025 p.109; AR_FY2026 p.114) — proof this is a pure presentation
reclassification within current liabilities, not a restated liability amount or a
prior-period error (the Statement of Changes in Equity's "Restated balance... due to
prior period errors" column is Nil in both years, standalone and consolidated,
AR_FY2026 p.118/166 — consistent with a reclassification, not a correction).

**Downstream ratio consequences, not flagged anywhere in the AR.**
Because Trade Payables' own closing balance for FY25 changed (Rs 521.65 lakh per
AR_FY2025's own report -> Rs 765.52 lakh per AR_FY2026's comparative, Note 20/19), the
Trade Payables Turnover ratio for the SAME FY25 year is reported differently in the two
Annual Reports:
  AR_FY2025's own Note 44 (standalone, p.146) and Note 43 (consolidated): FY25 = 18.06x
  AR_FY2026's Note 45 (standalone p.150, consolidated p.196): FY25 comparative = 25.52x
That is a 41.3% relative jump in the SAME historical ratio between the two Annual
Reports, with no note anywhere identifying it as a restated ratio.

Net Capital Turnover ratio for FY25 shows the same pattern, an even larger swing:
  AR_FY2025's own Note 44 (standalone, p.146): FY25 = 1.08x
  AR_FY2026's Note 45 (standalone, p.150): FY25 comparative = 2.73x
This ratio's denominator is Working Capital (Current Assets minus Current
Liabilities); since TOTAL current liabilities are unaffected by an intra-current
reclassification, the mechanism behind this specific swing is not fully traceable from
the two ratio tables alone — flagged as a further, separate unexplained restatement
rather than asserted to share the identical cause as the trade-payables move.
NOT FOUND IN DOCUMENT: any note reconciling either ratio's FY25 comparative to the
figure the company's own FY25 Annual Report originally reported.

Ratios NOT affected by the reclassification, checked as a control (confirms the
mechanism is confined to current-liability sub-lines and not a wider restatement):
Current ratio (4.46x AR_FY2025 -> 4.48x AR_FY2026 comparative, an immaterial 0.02x
drift, consistent with total current liabilities being unaffected by an intra-current
move), Inventory turnover (1.85x both), Net profit ratio (11.68% both) — all identical
or near-identical across the two ARs for FY25, as expected if the reclassification is
genuinely confined to the liability side.

**Why this matters.** Note 45's own "Reasons for Variance" commentary in the FY26 AR
attributes the FY26 trade-payables-turnover decline (25.52x -> 17.26x) to "a
substantial increase in average trade payables compared with the growth in purchases"
(AR_FY2026 p.150, quoted in Pass 1). That commentary is measured against a FY25 base
(25.52x) that is itself 41.3% higher than what the company's own FY25 Annual Report
reported for the same year (18.06x). A reader relying on the FY26 AR alone, with no
access to the FY25 AR, cannot know the FY25 comparator has moved. This is a
disclosure-transparency finding, not an evidence of purchases/payables manipulation:
the balance-sheet grand total ties out exactly, and MSME-specific figures (the more
sensitive sub-line) are untouched.

NEW FINDING 2 — ROE and ROCE for FY25 also restated across the two Annual Reports, on
BOTH standalone and consolidated bases, by a mechanism the trade-payables
reclassification above does NOT explain
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
🟡 WATCH, verging on 🔴 — extends and partially resolves the Stage-1 pre-run flag
FLAG-... (ROCE/ROE restatement), which named only the consolidated figures; this pass
adds the standalone comparison and shows the two bases moved in OPPOSITE directions.

| Ratio | Basis | AR_FY2025 own-report FY25 | AR_FY2026 comparative FY25 | Delta |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ROE | Standalone | 12.59% (Note 44, p.146) | 10.70% (Note 45, p.150) | -1.89pp, DOWN ~15% relative |
| ROE | Consolidated | 11.91% (Note 43, p....) | 15.28% (Note 45, p.196) | +3.37pp, UP ~28% relative |
| ROCE | Standalone | 17.01% (Note 44, p.146) | 17.68% (Note 45, p.150) | +0.67pp, up ~3.9% relative |
| ROCE | Consolidated | 16.11% (Note 43) | 17.18% (Note 45, p.196) | +1.07pp, up ~6.6% relative |

Control check performed to isolate the mechanism: the two inputs an ROE/ROCE
computation would need — FY25 closing PAT and FY25 closing Total Equity — are IDENTICAL
across the two Annual Reports. Standalone PAT FY25 = Rs 1,827.11 lakh in both AR_FY2025
and AR_FY2026 (AR_FY2026 p.114/161, matches Pass 1's own figure); consolidated PAT FY25
= Rs 2,153.16 lakh in both. Standalone TOTAL EQUITY as at 31-Mar-2025 = Rs 14,513.36
lakh in both AR_FY2025's own balance sheet (p.109) and AR_FY2026's comparative column
(p.114). Total Debt (the other Capital Employed input) is also unchanged: the
Debt-Equity ratio for FY25 is identically 0.02x in both Annual Reports (AR_FY2025
Note 44; AR_FY2026 Note 45), meaning the FY25 borrowings figure was not reclassified
between current and non-current in a way that would move Capital Employed.

Because the reported income and reported equity/debt figures are unchanged, the ROE
and ROCE deltas above cannot be explained by a restated balance-sheet or P&L line —
they can only come from a difference in how "Average Shareholder's Equity" or "Capital
Employed" (the ratio DENOMINATORS, whose precise computation the note discloses only
as a label, not a build-up) were computed between the two Annual Reports. No note in
either AR discloses the components of "Average Shareholder's Equity" or "Capital
Employed" beyond the label in the ratio table (Note 45/44/43, both years) — so the
mechanism cannot be fully reconstructed from the document. What can be established
firmly: (a) it is a real, non-trivial restatement on both bases, (b) standalone and
consolidated ROE moved in OPPOSITE directions across the two ARs (standalone down,
consolidated up), which flips which basis looks better — under AR_FY2025's own numbers
standalone ROE (12.59%) exceeded consolidated ROE (11.91%), i.e., the associate
was a drag; under AR_FY2026's restated comparative, consolidated ROE (15.28%) exceeds
standalone ROE (10.70%) by a wide margin, i.e., the associate appears to be a lift.
That reversal of which entity-level view looks stronger, for the identical historical
year, is the finding worth carrying to Stage 4/Role 2: a reader using only the FY26 AR
would conclude the associate materially helped FY25 group returns; a reader using only
the FY25 AR would have concluded the opposite. NOT FOUND IN DOCUMENT: any reconciliation
of either restatement, in either Annual Report.
