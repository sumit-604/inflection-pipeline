# Divgi TorqTransfer Systems Ltd (DIVGIITTS)

## BUSINESS UNDERSTANDING NARRATIVE

Divgi makes driveline hardware for four wheel drive and all wheel drive vehicles. Two products carry the business. The Transfer Case is about 50% of sales. It is the gearbox that splits engine torque between the front and rear axles in a 4x4 vehicle. Transmission Components, including precision machined gears, shafts and export parts, are about 28% of sales. A vehicle maker cannot swap either part quickly. Each part is designed to a platform, tooled, and qualified through a supplier approval process that runs for months, and it then stays on that platform for its multi year life. The smaller lines are E Gear Drive, the in house EV transmission, at about 8%, and Synchronizer Systems at about 6%.

The customers are vehicle makers, not consumers. Mahindra and Tata Motors are the two named anchor customers. The annual report lists eight prominent OEMs but discloses no top customer share, so the concentration number is NOT FOUND. Buying is slow and sticky. An OEM qualifies a supplier once per platform, funds the tooling, and rarely re changes mid cycle, which gives the incumbent a switching cost hold that resets only at the next platform.

Demand today tracks a small set of end programs. Mahindra Scorpio and Thar class SUV volumes drive core transfer case demand. The Indonesia Scorpio Pik Up export program, a 35,000 unit run confirmed by web verification, is the single largest confirmed export order. Tata Motors and Force Motors 4x4 and UV volumes are the diversification leg beyond Mahindra. All three feed the current order flow.

Four forward drivers could grow demand, each with an outside signal to watch. India EV passenger vehicle sales and PLI Auto policy drive the E Gear Drive line, trackable in SIAM EV data. A named North American OEM automatic transmission platform decision is a binary catalyst for a large future opportunity, visible in Divgi filings and the OEM's own capital plans. Project Mayflower, the US subsidiary, signals North American localisation, trackable through US state incorporation and capex filings. The BorgWarner technology licensing scope sets royalty terms and AWD technology access, visible in BorgWarner disclosures and Divgi's related party notes.

The competitive advantage sits mainly in one line. The Transfer Case line holds a switching cost moat from OEM qualification and tooling, scored STRONG in the moat scan, but the durability is medium because it resets at each new platform. The EV transmission line does not have a proven moat. Its launch slipped three straight quarters, and both anchor customers are building the capability in house, so the scan weighs its execution moat as weak. The China plus one export tailwind scores STRONG but is shared across all Indian exporters, so it is not a Divgi specific edge. The India cost advantage is narrow. The run did not establish a moat for the E Gear Drive line.

Transition unproven, cash rich, price too high

Divgi builds driveline parts for 4x4 vehicles. Two products, the Transfer Case at about half of sales and Transmission Components at just over a quarter, make almost 80% of revenue. Mahindra and Tata Motors are the anchor customers. The company does not disclose how much revenue rides on the single largest customer or program.

Revenue rose 61% in FY26 to Rs 353 Cr. Profit rose 92% to Rs 47 Cr. The gain was organic, with no exceptional items and no share dilution. Q1 FY27 kept the pace, with revenue up 85% and Transfer Case revenue up 93% year on year. Management flagged the Q1 29.4% EBITDA margin as a one off and guides back to 20% to 22%.

The balance sheet is genuinely clean. Net cash is about Rs 294 Cr, roughly Rs 95 to Rs 96 per share, and debt is effectively nil. Cash conversion is real, not cosmetic. The negative FY26 free cash flow of Rs 3 Cr came from Shirwal capex outrunning operating cash, not a working capital leak. One caveat sits on the cash line. There is no credit rating, so the framework cannot certify working capital quality, and the cash conversion determination stays INDETERMINATE.

Gate 0 scores AVERAGE at 47 out of 160. The drag is real, not a gap. Median ROCE reads 7.7% because the post IPO cash pile inflates capital employed. Stripped of that treasury cash, operating ROCE is about 12% in FY26, still below the mid teens the transition claims. EBITDA margin has compressed about 8 points over eight years even as revenue compounded. Growth is lumpy, with three of the last eight years down.

Management delivers on the flagship program and misses on the new one. The Indonesia ramp is on track at about 30% executed by Q1 FY27. Financial guidance landed. Against that, the EV transmission start slipped three quarters on the same externally blamed excuse, an automatic transmission contract milestone was dropped after its deadline passed, and export mix fell from 18% to 16% against a 20% to 25% target. Cash and order book figures were never quantified on any call. The credibility grade is B.

The peers do not confirm the industry story management tells. Divgi blames the EV delay on a flat India EV market. Three of four peers report the opposite, with Sona Comstar calling India the fastest growing EV market in the world and citing EV segment growth of 45% to 65%. The China to India desourcing tailwind is real across the peer set, but no peer names Divgi as a beneficiary, and no peer confirms Divgi's specific claims on transfer case market size, sole Tier 1 status, or the Indonesia tender savings.

The market runway is wide but not the constraint. The captured revenue path implies roughly 15% to 16% growth over three to five years, against a management FY30 target of Rs 1,000 Cr that reads reasonable at 1.4x the modeled capture. The binding question is not room to grow. It is price.

The valuation says avoid at today's price. Two exit multiple tracks frame it. The operator approved track sets a 30x forward operating multiple, peer justified against auto ancillary comps at 40x to 50x, and lands base fair value at Rs 815. That is Rs 24 of forward operating EPS times 30, plus Rs 95 of net cash. The framework floor track, built from the pillars, sets 17.9x to 20x and lands base fair value at Rs 525 to Rs 575. CMP Rs 1,175 sits 44% above the Rs 815 base and about double the floor. The 25% hurdle STOPs even on bull EPS. Probability weighted expected return from CMP is minus 4.4% a year. Fair value itself grows about 11% a year, below the hurdle, and there is no re rating lever left because the market already prices the higher rung.

The devil's advocate leaves the thesis weakened but alive. The entry zone Rs 570 to Rs 650 offers a 20% to 30% margin of safety against the Rs 815 base, but zero margin against the Rs 525 to Rs 575 floor. Strip the three stacked overrides and the entry zone is the floor, not a discount. The re rating premise, the R2 to R3 climb, is not started and is under threat from customer in sourcing. The FY28 line hides a cliff, because the expiring Rs 75 to Rs 95 Cr Indonesia batch has no contracted follow on. Governance is a compilation control cluster that reaches a sitting executive director.

The decision is WATCHLIST with a number. The entry zone is Rs 570 to Rs 650, the margin of safety price is Rs 570, the position is Small. The house rule defaults the position to AVOID until the 18 September 2026 AGM and the FY27 annual report clear the promoter concern.
