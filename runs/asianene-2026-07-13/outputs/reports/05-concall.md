## SECTION 3: COMPETITIVE INTELLIGENCE FROM CONCALLS

### 3A. What management says about competitors

- CHP/coal-logistics space: names **Samanta** and **Madhucon** as competitors (Nov25 call, Kapil Garg) — the only instance of a named competitor across all three calls.
- Combined asset-ownership-plus-services model: management asserts **"no comparable peer"** exists in India for the combined entity (Sep25, Charvin Chandrashekar's question; reiterated implicitly in later calls). Credibility check: literally true for the exact combined structure, but this framing avoids naming partial/segment-level competitors (O&M, drilling services) — a claim that should be tested against peer concalls rather than taken at face value.
- ONGC is characterised as a **partner** on the Vedanta field (not a competitor) and as a company potentially watching/adopting AESL's integrated-services model — an unverified, self-serving claim about industry influence that stage 6 should test.
- No E&P peer (HOEC, Deep Industries, Jindal Drilling, Selan Exploration, etc.) is named directly despite Kapil Garg referencing "HOEC and other companies" in passing (Sep25) only as an analogy for Assam gas infrastructure bottlenecks generally, not as a competitor discussion.

### 3B. Industry and market intelligence

- India crude import dependence ~85-90%, gas import dependence ~50% (May26, Kapil Garg) — used to frame the energy-security thesis.
- India has committed ~$100bn of oil & gas investment by 2030 (May26) — a large, headline macro claim requiring independent verification.
- GST on oil & gas E&P raised from 12% to 18%, industry-wide (Sep25/Nov25) — an unambiguous, dateable regulatory fact, confirmed by management as applying "to all E&P companies, not only to us."
- CHP/last-mile-connectivity coal opportunity sized at ~₹20,000cr over the next 5 years, citing publicly available Coal Ministry data (Nov25).
- DNPL pipeline: current capacity 1 MMSCMD, expansion plan to 2.5 MMSCMD, feasibility study underway for 6 MMSCMD (Sep25) — third-party (Assam Gas Company) infrastructure, not AESL capex.
- Policy tailwinds cited: ORDA Act (fiscal-guarantee stability, simplified litigation), OALP, DSF rounds, royalty rationalisation, National Monetisation Pipeline (Sep25 and May26).
- Kuiper's addressable market: framed as a tightening global offshore EPCI/drilling manpower market following "nearly a decade of constrained upstream capex globally from 2016" now reversing (May26) — a industry capex-cycle claim requiring peer corroboration.
- West Asia conflict (Strait of Hormuz) is described by management as double-edged: near-term disruption (Kuiper's Qatar operations affected in March 2026, since remobilised) but medium-term positive for the sector via elevated crude prices and accelerated capex (May26).
