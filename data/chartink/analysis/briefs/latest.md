## The picture in one paragraph

Thursday was a heavy, wide selling day that broke the base of the past week.
314 stocks fell four and a half percent or more, and only 19 rose that much.
Almost nine stocks in ten closed lower, and no stock index rose. The count of
stocks above the two-hundred-day average fell to 836, below the 1 October low
near 900 and the lowest since mid-April. The count above the fifty-day
average fell to 561. New yearly lows beat new highs by more than five to one.
Turnover ran about a third above a year ago, so sellers came with size. Fear
rose by a tenth. A small set of groups held up: jewellers, a few small metal
names, sugar, small pharma leaders and defence. Friday's data has not yet
arrived, so the market stands, for now, at a fresh lower low with no sign
yet of buyers returning.

## Where the market stands

Think of the market as about 2,260 stocks, each with three trend lines under
it. The short line is the twenty-day average. The middle line is the
fifty-day average. The long line is the two-hundred-day average. A stock above
its long line is in a long uptrend. A stock below it is in a long downtrend.
On Thursday only 836 stocks stood above the long line. That is about one in
three. Only 561 stood above the middle line, about one in four. Only 412 stood
above the short line, fewer than one in five. Every one of those counts is
the lowest in this stretch of the record.

The week before Thursday looked like a repair. On 1 October the long count
had fallen to about 900. Over the next three sessions it climbed back to 965.
The short count rose from about 480 to about 720. On Tuesday about 200 stocks
jumped four and a half percent in one day. Small companies held their trend
lines better than large ones. That is how a base looks while it forms. On
Thursday the whole repair gave way in one session. Each count fell through
its 1 October low. When a market falls through the low it just bounced from,
the bounce has failed. That is the plain meaning of Thursday.

The longer story shows the size of this. In March the market crashed. By
early April only about 400 stocks held the long line. April then brought huge
buying days, one with more than 1,100 stocks jumping. By early August the long
count stood near 1,370. September turned bad. On 15 September about 340
stocks fell hard in one day, and the long count dropped to about 1,040. It
kept sliding to about 900 on 1 October. Thursday took it to 836. The market
has now lost about 540 stocks from its long uptrend since August. It sits
back where it was in mid-April, when it was still climbing out of the March
hole. The direction now is the opposite.

Three shocks have hit since spring. On 12 May about 500 stocks fell hard. On
15 September about 340 did. On Thursday 314 did. Each shock was a little
smaller in count than the one before. But each one hit a weaker market, with
fewer stocks left in an uptrend to fall from. This is still far from a March
washout. In March the middle count fell near 210 and almost a thousand stocks
fell hard in a day. Today the middle count is 561. The damage is serious. It
is not yet the kind of panic that tends to end a fall.

The day itself had the marks of real selling. On Wednesday more than four
stocks in ten moved less than three percent all day. On Thursday fewer than
one in five stayed that calm. About four stocks in ten traded four percent
below their prior close at some point. More than half of those closed near
their low, so sellers held their ground into the close. Only about one stock
in twelve traded four percent above its prior close, and fewer than a quarter
of those kept the gain. Turnover came to about 520 crore against about 380 a
year ago. Heavy volume on a wide down day says the sellers meant it.

Fear woke up and gold gave no shelter. The India VIX measures what traders
pay to protect against big moves. It jumped about ten percent to about 15.3.
It now sits about eleven percent above its thirty-week average and is up
about thirty percent over three months. Gold funds fell about nine percent
over the month. The Nifty fell about seven over the same span. Money that left
stocks for gold in September did worse than money that stayed. Nothing in
this data offered a safe place on Thursday.

## What the sectors say

The sector table shows the turn underneath reversed. The sheet tracks, for
each of 28 sectors, what share of its stocks sits above weekly trend lines

short reading weaker than the medium one. On Wednesday that count was 18.
Across sectors, only about one stock in five holds its four-week line. About
one in three holds the forty-week line. The early turn the last few briefs
described is gone for now.

IT fell least of all, after months of selling. The IT index was almost
flat on a day the Nifty fell about one and a half percent. It is still down

worst day. That often means the sellers in it are tired.

Banks held better than the market, through the big lenders. The bank index

Defence keeps the best shape of any large sector. The defence index fell

## What is leading

The yearly high list shrank by more than half. Only 30 stocks closed within a
rupee of a yearly high, against 79 on Wednesday. Eight in ten are small
companies. Only one is large. Metals, textiles and financials supply four
each. When the high list loses more than half its names in one day, the
leaders are being sold along with everything else. Yet the 30 that held rose
a typical six or seven percent over the week. They held because buyers wanted
them, not because the tide lifted them.

Every trend screen shrank, and the fresh ones shrank most. The trend template
screen checks eight conditions of a healthy uptrend. It fell from 219 names to
175. The Darvas screen wants a stock that has doubled off its low and trades
near its high. It fell from 87 to 75. The breakout screen wants short averages
stacked above long ones near a ten-year high. It fell from 104 to 46. The
near-high screen with rising volume fell from 62 to 27. Old trends lost about
a fifth of their names. New setups lost about half. In a break, the newest
breakouts fail first.

Fresh momentum nearly vanished on the daily clock. The sheet uses a reading
called CCI, which measures how far price has stretched from its recent
average. Only 12 stocks crossed into strong daily momentum, against 57 on
Wednesday. ICICI Lombard and Nazara are the largest of them. On the weekly
clock, 33 crossed into strong momentum. Kotak Bank, Coforge, Motilal Oswal and
HFCL lead that list by size. Only 36 stocks crossed up from a deeply sold
daily reading, against 151 the day before. Infosys and three big banks lead
that bounce list. On a day this wide, very little turned up.

Jewellers formed the clearest group that held together. DP Abhushan rose about
63 percent over the week, the top of the weekly list. PC Jeweller, Shanti Gold
and Sky Gold closed at yearly highs. Augmont sits on the mid-cap list of
stocks trading above the day's average price. Senco crossed up from a deeply
sold reading. TBZ ranks among the one-month and three-month leaders. Gold
funds fell about nine percent over the month. So the jewellers rose while the
metal they sell fell. The cause sits in their own business, which this data
cannot show.

Telecom equipment kept part of its run. HTEL rose about ten percent on the
day and about 48 percent over the week. HFCL sits on five screens. STL Tech
sits on the large-cap list above the day's average price and ranks among the
six-month leaders. Optiemus sits on five screens. The cluster thinned on
Thursday, and it did not break.

Small pharma kept its top names and lost its second rank. Bafna sits on six
screens and ranks among the leaders on five time horizons, the most of any
name. Morepen sits on six. Kilitch and Amanta sit on four. The deep bench
below them thinned out. In a sell-off, the best of a group often holds while
the followers give way.

Large-cap momentum still lives in a few single names. The momentum ranking
divides a stock's long-run gain by how bumpy the ride was. Neuland, Bosch,
Apar, PTC Industries, Solar Industries, Hitachi Energy, Page Industries and
MTAR lead it. PTC Industries and Solar Industries also held above the day's
average price. Only 12 large companies made that list, against 35 on
Wednesday. The mid-cap list fell from 91 to 24, and the small-cap list from
122 to 42. Intraday buying dried up at every size.

Froth is low. Only 12 stocks rose ten percent or more on the day. Only 32
rose five percent or more. The single stock up twenty percent was a rights
entitlement, not a share. On the other side, 12 stocks are down twenty
percent or more over five days, the most in a month. MBECL and Swan Defence
show huge return figures that come from a relisting or a data gap. They do not
count as leadership.

Confluence thinned, and it still names the survivors. 112 stocks sit on three

is a stronger fact than a stock on one. Dhampur Bio Organics sits on seven.
Arfin, Avadh Sugar, Bafna, Cupid, Hi-Tech Corp, Indiabulls Ltd, Morepen, SBC
Exports and Sigma Advanced sit on six. Industrials, healthcare, metals and
chemicals supply the most names across the momentum screens.

## Who is buying

First a caveat in plain words. Many rows in the shareholding files show zero

names with a real three-quarter history. For foreign money that is 783 of

Domestic institutions buy platforms and smaller lenders. Swiggy, PolicyBazaar,

increases, and all three sit on leader screens.

## Thirty points

### For the trader

**1. The bounce is disproven.** The long count fell to 836, through the 1
October low near 900. The middle count fell to 561, through its own low. A
bounce that ends in a lower low has failed.

**2. This was a true shock day.** 314 stocks fell four and a half percent, and
19 rose that much. That is about sixteen fallers for every riser. The 15
September shock was about the same size.

**3. The swing vote went to the sellers.** Almost nine stocks in ten closed
lower. Net breadth, the share of big risers minus big fallers, sits close
to its 15 September low. No sector index stayed green.

**4. Volume backed the sellers.** Turnover ran about a third above a year ago.
A wide down day on heavy volume carries more weight than a quiet slide. It
says holders chose to sell.

**5. Sellers held into the close.** More than half the stocks that traded four
percent down closed near their low. Buyers did not step in late. A turn
needs that pattern to flip.

**6. Strength was sold during the day.** Fewer than a quarter of stocks that
traded four percent up kept the gain. On Wednesday about four in ten did. A
breakout that cannot hold its gain for one session is not a breakout.

**7. Short selling has the easier tape now.** About one stock in twenty is
down ten percent in five days. About one in sixty is up fifteen percent. The
fast tails lean down, so the down side pays more often.

**8. The deep down tail is the largest in a month.** 12 stocks are down twenty
percent or more in five days. Some names are breaking hard, not drifting. A
tail past about 15 would mark a more violent phase.

**9. No size offered shelter.** The mid and small indices fell more than the
Nifty. The microcap index fell almost three percent. Size did not protect
anyone on Thursday.

**10. IT and the big banks held best.** IT was flat, and the bank index fell
about one percent. Infosys and three large banks crossed up from deeply sold
readings. In a weak market, a turn tends to start in what was sold hardest
earlier.

**11. The metal index hides its small names.** The metal index fell most of
all 35 lines. Arfin, Oil Country Tubular and GF Steels closed at yearly highs.
Read the stocks, not the index, in this sector.

**12. Jewellers are the one group that rose together.** PC Jeweller, Shanti

**13. Parts makers still beat car makers.** The auto index is the weakest over

The spread between parts and cars stays wide.

**14. Fresh breakouts fail fastest in a break.** The breakout screen and the

**15. Intraday buying dried up at every size.** The lists of stocks above the
day's average price shrank by two thirds or more in large, mid and small caps.
These lists must grow again before any bounce counts.

**16. Froth is not the worry.** Only 12 stocks rose ten percent or more. The
penny tail is quiet. The risk in this tape sits on the down side.

**17. Watch for a thrust day.** In April the market answered its worst days
with huge buying days. A session with about 300 stocks jumping four and a half
percent would show buyers are back. Until one comes, the turn is not on.

## What would change this read

The read tonight is a failed repair and a market at a fresh lower low. It gets
worse if the long count keeps falling below about 800, under the mid-April
level. It gets worse if new yearly lows stay above about 200 for several
sessions, or if the deep down tail grows past about 15 names. It also gets
worse if the VIX keeps climbing while turnover stays above last year's, which
would mean heavy and frightened selling. The step after that is a March-style
washout, with the middle count falling toward about 300.

The cautious read fails if buyers return with force, and soon. The first test
is a thrust day of about 300 or more stocks jumping four and a half percent,
on turnover above its year-ago level. April showed how fast that can come
after a shock. The second test is the long count climbing back above about
900, the 1 October low, within a few sessions. The third is new yearly lows
falling back under about 100 while the VIX eases toward 14. Friday's session
is the first chance for any of these. Until one shows up, the data favours
caution over hope.
