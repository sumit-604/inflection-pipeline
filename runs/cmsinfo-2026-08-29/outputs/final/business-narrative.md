# CMS Info Systems (CMSINFO) — Final Business Narrative

## BUSINESS UNDERSTANDING NARRATIVE

CMS Info Systems runs three businesses that move, guard, and automate physical cash for India's banks. The largest, ATM Management Solutions at 58% of services revenue, owns the machines, replenishes the cash, monitors the sites, and bills a fixed monthly fee on contracts of five to ten years. A bank cannot easily pull this in house, because CMS carries the equipment risk and runs a cash in transit network with 97% geographic coverage that took sixteen years to build. Retail Solutions and Currency Logistics, 26% of services revenue, collects cash from organised retailers and moves currency and bullion on mostly fixed fee terms, and it is the legacy labour network the company is now pruning. Technology and Payment Solutions, 16% of services revenue and up 42% in FY26, sells HAWKAI vision AI site monitoring, the ALGO cash forecasting software that runs on roughly 65,000 machines, and payment cards. The customers are a short list of large banks, State Bank of India, HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank, and India Post Payments Bank, plus organised retail chains on the retail side. Banks buy through multi year tenders, and CMS won its flagship SBI mandate as the lowest qualified bidder, so switching costs and contract tenure hold the revenue more than pricing power does. Present demand rides on cash still in circulation and on the physical ATM estate, which the RBI currency in circulation series and the total India ATM count both track, and on the AGS Transact CIRP resolution, which is freeing share as a weaker rival exits. National cash fulfilment fell after the AGS collapse and CMS revenue dipped with it, so the national cash fulfilment rate signal tells whether that drag is a passing shock or a permanent step down. Demand should grow because CMS gains share as banks tighten credit on weaker operators, a claim the SBI, ICICI, HDFC, and IPPB contract ramp signals can confirm quarter by quarter. The externally verifiable evidence is real: ATM Management grew 5.3% in FY26 while national ATM withdrawal value fell about 7% on the RBI and NPCI series, a twelve point gap that only share gain explains. The counter signal is UPI transaction volume, tracked at NPCI, which measures how fast digital pulls demand off the cash rails over time. On competitive advantage, the moat sits mainly in ATM Management, where the scan credits a proprietary sixteen year transaction dataset across 150,000 touchpoints, route optimisation that cut costs over 10%, and embedded multi year bank contracts. Technology and Payment Solutions carries the platform and full stack hardware strengths, but the scan flags that ALGO's installed base is essentially one customer today and that no patent evidence backs the proprietary language, so its moat is promising rather than proven. Retail and Currency Logistics shows no durable moat in the scan, a labour network competing on price that is being shrunk by design. The industry consolidation benefit from the AGS exit is scored two sided and unresolved, a share gain that management itself concedes cuts both ways.

## Cash operator repricing on an unfired gate

CMS moves about Rs 14 lakh crore of physical cash a year for a handful of large banks. It owns the ATMs, runs the cash in transit fleet, monitors the sites, and increasingly layers software on top of the same network. FY26 revenue was Rs 2,487 Cr, PAT Rs 303 Cr, and the shares trade near Rs 243, about 11 times forward earnings. The balance sheet is debt free with roughly Rs 442 Cr of net cash and an ICRA AA+ rating. There is no promoter. A private equity sponsor sold out in early 2024 and the company was declassified to fully public.

What is changing is the pricing, not the ownership. The old ATM model paid CMS per transaction, so revenue stopped when withdrawal volumes stopped while the machines kept depreciating. That model killed AGS Transact. CMS has moved the new book to fixed monthly fees on long contracts, which means it must own the machine and fund it ahead of the revenue. National ATM withdrawal value has fallen three years running at an accelerating rate, so the shift was a necessity the company executed from a strong balance sheet, not a free choice. Because it was forced rather than chosen, the destination returns need not match the returns the old labour business earned.

The numbers say a real step down in FY26 sitting on a solid base. Gate 0 scored GOOD, held up by a pristine balance sheet and a nine year record. But post tax ROCE fell from 25% to 16.6% in one year, EBITDA margin slipped to 24%, free cash flow turned slightly negative as capex nearly tripled, and the three year PAT CAGR is essentially flat at 0.7%. The cash flag is the sharpest item: the one to two year overdue receivables bucket jumped to Rs 151.6 Cr from Rs 17.8 Cr the year before, more than a sevenfold rise, in a book that grew only 5%, while the standalone loss allowance closest to that book was released rather than built. ICRA called the receivables transitory on nine month data. The audited full year showed the opposite.

Management is candid on the numbers it chooses to defend and quiet on the ones that complicate the story. The margin call is reliable: it raised FY27 EBITDA guidance to about 27% while cutting revenue, backed by a printed 27.2% quarter. Revenue guidance is not reliable: three straight years of guiding, walking down within the year, and missing. Two commitments were dropped without comment, a 74,000 to 75,000 ATM target and a promise that DSO would normalise by March 2026, and segment profitability disclosure was withdrawn in the same window the weakest segment was underperforming. The grade is B minus.

Peers confirm the cost story and contradict the receivables story. Radiant and SIS both agree minimum wage inflation is sector wide, and both moved on IBA repricing on the same timeline CMS cites. But both showed DSO improving in the exact window CMS's worsened, Radiant to 65 days from 70 and SIS holding near multi quarter lows around 63 to 67 days. That removes the industry wide excuse for the CMS receivables jump. AGSTRA's history matters here, because chronic receivable drift preceded its own insolvency, which raises the stakes on the CMS flag rather than lowering them.

The opportunity is large and the constraint is execution, not runway. The three platforms address a market of roughly Rs 21,000 Cr of serviceable revenue against CMS's current 11% share, about 9 times revenue headroom, and the runway grades STRONG. The share of the market CMS can realistically win implies about 11% to 12% revenue CAGR over three to five years, close to but below management's own 13% to 14% organic target, so the plan is reasonable rather than inflated.

Valuation lands on WATCHLIST. The destination exit multiple is 17.0 times forward earnings, band 15.7 to 18.3 times, built from a 16.0 times ROCE base times a 1.15 cash multiplier, then trimmed by operator ruling from a raw 18.4 times because a 43% premium to peers is too much to carry for a company fresh off its worst year. A more conservative reversion track lands near 14.9 times. On the governing track the three year value is about Rs 555, the 25% entry price is Rs 284 with a band of Rs 263 to Rs 305, and the margin of safety price is Rs 227. CMP Rs 243 sits 14% below entry and 7% above the safety price, which is buy on dips territory on price alone, and the Hurdle Ratio passes at 2.20 against a 1.953 threshold. Fair value itself compounds at 13.8% a year, a HYBRID return where roughly half comes from earnings growth and half from the discount closing. One verifier caveat rides the safety price: it was set at the 20% row without stating the evidence basis, and the governing catalyst sits 18 to 24 months out, which on a stricter reading would widen the floor toward Rs 170; the decision does not change either way.

Two things cap the disposition below the price signal. FLAG-CASH is INDETERMINATE, refined to industry event induced, so it cannot resolve silently to a clean pass. The FY28 proof gate, consolidated post tax ROCE above 22% on flat capital, has not fired. Overall confidence is 64, inside the 60 to 74 band, which already pulls the gate verdict down one level to PROCEED WITH CAVEATS.

The thesis verdict is WATCHLIST with position Small on conversion only, and there is no position while it stays on the watch list. The devil's advocate leaves the case WEAKENED BUT ALIVE, with all four dimensions weakened and sizing held at Small. The strongest counters are that a single FY28 ROCE print carries both the return pillars and about half the total return, so a one year slip on a management with a three year miss streak pushes the exit toward 12 times and the CAGR below the hurdle; that the recovery is a lower plateau by construction, since capital is permanently about a third higher and the spread over cost of equity is only three points; that the cheap EV read imports a levered Brink's anchor and the P/E method still reads 17 times as 32% expensive; and that the transaction linked brown label book, the exact AGS failure mode, rose from 8% to about 12% of revenue against the transition direction and is unexplained.
