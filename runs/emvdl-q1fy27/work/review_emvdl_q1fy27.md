# MANDATORY PLAIN-LANGUAGE BRIEF

### 1. SUMMARY NARRATIVE
Embassy Developments reported a wider Q1 FY27 consolidated net loss of ₹234 crore [this quarter filing], up from ₹166 crore a year earlier, though the year-ago base is one the company itself calls "not comparable" because of the 2025 reverse-merger accounting [this quarter filing]. On the earnings call the CFO confirmed the same numbers verbatim — revenue ₹217 crore, EBITDA minus ₹106 crore, net loss ₹234 crore — and, more usefully, that operating cash flow was minus ₹285 crore for the quarter, a real cash burn [this quarter concall]. The standalone parent is essentially a holding shell — ₹13 crore of revenue against ₹44 crore of its own salary bill — so the real business lives in the roughly 184 subsidiaries below it [this quarter filing]. Reported profits look bad partly by design: the merger forced assets onto the books at fair value, which suppresses accounting margin without hurting cash [this quarter filing]. The number that actually matters for this developer is presales, and Q1 presales were ₹868 crore [this quarter filing] — triple last year's tiny ₹198 crore, but 67% below the ₹2,632 crore of the prior quarter and below the ₹1,200 crore quarterly floor our monitoring calls a Red signal [Notion memory]. Management still guides to ₹6,000 crore of owned presales for the full year, the exact level at which our thesis breaks, and hitting it now needs about ₹5,100 crore over the next three quarters, roughly double the pace just delivered [this quarter filing]. The promoter managing director was on the call and was candid in places — he owned up to "losses for the next few quarters," explained the ₹362.62 crore warrant to a single promoter entity at ₹111.51 (more than double the market price) as a conversion of related-party debt into equity rather than new cash, and put a date on the turn: he expects a cash "inflection" around April–May 2027 when construction reaches the slab-pouring stage and collections accelerate [this quarter concall]. Two things on the call were less comfortable. First, he disclosed the average cost of debt is "around 14%," which is above the 12.5% level our thesis treats as a break by FY28 and above the ~12.3–12.8% we had in memory — most likely because 14% is the current pre-refinancing book (it includes Blackstone's 15% accruing loan), with a promised refinance to "below 14%" only by March–April 2027 [this quarter concall; Notion memory]. Second, when an analyst directly noted "there is a pledge also on the promoters' side" and asked for a valuation, management answered the valuation part and simply did not address the pledge [this quarter concall] — the pledge is one of our thesis-break conditions and remains unconfirmed. On the good side, net institutional debt fell about ₹719 crore in the quarter to ₹3,363 crore, the company raised ₹1,020 crore of new debt after quarter-end to refinance, and the NCLAT threw out the insolvency case in May so the company is no longer under CIRP [this quarter filing]. The auditors signed clean but flagged that 76% of consolidated revenue and 27% of the loss sit in entities they did not review, on numbers handed to them by management [this quarter filing]. Cash conversion is INDETERMINATE — the ₹285 crore operating burn is normal for a construction-heavy quarter but cannot be judged structural until the half-year statement arrives [this quarter filing; this quarter concall]. Of the 13 questions we set last quarter, the call answered one fully, four partly, and left eight unaddressed. No pre-committed thesis-broken trigger formally fired, so the Decision Status stays WATCHLIST / AVOID, position EXITED, and none of the three re-entry gates cleared [Notion memory; this quarter filing]. The single thing to watch is H1 presales and H1 cash flow: at least ₹2,400 crore of presales keeps the ₹6,000 crore floor alive, and a swing back toward positive operating cash would validate the April–May 2027 inflection claim; presales below ₹1,900 crore arms a mechanical downgrade [this quarter filing; this quarter concall].

### 2. SECTOR INTELLIGENCE
EMVDL is an Indian residential-led real-estate developer with a commercial/SEZ tail, concentrated in Bengaluru (its strongest market, 72% of launched inventory sold within six months) and expanding into the Mumbai Metropolitan Region via marquee projects like Embassy Citadel, Worli [this quarter filing]. The sector is mid-cycle: management reiterated its view that India is in a "multi-decade housing cycle" consolidating toward branded developers [this quarter concall], and residential demand in Bengaluru and MMR has supported the +135% year-on-year area-sold read — but the business is long-cycle and revenue is recognised only on project completion, so quarterly P&L is lumpy and lags cash and presales by years [this quarter filing]. A concrete sector signal came from a peer: an analyst noted Prestige's Q1 call flagged four Bangalore projects slipping from Q1 to Q2 on RERA/building-plan delays, and management confirmed the Bangalore planning-authority (GBA) disruption after the change of chief minister is real — though it said this did not drive its own decision not to launch in Q1 [this quarter concall]. That approval bottleneck is the sector headwind to watch, because 7 of EMVDL's 11 FY27 launches are second-half weighted [this quarter concall]. The structural tailwind our thesis leans on is NAV accretion as the FY26–FY28 launch pipeline (₹19,400 crore of FY27 launch GDV) converts to presales and then to earnings [this quarter filing; Notion memory]. The key sector-level headwind for this name is cost of debt: developers live and die on financing spreads, and the call put the current average at ~14% — above the ~10% the thesis ultimately needs — with a refinance only promised for around March–April 2027 [this quarter concall; Notion memory]. Regulatory/legal overhang is elevated versus peers: a now-dismissed insolvency case, a live KIADB land-possession dispute at Whitefield, and a Nashik debonding process management said will take another 6–9 months through five government agencies with a sublessee now intervening [this quarter filing; this quarter concall]. Payer mix is retail homebuyers plus development-management fees; there is no lender-style dynamic here.

### 3. BUSINESS-MODEL INTELLIGENCE
The company makes money three ways: developing and selling owned residential inventory, earning fees on development-management (DM) projects it builds for landowners, and holding a 3,000-plus-acre land bank as optionality [this quarter filing; this quarter concall]. The CFO laid out the unit economics on the call: on a ~₹57,000 crore GDV portfolio, roughly 30–35% is land cost, 30–35% construction, and 30–35% margin, for a cash surplus above 50% on most projects (because much of the land is fully paid) that will "reflect in the P&L over about two years" [this quarter concall] — management estimates, not audited figures, so treat as indicative. The model's cash rhythm is: presales (booking) → collections (₹496 crore this quarter, phased ~10% on booking + 10% at 90 days) → construction spend (₹276 crore, ~56% of collections) → completion and revenue recognition years later [this quarter filing; this quarter concall]. The critical model tell this quarter is cash conversion: management admitted the collection-to-presales ratio was only ~35% last year, is targeted at ~50% this year, and industry runs ~70% — so this business converts sales to cash more slowly than peers, and that is the gap the April–May 2027 "inflection" is supposed to close [this quarter concall]. Model drift worth flagging: DM presales are guided at ₹2,000 crore but there is still NO separate DM-fee line — the fee economics stay buried in a single reportable segment [this quarter filing], exactly the "hidden DM fees" our monitoring flags Red [Notion memory]. The balance-sheet model is shifting too — the promoter is injecting ₹362.62 crore of fresh equity via above-market warrants to retire shareholder debt that was accruing at over 15%, effectively swapping related-party debt for related-party equity [this quarter concall]. Management is also "actively exploring" a switch to percentage-of-completion accounting, which would change how and when losses/profits appear [this quarter concall]. Deferred-tax assets are barely being recognised on mounting losses, signalling management doubt about near-term taxable profit [this quarter filing]. Liquidity is comfortable (₹1,202 crore cash) and deleveraging is real, but the model does not yet convert revenue into operating profit under current accounting, and the quarter burned ₹285 crore of operating cash.

### 4. COMPETITION INTELLIGENCE
EMVDL competes with large listed developers in its core geographies — most directly Prestige, Sobha and Brigade in Bengaluru, and Lodha, Godrej Properties and DLF in MMR/NCR [Notion memory]. This quarter gave one genuine peer read: management referenced Prestige's Q1 Bangalore launch deferrals and positioned itself as insulated (its Q1 non-launch was a deliberate choice, not an approval failure) [this quarter concall] — plausible but self-serving, and testable if EMVDL's own H2-weighted Bangalore launches slip. No full peer concall was available in the window to cross-check pricing or demand, stated explicitly. Where EMVDL WINS: brand and scale (the Embassy Group's delivered track record, listed-REIT lineage, a rare fully-paid 3,000-plus-acre land bank, and a genuine Bengaluru absorption edge at 72% sold in six months versus 60% in Mumbai) [this quarter filing; this quarter concall]. Where it is structurally WEAKER: balance-sheet and governance quality versus better-capitalised peers — a ~14% average cost of debt that is high for the sector, a slower ~35–50% cash-conversion rate versus a ~70% industry norm, a promoter pledge that management declined to quantify when asked [this quarter concall; Notion memory], a recently-dismissed-but-real insolvency history, heavy reliance on other-auditor/management-furnished subsidiary numbers, and a promoter-concentration pattern (single-allottee warrant, promoter-family SMP) that cleaner peers like Godrej Properties do not carry [this quarter filing]. The competitive risk to watch: peers with lower funding costs can out-launch and out-price EMVDL in MMR precisely as it makes its Worli/Panvel push, and management still would not give Citadel absorption or realised PSF, so we cannot yet confirm the MMR entry is competitively landing [this quarter concall]. Metrics the company did NOT disclose for competitive benchmarking: realised PSF, Citadel absorption %, the exact NCD coupon, an NAV number, and any peer-relative pricing — all deferred to IR follow-up / next quarter.
