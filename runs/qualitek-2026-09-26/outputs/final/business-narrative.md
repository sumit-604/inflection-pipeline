## BUSINESS UNDERSTANDING NARRATIVE

Qualitek runs accredited laboratories that test other companies' products against required standards and hand back a signed report. A factory needs that report before it can sell, export or certify what it makes. Transport, defence and mechanical testing is 40 to 45% of revenue: emission, vibration, durability, EV battery and EMI/EMC tests against BIS, ASTM and international standards. Food and feed testing is 25 to 30% and pharma testing is 15 to 20%, both run mostly through Interstellar Testing Centre, the subsidiary that supplied about 45% of FY26 consolidated revenue. The balance is new inspection, audit and certification work under a NABCB Type A accreditation, plus two small bought businesses, QTIPL in software and LabOps Global in lab operations, and neither reports material revenue. The customer cannot easily do without the report because a regulator or a buyer requires an accredited lab to sign it, and a validated product moved to a new lab must be validated again, although the run did not quantify that switching cost.

The customers are manufacturers and processors in automotive, defence, food, pharma, minerals and electricals, plus government bodies. Six of the 16 labs run in PPP mode, and the named public sector clients are Indian Oil at Panipat and at its 2G ethanol plant, and Western Coalfields through the Quality Council of India. Customers buy on purchase orders with no long term contracts. The last concentration figure is from FY23, before the acquisitions: the top 5 customers were 25% and the top 10 were 38% of standalone revenue. The run did not establish current customer concentration.

Present demand comes from mandates. New BIS Quality Control Orders, FSSAI food safety circulars and MoRTH vehicle safety and emission rules each create tests a manufacturer must buy. The NABL and NABCB accreditation status decides whether Qualitek may sell those tests at all. Indian Oil and other PSU work order renewals, the USFDA status of the Bengaluru pharma lab and the IIP manufacturing index are the other named demand signals.

Demand should grow as regulation widens. The run puts third party testing market growth near 6.5% a year on its conservative basis, and Vimta puts its own sector growth at 7.5 to 9%. Vimta also puts the electronics industry at about 4.5%, the slowest figure any peer gave, and that is the segment Qualitek plans to enter in South India. Each new Quality Control Order or FSSAI mandate is gazetted, so this driver can be checked outside the company. Qualitek names no market size and no industry growth rate in any filing.

The moat sits in accreditations and government relationships, not in price. Accreditation lock in scored Strong in the emerging moat scan, and the government customer book scored Moderate, but those contracts run 24 to 36 months and must be won again. Under verifier review the USFDA lab and first mover claims fell to inference, the international partner list fell to claim, and the corrected score of 12.5 sits 0.5 above the line between MODEST and NONE. The testing lines hold a partial moat through accreditation, but pricing power is weak because every job is priced per purchase order. The inspection line holds an accreditation with no disclosed revenue, and QTIPL and LabOps show no moat on any evidence. Categories 21 and 22, talent asymmetry and the cannibalization barrier, did not score and sit in the optionality register as unevidenced.

## Accredited lab network grows faster than cash

Qualitek Labs is a testing, inspection and certification company listed on the BSE SME board in January 2024. It runs 16 labs, 10 owned and 6 under PPP contracts, with more than 1,200 staff, 450 of them scientists, and it runs more than 5 million tests a year. FY26 consolidated revenue was Rs 125 Cr, up 77%, and profit after tax was about Rs 15 Cr. The parent company alone booked Rs 68 Cr. Employee cost is 36% of revenue and materials are 13%, so this is a people and accreditation business, not a manufacturer.

The company changed shape in 14 months. It bought half of Interstellar Testing Centre for about Rs 33 Cr in cash in July 2024 and the other half by share swap in September 2025, and 70% of the swap shares went to the promoter entity. It bought QTIPL for Rs 5 Cr, a Mumbai food lab by slump sale for Rs 8.8 Cr, and 74% of LabOps Global. Goodwill rose from Rs 33 Cr to Rs 78 Cr, about half of consolidated net worth. On the company's own FY25 walk through base of Rs 85 Cr, like for like FY26 growth was about 46.5%, not 77%. The parent's figures are not a clean organic series either, because they include the Mumbai lab from September 2025. The FY26 MD&A says borrowings funded a Bengaluru USFDA lab acquisition, but no Reg 30 filing and no audited acquisition note records that deal.

Gate 0 scored 58 out of 160 after verifier correction and classified AVERAGE. Growth took full marks: parent revenue compounded at 60% a year from FY21 to FY26 with no down year. Cash scored 5 of 20 and the balance sheet 6 of 20. The return block scored 8, exactly at the line where a deal breaker fires. Parent ROCE rose from 6.4% in FY21 to 15.4% in FY24, then fell to 7.4% in FY26 as new capital outran earnings. Gross margin rose from 78% to 87% over FY23 to FY26 while EBITDA margin fell from 28% to 23 to 24%, so the new labs are still a cost. The first sign of operating leverage came in H2 FY26: consolidated EBITDA margin went from 20.7% in H1 to 25.7% in H2, as the H1 filing had promised.

Cash has not followed profit. Operating cash flow was Rs 14 Cr against EBITDA of Rs 30 Cr, a conversion of 0.46. Free cash flow was about minus Rs 32 Cr, and the parent has not had one free cash positive year in six. Parent working capital days moved from minus 65 in FY21 to plus 80 in FY26. Receivables older than six months held flat at 22% of the total, but the one to two year and two to three year buckets more than doubled, mostly at Interstellar. There is no credit rating to cross check any of this. Debt to equity reads 0.45 for the parent and 0.61 for the group on the audited balance sheet, and 1.25 in the intake brief, which took it from a web article; the gap is not reconciled. The company took Rs 49 Cr of new long term loans in FY26, approved a Rs 65 Cr secured NCD whose security package contemplates a share pledge, and announced a Rs 47 Cr preferential issue in September 2026. Two readings fit. Capex for new labs explains the free cash deficit, but the 0.46 conversion and the ageing buckets are operating facts, and no filing shows how much of the book is government money.

Management holds no earnings calls, so credibility rests on filed guidance against filed results, and the grade is C. The company delivered the IPO fund use without deviation, repaid Rs 8.2 Cr of promoter loans, closed the Mumbai deal and delivered the H2 margin recovery. It missed its FY25 Rs 90 Cr revenue projection by about 5.6% on a like for like basis and never reconciled it. The Hyderabad acquisition was promised twice and has not closed, and the September 2026 raise does not name it among its objects while it sets aside Rs 22 Cr for unidentified acquisitions, including a new cybersecurity vertical. Three long term targets set in the May 2026 MD&A, operating margin above 30%, overseas revenue at 30% by FY31 and debt free by FY32, are missing from the annual report four months later. FY27 profit margin guidance reads 15 to 17% in the deck and 14 to 15% in the annual report. The claim that revenue doubled against a two year promise holds at about 2.0 times on the company's own combined base, not the 4.3 times one stage first computed.

The promoter verdict is CONCERN with no deal breakers. The Interstellar swap, worth about Rs 52 Cr, was priced about 65% above the arm's length cash round a year earlier, and the annual report names no valuer and no fairness opinion. The company secretary changed at least three times in 17 months, possibly four. A non executive director who chairs a board committee attended 4 of 8 board meetings, and two of his firms bill the company as related parties. The board proposed raising the managerial pay ceiling from 28% to 35% of profit, then withdrew the item before the vote without a reason. The same record carries real positives on the same evidence bar: clean audit opinions with no restatement and no going concern language, the pay item withdrawn, a cost accountant added as an independent director, and Ashish Kacholia as the largest allottee in the September 2026 issue.

Peers confirm the lab economics and contradict the disclosure. All three peers report that new labs lose money in their first year and take several years to reach normal margins, so Qualitek's H1 FY26 new lab loss of Rs 3.3 Cr matches the sector pattern. Krsnaa reports government receivables near 150 days, falling to 139, which supports long collection cycles in PPP work, but that rests on one peer. Metropolis discloses its acquisition multiple, under 2 times revenue and about 10 times EBITDA; Qualitek discloses nothing on the Interstellar swap. Vimta and Krsnaa both answer for missed targets on their calls, while Qualitek drops targets in writing without comment. Vimta already runs defence EMI/EMC testing in Hyderabad and says customers will not ship samples there from Pune, which reads as an incumbent in the region Qualitek plans to enter.

The market is not the constraint. The run sizes the conservative total market at Rs 75,840 Cr and the served market at Rs 18,800 Cr, and Qualitek holds 0.66% of the served market, a runway class of MASSIVE. Market share arithmetic gives Rs 331 Cr of revenue in three years and Rs 504 Cr in five, 38.6% and 32.3% a year, close to management's own Rs 500 Cr FY31 ambition. Both paths need about 42 labs against 16 today, which disclosed capex cannot fund, so both depend on more acquisitions at the pace already shown. Four market size reports span a fourfold range, and the prospectus printed its market figure in millions where the source says billions.
