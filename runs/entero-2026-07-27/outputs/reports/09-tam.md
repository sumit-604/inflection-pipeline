## SECTION 1: MARKET DEFINITION

### 1A. Precise boundaries

**Product scope (IN):**
- Pharmaceutical formulations distribution — branded, generic, specialty — across the full SKU catalogue (Entero handles 97,500+ SKUs FY26, sourced from 3,300+ manufacturers).
- MedTech distribution: IVD reagents/analysers (including reagent-rental annuity models), cardiology devices, orthopaedics, surgical/medical consumables. This is where Entero's own strategic focus sits ("current business and acquisitions are focused around IVD and Cardiology/Orthopedic devices segment," Q4FY26 & Q1FY27 investor decks, p.14/p.10).

**Product scope (OUT):**
- Manufacturing/API production (Entero is a pure trading/distribution entity per B04, business_type "trading").
- Capital medical imaging equipment (CT/MRI/large diagnostic imaging systems) and the "Others" bucket of the MedTech pie — these are typically OEM-direct-to-hospital sales bypassing the stockist channel, and are explicitly outside Entero's stated current focus.
- Direct-to-consumer e-pharmacy retail (Entero is B2B wholesale/stockist, not a retail chemist).

**Geographic scope:** India only. No disclosed international operations. Footprint as of Q1FY27: 138 warehouses, 45-50 cities, 475-523 districts, 19-21 states/UTs (Investor Presentations, Q4FY26 p.15, Q1FY27 p.11/p.15; AR FY26 p.6).

**Customer scope:** B2B — retail pharmacies/chemists (1,05,300+ retailer relationships FY26) and hospitals/institutions (3,600+ hospital customers FY26). Excludes patient/consumer-facing retail sales value (MRP) and excludes large corporate hospital chains' direct-to-manufacturer procurement that bypasses distributors.

**Channel scope:** Wholesale/stockist distribution + emerging reagent-rental/annuity MedTech model + the Enteropreneur partner-led franchise layer and HealthEdge chemist-engagement platform (both extensions of the same core distribution channel, not separate markets). Excludes government/PSU tender procurement channels that run through separate state medical corporations, to the extent CRISIL's own "distributor TAM" figure (used below) already scopes to the private-distributor-addressable channel.

**Price segment:** Full range — branded, generic, specialty, OTC. No segment exclusion by price tier; Entero's 97,500+ SKU catalogue is deliberately broad.

**Explicit inclusion:** the market is framed as the **distribution revenue pool** — i.e., the throughput value that flows through stockists/wholesalers at distributor selling price (the same basis on which Entero itself reports "sale of traded goods" revenue), **not** the margin/spread pool alone, and **not** end-consumer MRP retail sales value, which would double-count the retailer's own markup.

**Explicit exclusion:** manufacturing revenue, export pharma distribution outside India, hospital in-house/captive pharmacy dispensing that never touches a third-party stockist, and government tender-channel volumes (to the extent distinguishable from CRISIL's private-distributor scope).

## SECTION 2: TAM ESTIMATION, MULTIPLE METHODS

All figures rolled forward to an **FY26 reference year** (Entero's FY26 = Apr 2025-Mar 2026) unless noted. Every roll-forward calculation is shown.

### Method 1 — Top-down (primary)

**Pharma distribution leg**, from the CRISIL figure (RHP, Feb-2024): Rs 2,70,000 Cr, FY23, 10-11% CAGR FY23-28E.
- Conservative (10% CAGR, 3 years FY23→FY26): 2,70,000 × 1.10³ = 2,70,000 × 1.331 = **Rs 3,59,370 Cr**
- Realistic (11% CAGR): 2,70,000 × 1.11³ = 2,70,000 × 1.3676 = **Rs 3,69,260 Cr**

Cross-check against an **independent, non-company-commissioned** source: IQVIA/AIOCD-based Indian Pharmaceutical Market (IPM), reported at **Rs 2,40,672 Cr for CY2025, +8.1% YoY** (Tribune, 8-Jan-2026 — recent, not stale) — IQVIA MAT data through mid-2026 shows growth accelerating toward 10.7% (volume +3.2pp, price +4.5pp, new-product +3pp contribution). Rolling one year at 8.5%: Rs 2,40,672 × 1.085 ≈ **Rs 2,61,129 Cr FY26E**. This IQVIA figure is **narrower in scope** (retail/hospital-panel audited sales, excludes institutional tenders, exports-via-distributor, and non-drug SKUs) than CRISIL's "distributor TAM," so the ratio (CRISIL Rs 3.6-3.7L Cr ÷ IQVIA Rs 2.6L Cr ≈ 1.4x) is **explained by scope, not contradicted** — CRISIL's distributor-addressable definition is deliberately wider than the IQVIA retail-audit panel.

**MedTech leg**, from the company's unattributed industry slide: USD 13-13.5bn FY25E, 12-14% p.a.
- Conservative (13bn low end, 12% growth, 1 year to FY26E): 13 × 1.12 = **USD 14.56bn**
- Realistic (13.25bn midpoint, 13% growth): 13.25 × 1.13 = **USD 14.97bn**
- FX conversion assumption (flagged): **Rs 85/USD** (approximate FY26 average; not independently sourced in this run — carries FX risk both ways).
  - Conservative: 14.56bn × Rs 8,500 Cr/USDbn = **Rs 1,23,760 Cr**
  - Realistic: 14.97bn × Rs 8,500 Cr/USDbn = **Rs 1,27,245 Cr**
- Independent cross-check: a February-2026 Rubix Industry Insights estimate puts India medical devices at **USD 15.2bn (2025) → USD 50.1bn (2030)** — a similar endpoint reached on a faster (2030 vs FY35) timeline than the company's own slide, i.e., the company's own claim sits at the **conservative end** of external device-market growth-rate estimates, even though the two use different segment definitions ("medical devices" vs the company's broader "MedTech").

**Method 1 combined (pharma + MedTech, full industry size before SAM filters):**
- Conservative: 3,59,370 + 1,23,760 = **Rs 4,83,130 Cr ≈ Rs 4,83,000 Cr**
- Realistic: 3,69,260 + 1,27,245 = **Rs 4,96,505 Cr ≈ Rs 4,96,500 Cr**

### Method 2 — Bottom-up (retail-pharmacy channel only — a deliberate subset, not full TAM)

Addressable unit = retail pharmacy. Trade-body estimates for total Indian retail pharmacies range 7-10 lakh (IPA/AIOCD-adjacent commentary cited in secondary sources); take the **conservative low end, 8,00,000 pharmacies**.
Average annual patient-facing (MRP) sales per pharmacy, derived from IQVIA IPM CY2025 (Rs 2,40,672 Cr) ÷ 8,00,000 pharmacies ≈ **Rs 30.1 lakh/pharmacy/year at MRP**. Converting MRP to distributor-selling-price basis (removing the retailer's own 16-22% markup per the sourced margin-chain data) ≈ Rs 30.1L × 0.83 ≈ **Rs 25 lakh/pharmacy/year**.
Bottom-up retail-channel-only TAM = 8,00,000 × Rs 0.25 Cr = **Rs 2,00,000 Cr**.

This is **materially below** Method 1's pharma leg (Rs 3.6-3.7L Cr) **by construction**: it captures only the retail-pharmacy channel and excludes the hospital/institutional channel that CRISIL's broader distributor-TAM figure includes. It is presented as a **floor/sanity-check on the retail slice**, not a competing full-scope estimate — the divergence is explained by scope, not averaged away.

### Method 3 — Peer / market-share back-solve

Independent secondary-source data (not company-disclosed, MEDIUM confidence): *"The leading three national distributors — Keimed, Ascent Health & Wellness Solutions, and Entero — collectively held an estimated 8-10% market share in FY23"* of the pharma distribution market, consistent with the CRISIL organized-share figure above. Separately: *"Entero's market share in the healthcare distribution market has grown from 0.8% in FY21 to 1.3% in FY24"* of the **combined pharmaceutical and medical devices distribution market** (third-party analysis, Finance Pulse / Investing Stoics deep-dives, not primary-sourced).

Back-solving from Entero's own disclosed FY24 revenue (Rs 3,922 Cr, per AR FY26 "Building a Highly Scalable Business Model" chart, p.16):
Implied combined pharma+MedTech distribution TAM, FY24 = 3,922 ÷ 0.013 = **Rs 3,01,692 Cr**
Rolled forward to FY26 at an ~11% blended CAGR (2 years): 3,01,692 × 1.11² = 3,01,692 × 1.2321 = **Rs 3,71,655 Cr ≈ Rs 3,72,000 Cr**.

Also, named-competitor scale check: **Keimed (part of Apollo HealthCo)** reported **Rs 10,300 Cr revenue in FY24**, "more than twice the nearest competitor" — consistent with Keimed being materially larger than Entero (Rs 3,922 Cr FY24) and confirming the market remains led by a small number of scaling organized players inside a much larger unorganized base.

This Method 3 estimate (**Rs 3,72,000 Cr FY26E**) is **lower** than Method 1's combined estimate (Rs 4,83,000-4,96,500 Cr), most plausibly because the third-party market-share denominator used a narrower or slower-growing MedTech assumption than the company's own 12-14% p.a. slide claim. Per the conservative-bias rule, **this lower, independently-triangulated figure is adopted as the CONSERVATIVE anchor for the SAM/SOM cascade below**, rather than averaging it against Method 1.

### Method 4 — Import substitution: NOT APPLICABLE

Entero is a domestic distribution business; the relevant import/export dynamic (API imports, device imports) sits upstream at the manufacturer/OEM level, not at the distributor-margin-pool level being sized here. Not pursued further — the effect would only matter if it changed the *volume* moving through distribution, which is already captured in the top-down and peer-share methods above.

### Method 5 — Structure cross-check (organized vs unorganized, in place of a formal global per-capita benchmark)

Global per-capita pharma-spend benchmarking was **attempted and abandoned** for the headline number: available search results gave per-capita pharma spend for Canada (~$871) and Korea (~$818) but **no comparable India, China, or reliable global-average figure could be sourced within this run** (marked **NOT FOUND** for the headline India-vs-China-vs-global comparison; directional underpenetration is well-established in trade literature but not independently quantified here).
In its place, the **organized-vs-unorganized structure** data is used as the fifth triangulation lens: **~88.7% of India's retail pharmacies were unorganized local medical stores as of 2021**, and **~90-92% of distributor-level sales still ran through traditional local distributors** as of the most recent secondary-source estimate, versus **8-10% organized** (consistent with the CRISIL figure in 1B). This corroborates the scale of the formalization opportunity independently of the two revenue-based methods above.

## SECTION 3: SAM & SOM

### 3A. SAM

Base: **conservative TAM Rs 3,72,000 Cr FY26E**. Using the Method-1 relative pharma:MedTech weighting (≈74.4% : 25.6%) to split this blended figure for filtering purposes: **pharma ≈ Rs 2,76,800 Cr; MedTech ≈ Rs 95,200 Cr**.

1. **Product fit filter (SOURCED — company's own MedTech mix disclosure):** Entero's strategic focus explicitly excludes Imaging (14%) and most of "Others" (34%) — only IVD (17%) + Cardio/Ortho (15%) + Consumables (20%) = **52%** of the MedTech pie is product-fit-relevant. Pharma leg unchanged (full-catalogue distributor, no filter needed — 97,500+ SKUs already spans branded/generic/specialty).
   MedTech: 95,200 × 0.52 = Rs 49,504 Cr. Subtotal = 2,76,800 + 49,504 = **Rs 3,26,304 Cr**

2. **Geography filter (ANALYST JUDGMENT, informed by a sourced stat):** Entero covers 523 of India's ~766 total districts (≈68%, AR FY26 p.6 + district-count context), but already covers the highest-value urban/semi-urban districts first — apply a **15% discount** (less severe than the raw 32% coverage gap would imply, since remaining districts are lower-value on average).
   3,26,304 × 0.85 = **Rs 2,77,358 Cr**

3. **Capability filter (ANALYST JUDGMENT):** Excludes niche categories requiring capability not yet at scale — cold-chain biologics/oncology distribution, capital-equipment installation & service contracts. Apply a **5% discount**.
   2,77,358 × 0.95 = **Rs 2,63,490 Cr**

**SAM ≈ Rs 2,63,000 Cr** (rounded). **SAM as % of conservative TAM = 2,63,000 ÷ 3,72,000 ≈ 70.7%.**
(For context: applying the identical 52% / 0.85 / 0.95 cascade to the *realistic* TAM's components would yield SAM ≈ Rs 3,47,000 Cr — the conservative anchor is carried forward into the SOM cascade below per the conservative-bias rule.)

### 3B. SOM at 3 and 5 years

**Current SAM share:** Entero FY26 revenue Rs 6,591 Cr ÷ SAM Rs 2,63,000 Cr = **2.51% ≈ 2.5%**.
Cross-check: this is internally consistent with the independent 1.3% FY24 *TAM*-share figure (Section 2, Method 3) grossed up for SAM being ~70.7% of TAM (1.3% ÷ 0.707 ≈ 1.8% of TAM-equivalent-SAM-basis in FY24, rising to an implied ~1.77% of TAM / ~2.5% of SAM by FY26 given Entero's revenue nearly doubled FY24→FY26 while the market grew ~11-12%/yr) — the two independently-derived current-share figures agree closely.

**Share-gain trajectory:** Entero's own historical pace (TAM-share 0.8%→~1.3%→~1.77% FY21→FY24→FY26E) implies roughly +0.5-0.7pp of TAM-share per 3-year window historically, which is **within the framework's "normal" 1-2pp/3yr band** once rescaled to a SAM basis. The unorganized share here is ~90% (>40% threshold), which the framework explicitly permits to justify **faster-than-normal** share gain from formalization — but Entero's own B04 flags (debt-equity +92% YoY, ~Rs 351 Cr near-term put-option/deferred-consideration cash calls, high-credit-risk receivables +166% YoY) mean the "aggressive" 3-5pp band is **not yet evidenced** by demonstrated execution/financing capacity. A **moderately-elevated-but-not-aggressive** path is used:

- **3-year SAM share: 2.5% + 1.2pp = 3.7%** (upper end of "normal," justified by the formalization tailwind, short of "aggressive").
- **5-year SAM share: 2.5% + 2.0pp = 4.5%**.

SAM projected forward at the blended ~11% market CAGR (pharma 10-11%, MedTech 12-14%, weighted):
- SAM FY29 (3yr): 2,63,000 × 1.11³ = 2,63,000 × 1.3676 = **Rs 3,59,638 Cr**
- SAM FY31 (5yr): 2,63,000 × 1.11⁵ = 2,63,000 × 1.6851 = **Rs 4,43,181 Cr**

**SOM 3yr = 3.7% × 3,59,638 = Rs 13,307 Cr ≈ Rs 13,300 Cr**
**SOM 5yr = 4.5% × 4,43,181 = Rs 19,943 Cr ≈ Rs 19,900 Cr**

**Implied revenue CAGR (arithmetic shown — FORMAL handoff to Stage 11):**
- 3yr: (13,300 ÷ 6,591)^(1/3) − 1 = (2.018)^0.333 − 1 ≈ **26.4%**
- 5yr: (19,900 ÷ 6,591)^(1/5) − 1 = (3.020)^0.2 − 1 ≈ **24.8%**

**vs. management's 20%+ multi-year claim / FY27 guide of 23.0% (organic, ex-M&A):** the SOM-implied CAGR (26.4% / 24.8%) sits **above** management's stated organic floor, but **below** Entero's actual recent delivery (FY26 total LFL growth 31.5%, organic LFL 15.6% — i.e., roughly half of FY26's growth was M&A-driven). Read: the SOM-implied CAGR is a reasonable proxy for **continued market-share-gain-plus-formalization growth inclusive of some M&A**, consistent with, not contradicting, management's guidance — it simply assumes the roll-up engine keeps running at a *slower* pace than FY26's exceptional M&A year (7 acquisitions), not that organic growth alone reaches 26-27%.

### 3C. Capacity cross-check

**B07 capex-embedded-growth figure was not provided** (Stage 7 running concurrently, per task brief). Read directly from the FY26 AR instead: **Note 5(A), Property Plant & Equipment, p.197 (printed)** — organic/cash capex (PP&E additions during the year, excluding PP&E acquired through business combinations) = **Rs 159.39 Cr in FY26**, against Rs 6,591 Cr revenue (**2.4% of revenue**). This confirms the asset-light distributor profile already flagged in B04 — **fixed-asset capex is not the binding constraint** on SOM capture.

The real capacity constraint for a WC-intensive trading business is **working-capital funding**, not PP&E. Current Net Operating Working Capital runs ~59-68 days (Q4FY26 investor deck p.11). Scaling FY26 revenue (Rs 6,591 Cr) to SOM-3yr revenue (Rs 13,300 Cr) at a similar ~59-day NWC cycle implies incremental NWC funding need of roughly:
(13,300 − 6,591) × 59/365 ≈ Rs 1,084 Cr of additional working capital over 3 years.

**This is the gap, and the SOM is the optimistic side of it, not the capex plan** — B04 already flags debt-equity up 92% YoY, current ratio down 36% YoY, and ~Rs 351 Cr of near-term put-option/deferred-consideration cash calls with an undisclosed funding source. A further ~Rs 1,000-1,100 Cr of working-capital funding, on top of those existing near-term calls, is **not yet demonstrated as fundable** at the current leverage trajectory. FY26's positive operating cash flow (Rs 96 Cr, guided 50% OCF/EBITDA conversion for FY27) is a partial offset but is small relative to the incremental funding need.

**Capacity check verdict: gap of approximately Rs 1,000-1,100 Cr in incremental working-capital financing by year 3; the SOM revenue path is the optimistic side of this gap, not the balance sheet.**

### 4C. Market structure

- **Competitor count:** ~65,000 stockists nationally estimated (highly fragmented); three organized national-scale players named in independent sources — **Keimed (Apollo HealthCo, Rs 10,300 Cr FY24 revenue, "more than twice the nearest competitor")**, **Ascent Health & Wellness Solutions**, and **Entero (Rs 3,922 Cr FY24)**.
- **Top-3 concentration:** ~8-10% combined (FY23, CRISIL), rising toward 20-30% by FY28 (CRISIL + management).
- **Organized vs unorganized:** ~10% vs ~90% (FY23/24) — a genuinely fragmented market by the framework's own 30-60% unorganized benchmark, at the extreme end.
- **Consolidating or fragmenting:** Actively **consolidating** — Entero alone has completed 51 acquisitions since 2018 (7 in FY26), and peers are scaling similarly (Keimed under Apollo HealthCo).
- **Price vs differentiation competition:** Largely service-level (fill-rate, SKU breadth, credit terms, delivery speed) rather than product differentiation — distribution is intrinsically a pass-through, low-differentiation business, consistent with B04's "price-taker" pricing-power classification.
- **Entries and exits:** Heavy M&A activity among a small number of well-capitalised roll-ups; limited new entrants at Entero's scale given the capital intensity of roll-up execution. Q1FY27 deck also notes a "conscious exit from certain low-margin businesses" — a data point worth tracking (districts-served figure dipped from 523 FY26 to 475 in the Q1FY27 deck, alongside this stated exit).
- **Import share trend:** Not directly applicable at the distribution layer (products are already manufactured/imported before reaching the distributor); indirect exposure only.

## SECTION 5: SUMMARY & RUNWAY

### 5A. Funnel

```
TAM (conservative, FY26E)  : Rs 3,72,000 Cr   [Method 3, peer/market-share back-solve]
TAM (realistic, FY26E)     : Rs 4,96,500 Cr   [Method 1, CRISIL pharma + company MedTech slide]
   -> Product fit (MedTech 52% relevant)
   -> Geography (-15%, judgment)
   -> Capability (-5%, judgment)
SAM (FY26E)                : Rs 2,63,000 Cr   (70.7% of conservative TAM)
   -> Current share 2.5%, +1.2pp (3yr) / +2.0pp (5yr), SAM growing at ~11%/yr
SOM (3yr, FY29)             : Rs 13,300 Cr    (implied revenue CAGR 26.4%)
SOM (5yr, FY31)             : Rs 19,900 Cr    (implied revenue CAGR 24.8%)
Current revenue (FY26)      : Rs 6,591 Cr
```

### 5B. Runway assessment

- **Revenue headroom** = SAM ÷ current revenue = 2,63,000 ÷ 6,591 ≈ **39.9x**.
- **TAM growth rate** ≈ **~11% p.a.** blended (pharma 10-11% weighted ~74%, MedTech 12-14% weighted ~26%).
- **Company CAGR vs TAM:** Entero's FY26 reported revenue growth (29.3%, LFL 31.5%) runs at roughly **2.7-2.9x the underlying market growth rate** — the company is unambiguously **gaining share**, not merely riding the market, consistent with the 0.8%→1.3%→~1.8% TAM-share trajectory (Section 3B).
- **Years to saturate SAM at current growth:** at a sustained ~25% CAGR (using the SOM-implied rate as the more durable assumption than FY26's M&A-heavy 29-31%), ln(39.9) ÷ ln(1.25) ≈ **16.5 years** to fully saturate today's SAM — though in practice growth would decelerate well before then as easy share-gain opportunities are exhausted; this is the standard idealized runway metric, not a forecast.

### 5C. Runway classification

**STRONG** (bordering MASSIVE). ~40x revenue headroom against a double-digit-growing TAM, with the company demonstrably taking share 2.7-2.9x faster than the market, in a market that is ~90% unorganized. Held at STRONG rather than MASSIVE because (a) the TAM triangulation carries a genuine Rs 1.2 lakh Cr spread between conservative and realistic estimates, (b) the SOM capture path is capacity-gapped on working-capital financing (Section 3C), not merely a function of market size, and (c) the AR's own headline TAM claim (Rs 2.8L Cr equivalent) is itself the most conservative of all the figures triangulated here.
