## SECTION 3: BUSINESS CONDUCT & ETHICAL TRACK RECORD

**3A. Related-party transaction history — the single largest finding of this review**

Recomputing Note 50(c) of the standalone accounts (Revenue from Contracts with Customers, disclosure as per Ind AS 115): recognized revenue from related parties in FY24-25 was **₹23,552.60 Lakhs out of total ₹46,402.75 Lakhs = 50.75% (~50.8%) of standalone revenue** (✅ AR standalone Note 50(c), p.129), up sharply from ₹11,197.51 Lakhs / 30,455.98 Lakhs = 36.8% in FY23-24. This is the RPT-revenue concentration flagged by the prior notes analysis, confirmed here from primary source arithmetic.

Composition of the FY24-25 related-party revenue:
- Sale of services to **Oilmax Energy (parent)**: ₹1,872.01 Lakhs (✅ AR Note 44B.1)
- Sale of services to **Zuberi Asian Joint Venture** (49% AESL): ₹2,618.42 Lakhs
- Sale of services to **AESL FFIL Joint Venture** (49% AESL, partner = Furnace Fabrica (India) Ltd, a genuine third-party engineering contractor per 📰 industry-press coverage of an SCCL award to the JV): ₹3,948.77 Lakhs
- Sale of services to **Asian Indwell Joint Venture** (80% AESL, commenced March 2024): ₹10,812.34 Lakhs — by far the largest single related-party revenue line, in only its second year of operation
- Sale of services to **Asian Oilmax Joint Venture** (70% AESL, commenced August 2024): ₹52.39 Lakhs
(all ✅ AR Note 44B)

The structural pattern — public-sector contracts (SCCL, ECL, CCL, Eastern Coalfields) awarded to majority/near-majority AESL-controlled joint ventures, which then sub-contract execution back to AESL itself (Annexure B / AOC-2 to Board's Report, p.31) — means a large share of "core" revenue growth is intra-group pass-through rather than independently-won new business. This is disclosed and the JV partners appear to be real operating entities, but the **magnitude (half of standalone revenue) and its concentration in newly-formed, majority-AESL-controlled JVs is a material related-party dependency**.

**New payable to parent Oilmax:** Trade payables to Oilmax Energy Private Limited stood at **₹344.14 Lakhs at March 31, 2025, versus Nil a year earlier** (✅ AR Note 44C), alongside Other Financial Liabilities to Oilmax of ₹124.95 Lakhs (up from ₹70.83 Lakhs). Direction of the balance (AESL owing Oilmax) newly reversed in FY24-25 — worth monitoring but not itself adverse without more context on the underlying trade.

**Indrora oilfield — related-party bargain-purchase acquisition from the parent (FY23-24):** AESL acquired a 50% Participatory Interest in the Indrora oilfield (Gujarat) from Oilmax Energy for cash consideration of ₹1,770.00 Lakhs. The acquired asset was independently fair-valued (external valuer) at ₹2,181.00 Lakhs, generating a **₹411.00 Lakhs "bargain purchase" gain recognized directly in equity as capital reserve** (✅ AR standalone Note 55, consolidated Note 54). This RPT was priced *favourably to AESL* (i.e., to the benefit of listed-company minority shareholders, at the parent's implicit expense) rather than the more typical related-party pattern of value extraction from the listed entity — a point in the promoter's favour, though the valuation was performed by an external expert engaged by the same management, and the transaction was subsequently ratified by shareholders via postal ballot in January 2025 alongside the JV-related-party ratifications for Zuberi, AESL FFIL and Asian Indwell (✅ AR Corporate Governance Report §4(b), p.50) — process was compliant with LODR Regulation 23.

**Loan to subsidiary AOSL Petroleum Pte Ltd — recovery discipline concern:** ₹208.50 Lakhs principal, **100% credit-impaired and fully provided for since FY22-23**; interest has not been accrued in the books "considering the financial position of such subsidiary" since FY22-23; ₹84.41 Lakhs of interest is overdue for more than 90 days across 21 instances, and the auditor states explicitly: **"Reasonable steps have not been taken by the Company for recovery of such principal amounts and interest"** (✅ Statutory Auditor's Annexure A to standalone audit report, para (iii)(d), p.68). This is an auditor-flagged, unambiguous governance lapse — a related-party subsidiary loan the promoter-controlled management has not pursued for recovery.

**Director/professional-fee RPTs:** Mr. Mukesh Jain (Non-Executive, Non-Independent Director, practising lawyer specializing in banking/real-estate/insolvency law) was paid **₹72.00 Lakhs (excluding GST) as professional fees** in each of FY23-24 and FY24-25, separate from his director sitting fees (✅ AR Corporate Governance Report §3.B.vii.a, p.46-47; ✅ AR Note 44B.5.iv). Rent of ₹42.00 Lakhs/year each is paid to Kapil Garg and to Ritu Garg personally for company premises (✅ AR Note 44B.5.v). Both are disclosed, both are recurring, neither is illegal, but both represent cash flowing from the listed company to promoter-linked individuals beyond standard board remuneration.

Audit Committee (Borah, Mehta, Bansal — all Independent) and Nomination & Remuneration Committee composition are LODR-compliant.

**5C. Key-person risk and succession**

No named succession plan for the MD role was found in the AR or in web search.
