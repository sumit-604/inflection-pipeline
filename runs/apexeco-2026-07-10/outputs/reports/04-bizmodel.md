## SECTION 1: THE BUSINESS MODEL IN PLAIN ENGLISH

### 1A. One-line description
Apex Ecotech designs, engineers, fabricates, installs and commissions custom water and wastewater treatment plants (WTP/ETP/STP/ZLD/membrane recycling) for industrial and select overseas clients under turnkey (EPC-style) contracts, then earns a smaller ongoing fee for operating and maintaining those plants (AR p.2, p.19; MD&A AR p.32).

**Stream 2 — Operations, maintenance & aftersales ("Supply of Services")**
[Input: technicians, spares, chemicals] → [Apex operates/maintains plants it has already commissioned, plus supplies spares] → [delivers uptime and compliance continuity] → [same industrial client base, under AMC/service arrangements] → [pays a periodic service fee, recognised as the service is rendered (AR Note 1(e), p.49)]

### 1E. The chai-stall-uncle version
Think of Apex Ecotech like a contractor who builds custom water-purification and recycling plants for factories, not one who builds houses. A factory calls them because it is discharging dirty water and the pollution board says that can no longer continue. Apex designs a plant specific to that factory's wastewater, sources the pipes, membranes and machines (much of it bought in or licensed from global partners like Veolia and DuPont), builds it on site, and gets it running. The factory pays in instalments as work is completed, with a slice held back — just like a homeowner holds back the final payment from a contractor until the roof stops leaking. Once the plant is running, Apex can also earn a smaller, steadier fee to keep it maintained, a bit like an AMC on your fridge. The catch: Apex doesn't own big factories or machinery itself, so it isn't asset-heavy — but its cash gets tied up for a long time in unpaid client bills and retention money before it is actually collected, and the parts it buys to build each plant are eating a growing share of what it charges.

Growth stage. ### 3D. Unit economics — the physics of the business
**Define one unit:** A single turnkey treatment-plant project/contract, or alternatively revenue per MLD of treatment capacity delivered. Neither the AR nor the investor presentation discloses per-project or per-MLD revenue/cost, so precise unit economics cannot be derived from the provided documents.

- **Revenue per unit:** NOT FOUND — only aggregate revenue and cumulative capacity figures (145+ MLD water/wastewater, 5.5+ MLD ZLD, Inv. Pres. slide 6) are given, without matching per-MLD revenue attribution.
- **Cost per unit:** NOT FOUND.
- **Volume drivers:** Number of new turnkey contracts won / order-book conversion; expanding client base across 15 industries (Inv. Pres. slide 6); geographic expansion into Bangladesh and Vietnam (Inv. Pres. slide 18).
- **Price drivers:** Project technology/complexity (ZLD > membrane recycling > standard ETP/WTP in specification intensity), client size and negotiating power, competitive bid dynamics (AR MD&A p.33 flags "intense competition").
- **Cost drivers:** Raw material/equipment cost (membranes, MVR, pumps, steel — 68.9-75.6% of revenue and OEM-partner dependent, AR P&L p.47; Inv. Pres. slide 3); site execution costs ("Site Expenses" ₹71.21L FY25, AR Note 19, p.57); employee costs (₹653.70L FY25, ~9.2% of revenue, AR Note 17, p.57).
- **Incremental margin and operating leverage:** In FY26, EBITDA grew 96.82% YoY while revenue grew 109.50% YoY (Inv. Pres. slide 2) — incremental EBITDA margin ran below the average margin, implying limited operating leverage has been demonstrated so far, consistent with the rising material-cost ratio.

### 4C. Quarterly monitoring checklist (10-15 items)

1. Revenue from Operations QoQ/YoY vs order-book conversion pace — good: growth in line with backlog; trouble: revenue outrunning backlog replenishment
2. Order book (₹Cr) — good: growing faster than or in line with revenue; trouble: flat/declining
3. New large client wins/contract announcements — good: steady marquee additions; trouble: none for 2+ quarters
4. Cost of Material Consumed as % of revenue — good: stable/declining; trouble: continued climb past 75-76%
5. EBITDA margin % — good: stable/improving; trouble: compression below ~10-12%
6. Operating cash flow vs PAT — good: OCF tracking PAT; trouble: OCF persistently negative or well below PAT
7. Trade receivables (incl. retentions) growth vs revenue growth — good: receivables growth ≤ revenue growth; trouble: receivables outpacing revenue
8. Debtors/receivables turnover ratio — good: stable/improving from 4.61x; trouble: further decline
9. MSME/statutory dues overdue >6 months (LBT/Service tax/WCT already flagged FY25) — good: cleared; trouble: growing or recurring
10. Working capital as % of revenue — good: stable/declining from ~41.5%; trouble: rising further
11. Debt-to-equity — good: remains near-zero; trouble: rising as debt is used to plug WC gaps
12. Contingent liabilities/litigation updates (Note 25 items) — good: resolved/immaterial; trouble: new or escalating claims
13. KMP/board continuity — good: stable post-CS-transition; trouble: repeated turnover
14. Export revenue trend and new-geography (Bangladesh, Vietnam) order flow — good: building repeat pattern; trouble: one-off/lumpy with no follow-through
15. Foreign exchange fluctuation gain/loss — good: small and stable; trouble: growing volatility as export mix shifts
