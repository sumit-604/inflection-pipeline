# SASKEN Q1 FY27 Earnings Call — Plain-Language Brief + Business-Model / Sector / Competitor Intelligence (04-Aug-2026)
Operator-facing layer on the Role 5 concall analysis. Saved to Notion page body.

## The call in plain words

For the first time in its history, Sasken held a live earnings call and took analyst questions. That itself is the headline: for years the company only issued a written statement, so nobody could press management. Now they can, and management committed to keep doing it. The quarter's numbers were fine, but the tone on cash was the tell. The CFO admitted, unprompted, that cash flow has been negative for two years and that FY27 will also see "a large consumption of cash," because growth is eating working capital and they are stocking memory chips; she said they may use outside borrowing to fund it. When eight analysts pushed on cash, margins, customer concentration and Borqs, management stayed vague, gave almost no specific numbers, and declined to break out revenue by product or name customers. The one strategic target, "60x4x3," quietly slipped from three years to four or five. So the call improved transparency but weakened confidence on the single thing that matters, cash. Nothing broke a red line, so the view is unchanged: still AVOID at Rs2,121, with the real verdict waiting for the September cash statement that management has effectively pre-warned will look poor.

## Business-model transition

Sasken is deliberately turning from a narrow software-services house into a three-layer "chip-to-cognition" company: design the chip (Sasken Silicon), write the embedded software (the legacy services engine), and now build the finished device (Borqs ODM, acquired April 2025). The logic is stickiness: taking a customer from silicon to a shipped product wins bigger, longer contracts than a pure coder can, and management said the full-stack pitch is bringing in new customers. The cost of that transition is exactly what the numbers show: the hardware layer is low-margin and cash-hungry, so blended margin (9.5%) and cash have gone backwards even as the strategy advances. The model is moving up the value chain and getting more strategic, but it is trading its old cash-rich, high-margin simplicity for scale and optionality that are not yet proven to pay off.

## Sector

Sasken sits in India's engineering R&D (ER&D) services industry, one of the strongest structural stories in Indian IT: ~$45bn of work in 2023 heading toward $130-170bn by FY30, with digital-engineering demand compounding ~20% a year. Its lanes are the best ones: semiconductor design (fastest-growing vertical, helped by India's chip push and China-plus-one sourcing), automotive software (software-defined vehicles, the largest vertical), and 5G / Open-RAN telecom. The catch this quarter was sector-wide margin pressure and, for the hardware layer, a memory-chip supply-and-pricing squeeze that inflates revenue but not profit.

Sasken is the small, low-margin, fast-but-partly-inorganic name. It is 3x to 9x smaller than every listed peer and runs roughly half the pack's margin. Its 24% growth is the fastest in the group, but a large part is the Borqs acquisition rather than same-business demand, and the pure-services margin gap is structural: peers are higher-value, larger-scale, and (unlike Sasken until now) all host proper earnings calls. The first call narrows one gap, transparency; it does nothing yet for the scale and margin gaps, which are the ones that cap the valuation.
