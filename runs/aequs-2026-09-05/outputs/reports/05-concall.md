### 2B. Excuse pattern analysis

| Miss | Reason given | Classification |
|---|---|---|
| Hasbro exit | Customer's own "revised manufacturing and sourcing strategy"; "unexpected" | External-blame (thin: no elaboration on why the strategy changed, no discussion of whether pricing, quality, or relationship issues contributed) |
| PAT-timeline slip | Customer capacity-expansion requests requiring more capex/depreciation evaluation | External-blame, framed as good news (customer demand) rather than an admission of delay |

Pattern check: management blames externals for the Hasbro loss and the
PAT-timeline slip, but takes credit internally for the order-book growth,
margin delivery and utilization gains. No instance across the three calls of
management saying "we made a mistake" or naming an internal execution
shortfall. Hard topics (CFO departure, Hasbro exit) were raised proactively
in opening remarks, not only under analyst pressure — a genuine positive.
But specificity requests (customer splits, contract values, yield/rejection
rates, sub-segment margins) are almost uniformly declined, even when the
declining is reasonable on confidentiality grounds.

### 2C. Tone ratings (1-5, evidence)

- **Transparency: 3/5.** Proactively disclosed CFO exit and Hasbro loss.
  But three quarters running refused the consumer sub-segment split (2E)
  and never reconciled the FY26 net-working-capital-day figure (151 vs 127
  across two consecutive calls).
- **Specificity: 2/5.** Contract values (Safran wheels), yield/rejection
  rates, customer names, tier-wise splits, sub-segment splits all declined.
  Three separate on-call number misstatements needed written correction in
  the filed Q3 FY26 transcript alone (utilization 31%/quoted "35%"; aerospace
  utilization 71%/quoted "70%"; consumer 9M segment EBITDA loss corrected
  from a quoted "159 million" to "310 million").
- **Consistency: 2/5.** Net debt/equity 0.1x (9M FY26) to 0.23x (FY26 full
  year) within one quarter, not flagged as a reversal. NWC days for the same
  FY26 year-end reported as 151 one call, then referenced as 127 the next.
- **Accountability: 3/5.** Raised the CFO exit and Hasbro loss unprompted.
  Never used the words "we made a mistake"; framed the PAT-timeline slip as
  a byproduct of customer success rather than a company-side miss.
- **Defensiveness: 2/5 (low defensiveness, i.e. good).** Answered pointed
  questions (holdco-discount/spin-off, margin vs peers, China-precedent risk)
  directly rather than dismissing them, even where the substance of the
  answer was thin.
- **Over-promotion: 3/5.** Heavy framing language ("landmark year,"
  "definite MOAT," "exceptional broadening of our shareholder base") sits
  alongside genuinely delivered numbers; promotional tone is present but not
  extreme, and is not used to paper over the misses that were disclosed.

  — not from management, and no analyst asked either. Absence is data: the
  balance-sheet framing ("well capitalized," cash of Rs 3,015mn at FY26

- **CARO adverse remarks across seven entities and the audit-trail

- **Customer concentration** (four anonymised customers ~58% of revenue per

- **The France entity's near-full impairment** on the parent's books was

## SECTION 3: COMPETITIVE INTELLIGENCE FROM CONCALLS

### 3A. What management says about competitors (credibility check)

- On new Indian aerospace entrants (Q3 FY26, Shashi Kant): "We will be
  happy to supply to anybody who does commercial aerospace... these are all
  approved by the OEM... if they set up a facility here, it just translates."
  MANAGEMENT VIEW. This frames new domestic entrants as potential customers
  of Aequs' existing OEM-approved design base rather than as competitors for
  the same RFPs — a self-serving framing that is plausible but unverified;
  a genuine test for peer verification (does AZAD or UNIMECH describe new
  entrants the same way, or as direct competitive pressure?).
- On margin comparisons to peers (Q3 FY26, Rachna P asked why margins looked
  "less than 20%" for a Tier-1 supplier; Dinesh Iyer): "We will not comment
  on other companies... the scale at which we are operating is significantly
  larger." MANAGEMENT VIEW, declined to engage directly.
- Dynamic Industries confirmed as also a customer for a different Airbus
  door program (Q3 FY26, response to Jai Chauhan) — not framed as a
  competitor, but the exchange implies Dynamic Industries occupies adjacent
  aerostructure territory. FACT (named relationship), competitive
  implication is MANAGEMENT VIEW.
- No comment at any point across the three calls names a specific listed
  Indian aerospace peer (AZAD, UNIMECH, DYNAMATECH) by name.

### 3B. Industry and market intelligence dropped in the calls

- Aerospace TAM ~USD 447bn and consumer TAM ~USD 126bn by 2030 (Q3 FY26,
  Aravind, p.4). Unsourced. MANAGEMENT VIEW, testable.
- India's share of the global aerospace supply chain ~2% today, ~5% of
  global market share, targeted toward ~10% (Q3 FY26, p.4). Unsourced.
  MANAGEMENT VIEW, testable against AZAD/UNIMECH/DYNAMATECH framing of the
  same India-sourcing-shift narrative.
- Raw-material lead times: titanium ~52 weeks; some steels 65-75 weeks
  (Q4 FY26, Nikhil Jain exchange, p.13-14). FACT (company-specific
  experience), testable as an industry norm.
- West Asia crisis logistics disruption: sea freight +5-6 weeks, air freight
  +6-8 days; company raised material cover from ~70% to ~125% of quarterly
  need (Q4 FY26, p.7-8, p.13-14). FACT, testable industry-wide claim.
- China equipment-export restrictions affecting consumer-electronics tooling
  qualification (Q4 FY26, Nikhil Chowdhary exchange, p.11-12): "we have had
  gone through some of these challenges... it won't happen overnight."
  MANAGEMENT VIEW.
- Raw-material sourcing is ~99% imported for aerospace and consumer
  electronics, with "no possibility of changing that anytime soon" due to
  the absence of India-qualified sources (Q1 FY27, Rashmi/Bright Polytech,
  p.16). FACT, and it directly qualifies the "India sourcing shift" /
  "Make in India" narrative: final assembly is local, but input-material
  dependency on imports remains high.
- USD-linked revenue (incl. deemed exports) ~93-94% of total (Q4 FY26,
  Priyankar Biswas exchange, p.7-8). FACT.

1. **Q:** Is India's share of the global aerospace supply chain really ~5%
   today, targeted toward ~10% (Aequs, Q3 FY26 call)? **Why it matters:**
   this unsourced industry claim underlies the entire growth thesis.
   **Check peers:** AZAD, UNIMECH, DYNAMATECH.
2. **Q:** Do peers report similar raw-material lead times (titanium ~52
   weeks, some steels 65-75 weeks) and similar West Asia-driven logistics
   disruption (sea +5-6 weeks, air +6-8 days), as Aequs described (Q4 FY26)?
   **Why it matters:** tests whether Aequs' working-capital build is
   industry-wide or company-specific execution. **Check peers:** AZAD,
   UNIMECH; note DYNAMATECH's single Feb-2024 transcript predates this
   disruption and cannot confirm or deny it.
3. **Q:** Do other Indian aerospace precision manufacturers report segment
   EBITDA margins in the low-to-mid 20s (like Aequs' 20-27% band), or does
   Aequs run above or below the peer norm despite its "vertically
   integrated ecosystem" pitch? **Why it matters:** tests whether the
   ecosystem margin claim analysts kept pushing back on is real.
   **Check peers:** AZAD, UNIMECH, DYNAMATECH.
4. **Q:** Have Airbus/Boeing single-aisle (A320/737) rate ramps, which Aequs
   cites as its main demand driver, been corroborated by other Indian
   suppliers' order-book growth? **Why it matters:** tests whether the
   demand tailwind is industry-wide or an Aequs-specific framing.
   **Check peers:** AZAD, UNIMECH, DYNAMATECH.
5. **Q:** Is customer concentration in the range implied by Aequs' AR
   (~58% of revenue from four anonymised customers) typical of listed
   Indian aerospace component makers, or is Aequs unusually concentrated?
   **Why it matters:** Aequs declined every concentration question across
   all three calls; a peer benchmark tests whether that reticence is
   industry norm or an outlier risk. **Check peers:** AZAD, UNIMECH,
   DYNAMATECH.
6. **Q:** Do peer aerospace component makers run similar working-capital/
   net-working-capital-day norms (~120-150 days), or does Aequs run
   structurally higher working capital than the industry? **Why it
   matters:** ties directly to the AR's flagged DSO increase and negative
   FY26 operating cash flow. **Check peers:** AZAD, UNIMECH, DYNAMATECH.
7. **Q:** Has any peer disclosed a Western consumer-brand sourcing shift
   from China to India comparable to Aequs' Mattel/Hasbro experience, and
   has any peer experienced a comparable customer discontinuation?
   **Why it matters:** tests whether the "India sourcing shift" and
   customer-churn risk are durable industry patterns or Aequs-specific.
   **Check peers:** none of the three named peers serve the consumer/toys
   segment; flag this explicitly as a coverage gap for stage 6 rather than
   force a match.
8. **Q:** Do peer capex cycles show a similar pattern of accelerating
   capex commitments right after major order wins, and how do peers fund
   such capex (debt vs equity vs internal accrual)? **Why it matters:**
   tests whether Aequs' capex-acceleration-plus-possible-raise pattern is
   normal financing behavior or a sign of over-extension. **Check peers:**
   AZAD, UNIMECH.
9. **Q:** Is ~99% import dependency for aerospace/consumer-electronics raw
   materials typical for Indian precision manufacturers, or is Aequs
   unusually import-reliant given its "Make in India" positioning? **Why it
   matters:** could undercut the reshoring narrative used to justify the
   growth story. **Check peers:** AZAD, UNIMECH, DYNAMATECH.
