## SECTION 1: THE BUSINESS MODEL IN PLAIN ENGLISH

### 1A. One-line description
Susan buys aluminium and copper rods, turns some of it into winding wire, conductors and power
cables sold mostly to government electricity boards, and also resells a large slice of the same
raw rods, untouched, as a low-margin trading line (RHP p.130, 136).

### 1B. Money flow chain, one chain per revenue stream

**Stream 1: Manufactured cables and wires (59.71% of FY26 revenue, RHP p.136 / AR Note 18 p.75-76)**
[Aluminium/copper rod, kraft paper, PVC/XLPE compound bought from ~150+ domestic suppliers, RHP
p.145-146] → [drawn into wire, stranded into conductor, insulated and armoured across 3 Ghaziabad
plants, RHP p.141-142] → [delivered as LT/HT cable, MVCC cable, winding wire or conductor] → [state
DISCOMs via tender, or private EPC contractors via purchase order, RHP p.130] → [DISCOMs pay after
inspection + delivery instruction, on rate-contract terms; private buyers pay on negotiated credit,
RHP p.143-144].

**Stream 2: Traded aluminium products (38.38% of FY26 revenue, RHP p.136)**
[Aluminium rod/wire/ingot bought from the same supplier pool] → [company adds no processing, just
holds and re-sells] → [delivered as raw rod/wire/ingot, unchanged] → [cable manufacturers, EPC firms
and other buyers of raw metal] → [pay on commercial terms; RHP flags this as "relatively low-margin
and competitive," entry barriers "relatively low" (RHP p.24)].

**Stream 3: Job work + other operating revenue (1.91% of FY26 revenue, AR Note 18 p.76)**
[Customer sends winding wire/strip for processing] → [Susan processes it for a fee] → [returns
processed material] → [customer pays a service fee] → small, declining share (2.68% FY24 to 0.54%
FY26 of total revenue, RHP p.130).

### 1E. The chai-stall-uncle version
Susan is like a chai-stall uncle who does two things at once. First, he buys milk and tea leaves and
makes chai to sell to regular customers who order by the cup (that's the cable and wire manufacturing,
60% of revenue). Second, he also buys big milk cans and just resells them unopened to other stalls
down the street, making a tiny margin per can (that's the aluminium rod trading, 38% of revenue). His
biggest customers used to be government canteens that pay slowly but reliably; now most of his
business is private shops that pay faster but can walk away any month. He just bought a bigger stove
(new cable capacity) to make a fancier, higher-margin chai (HT/MVCC cable) that only 20 stalls in the
city are licensed to make, but the stove isn't fully running yet.

moat-type test), and neither should be read as confirming the other.

### 2D. Industry lifecycle stage
The Indian wires/cables/conductors industry is in a mature-but-growing phase: a large, mostly
unorganized base (RHP p.123) inside a market projected to grow from USD 10.32bn (2025E) to USD
22.35bn (2035P), 8.02% CAGR (RHP p.130), driven by RDSS (Rs 2.81 lakh crore sanctioned across 46
DISCOMs, RHP p.126) and grid/rural electrification programmes. Susan itself sits early in its own
company-level lifecycle within that mature industry: a small, newly-listed name attempting to climb
from commodity winding-wire/trading into the higher-value HT/MVCC niche, a company-specific
transition riding an industry-level structural tailwind.

### 3D. Unit economics (the physics of the business)
No single, clean "unit" exists because Susan sells tonnes of wire and kilometres of cable of widely
varying specification, and the RHP discloses neither a per-tonne nor per-km cost figure. A directional
approximation, flagged as derived, not disclosed:

- **Unit**: 1 tonne of manufactured winding wire (aluminium + copper blended, Unit-I output)
- **Revenue per unit**: ~Rs 2.59 Lakh/tonne FY26 (derived: Note 18 winding Al wire/strip Rs 4,675.36L +
  winding Cu wire/strip Rs 232.89L = Rs 4,908.25L manufactured revenue, AR Note 18 p.75-76, divided by
  RHP-certified Unit-I actual production of 1,893 tonnes FY26, RHP p.147). Caveat: aluminium and copper
  wire have very different per-kg prices; this blended figure understates the spread between the two.
- **Margin per unit**: NOT FOUND — no segment- or product-level cost is disclosed anywhere in the AR or RHP
- **Volume drivers**: DISCOM tender wins (order book Rs 142.39 Cr, 30-Jun-26 press release), capacity
  utilization (Unit II already at 93.65%, near ceiling; Unit III at only 64.13%, room to grow, RHP p.147)
- **Price drivers**: prevailing aluminium/copper LME-linked rod prices (pass-through, no disclosed hedging
  or escalation clause found in the corpus — NOT FOUND, check concall); DISCOM rate-contract pricing set by
  lowest-bid tender norms (RHP p.123)
- **Cost drivers**: raw material (87.1% of FY26 revenue, AR Note 20 p.77); freight, power (Note 25 p.78)
- **Incremental margin / operating leverage**: the whole transition thesis (LBF1, LBF4) rests on the claim
  that HT/MVCC and LT-cable incremental volume carries higher EBITDA per unit than winding wire (Sep-2026
  deck p.7, p.14: "improving EBITDA per unit across our product portfolio"). No segment EBITDA figure is
  disclosed in any filing read at this stage to confirm the claim quantitatively — the management-cited
  ~6.5% (LT) / ~7% (HT) segment EBITDA referenced in COMPANY MEMORY does not appear in the AR, RHP or the
  three investor decks read here; it remains unresolved and should be chased at the concall (LBF1 still
  open).

### 4B. Valuation method applicability

| Method | Applicable? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| P/E (normalized) | Yes — PRIMARY | Earnings-based business with a real (if recent) profit history; PAT must first be normalized for the FY26 inventory-build credit and the trading-segment swing before use |
| EV/EBITDA (normalized) | Yes — SECONDARY | Captures the rising net-debt load (borrowings +47.4%) that P/E alone would miss; use the RHP KPI-table EBITDA (Rs 32.08 Cr), not the AR highlights-page figure (which is PBT) |
| SOTP (manufacturing core vs trading slice) | Yes — TERTIARY | Trading is 38% of FY26 revenue at low value-add with explicitly "low" entry barriers (RHP p.24); flagged in COMPANY MEMORY (v3.11 Amendment 27.2/27.4) for Stage 11 to formally evaluate a lower-multiple trading slice |
| DCF | NOT APPLICABLE at this stage | Cash conversion has been negative for 3 straight years; the capacity ramp underlying any forecast is unproven (zero CWIP at FY26 close) |
| P/B | NOT APPLICABLE | Net worth base is about to be structurally reset by IPO fresh-issue proceeds (Rs 60.22 Cr); pre/post-IPO book value is not a comparable anchor |
| Dividend yield / DDM | NOT APPLICABLE | No dividend history; growth-stage capital allocation |
| Replacement-cost / asset-based | NOT APPLICABLE | Not an asset-light-but-capacity-constrained NAV story; capacity is being actively added, not scarce and fixed |

Cycle stage that matters for valuation: early-transition, revenue exploding off a small base with a
margin ramp underway (3.51% to 11.91% EBITDA margin, FY24-26, RHP p.135) but cash conversion
unproven. Entry timing should weight one or two more quarters of HT/MVCC delivery data before
leaning on a normalized-earnings multiple; do not exit-price this name off the FY26 print alone.

### 4D. Highest-value questions for management

1. What was the segment-level EBITDA margin for LT cables, HT/MVCC cables and winding wire separately in
   FY26 and Q1 FY27? Reassuring answer: HT/MVCC materially above the LT/winding-wire figure, consistent
   with the stated transition thesis. Worrying answer: management cannot or will not disclose a
   segment split, or the split shows HT/MVCC not yet ahead of the legacy lines.
2. Why did operating cash flow stay negative for a third straight year (per the RHP's own restated
   figures) even as PAT rose sharply? Reassuring: a one-time, quantified working-capital build tied to
   the capacity ramp with a stated unwind date. Worrying: an open-ended answer with no unwind timeline.
3. What is the current status of the capital expenditure for the 7,500-to-12,000 km/yr expansion, given
   zero CWIP and nil capital commitments at 31-Mar-2026? Reassuring: machinery ordered/installed,
   on track for Feb-2027. Worrying: no firm order placed yet, timeline slipping.
4. Who are the counterparties behind the FY26 aluminium rod trading line (26.77% of revenue) and the
   FY25 customer that alone was 35.53% of that year's revenue? Reassuring: named, arm's-length,
   diversified counterparties. Worrying: related-party or single-counterparty concentration.
5. What is the resolution status of the GST search-and-seizure matter (Rs 49.53L goods seized, RHP
   p.264), and why did the AR describe it only as a routine demand notice? Reassuring: resolved with
   no material liability. Worrying: ongoing, with confiscation risk still live.
6. Is there any raw-material price pass-through or escalation clause in DISCOM rate contracts, given no
   hedging is disclosed? Reassuring: a documented pass-through mechanism exists. Worrying: fixed-price
   contracts with full commodity-price exposure borne by Susan.
7. Why has repeat-customer revenue fallen from 89.47% (FY24) to 45.09% (FY26)? Reassuring: deliberate
   diversification into new, durable relationships. Worrying: customer churn/one-off order pattern.
