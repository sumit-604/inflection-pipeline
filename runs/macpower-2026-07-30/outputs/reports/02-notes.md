## PASS 3: PATTERN CHECK

Reviewing Pass 1 and Pass 2 together, cross-note, for contradictions, unreconciled figures, deliberately thin
disclosure, restatements, post-balance-sheet events, and going concern language.

**Pattern 1 — Contradiction on a related-party loan.** Note 12 states Nil loans to related parties/KMPs and
carries an explicit certification that no loans/advances in the nature of loans were granted to Promoters,
Directors, KMPs or their relatives repayable on demand or without repayment terms. Note 35 nonetheless
discloses a year-end "Loan Given" balance of ₹10.10 lakh to CFO Vishal Mehta (Note 12, p.151; Note 35, p.169).
This is not a rounding or presentation quirk; the two notes make opposite factual claims about the same
underlying fact pattern. Pass 2 adds that aggregate employee lending grew 54.3% and "notional interest on
interest-free loan to employee at EIR" grew 5.4x (Note 23, p.159; Note 12, p.151) in the same year, which is
directionally consistent with an outstanding interest-free/near-interest-free advance existing on the books —
reinforcing rather than resolving the contradiction. Confirmed as a genuine cross-note conflict, carried to
Section A/E below.

**Pattern 2 — A documentation-control pattern, not two isolated typos.** Three separate instances of stale
boilerplate/unreconciled template text appear in the notes, all previously identified individually across Pass 1
and Pass 2 but not connected as one pattern until now:
  (i) Note 34 (Gratuity, p.163): the "Parameter risk" narrative illustrates salary inflation of 5%, referencing
  actual practice of 10% increments, while the note's own disclosed assumption is 12.00% — none of the three
  numbers in the narrative match the number used in the calculation.
  (ii) Note 36 (p.170–171): the FY24 "Net Exposure to foreign currency risk" row for Euro/USD does not match the
  "Trade Payable" line directly above it in the same table (the ±10% sensitivity figures reconcile to the
  trade-payable line, not the printed net-exposure line) — an internal transposition.
  (iii) Note 36 (p.170): the qualitative Market Risk narrative claims exposure via "foreign currency trade
  payables and receivables," while the quantitative table shows Nil foreign-currency Financial Assets
  (receivables) in both years — text and table disagree.
  Three independent instances of unreconciled boilerplate within a 20-page note run is a pattern, not
  coincidence, and points to weak review/tie-out discipline in preparing the notes rather than to any single
  immaterial typo. This does not evidence earnings management, but it lowers confidence in the numbers that
  are NOT independently cross-checkable elsewhere in the document.

**Pattern 3 — Reported margin improvement is substantially a mechanical inventory effect, not demonstrated
cost control.** This is the most consequential pattern-pass finding. Read individually: net profit margin
improved to 12.00% from 11.71% (Note 38vi, p.185); Cost of Material Consumed fell 15.2% (Note 24, p.159). Read
together with Pass 2's cross-note reconciliation: Cost of Material Consumed is computed as opening stock +
purchases − closing stock, and the closing raw-material stock rose 50.9% (₹10,096.04 lakh, Note 7, p.148) while
net purchases were roughly flat (-0.5%). Combining Cost of Material Consumed with the Changes-in-Inventories
line (Note 25) shows the true underlying materials cost is flat YoY (+0.13%) against revenue growth of +8.6% —
a modest, credible improvement. But over the same year, Employee Benefit Expense rose 29.7% and Other
Expenses rose 38.1% (Miscellaneous Expense alone +97.0%, unexplained), both far outpacing revenue. The net
effect is that a large, unexplained surge in operating costs below the gross-margin line has been more than
offset, at the reported net-margin level, by a closing-inventory build that mechanically depresses reported
Cost of Material Consumed. If the raw-material build normalises (i.e., is consumed without a matching sales
uplift, or is written down), the cost inflation in employee and other expenses would show through directly into
margin compression with no further inventory cushion to absorb it. This reframes the "margin improved" headline
finding from Pass 1 into a genuine red flag on margin durability, elevated to a top finding in Section A below.

**Pattern 4 — Related-party receivable growth is a rounding error against the total receivables surge.**
Modern Machine Tools trade receivable grew 264% (₹10.90 lakh → ₹39.70 lakh, Note 35, p.169) inside a total
receivables base that grew 57.5% (₹2,179.51 lakh → ₹3,432.40 lakh, Note 9). The related-party growth rate is
higher in percentage terms but contributes under 2.5% of the total receivables increase in absolute rupees.
Confirms the total receivables deterioration (Section A #1) is a third-party customer phenomenon, not a
related-party-driven one — related-party dealings are directionally consistent with, but not a material cause
of, the working-capital strain.

**Contingent liability resolution, unexplained.** The FY24 disputed statutory-dues contingent claim of ₹28.35
lakh (Note 32) disappears to Nil in FY25 with no explanation of settlement, payment, or withdrawal disclosed
anywhere in the notes. Immaterial in quantum, noted for completeness; not elevated to a top-15 finding.

**Restatements / reclassifications:** No prior-year figures found restated or reclassified anywhere across
either pass; note numbering sequence itself is irregular in print layout (Note 3, PPE schedule, physically
placed after Note 21) but this is a print/layout artifact, not a restatement, and does not affect any reported
number.

**Events after the balance sheet date:** Only the Board's recommended final dividend of ₹1.50/share (same rate
as FY24), approved for the AGM, at Note 45 (p.184); financial statements approved by the Board on 29-May-2025.
No other post-balance-sheet event disclosed. The fire (2-Feb-2025) occurred before the 31-Mar-2025 year end,
not after, and is correctly treated as an in-year exceptional item, not a subsequent event.

**Going concern:** Explicitly and only affirmatively stated in Note 1 (Basis of Preparation, p.135) — "the
Company has prepared the financial statements on the basis that it will continue to operate as a going
concern." No qualifying, adverse, or hedged language found anywhere in the notes across all three passes.

No further material new findings emerged from the pattern-specific re-check beyond the four patterns above and
the items already carried from Pass 1/Pass 2.

### D. FIVE QUESTIONS FOR MANAGEMENT

1. Note 12 certifies Nil loans to related parties/KMPs, yet Note 35 discloses a ₹10.10 lakh "Loan Given" to CFO
   Vishal Mehta at year-end. Which disclosure is correct, and what are the terms (rate, tenure, security) of this
   balance?
2. Receivable days rose from ~26 to ~39 while payable days rose from ~70 to ~98 and MSME dues grew 81.4% in

3. Employee Benefit Expense grew 29.7% and Other Expenses grew 38.1% (Miscellaneous Expense alone +97.0%,

   the current net-margin improvement sustainable once the raw-material inventory build (which is mechanically
   depressing Cost of Material Consumed) normalises?
4. What is the current status and expected settlement timeline of the ₹362.39 lakh fire insurance claim
   (Unit-2, 2-Feb-2025), and how much of the 51.5% YoY capex increase relates to rebuilding that capacity versus
   unrelated expansion?
5. What was the rationale and timing for WTD & CEO Nikesh J. Mehta's individual shareholding reduction from

   and new entrant Vincy Rupeshbhai Mehta) rather than his own recorded family holding?

### E. NOTES-BASED RED FLAGS

- **Working-capital deterioration masking as margin improvement**: net margin optics (12.00% vs 11.71%) are
  substantially explained by a mechanical inventory-accounting effect (raw material build depressing Cost of
  Material Consumed) rather than genuine cost discipline, while underlying operating costs (employee, other
  expenses) grew 3-4x faster than revenue. This is the single most important finding of the three passes for
  earnings-quality assessment.
- **Cash-conversion cycle stress**: receivables +57.5%, payables/MSME +28-81%, inventory days +23%, investment
  book liquidated to zero, net cash cushion roughly halved — a coherent, multi-note pattern of working-capital
  strain, not isolated line items.
- **Related-party loan disclosure contradiction** (Note 12 vs Note 35) on a CFO loan — an undisclosed/
  inconsistently disclosed related-party credit exposure.
- **Unresolved fire-related insurance receivable** (₹362.39 lakh) with no interim update on settlement status.
- No evidence found of goodwill manipulation, aggressive revenue recognition, off-balance-sheet structures, or
  deliberate related-party value transfer at material quantum; the RPT loan issue is a disclosure-consistency
  flag, not evidence of a material undisclosed benefit.

### F. ONE-LINE NOTES VERDICT

The notes reveal moderate accounting practices with one concerning undercurrent. Key concern: reported margin
improvement is substantially a mechanical inventory-accounting effect masking employee and other expense growth
running 3-4x faster than revenue, compounding an already visible cash-conversion deterioration across
receivables, payables, and inventory. Key strength: disclosure is granular enough (tax reconciliation, ageing
schedules, ratio tables, actuarial assumptions) that an attentive reader can see the problem in the company's
own numbers, and no restatements, goodwill games, or going-concern doubt were found anywhere in three
independent passes. Overall accounting quality: 6/10.
