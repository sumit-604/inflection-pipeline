## 17.0 Classification gate

Every Section 1B derivation states upfront: **CONVERTER or NON-CONVERTER**, with one sentence of evidence.

CONVERTER requires all three:

- (a) the primary raw material is a traded or quoted commodity (resin, crude derivatives, rubber, steel, agri produce);
- (b) pricing is cost-plus or spread-based with limited formulation IP;
- (c) gross margin has historically co-moved with the input price.

Genuine formulation IP with demonstrated pricing power = NON-CONVERTER even on commodity inputs. The test is whether the spread is defended by IP or by nothing. Ambiguous cases classify CONVERTER (conservative default) with the ambiguity stated. This default stands as written (operator ruling 2026-09-15, OR-6).

## 17.1 Pillar 1 symmetric smoothing (converters only)

- The ROCE feeding Pillar 1 is through-cycle: the 5-7 year average, or the midpoint of current and cycle-trough ROCE where listed history is shorter.
- Spot-year ROCE is never the Pillar 1 input for a converter, in either direction: temporarily inflated ROCE is smoothed exactly as temporarily depressed ROCE is.
- Show both numbers: spot ROCE and the through-cycle ROCE used.
- The through-cycle input is computed after the declared Route A or Route B is applied, never as a second credit. It does not touch the formula or the 30x ceiling (chunk 01).

## 17.2 Pillar 2 third fork: input-price-driven working capital (converters only)

- Before assigning STRUCTURAL or GROWTH-INDUCED, run the volume test: recompute WC days on volumes or constant prices.
- If WC per unit is stable while WC in rupees swings, the swing is INPUT-PRICE-DRIVEN: 1.00x neutral multiplier, no growth offset, no structural penalty.
- All converter cash-quality trend claims, including the FTTCP Cash transition verdict feed, are made on volume-denominated WC only. A rupee-denominated "improvement" caused by a falling input is not a cash transition.
- Rating-agency language separating price-led from cycle-led WC is admissible evidence.
- The STRUCTURAL and GROWTH-INDUCED forks and their multipliers are unchanged; this third fork is open to converters only, via the volume test.

## 17.3 Projection discipline (converters only)

- The bear case contains at least one input-price mean-reversion year, with spreads compressing to the 5-year median spread, even where guidance says otherwise.
- Guidance delivered at a cycle top is the cycle delivering, not management. The promise-vs-delivery tracker notes cycle position beside each delivered guidance item.

## 17.4 Entry conjunction (Role 2 verdict card, converters only)

- A converter BUY verdict requires the input-cycle position stated: the named input, and its spot price against its 5-year range.
- Input in the TOP QUINTILE of its 5-year range → ceiling verdict WATCHLIST regardless of margin of safety on current earnings, with the cycle position as the named resolving condition and a review trigger when the input exits the top quintile.
- This is an entry gate only. Destination PE, fair values and the Hurdle Ratio are computed exactly as for any name.

## 17.5 What Amendment 17 does not do

- It does not lower or raise any sector cap; caps already price the sector through the cycle.
- It applies none of 17.1-17.4 to NON-CONVERTER names.
- It alters no FTTCP verdict machinery beyond the volume-denominating rule in 17.2.
- It does not interact with the Category-Break Override. A converter claiming the override would be extraordinary; if claimed, both rule sets apply and the four override conditions are tested on through-cycle numbers.

## The converter multiple in later layers

- Option slices (Amendment 18.3): a converter slice resolving successfully exits on its OWN converter PE. No slice inherits the core quality multiple by resolving.
- Step 1C (Amendment 20.3): a converter subject is placed against peers on through-cycle earnings, never spot. Step 1C does not lift a converter slice's multiple.
