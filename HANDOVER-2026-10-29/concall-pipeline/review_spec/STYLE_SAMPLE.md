## Transrail: the ₹574 Cr headline hides the quarter

The press release says order intake for the year is now ₹1,609 Cr. Notion logs Q1 intake at ₹1,034 Cr. So Q2 intake was about ₹575 Cr, and the filing is dated the last day of the quarter. This is effectively the H1 number.

Q1 revenue was ₹1,702 Cr. If Q2 revenue is anywhere near that, the unexecuted book falls from ₹15,635 Cr to roughly ₹14,400 Cr. That is about 8% down quarter on quarter, the second straight contraction. That is my arithmetic, not a filed figure.

Your re-underwrite gate for Q2 has three legs: H1 cash conversion of at least 0.8x, intake above ₹1,500 Cr, and the book stops shrinking.

- Book leg: fails now, whatever the cash figure shows.
- Intake leg: depends on how you read it. Q1's ₹1,034 Cr was logged red against ₹1,500, which suggests a per-quarter test. On that reading Q2 at ₹575 Cr is a deeper red. On an H1-cumulative reading, ₹1,609 Cr scrapes through.

Please rule on which reading the gate meant. Either way, the gate cannot pass as written. Q1 was logged as "strike one" and needed two weak quarters to re-weight; Q2 looks like strike two.

What it does to fair value: the ₹800–860 FY29 value rests on 20%+ revenue growth through FY28–29. H1 intake is running at about ₹3,200 Cr a year against a revenue run-rate above ₹7,000 Cr. If H2 does not lift intake hard, the book ends FY27 near ₹11,000 Cr. That cannot fund the growth in the FY28–29 years.

The bull reading is fair and has evidence behind it:
- Transmission tenders bunch into H2.
- The ₹694 Cr L1 position is not yet counted.
- HTLS reconductoring is a better product than plain towers.
- A large private developer is a new kind of customer, against the 46.8% concentration in two government clients.

The single observation that separates the two readings is Q3 intake plus L1 conversion. Below about ₹2,000 Cr would confirm the bear case.

The tower capacity filing on the same day makes this worse. Capacity went from 172,400 to 184,400 MTPA, with plans to exceed 200,000, while intake is falling. Q1 depreciation was already up 33% on 4% revenue growth.

Also, the order press release still says "doubled to 172,400", which was superseded by the capacity filing the same afternoon. That is minor sloppiness, not a flag.

The stock rose 5.5% to ₹476 on 30-Sep. It is still above the ₹415 add line, so price does not invite action either.

No thesis-broken trigger fires. On your rules, I read this as moving the name toward the weakened end of the range rather than toward an add. That is your call.

## Asian Energy: sanctioned, not effective, and a new dispute

The NCLT order, a filed document, settles three open items and opens one.

Settled:
- Merger timing has slipped. The sanction is subject to Petroleum Ministry approval, which had not been received as of the order date. The appointed date is the first day of the month in which the merger becomes effective. Your working projections assumed 1-Oct-26, which is now unlikely. FY27 consolidation shrinks with every month of delay.
- Pooling-of-interests accounting is confirmed. The NCLT order states the auditors certified Appendix C (common control) treatment. That removes weakest assumption #5 in your 2-Sep projections: there is no fair-value step-up and no goodwill amortisation dragging EPS back. Reported EPS will look cleaner than the bear case feared.
- Swap ratio confirmed at 117 AESL shares for every 10 Oilmax shares.

Opened: Assam Company (ACIL), the 50% non-operator partner in Amguri, filed objections. They are not in the Notion record.
- ACIL alleges that AESL, as Oilmax's affiliate O&M contractor, charged excessive amounts on Amguri. Because ACIL pays half, this is a related-party pricing allegation from an arm's-length party.
- ACIL argues that once the merger nets those transactions to intra-company, the excess becomes harder to trace.
- ACIL argues the merger is a transfer of interest that needs its consent under the operating and farm-out agreements.

The tribunal did not rule on the consent question. It ordered all ACIL claims to survive against AESL, and it ordered AESL to preserve the Oilmax–AESL books for eight years.

Two consequences follow:
- Line 4 (Amguri realisation), which you promoted to dominant variable one, now runs under an operating agreement where the partner is litigating against the operator.
- The related-party revenue line (₹240 Cr, receivables ~155 days) now carries a live overcharging allegation.

This is an allegation, not a finding. If it is substantiated, it is the integrity kind of concern that the Entrepreneur Ledger cannot offset. It does not change AVOID. It should be a named tripwire.

At about ₹518 against a base fair value near ₹319, AVOID is unchanged.
