## SECTION 2: CAPACITY & CAPEX PIPELINE (adapted for a balance-sheet lender)

**2A. Capex programme table**: NOT APPLICABLE in the literal sense — Aye is
a balance-sheet NBFC-ML with no plant, equipment or manufacturing capacity to
build. The only capital-deployment disclosure is the IPO Fresh Issue: Net
Proceeds ₹672.24cr (Gross ₹710.0cr less ₹37.76cr offer expenses), earmarked
generically "towards augmenting our capital base to meet our Company's
future capital requirements arising out of growth of our business and
assets," to be deployed within FY27, not itemised by project (Prospectus
p.125). None of the Objects have been appraised by a bank or agency
(Prospectus p.125) — stated explicitly, not a gap I am filling.

**2B. Utilisation trajectory (branch-maturation analog)**: branches ramp
over roughly four years rather than a single commissioning event. Average
AUM by branch vintage: up to 2 years ₹4.8cr, 2–4 years ₹8.8cr, 4+ years
₹14.5cr (Q3 FY26 call p.6) — i.e., a branch's productivity roughly triples
from inception to maturity. Growth attribution (FY26 to date, per the
concall): 60% from deepening AUM per existing branch, 39% from repeat loans,
only 1% from new branches added in the trailing year (Q3 FY26 call p.6).
This is the strongest evidence in the entire document set that growth is
now branch-productivity-led, not branch-count-led — cross-referenced under
A3 below.

**2C. Growth embedded in capex — substituted analog, arithmetic shown**:
the framework's "capex × fixed-asset turnover" calculation does not apply to
a balance-sheet lender (no fixed-asset base drives loan origination). The
lender-analog substitution used here (same convention as B01's LCR-for-
current-ratio substitution, named explicitly as non-framework-specified) is
capital-adequacy headroom to the rating agency's own gearing ceiling:

- Closing net-worth gearing (D/E) = 2.13x as of 30-Jun-2026 (Inv. Pres.
  slide 36, "Closing D/E is 2.13x").
- Net worth, 30-Jun-2026 = ₹2,603cr (Inv. Pres. slide 19/35).
- Borrowings, 30-Jun-2026 = ₹5,557cr (Inv. Pres. slide 35, balance sheet).
- ICRA's own negative rating sensitivity: "a continued increase in the
  managed gearing above 4.5 times... could exert pressure on the rating"
  (ICRA rating rationale, 12-Nov-2025, p.2, "Rating sensitivities — Negative
  factors").
- Headroom to that 4.5x ceiling = (4.5 × ₹2,603cr) − ₹5,557cr = ₹11,713.5cr
  − ₹5,557cr = **₹6,156.5cr** of additional borrowing capacity before the
  gearing level the rating agency itself has flagged as a downgrade trigger.
- Current AUM, 30-Jun-2026 = ₹7,324cr (Inv. Pres. slide 6).
- Implied incremental AUM capacity ≈ ₹6,156.5cr / ₹7,324cr ≈ **84%** above
  current AUM, if deployed roughly 1:1 into the loan book (ignoring cash/
  other-asset drag) — i.e., the existing capital base alone, without any
  further equity raise, could in principle fund another ~84% of AUM growth
  before the gearing-based rating-sensitivity ceiling binds.

This is a 🔍 analyst computation built entirely on 📄 anchored inputs
(D/E, net worth, borrowings from the filed Investor Presentation; the 4.5x
ceiling from the filed ICRA rating letter). It is a capital-headroom ceiling,
not a management-disclosed capex plan, and it says nothing about whether
management will actually choose to lever up that far, or whether asset
quality would hold if it did — flagged as a mechanical calculation, not a
committed growth plan.

**2D. New geography or market entries**: NO EVIDENCE FOUND. The Prospectus
states the opposite intent — "We intend to reduce the pace of opening new
branches as we have adequate geographic diversification" (Prospectus p.237)
— already PAN-India across 21 states/UTs (Prospectus p.237; Inv. Pres.
slide 9). This is deepening, not expansion.

## SECTION 4: REGULATORY & POLICY TAILWINDS (R1)

**4A. Regulatory approvals in pipeline**: NO EVIDENCE FOUND. Aye already
holds its NBFC-ML (Middle Layer) registration under Section 45-IA of the RBI
Act; no pending application for a higher regulatory layer (NBFC-UL) or new
licence category is disclosed in the Prospectus, the concall, or the
Investor Presentation.

**4B. Government policy tailwinds**: the RBI mandates domestic commercial
banks to maintain 40% of adjusted net bank credit as priority-sector-lending
(PSL) exposure (Prospectus p.60, risk factor 45) — MSME lending broadly
qualifies, which is the structural reason Aye can access bank funding (32%
of the borrowing mix, Inv. Pres. slide 18) and DFI/multilateral funding
(19.1% of the mix, same slide) at improving terms. This is a real, 📄
documented, structural enabler of Aye's funding profile. However: **no
company-specific percentage of Aye's own book that qualifies as PSL was
found in any of the four anchored source classes** (NOT FOUND, stated
explicitly rather than estimated) — the closest-looking figure, 55.74% of
interest income / 57.00% of AUM (Prospectus p.53), is in fact the five-state
*geographic concentration* risk factor, unrelated to PSL (see the correction
note under C2). Separately, the Prospectus itself flags a government scheme
as a *competitive risk*, not a tailwind: "The implementation of various
government schemes and initiatives, such as the Pradhan Mantri MUDRA Yojana
(PMMY), may lead to increased competition in our target segments" (Prospectus
p.60, risk factor 46) — cited here for balance, not folded into R1's score.
Duration: PSL classification is an ongoing RBI master-direction feature, not
a time-bound subsidy; enrolment is automatic classification, not an
application Aye files; **competitors share the benefit fully** — every
MSME-focused NBFC lender (MASFIN, SBFC, Northern Arc, etc.) qualifies
similarly under the same RBI norms, per the framework's explicit instruction
to weigh whether competitors share it.

**4C. Regulatory moat assessment**: ACTIVE (already realised in the funding
mix and CoB trajectory), not emerging — but not a moat in the differentiating
sense either, since it is structurally shared with the entire peer set and
is itself disclosed as a dependency that could reverse if RBI PSL norms
change (Prospectus p.60). Scored Weak rather than Moderate on that basis:
raw likelihood×impact = HL (High likelihood it remains active; Low impact on
*relative* competitive position because every peer has it) = 2, evidence
type 📄, adjusted = 2.

### 6D. Combined classification

Backward = AVOID (flagged; core-implied AVERAGE before the listing-recency
downgrade). Forward = MODEST (19.6/80 — real signal, but well short of the
STRENGTHENING (25–39) or EXPANSION (≥40) bar that HIGH POTENTIAL or
TURNAROUND would require under the standard matrix).

**This is explicitly NOT a HIGH POTENTIAL or TURNAROUND read.** Both of
those labels require an EXPANSION- or STRENGTHENING-grade forward score
layered on a GOOD/AVERAGE (HIGH POTENTIAL) or weak (TURNAROUND) backward
score — the transition setups this operation is built to hunt. Aye's
forward score does not clear that bar: four of twenty-one categories carry
genuine Strong/Moderate evidence, and even the two Strong categories (D1,
G1) have not yet been tested through a full credit cycle or a post-IPO
capital-normalisation cycle respectively (Section 6B). Calling this AVOID
outright would also double-penalise the same mechanical listing-recency
issue B01 has already flagged once (LIMITED-history downgrade, not
demonstrated deterioration) — before that downgrade, Gate 0's own
core-implied read was AVERAGE.

**combined_assessment = AVERAGE.** A financial-services company with one
confirmed backward moat (pricing power via improving net margin), a
genuinely differentiated but historically-unproven underwriting/data asset,
and a funding-cost advantage that is real today but partly an artefact of
a recent capital infusion. Worth tracking for the specific triggers in the
optionality register (above all, the Gen-AI pilot and a second full credit
cycle showing sustained relative asset-quality performance), not yet a
transition setup to act on.
