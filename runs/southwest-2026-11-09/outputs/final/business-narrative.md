# Business understanding narrative

South West Pinnacle drills holes and reads rock for clients who must know what lies underground. Coal bed methane (CBM) production drilling gave 37% of FY26 revenue, and its crews dewater coal seams so methane flows. Aquifer mapping gave 25%, and it shows government agencies where groundwater sits. Non coal mineral drilling gave 17%, and only core samples move a resource from inferred to proven. Seismic work gave 16%, and its sound wave surveys map the layers below before anyone drills. Three customers gave 72% of FY26 revenue, up from 48%, and the company does not name them. Named repeat clients include Reliance, Hindustan Zinc, Vedanta, Hindalco, Oil India, CMPDI and the Central Ground Water Board. Public work goes to the lowest qualified tender, private work weighs execution, and switching costs stay low. Demand today rests on the Reliance Industries CBM production drilling programme, Hindustan Zinc exploration drilling and CMPDI outsourced drilling tenders. Oil India and ONGC onshore seismic work adds project flow behind them. Forward demand turns on the Ministry of Coal exploration scheme FY27-31, which one unverified report doubles to ₹1,185 Cr a year. NMET approvals and mineral block auctions should widen non coal drilling, while copper, zinc and coal index prices set private budgets. CGWB NAQUIM 2.0 tenders must replace aquifer work now running off, and the run did not establish their budget. Advantage is narrow, with two Moderate categories and none Strong in the 22 category scan. Coal and geology work holds term bound Ministry of Coal and NABET accreditations, and others can apply. Seismic holds a three year Oil India empanelment and no other advantage. The CBM and mineral lines hold repeat contracts with Reliance and Hindustan Zinc, without pricing power. Aquifer mapping has no moat, and the two structural asymmetry categories, talent and cannibalization barrier, scored zero.

# Drilling grows while cash and disclosure lag

South West Pinnacle is a drilling and geoscience contractor. Vikas and Piyush Jain founded it in 2006, and clients pay per metre or per project. The fleet is stated at 43, 45 or 47 rigs, depending on the document. FY26 revenue was ₹243 Cr, profit after tax ₹33 Cr and EBITDA margin 24%. Sub contractors took a third of standalone revenue. The company also owns a coal block that has not produced. It holds 35% of an Oman mining services joint venture and 17.5% of an Oman exploration block.

The business is moving from small projects and coal trading toward large multi year contracts. Coal trading of ₹45 Cr ended after FY25, so core drilling revenue rose 80%, and part of that rise is mechanical. A ₹307 Cr Hindustan Zinc subsidiary order and a ₹166 Cr Reliance extension drove the order book. The book went from ₹412 Cr in September 2025 to ₹581 Cr in March 2026 and ₹761 Cr in July. Two contracts are 62% to 70% of it, depending on the CBM value used. The next bet is larger. The Jharkhand coal block holds 84 million tonnes and pays the government 24.25% of revenue. First coal is now planned for fiscal 2029, one year later than first stated. Phase 1 needs ₹200 Cr to ₹225 Cr, and management deflected the funding split on three calls. The mining plan was still pending in November. On management's own two year lead, the plan needs approval by about March 2027.

Gate 0 scored 72 of 160, made up of 62 of 100 on core and 10 of 60 on moats, with no deal breaker and a GOOD label. That label is conditional. One verifier scores promoter holding at zero until the September 2026 shareholding pattern arrives, which takes core to 57 and the label to AVERAGE. Exchange filings put promoters at 61.7% on 10 September, above the 40% line, but the pattern itself is missing. Return on capital rose from 12% in FY24 to 23% in FY26. Net debt is 1.2 times EBITDA, and CRISIL raised the bank rating to BBB+ in July 2026. The forward moat scan scored 12.8, MODEST, only 0.8 above the line, and one verifier convention gives 11.3 and NONE. Q2 FY27 revenue fell 7.5% to ₹58 Cr and EBITDA margin dropped to 14.4% from 24% in FY26. Management calls that a monsoon and maintenance dip. The other reading is that FY26 was a utilisation peak. Q3 FY27 margin and a disclosed rig utilisation number separate the two.

Cash is the weak line. FY26 operating cash flow was ₹12 Cr against profit of ₹33 Cr, or 0.38 times, down from 1.65 times in FY25. Free cash flow was minus ₹35 Cr after ₹48 Cr of capex. Receivables sat at about 175 days, and about ₹6 Cr of the operating cash was tax and statutory dues paid late. CRISIL says receivables stayed above 150 days for three years on retention money and unbilled work, and calls this inherent to the industry. The run reads the drag as structural. Three facts push the other way. Ten year cumulative operating cash flow matched cumulative profit at 1.03 times. About 71% of FY26 standalone receivables were not yet due. H1 FY27 operating cash flow was ₹16 Cr against profit of ₹13 Cr, though receivables fell because billing fell, and free cash flow stayed negative. Accounting quality scored 6 of 10. The 31 March 2027 balance sheet is the next test of the structural reading.

Management earns a B on the record, and a verifier pulls that to B minus with a C bias until Q3 FY27. The concall review scored 15 promises: 8 delivered, 4 partial and 3 missed. The stricter verifier count is 5, 4 and 3 of 12. FY26 revenue rose 35% against a 15 to 20% guide, the Reliance extension arrived and the coal geological report was approved. The misses sit on growth across all quarters, rising EBITDA and coal timing. Management did not reaffirm the 20% FY27 growth figure when asked twice in July. Analysts asked for order book execution phasing on all three calls, and management refused each time. Sustainable margin, bid win rate and funding split also went unanswered. Employee cost rose to 22.8% of Q2 revenue from 13.7%. Diesel sits inside materials, which rose 5.4 points on the quarter, so the company's explanation is only partly tested.

Promoters sold 26.6 lakh shares, about 8% of equity, in four market tranches between June and September 2026. Holding fell to 61.7% from 68.85%, and proceeds were about ₹63 Cr on media tier prices. The promoters also paid ₹30 Cr in cash for their warrants. On 22 July management said it could not answer on promoter dilution, and 13.9 lakh more shares sold within eight weeks. A ₹11 Cr loan went to an unnamed enterprise influenced by key managers, after a ₹9.5 Cr loan of the same shape the year before. The subsidiary South West Resources carries a going concern note, backed by a parent support letter with no stated amount. The promoter check closed at CAUTION with no deal breaker and no regulator action found. It closed partial, because SEBI, court and MCA lookups could not be queried from here.

Peers complicate the story and do not break it. None of the three is a coal, mineral or CBM exploration driller, so every read is partial. The order book at 2.95 times trailing revenue sits inside the peer range. Deep Industries shows onshore rigs at full use, which supports the shortage claim. Deep grows on volume at fixed tender prices, and Jindal Drilling took a day rate cut to $47,800 from a $62,000 bid. Jindal also reports global rates rising, so the rate contradiction holds for India only. Asian Energy lost most of a quarter to monsoon in civil work on coal handling plants, against the monsoon proof claim. South West Pinnacle grew 128% in that same quarter, so the work type matters. Only Deep has reported Q2 FY27, and it named no monsoon or diesel cost.

The market is smaller than management says. Management claims ₹15,000 Cr to ₹18,000 Cr, undated and unsourced, which is ten to twelve times the ₹1,498 Cr conservative estimate and three times the realistic ₹4,947 Cr. The serviceable market is ₹987 Cr, an upper bound, and the company holds 6.6% of its coal and mineral drilling pool. That leaves 4.1 times revenue headroom and a GOOD runway class. At the FY23 to FY26 revenue pace of 25.1%, the company saturates that market in about 10.9 years. The central path gives ₹363 Cr of revenue in FY29, or 14.3% a year. Management's 20% needs a 7.4 point share gain, or the coal scheme step up that gives 22.8%. The market stage closed partial, because no independent market report turned up in search.
