## PART 1: CLAIM-BY-CLAIM

### Q1. Order book, backlog, and government/defence share
- Claim: Kusumgar refuses an order book and a segment split; the peers show what a listed peer discloses.
- Verdict: PARTIALLY VERIFIED (segment split: peers disclose; order book: peers also do not).
- Peer evidence:
  - Mayur reads out channel revenue every quarter, e.g. "Export general 30.24, export OEM 73.56 ... auto OEM domestic 56.08" (MAYURUNIQ Aug, page 3), and in Nov export 90.46 and domestic 162.53 (MAYURUNIQ Nov, page 4). This contradicts the single-segment retreat as an industry norm.
  - Mayur gives no order book: "we don't get fixed orders from customers it's all projections" (MAYURUNIQ May, page 10). Asked for a US OEM order book, the answer was a growth view with no figure (MAYURUNIQ Aug, page 11). Asked in Nov, the question went unanswered (MAYURUNIQ Nov, page 10).
  - Arvind: asked about order book, answered "no obvious seasonality ... customer contracts ... about 50% share ... contractual life about 5, 6 years" (ARVIND May, page 15). On AMD it says "parts of this project business ... we had almost no defense orders for the first 2 quarters. A chunk of defense orders came in, in quarter 2" (ARVIND Jan, page 5). Arvind gives AMD revenue 496 crore and EBITDA 77 crore quarterly (ARVIND Jan, page 3), not an order book.
  - Government or defence share of revenue: no peer states one.
- Peers silent: neither gives a defence share. Arvind never states an AMD defence percentage.
- Net read: refusing an order book is not unusual (neither peer gives one). Withdrawing the segment split after listing is unusual; Mayur gives it every call. The defence-share part of the question is UNVERIFIABLE.

### Q2. Qualification cycle and programme stickiness
- Claim: "90% of what we do is linked to long-standing product approvals".
- Verdict: PARTIALLY VERIFIED (direction corroborated by two peers, but in automotive, not defence or protective fabric).
- Peer evidence:
  - Arvind (Dalco): "almost 85% or maybe slightly higher ... is sole sourced by the customer" (ARVIND May, page 7); "88% of the cases, it's only a product for that model" (ARVIND May, page 17); "life of a model is 6 to 7 years ... you don't normally exit the platform" (ARVIND May, page 15); contract life "about 5, 6 years" (ARVIND May, page 15).
  - Mayur: new OEM business takes "2, 3 years" from sampling to a purchase order: "when discussion starts and getting the orders, it takes 2, 3 years. It's a big test and sampling" (MAYURUNIQ Nov, page 9). Existing customers add models: "earlier, we were supplying materials for 2 models. Now they have given us orders for 5 models" (MAYURUNIQ Nov, page 11).
- Peers silent: neither describes a defence or protective-fabric qualification cycle or army inspection and acceptance.
- Net read: sticky, multi-year approvals are how auto-OEM suppliers describe themselves too. That supports the mechanism but not the defence case. Neither peer shows the approvals also bring timing lumpiness, though Arvind's defence remark does (Q1).

### Q3. Receivable days, cash conversion, government payment delay
- Claim: Kusumgar debtor days 26 to 123, H1 FY27 CFO 16.8% of EBITDA, ECL on government receivables.

### Q4. EBITDA margin band and swings
- Claim: Kusumgar margin 22% to 31% to 41%; FY27 "very similar"; 27-31% is high for coated fabrics.
- Verdict: PARTIALLY VERIFIED (Mayur corroborates the band and the swing driver; Arvind AMD sits far lower).
- Peer evidence:
  - Mayur: 24-25% margin on export mix (MAYURUNIQ Feb, page 6); exit-quarter margin about 30% (MAYURUNIQ May, pages 6-7); guidance "25%-30%" (MAYURUNIQ May, pages 8, 14); then "sustainable margin will be 25 plus 1% or 2%. But last quarter ... was abnormally high because of ... foreign exchange ... onetime" (MAYURUNIQ Aug, page 6). Drivers named: export mix, FX, operating efficiency, cost control (MAYURUNIQ May, page 14); freight 4x and raw material (MAYURUNIQ Aug, pages 8-9). Gross margin 52% in Q1 to 57% in Q2 FY27 (MAYURUNIQ Nov, page 6).
  - Arvind AMD: EBITDA margin 15.5% in Q3 FY26; "14% to 15% EBITDA ... some quarters will be below that, some quarters will be better" (ARVIND Jan, pages 3, 5). Swings come from "project business" and "chunky" orders.
- Peers silent: Arvind on FX as an AMD margin driver beyond a passing mention that "macroeconomic conditions of the dollar" were favourable (ARVIND Jan, page 2).
- Net read: a high-20s band is reached by an export-heavy coated-fabric peer, so it is not impossible. The peer reached it with FX help, and its own management called the peak one-time and the base 25% plus 1-2%. Kusumgar's 27-31% is therefore on the high side of the only like-for-like peer; the Q1 to Q2 FY27 move from 31.1% to 28.4% matches Mayur's pattern of a quarter-to-quarter fall. Basis caution: Mayur quotes may include other income (flag).

### Q5. US tariff effect and export mix
- Claim: US is 10-15% of Kusumgar revenue; a blocked Q4 volume shipped when tariffs lifted; customer arrangements cushion a repeat.
- Verdict: PARTIALLY VERIFIED (US exposure and the tariff presence corroborated; the blocked-then-released pattern and the cushioning are not).
- Peer evidence:
  - Arvind: US "around 20% of our business on fabric"; tariff-related discounts about INR 20-25 crore a quarter absorbed ("INR25 crores run rate for the quarter? ... I would think so") (ARVIND Jan, page 8); the discount "settled at this level" as brands demanded it. The supplier bore the cost.
  - Mayur: "we are not impacted right now" by tariffs (MAYURUNIQ Feb, page 5). Impact of the US duty "is not visible in the overall year"; the Q4 shortfall in general export came from the Middle East war hold, not a tariff block (MAYURUNIQ May, page 16). Automotive export prices are dollar-fixed: "if the price increases or decreases ... we have to take the impact" (MAYURUNIQ May, page 17). No export price increase taken (MAYURUNIQ Aug, pages 8-9, 11). In H1 FY27 the US auto market weakened and Mayur "tried to reduce our dispatches to U.S." (MAYURUNIQ Nov, page 5). Export is 42.5% of Mayur revenue (MAYURUNIQ May, page 6).
- Peers silent: neither mentions a tariff-triggered Q4 pull-forward.
- Net read: the peers show US exposure is real and that, where tariffs bit, the supplier paid, not the customer. Nothing supports Kusumgar's statement that tariffs were net beneficial; the customer-arrangement mechanism is UNVERIFIABLE on this set, and no peer shows customers cushioning.

### Q6. Utilisation, capex, revenue-to-asset
- Claim: Kusumgar utilisation 55-60% after large FY25-26 capex; no large capex planned.
- Verdict: PARTIALLY VERIFIED (patterns of staged, small capex corroborate; no defence capex data).
- Peer evidence:
  - Mayur: PVC utilisation 75-78% (MAYURUNIQ Aug, pages 6, 15); PU plant at 25% utilisation with 40% breakeven (MAYURUNIQ Nov, page 11), underused "for a long time" (MAYURUNIQ Aug, page 16). Capex: one coating line within the existing site, "within INR 50 Cr" (MAYURUNIQ May, page 15); that line adds 5 lakh meters a month and about INR 120-150 crore revenue (MAYURUNIQ May, page 16; Aug, page 5), an incremental revenue-to-capex of about 2.4x to 3x; a global plant is INR 250-300 crore, undecided (MAYURUNIQ Aug, pages 4, 12-13; Nov, page 10).
  - Arvind: denim at "absolute full capacity utilization" and not investing further in fabric (ARVIND Jan, pages 4-5); group capex "INR400-ish plus/minus INR50 crores" (ARVIND Jan, page 12); Dalco at 85% with a USD 5m line a year (ARVIND May, pages 4, 6).
- Peers silent: neither plans defence or technical-textile capex.
- Net read: no peer sits at 55-60% on its core plant. The closest case is Mayur's PU line, where under-utilised capacity persisted for years with a demand wait. Kusumgar's "ideally fully utilised next quarter" has no peer analogue. Peers phase capex to demand; Kusumgar's no-large-capex line is consistent with that, but its Certoplast JV capex is an open item.

### Q7. Market growth for defence or protective textiles; raw material pass-through
- Claim: defence spending and indigenisation are secular; most raw material rises pass through with a lag. Prospectus: Indian A&D aerospace fabric CAGR 25.5% FY25-30.
- Verdict: PARTIALLY VERIFIED (pass-through lag corroborated by two peers; market growth UNVERIFIABLE).
- Peer evidence:
  - Pass-through lag: Mayur: "it takes some time to pass on the price increase ... maybe a month or so" (MAYURUNIQ May, page 5); "in automotive it takes some time ... 1-2 months" (MAYURUNIQ May, page 11); minor rises not passed on at all (MAYURUNIQ Feb, page 12). Arvind: "polymer prices have gone up ... some amount of cost increases have been successfully passed on" (ARVIND May, page 15).
  - Defence or protective market growth: none given. Arvind states its own AMD aspiration, "18% to 20%" (ARVIND Jan, pages 3, 5), and auto-nonwoven market growth of 3-4% (ARVIND May, page 6). These are company aspirations or auto data, not Indian defence textile growth.
- Peers silent: both, on the Indian defence textile market rate.
- Net read: the pass-through lag is real at peers and often incomplete. The 25.5% CAGR has no peer corroboration; Arvind's own AMD aim of 18-20% is lower than the prospectus figure but is a company, not market, number.

### Q8. Export receivable hedging
- Claim: Kusumgar says "natural hedge"; forwards cover only 8.6%.
- Verdict: PARTIALLY VERIFIED (one peer, Mayur, relies on a natural hedge; no peer discloses forward cover).
- Peer evidence: Mayur: automotive prices fixed in dollars (MAYURUNIQ Feb, page 5; May, page 17); "10% more or less natural hedge has happened" (MAYURUNIQ Aug, page 9); imports about one third of raw material cost (MAYURUNIQ Feb, page 9); FX gain is booked in other income, about INR 30 crore for FY26 (MAYURUNIQ May, page 9) and it called the Q4 effect one-time (MAYURUNIQ Aug, page 6). No forward contract percentage is stated.
- Peers silent: Arvind (no hedging discussion of receivables in either call; its USD debt is priced at SOFR, ARVIND May, page 11).
- Net read: a natural-hedge posture is also what Mayur describes, with FX gains booked as income. It means the margin carries FX. The forward cover share has no peer comparison.

## PART 4: TRIANGULATION SUMMARY

- Claims verified: 0 of 8.
- Claims partially verified: 7 of 8. Claims unverifiable: 1 (Q3).
- Claims contradicted: 0 (nothing goes to synthesis as a priority contradiction).
- Most consequential finding: no contradiction, but the margin and tariff claims do not survive on peer behaviour. Mayur's only like-for-like peak (about 30%) was called one-time FX by its own management, and both peers absorbed tariff and freight shocks instead of passing them to customers (MAYURUNIQ Aug, page 6; ARVIND Jan, page 8). Kusumgar says tariffs were net beneficial and margins stay "very similar".
- Strongest confirmation: raw material pass-through with a lag, and the stickiness of qualified programmes, corroborated by both peers (MAYURUNIQ May, pages 5, 11; ARVIND May, page 15). The auto-OEM approval cycle is the part of the 90% approvals claim that peers do support.
- Overall: the peer set complicates the narrative. The mechanism (sticky approvals, lagged pass-through, staged capex) holds. The level (27-31% margin), the cash flag, the defence share, and the market growth rate have no peer anchor or are tested only against FX-aided Mayur numbers. No peer shows what makes Kusumgar's margin stay at its present level through a rupee reversal. The peer set neither undercuts nor supports the defence thesis, because defence is absent from it.

## PART 5: CROSS-PEER HYPOTHESIS

In the 2026 rupee window (about 85 to 92-94 per dollar), export-linked peers' margin step-ups are part currency and mix, not only structural, and the supplier carries the cost shocks (tariff, freight, raw material) while customers do not.

Pattern across peers: Mayur ties its peak margin to FX and calls it one-time (MAYURUNIQ Aug, page 6), and states a 10% natural hedge from the rupee move (MAYURUNIQ Aug, page 9); Arvind lists "macroeconomic conditions of the dollar" as a margin help (ARVIND Jan, page 2) while absorbing tariff discounts (ARVIND Jan, page 8); neither peer names this as a sector-wide tailwind. Test for Kusumgar: split its export share and the FX component of other income (the deck margin includes other income). If the rupee reverts toward 85, a Kusumgar margin that is mostly currency should fall by roughly export share times the currency reversal; a margin that holds would suggest a structural, mix-led advantage that the peers do not show.
