NONE FOUND in web search either.

### 1B. Promoter group entities — red flags
The RHP's own "Our Group Company" section (RHP p.219, ✅ VERIFIED) states explicitly there is
**no entity to be identified as a Group Company** under the SEBI ICDR Materiality Policy. This
sits in direct tension with the RHP's AS-18 related-party disclosure (Annexure Y), which classifies
**ABJ Rub Metalico** as an "Enterprise under significant influence of KMP or their relatives" — i.e.
a related party by accounting standard, but not a "Group Company" by SEBI ICDR definition. This is
a real, material **disclosure-framework contradiction**, not a resolved technicality: a related
entity trading up to 16.61% of FY23 revenue with the issuer is absent from the Group Company
list an investor would naturally check first (✅ VERIFIED, RHP; see 3A for RPT detail). Web search
for ABJ Rub Metalico's ownership/promoters returned only its own corporate website with product
information — no ownership disclosure was found publicly (❓ UNVERIFIED / NOT FOUND — ownership
structure of ABJ Rub Metalico could not be independently confirmed beyond the RHP's KMP-relative
characterisation). No other promoter-group entities, shell companies, dormant entities, or
struck-off RoC entities are disclosed in the RHP or found in web search. The amalgamated
**Cemcon Rubber Private Limited** (merged into Ameenji per NCLT order 29-Jul-2022) is a wound-up
predecessor, not a live parallel vehicle, but the RHP discloses unresolved integration items: three
godown properties still titled to Cemcon, and PAN/GST/bank accounts not yet fully surrendered, with
some Railway Price Variation Clause invoices still raised under the old entity name (✅ VERIFIED,
RHP risk factors) — an operational/administrative loose end, not a fraud signal, but a governance
housekeeping gap that persisted into the RHP itself.

## SECTION 2: LEGAL & REGULATORY RECORD

### 2A. SEBI actions

history for the promoters (RHP declarations — ✅ VERIFIED, self-disclosed but standard for RHP
promoter due-diligence certificates).

### 2B. Criminal cases
NONE FOUND. No economic offence, PMLA/ED action, Companies Act criminal prosecution (beyond the
compounded cash-flow-statement matter below, which is a civil/regulatory compounding, not a criminal
conviction), tax-evasion raid, or arrest was disclosed in the RHP or found in web search for any of
the four promoters or the company.

### 2C. Tax and revenue
RHP discloses only routine direct and indirect tax litigation: aggregate outstanding tax proceedings
of roughly ₹203-257 lakhs across the entity and the amalgamated Cemcon Rubber, including a pending
GST appeal of ₹89.51 lakhs (✅ VERIFIED, RHP litigation section). No IT search-and-seizure,
undisclosed income finding, transfer-pricing adjustment, benami property proceeding, DRI action, or
SFIO reference was disclosed or found. This is ordinary-course tax litigation typical of an SME
manufacturer, not an ethics-relevant finding.

### 2D. Other regulators (MCA/NCLT/RBI/etc.)
This is the most consequential sub-section. RHP Risk Factor #19 (✅ VERIFIED) discloses an extensive
history of Companies Act non-compliance, most materially: **cash flow statements were not filed/
prepared for nine consecutive years, FY2014-15 through FY2022-23.** The company regularised this via
an NCLT compounding application under Section 129 read with Section 441 of the Companies Act, 2013
— and that compounding application was filed on **20-September-2025, the same calendar date as the
RHP itself.** This same-day timing is a governance-quality red flag in its own right: a nine-year
disclosure failure was formally regularised on literally the day the company sought public capital,
which reads as compliance cleanup timed to the listing rather than a compliance culture operating
independently of the IPO. RHP Risk Factor #17 (✅ VERIFIED) separately discloses an unresolved
**FEMA/ODI gap**: the company's 90%-owned US subsidiary, Ameenji Rubber Inc., has not received its
equity funding remittance because the US bank account required for receipt has been delayed in
opening — meaning the Form ODI/FEMA reporting obligations tied to that investment remain outstanding
as of the RHP date, an ongoing (not historical) compliance gap. RHP Risk Factor #3 (✅ VERIFIED)
discloses the company does **not provide for interest on delayed payments to Micro/Small enterprises**
under Section 16 of the MSMED Act, despite MSME payables forming a substantial share of total trade
payables — a live, continuing accounting-policy non-compliance rather than a one-time lapse. No NCLT
oppression petition, CCI matter, NGT case, or labour-forum adverse finding was found. No MCA
director-disqualification listing for any of the four promoters was found in web search (search
returned only the general MCA disqualification framework, not a specific hit for these individuals
— treated as NONE FOUND, not verified-clean, since the MCA disqualified-directors database itself
was not directly queried in this run).

### 2E. Civil litigation

## SECTION 3: BUSINESS CONDUCT & ETHICAL TRACK RECORD

### 3A. Related-party transaction history
ABJ Rub Metalico, an AS-18 "enterprise under significant influence of KMP or their relatives,"
transacted with the company at **16.61% of revenue in FY23, 16.04% in FY24, declining to 4.45% in
FY25** (✅ VERIFIED, RHP Annexure Y). No arm's-length pricing methodology or benchmarking disclosure
is provided in the RHP for these transactions (✅ VERIFIED — absence confirmed by direct read of the
Annexure Y section and surrounding disclosure). Combined with the Group Company disclosure
contradiction in 1B, this is a material related-party governance gap: a KMP-relative-influenced
entity did double-digit-percentage business with the issuer for two straight years with no
independent pricing benchmark disclosed, while formally not being classified as a Group Company.
The declining trend (16.61% -> 16.04% -> 4.45%) is a genuine positive data point and is carried into
the Section 6D transition scan below. No loans to group entities, no guarantees for private
promoter entities, and no competing promoter-owned business were disclosed or found.

### 3B. Capital allocation behaviour
Company has no dividend or buyback track record to assess — it listed in October 2025 (NOT
FOUND / not applicable, new listing). Pre-IPO capital actions: a bonus issue of 59,80,000 shares
(13:5 ratio) on 4-March-2024, and family share transfers on 3-May-2024 to the Qutbuddin Family Trust
and other individual transferees at ₹22.31/share, alongside gift transfers among family members
(✅ VERIFIED, RHP capital history). These are standard pre-IPO structuring moves at a disclosed
price, not evidence of value extraction. Independent web data (📰 screener.in) shows FY25 operating
cash flow of ~₹8.10 crore against capex of ~₹11.50 crore, i.e. negative free cash flow of ~₹3.40
crore for the year — consistent with a capacity-expansion phase funded partly by the fresh IPO
proceeds rather than a distress or cash-diversion signal; this is a financial-analysis data point
noted here for completeness but is not itself a promoter-conduct red flag.

### 3C. Promoter share transactions
No pledge exists on promoter shares — 0% pledge confirmed in the RHP and independently corroborated
by no adverse pledge reporting found in web search (✅ VERIFIED). Minimum promoter contribution is
locked for 3 years per SEBI ICDR Regulations, with the balance staggered 1-2 years (✅ VERIFIED,
RHP). Promoter holding fell from 92.24% pre-issue to 67.71-67.72% post-listing (✅ VERIFIED, RHP;
corroborated 📰 scanx.trade Oct 2025) — this dilution is the mechanical result of a 100% fresh-issue,
Nil-OFS structure, not a promoter sale, and should not be read as promoter exit behaviour. No
creeping acquisition, no sales timed ahead of bad news, and no suspicious pre-announcement trading
were found (the company's only pre-IPO history to check against).

### 3D. Minority treatment
No proxy advisory (IiAS/SES/InGovern) coverage exists for this name — expected and confirmed
NONE FOUND for a company that IPO'd on the BSE SME platform roughly ten months before this run;
proxy advisors typically do not cover SME-platform micro-caps. No SCORES complaint, AGM controversy,
or selective-disclosure finding was found (no AGM has yet occurred as a listed entity within the
search window). The Group Company/RPT disclosure gap in 3A is itself a minority-relevant concern:
it reduces a minority investor's ability to fully assess RPT-entity relationships from the headline
Group Company disclosure alone.

### 3E. Auditor relationship
The prior statutory auditor, **M/s Sharma & Sastry, resigned on 12-September-2023 citing "paucity
of time"** (✅ VERIFIED, RHP auditor history) — a vague resignation reason for a listing-track
company, and this falls within 3 years of the current run date, directly triggering the Section 6C
deal-breaker check for "auditor resignation within 3 years." Current statutory auditor is M/s M M
Reddy & Co. Separately, and not previously flagged in the task context, cross-referencing RHP KMP
bios found that **outgoing CFO Tejaswini Kandra was previously employed as a Financial Analyst at
M/s M M Reddy & Co.** — the company's own current statutory auditor — before joining Ameenji as CFO
in November 2024 (✅ VERIFIED, RHP KMP bio for both individuals, cross-referenced). This is an
auditor-independence-adjacent observation: a CFO moving directly from the audit firm's staff into
the finance leadership seat of the audited client is not itself a violation (no evidence of concurrent
dual role, and this is common enough in Indian SME practice) but it narrows the independence distance
between management and the statutory auditor and merits disclosure. No audit qualification,
restatement, or whistleblower complaint was found or disclosed.

was found or disclosed within the last 5 years beyond routine appointments.

### 5B. Board quality
Board additions ahead of the RHP (November 2024) include Independent Director **Asfia Moin**, a
qualified Company Secretary with 11 years of secretarial/legal/compliance experience (✅ VERIFIED,

controlled private entities were disclosed beyond the promoter family's own directorships already
covered in 1A. Whether an audit-committee financial expert requirement is met was not independently
verifiable from the materials reviewed in this run (NOT FOUND).

### 5C. Key person and succession risk
The company is single-executive-promoter-dependent: Mufaddal Najmuddin Deesawala is the sole
executive-role promoter (Chairman & MD) with no disclosed deputy or family member in an executive
operating capacity per the RHP (see 1A). With the CFO seat now vacant following the July 2026
resignation and no successor announced, near-term finance-function continuity risk is elevated for
a company that just went public and is managing a fast-growing Railway order book (~₹67.85 crore of
incremental orders added between July 2026 alone, per the operator-supplied announcements file —
provenance noted). This combination — sole-executive-promoter concentration plus an unfilled CFO
vacancy at a newly listed company — is a genuine, current succession/key-person visibility gap.
