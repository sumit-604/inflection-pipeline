### 2B Excuse pattern analysis

For the misses: weather (agricultural/AC demand swings) is the dominant
external-blame explanation for muted country-level power demand across
every single call; the RPO-buyout-provision "confusion" is the dominant
external-blame explanation for the REC decline, used twice, six months
apart, without resolving; the Middle East/Iran conflict is cited for both
the IGX Q1FY27 softness (correctly, and conservatively) and for the Q4FY26
"other income" dip (mark-to-market from a market correction). Pattern check:
- Do they blame externals while crediting themselves? Largely no — volume
  growth in a flat-demand year (Q3FY26 call) is explicitly attributed to
  distribution-company optimization behaviour the company enabled, which is
  a defensible self-credit, not an obvious double standard.
- Do they ever say "we made a mistake"? Once, directly: on the TAM
  11-month-contract delay, Goel accepts the analyst's "it's been 2 years"
  framing outright ("You are right") rather than deflecting. This is a real
  data point in management's favour.
- Do they raise hard topics proactively, or only under analyst pressure?
  Mixed. The 20-40% coupling impact number and the detailed BESS arbitrage
  economics were volunteered proactively (positive). The rising
  non-operating share of PBT and the IGX OFS valuation/proceeds were raised
  by NEITHER side on ANY call (silence, not pressure-then-answer).
- Framing of adverse outcomes: the 13-Feb-2026 APTEL order — which found
  IEX "not an aggrieved party at this stage," i.e., dismissed the appeal on
  standing rather than deciding it in IEX's favour — is narrated in the
  Q4FY26 call's prepared remarks in neutral, procedural language, without
  ever characterizing it as a loss or setback for IEX's litigation position.

Classification: **external-blame-heavy**, with two notable exceptions (the
TAM-delay admission, and the Section-11 imported-coal/BESS arbitrage
disclosures) that keep it short of the worst tier.

## SECTION 3: COMPETITIVE INTELLIGENCE FROM CONCALLS

### 3A Competitor commentary and credibility check

- **PXIL, HPX** (named only implicitly, via Nitin Shakdher's Apr-26 question
  about smaller exchanges gaining share post-coupling): management does not
  name them directly in its own answer, instead reframing around "customer
  loyalty." Credibility check: plausible but unquantified — no market-share
  scenario is modelled aloud.
- **NSE vs BSE analogy** (Apr-26 call, in response to Analyst 6's direct
  DAM-market-share-loss question): "Today you have NSE and BSE and in spite
  of that NSE has retained the market share." This is a favourable but
  selective analogy — it compares to the cash-equity segment where BSE has
  historically struggled against NSE, without acknowledging that NSE is the
  incumbent leader in that comparison, not the challenger; the analogy's
  actual implication (the incumbent tends to keep its lead) supports IEX's
  point but was not defended with any numbers.
- **MSTC, M-Junction** (coal e-auction incumbents, Q1FY27 call): management
  is confident IEX will displace them once the coal exchange rule's
  six-month e-auction sunset takes effect ("we had no expertise when we
  started the Power Exchange and Gas Exchange... don't worry about that").
  Credibility check: reasonable given IEX's regulatory-capture track record
  in power and gas, but untested in a physically-delivered, logistics-heavy
  commodity like coal, which management itself flags as a genuine
  operational challenge (quantity, quality, transportation).
- **MCX** (electricity derivatives tie-up): described consistently as
  immaterial — "quantum in that is not significant," "revenue share is not
  meaningful" — across the Q2FY26 and Q1FY27 calls. Consistent, low-key,
  no over-claiming here.

### 3B Industry and market intelligence dropped in the calls

- GDP growth commentary softens across the four calls: IMF revised India's
  FY26 estimate UP to 7.3% (Q3FY26 call, Jan-26), but by the Jul-2026
  Analyst Meet, RBI's own FY27 projection is 6.6%, a step down from the
  6.8% FY26 forecast cited at the Q2FY26 call — a decelerating macro
  backdrop management is walking into its FY27 volume guidance against.
- Coal fuel availability consistently described as comfortable/surplus
  across all four calls (inventory 20-25 days, record production), removing
  a historical supply-side tail risk to thermal generation and exchange
  liquidity.
- BESS cost deflation is the single most consistently quantified sectoral
  trend across the corpus: tender prices fall from Rs1.78 lakh/MW/month
  (Oct-2025) to Rs1.48 lakh (Nov-2025, AP Transco) to "less than 2 lakhs"
  generally cited at the Jul-2026 meet, alongside a claimed ~70% battery
  cost decline over 3-4 years.
- AT&C loss improvement and DISCOM credit-rating upgrades (16 to 31
  A+/A-rated DISCOMs, FY24 to FY25) are cited only once, at the Jul-2026
  Analyst Meet, as a new structural-tailwind data point not mentioned in
  the three earlier calls — a genuinely NEW trigger worth testing for
  durability.
