## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

### 2A Demand environment

- Both peers describe demand as healthy in H1 FY27 but hit-dependent. UFO: "increasingly selective nature of theatrical audiences" (Mishra, Q2 p.3). Wonderla: strong first half, existing-park footfall +7% in H1 (Louiz, Q2 p.4).
- Consensus on direction, split on drivers. UFO's revenue rise is tied to tentpoles and equipment sales. Wonderla's rise rests on new capacity (Chennai gave about 73% of the Q2 EBITDA increase, Louiz Q2 p.3) and on per-head spend.
- Film supply fell. UFO: 386 releases in Q2 FY27 vs 462 in Q2 FY26 (Mishra, Q2 p.3), and 399 vs 456 in Q1 (Q1 p.3). That is -16% and -12.5% (derived). PVR's calls do not raise lower film counts (B05 does not list it).
- Forward: UFO expects Q3 and Q4 to be stronger on sequels and star slates (Bhardwaj, Q2 p.8) but gives no number. Wonderla gives no H2 guide ("we don't want to speculate", Choudhary, Q2 p.8).

### 2B Pricing and input costs

- Wonderla: ticket price flat to down (Q1 +2%, Q2 -3%), non-ticket spend up 11-20%, F&B cost inflation (Choudhary, Q1 p.11). Matches PVR's SPH and F&B story, not PVR's ATP story.
- UFO: ad revenue share paid to theatres rose from a legacy 30-35% to 59.5% of ad revenue in FY26 (Malushte and Bhardwaj, Q4 p.6), and 63.83% in FY25 (Jan call p.10). Reasons given: multiplex network mix, fixed minimum guarantees on a lower revenue base, and chains asking for more at renewal (Malushte, Jan call p.10-11). This is the exhibitor side of the ad business. It shows large chains winning more of each ad rupee. PVR's own ad margin and its UFO exposure (if any) are not in these calls.
- UFO operating leverage: the CFO says a Rs 6 Cr ad revenue increase would add about Rs 4 Cr PBT (Q2 p.7, 70% flow-through), so ad revenue swings drop through at 70-75% (Jan call p.8-9). The same logic applies in reverse to any exhibitor's ad line.

### 2C Capex cycle

- Neither peer is a screen builder, so no cinema capex race can be assessed from this set. PVR's screen expansion cannot be called lone-expander or industry race on this evidence.
- Wonderla expands slowly. Chennai cost Rs 570-611 Cr and opened Dec 2025 (Louiz Q1 p.9; Chittilappilly Q3 FY26 p.2). It has named "2 to 3 new locations" for FY28 but "no final call has been taken" (Chittilappilly, Q2 p.12), after a QIP about 18 months earlier (Q1 Manoj question, p.15). Its stated reasons are land, state-government approvals, and "50 to 60 licenses from the state governments every year" (Chittilappilly, Q4 p.12), described as "a structural thing in India".
- The licence and land friction at Wonderla is a partial parallel to PVR's Q1 FY27 zero openings blamed on licences (B05). It makes the cause plausible. It does not excuse the repeated FY27 guidance cut.
- Small-format analogue: Wonderla's Bhubaneswar Tier 2/3 park is "an experiment", footfall growth "slower" because it is "category-creating" (Chittilappilly, Q1 p.6); Q2 footfall -20% on weather (Louiz, Q2 p.4; Choudhary p.6); small-park payback "4 to 5 years" vs 6-8 for large (Louiz, Q1 p.9). A Tier 3-4 format ramps slowly in a peer product. It is weak evidence for PVR's SMART Cinema timeline, but it is the only small-town data in the set.
- UFO capex is maintenance at Rs 40-45 Cr (Malushte, Jan call p.11).

## cross_peer_hypothesis

Hypothesis: across the three out-of-home businesses, growth in H1 FY27 came from added capacity, per-visit spend and event concentration, not from more visits per existing asset. The two peers split on how to hold price. Wonderla trades ticket price for visits (ATP -3% in Q2, footfall +6%), while PVR trades visits for price (ATP +14%, admissions +4%). UFO's ad revenue rose 33% in Q1 on a Dhurandhar tail and then stalled in Q2 when titles under-performed, which suggests ad income follows hits, not footfall (visits).

No single peer states this. Wonderla attributes growth to Chennai and ARPU. UFO attributes ad moves to titles. PVR attributes its gap to content mix.

Testable: if PVR's admissions growth stays near +4% to +8% in Q3 FY27 while industry GBOC grows above 20% and its ATP grows above 10%, the gap is price-led share loss and not content mix. If PVR admissions growth rises toward +15% in a sequel-heavy quarter (Ramayana, Avengers, King, per UFO Q2 p.8) with ATP growth falling, the content-mix reading stands. Confidence: low to medium, because neither peer is an exhibitor.
