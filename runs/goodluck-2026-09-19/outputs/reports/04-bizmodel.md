## SECTION 1: THE BUSINESS MODEL IN PLAIN ENGLISH

### 1A. One-line description

Goodluck India makes steel tubes, sheets, structures and forgings for
autos, railways, solar and infrastructure customers, and since October
2025 also makes artillery shells for the Indian military through a
subsidiary that is 79.43% owned, not 100% (AR p.60-61, Form AOC-1; also
Note 15028 of the extraction, "Goodluck Defence and Aerospace Ltd. India
79.43%").

### 1B. Money flow chain, one chain per revenue stream

**1. CR Coils, Pipes & Tubes (the commodity base).**
[Hot-rolled steel coil bought in the open market] → [cold-rolled, cut and
shaped into coils, sheets, hollow sections and GI pipes at Sikandrabad and
Kutch] → [delivered to OEMs and government infrastructure buyers] → [buyer
pays on standard trade terms; price is a formula linked to the prevailing
HR coil price, revised with a 1-2 quarter lag] (AR p.20-21; Note 31.6,
"selling prices of steel prices and the prices of input raw materials move
in the same direction").

**2. Precision Pipes & Auto Tubes.**
[Steel coil/billet] → [drawn and welded into CDW/ERW precision, hydraulic,
engineering and boiler tubes to auto-OEM tolerance] → [delivered to
Volkswagen, Audi, BMW, Mercedes-Benz, TVS, Bajaj, Toyota, Tata Motors and
export customers] → [buyer pays under repeat, spec-in contracts; IATF
16949:2016 automotive quality certification is a condition of supply] (AR
p.18-19; AR p.72-73).

**3. Engineering Structures & Precision Fabrication.**
[Steel plate/section] → [designed and fabricated into bridges, girders,
solar and transmission structures, boiler/turbine structures] →
[delivered to EPC majors and PSUs — Indian Railways, L&T, NTPC, ABB,
Reliance, Toshiba, TRF, EIL, NPCIL] → [buyer pays against project
milestones/purchase orders; an L&T Letter of Launch for a USD 27 million
high-speed-rail bridge package is the current large order named in the AR]
(AR p.14-15; Inv. Pres. Q1FY27 slide 4, "Rs 2550 Mn... Secured order for
supply of ~14,500 MT of Transmission Line Structures, valued at USD 13.6
Mn").

**4. Forging Products.**
[Steel billet/ingot] → [forged, up to 14,000 kg single-piece capacity,
into flanges, shafts, gear rings, defence and aerospace components,
across stainless, duplex, carbon and alloy grades] → [delivered to
Reliance, L&T Defence, HAL, Indian Oil and other industrial/defence
buyers] → [buyer pays under contract; certified through the in-house
NABL-accredited lab] (AR p.16-17).

**5. Defence & Aerospace (Goodluck Defence and Aerospace Ltd, "GDAL").**
[Steel input, specialised forging/machining] → [manufactured into 105mm to
155mm artillery shell bodies — HE M107, ERFB, ERFB BB, ERFB BT variants —
under an Arms Act, 1959 Industrial Licence, DGQA-certified] → [delivered
to Government of India defence procurement and export buyers] → [buyer
pays against firm orders; Q1 FY27 alone booked Rs 255 Cr for ~50,000 ERFB
shells and Rs 52.2 Cr for M107 shells] (AR p.34-35; Inv. Pres. Q1FY27
slide 3).

### 1D. Simplified business model canvas

### 1E. The chai-stall-uncle version

Imagine a chai-stall that also sells samosas, sandwiches and, this year,
started catering weddings. The chai (CR steel products, a third of sales)
is the volume anchor but everyone sells chai, so the margin is thin and the
uncle just passes on the price of milk when it moves. The samosas and
sandwiches (precision tubes and forgings, more than 40% of sales combined)
need a bit of skill — regular customers keep coming back because they
trust the quality, so the uncle earns a bit more on them. The wedding
catering (artillery shells) is brand new: only a handful of caterers in
town have the licence to cook for a wedding this size, the first few
orders were unusually profitable because the stall wasn't yet running at
full capacity, and nobody yet knows if the uncle can deliver consistently
once he is cooking every week instead of once a month.

### 2A. Five forces, plainly

| Force | Reading | Helps / Hurts / Neutral |

| Competition count | CR/commodity steel products: many domestic converters (industry described in the AR as "a largely commoditised category," AR p.18-19). ### 2D. Industry lifecycle stage

- CR/commodity steel products: mature, arguably over-supplied — the AR's
  own industry-overview section cites the OECD warning that "global
  steelmaking capacity is set to keep rising through 2028 even amid
  sluggish demand, threatening to depress capacity utilisation and squeeze
  profitability further" (AR p.30-31 area, Industry Overview section).
  Goodluck sits as an established, government/PSU-qualified supplier
  within a structurally pressured segment.
- Precision tubes / forgings: growth phase, riding auto lightweighting,
  import substitution (hydraulic tubes) and export demand.
- Engineering structures: growth phase, riding India infrastructure capex
  (Gati Shakti, high-speed rail, 500+ GW renewable capacity).
- Defence & Aerospace: early/emerging — first full commercial year has not
  yet been reported; the FY26 EBITDA margin of 63-68% is explicitly
  flagged by the company itself as "not representative of a full year of
  operations" (AR p.34-35).

## SECTION 3: FINANCIAL METRICS THAT MATTER FOR THIS BUSINESS MODEL

### 3A. Ignore-these, track-these

| Commonly tracked ratio | Why it is misleading/irrelevant here |
|---|---|
| Standalone P&L in isolation | GDAL (the growth story) sits in the subsidiary; standalone numbers understate the defence ramp and its cash drag (GDAL ran negative standalone operating cash flow both FY25 and FY26, B02-notes finding #3) |
| Spot-year ROCE or a single-year P/E | FY26 blends an unrepresentative 63-68% GDAL EBITDA margin (AR p.34-35) into the consolidated base; a spot-year multiple prices a margin management itself says will normalise to 30-35% |
| Blended consolidated gross revenue growth % | Masks the mix shift — 4.1% total-income growth (AR p.28-29) sat alongside 26% EBITDA growth (AR p.28-29) because value-added mix rose from 64% to 66% (AR p.74-75); growth and margin quality are moving in different directions and must be read separately |
| Net Debt/EBITDA or interest coverage at a single point in the capex cycle | The company is mid-way through a multi-year, ~Rs 300-500 Cr/year capex programme (base capex plus GDAL Phase-2, AR p.34-35); a spot ratio does not show whether leverage is peaking or still climbing |
| Rupee-denominated WC trend or spot ROCE fed directly into a converter-style exit multiple | Per the framework's Amendment 17 binding rule, a CONVERTER-classified slice (the CR base) must not have spot-year ROCE or rupee WC trends fed into Section 1B or FTTCP; this business is a hybrid, not a pure converter, and Stage 11 must decide the applicable slice-by-slice treatment, not default to whole-company figures |

## SECTION 4: RISKS, VALUATION APPROACH & MONITORING

### 4A. Business-model-specific risks

| Category | Risk | First financial line item to watch |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue model | Engineering Structures is order-book/project-timed; large single orders (e.g. the L&T high-speed-rail package) can slip or lump revenue unevenly | ESF segment revenue growth decelerating or turning negative quarter-on-quarter |

| Execution | GDAL's 4,00,000-shell Phase-2 expansion, guided "within ~12 months" of the October 2025 inauguration (AR p.34-35), against company memory (non-anchored) reporting a 6-9 month slip to Q4 FY28 | GDAL quarterly utilisation missing the 75-80% FY27 guide, or Phase-2 capex/timeline commentary slipping further in quarterly disclosures |
| Structural | GDAL ownership/disclosure integrity: the AR's own narrative twice calls GDAL "wholly-owned" (Chairman's letter, MD&A p.74-75) while the same AR's Form AOC-1 discloses 79.43% ownership (p.60-61) — the minority investor's identity remains undisclosed (B03-ardeep) | Any new related-party flow, further stake dilution, or repeated disclosure inconsistency in the next AR |

### 4B. Valuation method applicability (handoff to Role 1 / Stage 11)

| Method | Applicable? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| SOTP (sum-of-the-parts: core manufacturing segments + GDAL subsidiary) | **PRIMARY** | This is a hybrid archetype — a commodity converter base, a value-added spec'd-supplier layer, an order-book fabrication business, and a licence-scarcity defence subsidiary at 79.43% parent ownership — not a single business model. A single blended multiple would misprice the GDAL optionality against the CR base's price-taker economics. Section 1B's destination-PE authority governs each slice's exit multiple per the framework's amendment layers; this stage names the need for slice treatment, it does not compute the multiple |
| EV/EBITDA (consolidated, cross-check) | **SECONDARY** | Normalises for the capex-heavy, rising-D&A, rising-interest-cost phase that distorts a P/E read during the capex build |
| DCF (segment-level or consolidated) | **TERTIARY** | Useful to test whether the ~Rs 300-500 Cr/year capex programme converts to free cash flow, but near-term confidence is low given CFO has trailed capex for two straight years (B02-notes, B03-ardeep) |
| Blended consolidated P/E | Not applicable as primary | FY26 earnings are distorted by GDAL's unrepresentative 63-68% margin; a blended P/E would either overpay for a margin management itself says will normalise, or underpay if the CR base is weighted too heavily |
| Dividend Discount Model | NOT APPLICABLE | Growth-capex reinvestment phase; payout ratio NOT FOUND in this corpus but a low payout is the more evidenced path given the capex intensity |
| Asset-based / NAV | NOT APPLICABLE | This is an operating manufacturing business, not a holding-company or asset-realisation story |
| Cycle stage that matters for valuation | Mid-capex-cycle, pre-full-ramp on the two newest capacity additions (defence, hydraulic tubes) — valuation should weight the normalised (post-ramp) margin and utilisation, not the FY26 spot figures | |

### 4C. Quarterly monitoring checklist (12 items)

1. Consolidated CFO/PAT ratio — good: sustained >1.0x; trouble: <1.0x a third consecutive period
2. GDAL quarterly defence revenue vs. the Rs 250-300 Cr FY27 guide (AR p.34-35) — good: on/ahead of pace; trouble: material shortfall
3. GDAL EBITDA margin trend toward the 30-35% steady-state guide — good: converging from FY26's 63-68%; trouble: falling below 25% or staying implausibly high with no explanation
4. Net Debt/EBITDA — good: stable/declining from 2.69x; trouble: crossing 3x
5. Interest coverage — good: holding above 3.5x; trouble: falling below 3x
6. Inventory + receivable days — good: stabilising near FY26 levels; trouble: further rise beyond 74.79/45.67 days standalone (AR Note 37, p.216)
7. Value-added revenue mix % (ESF+Forging+PPAT) — good: sustaining/rising above 66%; trouble: reverting toward the CR-heavy historical mix
8. GDAL 4,00,000-shell Phase-2 capex and timeline — good: on the ~12-month plan from October 2025; trouble: further slippage
9. GDAL minority (NCI) investor disclosure — good: named with terms; trouble: remains undisclosed
10. Promoter shareholding trend — good: stable/rising; trouble: further open-market sales (company memory logs a 2.45% sale on 29-Jun-2026, non-anchored in this corpus)
11. Export share of revenue — good: rising beyond 29% (Q1FY27); trouble: falling
