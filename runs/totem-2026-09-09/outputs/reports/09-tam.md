## SECTION 1: MARKET DEFINITION

### 1A Precise boundaries

**Product scope (IN):** precision metal-cutting ROUND TOOLS — HSS and
solid-carbide taps (machine, hand, NIB), dies, drills (HSS and solid
carbide, annular cutters), thread mills, end mills, reamers, deburring
tools/burrs, plus spring lock washers (a related fastener line made on
the same plant). Materials: predominantly High Speed Steel and
carbon-steel; solid carbide is a minority line (2024 Information
Memorandum installed-capacity table: Carbide Tools 26,000 units and
Carbide RM 180,000 units, versus HSS Taps 4,800,000, CST Taps 4,800,000,
HSS Drills 5,200,000, Hand tools 2,400,000 units — prospectus, p.57-58,
STALE, 2024 management estimate, pre-dates FY26 capacity additions but
FY26's own additions were also HSS-drill and heat-treat capacity, not
carbide, so the HSS weighting is current, Annual Report 2026 Annexure IV
p.41).

**Product scope (OUT):** indexable inserts (the tool-holder-mounted
carbide tip business that dominates global cutting-tool revenue),
tool holders, CNC/grinding/EDM machine tools (capital equipment, a
separate and much larger market), cutting fluids and coolants, gauges
and measuring instruments (only traded at 0.09% of FY26 revenue, no
manufacturing value-add), superabrasives/grinding wheels (Wendt India's
market), tool-holder/accessory business (Birla Precision's market).

**Geographic scope:** India domestic manufacture and sale, one plant
(Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar). Exports reach roughly 40 countries and were
12.7% of FY26 revenue (REVENUE_ANCHOR), but the FY26 AR MD&A calls the
export market "not very encouraging" and names US/Mexico tariffs as the
cause of a sales drop (AR2026 MD&A p.24, work/text/annual-report__Annual_Report_2026.txt
lines 1356-1364). Given this, TAM/SAM in this report are scoped to
**India domestic demand**; export revenue is treated as upside outside
the sized market, not folded into the headline TAM, because no
export-market-specific TAM figure exists in any source and the trend is
currently negative, not additive.

**Customer scope:** automotive and auto-components (largest, undisclosed
%), defence, railways, aerospace, oil and gas, general engineering — no
product-line or customer-industry revenue split is published anywhere
(single reportable segment, Note 38, confirmed by B04). That silence
constrains how finely SAM can be cut by customer; this report does not
invent a split.

**Channel scope:** B2B industrial distributor-led model (200+ partners,
12 divisional sales offices, 60+ application engineers per the 2024 IM,
not re-confirmed in the FY26 AR — B04 flag) plus some direct auto-OEM
accounts (IM p.56: "Company also have direct customer which are mostly
auto OEMs'"). Not retail, not e-commerce direct.

**Price/tier segment:** branded, mid-tier industrial consumable with
moderate pricing power (B04) — priced above unbranded/import commodity
tooling, below the top global-major premium tier (Sandvik, Guhring,
Walter, Kennametal's own top-line grades) where aerospace-grade,
highest-precision work concentrates.

### 1B Management's own TAM claim

No annual report, results filing, or the 2024 Information Memorandum
gives a quantified, dated, sourced Rs-crore TAM figure specific to
TOTEM's actual product range. The only forward-market language in any

vast and it will be a test of our sales channel ability to extract the
best from this markets" (AR2026, Report of Board and Management
Discussion and Analysis, PDF p.25, work/text/annual-report__Annual_Report_2026.txt
lines 1347-1349). No number, no method, no date-stamped market size.

The 2024 Information Memorandum (dated June 2024, now 2.25 years old at
run date — **STALE**) gives three narrower, non-TAM data points instead:
(i) TOTEM's own claimed market SHARE in three sub-segments — Threading
Solutions 26%, Rotary Burrs 56%, Dies/DN 45% (IM p.57) — with no
corresponding Rs-crore market-size figure and no independent source

INR 20000 Crores by 2024" (IM p.52, STALE, and a different product
category — dies/moulds/press tools — from TOTEM's round-tools core).

**Credibility read: BROAD.** Management offers no testable number.
`mgmt_claim_ratio` is NOT COMPUTABLE — there is nothing to divide by
the conservative TAM estimate. This is recorded as a flag, not
estimated.

## SECTION 2: TAM ESTIMATION, MULTIPLE METHODS

### Method 1 — Top-down (industry report)

IMARC Group, "India Cutting Tools Market" (base year 2025, scope
explicitly stated as covering both indexable inserts and solid round
tools, materials including carbide, HSS, ceramics, PCD/CBN):
**USD 3.1 billion (2025)**, forecast to USD 5.3 billion by 2034, CAGR
5.84% (2026-2034). Converting at an assumed ~Rs 85/USD (approximate 2025
average rate, stated assumption, not the depreciated Sep-2026 spot of
~Rs 94.8/USD which would overstate a 2025-vintage figure) gives
**~Rs 26,350 Cr** for the full India cutting-tools market (round tools +
indexable inserts, all materials).

Confidence: **LOW**. The report's methodology is not disclosed (paywalled
teaser), and IMARC's own separate "India Metal Cutting Tools Market" page
gives an internally inconsistent USD 171.8 million (2025) figure — lower
than Kennametal India's FY26 standalone revenue alone (Rs 1,510.7 Cr,
screening/KENNAMET-Data_Sheet.csv) — an impossible result for a market
Kennametal is only one participant in. That figure is **excluded** from
this analysis and flagged as a vendor-data-quality problem, not averaged
in silently (rule 5).

### Method 2 — Bottom-up (machine-tool consumption × consumables ratio)

IMTMA (Indian Machine Tool Manufacturers' Association) data, current FY26:
production Rs 17,387 Cr (+19% YoY), imports Rs 21,628 Cr (+16% YoY),
exports Rs 2,596 Cr (+76% YoY), **consumption Rs 36,419 Cr (+15% YoY)**
(mmindia.co.in, "Indian Manufacturing on an Upward Trajectory", published
2026-09-09, citing IMTMA; arithmetic checks: 17,387 + 21,628 - 2,596 =
36,419, exact). This is the **machine-tool** market (capital equipment),
not the cutting-tool consumable market, but Kennametal India's own
management has given an explicit ratio between the two: in the Mar-2024
concall, asked about TAM, Kennametal's CEO said the cutting-tool
addressable market was "about a $1 billion" while "the machines you're
talking about are already at 20,000 crores" (KENNAMET Concall Mar-2024
Transcript, p.20-21, lines 994-1022 — **STALE**, 2.4 years old, both
figures unsourced management estimates). $1bn at ~Rs 83/USD (2024
average) = Rs 8,300 Cr. Ratio: 8,300 / 20,000 = **41.5%**.

Applying that ratio to current FY26 machine-tool consumption:
36,419 × 0.415 = **~Rs 15,114 Cr** implied full India cutting-tools
market (round tools + inserts, all materials), FY26-equivalent.

Confidence: **LOW-MEDIUM**. Mixes a current, trade-body-sourced volume
figure with a 2.4-year-old, unsourced management ratio. Kennametal's own
CFO/CEO cautioned in the Mar-2023 call that an analyst's carbide-market
estimate was "actually much lower than what the real market size is"
(KENNAMET Concall Mar-2023 Transcript, p.7, lines 227-229 — **STALE**),
suggesting management color in this sector tends to understate rather
than overstate, which supports treating this bottom-up figure as the
conservative anchor rather than a downside outlier.

### Method 3 — Peer revenue aggregation (listed floor)

Round-tools-relevant listed players (FY26, screening CSVs): Kennametal
India Rs 1,510.7 Cr + TOTEM Rs 251.01 Cr = **Rs 1,761.71 Cr**. Wendt India
(Rs 236.32 Cr FY26) and Birla Precision Technologies (Rs 247.13 Cr FY26)
are excluded from this specific sum: Wendt is superabrasives/grinding
wheels and Birla Precision is tool holders/accessories — both adjacent
listed comparables (used elsewhere in this run as valuation peers) but
not round-cutting-tool revenue in the product sense defined in 1A.

This Rs 1,761.71 Cr is stated explicitly as a **FLOOR, not a market
size**: Sandvik, Guhring, Walter, Ceratizit and Kyocera all sell into
India through unlisted or subsidiary structures, and a large unorganised/
import segment exists (FY26 AR: ~31% of TOTEM's own raw material is
imported from Europe/China, B04 flag; IMTMA: China is 30% of FY26
machine-tool import value). No sourced percentage exists for the
unorganised-sector share of the India round-tools market specifically;
this report does not invent one. The listed floor (Rs 1,761.71 Cr) is
materially below every Method 1/2 estimate below, which is the expected
and correct relationship for a floor.

### Method 4 — Global benchmark (round-tools share of cutting tools)

Global cutting-tools market split (marketresearchfuture.com global
report, cited via search, global not India-specific): indexable inserts
~56% of value, solid round tools (drills, end mills, reamers) ~44%.
Applying this global ratio — as a stated, flagged assumption, since no
India-specific round-tools/inserts split was found in any source — to
the Method 1 and Method 2 full-market figures isolates the round-tools-only
portion of TOTEM's actual addressable scope:

- Method 1 base: Rs 26,350 Cr × 44% = **Rs 11,594 Cr**
- Method 2 base: Rs 15,114 Cr × 44% = **Rs 6,650 Cr**

### Method 5 — Global benchmark, direction only

India's machine-tool production was roughly 2% of global production and
China's machine-tool consumption alone is approximately USD 24.6 billion
versus India's USD 4.1 billion (IMTMA FY26) — India runs at roughly 17%
of China's machine-tool consumption scale (ITIF, "Mapping Industrial
Strength", Dec-2025, and Statista world machine-tool consumption by
country; **cutting-tool-specific per-capita comparison was searched and
not found — this sub-method informs direction only, not a headline
number**, consistent with the staleness/evidence discipline of this
report). Direction: India's industrial base is a small fraction of
China's on this proxy, which argues for a long runway rather than a
constraint on TAM size, but does not itself size the round-tools market.

**Conservative TAM (round-tools-scoped): Rs 6,650 Cr.**

below. The two estimates diverge by ~1.74x — flagged, not averaged; the
divergence traces to Method 1's opaque commercial-report methodology
versus Method 2's dependence on a single management anecdote, and both
depend on the same unverified global 44% round-tools ratio.

**Management claim vs conservative estimate:** NOT COMPUTABLE — no

## SECTION 3: SAM & SOM

### 3A SAM — five filters applied to conservative TAM (Rs 6,650 Cr) and
realistic TAM (Rs 11,594 Cr)

1. **Product fit:** already applied inside the TAM figures above (the
   44% round-tools carve from the full cutting-tools market). No further
   cut here to avoid double-counting.
2. **Geography:** both Method 1 (IMARC) and Method 2 (IMTMA) source
   figures are already India-domestic. No cut. Retained 100%.
3. **Channel:** TOTEM's distributor-plus-direct-OEM model matches the
   standard channel structure for this product category; no sourced
   evidence of a structurally excluded channel segment. Retained 100%,
   with a qualitative caveat: some large-account centralised-procurement
   panels may exclude a non-empanelled supplier for a portion of
   business; unquantified, not sourced.
4. **Customer:** TOTEM's stated customer base (auto, defence, railways,
   aerospace, oil & gas, general engineering) spans the categories the
   IM's industry section names as demand drivers. Retained 100% at the
   eligibility level — this states TOTEM can legally/technically sell
   into these categories, not what share it holds (that is SOM, 3B).
5. **Capability:** TOTEM's disclosed installed capacity and FY26 capex
   additions are both HSS/carbon-steel and heat-treat weighted (Carbide
   Tools capacity only 26,000 units of ~18.7 million total disclosed
   units, 2024 IM; FY26's only new capacity was 350,000 HSS drill units
   and a 90 MT austempering furnace, AR2026 Annexure IV p.41 — no carbide
   capacity was added). Within round tools, the highest-precision,
   highest-value, aerospace-grade business typically concentrates in
   carbide, where global majors and Kennametal compete. **No source
   quantifies the HSS-vs-carbide split of the India round-tools market
   itself**, so this filter is applied qualitatively, not numerically: it
   is named as a structural ceiling on how much of the round-tools SAM
   TOTEM can realistically contest at the premium end, and is carried
   forward as a confidence-lowering flag on SOM's upper bound rather than
   an invented percentage cut.

**SAM = Rs 6,650 Cr** (conservative TAM basis, per rule 6).
**SAM as % of TAM = 44.0%** (6,650 / 15,114) — the entire cut is the
product filter; all other filters passed at 100% because no sourced
evidence supports a further numeric cut. Realistic-basis SAM (Rs 11,594
Cr) is shown as the upper bound throughout.

### 3B SOM at 3 and 5 years

Current SAM share: TOTEM FY26 revenue Rs 251.01 Cr ÷ SAM Rs 6,650 Cr =
**3.8%** (`current_sam_share_pct`).

Share-gain sizing against the standard rules (1-2pp normal / 3-5pp
aggressive with capacity+execution / >5pp only on competitor exit or
acquisition / faster where unorganised share >40% is formalising):
TOTEM's FY26 capacity build is real and documented (B07: category A3,
Strong/DOCUMENTED — austempering furnace, CNC drill lines, robotic flute
grinding), which argues for the aggressive 3-5pp band. But the
qualitative promise-delivery record is weak (B07: F2 execution moat
splits Strong capex-completion vs a Grade C claims record — 1 delivered,
2 partial, 3 missed, including a MISSED reversal on exports and
inventory discipline), which argues against the top of that band.
Implied organised/listed share of SAM is only ~26.5% (Rs 1,761.71 Cr
peer floor ÷ Rs 6,650 Cr SAM), meaning unorganised/import share is
plausibly above the 40% "faster via formalisation" threshold the
framework names — a supporting factor, though not independently sourced
for this specific sub-market. Net: **+2.0pp by yr3, +3.5pp cumulative by
yr5** — low end of the aggressive band, reflecting proven capacity offset
by a weak claims-delivery record.

SAM itself is grown forward at the Method-1-sourced structural CAGR of
5.8% (IMARC), kept separate from IMTMA's cyclical +15% FY26 spike, which
is a one-year capex-cycle reading, not a sustained structural rate.

- SAM(yr3) = 6,650 × 1.058³ = **Rs 7,876 Cr**
- SAM(yr5) = 6,650 × 1.058⁵ = **Rs 8,816 Cr**
- SAM share(yr3) = 3.8% + 2.0pp = 5.8% → **SOM(yr3) = 7,876 × 5.8% = Rs 457 Cr**
- SAM share(yr5) = 3.8% + 3.5pp = 7.3% → **SOM(yr5) = 8,816 × 7.3% = Rs 644 Cr**

Implied revenue CAGR from FY26 base (Rs 251.01 Cr, screening/screener-Data_Sheet.csv):
- yr3: (457 / 251.01)^(1/3) − 1 = **22.1%**
- yr5: (644 / 251.01)^(1/5) − 1 = **20.7%**

**Both sit below the strategy's 25% CAGR hurdle.** This is the formal
handoff (`som_implied_revenue_cagr`) stage 11 will test the revenue
growth assumption against.

### 3C Capacity cross-check

B07's `capex_embedded_growth_pct` = **3.3%**, derived as CWIP Rs 3.56 Cr
× 3-year average fixed-asset turnover 2.32x ÷ FY26 revenue Rs 251.01 Cr —
a narrow, one-year-forward, currently-committed-capital signal, not a
multi-year capacity ceiling.

At a flat 3.3% p.a. organic pace, TOTEM's own revenue would reach only
251.01 × 1.033³ = **~Rs 277 Cr by yr3** — a **gap of ~Rs 180 Cr** against
the SOM(yr3) figure of Rs 457 Cr. A full physical-unit cross-check (can
the disclosed 350,000-unit HSS-drill and 90 MT furnace additions produce
Rs 457 Cr of incremental revenue) is **not possible**: per-unit price and
per-unit cost are NOT FOUND anywhere in the corpus (confirmed gap, B04
input_gaps), so unit capacity cannot be converted to Rs Cr.

**capacity_check: gap of ~Rs 180 Cr by yr3 vs the currently-disclosed
capex-embedded signal — the SOM side is the optimistic one**, unless
TOTEM discloses further committed capacity beyond FY26's additions (no
such announcement exists in the corpus; no second plant, per B04/B07) or
the growth is delivered through price/mix (carbide, high-performance
tools) rather than pure unit volume — a path management gestures at in
the FY26 MD&A ("confident of taking the right steps... solid growth
trajectory") but has not committed capital to (FY26's only new capacity
was HSS and heat-treat, not carbide).

### 5B Runway assessment

- **Revenue headroom** = SAM ÷ current revenue = 6,650 / 251.01 = **26.5x**.
- **TAM growth rate** = 5.8% structural (IMARC CAGR), with a current
  cyclical upswing of +15% YoY (IMTMA FY26 machine-tool consumption).
- **Company CAGR vs TAM:** TOTEM's own FY24-FY26 revenue CAGR = (251.01 /
  228.5)^(1/2) − 1 = **4.8%** (screener-Data_Sheet.csv), slightly BELOW
  the 5.8% TAM structural growth rate — on the historical trend, TOTEM
  was tracking-to-slightly-lagging the market, not gaining share.
  Q1 FY27's 28.9% YoY jump (Rs 67.55 Cr, per REVENUE_ANCHOR) would flip
  this reading to share-gaining if sustained, but B07 already flags that
  jump as largely opex-compression-on-volume against a low FY26 Q1 base,
  not a proven mix/yield story. **Two readings, one separating
  observation:** Reading A (historical FY24-26 trend) says TAM-tracking
  at best; Reading B (Q1 FY27 annualised) says an inflection has begun;
  the observation that resolves it is whether Q2 FY27 operating margin
  sustains above ~20% for a second consecutive quarter (B07 catalyst,
  due ~Nov 2026).
- **Years to saturate SAM:** under Reading A (4.8% company vs 5.8% TAM),
  TOTEM's share of SAM would gradually shrink — SAM is never saturated
  at this differential. Under Reading B (28.9% annualised vs 5.8% TAM),
  saturation of 100% of SAM would take **~16.6 years** — an extreme,
  illustrative upper bound, not a real target. Both readings confirm the
  binding constraint on this thesis is **execution/share-gain, not TAM
  size**.

### 5C Runway classification: **STRONG**

Headroom (26.5x) and TAM growth (5.8% structural, +15% current cyclical)
are both genuinely large and evidenced — this rules out MODERATE/LIMITED.
It does not reach MASSIVE: the SOM-implied CAGR (20.7-22.1%) sits below
the 25% strategy hurdle even before the Section 3C capacity gap (~Rs 180
Cr by yr3) and the mixed base-effect/claims-record evidence (B07) are
weighed in. STRONG: real, large runway; conversion into the 25% CAGR the
strategy targets is not yet supported by currently-disclosed capacity or
by a clean execution record.

### 5D SAM expansion levers actually being pursued

- **Carbide/high-performance tools mix shift** — named as an aspiration
  in the FY26 MD&A ("confident of taking the right steps... solid growth
  trajectory") but **no committed capital**: FY26's capex was HSS-drill
  and heat-treat capacity, not carbide (AR2026 Annexure IV p.41).
- **Export geographic expansion** — stated intent toward South Americas,
  GCC, Far East (FY26 MD&A) but **currently reversing**, not expanding:
  export market called "not very encouraging", US/Mexico tariffs cut
  sales (current, realized).
- **Thread-mill/Simufact software optionality** (B07 register) — could
  open premium high-performance-tool business but has "FY27+" as its
  first-appears window; not yet converting.

No lever currently has both a quantified potential Rs addition and
evidence of active capital commitment. Per the "never estimate a missing
number" rule, no potential-addition figure is given for any of these
three; they are named as watch items, not modelled.

### 5E Final output card

- TAM: Rs 6,650 Cr (conservative) / Rs 11,594 Cr (realistic), India,
  round-tools-scoped.
- SAM: Rs 6,650 Cr (44.0% of conservative TAM).
- SOM: Rs 457 Cr (yr3) / Rs 644 Cr (yr5).
- Runway class: STRONG. Revenue headroom 26.5x.

**Valuation implication line:** At 20.7-22.1% revenue CAGR implied by
SOM (yr5 / yr3), with margin trajectory normalising toward the FY24-25
healthy band of 17-19% (B04 must-track metric, versus the FY26 trough of
16.39% and the Q1 FY27 single-quarter peak of 22.9%), the earnings growth
embedded here is approximately 21-22% CAGR (EPS CAGR tracks revenue CAGR
under a flat-margin-in-band assumption; a one-time boost applies only if
margin recovers from the FY26 trough back into the healthy band, not a
compounding driver beyond that), which **does not support** the current
valuation of 25.2x P/E (screener, fetched 2026-09-09, step1-business-brief.md)
against the strategy's 25% CAGR hurdle — though it sits within reach of
it, and a genuine, capital-backed shift to carbide/high-performance tools
(currently unproven, no committed capex) is the named path that could
close the gap.
