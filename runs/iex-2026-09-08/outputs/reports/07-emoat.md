## THE MOAT QUESTION UP FRONT

CERC's market coupling order (23-Jul-2025) hits ONE segment: DAM,
39% of FY26 traded volume (AR p.61-62), down from 95% in FY16.
Coupling names Grid India as coupling operator for price discovery,
turning IEX into a bid-collection venue for DAM specifically. As
ordered, RTM coupling is deferred to "a later stage after gaining
operational experience" and TAM coupling is only at "shadow pilot"
feasibility stage (AR p.61). That distinction governs this entire
scan: DAM's liquidity network effect is the one THREATENED, not the
whole platform.

What the evidence shows:
- THREATENED: the DAM-specific liquidity network effect (D2 in part).
  Coupling does not touch RTM (34% of volume, +41% FY26), TAM, green
  markets, or certificates as currently ordered.
- UNTOUCHED, GENUINELY EMERGING: RTM leadership and BESS merchant
  arbitrage flow (both structurally different from DAM price
  discovery — RTM coupling requires separate CERC action management
  itself flags as not yet even scheduled); the Coal Exchange
  regulatory-forced migration; ICX's I-REC first-mover position;
  IGX's own scaling (equity-accounted, 47.28% stake).
- DECAYING, NOT EMERGING: none of the 22 categories shows outright
  decay other than the DAM slice of D2. REC market volume is falling
  (-86.6% YoY Aug-2026, B05) but that is a demand/policy-confusion
  problem the company itself has not resolved across two quarters of
  recycled excuses (B05), not a moat category this scan scores
  positively.

### FAMILY B — Supply Chain

**B1 Backward integration and RM security** — NO EVIDENCE FOUND. Not

**B2 Qualification lock-in** — NO EVIDENCE FOUND. Category (industrial

**B3 Supply chain network effect** — MODERATE. Coal Exchange is

IEX. Blended evidence, time to materialise 12-24m per management's own
FY27 target.

### FAMILY C — Customer

**C1 Customer ecosystem / embedded relationships** — STRONG. 📄
Nearly two decades of Business Development relationships with market

**C2 Customer concentration improving / base diversification** —

"continues to grow and diversify" framing.

### FAMILY D — Data & Digital

**D1 Proprietary data asset** — WEAK. 🔍 IEX has accumulated

**D2 Digital platform** — STRONG, WITH THE COUPLING CAVEAT. 📄 9,000+
registered participants (AR p.33); 72% of I-DAM cleared volume via

NEW network effect forming: BESS operators habituating to IEX as the
default merchant-arbitrage venue, a use case that did not exist at
scale two years ago. The caveat: this category's DAM component is
precisely what coupling threatens; the RTM/BESS/API-lock-in components
sit outside the coupling order as issued.

### FAMILY H — Ecosystem & External

**H1 Industry consolidation beneficiary** — STRONG. 📄 Under the Coal
Exchange Final Rules (notified 4-Jun-2026), "existing E-Auction
Platforms and Marketplaces are allowed to operate only upto 6 months

E-Auction by Coal India Limited" (Inv. Pres. slide 34). This is a
regulator-forced exit of incumbent trading channels (including Coal
India's own e-auction platform), not a competitor bankruptcy or
voluntary consolidation — the strongest single H1 fact pattern in this
scan, sized by management at ~100mt in year one (Inv. Pres. slide 34).
The unresolved condition: the licence for the "1st Coal Exchange" has
not yet been awarded to IEX specifically (🎙️ for capture, 📄 for the
regulatory mechanism itself).

**H2 Strategic partnerships** — WEAK. 🎙️ MoUs named in the AR's own

in the historical base with no evidence of ongoing activity; only
Powerledger (2026) is genuinely new, and it is a single timeline
bullet with zero supporting text.

**H3 ESG moat** — NO EVIDENCE FOUND for IEX's OWN competitive
advantage from ESG positioning. ICX enables CUSTOMERS to demonstrate
RE100/SBTi alignment via I-RECs (AR p.30) — that is a product/revenue
line, already captured under E1 and Section 1, not an ESG-driven cost
or reputation moat for IEX itself. No SBTi commitment, ESG rating
upgrade, or renewables-in-operations claim exists for IEX as an
entity.

### FAMILY I — Structural Asymmetries

**I1 Talent asymmetry** — 0. NO EVIDENCE FOUND for either required
leg. No named inventors, no ex-DRDO/ex-PSU technical-staff
concentration claim, no remuneration-annexure evidence of
above-sector-norm technical pay. This category requires structural
evidence a financial-market-infrastructure platform of this kind does
not generate; scored 0 by design, consistent with the operator ruling
that I1/I2 score 0 for typical companies.

**I2 Cannibalization barrier** — 0. Applying the test to every moat
claimed above: what must a best-resourced competitor destroy in its
own P&L or org to copy IEX's position? For DAM/RTM/TAM/Green: any new
private exchange entrant (HPX, PXIL already exist and compete) would
have to build the SAME customer relationships and API integrations
IEX has — that is an execution lead (time and relationship-building),
not a configuration a competitor must sacrifice something to copy.
For Coal Exchange: Coal India must exit its OWN e-auction platform
within 6 months of any Coal Exchange going live — but that sacrifice
is regulator-IMPOSED on Coal India, not a voluntary choice IEX forces
by its business model; Coal India isn't IEX's moat-mirror competitor
here, and no "best-resourced competitor" would have to destroy
anything to run a rival coal exchange under the same rules. The honest
answer across every category scanned is "nothing must be destroyed" —
scored 0 per instructions.
