Real growth on a negative equity base

JITF Infra Logistics builds water and wastewater plants, runs waste to energy concessions, and does a little steel trading. FY26 consolidated revenue was Rs2,808 Cr, up 24 percent. EBITDA was Rs604 Cr, up 35 percent. The owners' share of the result was a loss of Rs48 Cr, while the total group loss was only Rs10 Cr, the gap sitting with minority holders in the subsidiaries. The listed parent is a holding shell with about Rs4 Cr of its own revenue; the operating business and its debt all sit one level down. Shares trade at Rs348 for a market value of Rs893 Cr.

Water EPC is 80 percent of revenue, run through JWIL Infra. Waste to energy concessions are 16 percent, and steel trading is the last 5 percent. The water arm carries a Rs11,352 Cr order book, up 123 percent in one year. Waste to energy runs 110 MW today against a stated target of about 220 MW.

The transition case is a mix shift from fixed price EPC toward annuity and HAM style contracts that management says carry 18 to 25 percent margins. JWIL's entity rating was upgraded to A Stable by both CARE and CRISIL, and its cost of borrowing fell from 10.19 percent to 9.16 percent. Management targets 11 project completions in FY27 against 3 delivered in FY26.

The backward looking scorecard lands on AVOID. Net worth has been negative in all 10 fiscal years from FY17 to FY26, so return on equity has no meaning. Net Debt to EBITDA is 6.19x before the one off charge and 6.71x after it; both are well past the 3x line. Interest coverage is 1.251x before the charge and 1.14x after, so the operating business barely covers its own interest. FY26 audited return on capital was 15.25 percent, higher than the 13.31 percent screener figure, but the FY17 to FY24 history behind the 5.3 percent median rests on non audited screener data.

Operating cash flow was positive at Rs199 Cr, but free cash flow was negative for the second straight year as capex of Rs244 Cr outran it. Trade receivables grew 45 percent against 24 percent revenue growth. The share of receivables more than a year overdue roughly tripled to 11.2 percent, and the provision held against the whole book is only 0.12 percent. Retention money rose 34 percent. Over the full 10 year history cumulative operating cash flow was negative against cumulative profit, and borrowings now stand at Rs3,946 Cr, up 12.6 percent in the year.

With no earnings call available the credibility grade defaults to C. The testable promises this cycle were met: the rating upgrade landed and the named FY26 completions were reported done. The waste to energy buildout is only partial, with none of the four new plants commissioned and only Jodhpur carrying a firm date of October 2026. Two subsidiaries carry auditor going concern language, one at Naya Raipur over a lapsed concession and one at Bathinda over a municipal payment default. The board independence table claims 5 independent directors where 3 stand up to the test, and 45 percent of group finance cost is interest paid to two promoter group lenders.

Three of the four peers read, VA Tech Wabag, EIEL and Antony Waste, confirm a genuine sector wide growth phase. EMS Limited is the outlier, with revenue down 36 to 37 percent on weather, elections and government payment delays, a live risk JITF's own narrative leaves out. EIEL cut its FY27 margin guidance on commodity cost pressure. The claim that JWIL's cheaper borrowing reflects sector wide easing does not hold: EMS reported ballooning interest costs and Wabag's rating was reaffirmed, not upgraded. Across 16 peer transcripts no competitor names JITF or JWIL once, which is quiet for a company citing a Rs11,352 Cr order book.

The addressable market for government funded water and waste to energy work is Rs24,879 Cr to Rs38,608 Cr, with a serviceable slice of Rs14,146 Cr. JITF already holds about 19 percent of that serviceable slice, which leaves roughly 5.3x revenue headroom and a STRONG runway class. Management's own market sizing is 1.46x the independent estimate, close enough to read as reasonable rather than promotional.
