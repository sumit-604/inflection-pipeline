## SECTION 1: THE BUSINESS MODEL IN PLAIN ENGLISH

### 1A. One-line description
PML is a 65-year-old precision component and specialty-alloy manufacturer that designs and mass-produces magnetic, current-sensing and metallurgical parts to exact customer specification, sold to global automotive Tier-1s and the top electricity-meter OEMs, with two early-stage growth bets (specialty alloys for aerospace/oil & gas, and a rare-earth NdFeB magnet venture via subsidiary Quantum Magnetics) layered on top of the mature base (AR-FY26 p.18, "Company Overview"; AR-FY26 p.5).

### 1E. The chai-stall-uncle version
Think of PML like a trusted tailor who has been stitching custom uniforms for the same big schools and factories for 65 years — car makers and electricity-meter companies send exact measurements, and PML cuts precise metal and magnet "garments" to fit, nothing off-the-rack. Because the tailor has passed every quality inspection for decades, the big schools keep coming back and rarely switch, even though switching to a cheaper tailor is technically possible — it would mean months of re-testing. Now the tailor is also trying two new lines of business: fancy heat-resistant fabric for aerospace and oil-rig customers (the Alloys division, just getting going), and a brand-new attempt to make its own rare-earth "thread" instead of buying it from China (Quantum Magnetics) — both are promising but not yet paying their own way. Meanwhile, the tailor has had to buy new sewing machines on borrowed money, and some customers are taking longer to pay their bills.

### 2D. Industry lifecycle stage and PML's position
PML sits across multiple lifecycle stages simultaneously, mapped explicitly in its own strategy deck: electricity-meter current-sensing components are mature (8-10 year average product life), automobile non-current-sensing platforms are mature-to-declining (10-20 year cycle, associated with ICE), automobile current-sensing platforms are an emerging/shorter-cycle category, and gas-meter mechanical parts are in decline (AR-FY26 p.11). Layered on top are two genuinely early-cycle growth bets — Alloys (newly commercialised furnace, Q4FY26) and Quantum Magnetics/NdFeB (pre-revenue, Phase 2 due Q3FY27) — that the company itself frames as the "key drivers of future growth" (AR-FY26 p.19, Outlook). Net position: a mature core business subsidising and funding (via capex/leverage) two nascent growth options.
