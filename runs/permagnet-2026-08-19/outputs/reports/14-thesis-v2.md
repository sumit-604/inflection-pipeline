## NARRATIVE (plain English)

Permanent Magnets is a small maker of magnet parts and meter components. Its real edge is boring and durable.
Big meter makers approve it once, then keep buying, because re-approving a new supplier costs more than the
part. That is the tailor who stitches the same three schools' uniforms for decades.

Two things spoil the picture. First, the schools are shrinking. Itron is cutting; Landis+Gyr sold its
European meter business. The approved supplier now circles a smaller pond. Second, the two exciting bets,
relays and rare-earth magnets, have missed their dates three times and printed zero orders. So we price them
as lottery tickets, not as revenue. Even paying the operator's generous ticket prices, the business is worth
about Rs 704 to Rs 791 a share.

The price is Rs 882. It already assumes both lottery tickets win at full scale. To make 25% a year from here,
the stock would need to keep a ~38x multiple while earnings grow. That does not happen; the multiple falls to
16x-20x as the growth fades. So the maths STOPS. The core is fine, it clears its cost of capital, and the
options cost us nothing today. That is why this stays on deep watch, not in the bin. But the entry is at
Rs 252 to Rs 361, not Rs 882. We wait. And if the price ever falls into that zone because a rare-earth bet
died, we do the sum again first, because part of that zone is the rare-earth ticket itself.
