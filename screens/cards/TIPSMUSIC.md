## Business Understanding Narrative

**What does it do, and how does it make money?** Tips owns a music catalogue of over 38,000 songs in 25-plus
languages (IP-Q2, pp.4, 11). It licenses that catalogue to streaming platforms, broadcasters, brands and venues.
Digital platforms paid 73% of revenue in H1 FY27 (IP-Q2, p.4). Old songs carry the business: 85% of revenue comes
from content acquired more than three years ago (CC-Oct26, p.10). It expenses 100% of content cost in the quarter
of release (IP-Q2, p.4).

**What is changing?** The price per stream, management says, but not yet. Indian labels earn 4 to 10 paise per
stream; global labels earn 50 to 90 paise (AR26, p.5). The gap is subscription. Paid subscription is 10% to 15% of
Tips' digital revenue (CC-Oct26, p.11) and grows 40% to 50% a year (CC-Jul26, p.4). Today's growth is volume:
streams rose about 35% in Q2 FY27 and the ad-funded rate stays at 5 paise (CC-Oct26, pp.11, 13).

**Why now?** FY26 revenue rose 20.87% to Rs 375.51 crore and profit after tax rose 30.13% to Rs 216.75 crore
(AR26, p.35). Q2 FY27 revenue rose 35% to Rs 120.1 crore and PAT 23% to Rs 65.5 crore (PR-Q2, p.2). Spotify and
YouTube raised subscription prices late in 2025 (CC-Jul26, p.6).

**What must be true?** Platforms must move Indian listeners behind a paywall, and that money must reach labels pro
rata. Short-video platforms must move from fixed fees to revenue share. Tips' catalogue must keep its share of
listening as old songs age.

**What breaks it?** Concentration of buyers. Warner alone is 25% to 30% of revenue (CC-Oct26, p.14). Short-video
deals are lump sums Tips cannot convert to revenue share (CC-Oct25, p.7). One platform exit (Resso) cost about
USD 2 million a year (CC-Oct25, p.6). A second break is the price: 44.4 times trailing earnings (MD).

## 2. Business model and archetype

One segment, no subsidiary, 94 employees (R-2026-09, p.3, notes 6-7; IP-Q2, p.4).

The engine is a fixed-cost catalogue earning variable licence fees. Content cost was Rs 59.24 crore in FY26, 15.8%
of revenue, against Rs 71.0 crore and 22.9% in FY25 (R-2026-06, p.3, FY26 column, sum of acquisition, royalty and
promotion lines; IP-Q2, p.14). Employee cost was Rs 20.33 crore (R-2026-06, p.3). FY26 operating EBITDA was
Rs 275.8 crore, a 73.4% margin (derived from the same page). Content sources split in thirds: outside producers,
Tips Films (a promoter company), and in-house non-film music (CC-Jul26, p.12).

**Archetype: licence/scarcity business.** The "licence" is copyright, held for 60 years from production
(`credit-ratings/02-CARE.pdf`, p.2). Renewal risk sits on the platform contracts: Warner renews in FY28 and
YouTube Shorts, due June 2026, is still unsigned (CC-Oct26, pp.4, 14). There is no regulated price. Spotify pays
labels 55% of its revenue, split by consumption (CC-Oct25, p.11). Warner negotiates Instagram Reels and passes on
what it gets (CC-Oct26, p.14).

## 3. Competitive advantages

**Moat: present in the asset, absent in the price.** The catalogue is scarce. Nobody can re-record the 1990s. Viral
reuse keeps old songs earning: "Tera Naam Doon" crossed 1 billion Instagram views in Q2 FY27 (PR-Q2, p.3).
Management puts its share of industry revenue at 8% to 10% (CC-Oct26, p.11). Platforms need every major catalogue,
so Tips is a must-carry supplier.

Pricing power is weak, and the new transcript says so. Asked whether volume can lift the per-stream rate: "No,
amount won't change" (CC-Oct26, p.13). The only lever is paid subscription, "where the per stream rates are higher"
(CC-Oct26, p.13). Short-video deals pay fixed fees because the platforms set the model (CC-Oct25, p.7).

## 4. The promoters

The Taurani family held 64.15% at 5-Aug-2026 (AR26, p.25) and 64.32% at September 2026 after the buyback shrank
the share count (MD). Kumar Taurani holds 15.80% and Ramesh Taurani 15.76% (AR26, p.25). Each promoter declared no
encumbrance for FY26 (`filings/team_sandeshc_11052026110731_Disclsoure.pdf`, p.1). Kumar Taurani drew Rs 1.93
crore (AR26, p.157). The buyback excluded the promoters (AR26, p.22).

Skin in the game is high: "my family has 64% of this company" (CC-Oct26, p.9). The company pays out the prior
year's profit each year (CC-Jul26, p.13). FY26 dividends were Rs 166.18 crore (AR26, p.36). The buyback bought
6,71,946 shares, 0.53%, for Rs 45.15 crore including costs (R-2026-09, p.3). With the Rs 6.50 interim dividend,
H1 FY27 payout was Rs 127.1 crore (IP-Q2, p.6).

The related-party channel is step 9's first flag. The CEO, Hari Nair, left on 30-Apr-2026 (AR26, p.37); six months
on, "still search is on" (CC-Oct26, p.13).

**Classification: earnings-led.** Revenue growth comes from consumption of an existing asset. Margins move with
release timing, not with a structural shift.

## 6. The transition and the quality ladder

**No rung migration. FROM R5 TO R5.**

Tips meets two R5 tests: structural scarcity and ROCE above 30%. It fails the third, pricing power. R5 here means
scarcity, not price-setting. The platform fixes the rent: a pool share per stream. On pricing power alone it sits
nearer R4. And the 92% ROE overstates the rung, as step 5 explains.

The claimed transition is a change in yield inside R5. Ad-funded streams at 4 to 10 paise move toward subscription
streams that pay several times more. Management's claim, quoted once: "It is likely that India will be amongst the
top three music markets in the world by volume as well as value, over the next few years" (AR26, p.5). The Q2 call
dates none of it: subscription is "in that range only" and, on Shorts, "Wait for sometime" (CC-Oct26, pp.5, 12).

The ladder therefore names no re-rating engine.

## 8. Proof check

Revenue proof is clear. Year-on-year revenue growth was 21% in Q3 FY26, 32% in Q4 FY26, 21% in Q1 FY27 and 35%
in Q2 FY27 (IP-Q2, p.13). The CFO said Q2 held "no one-off in this revenue" (CC-Oct26, p.11).

Profit proof lags. H1 PAT grew 10.5%. The 20% PAT guide needs H2 PAT of Rs 150.7 crore against Rs 117.71 crore in
H2 FY26 (derived from AR26, p.35 and R-2026-09, p.2), a 28% rise. H2 content is Rs 19 to 29 crore against Rs 22.5
crore last year (IP-Q2, p.13), and Q3 and Q4 are the stronger quarters (CC-Apr26, p.17). H2 revenue up 20% with
Rs 25 crore of content gives H2 PAT near Rs 151 crore (derived from IP-Q2, p.17). The guide is reachable.

**The mechanism proof is absent, and the transcript says why.** No filed number splits revenue by platform,
subscription or price. Management declined each split (CC-Oct25, p.12; CC-Apr26, p.18) and gave only the Warner
range (CC-Oct26, p.14). Growth is "primarily volume-driven. Marginally, it could be related to subscription"
(CC-Oct26, p.11). The per-stream rate stays at 5 paise (CC-Oct26, p.13). The yield thesis has not fired. Annual
YouTube views fell from 228.3 billion in FY25 to 210.4 billion in FY26 (IP-Q2, p.11).

## 10. Independent check: the credit rating

**The ratings are old and say little about today.** CARE withdrew its CARE BBB+ (Credit Watch) at the company's
request on 14-Jan-2022, after a no-dues certificate (`credit-ratings/01-CARE.pdf`, p.1). The company has no debt,
so it needs no rating. The last live view, CARE on 19-May-2021, named strengths: promoter experience, stable
licence fees and strong liquidity. Weaknesses: moderate scale, a catalogue concentrated in 1990s and 2000s music,
content acquisition risk, format obsolescence and piracy (`credit-ratings/02-CARE.pdf`, pp.1-2). Its upgrade
triggers, music revenue above Rs 150 crore and ROCE above 35% (p.1), are long met. Today's filings win.

## 11. Posture: the Transition Decision Matrix

- **Proof gate: FIRED on growth, NOT FIRED on the yield mechanism.** Four quarters of 21-35% revenue growth are
  filed. Management attributes it to volume at a flat rate (CC-Oct26, pp.11, 13).
- **Ugliness: ARTIFACT-OF-CLIMB.** The H1 margin drop comes from expensing film content in the release quarter.
  Q2 recovered to 70.5% (IP-Q2, p.17).
- **Recognition gap: CLOSED.** Market capitalisation Rs 10,095 crore and trailing PE 44.4x (MD). The business
  already sits at the TO rung. 44.4x sits well above the R5 neighbourhood of about 24x; only a strategic premium
  bridges the difference.

**Posture: EARNINGS-ONLY.** The quality is real and priced. Returns ride EPS growth. Guided EPS growth of 20% sits
below the 25% CAGR target before any multiple change.

## 12. Verdict card

**WATCH.**

A good business at a full price, with no rung to climb. The new transcript strengthens the WATCH: growth is real,
volume-led, and priced at 38.8x the guided year. The yield thesis that would justify the multiple has not fired,
and management says the ad-funded rate will not move.

**Load-bearing facts a `/step1` would verify first**
1. Warner at 25% to 30% of revenue: the minimum-guarantee terms, the overflow test, and the FY28 renewal
   (CC-Oct26, p.14; CC-Oct25, p.13).
2. The Tips Films channel: FY27 spend against the Rs 60 crore limit, the valuer reports, and the Rs 15.53 crore
   advance (AR26, pp.20, 138).
3. H2 FY27 PAT of Rs 150 crore or more, and the YouTube Shorts terms once signed (CC-Oct26, pp.6, 12).

**What would change the view.** Up: a filed revenue-share deal for short-form video, or a subscription split that
shows yield rising; or a price that reopens the gap against guided growth. Toward PASS: a Warner renewal on worse
terms, related-party purchases above the Rs 60 crore limit, or receivables that keep outrunning revenue.

No Role 1 valuation was run and no target price is set. This verdict decides only whether the name earns a full
`/step1`, and it is not a position.

### Operator additions, 2026-10-29

**FY28 expected PAT: Rs 312.1 crore. Growth +20.0% on FY27E. Forward PE 32.3x (10,095 / 312.1). Tier C.**
No FY28 guide exists. The basis is management's standing frame of 20% top line, 20% bottom line and content at

**Confidence FY27: HIGH, about 75%.** The band is Rs 221 to 299 crore. H1 has banked Rs 109.4 crore; an H2 flat
on H2 FY26 gives Rs 227 crore, inside the band. The base is fully taxed with no one-off (CC-Oct26, p.11). The
mover: a Q3 FY27 PAT below Q3 FY26's Rs 58.7 crore (IP-Q2, p.13) drops it to MEDIUM.

**Confidence FY28: MEDIUM, about 50%.** The band is Rs 265 to 359 crore, which admits FY28 growth of 2% to 38% on
a guided FY27. Trend only; one film slate can swing content cost by Rs 30 crore in a quarter (IP-Q2, p.13), and
the largest customer renews that year. The mover: the Warner renewal terms. A renewal at or above the current
minimum guarantee lifts it to HIGH; a non-renewal, or a content budget above 25% of revenue, drops it to LOW.
