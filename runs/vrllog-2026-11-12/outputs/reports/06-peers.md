## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

### 2A Demand environment
Peers agree on growth, differ on pace and breadth. TCIEXP: Q2 FY27 surface +9%, other services 29-52%, "full momentum" in SMEs and large clients (TCIEXP Q2 FY27, Chander Agarwal, [page 6]). Delhivery: volumes "over 30%" YoY in Q2 FY27, October ahead of plan, but PTL tonnage slowed from +18% (Q1) to +14% (Q2). TCI: freight +11.5% to +12% mostly price (two-thirds), supply chain slower on a high base, auto "pullback", pre-stocking "not as robust" before Diwali (TCI Q2 FY27 [page 3]). TCI Q4 FY26 also warned of "some corrosion of demand" from inflation ([page 2]). VRL's framing of a strong October (+20%) is the strongest of the set; Delhivery and TCIEXP say strong but give no number; TCI says pre-festival stocking was softer than last year. This is a split, not a contradiction.

### 2B Pricing and input costs
All three took diesel pass-throughs with a lag of days to a month. Realised price moves: TCIEXP net 1.5% to 3%, TCI 1-3% fuel effect and two-thirds of freight growth from pricing, Delhivery PTL fuel share 6 of 37 paisa (Q1) and under 30% of the yield rise (Q2). Wages: TCIEXP +100 bp of total cost, Delhivery without a minimum-wage clause in Q1. Management at TCIEXP expects oil to fall (Q2 [page 7]) and says nobody asks for price cuts. VRL's 9% realisation rise and 23.3% margin sit above the peer range on both. Margin narrative: only partly matched (see Claims 2 and 4).

### 2C Capex cycle
Lone expander in sector terms? No. Peers are expanding too, in different forms: TCIEXP Rs 100 Cr plus a year on owned centres and automation after cutting its five-year plan from Rs 500 Cr to Rs 400 Cr (TCIEXP Q3 FY26 [page 6]; Q2 FY27 [page 9]); TCI Rs 500-600 Cr in FY27 on ships, warehouses, trucks and rakes, with a Rs 1,000-1,200 Cr three-year outline (TCI Q2 FY27 [page 5], [page 12]); Delhivery "capital intensity to less than 5% of revenue" (DELHIVERY Q4 FY26, Sahil Barua, opening). Call: a broad but uneven capex round, not an industry capacity race. TCI and TCIEXP expand through network assets; only VRL's Rs 308-318 Cr (DERIVED, B05) expansion is fleet plus property on a national LTL base. No peer is cutting.

### 2D Competitive mentions of VRL
One direct mention, TCI Q2 FY27, Vineet Agarwal [page 14], answering a question about pure-LTL competitors "whether it is VRL delivery or other plates": "we don't compete with delivery, and I think VRL perhaps is competing a little bit more on the delivery side as well as with the express business. I think they are also taking away some of that little bit of the express side because of the high, because perhaps a little lower pricing that they are offering other than the express companies, which I have been seeing for some time." Read: a peer says VRL is winning express-type volume with lower prices. This supports share gain and weakens any claim that VRL commands a price premium to organised express players. Related, TCI Q3 FY26 [page 12]: "sometimes you see some of the express guys also moving towards the LTL business with their pricing strategy." TCIEXP Q3 FY26 [page 9] names Delhivery, not VRL, as the large rival. No peer names VRL elsewhere (keyword search of all 12 text files).

### 2E Risks peers discuss that VRL does not
- Pass-through timing and partial pass-through: TCI "we do not end up passing on the entire increase" in LTL (TCI Q4 FY26 [page 6]); Delhivery one-month lag (Q1 FY27).
- Minimum wage change without a contractual cushion (Delhivery Q1 FY27).
- Rail and DFC shift at the expense of fleet owners (TCI Q2 FY27 [page 15]).
- Labour availability and elections-driven disruption: TCIEXP "voter-related SIR activities", labour going home (TCIEXP Q4 FY26 [page 3]; TCI Q4 FY26 [page 7]).
- Competition from express operators moving into LTL (TCI Q3 FY26 [page 12]), and from Safexpress and Delhivery in organised PTL (Delhivery Q3 FY26).
- Demand softness from inflation and auto (TCI Q4 FY26 [page 2]; Q2 FY27 [page 3]).
- Tight lead time: Delhivery notes it manages demand to protect service (Q2 FY27), a signal that peak-load service failures are a live risk in the segment.
- Related-party leasing: TCIEXP discloses one promoter-owned centre (TCIEXP Q2 FY27 [page 11]). VRL's promoter-owned bus business was left unquantified in B05.

## PART 4: TRIANGULATION SUMMARY

- Claims verified in full: 0 of 9. Claims partially verified: 9 of 9. Claims contradicted at claim level: 0 of 9; one sub-claim contradicted (Claim 2 Part C, "9% is mostly fuel", by Delhivery PTL attribution, adjacent-segment weight). Sub-claims verified: 4 (peers pass fuel on with a lag; VRL margin far above every peer; wage cost inflation; shift to organised). Sub-claims unverifiable: 6 (October +20%, premium to unorganised, 21-22% sustainability, own-fleet peak edge, increment timing, 70/30 split).
- Most consequential contradiction: the size and attribution of VRL's 9% realisation rise. On VRL's own Rs 2 = 1% rule, the pump rise of Rs 8-9 explains about 4-4.5% (DERIVED). Peers report 1.5-3% realised price (TCIEXP), 1-3% (TCI), and Delhivery says the majority of its PTL yield gain is not cost-linked.
