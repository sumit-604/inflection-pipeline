# Onward Technologies (ONWARDTEC): business narrative, phase 1

Phase 1 lite, run 2026-11-12. Evidence stages only. No valuation has run. The Mental Model is not signed, and the Halt 1 decision is with the operator.

## BUSINESS UNDERSTANDING NARRATIVE

Onward rents teams of engineers to large industrial and vehicle makers in North America and Europe. The teams do design, testing, embedded electronics and software work on the client's own products, mostly from centres in Pune and Hyderabad. Time and material work, billed by the hour or by the team month, made up 88% of FY26 revenue. Fixed price projects, such as the redesign of a 5 MW cooling unit, made up 12%, and 15% by the September 2026 quarter. A client keeps the same team for years because the engineers learn its products and processes, and average engagements run 5 to 8 years. The 72 active clients are industrial equipment and heavy machinery makers (63% of FY26 revenue), transportation and mobility makers (34%) and healthcare companies (3%). The top 5 clients give 48% of revenue, the top 25 give 87%, and one unnamed customer gave 18.3%, up from 12.7% two years earlier. These customers control approvals and rate cards, and with no disclosed volume floor they can cut hours at short notice. Present demand comes from original equipment makers (OEMs) that outsource engineering, a global vendor pool of about USD 81.5 bn in 2024. Two disclosed contracts show where it comes from: a Rs 33 Cr three year offshore centre for a power management maker's data centre unit, and a Rs 44.5 Cr renewal, 72% larger, with a construction and mining equipment maker. Those tie to the tracker signals "Order backlog of data-centre power and cooling OEMs", "Hyperscaler data-centre capex guidance" and "Construction, mining and heavy machinery OEM order books and R&D spend". Demand should grow with the outsourced engineering pool, which the run sizes at 8.6% a year, a figure the "Nasscom/Everest ER&D market releases" signal will update. Peer calls at LTTS and Cyient partly confirm the data centre and energy pull, so "Peer ER&D services quarterly revenue and deal wins" is the earliest outside check. Management says "US PERM and onsite work rules" push work offshore, but no peer confirms that link, and a weaker rupee added 4.7 points to reported growth in the September quarter, which the "USD/INR" signal tracks. The emerging moat scan scores 12.4, rated MODEST and only 0.4 above the line, carried by two categories at Moderate strength: qualification lock-in from three year contracts, and embedded customer relationships. The time and material line has that moderate switching cost but no evidenced pricing power, and its offshore cost edge is one every peer shares. The fixed price line and the 3% healthcare line show no moat in the run, and the cannibalisation tests (Categories 21 and 22) scored zero because a larger rival could quote the same offshore centre without giving up any revenue.

Margin turnaround done, cash and growth unproven

Onward is a Rs 544 Cr engineering services firm with 2,917 engineers at September 2026. It sells engineer time to 72 clients, two thirds of it to industrial machinery makers. Each engineer brought in about Rs 21.5 lakh of revenue in FY26. Employee cost takes 72.8% of revenue, against 50% to 59% at Tata Technologies, Cyient and LTTS. Part of that gap may be how peers classify people costs, and the run could not settle it. Onward is the smallest of the four by a wide margin. Its FY26 revenue was a tenth of the Rs 5,506 Cr at Tata Technologies, the nearest peer, so scale gives it no edge.

The change is in margin. EBITDA margin rose from 7.0% in FY22 to 13.2% in FY26. In FY26, EBITDA grew 61% on revenue growth of 10.7%, and revenue per head rose 15%. Growth then picked up: revenue for the half year to September 2026 was Rs 320 Cr, up 17.5%. The September quarter printed a record 15.6% margin. That figure includes a Rs 2.9 Cr UK R&D credit that management says relates to FY26. Without the credit the margin was 13.9%, below the 14.3% of a year earlier. Management credits offshore leverage, but the offshore share of revenue fell from 72% to 69% over the year. The sequential gain is real: margin excluding the credit rose from 12.3% in June to 13.9% in September. One reading says 14% is a new base built on utilisation. The other says the quarter was a peak helped by the credit and a weaker rupee. Margin excluding the credit in the December and March quarters, read with the offshore share, separates the two.

Gate 0, the backward quality scorecard, rates Onward GOOD+. It scores 79 of 100 on the core and 21 of 60 on moats, one point under EXCELLENT. The balance sheet carries the score: no financial borrowings, a current ratio of 3.66 and net cash of Rs 127 Cr at March 2026. ROCE, the return on capital employed, ran 19.8%, just under 15% and 20.1% over FY24 to FY26. ROE was 18.6% in FY26. The weakness is the record behind the score. Balance sheets and cash flows before FY24 are not in the corpus, and neither is the September 2023 shareholding pattern. Demand a full ten year series and the class falls to AVERAGE. Use a shorter promoter holding window and it rises to EXCELLENT. Profit growth of 30% a year also flatters, because it starts from Rs 4 Cr.

FY26 cash looked good. Operating cash flow was Rs 6,508 lakhs, 1.47 times profit, and only 0.14% of gross receivables were more than six months overdue. Underneath, 15% of that cash flow came from pay that was owed but not yet paid. Unbilled revenue grew 13%, faster than revenue at 11%, and the notes give it no ageing and no provision. Days to collect at the subsidiaries rose from 70 to 81, partly from currency. The half year to September 2026 turned that drift into a number. Operating cash flow fell 47% to Rs 1,845 lakhs. It covered about 41% of EBITDA, against 95% a year earlier. Days sales outstanding (DSO) rose from 70 at December 2025 to 80. Net cash fell from Rs 127 Cr to Rs 97 Cr after an Rs 18 Cr buyback and the dividend. Management blames a fast ramp and slow customer approvals, and targets 65 to 70 days within two quarters. Peers ran 78 to 112 days during their own ramps, which supports that reading. The auditor also could not comment on the audit trail in the ledger and billing system, for the second year running.

Management earns a B for credibility. Of 14 promises traced over four quarters, 7 were delivered, 3 in part and 4 missed. The headline guides held. FY26 promised double digit growth and delivered 10.7%. The FY27 guide then rose to 15 to 18% revenue growth and a 13.8 to 14.3% margin, the company's first numeric guide. The misses are operating dates and ratios: DSO, the Chennai AI lab, a client count of 50 that is still 72, and the offshore mix. Management has not said whether the margin guide counts the R&D credit. That gap is worth about 100 basis points of second half margin. It has also not said how much of the growth is currency. The filings contradict the calls in places. Calls say there are no India clients, while the deck shows India at 47% of revenue. Attrition reads 14.85% in one part of the annual report and 37.93% in another. The promoter check rates the Mehta family TRUSTWORTHY, at the lower bound. There is no pledge, the family bought shares at every date since March 2024, and it did not tender into the buyback. Against that, the family's holding fell by about 15 lakh shares between June 2023 and March 2024, and the run did not find the buyer. An independent director's firm billed Rs 9.76 lakhs while the governance report says there is no money relationship. One press source says the audit committee chair's term ended on 20 July 2026, and the run found no successor.

The peer read used eleven transcripts from LTTS, Tata Technologies and Cyient, seven of them in depth, and it complicates the story more than it confirms it. Peers confirm two things. DSO rises and unbilled builds during ramps. Wage rises cost 50 to 100 basis points of margin, which bears on Onward's second half guide. Peers contradict one claim. Management calls Onward the fastest organic grower, but Tata Technologies grew 22.2% in constant currency in the September quarter against Onward's 17.7%. That test covers one quarter, not the three to four year window the claim names. FY26 EBITDA margins were 17.6% at LTTS, 15.5% at Tata Technologies and 12.4% at Cyient, against 13.2% at Onward. All three peers are moving toward fixed price and outcome contracts, and LTTS passes AI productivity gains to clients in its bids. Onward still bills 84 to 85% on time and material. No peer links offshore share to US work permit rules.

The market is large and is not the limit. Outsourced engineering for North American and European OEMs is worth about Rs 9.1 lakh Cr and grows 8.6% a year. The run narrows that to Rs 1.2 lakh Cr for Onward's verticals, but every filter is an assumption, so the true figure could sit anywhere from Rs 0.64 lakh Cr to Rs 2.07 lakh Cr. Onward holds under half a percent of that pool. The market stage rates the runway STRONG. It sees revenue of Rs 856 Cr in three years and Rs 1,079 Cr in five, about 16% and 15% a year. That needs about 400 net hires a year, against 432 in the last six months. The binding limits are client concentration and utilisation, not market size or capacity. Management's own market figure is 30.7 times the vendor pool, because it counts R&D that OEMs do in house. FY27 has two readings: the guide implies Rs 625 Cr to Rs 642 Cr, while the September quarter run rate implies Rs 660 Cr. December quarter revenue against Rs 170 Cr separates them.
