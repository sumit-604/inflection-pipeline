## Where the market stands

Start with the three trend lines under each of about 2,250 stocks. The short
line is the twenty-day average. The middle line is the fifty-day average. The
long line is the two-hundred-day average. On Tuesday 902 stocks stood above
the long line, four in ten. 666 stood above the middle line, about three in
ten. 670 stood above the short line, also about three in ten. All three
counts rose for the third day in a row. The short count gained the most, about
100 stocks in one day. The long count gained about 25.

The long count matters most tonight, because it passed its test. On 1 October
it had fallen to 901. On Thursday 8 October it broke to 835. Since then it
climbed 857, 876 and now 902. It stands one stock above the 1 October mark.
That is a pass, and it is the thinnest pass possible. The level on 7 October,
the day before the break, was about 965. So the count has made back about half
of what Thursday took. It has not made back all of it.

Put that in a longer story. On 7 August about 1,370 stocks held the long line,
the high of this history. On 23 March only about 300 did, the low. The market
fell from the August top through September. The 15 September shock pushed
about 350 stocks down four and a half percent in a day. The 8 October shock
pushed about 310 down. Each time the long count dropped and then crept back.
Tonight it sits a little past halfway from the March low to the August
high. Most stocks remain in long downtrends. The decline is serious. It is far
from a March.

The day itself was broad. About six stocks in ten closed higher, against
about half on Monday. 101 stocks rose four and a half percent or more, and 28
fell that much. That ratio is better than Monday's. The count of big risers
is lower. A thrust day, the kind that marks a real turn, looks different in
this history. On 1 April more than 1,100 stocks jumped. On 8 April almost 900
did. On 12 June about 340 did. Tuesday had about 100. It was a good ordinary
day, not a thrust.

Inside the day the buyers held more and the sellers pressed less. About one

did. About one stock in eight traded four percent below its prior close. Only

## What would change this read

The read tonight is a broad, quiet repair that has just reached its 1 October
level. It turns worse if the long count falls back below 900 and then below
about 835, Thursday's low. It turns worse if new yearly lows climb back above
about 200, or if the deep down tail grows past about 15 names. A third shock
day with about 300 hard fallers would match the September and October pattern
and open the way to a lower low. A VIX back above about 15 while the Nifty
falls would say the index worry has returned. The middle count slipping back
under about 620 would undo Monday's passed test.

The cautious read fails if the repair gets volume and follow through. The
first test is a thrust day of about 300 or more stocks jumping four and a half
percent, on turnover well above its year-ago level. The second is the long
count holding above 900 and climbing toward about 965, the level the day
before the 8 October break. The third is new yearly lows staying under 100
while the VIX closes under 14. A second strong day for IT and banks, with the
sugar, chemicals and defence groups holding, would support all three. Until
the thrust or the volume arrives, the data favours patience over hope.
