## SECTION 1: THE BUSINESS MODEL IN PLAIN ENGLISH

### 1A. One-line description
Aarti Surfactants makes the surface-active chemical ingredients (surfactants, mild surfactants, UV blockers, preservatives, pearlising agents, blends) that FMCG companies mix into shampoos, soaps, detergents, dishwash liquids and skin/hair-care products — it does not sell to end consumers, it sells to the companies that make the products consumers buy (AR FY2020-21 p.4, p.10).

### 1B. Money flow chain (by revenue stream)

**Domestic sale of surfactants/specialty ingredients**
[Palm/fatty-alcohol-derived raw materials + ethylene oxide-type feedstock, per Cost of Materials Consumed note] → [company reacts/blends/formulates at Pithampur (MP) and Silvassa plants to customer specification] → [delivers bulk/drummed product to Indian FMCG manufacturers] → [FMCG manufacturer's procurement department pays] → [normal trade credit terms, receivables mostly collected within 6 months — 5,433.27 of 5,443.20 lakh trade receivables at less-than-six-months, AR FY2020-21 p.70, Note 5].

**Export sale of surfactants/specialty ingredients**
[Same manufacturing base] → [same production process] → [delivers to FMCG manufacturers across Asia, Europe, Africa and the Americas] → [overseas customer pays] → [trade credit, in foreign currency, export benefits/incentives booked separately] (AR FY2020-21 p.75, Note 17; Inv. Pres. slide 5-6).

**Other operating revenue (export incentives, scrap sales, state government industry-promotion grant)**
[By-product of core operations / policy incentive] → [no separate production process] → [booked on realisation/claim admission] → [cash or credit] (AR FY2020-21 p.75, Note 17.1).

### 1E. The chai-stall-uncle version

Think of a chai-stall that doesn't sell tea to you directly — it sells the tea leaves, sugar and milk powder in bulk to every other chai stall in the market. Aarti Surfactants doesn't make the shampoo or dishwash liquid you buy; it makes the "foaming, cleaning, softening" chemical ingredient that goes inside the bottle, and sells that in bulk to the big FMCG companies who bottle and brand the final product. Its raw material bill (mostly fatty-alcohol and oleochemical derivatives) eats about three-quarters of every rupee of revenue, so if those raw material prices jump and it can't pass the increase on fast enough, profit gets squeezed hard and fast — which is exactly what happened in the year to March 2025, when revenue grew 12% but profit fell 32% (AR FY2020-21 p.75; Inv. Pres. slide 7). It sells to a small number of big buyers (two of them were more than a third of FY2020-21 revenue) so losing even one is a real risk, and new competitors keep entering the space, so it has to keep proving itself on quality, certifications and reliability rather than relying on a strong brand — because, unlike the shampoo brand on the shelf, nobody outside the industry has ever heard of Aarti Surfactants.

### 2D. Industry lifecycle stage
The global surfactants market is projected to grow from USD 42.1bn (2020) to USD 52.4bn (2025), a 4.5% CAGR (source: Markets and Markets Research, cited AR FY2020-21 p.9) — this is a mature, steadily growing (not high-growth, not declining) industry. The company itself has compounded sales at a 14% CAGR over six years per its own disclosure (Inv. Pres. slide 4), i.e. growing faster than the underlying global market, likely via India/domestic-market share gains and mix shift rather than industry tailwind alone (unconfirmed — driver breakdown NOT FOUND, check investor presentation or concall). Aarti Surfactants sits as a mid-size, two-plant domestic-plus-export player within a mature, fragmenting-competitive industry.
