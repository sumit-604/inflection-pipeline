### PART A: THE FROM STATE

**A1. Archetype (per line).**
- Drilling, seismic and aquifer services (the coal segment books nil revenue, Results_Q2FY27 p.11, B00): ORDER-BOOK BUSINESS. Rig capacity fill, order inflow, book-to-bill, execution pace and working capital decide it (B04 must-track list). The Reliance CBM line carries Outsourcing-partner traits (wallet share, capacity fill, contract stickiness) (B04, B05).
- Jharkhand coal block (pre-production, 84 MT, 24.25% government revenue share): LICENCE/SCARCITY BUSINESS, held as an option with no stated capex or funding (B04, B05, B07).

**A2. The simple analogy.** The company rents out drilling crews and survey teams, with their machines, to people who need to know what is underground or underfoot. The client pays by the metre or by the project. Think of a specialist contractor who owns the heavy tools, hires the crews, and wins work by bidding against others. When the order book is full and the crews are busy, the contractor earns well. When crews wait for a site, rain, or a payment, the cost of keeping them still falls on the contractor. The firm also holds a mining licence that has not yet produced a tonne. Today three customers pay 72.27% of the money coming in, and the firm does not name them (B02).

### PART B: THE TRANSITION

**B1. FROM to TO (quality ladder).**

## SECTION 3: BUSINESS UNDERSTANDING NARRATIVE (draft from B01 to B09; stage 13 holds the final version)

South West Pinnacle drills holes and runs surveys for clients who must know what lies underground. Coal bed methane production drilling gave 37.19% of FY26 operating revenue; the crews drill and maintain production wells and dewater coal seams so methane flows (B04). Aquifer mapping for government agencies gave 24.93%, non-coal mineral exploration drilling gave 17.21% and seismic work gave 16.13% (B04). Core drilling matters because it returns the core samples that miners need to prove an ore body (B04). Customers are Reliance Industries on CBM, Hindustan Zinc, Vedanta, Hindalco and Hindustan Copper on minerals, and CMPDI, the Central Ground Water Board and Oil India on government work (B04, B09). Three customers gave 72.27% of FY26 revenue, up from 48.00%, and the company does not name them (B02). Public work goes through tenders, switching cost is low, and the AR itself lists tender rate pressure as a weakness (B04). The order book is 77% private on the deck against a credit note that calls the major customers public-sector, and the run did not establish which is right (B04, B03). Demand today rests on the Reliance CBM production programme, Hindustan Zinc exploration drilling and CMPDI outsourced drilling tenders, with Oil India and ONGC onshore seismic orders behind them (B09). Demand should grow with the Ministry of Coal exploration scheme FY27-31, NMET approvals and mineral block auctions, and CGWB NAQUIM 2.0 tenders, which must replace aquifer work that is running off; the NAQUIM 2.0 budget was not found (B09). The central path gives 14.3% revenue growth a year to FY29, and a second reading that rests on one unverified report on the coal scheme gives 22.8% (B09). The moat is narrow: the emerging moat scan finds two Moderate categories, qualification lock-in and customer ecosystem, and scores 12.8, MODEST (B07). In coal and geology work, the Ministry of Coal and NABET accreditations are term-bound and others can apply (B04). The seismic line holds a three-year Oil India empanelment and nothing else (B07). The CBM and mineral lines hold repeat contracts with Reliance and Hindustan Zinc without evidence of rate-setting power (B04, B07). Aquifer mapping has no moat (B07). The run did not establish the names of the top three customers, rig utilisation, or cost per metre (B04).
