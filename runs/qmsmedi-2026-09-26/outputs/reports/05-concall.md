### 1C Trigger evolution across quarters

- **Q-Devices**: STRENGTHENING then WEAKENING then quiet reset. Q1 FY26 call: Rs 10 Cr in one quarter, target raised to "at least Rs 25 Cr" for the full year, management called its own target "conservative." Full FY26 actual (per Q1 FY27 call, in passing): Rs 14 Cr — a 44% miss against the Rs 25 Cr floor, and roughly flat with the Rs 10 Cr already banked in Q1 FY26, implying the brand went almost dormant for three quarters. No call between Q2 FY26 and Q1 FY27 acknowledges this miss directly; the goalpost was quietly redefined from an absolute rupee number to a "10-15% of product revenue" share metric (Q1 FY27 call). This is a DROPPED/DILUTED metric, not a resolved miss.
- **PSP / Saarathi revenue**: STRENGTHENING. Target moved from "at least Rs 60 Cr" combined services FY26 (Q1 FY26 call) to actual ~Rs 53.9 Cr FY26 (per B00 segment read), then to a much larger FY27 ask: Rs 90-105 Cr from PSP alone plus Rs 18-20 Cr camps. The ramp assumption is steep and rests on GLP-1-linked contracts signed but not yet billed at scale.
- **Camps revenue reporting**: WEAKENING credibility, not the trigger itself. The rupee figure for camps revenue has been inconsistent twice in three quarters: Q4 FY26 call, an analyst (Nish Shah) flagged that the investor presentation showed ~Rs 16 Cr camps revenue for 9 months FY26 while management's full-year figure was Rs 13 Cr — unresolved on the call ("we will send you the details"). Q1 FY27 call, the Finance Team live-corrected a presentation figure of Rs 13.68 Cr camps revenue down to Rs 6.4 Cr, calling the original number "misprinted." Two consecutive quarters of unreconciled camps-revenue arithmetic is a data-quality flag, independent of the underlying growth story.
- **Margin normalization**: repeatedly promised, repeatedly pushed out. Q2 FY26 call (Nov-2025): margins to normalize "in another year's time," management explicitly declined to commit to a 20% number. Q4 FY26 call (Jun-2026): FY26 margin came in at ~15% against a previously implied ~17%, guided FY27 at 18-19%. Q1 FY27 call (Aug-2026): delivered margin 14.6%, still below the 18% guide, management says "too early to revise" the guidance rather than walking it back. TIMELINE SLIPPING, one quarter at a time.
- **NEW trigger, why now**: GLP-1 (Semaglutide) launch tailwind first appears in the Q4 FY26 call and is expanded in the Q1 FY27 call. This coincides with the actual Indian patent expiry and generic launch of Semaglutide, so the timing is externally dated and plausible, not an invented narrative.
- **DROPPED**: non-pharma camps expansion (corporates, co-working spaces, government programmes), raised as a growth avenue in the Q1 FY26 call, never mentioned again in the two subsequent calls. Also dropped: the "B2B space" inorganic discussions flagged in the Q1 FY26 call ("there are other discussions already in the B2B space also happening right now") — no follow-up in any later call.
- **Personnel change, unexplained**: Tanay Surkund (Head of Institutional Business & Special Projects), a named management speaker on the Q1 FY26 and Q2 FY26 calls, is absent from the Q4 FY26 and Q1 FY27 management rosters with no announcement or explanation on any call. Flag for stage 3 (management/governance) to check against Reg 30 filings for a resignation.

### 2B Excuse pattern analysis

- Margin miss (Q2 FY26 call): EXTERNAL-BLAME + SELF-CREDIT MIX — "pricing discount structures on camps... we have invested heavily in new people and new infrastructure." Both an external factor (competitive discounting) and a self-inflicted one (investment) are named in the same breath; management does not separate which drove more of the miss.
- Product revenue slowdown H2 FY26 (Q4 FY26 call): EXTERNAL-BLAME — "disruptions in delay in orders also in delay in supply of materials," described as a supply-chain/shipping issue. Specific enough to be checkable (stage 6 should verify against ENTERO and POLYMED transcripts, which sit in the same distribution chain).
- Employee cost overshoot (Q1 FY27 call): DEFLECTION-ADJACENT — reframed as a structural feature of cost-plus contracts rather than addressing why the Q4 FY26 framing ("leverage coming") did not hold.
- SEBI/IPO-fund-misallocation article (Q2 FY26 call): HONEST-ADMISSION / PROACTIVE — management answered a hard, reputational question directly and with specifics (correspondence dates, auditor sign-off, subsequent rights-issue approval) rather than deflecting. This is a genuine positive data point on transparency, separate from the underlying governance risk itself (which stage 3/8 must still verify independently).
- Pattern check: management credits itself for growth (revenue, camp counts, contract wins) more readily than it owns misses (Q-Devices target, camps revenue inconsistency). It DOES proactively address a reputational risk when asked directly (SEBI article) — a genuine "we have nothing to hide" moment, the single clearest example of accountability in the transcript set. It does NOT say "we made a mistake" on the Q-Devices miss, the margin guidance miss, or the camps revenue error; each is absorbed into a forward-looking reframe instead.

### 3B Industry and market intelligence

- Global PSP market sized at "$70 billion" (Q1 FY26 call, repeated implicitly through the "one-stop shop" framing in later calls) — never re-cited with a source; peer stage should check INDGN (which operates in the same commercialisation space) for an independent market-size figure.

- PSP customer concentration: "most of the programs that we are running currently are with Top 20 or Top 30 companies" (Q1 FY27 call, Mohit Tamhankar) — a direct concentration admission, useful for risk assessment.
- Contract stickiness: "60% to 70%" rollover of PSP contracts year-on-year over "last 4 or 5 years" (Q1 FY27 call).
- Price escalation clauses: 5-7% to 12% annual, embedded in agreements (Q1 FY27 call).
- GLP-1/Semaglutide: patent on the molecule "has actually lost patent... a lot of Indian generic companies... come in" (Q1 FY27 call) — externally verifiable, dated event underpinning the PSP growth narrative.
