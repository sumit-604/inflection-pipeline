### 2B. Excuse pattern analysis

The single clear miss (#2 above, the Q4 margin compression) is explained with a **consistent
external-blame narrative**: AI-hardware/GPU demand from "AI factories" has tightened the global
technology supply chain and pushed up component costs; management calls this "short-term," "a
temporary blip," "normal quarterly variations... rather than any structural change," repeated in
near-identical language to four separate Q4 FY26 analysts (Ankush Agrawal, Aditya, Saurabh Gupta,
Dhirendra Patro). This is externally corroborated at the industry level (AI-driven hardware
demand is a real, widely reported 2026 phenomenon) but management never volunteers any
Dynacons-specific mitigation metric (e.g., how much margin was clawed back, timeline to
normalize) beyond directional reassurance.

Pattern check:
- Do they blame externals while crediting themselves for wins? Partially — wins (RBI, NABARD,
  Punjab & Sind Bank) are framed as pure execution strength; the one miss is pure external blame.
  No internal execution factor is ever cited as a contributor to the Q4 dip.
- Do they ever say "we made a mistake"? No instance found in either call.
- Do they raise hard topics proactively or only under analyst pressure? Mixed. The Q4 margin dip
  is raised proactively at the top of the Q4 call (Dharmesh Anjaria's opening remarks) rather than
  only surfacing under questioning — a point in their favour. But the receivables/WC deterioration
  and the DC/cloud mix dip are only addressed once analysts raise them directly.

Overall classification: **external-blame-heavy on the single material miss, but proactive
disclosure of that miss and otherwise consistent, non-defensive messaging elsewhere.**

### 2D. What they are NOT saying

- **Unit economics of the As-a-Service/opex pivot** (IRR, ROCE, payback, gross margin) — never
  quantified despite three separate analysts asking across both calls; one investor (Ankush
  Agrawal, Q4 FY26 call) states outright: "it would be very difficult for us to judge" the new
  model's quality without this. Likely reason for silence: either the economics are less
  favourable than the legacy EPC model, or the figures are considered competitively sensitive.
- **Reconciliation of the "17-day net working capital" claim against gross debtor days.**
  Management never volunteers the gross (Acuité-reported) debtor-day figure and frames the metric
  only net of OEM/distributor payable credit. Likely reason: net framing looks materially better
  than gross framing.
- **BHEL order status** — flagged in Q3 FY26 as a live new contributor, never mentioned again in
  Q4 FY26. Likely reason: either immaterial/on-track (routine, not newsworthy) or execution has
  stalled; the transcripts give no basis to distinguish.
- **Named competitor identification** — in Q3 FY26, management engaged directly when an analyst
  named a competitor (Unified Data, citing its 15-20% margins); in Q4 FY26, when asked directly
  "whom do you consider as your nearest competitor," management declined to name any. A retreat
  in specificity on competitive positioning.
- **Any post-4-May-2026 order (e.g., a Central Bank of India AI-ready private-cloud/NVIDIA H200
  order reportedly dated 8-Jun-2026 per the operator digest)** is absent from the Q4 FY26 call
  transcript — but this is a timing artefact (the call was held 2-Jun-2026, before that date), not

## SECTION 3: COMPETITIVE INTELLIGENCE FROM CONCALLS

### 3A. Competitor commentary and credibility check

- **Q3 FY26 call:** analyst Madhur Rathi directly names Unified Data (a soon-to-list competitor
  citing Dynacons in its RHP) and states it earns 15-20% margins vs Dynacons' then-11.9%.
  Management does not dispute the margin gap; it responds by pointing to the rising DC/cloud mix
  (14%→37%) as the path to closing it. Credibility check: candid, non-defensive engagement with an
  unfavourable comparison — a positive tone signal.
- **Q4 FY26 call:** analyst Akash Jain asks directly who Dynacons considers its nearest
  competitor; management declines to name anyone ("I would rather not name them on a public
  platform"), falling back to generic claims of competing successfully against "companies
  significantly larger than us." Credibility check: less transparent than the Q3 FY26 exchange;
  no verifiable specifics offered.
- Recurring claim across both calls: Dynacons wins against larger competitors on the strength of
  pre-qualifications, nationwide delivery track record, and CMMI Level 5/ISO certifications. This
  is asserted, not evidenced with named win/loss data — a claim for peer verification (see 4B).

### 3B. Industry and market intelligence

- India IT spending "expected to exceed USD176 billion by 2026" (Q4 FY26 call, Parag Dalal,
  management view, uncited source).
- India's data-centre capacity "expected to grow significantly over the coming years" (Q4 FY26
  call, management view).
- Historical bid-to-win conversion ratio ~30% on the bidding pipeline, reaffirmed in Q4 FY26 call
  as still broadly intact despite scale growth.
- Global technology supply chain "severely hit" by AI-factory/hyperscaler demand, driving hardware
  (server/chipset/memory) price increases — raised in both calls (Q3 FY26: Madhur Rathi Q&A on
  RBI order pass-through; Q4 FY26: repeated across four analyst exchanges as the cause of the
  margin miss).
- Government directive pushing the BFSI sector to reassess cybersecurity requirements, cited as
  driving new pipeline (Q4 FY26 call, Dev Shah Q&A) — a fresh regulatory-driven demand signal not
  present in the Q3 FY26 call.
- No seasonality in order book by quarter any longer ("erstwhile" budget-driven seasonality from
  banks/PSUs no longer applies) — Q3 FY26 call, Binoy Jariwala Q&A.
- Order execution/billing pattern: revenue recognized upfront through go-live (typically 6-12
  months), balance billed over an O&M period (multi-year, sometimes 5 years); go-live vs O&M
  revenue split roughly "60:40 to 70:30" depending on project (Q3 FY26 call).
