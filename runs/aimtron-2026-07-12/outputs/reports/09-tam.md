## SECTION 1: MARKET DEFINITION

### 1A — Precise boundaries

**Product scope (in):** Contract PCB design and assembly (PCBA), Box Build (full electromechanical/system assembly), and ODM/end-to-end design services — the three segments Aimtron itself reports (FY26 standalone mix: PCBA 28.6%, Box Build 68.8%, ODM 2.6%; source: Investor Presentation H2&FY26, p.33).

**Product scope (out):** Bare/raw PCB fabrication (Aimtron assembles, does not fabricate bare boards — AR industry section discusses the bare-PCB market only as market context, not as a business Aimtron is in); semiconductor wafer fabrication/packaging; pure component distribution; high-volume consumer/mobile-phone mass assembly (a structurally different competitive segment served by Foxconn/Tata Electronics/Dixon-scale players, not Aimtron's certification-led, high-mix low-to-medium-volume model).

**Geographic scope:** India (74.3% of FY26 standalone revenue; facilities in Vadodara and Bengaluru) plus a US manufacturing/sales footprint via two wholly-owned subsidiaries — Aimtron Electronics LLC (Texas, est. Jan 2025) and Aimtron International Controls LLC / ICS (Decatur, Illinois, acquired Jan 2026) — contributing USA 11.1% + North America 7.9% of FY26 standalone revenue (some overlap likely in these two categorisations; source: Investor Presentation p.33). Nascent Europe (Spain 3.3%, Australia 1.6%, Others 1.8%) post India-EU FTA entry (Investor Presentation p.31, AR p.14 growth strategy). (Source: Investor Presentation H2&FY26, p.33.)

**Customer scope:** B2B OEM/ODM contract-manufacturing customers only, across medical devices/healthcare, automotive/EV/BMS, aerospace & defence, industrial automation, telecom & network security, IoT/robotics, railways, power/energy, and gaming verticals (100+ customers globally per Investor Presentation p.5). Explicitly excludes B2C.

**Channel scope:** Direct OEM/ODM contract relationships; no distributor/retail channel.

**Price/certification segment:** Certified, high-mix, precision-engineering segment gated by ISO 13485 (medical), IATF 16949 (automotive), AS9100D (aerospace & defence), CDSCO (medtech), and RDSO (in progress, railways) — not the commodity, high-volume, low-certification segment. (Source: Investor Presentation p.17; AR p.74 notes AS9100 "in process" as of FY24-25, shown as achieved by H2FY26 presentation — certification stack has advanced during the year.)

**Inclusions:** PCBA + Box Build + ODM/design revenue across the seven-plus verticals above, in India and via the US subsidiary structure.
**Exclusions:** Bare PCB fabrication, semiconductor fab/packaging, mobile-phone/consumer mass assembly, distribution-channel electronics trading.

A wrong definition makes every later number useless — this boundary explicitly removes the single largest sub-segment of "India EMS" (consumer/mobile assembly, ~39% of Indian EMS demand per Section 2) from the very first step, because Aimtron does not compete there.

Sourced by the company to IBEF, SICCI, MEitY, ET Government, Policy Circle — no single primary date given; this is the long-standing national electronics-manufacturing-output target (Government of India / National Policy on Electronics family of targets), reiterated without a fresh primary citation. Exact original publication date: NOT FOUND (AR text separately cites a related interim milestone — "production expected to reach USD 300 billion by 2026" — footnoted to a April-2025 PIB release, AR p.72-73, footnote 14).

**Credibility read: BROAD.** The Rs43.1 lakh crore / $500bn figure is India's *entire* national electronics manufacturing output target — it includes mobile-phone assembly, IT hardware, consumer electronics/appliances, semiconductors, and lighting, most of which Aimtron does not address per the 1A product-scope exclusions. It is not Aimtron's addressable market; it is the macro backdrop the company is citing to imply runway. This is quantified and flagged in Section 2's triangulation.

## SECTION 2: TAM ESTIMATION, MULTIPLE METHODS

### Method 1 — Top-down (primary, used as the conservative/realistic anchor)

Step 1: India EMS market size, FY25. Independent sources diverge materially:
- KPMG (report dated June 2026, most recent, big-4 methodology): India EMS market "~$40-45bn in FY25, growing to $150bn by FY30" (WebSearch: KPMG "India's Electronic Manufacturing Services (EMS) opportunity," kpmg.com, 2026-06).
- PS Market Research: India EMS market value "$65.0bn in 2025," growing to $197.8bn by 2032 at 17.5% CAGR (WebSearch, psmarketresearch.com, current).
- Aimtron's own AR (FY24-25, citing SICCI): India's EMS market "hit around USD 80 billion in 2024," projected to $186bn by 2035 at ~7.9% CAGR (AR_2025.txt, p.70-71, footnote 6/7, sourced to sicci.in report).

These three credible sources span $40bn to $80bn for essentially the same period — a ~2x divergence, flagged rather than averaged, per instructions. **Conservative bias: KPMG's $40bn FY25 lower bound is used as the primary anchor** (most recent, big-4 sourced, most conservative); PSMR's $65bn and AR's $80bn are noted as upper alternatives not adopted.

Step 2: Convert to INR. $40bn x Rs84 = **Rs3,36,000cr** (India EMS market, all segments, FY25, conservative).

Step 3: Subtract non-relevant segment — consumer electronics/mobile-phone assembly, which dominated India EMS demand at **38.79% in 2025** (Business Research Insights, WebSearch, current) and which Aimtron's product scope (1A) explicitly excludes. Non-consumer/B2B remainder = 61.21%.
Rs3,36,000cr x 61.21% = **Rs2,05,646cr ≈ Rs2,05,600cr — TAM (conservative)**.

Step 4: Realistic band using KPMG's $45bn FY25 upper bound: Rs3,78,000cr x 61.21% = **Rs2,31,394cr ≈ Rs2,31,000cr — TAM (realistic)**.

Confidence: **M** (multiple independent, current sources for the base market size; the 38.79% consumer-exclusion factor is a single-source approximation, one methodological weak point). Staleness: current (KPMG June 2026, BRI 2025).

### Method 2 — Bottom-up (vertical sum; used as a sanity-check upper bound, not adopted)

Summing India end-market sizes for Aimtron's target verticals (note: these are end-market/component-value sizes, not EMS-outsourcing-specific — no outsourcing-penetration discount applied, so this method structurally overstates true EMS TAM):
- Medical device contract manufacturing, India: $5,701.7mn (2024) → **Rs47,900cr** (Grand View Research, WebSearch; this one is genuinely EMS/CM-specific, best-fit input in this method).
- Aerospace & defence electronics (assumed 15-20% of total India A&D market of $30.72bn in 2025, an unverified assumption for the avionics/electronics content fraction): $4.6-6.1bn → **Rs38,640-51,240cr**, midpoint ≈ **Rs44,940cr**.
- Telecom equipment manufacturing, India: Rs50,000cr cumulative production over 3 years of the Telecom PLI scheme (PIB, WebSearch); annualised (assumption: even 3-year split) ≈ **Rs16,667cr/yr**.
- Industrial electronics: ~11% of India's total electronics production of $144bn (FY26, WebSearch) → $15.84bn → **Rs1,33,056cr**.
- Automotive electronics, India market: $12.6bn (2025) → **Rs1,05,840cr** (PS Market Research, WebSearch).

**Sum ≈ Rs3,48,400cr.**

This is ~1.5-1.7x Method 1's conservative-to-realistic band (Rs2,05,600-2,31,000cr). The divergence is explained, not averaged away: Method 2 sums full end-market electronics value including in-house OEM manufacture that is never outsourced to an EMS provider, whereas Method 1 is already an EMS-industry-specific figure. Confidence: **L**. Not adopted as the TAM anchor; used only to confirm Method 1's order of magnitude is not wildly conservative.

### Method 3 — Peer revenue aggregation (directional floor only)

Listed organised EMS peers with overlapping verticals (auto/industrial/medical/aerospace-defence/telecom, non-mobile):
- Kaynes Technology, FY26: **Rs3,626cr** (WebSearch, current).
- Syrma SGS Technology, FY25: **Rs3,836.1cr** (verified directly — an earlier search result of Rs9,043cr was found to be erroneous and was corrected via a second, source-specific search; FY26 guided ~+23% → ~Rs4,718cr estimated).
- Avalon Technologies, FY25: **Rs1,098cr** (WebSearch).
- Cyient DLM, FY25: **Rs1,520cr**; FY26 guided to *decline* ~13.6% → ~Rs1,313cr estimated (WebSearch).
- Aimtron itself, FY26 consolidated: **Rs301.2cr**.

Subtotal (5-6 listed peers, mixed FY25/FY26 vintage) ≈ **Rs11,056cr**. This deliberately excludes hundreds of private/unlisted EMS companies (e.g., Vinyas, Elin Electronics, SFO Technologies, VVDN, Centum Electronics, Astra Microwave, PCB Power) and the unorganised MSME EMS base, plus global players' India units (Foxconn, Flex, Jabil) serving overlapping accounts. Per the pipeline's own standard assumption that 30-60% of Indian manufacturing sub-sectors is unorganised, and given the listed set here is a small fraction of the organised universe too, this method structurally *undershoots*. Confidence: **L**, used only as a floor/sanity check — it is directionally consistent with (does not contradict) Method 1's much larger figure once organised-non-listed and unorganised layers are added back, but does not independently confirm the specific number.

### Method 4 — Import substitution (supporting context, not a standalone TAM)

- Rs22,919cr PLI scheme for passive components (AR_2025.txt p.14, Chairman's letter, sourced to PIB) — unlocking opportunities in telecom, EV, medical devices, power electronics; directly relevant to Aimtron's stated backward-integration strategy.
- Electronics Components Manufacturing Scheme (ECMS): Rs40,000cr (Investor Presentation p.41, sourced PIB/Invest India/PwC) — the scheme Aimtron Mechatronics has applied under for its optical-transceiver/SFP project (Investor Presentation p.22; approval pending as of the report date, no revenue figure disclosed for this specific sub-project — NOT FOUND).
- Telecom PLI: Rs50,000cr cumulative production over 3 years, Rs10,500cr exports (PIB, WebSearch).
- Bare PCB import dependence: ~$5bn market, 88% import-dependent (source: Avendus/Circuit Digest, WebSearch; publication date NOT FOUND, flagged for staleness risk) → ~Rs42,000cr import-substitution opportunity, adjacent to but outside Aimtron's current product scope (1A) given it does not fabricate bare boards, though relevant to its disclosed "backward integration" ambitions (AR p.14: "new cable assembly lines, added SMT capacity, and in-house component manufacturing").

These are government scheme-size and import-dependency figures, not an independent TAM estimate; they corroborate that policy tailwinds are real and roughly Rs20,000-50,000cr sized per scheme, reinforcing (not adding to) Method 1's growth-driver case.

### Method 5 — Global benchmark

Global EMS market: $577.32bn (CY2024, Precedence Research, per AR_2025.txt p.70, graph data) vs $648.11bn (2025, Fortune Business Insights, independent WebSearch) — two independent sources within ~12% of each other, reasonable cross-confirmation. India's EMS market ($40-80bn depending on source) is roughly 6-14% of the global figure, consistent with the independently-sourced "India currently accounts for only about 5-6% of global EMS manufacturing" (WebSearch). If India's global EMS share were to rise from ~5-6% toward a still-conservative 12-15% (well short of China's dominant incumbent share, itself NOT FOUND precisely in this run) over the next decade, India's EMS market would imply **≈$78-97bn (~Rs6.6-8.1 lakh crore)** at the 2025 global market size — i.e., roughly in line with, not materially exceeding, the KPMG FY30 $150bn (~Rs12.6 lakh crore) trajectory already embedded in Method 1's growth-rate assumption. This benchmark corroborates rather than expands the headline range. Confidence: **L**, long-dated (2030+ horizon), directional only.

## SECTION 3: SAM & SOM

### 3A — SAM (five filters applied to TAM conservative, Rs2,05,600cr)

1. **Product fit** (PCBA + Box Build + ODM vs. the fuller EMS category which also includes cable/harness-only work, test-only services, and component-distribution-model EMS that Aimtron does not do as standalone lines of business): retain **65%** → Rs1,33,640cr.
2. **Geography** (India-serviceable footprint plus the disclosed US subsidiary nexus; a modest cut for demand in Indian regions/logistics corridors outside Aimtron's Vadodara/Bengaluru-anchored reach): retain **90%** → Rs1,20,276cr.
3. **Channel** (direct OEM/ODM contract vs. distributor-channel EMS, a small share of this market and not Aimtron's model): retain **95%** → Rs1,14,262cr.
4. **Customer** (mid-market OEM programme sizes realistically targetable by a Rs301cr-revenue company, excluding both mega-programmes requiring Flex/Jabil/Foxconn-scale balance sheets and the smallest unorganised jobs below Aimtron's certification-driven cost floor): retain **50%** → Rs57,131cr.
5. **Capability** (certification-gated verticals Aimtron actually holds or is pursuing — ISO 13485, IATF 16949, AS9100D, CDSCO, RDSO-in-progress — excluding sub-segments requiring certifications it lacks, e.g. full semiconductor-grade packaging or hyperscale data-centre EMS): retain **85%** → Rs48,561cr.

**SAM ≈ Rs48,500cr. SAM as % of TAM (conservative) ≈ 23.6%.**

Current SAM share = Rs301.2cr (FY26 consolidated revenue) ÷ Rs48,500cr = **0.62%**.

### 3B — SOM at 3 and 5 years

Share-gain rules applied from a current base of 0.62% of SAM:
- Normal case (1-2pp gain in 3 years): implies SOM3yr share of 1.62-2.62% of SAM → Rs786-1,271cr.
- Aggressive case (3-5pp gain in 5 years, "with capacity and execution"): implies SOM5yr share of 3.62-5.62% of SAM → Rs1,756-2,726cr.

**SOM3yr is anchored at Rs950cr** (1.96% of SAM, +1.34pp gain) — within the normal 1-2pp band, and cross-checked against the capacity ceiling in 3C below, where it fits comfortably.

**SOM5yr is anchored at Rs1,300cr** (2.68% of SAM, +2.06pp gain) — this sits *below* the aggressive-case share-gain band (which would imply Rs1,756-2,726cr), because the disclosed capacity ceiling (3C) caps it lower. Physical capacity, not addressable market share, is the binding constraint at the 5-year horizon given currently disclosed capex plans.

Arithmetic, implied revenue CAGR from FY26 base (Rs301.2cr consolidated):
- Yr3: (950 / 301.2)^(1/3) - 1 = 3.154^0.333 - 1 ≈ **46.6%**
- Yr5: (1,300 / 301.2)^(1/5) - 1 = 4.316^0.20 - 1 ≈ **34.0%**

### 3C — Capacity cross-check (against B07's capex-embedded-growth figure)

B07 discloses: India capacity target Rs1,000cr (Rs500cr existing + Rs500cr greenfield Vadodara/Mechatronics facility, ~6 additional SMT lines, targeted commissioning end-FY27); this represents "~331% of current revenue (asserted-capacity-derived, not audited)." Separately, the ICS US subsidiary is guided to USD 25-30mn p.a. (~Rs280-300cr) at 90% utilisation, ramping from ~54% today over ~3 years (Investor Presentation p.13).

Combined disclosed capacity ceiling ≈ Rs1,000cr (India, at full utilisation, once Vadodara ramps post end-FY27 commissioning) + Rs280-300cr (US, at 90% utilisation, ~3-year ramp) = **~Rs1,290-1,300cr**.

- SOM3yr (Rs950cr) sits comfortably under the India capacity target alone and is consistent with a partial-year Vadodara ramp plus a partial US ramp — **capacity sufficient**.
- SOM5yr (Rs1,300cr) sits at **almost exactly the combined disclosed capacity ceiling, with effectively zero margin of safety**. Any slippage in Vadodara commissioning (beyond end-FY27) or a slower-than-guided US utilisation ramp directly caps SOM5yr below Rs1,300cr.

**Which side is optimistic:** the capacity plan itself carries flagged credibility risk (B07): Aimtron's own disclosed productivity assumption of ~Rs100cr revenue per SMT line is far below peer Vinyas's disclosed Rs500-600cr/line, and no independently audited total Vadodara project cost has been disclosed. Two readings are both plausible and cannot be resolved with the evidence in hand: (a) if Aimtron's true achievable per-line productivity is closer to peer benchmarks (given its Box Build-heavy, higher-value mix), the Rs1,000cr India capacity figure could be *conservative*, and the true ceiling higher than SOM5yr requires — in which case SOM5yr is the more cautious number; or (b) if the Rs100cr/line figure reflects Aimtron's genuine current productivity (a function of product mix, automation level, and customer programme size, not directly comparable to Vinyas), the capacity target itself may be the optimistic side, and SOM5yr's "sufficient, zero-margin" read is generous. **This is flagged explicitly, not resolved** — the underlying capacity number is asserted, not audited.

**capacity_check: sufficient but tight — SOM5yr equals the combined disclosed capacity ceiling (~Rs1,290-1,300cr) with no margin; the ceiling itself carries credibility risk per B07 (unverified per-line productivity, no audited total project cost).**

### 4C — Market structure

- **Fragmented overall, concentrated at the top.** Hundreds of EMS providers across India span from MSME job-shops to a handful of listed leaders (Kaynes, Syrma SGS, Dixon, Amber, Avalon, Cyient DLM) plus large global players' India operations (Foxconn, Flex, Jabil, Tata Electronics) dominating the high-volume consumer/mobile segment that Aimtron does not compete in.
- **Top-3 concentration:** at the listed-peer level (Kaynes, Syrma SGS, Dixon) concentration is meaningful within the organised non-mobile segment; overall India EMS (including mobile assembly) is more concentrated still, given mobile-phone assembly scale.
- **Organised vs unorganised split:** typical India manufacturing sub-sector unorganised share of 30-60% is applied here as an unverified general assumption (not independently sourced for EMS specifically) — flagged as an estimate, not a sourced fact.
- **Consolidating at the top.** Aimtron's own ICS (Decatur) acquisition is itself an example of the M&A-led consolidation trend visible among listed peers.
- **Differentiation, not price, competition** in Aimtron's target segment: medical/aerospace/defence/auto customers select on certification, quality, and reliability first; this differs structurally from the price-driven consumer/mobile EMS segment.
- **Entries/exits:** PLI/ECMS incentives are attracting new entrants and capacity expansion (including Aimtron's own greenfield); rising certification requirements are simultaneously a barrier to new entrants.
- **Import share trend:** components (chips, bare PCBs, connectors) remain heavily import-dependent (88% for bare PCB) even as assembly/value-add localises — India's EMS growth to date is more an assembly/value-add story than a full backward-integrated one, an ongoing risk and opportunity simultaneously.

## SECTION 5: SUMMARY & RUNWAY

### 5A — Funnel

```
TAM (conservative)  Rs2,05,600cr   [India, non-consumer B2B EMS/ESDM, FY25]
   -> SAM            Rs48,500cr    (23.6% of TAM)
      -> SOM 3yr      Rs950cr      (1.96% of SAM)
      -> SOM 5yr      Rs1,300cr    (2.68% of SAM)
Current revenue (FY26 consolidated) Rs301.2cr = 0.62% of SAM, 0.15% of TAM
```

### 5B — Runway assessment

- **Revenue headroom** = SAM ÷ current revenue = Rs48,500cr ÷ Rs301.2cr = **~161.0x**.
- **TAM growth rate:** across sources, cited India EMS/ESDM CAGRs range ~17.5% (PSMR) to ~20-25% (tradebrains, citing the Rs3.1L cr FY25 to Rs7-8L cr FY30 ESDM trajectory) to an implied ~28-30% (KPMG's $40-45bn FY25 to $150bn FY30). **Conservative anchor used: tam_growth_pct = 20%.**
- **Company CAGR vs TAM:** Aimtron's own FY25->FY26 consolidated revenue growth was **+89.2% YoY** (Rs1,591.8mn to Rs3,011.6mn, Investor Presentation p.30) — vastly above the ~20-30% TAM growth range, meaning the company is currently gaining share aggressively (a small-base effect at 0.62% SAM share, not yet a signal of market-level dominance).
- **Years to saturate SAM at current growth:** at a sustained 40% CAGR (below the FY26 actual, but above the SOM-implied 3-5yr range), years to close the gap from Rs301.2cr to Rs48,500cr = ln(161.0)/ln(1.40) ≈ **15.1 years** — well beyond any 3-5yr investment horizon. Even at the SOM-implied 34-46.6% capacity-capped CAGR, SAM saturation is not a near-term concern; the binding constraint over the next 5 years is capacity and execution, not market headroom.

### 5C — Runway classification

**runway_class: STRONG.** Revenue headroom (~161x) and TAM growth (~20-30%) both individually support a MASSIVE read, but three factors temper it one notch: (1) TAM estimates diverge up to ~2x across independent sources even before SAM filtering, reducing confidence in the denominator; (2) the capacity ceiling (3C) caps realistic 5-year growth at ~34% CAGR regardless of how large the theoretical SAM/TAM headroom is — market size is not currently the binding constraint, execution is; (3) customer concentration is undisclosed (per B04), leaving execution risk not fully assessable. STRONG rather than MASSIVE reflects that the operative constraint for the next 3-5 years is capacity and execution credibility, not market size.

### 5D — SAM expansion levers actually being pursued

- **US subsidiary ramp** (ICS Decatur + Aimtron Electronics LLC Texas) — already embedded in the SOM/capacity cross-check above (~Rs280-300cr addition); not incremental to the figures already stated.
- **Backward integration** (in-house cable assembly, component manufacturing) — cited as a strategic priority (AR p.14) but **no quantified revenue-addition figure disclosed — NOT FOUND**.
- **Optical transceiver/SFP ECMS project** (Aimtron Mechatronics) — application submitted, approval pending; addresses genuine import-substitution demand ("India demand expected up to ~40G capacity range," Investor Presentation p.22) but **no revenue figure disclosed for this specific initiative — NOT FOUND**.
- **Railways vertical entry** (RDSO approval in progress, Kavach ATP systems) — a new vertical outside the current SAM's five-filter base; **no quantified TAM/revenue addition disclosed — NOT FOUND**.
- **Europe/Australia expansion** — already partially realised and captured in current FY26 revenue mix (Spain 3.3%, Australia 1.6%); incremental potential beyond current run-rate not separately quantified.

Because none of these levers carry company-disclosed sizing, no revised-headroom figure is computed; if they materialise, the effective addressable base would broaden beyond the current SAM definition, but this remains directional only.
