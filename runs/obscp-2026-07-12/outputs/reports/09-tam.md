## SECTION 1: MARKET DEFINITION

### 1A Precise boundaries

**Product scope**: precision engineered metal components manufactured to customer print (build-to-print, not own-IP): CNC-machined shafts/rods/bushes/fittings/sensor bosses/nut fasteners, investment-cast components, cold- and hot-forged parts, and (from 2026) stamped parts and sub-assemblies. Excludes: raw steel/bright-bar production (upstream, done by promoter group Anglian Omega, not OBSCP), finished/branded shock absorbers or steering systems (OBSCP supplies the machined rod/component INTO Tenneco/ZF/JTEKT assemblies, not the finished assembly), tyres, glass, batteries, electricals/electronics, plastics/rubber parts, large stamped body panels, engine blocks/cylinder heads (large-tonnage casting outside OBSCP's process scale).

**Geographic scope**: India-based manufacturing (6 plants: Pune, Chennai, Faridabad; Sanand and Supa under development), serving both domestic OEMs/Tier-1s (80.4% of FY26 revenue) and exports to 17 countries (19.6%, Investor Presentation p.19). TAM is sized as India-domestic-production value (i.e., component manufacturing capacity/output based in India), which captures both domestic sale and export-from-India in one basis — consistent with how AIFI and ACMA report the Indian forging/component industry.

**Customer scope**: Tier-1 auto component integrators (ZF, Tenneco, JTEKT, Faurecia, MAHLE, Lear, Tata AutoComp) and Defence PSUs/private primes (BEL, and DPSU/private ordnance value chain) — nomination-based B2B, not aftermarket, not retail/distribution (B04 handoff: "manufacturing; build-to-print B2B contract manufacturer, nomination-based").

**Channel scope**: OEM-nominated supply only. Aftermarket auto-parts distribution is explicitly excluded from TAM (ACMA reports OEM and aftermarket separately; OBSCP's 100% of disclosed revenue is OEM-nominated).

**Price segment**: mid-to-high precision tier (tolerances to 4 microns, IATF16949/ISO9001 certified, single-source on several programs) — not commodity/low-tolerance job-work.

**Explicit inclusions**: automotive (85.6% of FY26 revenue — the dominant segment), defense ordnance components (ammunition casings, ignition primers, fuze parts — 5.6%), marine components (cable fittings, steering shafts, tubular products — 6.3%), renewables & others (critical shafts for windmills, solar fasteners — 2.5%). Emerging (not yet revenue-material, sub-1% combined): aerospace (AS9100D certification pending), humanoid robotics (cold-plate parts, first small batch H2 FY26), medical implants (orthopedic casting, first order).

**Explicit exclusions**: aftermarket auto parts, raw material/steel production, finished branded assemblies sold direct to end consumer, non-metal components, and — per the run brief — the manifest's "EPC/Civil construction" sector_cap_row, which is a confirmed collector defect and is not used anywhere in this analysis.

## SECTION 2: TAM ESTIMATION — MULTIPLE METHODS

### Method 1 — Top-down (India precision metal-component market: forging + machining, auto-weighted)

Step 1: India Metal Forging Market = **USD 11,346.9 million** (2025, Persistence Market Research / cross-checked directionally against Fortune Business Insights and AIFI's installed-capacity disclosure of 4.8 million MT / US$3.8bn invested — AIFI, IFC 2025 conference materials). Not stale (2025 estimate, <2yrs old).
Step 2: India Precision Machining Market = **USD 6,566.0 million** (2025, Grand View Research), India = 5.3% of the global precision machining market. Not stale.
Step 3: FX conversion at USD/INR 95.5 (spot, July 2026 — Federal Reserve H.10 / x-rates.com):
- Forging: 11,346.9 × 95.5 ÷ 10 = **₹1,08,363 Cr**
- Machining: 6,566.0 × 95.5 ÷ 10 = **₹62,705 Cr**
Step 4: Auto-sector share. Neither source publishes a process-wise ACMA split for FY26 (searched, not found — this is a genuine gap, flagged). AIFI states the forging industry serves "automotive, railways, aerospace, defence, construction equipment, general engineering" without a current % split; industry convention (multiple secondary sources, not independently verified here) puts automotive at roughly 55-65% of Indian forging off-take. Conservative bias: apply **55%** to forging and a more conservative **30%** to the broader/more diffuse precision-machining market (which serves aerospace, medical and general engineering roughly as much as auto):
- Auto forging: 1,08,363 × 0.55 = **₹59,600 Cr**
- Auto machining: 62,705 × 0.30 = **₹18,812 Cr**
Step 5: Overlap discount (forged parts frequently get subsequently machined — avoid double-count): sum × 0.85 = (59,600+18,812) × 0.85 = **₹66,650 Cr** — India auto-only precision metal component TAM.

Step 6: Add defense ordnance-component TAM (Method 4 below, ₹2,800 Cr) → **Method 1 combined = ₹69,450 Cr ≈ ₹69,500 Cr**.
*Confidence: Medium-Low — multiple compounding, explicitly-flagged assumption layers (auto-share %, overlap %). Presented as the "realistic" anchor, not the conservative one.*

### Method 4 — Import substitution / segment-specific (Defense)

India ammunition/ordnance manufacturing market (bullets, cartridges, grenades, aerial bombs, propellants, artillery shells): **₹6,800-7,200 Cr FY2024-25** (thesecretariat.in / Ken Research aggregate estimate), up from ₹4,900-5,200 Cr FY2023-24; forecast to exceed **US$1 billion (~₹9,550 Cr) by 2028**. Private-sector participation is explicitly growing (hybrid DRDO-design / DPSU-mass-production / private-innovation model; Adani, Solar Industries, Tata cited as entrants) — directly evidenced in OBSCP's own materials (BEL Zero Defective Award FY25, ignition-primer mass production started this month per May 2026 concall p.7, MK-84 bomb fin parts, defense revenue growth "3x this year" per May 2025 concall). OBSCP addresses the **metal-component/casing/primer manufacturing slice** of the ammunition value chain, not propellants/explosives/final assembly — apply a conservative **40%** metal-component share (flagged estimate) to the ₹6,800-7,200 Cr range: **₹2,720-2,880 Cr, midpoint ₹2,800 Cr**.
*Confidence: Medium — market size sourced and current (<2 yrs); the 40% component-share split is an explicit estimate.*

### Method 5 — Global benchmark (directional only, not used for headline number)

Global automotive forging market estimates diverge materially by source: Fortune Business Insights / Global Growth Insights put it at **USD 54-55 billion (2025)**; Future Market Insights puts it at **USD 25.0 billion (2025)** — a >2x divergence, flagged per the staleness/divergence rule rather than averaged silently. India's forging output (USD 11.35bn) is therefore somewhere between 21% and 45% of the global market, and AIFI confirms India is already the **world's second-largest forging producer**. This benchmark says India is not an underpenetrated forging base needing "catch-up" — the growth story is more export-share-gain (China+1, explicitly cited by management in both concalls) than domestic-market creation. Not used to set a headline TAM number given the source divergence; used qualitatively in Section 4A.

### 3B SOM at 3 and 5 years

Current SAM share = 219.5 / 18,600 = **1.18%**.

Share-gain rule applied: OBSCP is growing at 38.6% CAGR (FY24-26, presentation p.27) vs. a TAM growing at low-double-digits (Section 5B), has an order book of ₹1,200+ Cr converting at ₹100-200 Cr/year (management, May 2026 concall), is actively adding process capabilities (forging, stamping, in 2025-26) and entering new verticals from a near-zero base (defense, marine, aerospace-pending, robotics, medical) — this supports the **upper end of "aggressive" (3-5pp)** share gain, but conservative bias pulls the headline toward the top of "normal" for 3-year and mid-aggressive for 5-year (rather than the full 5pp, which the rules reserve for competitor-exit/acquisition scenarios that do not apply here):

- **3-year**: +2pp → share = 1.18% + 2.00% = 3.18%
  SOM₃ = 18,600 × 3.18% = **₹591 Cr**
  Implied CAGR: (591/219.5)^(1/3) − 1 = (2.693)^(0.333) − 1 = **39.1%**

- **5-year**: +4pp cumulative (i.e., a further +2pp from yr3 to yr5, reflecting Sanand/Supa/stamping capacity maturing and export mix rising) → share = 1.18% + 4.00% = 5.18%
  SOM₅ = 18,600 × 5.18% = **₹964 Cr**
  Implied CAGR: (964/219.5)^(1/5) − 1 = (4.392)^(0.20) − 1 = **34.4%**

These are broadly consistent with — and slightly more conservative than — OBSCP's own trailing performance (53.8% FY26 growth, 38.6% FY24-26 CAGR) and its own FY27 guidance (40-45% revenue growth, reaffirmed twice by management, May 2026 concall p.4 & p.11). The deceleration built into the SOM (39.1% yr3 → 34.4% yr5, vs. ~40-54% trailing) reflects conservative bias and the natural moderation expected as the revenue base scales.

### 3C Capacity cross-check (against B07 capex-embedded growth ~18%)

Installed/committed capacity picture (Investor Presentation p.17, concalls):
- **Machining**: FY26 installed 451.5 lakh units p.a., produced 383.6 lakh units → **85% utilised** — limited near-term headroom without capex.
- **Investment casting**: installed 900 tons, produced 700 tons → **77.8% utilised**.
- **Forging (cold+hot)**: installed 12,000 tons p.a. combined, **utilisation only ~30%** as of Q4 FY26 — substantial existing headroom (could triple forging output before needing new forging capex).
- **Stamping**: ~70% of capex done, ~₹2 Cr/month recurring revenue already, additional new-order capacity coming online Q1 FY27.
- **Sanand (Tenneco-dedicated)**: ~₹40 Cr incremental revenue expected, plant already set up.
- **Supa mega-factory**: management's own claim is "₹700 Cr, 800 Cr on its own... maybe more at peak efficiencies," but explicitly undisclosed capex quantum and timeline ("I don't have the exact numbers," "it's a long journey," "we test the soil first" — May 2026 concall p.11-12). Only 8.4 acres acquired to date (2026); this is a multi-year, not a 3-year, project on management's own framing.
- **Disclosed FY27 capex**: ₹15-20 Cr incremental, explicitly stated to "sustain us for the next two years" (per B07 handoff) — i.e., this quantified capex plan underwrites roughly the FY27 guidance level (₹307-318 Cr, at 40-45% growth) and perhaps into FY28, but **not** the ₹591 Cr yr-3 SOM level.

**Capacity check: gap of approximately ₹150-250 Cr** between the yr-3 SOM (₹591 Cr) and what the currently-quantified capex plan (₹15-20 Cr, "~2 years") can be shown to support (~₹300-450 Cr range). Forging headroom (only 30% utilised) and the Sanand line (₹40 Cr) help close part of this gap without new spend, but the balance depends on Supa mega-factory capacity that management has explicitly declined to quantify a capex figure or firm timeline for. **The SOM is the optimistic side of this pairing** unless Supa's capex/timeline (currently undisclosed) accelerates materially beyond management's own "phased and calculated," "long journey" framing.

### 4C Market structure

Organized-sector top tier is dominated by a handful of much larger diversified players (Bharat Forge ₹16,812 Cr consolidated, Craftsman Automation ₹8,069 Cr, Sundram Fasteners ₹6,368 Cr) — but the specific sub-₹500 Cr precision-component niche OBSCP occupies is a long fragmented tail of mid/small organized players plus a large (unquantified in this search) unorganized job-shop base; not top-3 concentrated at OBSCP's scale tier. Trend is **consolidating** at the capability level: mid-tier organized players (including OBSCP itself) are adding processes (forging, stamping, casting) to move up the value chain and become one-stop suppliers — explicitly OBSCP's stated strategy ("the beauty of business" — fungible machines, multi-process in-house). Competition is **differentiation-oriented**, not pure price (IATF16949/ISO9001 certification, micron-level tolerance, single-source nominations, BEL Zero Defective Award, Tenneco Special Recognition for Quality). New entrants are visible at the policy-driven edges: private defense-ordnance entrants (Adani, Solar Industries, Tata cited industry-wide) and OBSCP's own entry into aerospace/robotics/medical. India auto-component exports rose 5% to USD 24bn in FY26 while imports rose 13% to USD 25.4bn (ACMA) — the broader industry is a net importer, but OBSCP itself states it is a **net exporter**, "hardly importing anything" (May 2026 concall p.8).

### 5B Runway assessment

- **Revenue headroom** = SAM ÷ current revenue = 18,600 / 219.5 = **84.7x**.
- **TAM growth rate**: India auto-component industry (ACMA, FY26) grew **12.7% YoY** in rupee terms (to ₹7.6 lakh Cr total industry, of which OEM supply ₹6,62,893 Cr, +16.3% YoY); 5-year FY21-26 CAGR was 17% (post-COVID low base, higher than the current run-rate — the 12.7% current-year figure is used as tam_growth_pct, being the more representative recent figure).
- **Company CAGR vs TAM**: OBSCP's FY24-26 revenue CAGR is 38.6% vs. TAM growth of ~12.7% — the company is **gaining share** at roughly 3x the market growth rate, not merely riding the market.
- **Years to saturate SAM at current growth**: ln(18,600/219.5) / ln(1.40) = ln(84.7)/ln(1.40) = 4.44/0.336 ≈ **13.2 years** at a (illustrative, non-decelerating) 40% growth rate — confirms headroom is not a near-term constraint even though growth will mechanically decelerate before then in reality.

### 5C Runway classification

**MASSIVE** — 84.7x revenue headroom, TAM/SAM structurally large relative to current revenue base, TAM itself growing double-digit, and the company demonstrably gaining share at multiples of the market growth rate. This classification is driven by the company's very small current base (₹219.5 Cr) relative to even the conservative, heavily-filtered SAM, not by TAM being unusually large in absolute terms.
