## SECTION 1: THE BUSINESS MODEL IN PLAIN ENGLISH

### 1A. One-line description
Azad makes the small, ultra-precise metal parts (airfoils, blades, engine and turbine components) that go inside jet engines, gas/steam/nuclear turbines, and oilfield equipment — parts that cannot fail — under long-term supply contracts with the world's biggest OEMs (GE Vernova, Siemens Energy, Mitsubishi Heavy Industries, Rolls-Royce, Honeywell, Baker Hughes) (AR p.2, p.6).

### 1B. Money flow chain, by revenue stream

**Stream 1 — Energy & Oil & Gas components (79.1% of FY25 revenue)** (AR p.18)
[Titanium/superalloy forgings and raw material] → [Azad machines/finishes compressor airfoils, specialised machine parts (SMP), critical machine parts (CMP), combustion-section components, drill bits, bonnet frames, slips, hatch covers to exact OEM blueprint and metallurgical spec] → [delivers qualified, audited components to OEM plants globally] → [global turbine/oilfield OEMs — GE Vernova, Siemens Energy, MHI, Baker Hughes — pay] → [paid per purchase order drawn against multi-year long-term contracts/LTCPAs, largely export invoiced in USD/EUR] (AR p.5, p.12; Inv. Pres. slide 9-10).

**Stream 2 — Aerospace & Defence components (17.9% of FY25 revenue)** (AR p.18)
[Same raw material base] → [Azad machines APU/engine components, unison rings, actuator assemblies, structural/airframe parts] → [delivers to aircraft OEM/tier-1 supply chains for Boeing 737, Airbus A320/A350, Gulfstream G550 programs, plus DRDO/GTRE defence programs] → [Boeing/Airbus supply chain, Honeywell, Rolls-Royce, Tata Lockheed Martin, GTRE(DRDO) pay] → [purchase orders against phased contracts, e.g. Honeywell Phase-1 USD 16mn award] (AR p.5, p.12; Inv. Pres. slide 9-10).

**Stream 3 — Scrap & export incentives (~3.0% of FY25 revenue, ₹136mn)** (AR p.18)
[Metal scrap generated during machining] + [Government export-promotion scheme benefits on qualifying exports] → [sold/claimed] → [scrap buyers / Government of India] → [cash on sale / scheme credit] (AR p.18; accounting policy note on export benefits, AR standalone notes).

### 1E. The chai-stall-uncle version
Imagine a locksmith so trusted that Boeing, GE and the Indian defence ministry all hand him their vault keys — but it took him three years of background checks before any of them would let him near the lock. That's Azad. It doesn't sell a "product" off a shelf; it spends years getting certified to make one exact bolt, blade or airfoil for one exact customer, and once certified, that customer rarely switches locksmiths because re-certifying someone else is expensive and risky for them too. Azad's factory floor looks like any precision workshop, but what it's really selling is trust built over a decade, sold in the form of titanium and steel parts that must never fail mid-flight or mid-turbine-spin. The catch: only a handful of global OEMs matter, so if even one or two of them slow down, Azad feels it immediately — like a locksmith with five very large, very important clients and no small retail walk-ins.

### 2D. Industry lifecycle stage and Azad's position
The end markets Azad sells into are each at a different stage: global gas-turbine/energy demand is in a structural upcycle (electricity demand growth 3.3-3.7% globally, energy-turbine TAM growing 7% CAGR to 2027, AR p.16), commercial aviation is in a multi-decade fleet-replacement and growth cycle (RPK doubling 2022-2029, AR p.5), and Indian defence manufacturing is described as "at a defining inflection point" with production targeted to nearly triple by FY29E (AR p.16). Within this, Azad itself is in a capacity-scaling phase: it just finished a ₹700 crore QIP (Feb-2025) and is mid-way through a ~94,899 sqm Phase-1 expansion (of which four customer-dedicated facilities were inaugurated between March 2025 and April 2026) plus a further 67,267 sqm Phase-2 (AR p.11; Inv. Pres. slide 8, 23). It is a small, fast-scaling supplier riding sector-level upcycles it does not control.

## SECTION 3: FINANCIAL METRICS THAT MATTER FOR THIS BUSINESS MODEL

### 3A. Ignore-these-track-these

| Commonly tracked ratio | Verdict | Why it misleads here |
|---|---|---|
| Inventory turnover vs. general manufacturing benchmarks | IRRELEVANT (as commonly benchmarked) | Long qualification-linked build cycles and customer-dedicated WIP make elevated inventory/receivable days structurally normal, not a sign of poor operations (AR p.19 shows multi-year rising WC days alongside revenue growth) |
| Current ratio / quick ratio in isolation | MISLEADING | A capex-and-WC-heavy scaling manufacturer can show a "weak" current ratio purely because cash is deployed into CWIP and inventory ahead of revenue recognition — see net cash position (FY25) turning back to net debt as capex resumes (FY26) |
| Revenue growth YoY without segment mix adjustment | MISLEADING | A&D grew 84.1% off a small base (₹807mn) while Energy grew 26% off a much larger base (₹3,586mn) — blended growth hides very different maturity/margin profiles (AR p.18) |
| Revenue per employee vs. generic industrial peers | MISLEADING | This is a skilled-engineer, low-headcount-per-revenue model (~1,453 employees for ₹4,529mn FY25 revenue, AR p.20) not comparable to mass-manufacturing headcount ratios |
| Same-store/like-for-like growth concepts | IRRELEVANT | Not a multi-site retail or franchise model; growth is program- and qualification-driven, not footprint-driven |

### 4B. Valuation method applicability — HANDOFF TO ROLE 1 VALUATION STAGE

| Method | Applicability | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| EV/EBITDA | **PRIMARY** | Capital-intensive industrial manufacturer mid-capacity-ramp; EBITDA better normalizes for the currently elevated depreciation/capex phase and for company-flagged non-recurring items (fire costs, litigation, forex, COVID) already isolated in "Adjusted EBITDA" (AR p.9, notes) |
| DCF | **SECONDARY** | Multi-year revenue visibility from the disclosed ~₹6,080cr order book and LTAs supports explicit near-term cash-flow modelling; used as a cross-check given execution/ramp uncertainty at new facilities and the qualification-lock-in that should support a durable terminal value if programs persist |
| P/E | **TERTIARY** | Usable given a now-consistent post-QIP PAT growth trajectory, but the FY23 PAT margin anomaly (3.4% vs. 11-22% in surrounding years, driven by one-off IPO-related finance costs/gains, Inv. Pres. slide 30) shows reported PAT can be noisy — cross-check only, not primary |
| P/B | NOT APPLICABLE | Book value excludes the real driver of value (accumulated qualifications/OEM relationships), which sit off balance sheet |
| Sum-of-the-parts | NOT APPLICABLE | Single reportable segment under Ind AS 108 ("manufacturing high precision and OEM components" treated as one segment, AR p.229) despite voluntary Energy/A&D/O&G disclosure — insufficient standalone segment financials for SOTP |
| Dividend discount model | NOT APPLICABLE | No dividend paid or proposed for FY25; capital being reinvested into capacity (AR Board's Report, p.75-ish "no dividend recommended") |
| Replacement cost / asset-based | NOT APPLICABLE | Understates the qualification/relationship moat that is the actual source of value |

Cycle stage that matters for valuation: Azad is in a **capacity-scaling phase within a favourable sector upcycle** (gas-turbine capex, aviation fleet growth, defence indigenisation, per AR p.16) — valuation should weight near-term order-book conversion and facility-ramp execution more heavily than long-run terminal assumptions at this stage.

### 4D. Highest-value questions for management

1. **Q: What is current capacity utilization at the existing facilities versus the newly commissioned GE Vernova/MHI/Siemens Energy/Baker Hughes dedicated plants, and when do they reach steady-state utilization?**
   Reassures: utilization ramping on plan with a clear revenue-conversion timeline.
   Worries: utilization lagging plan with idle depreciation dragging on margins.

2. **Q: How much of the ~₹6,080cr order book is firm/binding versus indicative, and what is the expected revenue-conversion cadence over the next three years?**
   Reassures: majority firm, with a clear multi-year drawdown schedule.
   Worries: a large indicative/non-binding component, or a backend-loaded conversion profile.

3. **Q: What is the qualification pipeline for new parts/programs in FY26-FY27, and how many are expected to convert to revenue?**
   Reassures: several new qualifications converting each year, diversifying beyond the top-5 customers.
   Worries: the pipeline slowing or remaining concentrated in the same few OEMs.

4. **Q: How is raw material (titanium/superalloy) cost and forex exposure managed, given 90%+ export revenue — are LTA contracts pass-through or fixed-price?**
   Reassures: contractual pass-through or effective natural/financial hedging.
   Worries: fixed-price long-term contracts with no input-cost escalation clause.

5. **Q: What is the capex plan beyond Phase-1/Phase-2, and how will it be funded now that QIP cash is being deployed (internal accruals vs. fresh debt)?**
   Reassures: funded mainly by internal accruals/cash on hand with modest incremental debt.
   Worries: renewed reliance on debt before the new capacity is generating matching returns.

6. **Q: What drove the FY23 anomalous PAT margin (3.4% versus 11-22% in surrounding years), and is there residual risk of similar one-off items recurring?**
   Reassures: fully resolved, IPO/CCD-conversion-related one-offs with no residual exposure.
   Worries: a recurring pattern of "non-recurring" adjustments appearing each year.

7. **Q: What is management's target for reducing customer concentration (top-5 currently 51.1% of revenue) and geographic concentration (90%+ export) over the next 3-5 years?**
   Reassures: an active, quantified diversification program.
