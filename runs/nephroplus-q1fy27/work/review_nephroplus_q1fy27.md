Revenue rose 23.7% to ₹281.75 Cr and operating EBITDA rose 34.3% to ₹63.88 Cr, lifting the operating margin 180 basis points to 22.7% (R L582-593). Reported PAT grew 34.9% to ₹31.97 Cr; the company's headline "Adjusted PAT ₹36.8 Cr, +41.7%" adds back a ₹3.6 Cr Saudi joint-venture loss and ₹1.3 Cr of ESOP cost, so anchor to the statutory ₹32.0 Cr (P L342, A16). ### 18.2 SECTOR INTELLIGENCE

Dialysis is a chronic, recurring, life-sustaining service: guests need roughly three sessions a week and cannot survive beyond about two weeks without treatment, which produces sticky, annuity-like revenue (95% stick to the same clinic; 15+ hours a week on site) (Presn, P L744-761). *(Provenance: this-quarter filing / F&S-sourced deck; corroborates the Notion sector read of a structural, non-cyclical demand base.)* India penetration is ~7% against ~16% Philippines, ~27% Uzbekistan and ~35% KSA, and the India dialysis market is guided to grow 20-22% to FY29 versus ~7% globally (P L817, L833). *(Provenance: deck / F&S; consistent with the Notion "~7% penetration, structural tailwind" thesis.)* The demand driver is the diabetes and hypertension burden (14% adult diabetes prevalence; 1 in 6 hypertensive) feeding chronic kidney disease (P L748-773). The payer structure matters: 76.5% of guests use public insurance (P L1542), so reimbursement policy and government dialysis schemes are the load-bearing regulatory variables, and the business is explicitly built around public-private partnerships inside government and private hospitals. The structural shift from unorganised to organised provision (India organised share rising ~15%→19% of guest volumes, US/EU already 70-80% organised) is the second tailwind and the one NephroPlus is positioned to harvest (P L1017-1032). No sector headwind surfaced this quarter beyond the generic reimbursement-dependence risk inherent to a public-payer-heavy model.

### 18.3 BUSINESS-MODEL INTELLIGENCE

NephroPlus runs an asset-light, revenue-share dialysis network: ~52% of clinics sit inside partner hospitals under captive (272) or PPP (200) arrangements, with only 78 standalone, so upfront capital per clinic is low (₹10-15mn vs ₹1,000-1,500mn for oncology) and payback is 1.5-2 years (P L1098, L840-844). *(Provenance: this-quarter deck; extends the Notion "asset-light specialty chain" model note.)* The economic engine is volume × price: treatments grew 13.3% to 10.31 lakh and revenue per treatment grew 9.2% to ₹2,733 (PR L62-63), so the top line compounds on both levers. Unit economics improve through scale-led procurement, ~20%-of-revenue HR cost, in-house biomedical maintenance, and a training academy that de-bottlenecks nurse supply (P L1230). This quarter's model signal is the **geographic margin structure**: the India parent runs a ~14% operating EBITDA margin while the consolidated group runs ~22.7% (Steps 1c, 2b), meaning the higher-price international clinics (Philippines US$111, Uzbekistan US$72.5, KSA US$300 per treatment vs India US$22, P L813) carry the margin. The "India cost playbook exported to higher-price geographies" is therefore the real value driver, and it is working (international mix 44.9%). The model drift to watch is that headline profitability is presented on an adjusted basis that removes the Saudi pre-revenue drag; as KSA and Kazakhstan scale, the gap between reported and adjusted profit is the honest gauge of whether international expansion is yet accretive. *(Provenance: this-quarter filing; the S-vs-C margin gap is A3 finding F2.)*

### 18.4 COMPETITION INTELLIGENCE

NephroPlus is the largest Indian and Asian dialysis network, #5 globally, at 550 clinics, with >50% share of the Indian organised market and 4.4x the next-largest organised Indian chain (P L545-553). *(Provenance: this-quarter deck / internal-audited claims; aligns with the Notion "market leader, moat expansion underway" positioning, EM 46/100.)* Where it wins: scale-led procurement and a standardised protocol (RenAssure, Renova dialyzer reprocessing), an 80%-Tier-2/3 footprint that is hard to replicate, deep hospital relationships (Fortis, Medanta, Care, Max), and the only Indian chain to have scaled internationally, which gives it a first-mover claim on high-price, under-penetrated markets (P L601, L1091). Its structural edge over hospital-owned or single-clinic operators is cost per treatment at the lowest global price points. Where it is structurally weaker / the competitive risk to watch: it competes internationally against established global dialysis majors (the deck names Diaverum via a board member's background, P L1296) that have deeper balance sheets in the very high-price markets (KSA, US$300/treatment) NephroPlus is now entering as a minority-influenced JV partner; the KSA vehicle is a 51:49 JV with a local listed partner (Tibbiyah), so control and economics there are shared, not owned (P L683). The public-payer dependence (76.5% insurance-funded) also means competition is partly a tendering game where a lost MoH tender is a lost market, which is exactly why the pending Saudi RFI outcome is the key competitive as well as growth variable. No peer dialysis-chain concall reported within the ±4-week window, so no direct peer-narrative cross-check was available this quarter. *(Provenance: this-quarter deck for positioning; peer/sector framing reconciled with Notion memory.)*
