===== PAGE 2 =====
"India Ratings Affirms Steel Strips Wheels's Bank Loan Facilities at 'IND AA-'/Stable;
Rates Additional Limits", Feb 18, 2026, Auto Components & Equipments.
Analytical Approach: Ind-Ra takes a fully consolidated view of SSWL and its wholly owned
subsidiary AMW Auto Component Limited, given strong operational and financial linkages.
Key Rating Drivers.
Strengths: strong business profile; sustained growth in revenue; stable credit metrics
during FY25-9MFY26; rangebound EBITDA margins; locational advantage, strong customer
relationships; diversified revenue base.
Weaknesses: capex-heavy business.
Strong Business Profile: SSWL is one of the leading manufacturers of alloy wheel rims,
total installed capacity 5 million units as of 1HFY26 (FYE25: 4.2 million units).
Operated at about 80% capacity in FY25. Alloy capacity to grow further by 1.2 million
units at Bhuj, Gujarat, planned to be executed in two phases over FY27-FY28.

===== PAGE 3 =====
Continued: steel wheel rim market position - 34% PV, 52% MHCV, 42% tractors, 35% OTR,
39% two/three-wheelers. High share of business with domestic OEMs. FY25 foray into
aluminium knuckles, combined capacity 0.3 million as of 1HFY26, contribution to overall
revenue "minuscule".
Sustained Growth in Revenue: revenue grew 16.1% YoY to INR37,082 million in 9MFY26
(FY25: INR44,290 million, FY24: INR43,571 million), driven by alloy wheel sales
increase, GST 2.0 demand recovery (September 2025), new customer/share-of-business
additions. Partly offset by export decline to US markets in 3QFY26 on US reciprocal
tariffs (August 2025). Ind-Ra expects FY26 revenue +5-7% YoY, and FY27 revenue upwards
of INR50,000 million (Rs 5,000 crore).
Stable Credit Metrics during FY25-9MFY26: EBITDA largely stable (9MFY26: INR3,604
million, FY25: INR4,843 million, FY24: INR4,640 million). Adjusted net leverage
deteriorated to 3.2x as of 9MFY26 (FY25: 2.8x, FY24: 3.1x) on debt for alloy wheel/
knuckle capacity expansion. Reported gross debt INR9,507 million at 9MFYE26 (FY25:
INR8,274 million; FY24: INR10,479 million); LC-acceptance-adjusted gross debt ~INR15,264
million at 9MFYE26 (FYE25: INR13,793 million; FYE24: INR14,608 million).
Expansionary capex likely to continue FY26-FY27; peak revenue/EBITDA likely only from
FY28; net adjusted leverage likely to remain above 2.5x over FY26-FY27. Total outside
liabilities ((gross debt+creditors)/EBITDA) 3.3x in 1HFY26 (FY25: 3.3x, FY24: 3.6x).
Interest coverage (EBITDA/interest expense) deteriorated to 3.9x in 9MFY26 (FY25: 4.1x,
FY24: 4.5x), likely to remain around 4.0x over FY26-FY27.
Rangebound EBITDA Margins: 10-11% over FY24-9MFY26 (9MFY26: 9.7%, FY25: 10.9%, FY24:
10.6%). 9MFY26 margin decline due to aluminium price at a record high of INR265-270/kg;
company passes on price increases to OEMs with about a three-month lag, protecting
margins to an extent. Ind-Ra expects EBITDA margins of 10-10.5% during FY26-FY27.
SSWL's RoCE improved to 15.8% in FY25 (FY24: 14.4%) per Ind-Ra's own calculation basis
(differs from the company's own reported RoCE of 20% for FY25 in its investor
presentation; methodology not reconciled in this corpus).
Locational Advantage; Strong Customer Relationships: Chennai plant near port and
Renault Nissan / Hyundai plants, low freight cost. Jamshedpur plant near Tata Motors and
Tata Steel ('IND AAA'/Stable), lower logistics/raw material cost. Mehsana alloy plant
near Tata Motors. Strategic supplier relationships with Tata Steel (6.93% stake) and
Sumitomo Metal Industries Ltd (5.42% stake) at end-March 2025. Bhuj (Gujarat) expansion
planned to cater entirely to exports given port proximity.
Diversified Revenue Base: PV 51% of revenue in 1HFY26 (FY25: 51%, FY24: 48%), CV 24%
(28%, 31%), tractors 12% (11%, 10%), two/three-wheelers 2% (2%, 2%). Export revenue
contribution dipped to 13% in 1HFY26 (FY25: 11%, FY24: 15%) on global uncertainties.
Alloy wheel rim revenue contribution rose to 32% in FY25 (FY24: 28%), further to 36% in
1HFY26.

===== PAGE 4 =====
Capex-Heavy Business: continuous capex totalling INR11,212 million over FY19-FY25.
Auto industry cyclical; FY20-FY21 slowdown (COVID) caused capacity-utilisation decline
and affected return ratios. At FYE25, operating close to 80% capacity in most existing
plants. Plans incremental capex ~INR4,300 million for alloy wheels and ~INR1,400 million
for knuckles at Bhuj over FY26-FY27. Any greenfield acquisition could delay
deleveraging.
Liquidity: Adequate. Free cash flow turned positive to INR1,736 million in FY25 (FY24:
negative INR2,398 million) on improved profitability and lower capex. Net working
capital cycle (creditors excl. LC acceptances) moderated slightly to 90 days in FY25
(FY24: 85 days), on higher inventory for exports and higher receivables. Unencumbered
cash and bank balances INR82 million at 9MFYE26 (FYE25: INR74 million; FYE24: INR403
million). Of total FY25 capex INR2,123 million (FY24: INR3,829 million), 26% Mehsana
alloy expansion, 23% knuckle plant capacity, remainder general maintenance/automation.
Plans capex ~INR2,500 million FY26 and INR4,000-4,500 million FY27 for Bhuj expansion.
Ind-Ra expects FCF to turn negative FY26-FY27. Average peak utilisation of sanctioned
fund-based limits 92% (12 months to Dec 2025); non-fund-based limits 89%. Term debt
repayment obligations INR1,222 million FY26 and INR1,027 million FY27, expected met via
internal accruals.
Rating Sensitivities. Positive: sustained revenue/profitability improvement, higher
proportion of higher-margin business, reduced working capital cycle, positive free cash
flow, adjusted net leverage below 1.5x sustained. Negative: revenue/profitability
decline, further stretch in working capital cycle, liquidity cushion decline, large
debt-funded capex, adjusted net leverage remaining above 2.5x sustained.
