# ANTELOPUS — Antelopus Selan Energy Ltd

Shallow read, 2026-09-16. Framework: screens/SHALLOW_ANALYSIS_FRAMEWORK.md v1.0.
Corpus: screens/corpus/ANTELOPUS/. BSE 530075, NSE ANTELOPUS, ISIN INE818A01017.
Formerly Selan Exploration Technology Ltd.

## Business Understanding Narrative

**What it does, and how it makes money.** The company pumps oil and gas in India and
sells it. It holds participating interest in nine contract areas. Five produce today:
Bakrol, Karjisan, Lohar and Cambay in the Cambay basin of Gujarat, and Dangeru in the
Krishna Godavari basin. Four more sit in development. Revenue is a volume multiplied by a
price it does not set. The results filing states this plainly. The selling price of crude
"is determined at the prevailing international market rates in US Dollars" (Results
Q1FY27, p.4). Gas prices are fixed by the Petroleum Planning and Analysis Cell. Fixed
operating costs run at 80 to 85 percent of total costs (India Ratings, p.3). So every
extra barrel drops almost straight through to profit, and every dollar of price does the
same. FY26 revenue was Rs 278.9 cr net, with EBITDA of Rs 167.5 cr at a 59 percent margin.

**What is changing.** Selan was a slow-moving small producer. Antelopus Energy Private
Limited merged into it under an NCLT Chandigarh scheme effective July 2025, and the
company was renamed. The acquirer and its persons acting in concert hold 69.94 percent
(SAST disclosure, p.4). New management is drilling hard. Average production rose from 778
boepd in FY24 to 1,240 in FY25 to 1,402 in FY26 (India Ratings, p.3). Management guides to
2,500 boepd in FY27, self-funded.

**Why now.** Two things arrived together. The drilling campaign began to deliver from
Q3 FY26, and oil prices rose from March 2026. Q1 FY27 shows both. Total income reached
Rs 132 cr and PAT reached Rs 53.8 cr, against Rs 104 cr and Rs 38.0 cr in Q4 FY26.

**What must be true.** Volumes must reach 2,500 boepd and stay there. The frac campaign at
Bakrol must lift the new wells. Crude must hold near current levels. And the production
sharing contracts must be extended, because the ones carrying almost all the output expire
inside a normal holding period.

**What breaks it.** A fall in crude breaks the earnings without touching the volumes. A
slow frac response breaks the ramp. And the contract clock breaks the reserve base itself.

## 2. Business model and archetype

One segment. Ind AS-108 segment reporting does not apply (Results Q1FY27, p.4). FY26
product mix was 82 percent oil and 18 percent gas; Q1 FY27 was 80 and 20. Margin comes
from price and from spreading fixed cost over more barrels, not from any product
advantage. India Ratings puts the mechanism in one line: improved production "enabled
better absorption of largely fixed operating costs", lifting EBITDA margin to 57 percent
in FY26 from 44 percent in FY24 (p.3).

**Archetype: commodity price-taker, bounded by a licence.** It draws on two library
entries at once. It has the price-taker economics of a commodity business and the licence
validity and capacity cap of a licence or scarcity business. It is not a converter. There
is no input price and so no spread, which means Section 1B v3.7 Amendment 17 does not
apply. No sector cap row fits. The nearest is Mining and mineral exploration at 20x. That
needs an operator ruling before any valuation.

## 3. Competitive advantages

None in the pricing sense. The company cannot set a price for either product. What it
holds is acreage. The 2P reserve base at FYE26 is 10.68 MMbbl of oil and 296 bcf of gas,
against in-place volumes of 195.13 MMbbl and 1,371.65 bcf (India Ratings, p.3). That gap
is not a hidden asset. The agency explains why: "2P reserves reporting is restricted to
the duration of the current RSC/PSC validity period". The moat is a government contract
with an end date, not a customer preference. **Moat: absent.** The acreage is an option on
extension.

## 4. The promoters

Antelopus Energy Private Limited took control through the scheme. It and its PACs hold
69.94 percent with nil encumbrance (SAST, p.3-4). The corpus carries no promoter biography
and no web check was run, per the shallow rules. Two governance items surfaced in the last
two months. Ms. Payal Upadhyay applied to move from Promoter Group to Public, and the
board took it on record on 27-Jul-2026. Mr. Manjit Singh ceased as Independent Director on
09-Aug-2026 at the end of his term, and with it as chair of the Audit Committee. Both are
surfaced, not investigated. Neither is an allegation.

## 6. The transition and the quality ladder

Management's claim, quoted once: "Road to 2,500 boepd in FY'27 - entirely self-funded"
(FY26 deck, p.4).

Read that claim against the ladder. It is a volume claim. It is not a rung claim.

- **FROM: R1 commodity price-taker.** No pricing power, ROCE cyclical around the oil price.
- **TO: R1 commodity price-taker.** Bigger, at the same rung.

Nothing in the corpus proposes a mechanism that would give this business pricing power or
durable ROCE independent of crude. A larger price-taker is still a price-taker. **There is
no ladder climb on offer here.** That is the finding, and it is what separates this name
from the other two on this card set. Transition alpha needs a rung migration the market has
not priced. This is a volume and commodity story instead.

## 8. Proof check

The proof point for the FY27 guidance is a rising volume line. The last two quarters do not
give it. Q4 FY26 sales were 1,758 boepd. Q1 FY27 sales were 1,705 boepd. That is flat to
slightly down, against a target of 2,500 by the year's exit. Management explains the flat
quarter with an inventory build, because custody transfer to IOCL ran for only 86 to 87
days. The explanation is plausible and it is also unaudited and unverified here.

So the earnings proof fired and the volume proof did not. Profit rose 42 percent on a
falling volume and a rising price. **Proof gate: NOT FIRED** for the transition being
claimed. The whole guidance rests on the August frac campaign, which had not started when
the deck was filed.

## 9. Flags from the documents

- **Customer concentration 77.27 percent.** Revenue from the top customer was 77.27 percent
  in FY25, up from 64.13 percent in FY24. Top five were 91.65 percent (AR FY2025, p.141).
  For an Indian producer selling crude to a nominated buyer this is partly structural. It is
  still the highest single-customer figure on this card set by a wide margin.
- **Production concentration 91 percent.** Bakrol and Karjisan gave 91 percent of FY26
  production. The agency notes the drilling plan increases that reliance (p.3).
- **Contract expiry inside a normal hold.** PSCs for Bakrol, Karjisan and Lohar run to
  2030, Cambay to 2029 (India Ratings, p.3). A three to five year hold from today ends at
  or beyond that. The 2P reserve base is measured only to those dates.
- **Impairment with a regulator in the loop.** Rs 10 cr written against Elad Field capital
  work in progress, because DGH approval "has not yet been received" (Results Q1FY27, p.5).
- **Cash down 80 percent** at FYE26 with working capital up. Growth capex of INR 2,000 to
  2,100 mn is planned for FY27 from internal accruals.
- **Unit error in the filed deck.** The inventory build is printed as "6,500 bopd" and
  "~6500 boepd", which are rates where a stock is meant.
- **No transcript exists.** Management has filed no earnings call transcript. For a name
  whose thesis is a drilling schedule, that removes the normal place to test the schedule.

## 10. Independent check: the credit rating

India Ratings assigned IND A/Stable/IND A1 to proposed bank facilities of INR 3,000 mn on
09-Jun-2026. Net leverage is "NM". Interest coverage is 217.3x. Liquidity is "Adequate".

The agency agrees with the strength and names the same limits this card does. Strengths:
sizeable reserve base, diversified asset base, EBITDA to increase in FY27. Weaknesses:
"Concentration risk as production skewed towards two assets", "Profitability dependent on
crude and natural gas prices", "Geological risk". On the reserves it adds the sentence that
matters most: "the current production relative to reserves is quite low, making the ramp-up
in production crucial" (p.3). Its positive sensitivity list includes "extension of PSCs",
which confirms that the contract clock is a live credit question and not a technicality.

The rating supports the balance sheet. It does not support a quality transition, because it
does not claim one.

## 11. Posture: the Transition Decision Matrix

- **Proof gate: NOT FIRED.** Volumes were flat across the last two quarters against a
  2,500 boepd target. The frac campaign that carries the guidance had not begun.
- **Ugliness: none to classify.** Optics are clean. A 70 percent EBITDA margin, no debt,
  217x interest cover. Nothing ugly is warning anyone off. Treated as ARTIFACT by default,
  because there is no structural defect to disprove.
- **Recognition gap: INDETERMINATE, and it moves with crude.** Corrected 2026-09-17 on a
  dated price. At Rs 961.80 on 07-Sep-2026 the market cap is Rs 3,365.57 cr. FY26 PAT of
  Rs 89.6 cr is 37.6 times trailing. On the FY27 paths in the addendum below the forward
  multiple runs from 10.9 to 30.8 times, depending almost entirely on the realised price
  per barrel. **An earlier version of this card read the gap as CLOSED on a trailing
  multiple of roughly 41 times, taken from an undated market cap. That read does not
  survive the forward arithmetic.** The gap cannot be called at shallow depth on a
  price-taker. No valuation is performed here and no fair value is stated.

**Posture: RESEARCH / WATCH, not PRICED NARRATIVE.** Corrected 2026-09-17. Proof not
fired plus artifact plus gap OPEN or indeterminate maps to RESEARCH / WATCH, not to the
trap cell. The earlier card put this in PRICED NARRATIVE (TRAP) on the strength of a
closed-gap read that the forward arithmetic does not support. The correction changes the
cell. **It does not change the verdict**, because the verdict below rests on the absence
of a rung climb and on the contract clock, and neither depends on the multiple.

## 12. Verdict card

**PASS.** Do not spend a full /step1 on this name now.

The reason is structural, not a quibble about the quarter. The framework hunts businesses
climbing the quality ladder before the market reprices the climb. This business is not
climbing. It is a competent, debt-free, well-run commodity price-taker that is getting
bigger. That can make money. It is not transition alpha, and it should not be dressed as
such to fit the pipeline.

**Load-bearing facts to verify first, if the operator overrides this verdict.**

1. **PSC expiry and extension status.** Bakrol, Karjisan and Lohar run to 2030 and Cambay
   to 2029. Those assets carry 91 percent of production. Get the filed extension
   applications and the government's position under the Oilfields (Regulation and
   Amendment) Act, 2025. A three to five year hold ends at the contract wall.
2. **The frac result.** The August 2026 Bakrol frac campaign is the single event the FY27
   guidance rests on. Read the Q2 FY27 filing for the post-frac production rate, not the
   commentary.
3. **The Q1 FY27 inventory build.** Confirm the 86 to 87 day custody transfer window and
   the crude inventory carried at 30-Jun-2026 against the balance sheet. It should reverse
   in Q2. If it does not, the volume line is weaker than it looks.
4. **Cash and working capital.** Free cash fell to INR 228.8 mn from INR 1,163.4 mn while
   FY27 capex of INR 2,000 to 2,100 mn is to be self-funded. Get the FY26 cash flow
   statement and resolve the agency's working-capital magnitudes against the PDF.

**What would change the view.** A filed rung mechanism would change it. That means
evidence of durable returns that do not track crude: a long-dated gas offtake at a
contracted price, a grid tie-in that fixes realisation, or a PSC extension deep enough that
2P reserves stop being clipped by the contract date. Sustained production above 2,500 boepd
with an extended contract would make this a different analysis. Absent that, a falling
crude price is the only thing that would make this name interesting, and for the opposite
reason.

**Nothing here is a price, a target or a position.** The shallow verdict decides only
whether the name earns a full run.

# Addendum: FY27 top line, bottom line and forward PE

Added 2026-09-17 at the operator's request. **This section sits outside the shallow
framework**, which stops before any forward estimate. It states no fair value, no exit PE
and no target price. Section 1B is not run and the Mental Model Declaration is unsigned.

## Why this name has no single forward number

The company publishes **no revenue or profit guidance**. The only forward figure it gives
is a volume, 2,500 boepd, and both the company and India Ratings frame it as a year-end
exit rate rather than a full-year average: "The company expects production to increase to
around 2,500boepd by FYE27" (India Ratings, p.3). So a top line has to be built from a
volume path and a price, and the price is the one input the business does not control.

**Realised price per barrel of oil equivalent, derived from filed figures.** FY26 total
income of Rs 288 cr over 1,355 boepd for 365 days is **Rs 5,823 per boe**. Q1 FY27 total
income of Rs 132 cr over 1,705 boepd for 91 days is **Rs 8,508 per boe**, which is 1.46
times the FY26 level. This is a rupee-denominated comparison and needs no exchange-rate
assumption. Both figures are derived by me from filed numbers and are not disclosed as
such.

**Volume path, mine and stated as mine.** Q1 actual 1,705 boepd. The frac campaign starts
early August, which is mid-Q2. Taking Q2 at 1,950, Q3 at 2,250 and Q4 at 2,450 to reach
the guided 2,500 exit gives an **FY27 average of 2,089 boepd**.

**Cost base, from Q1 actuals.** Q1 operating cost was Rs 38.8 cr, so Rs 155.2 cr
annualised at Q1 volume. India Ratings puts fixed costs at 80 to 85 percent of total, so
only about a fifth of that scales with volume. Depreciation is unit-of-production
depletion, so it scales with volume; FY26 depletion is derived at about Rs 48 cr. Tax at
25 percent. Interest is immaterial at Rs 0.73 cr in FY26.

**Most evidenced single path: S1.** India Ratings expects crude "to remain elevated at
about USD 90-100/bbl in the short term due to the ongoing West Asia conflict" (p.4), and
management says the frac lands in Q2. On the framework's instruction to state the most
evidenced path rather than shade inputs, S1 is it: top line about **Rs 649 cr**, bottom
line about **Rs 309 cr**, forward PE about **10.9 times**.

## What that number is, and is not

An 11 times forward multiple looks cheap and is not a margin of safety. Read the table
across, not down. The bottom line swings from Rs 109 cr to Rs 309 cr, a factor of 2.8,
and the single largest driver is a barrel price that no filing forecasts. The EBITDA
margin swings 57 to 75 percent on the same volumes, because 82 percent of the cost base
is fixed. That is operating leverage running in both directions.

So the honest statement is not "this is cheap at 11 times". It is: **at Rs 961.80 the
market is priced for crude staying near current levels, and the multiple re-rates to the
high twenties if crude returns to where it sat twelve months ago.** Buying this is taking
a view on crude, with a drilling ramp as a second-order kicker.

**The verdict does not change.** PASS stands, and for the reasons in step 12 rather than
for the multiple: no quality-ladder climb is on offer, and the PSCs carrying 91 percent of
production expire in 2029 and 2030, inside a three to five year hold. A cheap forward
multiple on a commodity does not make a transition thesis.
