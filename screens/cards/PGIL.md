## Business Understanding Narrative

**What does it do, and how does it make money?** Pearl Global makes apparel for global brands. It runs factories in India, Bangladesh, Vietnam, Indonesia and Guatemala. It earns a margin per garment shipped. FY26 revenue was Rs 5,025 crore and adjusted EBITDA Rs 468 crore at 9.3% (Q4 FY26 investor presentation, doc ad02a6fc, page 17). It shipped 78.1 million pieces against 74.3 million in FY25 (Q4 FY26 investor presentation, doc 33632b0f, page 6).

**What is changing?** Margin, through spread across countries. EBITDA margin was 7.0% in H1 FY23 against 5.2% in H1 FY22 (H1 FY23 investor presentation, doc 589777bd, page 6). It reached 10.3% in Q4 FY26, "the highest ever EBITDA margin in any quarter so far" (Q4 FY26 call, doc 85496042, page 4).

**Why now?** Capacity is in place. Installed capacity reached 101 million pieces, "significantly ahead of our earlier target of H1 FY '27" (doc 85496042, page 2). Vietnam utilisation is above 80% against 63%, and Indonesia 47% against 39% (doc ad02a6fc, page 17; call transcript, doc 1a5d0e97, pages 5 and 6).

**What must be true for the thesis to work?** Margin must reach the guided 10% to 12% band on 12% to 14% revenue growth (doc 85496042, pages 12 and 13). Guatemala must break even in FY27 (page 9).

**What breaks it?** Two things. Margin could stall below 10% as US tariffs bite; they cost Rs 19 crore at standalone level in FY26 (doc 1a5d0e97, pages 5 and 6). And the price already pays for the guide. Market capitalisation of Rs 11,035.38 crore is 42.1 times FY26 PAT after minority interest (derived: 11,035.38 / 262).

## 2. Business model and archetype

One engine: pieces shipped times price per piece, less conversion cost. Scale spreads fixed cost across five countries. Standalone India FY26 revenue was Rs 1,081 crore with PAT of Rs 69 crore against Rs 55 crore (doc ad02a6fc, page 17). So about 78% of revenue sits outside the standalone entity (derived: 1 less 1,081 / 5,025).

India partnership factories rose to 21% of India revenue from 9% in H1 FY23 (doc 589777bd, page 9). That is an asset light layer on owned capacity.

**Archetype: Outsourcing partner.** The binding variables are client concentration, wallet share, capacity fill, contract stickiness and price per piece.

## 3. Competitive advantages

Forming. The advantage is multicountry sourcing for a brand that must spread tariff and supply risk. That earns wallet share, not pricing power. Peer comparison is out of scope for a shallow read.

Switching cost is moderate. A brand can move an order next season. Cost advantage comes from Bangladesh and Vietnam labour and from fill. In house laundry is guided to earn ROCE of 18% to 20% (doc 33632b0f, page 9). Verdict: **moat forming**, resting on footprint and fill.

## 11. Posture: the Transition Decision Matrix

- **Proof gate: partly FIRED.** Q4 FY26 margin reached 10.3%. PAT after minority grew 23%.
- **Ugliness: ARTIFACT-OF-CLIMB.** The US tariff cost of Rs 19 crore is an external shock, not a decay in the business.
- **Recognition gap: CLOSED.** Forward PE of 28.7x to 35.0x and trailing PE of 42.1x on PAT after minority interest.

**Posture: EARNINGS-ONLY.** The climb is real and already priced. Returns ride EPS growth of 20% to 47%. Only the top of the guided band clears the 25% hurdle without a rerating.

Two readings on the guidance date. Reading A: 10% to 12% arrives in FY27, and the top of the step 13 range holds. Reading B: it arrives by FY28, and FY27 sits near the bottom. The separating observation: the H1 FY27 EBITDA margin.

## 12. Verdict card

This is a good business at a full price. The strategy hunts a climb the market has not priced. Here the market has priced it.
