## PLAIN-LANGUAGE BRIEF (mandatory, four parts; provenance-labelled)

### Part 1 — SUMMARY NARRATIVE

Arisinfra reported Q1 FY27 (quarter to 30-Jun-2026) with consolidated revenue of Rs 290.8 Cr,
up 37.1% from Rs 212.1 Cr a year ago, and PAT of Rs 20.0 Cr versus Rs 5.1 Cr, close to four
times higher [this-quarter filing]. Operating EBITDA margin rose to 10.5% from 8.6% [computed
from filing]. On the surface this is a strong start to the year, and the press release and
deck lead with exactly that [press release l.59-64; deck sl.34]. Underneath, three things
temper it. First, the four-times profit jump is roughly half real operating improvement and
half a one-time balance-sheet reset: finance costs fell 46% because IPO money repaid debt,
and last year's quarter carried a Rs 2.9 Cr one-off IPO-listing charge that is simply absent
this year [filing, PAT bridge]. Together those account for about 40% of the pre-tax profit
increase and do not repeat at that size, so annualising this quarter's earnings per share
overstates the sustainable base. Second, the company told a year-on-year story and left out
that revenue actually fell 15.3% and earnings per share fell 20.5% versus the prior quarter,
on a rising share count [deck sl.37]. The run-rate is decelerating sequentially, not running
ahead. Third, and most important for our thesis, the results filing is a profit-and-loss
statement only, with no balance sheet, so the one number we most need, debtor days, is not
disclosed this quarter, even though this quarter was our pre-committed test of it
[this-quarter filing; deck sl.39, F16-3]. On top of that, the company booked zero bad-debt
provision this quarter against a roughly 140-day receivable book, after charging Rs 4.5 Cr
last quarter [filing l.482]. That combination, an undisclosed master metric plus a suspicious
zero provision, is why the cash-quality read is INDETERMINATE and why the verdict is capped.
No pre-committed thesis-broken trigger has actually fired, so the position stays HELD and
unchanged [Notion Decision Status]. But we should not add: the stock was last near Rs 124,
above our Rs 110 entry ceiling, and the receivables question is open. The 06-Aug concall is
where debtor days and the zero-provision question must be answered.

### Part 2 — SECTOR INTELLIGENCE

ARIS sells into Indian construction and infrastructure, a structurally growing but deeply
cyclical and credit-sensitive end market. The company's own deck frames a roughly Rs 50 lakh
crore construction-materials industry that is highly fragmented and largely unorganised
(aggregates >80% unorganised, RMC ~55-60% unorganised) [deck sl.4-5, Redseer-sourced, prior
context]. The structural tailwind is real: public infra capex, urbanisation, and private
capex support multi-year demand [deck sl.3, macro/prior]. The sector-specific risk that bears
directly on ARIS is payment behaviour. A materials aggregator that extends ~140 days of credit
to developers and EPC contractors [Notion/AR context; FY26 140 days] is effectively financing
its customers' construction cycles, so a downturn or a single large customer stress shows up as
receivable losses before it shows up in revenue. The Notion bear frame calls this a "disguised
NBFC at a platform multiple" and notes the ~8pp EBITDA spread over an asset-owning peer is
largely a credit-risk spread [Notion/prior]. Concentration compounds it: Maharashtra is 65% of
revenue [deck sl.14 L426, this-quarter] and large infra/EPC players are 58% of the customer mix
[deck sl.15, this-quarter], so state-level or a few-account construction slowdowns are the
sector channel through which the thesis breaks. Regulation and payer mix are not the issue;
customer credit quality across the construction cycle is.

### Part 3 — BUSINESS-MODEL INTELLIGENCE

ARIS runs three streams: B2B Supply (thin-margin materials trading, ~37% of revenue, 2-2.5%
EBITDA), Contract Manufacturing (asset-light third-party plant tie-ups, ~53% of revenue, 9-9.5%
EBITDA) and Developer-as-a-Service or DaaS (fee-based, ~10% of revenue, headline 55-60% EBITDA)
[deck sl.18, this-quarter]. The model is deliberately asset-light: it aggregates vendors and
"reserves" plant capacity rather than owning it [press release l.52; deck sl.27-28]. This
quarter's margin gain is a genuine mix shift toward CM and DaaS: management says CM+Services
were 63% of revenue versus 49% a year ago [press release l.65-66, this-quarter]. Two model
cautions surface in the numbers. One, the profit is not in the listed parent. Standalone PAT is
only 37% of consolidated, and the parent's core profit before other income is negative in three
of the last four periods [this-quarter filing, Step 1C/2C], meaning the economics live in seven
unlisted subsidiaries that the new auditor did not review [filing l.387, A3-03]. Two, the DaaS
"pipeline" is quoted as Gross Development Value, the total third-party project value of
Rs 18,391 Mn [deck sl.29/35], but ARIS earns only a 10-14% fee on that, so the Rs 6,500 Mn
Wadhwa "contract" is Rs 650 Cr of GDV worth perhaps Rs 65-91 Cr of ARIS fee over 18-24 months
[deck L1000 reconciled with press release l.70, F16-5]. The model works only if working capital
stays disciplined; the headline working-capital-days improvement (66 to 56) is partly a
payables-stretch, not collection [deck sl.40, A3 arithmetic]. Model drift to watch: whether
margin is being bought with credit risk.

### Part 4 — COMPETITION INTELLIGENCE

ARIS positions itself as a full-stack, tech-enabled orchestration layer (CARA AI, ArisGPT)
across sourcing, contract manufacturing and developer services, and claims that breadth as its
moat [press release l.118-127; deck sl.6-7, this-quarter/prior]. Where it wins: an asset-light
network that it says can handle 10x volume on the same infrastructure [deck sl.32], a stated 82%
repeat-order rate [deck sl.9, this-quarter], and a services arm that turns execution risk into
developer fees. Where it is structurally weaker: the named peer benchmark, Shankara Building
Products, trades at roughly 11.3x EV/EBITDA [Notion/prior], and ARIS's ~8pp CM EBITDA premium
over an asset-owning peer at ~1.7% margin is, on the bear read, a credit-risk premium rather
than a technology premium [Notion/prior]. The anchor customer, Capacit'e Infraprojects
(Rs 800 Cr / 5yr MoU), is also in our own portfolio universe, so ARIS and its largest customer
share the same construction-cycle risk [Notion/prior]. The competitive risk to watch is not a
better-funded platform rival; it is that the "moat" (extending credit and capital to developers,
per the ArisUnitern case studies that tout infusing Rs 250 Mn of working capital and lifting a
developer's sales 3x [deck sl.30]) is capital-intensive underwriting dressed as software. If
receivables sour, the differentiation and the multiple both compress at once. No peer reported
within the +/-4-week window in the supplied material, so no peer concall cross-check is
available this run.
