## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

### 2A. Demand environment

No peer discusses "pharma/life-sciences demand" in Indegene's specific commercial sense, because none operate there (see Q1). What the peer set does show: eClerx is consistently "cautiously optimistic" across all four quarters, repeatedly citing "delayed decision-making" as real but not worsening (ECLERX Jul 2025 call, Aashray Vasa Q&A: "the only thing we have experienced is a delayed decision-making"), while actual YoY growth held in a 17%-20% band throughout — caution in language did not translate into a growth crisis. IKS Health frames demand almost entirely through its own "12% TAM growth" yardstick and shows healthy but decelerating growth (24%→18.5% YoY into Q4 FY26). Sagility is the most bullish of the four, raising its FY26 growth guidance mid-year from "20%+" to 22.5% (SAGILITY Feb 2026 call) on the back of structural payer cost pressure that is idiosyncratic to US healthcare payers, not comparable to pharma demand. Tata Elxsi is bifurcated by vertical: transportation/auto recovering strongly, Healthcare & Life Sciences repeatedly promising and then missing a return to growth. **Net: the peer set shows resilient-but-uneven demand with real pockets of softness (Tata Elxsi HLS, IKS Q4 deceleration) rather than either an industry-wide boom or an industry-wide deferral crisis — it does not corroborate or contradict Indegene's specific pharma demand framing, because no peer is in that specific market.**

### 2B. Pricing and input costs

No raw-material commentary anywhere (confirmed, Q8). On pricing: the peer set's margin-pressure narratives center on wage hikes, FX movements, bonus/variable-pay provisioning, and client-demanded cost-takeout — **not on "GenAI licensing costs" as a distinct line item**, which is the specific framing in Indegene's own B05 margin narrative (GenAI licensing + senior hires). IKS Health is the closest match, citing rising R&D investment (~4.7-5% of revenue) explicitly tied to AI build-out and stating this will "continue to inch up not down" (IKS Feb 2026 call) — a genuine analog to "GenAI investment as a margin headwind," though framed as R&D/build cost rather than third-party licensing. eClerx and Sagility do not name AI licensing costs as a margin driver at all in any transcript. This is a gap: the peer set does not test the specific "GenAI licensing" component of Indegene's margin-pressure claim, only the more general "AI investment as an opex headwind" idea, which IKS partially corroborates.

### 2C. Capex cycle

No capacity race. eClerx is the only peer visibly adding new delivery seats/centers in this window (Cairo, Lima, Peru, Mohali, Chandigarh) — but this is routine services-sector seat expansion tied to revenue growth, explicitly "demand-led," not a capital-intensive buildout. Tata Elxsi is the opposite case — sitting on spare installed capacity and planning to lift utilization from ~75% toward 80-85% before adding more (TATAELXSI Jan 2026 call) — a genuine "holding" case. IKS and Sagility show no meaningful organic capex commentary at all; IKS's only large capital commitment in this window was debt-funded M&A (TruBridge), not organic capex. **This is neither a lone-expander situation nor an industry-wide capacity race — it is a normal, low-intensity, growth-linked seat-expansion pattern typical of asset-light BPM, consistent with Indegene's own no-capex-cycle framing.**

### 2D. Competitive mentions

A full-text search for "Indegene" across all 15 transcripts returns zero hits. No peer mentions Indegene by name or by an unmistakable description (none discuss a pharma-commercialization/MLR-review/omnichannel-orchestration competitor in language that could only mean Indegene). This is expected given the peer-fit gap described above — these four peers are not competing directly with Indegene in the markets discussed on these calls.

### 2E. Risks peers discuss that Indegene's own concalls do not

- **US offshore call-center restriction risk (proposed NPRM)**: eClerx explicitly flags a proposed US rule that could cap the percentage of contact-center work telcos/cable companies can offshore, and says NASSCOM has lobbied against it (ECLERX May 2026 call, Kapil Jain, Varun Bang Q&A). Indegene's B05 does not surface any comparable US offshore-delivery regulatory risk.
- **CMS/payer regulatory risk register (Medicare Advantage rate resets, ACA subsidy extension, Medicaid expansion rollback)**: extensively discussed by Sagility and IKS (SAGILITY May 2026 call; IKS Feb 2026 call) as both risk and tailwind depending on client type. Not a direct parallel to Indegene's pharma-side risk set, but illustrates that "life-sciences/healthcare-adjacent" services peers carry a distinct regulatory risk axis Indegene's own concalls never engage with (Indegene's regulatory discussion in B05 centers on tariffs/MFN pricing/H-1B, not payer reimbursement policy).
- **System-of-record vendor encroachment**: IKS Health names Epic/Cerner/Athena as potential future competitors as EHR incumbents build their own agentic-AI task-elimination tools using their data advantage (IKS Feb 2026 call, Sachin Gupta) — a specific platform-convergence competitive risk with no analog surfaced in Indegene's own transcripts.
- **Frontier AI lab disintermediation risk**: both eClerx and IKS field direct analyst questions about whether Anthropic/Claude-type tools could let clients or new entrants build competing capability in-house (ECLERX May 2026 call; IKS Feb 2026 call). Indegene's own concalls, per B05, do not record an analyst pressing management on this specific frontier-AI-disintermediation risk, even though B05 separately flags "can domain-expertise moat be commoditized by frontier LLMs" as management's own single largest medium-term risk (Prolin Nandu Q&A, B05 3C) — the peer set shows this is a live analyst concern industry-wide, reinforcing that this risk deserves standing attention.
- **FX hedge-book asymmetry as a quantified margin swing factor**: eClerx quantifies FX contribution to margin bps explicitly every quarter (e.g., "200 bps of which came from FX," ECLERX Oct 2025 call) and discloses hedge-book rates. Indegene's own concalls, per B05, do not appear to break out FX with this granularity.

## PART 4: TRIANGULATION SUMMARY

- **Claims verified**: 2 of 9 (Q8 raw material, Q9 capex — both mandatory checklist items, confirmed not-applicable by peer silence)
- **Claims partially verified**: 3 of 9 (Q2 GenAI TAM-expander, Q5 organic growth deceleration, Q7 large-account conversion-cycle lengthening)
- **Claims contradicted**: 1 of 9 (Q4 — 6-8 quarter margin-recovery precedent; peers with comparable cycles either resolve faster or beat their own timeline, undercutting the "we've done this before" framing) — **this is the priority item for synthesis**
- **Claims unverifiable**: 3 of 9 (Q1 pharma industry CAGR, Q3 RPE magnitude, Q6 EBITDA multiples for named peers) — driven mainly by the peer-fit gap, not peer disagreement

**The single most consequential contradiction**: Q4. Indegene's B05 flagged softening specificity in its own 6-8 quarter margin-recovery framework as a flag-worthy credibility item. The peer set does not offer a reassuring precedent — the one peer with a genuinely comparable multi-quarter margin-recovery arc (IKS/AQuity) beat its own timeline by 3-4 quarters, and the two peers named in the original question (eClerx, Sagility) show no margin event of that duration at all, resolving in one quarter or within the same fiscal year respectively. This means Indegene's own slipping timeline looks more like an outlier than an industry norm.

**The single strongest independent confirmation**: Q9/Q8 (the two mandatory checklist items) are cleanly confirmed by complete peer silence on raw material and capex cycles across all 15 transcripts — a low-stakes but genuinely clean corroboration that Indegene's asset-light services framing is not an aberration but standard for this business-model class.

**Overall — does the peer set support, complicate, or undercut the main company's narrative?** It mostly **complicates** rather than cleanly supporting or undercutting. On the low-stakes structural items (no raw material, no capex cycle), the peer set is a clean, confirming match. On the higher-stakes narrative claims — the pharma demand thesis, GenAI as a pure tailwind, and the margin-recovery precedent — the peer set cannot fully test the first (no peer overlap), only partially corroborates the second (GenAI benefit is real but not risk-free even in peers that lean bullish), and actively undercuts the third (peers with comparable margin-recovery cycles moved faster, not slower, than Indegene).
