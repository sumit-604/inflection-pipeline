## PASS 3 — PATTERN PASS (new findings, on top of Pass 1 and Pass 2)

Method: re-read for contradictions between notes, numbers not tying to the main statements, deliberately vague/minimal disclosure, restated/reclassified prior-year figures, subsequent events, and going-concern language. Also cross-checked both Independent Auditor's Reports (standalone p.114-118, consolidated p.183-186) in full, including Key Audit Matters, Section 143(3)/Rule 11 "other matters," and CARO Annexures A/B, which had not been read end-to-end in Pass 1/2. Searched the source document for "restated" and "reclassif*" — no matches.

Two genuinely new, material findings emerged:

1. **🟡 ₹637.54 Cr (94% of net QIP proceeds) still sitting idle in bank deposits/escrow at FY25 year-end, only disclosed via the CARO annexure, not flagged in the notes' QIP narrative.** CARO Annexure B, standalone auditor's report, clause x(b) (p.121): "the amount raised has been used for the purposes for which they were raised except for idle funds amounting to ₹6,375.38 Mn which were not required for immediate utilization, and which have been invested in deposits with scheduled banks. The maximum amount of idle funds invested during the year was ₹6,812.10 Mn, of which ₹6,356.16 Mn was outstanding at the end of the year in fixed deposits and ₹19.22 Mn maintained with monitoring/escrow account." Against the ₹700 Cr gross / ₹681.21 Cr net QIP raised in Q4 FY25 (Note 43 standalone, p.180, cross-referenced but not itself disclosing this idle-funds detail), ₹637.54 Cr — roughly 91% of gross proceeds — remained undeployed as fixed deposits/escrow balances at March 31, 2025. By contrast, the CY23-24 IPO proceeds are confirmed FULLY UTILISED by FY25 year-end (CARO clause x(a), p.121: "maximum amount of idle/surplus funds invested during the year was ₹300 Mn and as of March 31, 2025 the company has fully utilised the amounts for which they were raised"). Given the QIP closed late in Q4 FY25, some lag is expected and this is not itself a governance red flag, but the magnitude (nearly the entire raise still parked in FDs) is a genuine capital-deployment/execution item that the notes themselves do not surface as clearly as the CARO annexure does — a case of the auditor's statutory annexure carrying more decision-relevant granularity than the notes' own QIP disclosure. Worth tracking FY26 capex conversion against the already-elevated capital advances (see Pass 2 finding #3, ₹108.43 Cr, +182% YoY).

2. **🟡 Both Independent Auditor's Reports (standalone p.114 and consolidated p.183) name exactly ONE Key Audit Matter — routine revenue-recognition cut-off — and are silent on the ₹22.56 Cr direct-to-equity derivative/put-option charge on the Azad Prime NCI (Pass 1's #1 finding, 25.8% of consolidated PAT).** This is a genuine disclosure-emphasis pattern worth surfacing: the single largest, most judgment-intensive, P&L-bypassing accounting item in the entire filing (a written-put valuation at the higher of fair value or a ₹22.56 Cr fixed floor, resulting in a ₹22.56 Cr equity debit and a ₹17.15 Cr non-current redemption liability) did not rise to the level of a communicated Key Audit Matter in either report, while a comparatively standard revenue point-in-time cut-off risk did. This does not imply an audit deficiency (KAM selection is an auditor judgment call and the item is fully described in the notes themselves, Note 43 consolidated p.243), but it is a useful signal for how much weight the auditor placed on the item relative to its size, and reinforces the case for a direct management question on the derivative structure (see Section D below).

No going-concern language was found anywhere in either auditor's report (both are clean, unqualified opinions; Section 143(3)(f)/Rule 11(g) reservation is limited solely to the previously-identified audit-trail database-level exception, consistent with Pass 1 finding #9); the Board's own going-concern responsibility paragraph is boilerplate in both reports. No restatements or reclassifications of prior-year figures were found anywhere in the document. CARO clause (xi)(a) also independently confirms "no fraud by the company or... on the Company has been noticed or reported during the year," and clause (ix)(a)-(b) confirms no loan/borrowing default and no wilful-defaulter declaration — both corroborative, not new risk signals.

All other notes, sub-notes, footnotes, RPT items, contingent liabilities, and financial-instrument disclosures were re-checked against Pass 1 and Pass 2 on this pattern read and found consistent with what was already extracted; no further contradictions or numeric mismatches between the notes and the main financial statements were identified.

### D. FIVE QUESTIONS FOR MANAGEMENT

1. What is the technical/engineering basis for the 15-year useful life applied to plant & machinery versus the Schedule II default of 7.5 years, and is an independent third-party technical assessment available for review?
2. Can management reconcile the contradictory customer-concentration disclosures in the consolidated notes — Note 31(c), which states 11 customers each individually exceed 10% of revenue, versus Note 34(B), which states only one customer exceeds 10%?
3. What caused the ₹1.91 Cr "Other timing difference" deferred-tax line in FY25, and why do the standalone and consolidated tax-rate reconciliations allocate the same ₹1.97 Cr earlier-year tax charge so differently between the two sets of notes?
4. What is the deployment plan and timeline for the ₹637.54 Cr of QIP proceeds still held in fixed deposits/escrow at FY25 year-end, and what is the expected capitalisation schedule for the ₹108.43 Cr of capital advances already paid out?
5. Under what circumstances could Leo Primecomp exercise its put option on the 49% Azad Prime stake (fixed floor ₹22.56 Cr), and what is the Company's funding plan should the ₹17.15 Cr redemption liability crystallise?

### E. NOTES-BASED RED FLAGS

- **Aggressive accounting:** the plant & machinery useful-life extension to 15 years (double the Schedule II statutory default) with no independent technical validation disclosed is the primary aggressive-accounting signal in the filing (Note 2.2.E(ii), p.136).
- **Earnings-management-adjacent (not outright manipulation):** the ₹22.56 Cr direct-to-equity derivative charge and the FY24 one-off-inflated "other income" (86% of that year's total) both sit outside normal P&L/YoY comparability channels; neither is manipulation, but both require explicit normalisation in any valuation work built on reported PAT trends.
- **Undisclosed/under-disclosed risk indicators:** the contradictory customer-concentration note; capitalisation threshold NOT FOUND IN DOCUMENT; unquantified litigation exposure on the Mangampet land writ petition (Note 3(a) note 3, p.145); an unexplained personal guarantor (Mr. Ravi Kumar Viswanadha) securing a subsidiary-level equipment-finance loan with no disclosed relationship to the Company (Note 15/16 consol, p.219); the QIP idle-funds detail surfacing only via the CARO annexure rather than the notes' own QIP disclosure.

### F. ONE-LINE NOTES VERDICT

The notes reveal moderate accounting practices. Key concern: an aggressive plant & machinery depreciation policy (15-year life vs Schedule II's 7.5-year default) compounded by a ₹22.56 Cr equity-bypass derivative charge on subsidiary NCI that neither Key Audit Matter addressed. Key strength: a clean, low-judgment point-in-time revenue model with zero contract assets/liabilities across both years and an unqualified audit opinion with a clean internal-financial-controls opinion. Overall accounting quality: 6/10.
