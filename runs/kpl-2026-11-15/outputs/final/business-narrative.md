## Business understanding narrative

Kwality Pharmaceuticals, founded in Amritsar in 1983, makes finished generic medicines in five plants. Injectables were about 48% of FY26 sales and tablets 38%, with capsules and other forms making up the rest. About 90% of sales come from supply under registrations, which are government permits to sell one drug in one country, and contract manufacturing to a client's own specification makes up the other 10%. A buyer cannot easily drop a supplier whose product sits on a local registration, because a switch needs a fresh filing and a fresh approval. The filings do not say whether KPL or its partners hold most of those registrations, so the run did not establish who owns that switching cost. The customers are more than 100 distributors, licensees and Indian traders across Latin America, the Middle East, Europe, Asia and Africa. Half of FY26 sales were booked in India, and the rating agency says many of those buyers export the drugs again. SD Pharma, the Indian arm of Mana Pharma, is one of them, which makes SD Pharma and Mana Pharma purchase volumes a signal to track. No customer share, country split or contract term is filed, though management put Algeria alone at Rs 80 to 90 Cr in FY26, about 17% of sales. Present demand comes from distributors and tender buyers in semi regulated markets, and each new approval opens a market. COFEPRIS Mexico product approvals stand at 18 to 19 of about 70 filings, and INVIMA Colombia approvals and plant audit gate the Colombian leg. India pharma exports to LAC and Africa grew about 17% and 6% in H1 FY27, and peers grow 10 to 30%, so KPL's faster growth reads as share gain, not a market boom. Future growth rests on Saudi SFDA registration of filed products, on EU-GMP status and EU registrations, and on an oncology expansion due in June 2027. The hormone plant and the biologics wait on CDSCO approvals: Unit 6 licence, EPO trial, Pembrolizumab. Management gives no market size, plant capacity and approval speed bind before the market does, and USD/INR sets what each export earns in rupees. The emerging moat scan scores 10.3, class NONE, and the supply line earns its only Moderate readings for filed EU GMP certificates at four of five plants and for its dossier library. A plant certificate does not lock in a customer, and a rival with equal plants has an equal route, so even the supply line has no proven moat. The contract manufacturing line shows no moat, talent asymmetry and the cannibalisation barrier both score zero, and oncology, hormones and biologics are options until they sell.

Growth shows in sales, not in cash

KPL sold Rs 503 Cr of generic medicines in FY26, up 36%, and earned Rs 67 Cr of profit after tax. Revenue has doubled from Rs 251 Cr in FY23. Half the sales go to more than 70 countries. The other half is booked in India, partly with traders who ship abroad. The founding family runs the company. Promoters own 54.9% and have pledged nothing.

The company wants to climb from plain generic supply into oncology, regulated European markets, hormones and biologics. Oncology was 22% of Q2 FY27 sales. Gross margin fell from 50.7% in FY25 to 48.6% in FY26 and to 46.2% in Q1 FY27, then rose to 52% in Q2. EBITDA margin moved from 23.5% in FY26 to 27.5% in Q2 FY27. Management raised the FY27 revenue guide twice, from Rs 650 Cr to Rs 700 Cr and then Rs 720 Cr, and H1 came in at Rs 344 Cr. The claimed engine is a shift toward registrations KPL holds itself, which earn more than partner held ones. No filed document gives that split, so the engine is untested. Among peers, only Senores shows a 40% margin on regulated sales, and Senores owns those approvals.

The backward scorecard rates KPL GOOD, at 80 of 160, with a core score of 61 against a pass line of 60. Profit compounded at 39.5% a year, and return on capital rose from 17% in FY17 to 22% in FY26 on a conservative proxy. Cash caps the grade. The cash block scores 4, below the floor of 8, so the grade cannot rise above GOOD. The framework verifier found return on capital computed on a substitute basis. On the strict reading the core score falls to 52 and the grade to AVERAGE. Two other small changes also reach AVERAGE, at 59, so GOOD sits on a knife edge.

FY26 profit was Rs 67 Cr, but operating cash flow was about Rs 17 Cr, a conversion of 25%. Receivables rose 82% to Rs 280 Cr while revenue rose 36%. They stood at about 203 days of sales, with no provision for bad debts. At least Rs 1,166 lakh of balances overdue by more than a year left the ledger in FY26 with no disclosed route. Suppliers carried the gap. Payables rose 82% to Rs 102 Cr, and without that rise operating cash flow would have been negative. Over ten years, free cash flow on the proxy sums to about minus Rs 2.5 Cr. H1 FY27 conversion improved to 50%, yet receivables rose another Rs 55 Cr to Rs 335 Cr. The reported fall to 178 debtor days comes from a larger sales base, not from collection. The rating agency calls the long credit a feature of a growth phase in semi regulated markets. One reading is growth credit that will unwind. The other is a model that sells on long credit for good. Receivables ageing by customer and country would separate them, and the company has not filed it.

Management beats its sales and margin promises and misses its dated ones. Q1 FY27 revenue of Rs 162 Cr beat a Rs 150 to 160 Cr guide, and Q2 revenue of Rs 181 Cr beat Rs 160 to 170 Cr. The CFO says he guides low so that fewer questions come. Every dated plant and pipeline milestone slipped. EPO moved by about a year, hormone plant approval by seven to eight months, and the oncology expansion from March to June 2027. The FY27 oncology revenue figure fell from Rs 150 Cr to Rs 100 to 110 Cr with no acknowledgement. The debtor day target loosened from 150 to 160 days to 165 to 170, and H1 came in at 178. The filed Q2 commentary calls Rs 50 Cr of EBITDA profit after tax, while the filed profit is Rs 29 Cr. Q2 tax ran at 31.2%, and the Rs 122 Cr profit guide needs about 22% tax in H2. The concall stage grades management B, on 9 promises delivered, 7 partial and 5 missed of 21. The red flag verifier reads C, because two questions were dodged on both calls: which global auditor, and how large the Middle East debtors are.

The statutory auditor, Vijay Mehra & Co., resigned on 12 November 2026, about 25 months into a five year term. Its letter gives one reason: "Due to Pre-occupation in other assignments". B S R & Co. LLP took its place the same day, subject to member approval. Management had named KPMG on both calls, and the Q2 deck still says KPMG. All five executive directors come from one family, and two independent directors left before their terms ended, within 11 months. Two new companies with a director interest traded with KPL in FY26. One, Kaler Biopharmachem, returned Rs 263 lakh of goods against Rs 121 lakh bought, while the board report says no conflicted deal took place. On the other side, there is no pledge and no promoter selling. ICRA upgraded the rating to BBB+ in May 2026, after 32 months of the company not cooperating with the agency. The promoter check rates governance CAUTION. Web reports that tie an ED attachment and a SEBI order to KPL concern Kwality Ltd, a dairy company.

Peers do not support the cash story. KPL's 178 debtor days compare with 117 to 136 at Caplin, 90 to 104 working capital days at Senores, and 89 to 101 at Lincoln on an old call. The day bases differ, so the comparison is directional. No peer says the 2026 Gulf disruption slowed its own collection. Caplin's management said "100 to 130 days even is probably still okay". Peers fund capex from cash, while KPL's gross borrowings rose from Rs 130 Cr to Rs 146 Cr in H1. Management says LATAM has very little competition, yet Caplin and Senores are both entering Mexico. KPL's Mexico approval rate of about 27% trails Caplin and Senores, though the verifier notes the ratios use different bases.

Management gives no market size. The run's estimate puts the serviceable market at about Rs 54,000 Cr, where KPL holds about 0.45%, so the market does not bind. Capacity does. The FY27 guide already uses about 93% of a rough full use ceiling. The FY29 target of Rs 1,000 Cr needs 26% a year from FY26. It sits Rs 112 to 203 Cr above the ceiling on one capacity method and needs 93% use on the other. New registrations at the stated pace explain only 11 to 40% of the step up to FY29. Management's own downside without hormones and biologics is Rs 800 to 850 Cr, so those unproven lines sit inside the Rs 1,000 Cr target. The market stage rates the runway STRONG, but nobody has written down the thresholds for that label.
