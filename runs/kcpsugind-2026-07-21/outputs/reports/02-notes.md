## PASS 3 PATTERN FINDINGS

### P3-1. Disclosure-granularity asymmetry — a recurring pattern across the notes, not an isolated item
Laid side by side, the notes show a consistent split: items involving cash paid to identifiable individuals are disclosed to the rupee (KMP remuneration table, Note 52, pp.114–115: nine line items each stated to single-rupee precision), while items involving larger, more consequential balances are left qualitatively undescribed:
- "Claims receivable" ₹183,962,074 / "Claims Received" ₹54,980,802 — no nature disclosed anywhere (Note 13 p.98; Note 28 p.102; Pass 2 Finding 1).
- Engineering Division land/building purchase, ₹117.3mn combined outlay — no budget, timeline or return expectation (Note 55, p.115; Pass 1 Finding 4).
- Capital work-in-progress, +144.5% YoY to ₹26,473,489 — no project-level itemization (Balance Sheet p.80; Note 3, p.94; Pass 2 Finding 14).
- Pending title-deed transfer covering land **and buildings** from the "The KCP Limited" demerger — no quantum disclosed at all (Note 3.1 p.94; Auditor Annexure A(i)(c) p.75; Pass 2 Finding 5).
- Three named related parties (V M Rao Consultants, Anibrain Digital Technologies, Sethi Funds Management) with zero disclosed transactions or balances anywhere in 265 pages (Note 52(A) p.114; Pass 2 Finding 4).

🟡 Watch — pattern-level finding. None of these five items individually breaches a bright-line materiality threshold, but the pattern of granular disclosure for small, individually immaterial KMP items alongside silence on several multi-crore or structurally significant items is a disclosure-quality signal in its own right, distinct from any single line item.

### P3-2. Auditor's Key Audit Matter scope does not reach the year's most consequential accounting judgment
The Independent Auditor's Report designates a single KAM: "Determination of net realizable value of inventory of sugar" (p.71), addressing a ₹210.71cr carrying value. The auditor concludes management's NRV determination "is considered to be reasonable." No KAM is designated for (a) the ₹111.24mn deferred tax asset recognized on current-year business losses (Note 21, p.101; Pass 1 Red Flag #2) or (b) the classification/measurement of the ₹315.81mn unrealized fair-value gain that is the dominant driver of reported PBT (Note 28, p.102; Pass 1 Red Flag #1) — both judgments involving materially larger rupee amounts and, in the DTA case, a forward-looking "reasonable certainty" judgment that is harder to verify than a physical-inventory NRV calculation. 🟡 Watch — a scoping observation, not an assertion that the audit itself is deficient; simply that the disclosed KAM does not track the disclosure item an investor would most want independently tested.

### P3-3. CSR exemption corroborates a multi-year (not single-year) core-profitability weakness
Required CSR spend was Nil for FY21 because average net profit over the statutory three-year lookback (Sec.135(5)) was negative (Note 37, p.104; Directors' Report, p.44; Pass 1 §12f). Read together with the FY20 reported loss (Pass 1 §12a: FY20's ₹119.46mn fair-value *expense* pushed FY20 to a loss) and the FY21 core operating loss of ₹331.98mn before Other Income, the CSR-exemption trigger independently corroborates that the operating weakness is a two-to-three-year pattern, not a single bad year — relevant context for the borrowing-ceiling increase and diversification narrative (Pass 2 Finding 3). 🟡 Watch — synthesis point, not a new document fact; strengthens the read on Pass 1 Red Flag #1 and #4 rather than standing alone.

### P3-4. Numbers that do not reconcile between two notes describing the same underlying activity
Note 56 (Segment Reporting, p.116) reports "Others" segment external revenue of ₹207,372,029, but the only product the segment footnote names ("Bio Fertilizers") corresponds to just ₹67,120,243 in Note 38 (Product-wise revenue, p.104) — a ₹140.15mn gap that reconciles almost exactly to the new bought-out sugar trading revenue (₹129.70mn export-trading + ₹10.45mn domestic-trading, Note 27/38; already surfaced in Pass 2 Finding 9). This is a direct instance of the "numbers in the notes not matching across disclosures" pattern named in this pass's brief, now framed explicitly as a reconciliation gap rather than a revenue-quality point alone. 🟡 Watch.

### P3-5. No going-concern language, no restatements — confirmed on the pattern re-read
Consistent with Pass 1 §12f: no going-concern qualification or emphasis-of-matter paragraph appears anywhere in the Auditor's Report; the Directors' Responsibility Statement affirms preparation "on a going concern basis" in standard boilerplate only (Item 28(d), p.27). No restatement of prior-year comparatives was found in either pass; the new "Assets held for sale" line (Note 15, p.99) is a first-time presentation with PY shown as Nil, not a restatement. No events after the balance sheet date materially changing the picture were disclosed beyond the standard negative-assurance statement (Directors' Report Item 19, p.29).

No further material new findings emerged from the pattern re-read beyond P3-1 through P3-4 above.

### D. FIVE QUESTIONS FOR MANAGEMENT

1. What is the operating rationale, capital budget, and expected return for the new Engineering Division at Trichy, and how does the enhanced ₹300 crore borrowing ceiling relate to further diversification plans beyond the ₹11.7cr already committed?
2. What is the nature of the ₹183.96mn "Claims receivable" balance and the ₹54.98mn "Claims Received" income (Note 13, Note 28), and what is management's basis for assessing collectability and recurrence?
3. Given the core business posted an operating loss of ₹331.98mn in FY21 (before Other Income), what is management's basis for concluding "reasonable certainty" of sufficient future taxable profits to recognize a ₹111.24mn deferred tax asset on current-year business losses?
4. Why has the doubtful-debt allowance on trade receivables remained flat at ₹2.75mn for two consecutive years despite the >180-day overdue bucket growing from ₹69.53mn to ₹75.13mn, and is the Ind AS 109 "simplified approach" lifetime-ECL model being reassessed on a portfolio basis each period?
5. Is there a revival plan, sale timeline, or updated impairment assessment for the Lakshmipuram unit's ₹78.58mn held-for-sale assets, and what is the quantified value of the pending land-and-building title-deed transfer arising from The KCP Limited demerger?

### E. NOTES-BASED RED FLAGS

1. Reported PAT (₹191.64mn) is a product of a ₹315.81mn unrealized equity fair-value gain and a ₹75.997mn deferred-tax credit, together far exceeding the ₹331.98mn core operating loss (Statement of P&L p.81; Notes 27–34, 28, 21).
2. ₹111.24mn deferred tax asset recognized on current-year business losses, premised on "reasonable certainty" of future profits in a year whose only profit source is a non-recurring fair-value gain (Note 21, p.101).
3. Permanent closure of the Lakshmipuram sugar unit; ₹78.58mn of plant assets reclassified to held-for-sale with no disclosed Ind AS 105 impairment test (Notes 15, 43, pp.99, 106).
4. Over ₹11.7cr deployed into an unrelated engineering/fabrication business via an NCLT insolvency e-auction with no disclosed strategic rationale, budget, or return expectations, alongside a shareholder resolution raising the borrowing ceiling to ₹300cr explicitly for "further diversification" (Note 55 p.115; AGM Notice Item 4, p.5, p.13).
5. Eimco-K.C.P.'s (subsidiary) reported PAT was inflated by more than a quarter through an unexplained ₹10.32mn partnership-firm profit swing (from a ₹1.33mn loss the prior year), a subsidiary-level repeat of the parent's non-operating-earnings pattern (Eimco Note 25 p.159; Note 5 p.155; Cash Flow p.141).

### F. ONE-LINE NOTES VERDICT

The notes reveal concerning accounting practices. Key concern: reported PAT is entirely a function of a non-recurring equity fair-value gain and a deferred tax asset premised on that same non-recurring profit, while the sugar/chemicals/power core business posted an operating loss and is being diluted by an unexplained diversification into engineering. Key strength: no restatements, no going-concern language, an unqualified audit opinion, and no undisclosed policy changes; disclosure mechanics for KMP pay, gratuity, and contingent liabilities are granular and well anchored. Overall accounting quality: 4/10.
