## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

**2A. Demand environment.** MEDPLUS's demand story is internally generated (incentive-structure changes, new-customer acquisition advertising), not a claimed industry-wide acceleration — it does not echo an "IPM is accelerating" narrative in any form. REDINGTON and RPTECH both describe a genuine, quantified, industry-wide IT hardware demand supercycle tied to the Windows 10 end-of-support transition (October 2025) and AI PC adoption: RPTECH calls FY26 "the most consequential year for Indian ICT distribution industry in over a decade" with record 15.9mn PC units shipped (+10.2% YoY, RPTECH May-2026 call); REDINGTON's ESG (PC) segment grew 21-28% YoY across the same window. This is a real, named, industry-wide demand story in tech distribution — a different pattern from what MEDPLUS discloses in pharma retail, and neither maps onto Entero's IPM-acceleration claim (see Q1).

**2B. Pricing and input costs.** REDINGTON and RPTECH both report a persistent, structural component-cost inflation cycle (DRAM/NAND/GPU pricing up 20-300% depending on component, RPTECH Feb-2026 call) passed through to end customers via ASP increases rather than absorbed into distributor margin ("we are not seeing any advantage on additional gross margins for us, but the price is all being passed on to the customer," REDINGTON Feb-2026 call). This is a commodity-hardware pricing dynamic with no pharma analogue in MedPlus's disclosures. It does not corroborate or contradict Entero's margin-mix (MedTech/commercial-role) narrative directly, but it does illustrate that in an adjacent distribution industry, margin expansion is coming from mix shift toward solutions (SSG/semiconductor), not from pricing power on the commodity base — structurally consistent with, though not proof of, Entero's own mix-driven (rather than pricing-driven) margin story.

**2C. Capex cycle.** Not a lone-expander situation. Both tech-distribution peers are simultaneously investing in higher-margin adjacent capability at the same time: REDINGTON is building out SSG capability (CloudQuarks digital platform, Redington Academy, professional-services headcount, AI Exchange) and exploring inorganic bolt-ons in the same space; RPTECH is building a semiconductor subsidiary (131% YoY growth off a small base, RPTECH May-2026 call), moving into embedded/AI solutions, and expanding branch footprint into Tier-2/3/4 towns. This is an industry-wide capacity/capability race toward higher-margin adjacent categories among the two comparable ICT distributors, not one outlier chasing growth alone — directly relevant to the Cross-Peer Hypothesis below.

**2D. Competitive mentions of Entero.** None. Entero is not named or described (directly or by unmistakable reference) in any of the 12 transcripts. This is unsurprising given none of the three peers competes in B2B pharma wholesale distribution.

**2E. Risks peers discuss that Entero does not (feeds missing-risks analysis).**
- Geopolitical/macro revenue-exposure quantification: REDINGTON quantifies its West Asia conflict exposure precisely ("about 30 to 35 percentage of our business happens from this market," REDINGTON May-2026 call) and tracks the quarter-by-quarter P&L impact (INR44 crore Arena exit-cost loss, impairment of INR75.2 crore group PAT impact, same call). Entero (per B05) offers no comparable quantified-exposure disclosure practice for its own concentration or macro risks.
- Receivables transparency under stress: RPTECH proactively discloses and quantifies large single-customer receivable delays (Yotta AI data-center deal, Tamil Nadu government deal) and their cash-flow impact quarter to quarter (RPTECH Aug-2025, Nov-2025 calls). This directly contrasts with Entero's refusal to disclose debtor/receivables aging when asked directly (per B05 red flag), against a backdrop of receivables growing 50.6% vs revenue growing 29.4%.
- Single-vendor/brand concentration risk: RPTECH explicitly quantifies and discusses the business impact of a principal (Micron) declaring a "crucial" consumer product line end-of-life, naming the expected revenue hit and mitigation plan (RPTECH Feb-2026 call, response to Gaurav Bhanshali). No equivalent single-principal concentration risk disclosure appears in Entero's materials as summarised in B05.
- Disintermediation/channel-shift risk: MEDPLUS treats quick-commerce as an ongoing, openly discussed competitive risk across its calls, distinct from the single unnamed-peer Amazon-exit anecdote Entero appears to lean on (per B05 peer_questions) without offering its own quantified channel-risk framework.

## PART 5: CROSS-PEER HYPOTHESIS

Redington and RPTECH are structurally different businesses (large diversified conglomerate distributor vs a mid-sized regional ICT distributor) that are, independently and at the same time, running the identical strategic playbook: shift mix from pure hardware/commodity distribution toward "solution-driven economics" adjacent categories (software/cloud/security for Redington's SSG; semiconductor/embedded/AI for RPTECH), each explicitly citing the same underlying mechanism — that providing solutions rather than moving boxes earns structurally higher margin. Neither peer frames this as an industry-wide trend in its own commentary; each describes it purely as its own strategic choice. But the fact that two competitors in the same core industry, of different sizes, are pursuing the same pivot at the same time, for the same stated reason, suggests the underlying driver is not company strategy but industry-wide margin compression in the commodity core (visible in REDINGTON's TSG margin compression and "hardware part of the market is getting more commoditized... expectation from brands and OEMs is to really give lower and lower margins," REDINGTON Nov-2025 call) that is forcing every distributor in the category toward the same adjacent-category climb.

This is testable: if commodity/core-distribution gross margins at both peers continue compressing over the next 3-4 quarters while their respective solution/adjacent segments keep growing at 30%+ with margin premium intact, the hypothesis (sector-wide commoditization forcing a common margin-mix pivot across distribution business models generally, not specific to IT/ICT) is supported — and it would suggest Entero's own MedTech/commercial-role pivot is not a company-specific insight but participation in a broader, cross-industry distributor response to core-margin compression. If either peer's core hardware margin stabilizes, or if one peer's adjacent-category growth proves to be a one-off (e.g., RPTECH's semiconductor line fails to scale beyond its current small base), the hypothesis is falsified for that peer.
