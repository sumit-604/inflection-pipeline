### 2C. The 3.6-vs-1.33 Chemdist reconciliation (RESOLVED as far as disclosure allows; residual FLAGGED)

Management's opening bridge is: standalone EBITDA **14%** on Rs 170 Cr, then "chemist…operating loss of about **3.6 crores**…that's why EBITDA has come down to about **11.8%**" (turn 32). But the consolidated segment disclosure prints the Process-Equipment **segment result at −Rs 1.33 Cr** (L606), and the implied consolidated EBITDA drag (std reported EBITDA 23.78 − consol reported EBITDA 21.95) is only **~Rs 1.8 Cr**. Three figures, three bases:
- **Rs 3.6 Cr** = the Chemdist **entity-level operating loss** (its own P&L, gross of other income and before consolidation eliminations) — the figure management used verbally.
- **Rs 1.33 Cr** = the **consolidated segment RESULT** (PBIT-type, after allocations/eliminations) — the disclosed, line-anchored number.
- **~Rs 1.8 Cr** = the arithmetic **EBITDA bridge** std→consol.

The ~Rs 2.3 Cr gap between the spoken 3.6 and the disclosed segment result 1.33 is **not reconcilable from disclosed data**. It is consistent with (a) an entity-standalone vs consolidated-segment basis difference, and (b) the large **NCI absorption of −Rs 1.84 Cr** (Role 4 §S-vs-C), which itself signals the minority holds a big stake — reinforcing the unresolved "JV vs subsidiary" label question (A3-F14-02). **Flagged UNRECONCILED → Questions-for-Management (Q C2).** Do not let the smaller disclosed −1.33 lull the read: management's own words put the Chemdist operating hole at Rs 3.6 Cr.

### 2D. GUIDANCE-CHANGE FINDINGS — both rails are effectively cuts (stated plainly)

1. **Revenue guidance 20-25% now vs 25-30% recorded in the thesis (A3-F6-02).** This is a **downward reset of ~500 bps at the top end**, delivered with the words "remains intact." It is not intact; it is lower.
2. **EBITDA margin 12-14% now vs 14-15% structural in the thesis (A3-F6-01).** A **~100-200 bps downward reset.** When an analyst (Kamesh Bhandari, R2, turn 118/133) explicitly challenged the change — "last concall we were guiding roughly 14 to 15%… now 12 to 14… can you clarify… does it include other income" — management answered "for our understanding what we have announced was **12 to 14%. But if for some reason we are missing something… these numbers we will recheck it**… **there is no change… it remains the same**" (turn 119/134). So management (i) could not confirm its own prior rail, (ii) promised to "recheck," and (iii) asserted "no change" over a number that is demonstrably lower, and (iv) **left unanswered whether 12-14% is on an ex-other-income basis** (A3-F17-04) — material, because the clean operating margins this quarter (std 10.5%, consol 8.8%) sit **below even the reset 12-14% floor**.

**What the resets do to the FY27 model (base case: Rs 915 Cr revenue / 13.5% EBITDA / Rs 81 Cr PAT — derived, provenance [N], illustrative arithmetic, not an extraction cell):**
- **Revenue.** FY26 consol actual = Rs 818.55 Cr (filing). New 20-25% → **Rs 982-1,023 Cr** (consol basis) — *above* the Rs 915 base. On the standalone FY26 base (755.61), 20-25% → **Rs 907-945 Cr**, which brackets Rs 915. So the revenue rail, even reset, does not by itself threaten the base-case revenue line; the Rs 915 base looks conservative-to-mid on a consolidated read. **Revenue is not the problem; the margin is.**
- **Margin.** Base case assumed **13.5% EBITDA**. That sits at the **top of the new 12-14% band and below the old 14-15% band.** At the **midpoint (13%)** the base-case margin is missed; only hitting the top of the reset band preserves it. This is a downgrade of the central margin assumption from "comfortably inside the band" to "requires the top of a lowered band."
- **PAT.** The Rs 81 Cr base-case PAT is a **13.5%-margin construct.** Holding revenue at ~Rs 982 Cr (consol, +20%) but taking EBITDA to the **13% midpoint** instead of 13.5%, and carrying the consolidated tax/NCI drag this quarter exposed (ETR 34.2%, NCI −1.84 Cr while Chemdist loses money), pushes realistic FY27 PAT **below the Rs 81 Cr base**. Net: **the FY27 model's revenue line survives the reset; the margin and PAT lines are pressured downward.** Formal re-forecast is deferred to Role 1 with the Q2 balance sheet; this review flags the direction as **downward on margin/PAT**.

**Diagnostic answers:** management **lowered** (not tightened) both load-bearing rails → increasing uncertainty, not confidence; the lowering was **not explicitly acknowledged** (major credibility flag, Step 3); guidance is **internally consistent** at the reported-incl-OI level but **quality-inconsistent** (the spoken margins embed other income the operating figures strip); vs the four-pillar/thesis base, the margin guidance is now **below** the prior structural assumption; and the guidance analysts pressed for and did **not** get was the **ex-other-income margin definition** and the **CFO/debtor-days** numbers.

## MANDATORY PLAIN-LANGUAGE BRIEF (updated for the concall)

Provenance key: **[Q]** = this quarter's filing/deck/press release/concall; **[N]** = Notion prior/peer memory. Metrics the disclosures did not provide are named as such.

### Part 1 — Summary narrative (what happened and what it means)
JNK India's Q1 FY27 concall confirmed the numbers but weakened the story. On the filing **[Q]**, consolidated revenue rose 80.6% to Rs 186 Cr and consolidated PAT was Rs 9.6 Cr, and management repeated both on the call. The important news was in the guidance and the evasions. Management now guides FY27 revenue growth to **20-25%** and EBITDA margin to **12-14%**, and called both "intact / unchanged" **[Q]** — but the thesis record was **25-30% revenue and 14-15% margin [N]**, so both rails were quietly lowered while being described as the same. When an analyst pointed this out, management said "we will recheck… nothing has changed," and would not say whether 12-14% is measured before or after other income **[Q]** — which matters, because the clean operating margins this quarter are only 10.5% standalone and 8.8% consolidated **[Q]**, below even the lowered floor; the 14% and 11.8% management quoted include other income. The consolidated margin fell to 11.8% because the Chemdist green-hydrogen unit lost money; management called that an "operating loss of about Rs 3.6 Cr," which does not tie to the Rs 1.33 Cr segment loss in the filing **[Q]** — an unreconciled gap we are flagging. Three of the five conditions that would justify adding to the position — operating cash flow positive, debtor days under 180, and a permanent CFO — were **neither disclosed nor even asked** on the call **[Q]**; there was no CFO on the line at all. So the binding gate is still **0 of 5**, and cash conversion, the standing deal-breaker, stays **indeterminate [Q/N]**. Two genuinely new items: a large export order booked on 8 June was cancelled because a third-party licensor (the end-user's, not JNK Global's) withheld approval in time — proof that even directly-awarded export work can die on a licensor **[Q]**; and there is an **activist-investor lawsuit at the parent JNK Global** over its board decisions, which matters because JNK Global posts the bank guarantees and channels the payments for the Nigeria/Africa export orders that are ~82% of the book **[N]** — management downplayed it. On the positive side, management explained a real working-capital comfort (the parent posts BGs and passes payments; Reliance and BPCL Bina pay on friendly terms), promised no equity or debt raise for 4-6 quarters, and dated the Chemdist break-even to FY27 year-end **[Q]** — but the WC comfort runs through the very parent now in court, and it is not a substitute for the cash-flow number they did not give. Net: the core business still looks operationally real, but the guidance was cut and denied, the cash question was dodged, and a new parent-litigation risk appeared. **Decision: HOLD the 4% position, no add-back (gate 0/5), no exit** — and treat the Q2 half-yearly cash flow as the event that resolves it.

### Part 2 — Sector intelligence
JNK sells fired heaters, reformers and cracking furnaces into refining, petrochemicals, fertilisers, steel and hydrogen — a project-capex business whose order book tracks process-industry investment **[N]**. The call reinforced two sector truths. First, **licensor and technical approvals are the real entry barrier** in this industry, and they cut both ways: the same barrier that protects JNK's qualified positions also killed a directly-awarded export order when the end-user's licensor did not approve JNK in time **[Q]**. Second, the demand picture is a large but slow-converting pipeline: management claims a **>Rs 6,000 Cr opportunity pipeline, 50/50 domestic/export [Q]**, but guides the new-segment win rate to only **10-12% versus 20-25% in heating [Q]** — conversion, not pipeline size, is the constraint, and the order book actually fell 8.2% sequentially **[Q]**. The structural tailwind the thesis leans on — India refinery/petchem capex and energy-transition work (hydrogen, flares/incinerators, offshore) — is real but early; JNK's own energy-transition unit (Chemdist) is still loss-making **[Q]**. Regulation of note: nothing new beyond the known Labour Codes. No payer-mix dynamic (capital-equipment EPC, not a regulated-payer business). No peer concall in the window to cross-check **[Q, stated not fabricated]**.

### Part 3 — Business-model intelligence
JNK earns by engineering, fabricating and commissioning project equipment against an order book, so revenue is lumpy and backended — management reiterated that Q1 is only 10-15% of the year and H2 is 60-70% **[Q]**. Unit economics this quarter: gross margins are healthy but operating EBITDA margin is only 10.5% standalone / 8.8% consolidated once other income is stripped **[Q]**, below the 12-14% the company now guides and the 14-16% the thesis wanted **[N]**. The model's real question is cash: it is working-capital intensive, cumulative CFO/PAT is −0.15x with structurally elevated WC per CRISIL **[N]**, and the call disclosed **no cash-flow number** while changing revenue recognition from the output to the **input method** (which pulls revenue forward) and carrying **~Rs 200 Cr of unbilled revenue [Q]** — an earnings-quality combination that needs the Q2 schedules. The most important model insight from the call is how the working capital is actually financed: **JNK Global (the parent) posts the bank guarantees and passes customer payments through to JNK India**, so JNK executes large export orders without posting its own BG **[Q]**. That is a genuine advantage and a genuine dependency at the same time — it is why the parent's litigation is a model risk, not just a governance headline. Model drift to watch: growth is being sourced from a loss-making 51%-type subsidiary (Chemdist) and the charter is being widened into offshore/metals EPC and an Iraq branch, with **capex left un-numbered [Q]**.

### Part 4 — Competition intelligence
JNK's edge is qualified, complex fired-heater/reformer/cracker capability plus the JNK Global (Korea) partnership for technology and references, behind high approval barriers **[Q/N]**. The call sharpened the dependency: management confirmed that **export fired-heaters/reformers/crackers route back-to-back through JNK Global**, while only incinerators/flares and domestic work are booked directly with "no JNK-Global qualification needed" **[Q]** — so the parts of the book that are truly independent are the smaller, adjacent ones. Four different analysts pressed the parent relationship **[Q]**, confirming the market shares our 82%-dependency concern **[N]**. Where JNK wins: mid-size ($30-60mn) complex packages that L&T and larger EPC players skip, from a Mumbra fabrication base **[Q]**. Where it is structurally weaker: the new offshore/metals/renewables verticals, where it admits it needs **third-party technology partners (3-4 tied up)** and a lower 10-12% win rate, and where the Thermax Bio-CNG retreat is the cautionary peer precedent **[N]** — validated this quarter by Chemdist's own loss **[Q]**. The competitive risk to watch: if the JNK-Global share of the book is not falling, if directly-awarded export wins keep dying on licensor approvals, and if the parent litigation disrupts the BG channel, the moat is narrower and more parent-dependent than the thesis assumes — and the offshore/heavy-EPC pivot would put JNK against entrenched large-cap contractors where it has no track record. Named peer benchmarks disclosed this quarter: none (no peer concall in window) — flagged, not fabricated.
