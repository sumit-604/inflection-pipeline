## PASS 3: PATTERN-BASED RE-READ

Read the notes a third time looking specifically for contradictions between notes, numbers that do not tie out to the main statements, deliberately thin disclosure relative to surrounding detail, prior-year restatements/reclassifications, post-balance-sheet events, and going-concern language.

**New pattern-level observations (incremental to Passes 1-2, not repeating them):**

1. **Loss-allowance table notation ambiguity.** Pass 1's consolidated loss-allowance comparative for FY25 (₹192.33 Lakh) is presented identically in both the FY26 report's own comparative column and appears difficult to independently cross-foot against the RHP's FY25 closing allowance because the RHP uses a different note-numbering convention. This is a presentation/comparability nuisance, not evidence of a restated number — flagged only so a reader reconciling loss-allowance movement year over year knows the comparative is a same-report carry-forward, not an independently re-verified figure. 🟡 Watch, low materiality.

2. **Interest income on related-party loans implies a rate above the disclosed 6% p.a.** Note 8 discloses loans to related parties (standalone) at ₹3,596.41 Lakh, 6% p.a., repayable on demand. Pass 2's granular interest-income breakdown (Note 47(II)) sums to ₹336.19 Lakh (Fabtech Technologies International ₹45.42 + FABL ₹66.54 + Fabtech Technologies LLC UAE ₹214.23 + Mark Maker ₹10.00 Lakh) against a year-end balance of ₹3,596.41 Lakh — a ~9.3% implied yield if the balance were static all year, versus the disclosed 6% rate. This is explainable by balances building through the year (average balance below year-end balance) or a blended rate across counterparties not uniformly at 6%, but the note does not disclose a per-counterparty rate to confirm either explanation. 🟡 Watch — NOT FOUND IN DOCUMENT: per-counterparty interest rate reconciliation.

3. **No contradiction found between the standalone and consolidated Independent Auditor's Reports** on the going-concern question — both unmodified, both silent on material uncertainty. Confirmed a third time. NONE.

4. **No further restatements or reclassifications of prior-year figures found** beyond the already-flagged FABL deconsolidation (Note 52/59, driving the inventory and goodwill movements in Pass 1 Section 5/6) and the RHP-vs-AR structural change in reporting entity (RHP covers a slightly different consolidation perimeter pre-IPO). No note uses the words "restated" for FY26 vs FY25 AR-to-AR figures.

5. **Post-balance-sheet events remain limited to the dividend proposal** (Pass 1 Section 12) — the pattern-level observation is that this is the only disclosed subsequent event in a year that also saw ~₹179 Cr of IPO proceeds sitting unutilized and an M&A ("inorganic growth") tranche of the IPO proceeds at 0% deployment; no forward-looking disclosure on planned use is present despite the RHP-era practice (Note 56) of disclosing forward-looking pro-forma impacts for pending transactions. 🟡 Watch — a deliberately thinner disclosure posture post-listing versus pre-listing on forward-looking items, worth noting as a pattern rather than a single-note flag.

No further material new findings emerged from the pattern re-read beyond the above five secondary points; all financial-statement figures reconciled internally (CARO guarantee total to contingent liability note already cross-checked and confirmed clean in Pass 2).

## E. NOTES-BASED RED FLAGS

- **Undisclosed related-party capital transaction:** ₹19.71 Cr freehold land purchase from a related party omitted from the RPT note.
- **Undisclosed likely related-party lease:** ~11x, ₹26-29 Cr lease-liability build-up with no lessor named; circumstantial evidence points to a related party.
- **Provisioning inconsistency risk:** ECL curve-shape change concentrated away from the auditor's own KAM bucket, with no disclosed rationale.
- **Aggressive related-party funding footprint:** loans to related parties at 99.37% of all loans given; 55.7% of consolidated current borrowings owed to a related party; purchases ~22% of total purchases from related parties.
- **Earnings-quality flag:** bad-debt provision step-up (nil/₹7.74L to ₹530-544L) landing in the IPO year.
- **Revenue-recognition judgment risk:** shipment-based recognition trigger for turnkey contracts with no contract asset/unbilled revenue ever disclosed, despite the natural expectation of one.
- **Customer-concentration instability:** an 8x YoY jump by a single customer to 12.55% of revenue alongside near-halving of three prior top customers.

No evidence of outright earnings manipulation (e.g., channel stuffing, fictitious revenue) was found; the flags above are disclosure-completeness, provisioning-judgment, and related-party-fairness concerns rather than confirmed fraud indicators.

## F. ONE-LINE NOTES VERDICT

The notes reveal concerning accounting practices. Key concern: an undisclosed ₹19.71 Cr related-party land purchase and an unexplained ~11x related-party-linked lease build-up sit alongside a deteriorating, auditor-flagged receivables position with an unexplained ECL curve-shape change. Key strength: clean statutory-compliance disclosures (Note 55), no going-concern language, and candid disclosure of exceptional-item run-rate distortion and CARO reconciliation. Overall accounting quality: 4/10.
