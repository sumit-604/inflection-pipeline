## 2. Findings

### D-1 (MAJOR): Cross-peer misattribution in Part 2E ("Risks peers discuss")

B06's Part 2E states: *"High-grade iron ore import dependency: SARDAEN Q3 FY26 notes Indian
iron ore imports reached a seven-year high of 12+ MT in FY26 despite higher domestic
production, 'highlighting robust consumption trend' but also a persistent high-grade ore
supply gap for the sector (implicit in GPIL's May 2026 call, p.4-5)."*

I read the entire SARDAEN Q3 FY26 transcript (SARDAEN-Concall_Feb_2026_Transcript.pdf, all 12
pages). This statistic — "seven-year high," "12 million ton(s) plus," and the phrase
"highlighting robust consumption trend" — does not appear anywhere in that transcript.

The exact language, verbatim, is in **GPIL's Q4 FY26 call** (GPIL-Concall_May_2026_Transcript.pdf,
Page 5 of 23): *"India witnessed a sharp rise in the iron ore imports and reached a seven year
high of 12 million ton plus in FY '26 driven by strong steel demand and shortage of high-grade
ore required by domestic mills. Despite higher domestic production, imports increased
significantly highlighting robust consumption trend in the Indian steel sector."*

B06 has the attribution backwards: it credits SARDAEN with the primary finding and downgrades
GPIL's language (which is explicit, not "implicit") to a secondary corroboration. This is a
sourcing error, not a fabrication — the fact itself is real and correctly quoted somewhere in
the 13 transcripts — but citing it to the wrong peer/call, and mischaracterizing an explicit
statement as merely "implicit," fails the "SUBSTANTIVE [claim] without a real, findable
citation [at the stated source]" bar for this specific item. This also means B06's stated
peer-count-of-two for the demand/pricing-and-input-costs synthesis in Part 2B/2E is
overstated by one for this particular sub-claim: it is a single-peer (GPIL-only) finding
dressed up as two-peer corroboration.
