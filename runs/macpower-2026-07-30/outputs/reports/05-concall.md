### 1C. Trigger evolution across the three quarters

- **Capacity (2,000→2,500)**: STRENGTHENING. Delivered on schedule in Q2 FY26 call itself; utilisation ~85% cited Q3 FY26, ~80% FY26 actual / 90% target FY27 in Q4 FY26 call.
- **NEXA mix**: STRENGTHENING, consistently. 27% executed (Q2 FY26) → 39% of order book (Q3 FY26) → 40% of pending order book (Q4 FY26). The one trigger with a clean, unbroken up-trend across all three calls.
- **60-acre govt land**: WEAKENING / repeated timeline slippage. Promised "December end" (Q2 FY26 call, itself already a restatement of a Q1 FY26 promise) → pushed to "March 2026" (Q3 FY26 call) → still unsigned as of Q4 FY26 call, now vaguely "3-4 months." Flagged directly by analyst Hiren Modi in the Q4 FY26 call, who noted the plan was first announced in March 2024. **Timeline slippage, three-plus quarters running.**
- **JV/technology transfer**: UNCHANGED/stalled. NDA signed with 3 of 5 shortlisted foreign partners by Q2 FY26; "finalization stage" by Q3 FY26; still "waiting for land" by Q4 FY26 — effectively frozen because it is entirely gated on the slipping land trigger.
- **Defence/aerospace vertical**: bid pipeline STRENGTHENING (₹319 cr, Q3 FY26 → ₹376 cr under evaluation, Q4 FY26) but actual revenue contribution admitted to be "in single digit" of total revenue as of Q4 FY26 call, despite two years of high-profile customer name-drops (HAL, BHEL, Ordnance Factory, ISRO/Chandrayaan references). Narrative outpaces revenue reality.
- **Backward integration / 25% EBITDA margin**: UNCHANGED, and goalposts moved. Q3 FY26 call frames 25% as achievable "after fully operation of new plant" in "2-3 years." Q4 FY26 call, under direct questioning from analyst Piyush Jain, clarifies the 25% is now specifically tied to the (still-undated) 60-acre land, not the nearer 13-acre facility. This is a subtle re-anchoring of a headline target to the least certain input.
- **Export markets**: WEAKENING / DROPPED IN PRIORITY. Q2 FY26 call: first international show (EMO Germany) in years, machines sold in Europe, active dealer expansion. Q3 FY26 call: "significantly increase our export business," gradual entry. Q4 FY26 call: explicit reversal — "I will not focus that much on export now... I have low hanging fruits [domestically]." A trigger that was gaining rhetorical momentum for two quarters was explicitly deprioritised in the third.
- **Car-case business (2 large OEM clients)**: DROPPED TRIGGER. Mentioned with a concrete near-term milestone in Q2 FY26 call ("we'll start the sampling in a quarter four"). Never mentioned again in Q3 FY26 or Q4 FY26 calls — no update on the sampling outcome, no explanation for the silence.
- **Finished-goods inventory (~80 machines)**: NEW TRIGGER, appearing only in Q4 FY26 call. Framed by management as a deliberate strategic move (de-risking a 1-year import pipeline of ball screws, LM guideways, rotary tables, encoders) that also happens to coincide with a first-time cost-without-matching-revenue booking driven by customer loan/subsidy documentation lags. Worth watching for recurrence.

### 2B. Excuse pattern analysis

- Both outright misses (land timeline, twice) are **external-blame**: government approval processes, a defence-and-aeronautics land policy that expired and was extended amid Gujarat local elections. The explanations are detailed and mechanistically plausible (named policy triggers, specific dates), not vague hand-waving — this is a mitigating factor.
- Management is **proactive** on the land topic: in the Q4 FY26 call, Rupesh Mehta volunteers the land status in his opening remarks before any analyst asks (p.3-5), and in each call the land update is delivered unprompted.
- On the **margin-dip** question in Q4 FY26 call (Garvit Goyal), management gave a specific, non-defensive breakdown: marketing cost from three roadshows/exhibitions, plus job-work cost from limited in-house component capacity, plus the finished-goods inventory cost booked without matching revenue. This reads as **honest-admission** style, not deflection.
- On the **market-share inconsistency** (4.5% cited Q2/Q3 FY26 calls vs 1% of consumption/2% of production cited Q4 FY26 call), management responded to direct questioning (Kushal Mondal) with a denominator explanation but did **not** explicitly acknowledge having previously used a different, higher number — closer to **deflection** than honest-admission.
- On **"number of machines sold"**, asked in every single call, management refuses every time, citing competitive sensitivity ("it is helping our peers' company to mapping," Q2 FY26 call p.20; "I will not explain," Q3 FY26 call p.8; declined again implicitly in Q4 FY26 call p.9-10) — a consistent, explained **silence**, not random dodging, but it removes an independent check on realisation-times-volume math every quarter.
- No instance across the three calls of management explicitly saying "we made a mistake" or "we got this wrong."
- **Pattern check verdict: external-blame-heavy on the one recurring miss (land), but with unusually detailed and proactive disclosure; one unresolved data-consistency deflection (market share); one goalpost-narrowing instance (margin timeline) surfaced only under analyst pressure, not proactively.**

### 2D. What they are NOT saying

- **Exact machine unit volumes** — withheld in all three calls despite repeated analyst requests, which prevents an independent cross-check of the realisation × volume = revenue arithmetic that management's own guidance depends on.
- **A firm, binding date for the 60-acre land** — every quarter offers a new estimate, never a signed date; by Q4 FY26 call the number itself (60 acres, ~₹40 cr market value cited, p.23) is being renegotiated against a still-unpublished Gujarat defence policy.
- **Reconciliation of the market-share figure** — the shift from "4.5%" to "1% of consumption / 2% of production" is never explicitly reconciled as the same metric restated, even though an analyst asked directly.
- **Named JV/technology-transfer counterparty** — after 2+ quarters of "NDA signed with 3 of 5 shortlisted companies," no company, country, or product category is ever disclosed, beyond generic references to Europe/Japan/Korea.
- **Follow-up on the car-case business** — raised with a specific Q4 FY26 sampling milestone in the Q2 FY26 call, never mentioned again; management is not asked about it again either, so the silence is mutual rather than management alone avoiding it.
- The financials and industry situation (heavy dependence on a single land parcel for the entire margin-expansion and JV story) would reasonably prompt a contingency plan disclosure if the 60-acre land falls through entirely; management discusses the 13-acre land as a partial hedge but never directly addresses what happens to the 25% EBITDA margin target or the 10,000-machine plan if the 60-acre parcel is never secured.

## SECTION 3: COMPETITIVE INTELLIGENCE FROM CONCALLS

### 3A. Competitor commentary and credibility check

- Management repeatedly frames the Indian CNC machine-tool market as "6-7 players neck and neck," claiming Macpower is "in the same cube" on quality/IS-standard compliance and "also number one" in some unnamed segments with 30-40% segment share (Q4 FY26 call, p.15-16) — a specific-sounding claim with no named segment, moderate credibility.
- On German/Italian competitors: management concedes India (and Macpower) does not yet manufacture certain higher-end categories (some 5-axis, certain HMC variants) that these players supply — a candid admission of a real capability gap, which lends credibility to the more confident claims elsewhere.
- On Chinese competitors: management is dismissive — "reliability of Chinese machine is zero," Chinese share "not more than 5% [by value]," limited to "tiny," entry-level, one-year-life machines bought by first-time entrepreneurs (Q3 FY26 call, p.17-18). Management cites an upcoming BIS/BRTS-type standard to curb Chinese CKD assembly, but the effective date itself shifts — "September-October" (Q3 FY26 call, p.17) becomes "October-November" (Q4 FY26 call context) — a minor but real inconsistency in an otherwise confident claim.
- Analysts (not management) separately note peers Jyoti CNC ("coming up with capacity, will take one more year") and Lokesh Machines ("has been sanctioned") as evidence of industry-wide capacity tightness (Q2 FY26 call, Runit Kapoor, p.9) — management does not dispute or elaborate on this framing.

### 3B. Industry and market intelligence

- IMTMA data cited by management (Q4 FY26 call, p.4, sourced to "Indian Machine Tools" data released at a Goa summit): Indian machine-tools domestic consumption ~₹35,000 cr (2026), domestic production ~₹16,000 cr, consumption projected to reach ₹54,000 cr by 2030 and ₹2,30,000 cr by 2049; India ranked 9th globally in production, 4th in consumption; global machine-tools market ~$82 bn, India's share ~5%.
- Sector growth rate cited variously as 15-25% (Q3 FY26 call, p.12) and 20-30% for the import-substitute CNC segment specifically (Q3 FY26 call, p.15).
- Import dependence: management states ~45-55% of Indian CNC consumption is still imported (Q3 FY26 call, p.15, with some internal phrasing ambiguity between "45-50% import" and "50-55% domestic"); turning machines ~90% domestically produced by value of consumption, VMC ~80%, HMC ~60-70% (up from ~5-10% domestic a decade ago, per management, Q2 FY26 call p.6).
- Customer financing dynamic: PSU/government-linked customers receive 25-35% capital subsidy plus interest subsidy via bank/NBFC loans, which creates a structural payment-timing lag that management says it has now partly solved via an NBFC tie-up (Q3 FY26 call, p.5).
- Pricing: imported machines carry a ~2-2.5x price premium in India versus domestic due to duty and ~25-30% logistics cost; conversely the EU tariff on Indian exports into Europe is only 7.5%, a relative advantage Macpower says it is starting to exploit post-EMO Germany (Q3 FY26 call, p.14).
- Regulatory pipeline: a new BIS-type standard (referred to as "BRTS" by management) intended to restrict low-cost Chinese CKD-assembled machine imports, expected to bite from around September-November 2026 (dates vary slightly by call).

### 3D. Customer and order-book signals

- **Wins/reference accounts**: expanding beyond tier-3/tier-4 base into named corporates and defence/PSU accounts — BHEL, HAL, Ordnance Factory, Polycab, Bhagat Forge, KM, GNA Gears, Amic Forging, Emerald Tyre, NIPHA, Kirloskar (Q4 FY26 call, p.3); Kirloskar and Graphite India named in Q2 FY26 call before management pulled back on naming further customers ("not good to discuss," p.6).
- **Deliberate concentration strategy**: management explicitly avoids tier-1/tier-2 bulk-discount-seeking customers due to capacity constraints, preferring margin-protective tier-3/tier-4 focus, financed via third-party NBFC/financial-institute credit rather than direct company credit (Q2 FY26 call, p.9-10).
- **Geographic spread**: new branches opened in Baroda and Gurgaon; Nashik and Kolkata upcoming; new tech centres in Pune, Kolhapur, Mumbai (West); network at 9 branches/8 tech centres by Q4 FY26 (p.4).
- **Payment-term renegotiation**: receivable days trending up (from ~33 days FY25-ish base to ~45-46 days per Q2 FY26 call, p.7); defence customers specifically carry ~90-day terms; management manages this via NBFC-intermediated financing rather than extending direct credit (Q2 FY26 call, p.9-10); receivables reported at ₹46 cr in Q4 FY26 call, largely tied to government/PSU loan-sanction timing (p.19).
- **Order book trajectory**: ₹350 cr (Q2 FY26 call) → ₹375 cr, +17% (Q3 FY26 call) → ₹406 cr, +23% YoY (Q4 FY26 call) — steady sequential growth consistent with the "25% new order each quarter" framing.
