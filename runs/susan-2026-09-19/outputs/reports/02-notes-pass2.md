## PASS 2 NEW FINDINGS

### F1. Government-vs-private customer mix reversed in two years — the AR
never discloses this at all (RHP-sourced; resolves the LBF3 gap)
RHP's "Internal Risk Factors" revenue bifurcation table (RHP p.19, l.1612-1631):

| | FY26 | % | FY25 | % | FY24 | % |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Government entities | 9,638.23 | 35.78% | 6,705.23 | 49.40% | 9,370.37 | 90.55% |
| Private Entities | 17,297.43 | 64.22% | 6,868.75 | 50.60% | 977.84 | 9.45% |

Government share of revenue fell from 90.55% (FY24) to 49.40% (FY25) to
35.78% (FY26); private-entity share rose from 9.45% to 64.22% over the
same two years. 🔴 Red Flag (undisclosed in the audited AR). The AR's
notes give no customer or channel disaggregation anywhere (Pass 1 already
recorded this as NOT FOUND); the RHP shows the number was always
available, was material, and shows the company's revenue base flipped
from government-DISCOM-led to majority-private-buyer-led inside the same
two years that trading revenue (Note 18) went from 14.4% to 38.4% of
revenue. The two shifts move together and were not disclosed together
anywhere the audited investor would see them.

### F2. RHP's own restated financial statements show the AR's Note 3
"₹8.89 Lakh Prior Period Adjustment" is a mis-dated FY25 item, not a
legitimate FY26 bypass-of-P&L charge
RHP Annexure 7 "Details of Reserve & Surplus as Restated" (RHP p.207,
l.13436-13441): Retained-earnings opening balance FY26 = ₹743.24 Lakhs;
"Less: Prior Period Expenses" = "-" (nil) for FY26, FY25 and FY24 alike.
The AR (Note 3, p.66, per Pass 1) shows an FY26 opening balance of
₹752.14 Lakhs and a ₹8.89 Lakh "Prior period adjustment" charged directly
against FY26 reserves. ₹752.14 − ₹743.24 = ₹8.90 Lakhs, matching the
AR's ₹8.89 Lakh item (rounding). 🔴 Red Flag, escalating Pass 1's finding
#3: the RHP's own restatement work confirms this item belongs to FY25
(it needed to sit in FY25's opening reserves, per AS-5 prior-period
treatment), not FY26. The AR chose instead to bypass FY26's P&L and hit
FY26 reserves directly — the treatment the RHP's own restated numbers
show was wrong. This is not a labelling quirk; it is a demonstrated
non-compliant P&L bypass in the audited AR, evidenced by the company's
own later restatement.

### F4. RHP litigation section: the FY26 "GST demand" in AR Note 32 was
actually a departmental search-and-seizure action — not disclosed as such
anywhere in the AR
RHP "Outstanding Litigation and Material Developments" (RHP p.264,
l.16711-16726): a GST inspection was conducted 07-Jan-2026 at the
company's Sahibabad Industrial Area (Ghaziabad) premises; aluminium wire
and packing material valued ₹49.53 Lakhs were found and treated by the
authorities as "unaccounted stock liable for confiscation," seized under
Section 67(2) of the UP GST Act; a show-cause notice dated 16-Jan-2026
followed; a demand order (DRC-07) dated 27-Jan-2026 imposed a ₹8.91 Lakh
SGST penalty and required the seized goods be released only on payment;
the company appealed 13-Mar-2026 and the appeal is pending. 🔴 Red Flag.
AR Note 32 (per Pass 1) discloses only "New GST demand ₹8.92 Lakhs,
notice dated 16-Jan-2026" — the inspection, the seizure of goods, the
"unaccounted stock" characterisation and the confiscation threat are
absent from the AR entirely. This is a materially thinner disclosure of a
regulatory enforcement action than what actually happened, in a document
signed after the underlying facts (inspection 7-Jan-2026, order
27-Jan-2026) were already known to management (AR signed 25-May-2026).

### F6. RHP's 3-year KPI history shows the working-capital deterioration
began between FY24 and FY25, not FY25-to-FY26 as the AR's 2-year table
implies
RHP Annexure 33 (p.234, l.14773, l.14777): Inventory Turnover Ratio 5.60x
FY26, 5.76x FY25, **13.41x FY24**; DSCR 3.57x FY26, 2.98x FY25, **0.89x
FY24**; Net Working Capital Turnover 4.10x FY26, 4.04x FY25, 6.95x FY24.
🟡 Watch. The AR's own Note 33.12 (2 columns only, FY25 vs FY26) reads as
"roughly stable" on inventory turnover (Pass 1's characterisation).
Extending the same ratio back one more year shows inventory turnover more
than halved between FY24 and FY25 (13.41x → 5.76x, i.e. inventory days
roughly tripled from ~27 to ~63) and stayed there in FY26 — the
deterioration is an FY24-to-FY25 step change that had already happened
before the two years the AR itself tabulates, not a trend visible only in
the audited AR's own comparatives. The FY24 DSCR of 0.89x (RHP) also
means FY24 operating cash flow did not fully cover that year's debt
service — a weaker starting base than the AR's own 2-year window ever
shows an investor.

RHP "Our Business" product-revenue table, 3-year view (RHP p.136/l.1889-1933,

### F10. Accounting policy 1.14 (Preliminary Expenditure) states such
costs are "amortized fully in the year in which they are incurred";
Note 17 instead capitalises IPO costs as a short-term advance
Note 1.14 (p.61 per AR text, l.2342-2343): "Preliminary Expenditures are
amortized fully in the year in which they are incurred." Note 17 (p.74,
per Pass 1) shows "Preliminary Expenses-IPO ₹35.27 Lakhs" sitting as a
short-term advance (a balance sheet asset) at 31-Mar-2026, not expensed.
🟡 Watch. Share-issue expenses are commonly adjusted against securities
premium rather than expensed (a defensible alternative to 1.14 on its
own terms), but the item is labelled "Preliminary Expenses" — the exact
term Note 1.14 governs — and the stated policy for that term is full P&L
amortisation in the year incurred. The AR does not explain why this
item, so labelled, sits outside its own stated policy.
