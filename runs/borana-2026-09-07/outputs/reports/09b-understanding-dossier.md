### PART A — THE FROM STATE

**A1. Archetype.** One business line: grey (greige) fabric weaving is 90.95% of FY26
revenue (B04 revenue_streams). Archetype: **Commodity converter** (CLAUDE.md
ARCHETYPE LIBRARY — "spread, utilisation, cycle position, cost-curve rank";
Section 1B v3.7 Amendment 17 binds). Texturised (PTY) yarn (4.56% of revenue) and
beam/downgrade/waste sales (4.49%) are byproducts of spare capacity, not separate
business lines (B04 revenue_streams).

**A2. The simple analogy.** Borana Weaves buys polyester filament yarn, texturises
some of it in house, and weaves the rest on water jet looms into unbleached grey
cloth. It sells that cloth to traders and to dyeing-and-printing houses next door in
Surat, who dye, print and finish it before it becomes dress material or home
furnishing fabric (B04, business-narrative.md). The looms themselves are off-the-shelf
machines, about USD 9,500 each (B09-derived per business-narrative.md), bought by
any funded entrant (B04 moats_present). No customer is named in the annual report;
the largest disclosed buyer in every period FY22 to 9M FY25 is Borana Industries
LLP, a promoter-group firm, at 5.54% to 8.34% of grey-fabric revenue, with no
disclosed price basis (B02 top_findings, B04 flags). There are no long-term
agreements with either customers or suppliers (B07 flags, RHP p.1535, p.3485).

### PART B — THE TRANSITION

**B1. From → To.** FROM: **R1 COMMODITY PRICE-TAKER** on the CLAUDE.md QUALITY
LADDER — no durable pricing power, cash generation volatile. This reading is drawn
directly from B04, which scores `pricing_power: "weak"` and classifies the business
as a "thin-moat converter whose margin edge is mostly product-mix plus two subsidies

moat scorecard (B01), which reads FORTRESS with a Core score of 79 and four of

analyst_note calls that moat "narrow-and-deep," with zero contribution from scale,
regulation, distribution or float (B01 analyst_note). The operator should
stress-test this tension directly: Gate 0's structured scorecard and B04's narrative
judgment do not fully agree on how much pricing power exists today.
TO (claimed, not evidenced): **R3 VALUE-ADDED / SPEC'D SUPPLIER** — partial pricing
power from a richer value-added/technical-textiles mix, captive renewable power as a

## SECTION 3: BUSINESS UNDERSTANDING NARRATIVE

Drafted per prompts/13-synthesis-pipeline.md BUSINESS UNDERSTANDING NARRATIVE
section, the five-question spec, from B03/B04/B06/B07/B09. This is a Halt 1 draft;
Stage 13's copy is the final version.

**1. What the products are and why they matter.** Grey (unbleached) polyester
fabric is 90.95% of FY26 revenue (B04 revenue_streams). It is a raw material, not a
finished good: the buyer dyes, prints and finishes it before it becomes dress
material or home-furnishing cloth, and every printed polyester length sold in the
Surat trade starts as woven greige cloth someone had to make (B04, narrative
synthesis). Texturised (PTY) yarn is 4.56% of revenue and beam/downgrade/wastage
sales are 4.49%, both byproducts of spare texturising capacity rather than separate
product lines (B04 revenue_streams).

**2. Who the customers are.** No customer is named in the FY26 annual report; the
RHP's disaggregated data (FY22 to 9M FY25) shows the largest single buyer of grey
fabric in every disclosed period is Borana Industries LLP, a promoter-group firm,
at 5.54% to 8.34% of grey-fabric revenue, with no disclosed price basis (B02
top_findings rank 1; B04 flags). Buyers overall are traders and dyeing-and-processing
houses, roughly 85% concentrated in south Gujarat, with nil export revenue
(B04 business_type, market_definition per B09). The RHP discloses no long-term
agreements with either customers or suppliers, so switching costs are structurally
low (B07 flags, RHP p.1535, p.3485). Customer concentration is worsening, not
improving: the top ten grey-fabric buyers rose from 30.82% of that revenue in FY22
to 40.27% in 9M FY25 (B07 flags).

**3. Why there is demand.** Present demand comes from Surat processing houses
buying cloth to dye and print for the domestic market. Two B09 SECTION 6 downstream
candidates track it directly: Borana Industries LLP grey-fabric offtake (the
largest disclosed customer relationship) and China POY/PFY/PTA import volumes and
pricing into India, which set the input-cost floor for every weaver in the cluster
(B09 downstream_candidates).

**4. Why demand should grow.** Three forward drivers are externally checkable and
one is not yet. The Ministry of Textiles PLI Scheme (MMF Fabrics) Round 3 gave the
company a Letter of Approval, but no incentive amount or milestone is disclosed
anywhere in the corpus (B09 downstream_candidates, B07 flags). Gujarat Textile
Policy 2024 subsidy notifications run against growth, since the five-year-per-unit
power-and-interest subsidy window starts lapsing for Unit 1 in 2026 (B04 flags,
B09 downstream_candidates). BIS QCO litigation status on polyester yarn imports is
the live, checkable one: a High Court stayed the November-2025 removal and ordered
the quality-control order to remain in force until further orders (B09 flags,
downstream_candidates). Not yet checkable: whether the claimed 20-25% value-added
mix share is actually rising, since no deck discloses a quantified figure (B04
mgmt_questions, B07 optionality_register). The defined market grows about 7.3% a
year and the company holds 7.1% of the addressable slice, roughly 14x revenue
headroom (B09 tam_growth_pct, current_sam_share_pct, revenue_headroom_x).

**5. Where the competitive advantage sits.** On the single business line (grey
fabric), the run found no durable moat it could score. The emerging-moat scan
returned em_score 3.1 on a scale whose ceiling is near 90, classification NONE,
with zero of 22 categories (plus Family I) reaching even Moderate strength; the
only two rows to score anything (H3, renewable-power build; R1, PLI approval) were
both Weak (B07 em_score, em_classification, active_categories). The looms
themselves are off-the-shelf machines any funded entrant can buy; the cost
advantage that exists is a time-limited state subsidy, not a structural position
(B04 moats_present, B07). The texturised-yarn and waste lines carry no separate
moat claim; they are byproducts of spare capacity (B04 revenue_streams,
unit_economics). Category 21/22 (per B07's scan structure) returned no evidence for
this business: 18 of 22 categories plus both Family I rows returned NO EVIDENCE
FOUND, and the customer-concentration and cash-generation categories returned
evidence running opposite to what an emerging moat requires (B07 analyst_note).

## SECTION 5: PLAIN-LANGUAGE SUMMARY

1. Borana Weaves buys polyester yarn and weaves it into unbleached grey fabric on
   water jet looms in Surat, Gujarat.
2. Grey fabric is 91 percent of revenue. Texturised yarn and scrap sales fill out
   the rest, both leftovers of spare capacity, not separate businesses.
3. FY26 revenue was Rs 389 crore. EBITDA was Rs 92 crore, a 23.6 percent margin.
4. Buyers are traders and dyeing and printing houses, mostly in south Gujarat. No
   customer is named in the annual report.
5. The largest buyer of grey fabric in every disclosed period is Borana Industries
   LLP, a promoter family firm, with no disclosed price basis.
6. Demand exists because every printed or dyed polyester length sold in the Surat
   trade starts as this raw grey cloth someone has to weave.
7. The addressable market has room. The company holds about 7 percent of its slice,
   and that slice grows about 7 percent a year, leaving roughly 14 times headroom.
8. Growth still depends on execution, not market size. Three-year capacity is
   sufficient only if a Rs 350-400 crore expansion gets funded, and it is not
   funded yet.
9. The run found no durable competitive moat on the grey-fabric line. The emerging
   moat scan scored 3.1 on a near-90 point scale, with zero categories reaching even
   moderate strength.
10. The cost edge that exists today comes mostly from a state subsidy and modern
    machinery, not from pricing power or customer lock-in, and the subsidy starts
    lapsing in 2026.
11. The business is trying to climb from a plain fabric maker toward one with
    pricing power, built on scale, captive power, and a richer product mix. The
    evidence for that climb is thin so far.
12. Cash generation is the weak point. Five years of cash flow add up to a loss of
    Rs 195 crore, and only one of those five years was cash-flow positive.
13. Management communicates rarely, and some of what it does say does not hold up.
    One investor deck doubled every margin on one printed page, and a stated power
    saving is larger than the entire expense line it claims to save.
14. Governance carries an unresolved thread. Five of six board seats also sit on
    the board of a related company that regulators searched in April 2026, and the
    subject of that search is still undisclosed.
15. The corpus could not establish the current rating agency's read on working
    capital and debt cover, the SEBI search's subject matter, or whether the FY26
    margin band is durable converter economics or a reversible import-cost accident.
