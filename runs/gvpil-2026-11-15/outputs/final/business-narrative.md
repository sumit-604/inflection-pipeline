Service orders climb while profit quality lags

## BUSINESS UNDERSTANDING NARRATIVE

GE Power India repairs, upgrades and supplies spare parts for coal boilers, steam turbines and generators in India, most of them GE designs or BHEL builds. The filings split FY26 revenue by accounting nature only: 60% came from contracts booked over time and 39% from product and spares sales. Management put core services at about 55% of revenue in Q2 FY26, and the run did not establish the full year share by business line. The core work is short cycle: a generator stator rewind, a set of turbine blades, a precipitator refurbishment, spares for a planned outage. A coal unit that trips loses generation, and its critical rotating parts need OEM drawings, which the company takes from its parent under licence. The legacy lines, FGD pollution control equipment and new build projects, are being closed out, and the Durgapur fabrication factory leaves for JSW Energy. The buyers are central, state and private coal generators, and the annual report names NTPC, Adani, Monnet Power, RattanIndia Power and RRUVNL among them. The top two customers took 38.6% of FY26 revenue against 34.8% in FY25, and the filings do not name them. Customers order per job, the company executes inside about twelve months, and its wins on rival fleets show that buyers do move between repairers. Present demand comes from outages and breakdowns on an ageing fleet, tracked as "NTPC and state genco outage plans and spares tenders" and "All-India coal PLF and generation". "Non-GE fleet owners (Adani Power, JSW Energy, Chinese-make operators)" gave 53% of core orders in Q3 FY26 but 43.6% over the full year. Forward demand rests on "CEA R&M and life extension programme phases", where a 2022 list holds 63.4 GW of units, and on upgrade awards that take three to four years to commission. "MoEFCC FGD applicability notification and compliance dates" now work against the company, because only about 8 GW of category A plants must still fit FGD. "BHEL spares and services bookings" measure the rival on the same fleets, and "JSW Energy Durgapur demerger completion and supply terms" track an entity that is both customer and future supplier. On its own fleet the company holds an installed base pull: GE fleet orders rose 7.3% to Rs 414 Cr, and the moat scan scores it Moderate on management claims. On other makers' fleets the scan scores a Moderate engine, also on claims, but found nothing a competitor must give up to copy it, and ICRA reports "intense competition". The FGD, new build and Durgapur lines have no moat. With the talent and cannibalization barrier tests both at zero, the emerging moat score of 11.6 reads NONE, 0.4 under the band edge.

## What is changing

Core services order intake rose from Rs 299 Cr in FY22 to Rs 734 Cr in FY26, about 25% a year. Orders from units built by other makers, which management calls the oOEM fleet, doubled from Rs 162 Cr to Rs 320 Cr in FY26. Total intake fell from Rs 2,183 Cr to Rs 877 Cr, because FY25 held Rs 775 Cr of FGD contracts and Rs 591 Cr of large upgrades that did not repeat. The order book fell 38.9% to Rs 1,628 Cr at March 2026. Most of that fall is the termination of two Jaypee FGD contracts worth Rs 775 Cr, and like for like the book fell 13.8%. The gas business went to a GE group company for Rs 43.86 Cr in 2024 and hydro went for one rupee in FY25. NCLT sanctioned the Durgapur demerger to JSW Energy on 1 Oct 2026, and it was not yet effective at 30 Sep 2026. Against the climb: the backlog stood at Rs 1,527 Cr at Q2 FY27, 16.3% lower than a year earlier. The Rs 550 Cr Shoaiba award in Saudi Arabia, notified on 12 Aug 2026, does not yet show in that backlog.

## What the numbers say

The Gate 0 backward screen scores 44 of 160, with a core score of 33 of 100 and the class AVOID. Verifier C recomputes the core at 31, and the class holds under every reading. The depressors are history: losses in FY22 to FY24, Rs 171 Cr in FY24 alone, and negative operating cash in FY19 to FY21. FY26 return on capital was 48.4%, against a ten year median of 11.5%. Revenue grew 21.2% to Rs 1,269 Cr in FY26 and 9.3% in H1 FY27. Material cost fell from 67.3% to 60.2% of revenue and employee cost from 18.5% to 14.5%, and those are real gains. The reported 27.0% FY26 margin is not run rate. It divides by total income and carries a Rs 102 Cr loss allowance reversal, write-backs, insurance and group income. Excluding other income and that reversal, the FY26 margin was 12.4%. The same measure was 14.5% in Q1 FY27 and 9.8% in Q2 FY27, 12.2% for the half. Other income made up 59.0% of Q2 FY27 profit before exceptional items and 51.9% for the half. Of the Rs 317 Cr FY26 profit step up, 55.2% came from the allowance swing and higher other income.

## Cash reality

FY26 operating cash flow was Rs 469 Cr against profit of Rs 253 Cr. It came from a Rs 514 Cr fall in receivables, including Rs 343 Cr of BHEL settlement cash, which cannot repeat at that size. Customer advances and billing in excess fell Rs 211 Cr in the same year, and inventory rose 69.9% on revenue up 21.2%. The company then placed Rs 450 Cr, 96% of the year's operating cash, as an unsecured demand deposit with LM Wind Power Blades (India), the GE Vernova cash pool header. Shareholders were asked to raise the ceiling to Rs 1,000 Cr. Net cash is Rs 847 Cr with that deposit and Rs 397 Cr without it. The balance sheet carries no term debt, and receivable days fell from 409 to 219. Bid capacity rests on one guarantor: Rs 798 Cr of GE Vernova guarantees is in use, Rs 1,600 Cr is needed, and the banks require GE Vernova to keep majority ownership. ICRA upgraded the rating to BBB+ in March 2026 and named a longer receivables period as a downgrade trigger. Whether cash conversion holds without the one time release is not yet known. The half year cash flow in the Q2 FY27 results was not legible in this run.

## What management says against what it does

Management kept seven of eight tested promises, with one partial and none missed, which earns a credibility grade of B. BHEL paid Rs 343 Cr as promised, the Jaypee dispute settled, and quarterly revenue of Rs 309 Cr and Rs 311 Cr sat inside the Rs 300 to 320 Cr band. The 11% margin in the Step-1 brief is the FY26 company level figure including the Durgapur loss, not an FY27 guide. Asked for a range, the CFO said "I can't give you a range", and the 85% to 90% execution figure refers to the whole order in hand, not to a named services backlog. That CFO left on 13 May 2026, and no earnings call explains Q1 or Q2 FY27. Four questions went unanswered or changed basis across calls: cash deployment, segment margin, the basis of the normalised margin, and the market size. Verifier B found two serious items the concall stage missed. The CFO said on one call that the BHEL reversal sat in cost and on the next that about Rs 41 Cr should come off Q4 revenue, which would make underlying Q4 FY26 revenue about Rs 275 Cr, not Rs 316 Cr. The BHEL settlement also involved the promoter's offshore Vernova entities, and the group side was not disclosed when an analyst asked.

## Promoter and governance

The promoter scan returns CAUTION, a structure concern rather than an integrity concern, and found no SEBI, criminal or auditor resignation record. The GE promoter entity holds 68.58%, unchanged for twelve quarters, and withdrew its plan to exit in July 2024. A new CFO and company secretary joined on 19 Jun 2026, and Ernst & Young took over internal audit. Against that, the Rs 450 Cr group deposit sat unsecured at March 2026, and the guarantee covering it was only proposed at the August 2026 meeting. Royalty to the group is Rs 43.6 Cr in the related party note but Rs 12.6 Cr in the profit and loss line, a Rs 31 Cr gap with no stated booking location. The CFO, company secretary and a senior manager all left in early 2026, and permanent staff turnover was 71.11% against 28.05% a year earlier. Contingent liabilities equal 84.2% of net worth, and the auditor's disputed dues list runs Rs 345 Cr above the note. The promoter scan is partial: AGM vote tallies, proxy adviser reports and registry searches were not reached.

## What the peers confirm and contradict

The peers verify none of the company's specific claims and contradict none. Four claims are partly verified and four cannot be tested, including the oOEM share, the market size and the Saudi order. No peer transcript names GE Power India. Power Mech and ISGEC confirm that FGD work is ending with no new award named. Power Mech earns 15% to 18% on operations and maintenance, above the company's 10% to 12%, but its figure includes other income and its work is manpower based. ISGEC margins rose in the same July to September 2026 quarter in which the company's margin fell 4.7 points, so sector input costs explain the direction of the fall but not its size. All BHEL evidence dates from 2021 to 2024.

## Where the market opportunity sits

Management defined a Rs 2,500 Cr annual market in February 2026, about Rs 500 Cr on its own fleet and the rest on similar fleets built by others. FY26 core intake of Rs 734 Cr is 29.4% of that. In May 2026 management restated the market at Rs 3,500 to 4,000 Cr with no definition or bridge. No external source sizes the India thermal aftermarket in rupees. The conservative total market is Rs 4,234 Cr, and the realistic figure was not found. The runway is MODERATE, with 3.4 times revenue headroom on the defined base. On the defined market, core intake reaches about Rs 883 Cr in three years, 6.4% a year, far below the 25% of the last four years. Faster growth needs the larger market to be real. The observation that separates the two readings is a fleet MW or unit list behind the denominator on the next call. The market sizing stage is partial: BHEL FY26 service bookings, the CEA renovation document and a competitor revenue sum were not reached.
