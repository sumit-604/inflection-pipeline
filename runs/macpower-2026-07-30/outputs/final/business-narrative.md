CNC maker premiumising into defence, cash unproven

Macpower CNC Machines builds computer controlled metal cutting machines, the VMC, turning, HMC, DCM and VTL families, from Metoda in Gujarat. The company was incorporated in 2003 and is run by the Mehta family, which holds about 73.2%. Product sales are 99% of revenue and services are half a percent, so every machine is a one time capital equipment sale, roughly Rs 18.8 lakh each in FY25 and rising toward Rs 20 lakh in FY26. Installed capacity is 2,500 machines a year, running near 80% utilisation.

The shift underway is from a base CNC supplier to a premium and defence supplier. The NEXA high end range now sits at about 40% of the order book at Rs 29 to 32 lakh a machine against Rs 15 to 16 lakh for the base range. Management has placed 200 plus machines with 35 defence factories and 100 plus with aviation and aerospace buyers, though defence is still single digit percent of revenue. The unexecuted order book reached Rs 406 cr at March 2026, up 23% on the year.

Revenue grew 27% in FY26 and Q1 FY27 revenue grew 56% year on year, the highest ever first quarter. EBITDA margin was 16.2% in FY26 against 15.9% in FY25, and FY26 EBITDA was Rs 53.9 cr. The backward looking Gate 0 score is 81 of 160, classification AVOID. That verdict is mechanical, not a demand call. The cash block scored 2 of 20 because cumulative operating cash flow to profit ran 0.45 over three years, below the 0.50 floor, and a three year record triggered a one tier downgrade. The demand, margin and leverage blocks all scored strong.

Cash conversion is the open question. Operating cash flow to profit fell from 0.70 in FY24 to 0.27 in FY25 and recovered only to about 0.41 in FY26. Free cash flow was negative in FY25 at about Rs 9 cr. Receivables rose 57.5% against revenue up 8.6%, raw material inventory rose 50.9%, and the company built about 80 finished machines worth roughly Rs 100 cr that it has not recognised as revenue because customer subsidy and loan paperwork is pending. On the Q4 FY26 call management said cash conversion will not improve rapidly.

Credibility grades B. Management delivered or beat every FY26 number it guided, 25 to 30% growth, a Rs 50 cr EBITDA target against Rs 53.9 cr actual, and an order book target of Rs 300 to 330 cr against Rs 406 cr actual. Against that, the 60 acre government land plan slipped three quarters running from December 2025 to undated, the market share figure moved from 4.5% to 1 or 2% under direct questioning with no reconciliation, and machine unit volumes were withheld on every call.

Accounting quality scores 6 of 10. The annual report states net margin improved to 12.00% while its own profit and loss statement and cover chart show margin fell to 9.72%, a 2.3 point gap left unreconciled. One note certifies nil loans to key management while another discloses a Rs 10 lakh loan to the CFO. A February 2025 fire destroyed Rs 4.4 cr of finished goods, and the Rs 3.6 cr insurance claim was still unsettled at year end.

Three peers read across the story. JYOTICNC and KLBRENG show capacity constrained demand, JYOTICNC above 20% CNC consumption growth and KLBRENG's inquiry pipeline doubled to Rs 4,000 cr, while ADOR grows 3 to 5%. Peers confirm the import component stocking, the government land approval delays, and the multi year path from high teens to 25% EBITDA margin. Peers contradict the defence claim: JYOTICNC executed over Rs 800 cr of aerospace and defence in FY26 against Macpower's single digit revenue share and 10 to 13% bid conversion.

The market runway is large. India CNC metal cutting TAM sits at Rs 20,000 to 24,000 cr, the serviceable market at Rs 14,900 cr, and Macpower holds about 2.2% of it, leaving roughly 45 times revenue headroom, though management's own TAM claim runs about twice the best independent estimate and this sizing is partial. The balance sheet is genuinely debt free at net cash with D/E 0.02, promoter pledge has been zero across twelve quarters, and the promoter read is trustworthy. The CNC controller, sourced 99% from FANUC, Siemens and Mitsubishi, still caps the backward integration margin story.
