### 2C. Growth embedded in capex — arithmetic shown

Inputs (consolidated, FY26, ₹Cr):
- Capital Work-in-Progress (balance, not yet productive): 5.08 (Statement of Assets & Liabilities, RESULTS-Q4FY26.txt p.9)
- Capital Advances for PPE (balance): 15.81 (AR Note, consolidated p.147 equivalent / RESULTS-Q4FY26 p.9)
- Capital Commitments (PPE, contracted but not yet advanced): 4.59 (AR Notes, consolidated p.152)
- **Capex under execution (sum of the above, not-yet-revenue-generating):** 5.08 + 15.81 + 4.59 = **₹25.48cr**
- Consolidated Revenue from Operations FY26: ₹226.24cr (AR MD&A p.19)
- Consolidated net fixed asset base FY26 (PPE ₹61.34cr + ROU assets ₹16.42cr + Intangibles ₹0.19cr, excluding CWIP): ₹77.95cr (AR/RESULTS-Q4FY26 Statement of Assets p.9)
- **Historical fixed asset turnover** = Revenue / Net fixed assets = 226.24 / 77.95 = **2.90x**

**Implied incremental revenue** = capex under execution × historical turnover = 25.48 × 2.90 = **₹73.9cr**, or **32.7% above FY26 consolidated revenue of ₹226.24cr**.

Caveats (stated, not smoothed over): (1) PML does not publish a formal capex programme table with itemised project costs, so this figure is built from balance-sheet residuals (CWIP + advances + commitments), not a disclosed total project budget — likely understates the true multi-year Quantum Magnetics FY31 target capex. (2) The 2.90x turnover ratio is a single-year snapshot on a small asset base and is sensitive to the large FY26 land/ROU additions; it should be treated as indicative, not precise. (3) It assumes committed capex converts to revenue at the company's historical (blended, mature-business) turnover rate — a generous assumption given the Relay and Quantum Magnetics execution record documented in Section 3, F2 below.

**A1 — Rare manufacturing capability.** PML describes itself as "often one of only two or three suppliers for specialized products and the exclusive supplier for several key customers," "preferred partner for ~50% of the world's tier-1 automotive manufacturers," and supplier to "the top 3 global electricity meter manufacturers" (AR MD&A p.31, PML Advantage p.14-15). 🎙️ management claim in the AR narrative, no independent verification, no global manufacturer count, no qualification-timeline data given. **Strength: Weak.**

**A2 — Patent and IP pipeline.** NO EVIDENCE FOUND. No patent filings, applications, or licensing revenue are mentioned anywhere in the AR (full-text search returned zero hits for "patent").

**A3 — Process innovation.** R&D expenditure disclosed: Capital ₹0.44cr + Recurring ₹1.02cr = Total ₹1.46cr, "0.65% of total turnover" (Annexure F, Technology Absorption, p.64). 📄 the amount is documented; the stated benefits ("enhanced ability to consistently deliver high-quality products," "achieved significant cost efficiencies") are generic qualitative claims with no yield or unit-cost data. **Strength: Weak.**

### FAMILY B — Supply Chain

**B1 — Backward integration and RM security.** Imported raw material share fell from 49.36% to 37.79% of total RM consumption FY25→FY26, with indigenous share rising from 50.64% to 62.21% (AR Note 14B, standalone, p.123) — 📄, real one-year data point (not a multi-year trend, only two years shown). Separately, Quantum Magnetics' phased roadmap explicitly targets "progressive backward integration into alloy, metal, and oxide integration" for NdFeB, backed by a committed ₹47.81cr ECB (AR Note 16, p.132; Strategic Priorities p.17). **Strength: Moderate.**

**B2 — Qualification lock-in.** IATF 16949:2016, AS9100D:2016 (won this year for Alloys, "unlocking new opportunities in the aerospace sector"), ISO 14001:2015, ISO 9001:2015, OHSMS 45001:2018 are all held certifications (AR p.15, p.10) — 📄 documented. No explicit disclosure of design-in language, customer-funded tooling, or multi-year post-qualification agreements, so the "lock-in" mechanism itself (vs. the certification) is inferred. **Strength: Moderate.**

## SECTION 4: REGULATORY & POLICY TAILWINDS (R1)

### 4A. Regulatory approvals in pipeline

NOT FOUND as a company-specific regulatory application (e.g., no BIS/ECMS-type approval process for PML itself is named anywhere in the AR). AS9100D:2016 (aerospace quality management certification, already achieved, not "pending") is the closest analogue and is scored under B2 above, not here.

### 4B. Government policy tailwinds

**Revamped Distribution Sector Scheme (RDSS)** — the dominant, well-documented tailwind:
- Total outlay ₹3,03,758cr, including ₹97,631cr central budgetary support (AR MD&A p.28).
- Target: 250 million smart meters nationally; as of 30 April 2026, 224 million sanctioned, 151 million awarded, 65 million physically installed (up from ~28 million as of May 2025) (AR MD&A p.28-29).
- AT&C losses down from 21.91% (FY21) to 15.04% (FY25) (AR MD&A p.28).
- Scheme extended to FY2027-28; estimated cumulative investment to complete the programme ~USD 30 billion (AR MD&A p.28-29).
- Funding is performance-linked at the DISCOM level (tariff-petition filing, audited financials, subsidy clearance) — a structural, not merely announced, funding mechanism (AR MD&A p.28).
- Electricity meters were 40% of PML's standalone FY26 application mix (down slightly from 43% FY25, AR p.13) — PML is a direct beneficiary via shunts, CTs, flux concentrators and (prospectively) relays sold into this value chain, though PML itself has no exclusive or company-specific claim on the scheme (all qualified suppliers to AMISPs/meter OEMs benefit equally).
- Generic reference to "PLI schemes" as a growth support is made once, without naming a specific PLI scheme PML is enrolled in, amounts received, or duration (AR MD&A Risk section, p.34) — 🎙️, NOT a company-specific, quantified benefit. Distinguish from RDSS, which is well quantified.

### 4C. Regulatory moat assessment

RDSS is an **active, not merely emerging**, tailwind — the scheme has been running since 2021 and is in an accelerating installation phase (65M installed vs 28M a year earlier). Time to further kick in: ongoing through FY28 per the scheme's extended timeline. Sustainability: structurally supported by DISCOM-level performance conditionality, but reliant on continued state-level execution and central disbursal discipline — a macro/policy risk outside PML's control. It is not a moat unique to PML; it is a sector tailwind PML is positioned to capture alongside all qualified competitors.
