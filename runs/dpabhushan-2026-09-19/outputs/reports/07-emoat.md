### 2C Growth embedded in capex — arithmetic shown

Net fixed assets (PP&E) at 31-Mar-2026: Rs 60.14 Cr (Note 3A, AR p.90:
Total cost Rs 8,424.91 lakh less accumulated depreciation Rs 2,410.81
lakh = Rs 6,014.10 lakh). FY26 revenue: Rs 4,070.33 Cr (AR KPI page 11).

Historical fixed asset turnover = 4,070.33 / 60.14 ≈ **67.7x**.

Capex under execution (documented + guided, FY27):
- Documented (2 COCO stores, Dahod + Jodhpur, at the disclosed
  Rs 2.5-3 Cr small-format bracket): ~Rs 5.5 Cr (Jabalpur excluded —
  FOCO, franchisee-funded, no disclosed company capex).
- Guided balance (2-3 further FY27 stores at the same bracket): ~Rs 8.25 Cr.
- **Total capex under execution ≈ Rs 13.75 Cr (documented + guided).**

Implied incremental revenue = Rs 13.75 Cr × 67.7x ≈ **Rs 931 Cr**, or
**~23% of FY26 revenue** (Rs 4,070.33 Cr).

**Caveat, stated explicitly because the arithmetic is misleading here**:
this business is asset-light by design — PP&E is leasehold
improvements and fixtures, ~1.5% of FY26 revenue — so the fixed-asset-
turnover multiplier is a statistical artefact, not a real capital-
efficiency signal. Revenue growth in this model is funded by inventory
(gold stock), not by fixed-asset capex, and inventory growth has been
funded entirely by fresh bank debt with CFO negative three years running
(B01/B02: FY26 CFO -Rs 97.31 Cr). A store-count sanity check is more
honest: 2 documented COCO stores under execution / 12-store base = +17%;
5-6 total FY27 guided additions / 12-store base = +42-50%. New stores
under-index on revenue in their ramp-up year, so even the store-count
read overstates near-term revenue impact. capex_embedded_growth_pct in
the YAML block carries the Section 2C figure (~23%) with this caveat
attached; it should not be read as a clean capital-efficiency signal.

Active, not emerging: the hallmarking-driven formalisation shift has
been running for several years already (phased district expansion since
before FY24) and is explicitly framed as industry-wide in the AR's own
MD&A ("favouring organised retailers over informal operators," not
DPABHUSHAN specifically). Sustainability is high (a structural, likely
irreversible formalisation trend) but exclusivity is low — it does not
differentiate DPABHUSHAN from SENCO, PNGJL, MOTISONS, or larger national
organised players, several of which are better capitalised to capture
the same shift faster. Scored Moderate on the raw likelihood x impact
matrix (see Section 5) precisely because likelihood is high but impact
is diluted by the shared-benefit caveat.
