## SECTION 1: MARKET DEFINITION

### 1A — Precise boundaries

RAMRAT's revenue splits 76% enamelled winding wires & strips, 22% copper
tubes & pipes, ~2% other (Tefabo fabrication contribution; Epavo is a
50:50 JV, equity-accounted, not consolidated at the revenue line) —
(Investor Presentation Q4 FY26, p.7; B04-bizmodel.yaml). This stage's
core TAM covers the 98% (winding wire + copper tube). Tefabo/Epavo are
treated as **SAM-expansion levers** (Section 5D), not part of the core
TAM — consistent with the instruction not to conflate adjacent
JV/subsidiary opportunities with the company's primary addressable
market.

- **Product scope — included:** enamelled copper & aluminium winding
  wire and strips (0.016mm–5.000mm), submersible winding wire,
  paper-covered strips, fine/ultra-fine/Litz wire, EV-grade
  conductors/strips (Investor Presentation p.13); copper tubes & pipes —
  straight pipe, Level Wound Coil (LWC), capillary tubes, Pancake Coils
  (PCC), PVC-coated tubes, Inner Grooved Tubes (IGT, in trials)
  (Investor Presentation p.14).
- **Product scope — excluded:** raw copper cathode/rod trading; insulated
  power cables and conductors (the Polycab/KEI/Havells/RR Kabel
  business, a structurally distinct, larger-scale market); large-format
  CTC/HVDC conductors (RAMRAT is at "entry stage" here, per injected
  product list, not yet a meaningful revenue line); BLDC motors and wind
  -tower fabrication (Epavo/Tefabo — separate JV/subsidiary TAMs, kept
  out of the core to avoid double-counting against Stage 7's Emerging
  Moat scan).
- **Geographic scope:** India-domestic, since 93% of FY26 revenue is
  domestic (Investor Presentation p.7). Export TAM is treated as an
  expansion lever (Section 5D), not core SAM, given exports are a stated
  Weakness (B04; AR p.56 SWOT).
- **Customer scope:** OEMs (electrical equipment, automotive, HVAC/
  appliance, motor/transformer manufacturers) — RAMRAT supplies 70–75%
  of output to large OEMs (Investor Presentation p.11; AR p.36 "Embedded
  in OEM Ecosystems"). Retail/unbranded rewinding-shop demand is present
  in the broader market but weakly addressable by an OEM-qualified
  player — handled as a channel/customer filter in Section 3A, not
  excluded from TAM outright.
- **Channel scope:** direct-to-OEM plus a PAN-India distribution network
  across 200+ cities (Investor Presentation p.15).
  **Price segment:** spans commodity-grade bulk wire/tube through
  higher-value technical grades (fine/ultra-fine, Litz, EV-strip,
  Kapton-covered) — AR p.55 notes ongoing mix-shift toward the latter.

## SECTION 2: TAM ESTIMATION, MULTIPLE METHODS

### Method 1 — Top-down (industry reports, India-domestic)

**Winding wire & strips.** IndexBox's *India Winding Wire Market Report
2026* values India's winding wire market at **USD 2.7bn in 2024**
(India ranks 3rd globally behind China $4.3bn and the US $3.9bn) = **Rs
22,410 Cr** at ₹83/USD. No subtraction needed for product/customer/
channel scope — RAMRAT's product range (copper + aluminium + submersible
+ strip) matches the report's apparent scope closely; this is already an
India-domestic figure, so no geography subtraction. 6Wresearch (cited
by RAMRAT itself, IP p.26) projects ~9% CAGR 2026–2032 for this segment;
PhillipCapital (via Tribune, undated 2026 coverage) affirms a
"structural growth phase" qualitatively without a numeric CAGR, citing
India's plan to nearly double installed power-generation capacity from
~442GW (FY24) to ~900GW (FY32) backed by >Rs9 trillion of transmission
investment. **Conservative CAGR assumption: 8%.** Projected to the
run-date year (2026, 2 years out): Rs22,410cr × 1.08² = **Rs 26,140 Cr**.

**Copper tubes & pipes.** Grand View Research (via search snippet)
values India's copper pipes & tubes market at **USD 1,217.8mn in 2024**
= **Rs 10,108 Cr**. IMARC (volume terms) shows India copper pipes &
tubes at 0.50 million tonnes (2024) → 0.60mn tonnes (2033), a **2.20%
volume CAGR** — the most conservative growth read available. Mordor
Intelligence/BlueWeave show value CAGR nearer **5.1–5.4%** to 2030
(BlueWeave: India market to surpass USD2.4bn by 2030). Per conservative
bias, the low end (2.2%) anchors the conservative case; 5.4% anchors the
realistic case. Projected to 2026: conservative Rs10,108cr × 1.022² =
**Rs 10,558 Cr**; realistic Rs10,108cr × 1.054² = **Rs 11,231 Cr**.

**Combined top-down TAM (2026):** conservative Rs26,140 + Rs10,558 =
**Rs 36,698 Cr**; realistic Rs26,631 (wire at 9% CAGR) + Rs11,231 = **Rs
37,862 Cr**.

*Note on source divergence:* BlueWeave's USD1.73bn figure for **2023**
is inconsistent with GVR's lower USD1.22bn for the later year **2024**
— different methodologies/scopes, not a real market contraction. GVR
(lower) is used per the conservative-bias rule.

### Method 2 — Bottom-up (unit × price)

**Winding wire.** Unit = 1 tonne of finished wire/strip. IndexBox's same
report states India **production of 259,000 tonnes** (2024, same
vintage as the $2.7bn figure). Revenue-per-tonne is not disclosed by
RAMRAT (B04: "NOT FOUND"), so it is triangulated from a pure-play peer:
Precision Wires India — FY25 revenue Rs4,040cr on ~55,000 MTPA capacity
(as of Jun-2025); assuming ~50,000 MTPA average FY25 capacity at ~88%
utilisation ≈ 44,000 tonnes produced ⇒ **≈ Rs9.2 lakh/tonne (₹92/kg)**.
Using a conservative **Rs8.5 lakh/tonne (₹85/kg)**: 259,000t × Rs8.5
lakh/t = **Rs 22,015 Cr** — within 2% of the Method 1 top-down figure
(Rs22,410cr, 2024 basis). **Two independent methods converge on ~Rs
22,000–22,400 Cr for the 2024 winding-wire market** — a strong
triangulation.

**Copper tubes.** A parallel bottom-up on the 500,000-tonne IMARC volume
figure, using a copper-cost-plus-conversion-premium price (~₹8.8
lakh/tonne at then-prevailing copper prices), would imply ~Rs44,000cr —
roughly 4x the GVR value estimate. This gap indicates the IMARC volume
figure is scoped more broadly than GVR's value figure (likely including
non-copper or larger-diameter industrial piping not in RAMRAT's product
range). **This inconsistency is flagged rather than resolved**; the
value-based GVR figure (Rs10,108cr, 2024) is retained as the more
scope-consistent, and lower, of the two — again per conservative bias.

### Method 3 — Peer revenue aggregation

Known organised-sector winding-wire revenue (most recent FY disclosed):
- RAMRAT (winding wire only, 76% of consol FY26 Rs5,176.6cr): **Rs3,934cr**
- Precision Wires India (FY25, pure-play): **Rs4,040cr**
- Vidya Wires (FY25): **Rs1,486cr**
- Bhagyanagar India (FY26, broader copper-products mix incl. flats/bus
  bars/rods/foils, not pure winding wire — partial overlap only):
  **Rs2,378cr** (up from Rs1,625cr FY24)

Sum of the three cleanly comparable pure/near-pure winding-wire peers
(RAMRAT + Precision Wires + Vidya Wires) = **Rs9,460 Cr**, or ~42% of
the Method-1/2 triangulated Rs22,000–22,400cr India market. The
residual ~58% is consistent with DCMSME's characterisation of the
industry as "500 units, majority small" with cumulative installed
capacity of 350–400 KMT/annum at only ~60% utilisation (see staleness
flag below) — i.e., a long tail of smaller organised and unorganised
capacity. An explicit unorganised-sector estimate of **35–45% of the
market** is used, consistent with the adjacent wires-&-cables market's
documented 55–60% unorganised share (Section 4C), moderated down given
winding wire skews more OEM-qualified/organised than commodity house
wiring cable. This peer-aggregation method is **directionally
consistent with, not contradictory to,** Methods 1–2, reinforcing the
~Rs22,000–22,500cr 2024 figure for winding wire specifically.

### Method 4 — Import substitution (copper tubes)

RAMRAT's own claim: "~70% of India's copper-tube demand is historically
import-driven" (Investor Presentation p.15; AR SWOT p.56/57 "Continued
dependence on imported copper tubes presents a strong import
substitution opportunity"). **This specific percentage could not be
independently corroborated** with a single consolidated customs
(DGCIS/HS-code) aggregate within the searches performed. What was
independently confirmed:
- India has an **anti-dumping duty on Chinese copper tubes/pipes,
  extended to January 2027** (Business Standard, duty range
  USD961.33–1,610.67/tonne) — confirms import pressure was/is real and
  material enough to warrant trade-remedy action.
- India imported **USD127.84mn of copper tubes/pipes from Malaysia
  alone in 2024** (Trading Economics) — on a ~USD1.2bn India market
  (GVR), that single non-China origin alone represents ~10% of the
  market; multiple other origins (Vietnam, Thailand, Indonesia — all
  separately subject to CVD proceedings on copper wire rod, a related
  input) plausibly push aggregate import dependence materially higher.
  **Directionally supportive of a high import share; the specific "70%"
  figure remains a company claim, not independently verified.**

### Method 5 — Global benchmark comparison (copper tubes)

Mordor Intelligence: **global** copper pipes & tubes market USD36.44bn
(2025) → USD48.28bn (2032), 4.1% CAGR (via OpenPR syndication of the
Mordor report). Management's claim of a global market "exceeding USD70bn
by 2032" is **1.45x** Mordor's USD48.28bn 2032 figure — within the
"reasonable" band (<1.5x) per the standard read. **This is the
important nuance:** the *global* headline figure is roughly defensible
against at least one credible aggregator, but the *India-specific*
16–17% CAGR companion claim is not — see the triangulation table below.
India's own installed wind-tower and RAC/BLDC benchmarks (AR p.53–54)
were independently cross-checked and held up well: India's electric
motor market size (INR13,131cr in 2025, ~11% CAGR to 2035, per
ExpertMarketResearch) closely matches AR's own citation of an ~11% CAGR
2025–2034 rotating-machines claim — **this specific AR claim is
independently corroborated**, unlike the copper-tube CAGR claim.

## SECTION 3: SAM & SOM

### 3A — SAM

Applying five filters to the combined conservative TAM (Rs36,698cr,
2026):

| Filter | Retention | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Product fit | 100% | RAMRAT's wire (0.016–5.000mm) and tube (LWC/IGT/capillary/PCC) ranges span nearly the full breadth of both TAM segments |
| Geography | 100% | TAM already India-domestic scoped, matching RAMRAT's 93% domestic mix; export TAM handled separately (5D) |
| Channel | 90% | Informal/loose-wire retail and unbranded local-fabricator channel is part of the TAM but weakly reachable for an OEM-qualified, distributor-network player |
| Customer | 95% | Very small/tiny unorganised rewinders and fabricators below OEM/qualified-distributor scale are only marginally addressable |
| Capability | 90% | CTC/HVDC "entry stage" and IGT "in progress/evaluation" (Investor Presentation p.14–15) — some premium/large-format niches not yet commercialised |

Combined retention = 1.00 × 1.00 × 0.90 × 0.95 × 0.90 = **77.0%**

**SAM = Rs36,698cr × 77% = Rs 28,260 Cr. SAM as % of TAM (conservative)
= 77.0%.**

### 3B — SOM at 3 and 5 years

Current combined core revenue (winding wire + copper tube, FY26
consolidated) = 76% + 22% = 98% × Rs5,176.6cr = **Rs 5,073 Cr**.

**Current share of SAM = 5,073 / 28,260 = 17.95% ≈ 18%.**

Share-gain trajectory: RAMRAT is mid-capex-build (Bhiwadi copper-tube
ramp, B07 capex-embedded growth of 14%), operating in a segment where an
industry-wide capacity race is underway (peer B06: Vidya Wires
19,680→37,680 MTPA, Bhagyanagar 30,000→35,000 MTPA, plus new entrants
Adani and Hindalco flagged as an overcapacity risk) and where the
copper-tube segment specifically has a formalising, import-substituting
dynamic (Section 2, Method 4). This supports a share gain toward the
upper end of "normal" at 3 years, tempered by the overcapacity risk, and
"aggressive" by 5 years as Bhiwadi's ramp completes:
- **3-year: +2pp (18% → 20% of SAM)** — normal-band, capacity- and
  execution-supported but capped given the peer capacity race.
- **5-year: +4pp (18% → 22% of SAM)** — aggressive-band, justified by
  full Bhiwadi utilisation plus copper-tube formalisation, but this is
  the more optimistic of the two horizons and is flagged as such.

SAM itself grows with the blended TAM CAGR (winding wire ~71% of
combined TAM weight × 8% + copper tube ~29% weight × 3.8% midpoint ≈
**7.1%, rounded to 7%**):
- SAM (2029, 3yr) = Rs28,260cr × 1.07³ = **Rs 34,620 Cr**
- SAM (2031, 5yr) = Rs28,260cr × 1.07⁵ = **Rs 39,632 Cr**

**SOM (3yr) = Rs34,620cr × 20% = Rs 6,924 Cr**
**SOM (5yr) = Rs39,632cr × 22% = Rs 8,719 Cr**

**Implied revenue CAGR (arithmetic shown):**
- 3yr: (6,924 / 5,073)^(1/3) − 1 = (1.3648)^(0.333) − 1 = **10.9%**
- 5yr: (8,719 / 5,073)^(1/5) − 1 = (1.7186)^(0.20) − 1 = **11.4%**

**These two figures (10.9% / 11.4%) are the formal handoff to Stage 11
as the SOM-implied revenue CAGR cross-check.**

### 3C — Capacity cross-check

B07's capex-embedded growth figure = **14%** annual revenue CAGR implied
by committed capex (Bhiwadi copper tube 24,000 MTPA commissioned
Jun-2025, expanding toward 36,000 MTPA with GCPL scaling 7,200→12,000
MTPA; winding wire at 41,400 MTPA across the three DNH facilities —
CARE Ratings press release, Sep-2025; Investor Presentation p.17).

- At 14% CAGR, 3yr revenue = Rs5,073cr × 1.14³ = **Rs 7,516 Cr** — a gap
  of **Rs 592 Cr** above the SOM_3yr figure (Rs6,924cr).
- At 14% CAGR, 5yr revenue = Rs5,073cr × 1.14⁵ = **Rs 9,769 Cr** — a gap
  of **Rs 1,050 Cr** above the SOM_5yr figure (Rs8,719cr).

**Physical capacity is not the constraint.** The installed and committed
MTPA (36,000t copper tube, 41,400t winding wire) is large enough, on the
bottom-up Rs/tonne math in Section 2, to physically produce even the
higher, capex-embedded revenue trajectory. **The gap is on the
demand/share-capture side: the capex plan (14% CAGR) is the more
optimistic assumption of the two, running ~3pp and Rs590–1,050cr ahead
of the SOM's conservative-to-moderate 10.9–11.4% CAGR** at the 3- and
5-year horizons respectively. This should be read alongside the peer
overcapacity risk (Vidya, Bhagyanagar, Adani, Hindalco) — if that
capacity race compresses realisable pricing/share, the capex plan's
implicit growth assumption is the side more likely to disappoint.

### 4B — TAM risks

| Risk | Monitoring signal |
|---|---|
| Import competition (non-China origins) | Malaysia alone ~USD128mn imports (2024); Vietnam/Thailand/Indonesia under separate CVD proceedings on copper wire rod — watch DGTR case list and customs HS-code import trend |
| Overcapacity from industry-wide capex race | Vidya Wires (19,680→37,680 MTPA), Bhagyanagar (30,000→35,000 MTPA), and new entrants Adani/Hindalco (per B06) — watch realised utilisation and blended conversion-premium trends across listed peers |
| Cyclical downturn | Business tied to automotive, industrial capex, construction cycles (AR SWOT, Weaknesses: "business performance is partially influenced by broader industrial and infrastructure cycles") |
| Copper-price volatility | LME copper hit an all-time high of ~USD14,527.50/t on 29-Jan-2026 (Benchmark Minerals); margin/WC risk already flagged in B04 (FLAG-CASH) |
| Substitution — aluminium micro-channel coils | Real technology globally, but **historically de-risked in India specifically**: microchannel aluminium heat exchangers proved incompatible with India's hot, humid climate (corrosion/pitting), and OEMs reverted to copper after early adoption attempts (copper.org industry sources) — a risk that is monitored, not currently active, in the Indian market |
| Substitution — aluminium winding wire | Present at the margin in cost-sensitive applications; RAMRAT itself makes both copper and aluminium winding wire, partially hedging this risk internally |
| Regulatory — BEE efficiency standards | Net tailwind, not risk, for RAMRAT: BEE's ceiling-fan star-rating downgrade increases demand for BLDC (copper-intensive) motors (Investor Presentation p.22) |

### 4C — Market structure

- **Winding wire:** ~500 units in India, majority small (DCMSME — dated,
  see staleness flag); cumulative installed capacity 350–400 KMT at
  ~60% utilisation. Estimated top organised players by revenue:
  Precision Wires (~18% of the ~Rs22,400cr market), RAMRAT (~15–18% by
  revenue-share calculation in this report — a third-party estimate of
  3.37% could not be verified and appears to use a different, larger
  denominator; flagged, not adopted), Vidya Wires (~6.6%). Top-3
  concentration on this basis ≈ 40%, broadly consistent with the
  adjacent wires-and-cables market's documented ~40–45% organised /
  55–60% unorganised split (Polycab ~27% of that separate market, KEI
  ~9%, Havells ~7.9%, RR Kabel ~5.2% — a different, larger market,
  cited only for directional context).
- **Copper tubes:** RAMRAT positions itself as operating "one of the
  largest" copper-tube facilities in India (Bhiwadi) with "advanced Cast
  & Roll technology" (AR p.34) — a newer, more concentrated, import-
  substitution-driven segment; precise organised/unorganised split not
  independently found.
- **Consolidating vs fragmenting:** consolidating at the top (capacity
  race among listed players) while a long unorganised tail persists —
  both dynamics active simultaneously.
- **Competition basis:** mixed — commodity pass-through pricing at the
  base (weak pricing power, per B04) with differentiation emerging at
  the technical/EV-grade/IGT end.
- **Import share trend:** anti-dumping protection in place for Chinese
  tubes/pipes to Jan-2027; non-China import share (Malaysia, Vietnam,
  Thailand, Indonesia) appears to be a live, monitored risk rather than
  a closed issue.

### 5B — Runway assessment

- **Revenue headroom = SAM ÷ current consolidated revenue** = Rs28,260cr
  / Rs5,176.6cr = **5.46x**.
- **TAM growth rate (blended)** ≈ **7%** p.a.
- **Company CAGR vs TAM:** RAMRAT's own 5-year revenue CAGR is 28%
  (Investor Presentation p.11 "At a Glance" — inflated by copper-price
  pass-through and the GCPL merger, not a clean like-for-like organic
  figure); even the more conservative SOM-implied forward CAGR of
  10.9–11.4% still runs ahead of the 7% TAM growth rate — RAMRAT is
  **gaining share**, not merely riding the market, under this stage's
  assumptions.
- **Years to saturate SAM:** at the SOM-implied ~11% revenue CAGR
  against a ~7% SAM growth rate, the share-gain differential is ~3.7pp
  p.a.; extending that from the current 18% share to full SAM capture
  (100%) is a multi-decade exercise (order of ~45+ years) — i.e., SAM
  saturation is not a near-term constraint under any realistic
  execution scenario.

### 5C — Runway classification

**STRONG** (5–10x headroom band). Revenue headroom of 5.46x sits
comfortably above GOOD (3–5x) but short of MASSIVE (>10x); combined with
a TAM still growing at ~7% (itself a moving target extending effective
runway further) and RAMRAT still gaining share, this reads as a strong,
not yet abundant, runway — meaningfully constrained relative to the 25%
CAGR strategy target, since even the SOM's aggressive 5-year case
implies only ~11% revenue CAGR.

### 5D — SAM expansion levers actually being pursued

- **Copper tube product-range completion (IGT, in progress):** would
  widen product-fit retention from 90% toward 95–100% on the capability
  filter; modest addition, single-digit Rs hundreds of crore over 3-5
  years once commercialised.
- **Export growth (stated Strategic Vision, IP p.25, "preferred
  domestic and export supplier"):** currently only 7% of revenue and a
  stated Weakness (AR p.56); if exports were to reach even 15% of
  revenue on the current base, that is a levered addition outside the
  current India-only SAM — but this remains aspirational, not yet
  executed at scale.
- **Tefabo (wind-tower fabrication, 64%-owned subsidiary):** India
  wind-tower market ~USD1.5–1.6bn (2025) → USD2.4–2.5bn (2030); Tefabo
  claims 30–40% share "in key product segments" (unverified, management
  claim) and is adding Vadodara capacity (two phases, 20 towers/month
  each, doubling Bangalore's ~40 towers/month) — a real but currently
  small (~part of the 2% "Others" line) lever.
- **Epavo (BLDC motors, 50:50 JV, equity-accounted):** India BLDC-
  relevant RAC demand ~22–23mn units/yr rising to ~32–36mn by FY2030
  (AR p.54) — currently a drag (share of JV loss, Rs3.9cr FY26, per AR
  P&L), so this lever is a call option on a turnaround, not a
  near-term revenue addition.
- Revised headroom if exports reached 15% of revenue and Tefabo/Epavo
  scaled to a combined ~5% of consolidated revenue: incremental SAM-
  adjacent opportunity plausibly adds another 1.5–2x to the current
  5.46x headroom over a 5-8 year view — **directional, not anchored**,
  since none of these levers has disclosed unit economics sufficient
  for a hard number.

### 5E — Final output card

- TAM (conservative / realistic, 2026): **Rs 36,698 Cr / Rs 37,862 Cr**
- SAM: **Rs 28,260 Cr (77.0% of TAM)**
- SOM 3yr / 5yr: **Rs 6,924 Cr / Rs 8,719 Cr**
- SOM-implied revenue CAGR: **10.9% (3yr) / 11.4% (5yr)**
- Current SAM share: **~18%**
- Revenue headroom: **5.46x**
- Runway class: **STRONG**
- Management claim read: **inflated** (India copper-tube CAGR claim
  ~2.9x the independently-triangulated conservative projection to the
  same 2032 endpoint; the separately-cited *global* copper-tube 2032
  figure is more defensible, at ~1.45x an independent aggregator's
  figure — reasonable band)

**Valuation implication line:** At **~11%** revenue CAGR implied by SOM
(midpoint of the 10.9–11.4% range), with an EBITDA margin trajectory
assumed to widen from 5.1% (FY26, consolidated) toward roughly 6.5% over
5 years as the copper-tube mix keeps rising (per B04's "healthy" ROCE/
mix-shift narrative — not a hard company target), the embedded EBITDA
growth is approximately **14% CAGR**, which **does not fully support**
the current standalone/consolidated trailing P/E of **~24.8x**
(market cap Rs2,697cr per AR "Performance at a Glance," p.38, ÷
consolidated FY26 PAT Rs108.6cr) — a PEG-like ratio near 1.8x is
stretched for this growth rate. Note, per B04's own valuation-method
ruling, **P/E is only the secondary method here; EV/EBITDA is primary**
for this capital-intensive conversion manufacturer. On that basis, EV
(market cap Rs2,697cr + net debt ~Rs640cr ≈ Rs3,337cr) ÷ FY26 EBITDA
Rs263.6cr ≈ **12.7x EV/EBITDA**, which reads as more supportive of the
~14% EBITDA CAGR (sub-1x growth-adjusted multiple) than the P/E lens
does — but both readings fall well short of the 25% CAGR strategy
target, reinforcing the STRONG-not-MASSIVE runway call above.
