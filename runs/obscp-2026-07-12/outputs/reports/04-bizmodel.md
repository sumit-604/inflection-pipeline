## SECTION 1: THE BUSINESS MODEL IN PLAIN ENGLISH

### 1A. One-line description
OBSC Perfection is a contract manufacturer of ultra-precise metal parts (down
to 4-micron tolerance) — it buys steel and aluminium stock, machines/forges/
casts it into components that automotive, defence, marine and (emerging)
aerospace/medical/humanoid-robotics customers have designed, and gets paid
per part under multi-year "nomination" supply agreements with global OEMs
and Tier-1s (Inv. Pres. slides 12, 15, 21).

### 1E. The chai-stall-uncle version
Imagine a workshop that doesn't make its own product — instead, big companies
like ZF, Tenneco and Bharat Electronics hand it a technical drawing and say
"make me a million of these, exactly this precise, for the next five years."
OBSC Perfection buys steel bars, machines/forges/casts them into that exact
shape, and gets paid per piece. It started doing this only for car and truck
parts (shock absorbers), but has spent the last two years adding new
"kitchens" — casting, forging, stamping — so it can cook more kinds of metal
dishes, and it has started serving new customers too: the army (bullet
casings, fuze parts), ships, and even the makers of humanoid robots. The
catch is getting a new customer to trust you takes almost a year of audits
and testing, so once you're in, you tend to stay in — but you also don't set
your own price; the big customer still holds most of the cards.

### 2D. Industry lifecycle stage
The broader Indian auto-ancillary/precision-engineering supply industry is
mature and cyclical, tracking underlying vehicle production. OBSCP itself,
however, sits in an early/scale-up phase within that mature industry —
revenue grew from ₹774 lakh (FY20) to ₹22,351.8 lakh (FY26), a 75% CAGR
(Inv. Pres. slide 13), driven by wallet-share gains with existing customers,
new-process additions (casting 2022, cold/hot forging 2025, stamping 2026),
and diversification into non-automotive end-markets. Management explicitly
frames this as still early: "we've barely touched the tipping point now"
(Concall May-26 p.6).

### 3D. Unit economics — the physics of the business

- **Unit**: one precision-engineered metal component (machined, forged, cast
  or stamped to customer print), ranging from simple fasteners to complex
  aluminium-milled cold-plate parts. The company itself avoids per-unit
  averaging language, describing itself as covering "26 broad categories"
  across "500+ different part numbers" (Concall May-25 p.7) — the SKU base is
  too heterogeneous for a single meaningful average selling price.
- **Revenue per unit**: NOT FOUND — no average selling price disclosed;
  capacity is instead reported in lakh pieces per annum for machining
  (installed 451.5 lakh, produced 383.6 lakh in FY26, 85.0% utilisation) and
  tons per annum for investment casting/forging (Inv. Pres. slide 17).
- **Margin per unit**: NOT FOUND — no per-part cost/margin breakdown
  disclosed.
- **Volume drivers**: number of qualified/nominated part numbers (500+),
  underlying OEM production volumes (auto/CV cycle), capacity utilisation
  headroom (machining already at 85%, limited near-term headroom without
  further capex; forging at only ~30% utilisation with substantial room to
  grow, Inv. Pres. slide 17).
- **Price drivers**: part complexity/precision tolerance (down to 4 microns,
  Inv. Pres. slide 12), process value-add (assembly/sub-assembly > forging >
  casting > machining in margin terms per CFO commentary, Concall May-25
  p.17), export premium of 10%+ over domestic pricing (Concall May-26 p.12),
  sector mix shift toward higher-margin defence/marine/aerospace work
  (Concall May-25 p.14, May-26 p.4).
- **Cost drivers**: raw material (steel/aluminium bright bar, partly sourced
  from related-party Omega Bright Steel), power/utilities, contract labour
  (~700 blue-collar workers), degree of machine automation (50 of ~200
  machines are fully automated as of May-26, Concall May-26 p.14),
  freight/logistics on export shipments.
- **Incremental margin / operating leverage**: management guides EBITDA
  margin expansion of roughly 1-2 percentage points per year near-term,
  rising to a cumulative 4-5 points over three to four years, driven by
  in-housing previously outsourced processes (e.g., cold/hot forging that
  was formerly bought in) and mix shift toward defence/aerospace/marine
  (Concall May-25 p.13-14). However, near-term operating leverage on the
  *existing* machining base is capped because that base is already running
  at 85% utilisation — further growth requires new capex/new plants (Supa,
  Sanand) to ramp before their own operating leverage kicks in (Inv. Pres.
  slide 17; Concall May-26 p.5-6, 11).
