## Business Understanding Narrative

KRM Ayurveda treats sick people with Ayurvedic medicine, mostly kidney patients, and sells
Ayurvedic products to the same audience. It runs 7 hospitals and 8 clinics across 7 states,
246 beds, 69% occupancy, average revenue per occupied bed about Rs 8,400 (IP p.5, p.13). It
manufactures its own formulations at a GMP certified unit at Kundli in Haryana (IP p.10).

Patients arrive through YouTube. Three channels carry over 8 lakh subscribers, the largest
being "Kidney Treatment by Karma Ayurveda" at 352,000 subscribers and 2,600 videos, whose
stated theme is "Kidney Treatment Without Dialysis" (IP p.18). That funnel is the real
asset. It is cheap patient acquisition into a high margin service.

What is changing, and this part is genuine: the business is turning from a product seller
into a hospital operator. Product revenue fell from Rs 60.6 crore in FY24 to Rs 49.2 crore
in FY25 to Rs 47.0 crore in FY26, while service revenue rose from Rs 6.5 crore to Rs 27.3
crore to Rs 54.7 crore (IP p.17). Services passed products in FY26. All the growth, and more
than all of it, is hospitals.

Why now: the company listed in January 2026, raised Rs 77.49 crore, cleared its debt, and
reported FY26 revenue of Rs 101.7 crore with a 19.8% net margin (IP p.28, p.32).

What must be true: the reported profit must turn into cash, and the funnel must stay legal
and credible.

What breaks it: it has not turned into cash. FY26 profit after tax was Rs 20.12 crore and
cash from operations was Rs 1.12 crore (IP p.31, p.33).

## 2. Business model and archetype
Three arms, all described by the company as asset light (IP p.10). Hospitals and clinics
with in-patient, out-patient, Panchakarma and teleconsultation. Products manufacturing at
Kundli. D2C digital distribution run through YouTube, Google and Meta.

Reported returns are high: return on fixed assets 76%, ROCE 26%, ROE 29% (IP p.10). Fixed
assets are only Rs 26.3 crore against Rs 101.7 crore of revenue (IP p.32), which is what
asset light means in practice. The clinical staff is 40 plus BAMS physicians and 71 plus
therapists (IP p.14).

Archetype: brand and franchise consumer, run as a services business. The economic engine is
a low cost content funnel converted into high margin treatment episodes. The scarce input is
trust, not capital.

## 3. Competitive advantages
The funnel is the advantage. 8 lakh subscribers and 6,100 videos built over years is a real
acquisition moat in a category where patients search before they choose, and it is
personal: the largest channel is the managing director's own name (IP p.18). NABH
accreditation for the Delhi and Gurgaon hospitals, plus CGHS and ECHS empanelment, give
institutional credibility (IP p.21).

The weakness is the same asset. The moat is one doctor's personal brand, in a category where
credibility is the product. There is no evidence in the corpus of clinical outcome data, and
the branded entity behind the funnel, "Karma Ayurveda ... Established in 1937", predates the
listed company by eighty years (IP p.18). Who owns that brand is NOT FOUND. Moat verdict:
present but personal, and dependent on a reputation the documents show is contested.

## 4. The promoters
Puneet Dhawan is Managing Director and promoter, with 12 years in renal and lifestyle
treatment. Tanya Dhawan is a Non-Executive Director. Sanchit Hans is Whole-time Director,
with the company since 2021 (IP p.8). CARE names the promoters as Puneet Dhawan and Tanya
Dhawan (Monitoring p.4).

The insider record is a genuine positive and is recorded as such. In March 2026, weeks after
listing, Som Dev Dhawan bought 199,000 shares for about Rs 3.94 crore and Puneet Dhawan
bought 115,000 shares for about Rs 2.33 crore, all from the open market at Rs 194 to Rs 207
(market file). No insider sale appears. Promoters putting Rs 6 crore of their own money in
at market prices is not a cheap signal.

Set against that, four bulk deals moved between 1.13 lakh and 2.11 lakh shares each in March
to May 2026, including a fund seller and a broking entity, in a company this size (market
file). Holding percentage, lock-in expiry and pledge are NOT FOUND.

## 5. Financial trajectory
Three years of revenue (IP p.17): Rs 67.2 crore, Rs 76.6 crore, Rs 101.7 crore. FY26 growth
32.8%.

FY26 against FY25 (IP p.30, p.31): EBITDA Rs 31.1 crore against Rs 19.1 crore, margin 30.6%
against 25.0%. PAT Rs 20.1 crore against Rs 11.2 crore, margin 19.8% against 14.7%. The H2
FY26 half alone grew EBITDA 102% and PAT 149% (IP p.28).

The balance sheet was transformed by the IPO (IP p.32). Net worth went from Rs 23.9 crore to
Rs 114.7 crore, including Rs 64.4 crore of share premium. Borrowings went from Rs 31.2 crore
to zero. Current ratio went from 1.65 to 13.82 (IP p.34).

Now the line that governs this card. Cash from operations in FY26 was Rs 1.12 crore against
Rs 20.12 crore of profit after tax (IP p.33, p.31). In FY25 it was Rs 3.49 crore against
Rs 11.21 crore. Where the profit went is visible on the balance sheet: trade receivables
rose from Rs 12.13 crore to Rs 22.73 crore, and other current assets from Rs 8.21 crore to
Rs 14.51 crore (IP p.32). Receivables are 82 days of revenue, against 58 days a year earlier
(derived). For a business that is part cash-paying hospital and part online D2C, 82 days is
the question this card cannot answer.

Classify the inflection: margin led on the reported P&L, and unconverted in cash.

## 6. The transition and the quality ladder
FROM R2 to R3, a product seller with a content funnel. TO an R4 claim, a franchise with
repeat economics and captured patients across hospitals, telemedicine and medical tourism.
The mechanism is the mix shift already visible in the numbers: services from 9.7% of revenue
in FY24 to 53.8% in FY26 (IP p.17).

Management's claim, quoted once: "Our FY26 performance marks a transformational year for KRM
Ayurveda as we crossed the Rs 100 Crore Revenue from operations milestone ... Our strategic
focus on expanding hospitals, specialty care, and digital health is driving stronger
margins, improving revenue quality, and deepening consumer trust" (IP p.29).

The mix shift is real and documented. The rung it lands on depends entirely on whether the
service revenue collects.

## 8. Proof check
The triggers claim a growth build funded by the IPO. The monitoring agency's item-wise table
says otherwise (Monitoring p.8, p.9, p.10). Of Rs 77.49 crore raised, Rs 47.33 crore was
utilised by 30 June 2026. That went Rs 22.77 crore to working capital, Rs 12.50 crore to
repaying loans and Rs 11.77 crore to issue expenses. The growth objects received Rs 0.29
crore in total: Rs 0.05 crore of salary and Rs 0.24 crore of office renovation. Telemedicine
capex and CRM, the two things the money was raised to build, stand at nil.

The working capital draw has its own proof problem. The prospectus planned Rs 8.00 crore of
IPO money into working capital in FY26 and Rs 14.90 crore in FY27. The company drew
Rs 22.77 crore in FY26, almost the entire two year allocation in year one (Clarification
p.4). Read that against cash from operations of Rs 1.12 crore and the picture is consistent:
the reported growth is consuming cash faster than the plan assumed.

Proof of revenue growth: delivered. Proof of profit: delivered on the P&L only. Proof of
cash: not delivered, and moving the wrong way.

## 9. Flags from the documents
- Cash conversion. FY26 PAT Rs 20.12 crore, cash from operations Rs 1.12 crore (IP p.31,
  p.33). This is the CLAUDE.md cash conversion guard firing, on the Kernex and Tipco
  pattern. It caps any later verdict at PROCEED WITH CAVEATS with the missing evidence
  named, and the missing evidence here is a receivables ageing.
- The monitoring agency cannot trace the money. Verbatim: "the commingling of issue proceeds
  with other operational funds has restricted our ability to independently trace and
  ascertain the end use of the issue proceeds. Accordingly, we rely on management
  representation" (Monitoring p.10). CARE still reports deviation from objects as Nil, on
  that basis.
- NSE queried the FY26 results three times. Results were filed 1 May 2026; the exchange
  wrote on 8 May, 13 June and 20 July about "certain deficiencies", and the company replied
  four times (Clarification p.1, p.5, p.6). The first missing items were a working capital
  certificate, a note on the balancing figure, and a declaration of unmodified opinion,
  omitted by "inadvertent oversight". This was the company's first annual result as a listed
  entity.
- An allocation change between objects was approved by the board but not by shareholders,
  and the monitoring agency answered "Yes" to whether anything could materially affect
  investor decisions (Monitoring p.5). The amount is small at Rs 0.20 crore. The sequencing
  is the flag.
- Issue expenses of Rs 11.77 crore on a Rs 77.49 crore raise is 15.2% of the money (derived
  from Monitoring p.7).
- Reputation risk, surfaced by the company itself. The investor presentation reproduces six
  patient testimonials, and five of them raise the question of fraud in their own words:
  "queries if it was a scam or fraud", "some were of the opinion that it's fraud", "The
  treatment at Karma Ayurveda is not fake or fraud", "confused in starting to think if it is
  fake or fraud", "confused looking at the complaints and negative reviews", "Many friends
  thought it is scam, fraud" (IP p.19). Nothing here establishes wrongdoing. What it
  establishes is that the company knows its brand carries a contested reputation and chose
  to answer it inside an investor document.
- Claims risk alongside it. The largest channel is branded "Kidney Treatment Without
  Dialysis" (IP p.18) and the same testimonial page carries "chronic renal disease does not
  always necessitate dialysis or transplantation" (IP p.19). Advertising of treatment claims
  for specified diseases is regulated in India. Whether these claims comply is NOT FOUND in
  this corpus and is a question for a deeper read, not a conclusion here.
- Statutory auditor is Shiv & Associates, a Faridabad firm (Clarification p.3). Small
  auditor, newly listed company, Rs 625 crore of market value. Surfaced, not investigated.
- Brand provenance. "Karma Ayurveda ... Established in 1937" against a company incorporated
  in 2019 (IP p.18). Ownership of the brand and any licence fee is NOT FOUND.
Not seen in corpus: related party transactions, promoter holding, lock-in expiry, the
August 2026 management change.

Proof not fired, plus clean optics, plus a closed gap, is the PRICED NARRATIVE cell. The

the downside is double, the transition failing and the multiple compressing.

## 12. Verdict card
**PASS. No spear pass, no /step1.** The transition here is real on the revenue line, and the
promoter buying is a real signal. Neither survives contact with the cash flow statement.

The case against is not one weak number. It is four documents pointing the same way: Rs 1.12
crore of operating cash on Rs 20.12 crore of profit, receivables up 87% on revenue up 33%,
the entire two year working capital allocation drawn in one year, and a monitoring agency
stating it could not trace the end use of the IPO proceeds because they were commingled. The
growth projects those proceeds were raised to build have not started.

Load-bearing fact to verify first: the receivables ageing at 31 March 2026 and 30 September
2026, with the counterparties named. If the Rs 22.73 crore collects, most of this card is
answered. If it does not, nothing else matters.

Verify next: (2) the FY26 annual report and the related party schedule, including who owns
the "Karma Ayurveda" brand; (3) the 31 August 2026 management change filing; (4) the
regulatory position on the treatment claims made in the company's own marketing channels;
(5) the June and September 2026 quarters, neither of which is in this corpus.

What would change the view: two consecutive quarters of operating cash flow at 70% or more
of profit after tax, plus the telemedicine build actually starting, would put this back on
the list as a research candidate. Another year of profit that does not collect would confirm
the PASS permanently. No price here.
