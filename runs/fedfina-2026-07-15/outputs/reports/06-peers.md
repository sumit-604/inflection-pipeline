## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

**2A Demand environment.** Peers describe a bifurcated demand picture, not a uniform boom. Gold-loan demand (Manappuram) is structurally supported by the RBI crackdown on unsecured lending and MFI stress pushing borrowers toward secured gold products, but Manappuram's own branch count and customer count are largely flat — its AUM growth is a price/ticket-mix story, not a volume story ("AUM growth is driven by the demand... unsecured borrowing opportunities have come down," Aug 2025). Secured MSME/LAP peers (SBFC, MASFIN, Five-Star) describe steady structural demand (SBFC: MSME TAM growing at a 24% CAGR "through peaks and drops... neither accelerated nor decelerated," Nov 2025) but are *actively suppressing* their own disbursal growth via deliberate underwriting tightening at the low-ticket end because of MFI-linked overleverage stress (SBFC: "leaving almost 10% on the table," Nov 2025). No peer describes anything resembling Fedfina's 52% Y-o-Y gold AUM print or a broad demand boom of that magnitude; the closest comparable (Manappuram, ~22-23% quarterly growth) is roughly half Fedfina's pace.

**2B Pricing and input costs.** Gold-loan yields are compressing sector-wide as leading players (Manappuram explicitly benchmarks itself against "leading gold loan players") cut rates to win larger-ticket, lower-risk customers — a genuine competitive dynamic, not purely a mix artifact, even though management frames it as "mix." On the secured MSME/LAP side, yield moves are smaller and mostly risk-driven (SBFC cutting small-ticket LAP) rather than competitive-pricing-driven. This partially supports but also partially complicates Fedfina's "mix effect, not competitive pricing" framing for its own yield compression — the peer gold-loan story shows both forces operating simultaneously in that sub-segment.

**2C Capex cycle.** All three secured-MSME/LAP peers (SBFC, Five-Star, MAS Financial) are expanding branch networks in FY26 at a steady, modest pace (20-90 branches/year depending on peer size), each tying expansion to unit-economics thresholds (SBFC's AUM/branch profitability gate; Five-Star's 95% new-branch breakeven rate). This is an industry-wide, not lone-company, expansion pattern — but at a materially smaller scale than Fedfina's single-year, single-product 100-150 branch program. Manappuram, by contrast, is *not* branch-expanding (an analyst notes "our branch count has remained static," Nov 2025) and is instead investing in digital/online gold-loan penetration. Verdict: this is closer to an industry-wide but modest capacity build among the secured-lending peers, with Fedfina's gold-branch program a materially more aggressive single-year commitment than any peer in this set — not a lone-expander-in-a-cautious-peer-set situation, but also not a matched capacity race at Fedfina's scale or funding logic.

**2D Competitive mentions.** No peer transcript in this set mentions Fedbank Financial Services, Fedfina, or Federal Bank by name or unmistakable description. Search across all 15 files for "Fedfina," "Fedbank," and "Federal Bank" returned zero matches. This is consistent with Fedfina's small/micro-cap size relative to the peer set and is itself uninformative (absence of mention is not evidence either way).

**2E Risks peers discuss that Fedfina does not.** (i) State-level political/regulatory risk: SBFC repeatedly names a Karnataka ordinance that "rudely shook up" a "superb portfolio" in two months (Nov 2025) as a distinct, non-credit risk category management now treats as structural ("what has changed over the years are the two risks that did not exist to this level earlier, political risk and climate risk," Aseem Dhru, Nov 2025) — Fedfina's B05 record does not name a comparable state-ordinance/political risk category for its own book. (ii) Climate risk as a named, tracked category (MAS Financial, Feb 2026: "climate risk, which has huge consequences... we are monitoring those also") — not referenced in Fedfina's B05 output. (iii) New RBI co-lending guideline compliance uncertainty flagged as an open operational risk requiring "fresh guard" on rate-of-interest and fee-structure setting for both banks and NBFCs (MAS Financial, Feb 2026) — Fedfina's B05 record cites the accounting effect of this change but not the broader compliance-uncertainty risk peers flag. (iv) The "hand loan" blind spot — SBFC candidly concedes undisclosed informal borrowing by MSME customers is "a grey area for the entire industry" that underwriting cannot fully capture (Jul 2025) — a structural industry risk with no analogue raised in Fedfina's own concalls per B05.

## PART 4: TRIANGULATION SUMMARY

- Claims verified: 2 of 9 fully (Q3 sub-5-7L MFI spillover, Q5 cost-to-income gap), 5 of 9 partially (Q1, Q2, Q6, Q7, Q8), 2 of 9 contradicted (Q4, Q9), 0 of 9 wholly unverifiable.
- Claims contradicted: 2 — these are priority items for synthesis, but of opposite character: Q9 (tonnage growth) is an unfavorable contradiction that undercuts a growth-quality justification; Q4 (Maharashtra/Tamil Nadu stress) is a contradiction of the *question's premise* that, read against Fedfina's own framing, is actually favorable — it supports management's claim that the MH/TN stress is company-specific rather than sector-wide.
- The single most consequential contradiction: Manappuram's disclosed Y-o-Y tonnage growth is 2.8% (Feb 2026 call), roughly a quarter of the 10-12% CAGR figure Fedfina used in its Q3 FY26 call to justify strong AUM growth despite a historically flat branch count. If the one peer with disclosed tonnage data runs at barely a quarter of the cited industry pace, the tonnage-growth justification for Fedfina's own AUM expansion does not hold up against available peer evidence.
- The single strongest independent confirmation: three independent peers (SBFC 35%, Five-Star 31-41% depending on credit-cost inclusion, MAS Financial 36.6%) all quantify cost-to-income ratios materially below Fedfina's ~56-57%, converting management's qualitative concession (per B05) into a hard, multiply-corroborated, large competitive gap.
- Overall: the peer set **complicates** Fedfina's narrative. It supports two structurally important elements — the sector-wide MFI-spillover mechanism behind ST LAP stress (Q3), and the fact that Maharashtra/Tamil Nadu stress is not a sector-wide pattern peers also see (Q4, favorable to Fedfina) — while directly undercutting the tonnage-growth justification for gold AUM expansion (Q9) and hardening, via three-peer quantification, the scale of Fedfina's cost-to-income disadvantage (Q5, a known but now sharply quantified weakness). The branch-expansion, yield-compression, and LTV-comparison claims all get partial, mechanism-level support without full magnitude confirmation, because either the specific named peer (Muthoot) or Fedfina's own comparison figures were absent from the transcript set. Read symmetrically, this is not a peer set that either fully validates or fully demolishes Fedfina's management narrative — it validates the qualitative direction of most claims while failing the one quantitative test (tonnage) that management leaned on most heavily to defend an above-peer growth number.
