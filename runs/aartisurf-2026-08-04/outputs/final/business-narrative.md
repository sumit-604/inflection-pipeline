Surfactants maker judged on stale numbers

Aarti Surfactants makes surfactants and specialty oleochemical ingredients, sold B2B to FMCG home care and personal care makers. Two customers were 35% of FY21 revenue, and exports were 28% of FY21 revenue before slipping to 20% by FY25. FY26 sales were Rs859 Cr per the screener data sheet. The shares trade at Rs481 for a market cap near Rs407 Cr. The company listed around 2019 via a demerger from Aarti Industries.

The only annual report in this run is FY2020-21, about 5.3 years old. There are no results PDFs, no credit rating document, no shareholding pattern, and no exchange announcements. Everything current comes from one screener data sheet running FY20 to FY26 and a nine slide investor deck dated September 2025 that carries FY25 versus FY24 headline numbers and nothing more. That gap sits under every conclusion below.

CWIP rose from Rs7.64 Cr to Rs14.65 Cr to Rs41.03 Cr across FY24 to FY26, and the company discloses no project, no timeline, and no capacity target behind it. The best fit read is a post COVID turnaround, and it is unconfirmed. A GARP transition is a live candidate on paper and also unconfirmed, because the years that would prove it are the years with no report.

Gate 0 scores AVOID. Core is 35 of 100 and the grand total is 43 of 160. Median ROCE is 8.36% and median ROE is 6.45% across FY20 to FY26, and neither ever clears 12%. Peak ROCE was 14.53% in FY24, and FY20 was an anomalously depressed base year at 0.64% net margin. The forward scan agrees: Emerging Moat is NONE at a score of 4 of 80, zero categories Strong or Moderate. Combined verdict AVOID.

FY21 cash conversion deteriorated on both sides of working capital. Trade receivables rose 203.7% against revenue up 43%, so receivable days doubled to 42.6 from 20.1. Trade payables rose 305.9%, so payable days nearly tripled to 80.1 from 28.3. Operating cash flow then swung hard: Rs51.96 Cr in FY24, down to Rs11.14 Cr in FY25 at 0.77 times that year's profit, and back up to Rs76.65 Cr in FY26 at 6.21 times profit per the screener. Whether the FY26 rebound is real or a payable stretch that will unwind cannot be settled without current filings. Accounting quality on the one available report is 4 of 10.

Credibility grades C. One trivial promise landed, the FY21 dividend. Two substantive qualitative commitments missed: cost and margin discipline, where EBITDA margin fell from 10.70% to 7.56% across FY24 to FY25, and export diversification, where the 30 plus countries message repeated while export share dropped to 20%. The September 2025 deck titled slide 7 "Financial Performance and Rating Upgrade" while both CARE ratings on it read as reaffirmed, not upgraded.

Promoter conduct reads CAUTION with no deal breakers. The FY21 shift that looked like a stealth grab, Jaya Gogri moving from 5.27% to 21.17% while HDFC Trustee exited the above 5% table, was a properly disclosed SEBI SAST open offer at a reasonable price. Two governance items remain: the 0% Redeemable Preference Shares stayed unlisted about 7 years because the CARE rating sat four notches below the AA minus SEBI listing floor, resolved only by a July 2026 redemption, and October 2025 added two more family pattern directors rather than outside professionals.

Peers contradict two of the company's own framing claims. Galaxy Surfactants cites 2% to 4% current India underlying volume growth against AARTISURF's stale 8 to 9.6% CAGR figure from 2020. Rossari rephased a Rs192 Cr capex plan down in the same window that AARTISURF's CWIP accelerated, and Galaxy wound its own capex to maintenance only, so the AARTISURF build is a lone expander, not an industry race. Peers do confirm raw material cost inflation in direction, but at much smaller magnitude. TAM runway grades GOOD at 5.4 times revenue headroom, though that headroom rests on the same 2020 demand growth basis the peers now undercut.
