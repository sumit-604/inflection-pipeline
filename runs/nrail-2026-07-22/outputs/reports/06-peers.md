# Stage 6: Peer Concall Verification — N R Agarwal Industries Ltd (NRAIL)
Run date: 2026-07-22 | Model: claude-sonnet-5 | Protocol: v1.1

## Scope note on peer set
Two peer tickers were flagged by Stage 5: JKPAPER (JK Paper Ltd) and KUANTUM (Kuantum
Papers Ltd). Eight transcripts were supplied. Critically, the two peers are NOT
symmetric substitutes for NRAIL's business:
- **JKPAPER** makes virgin-fibre packaging board (VFB), coated paper and writing/printing
  paper — the closest analogue to NRAIL's paperboard business, but its four supplied
  calls run May 2022 to May 2024, i.e. they stop roughly a year before NRAIL's disclosed
  FY24-25 realization trough (Rs41→Rs37/kg). JKPAPER explicitly states its board is
  100% virgin pulp with "no recycled paper or recycled fiber involved" (JKPAPER,
  Q1 FY23 call [2-Aug-2022], p.9) — it does not use waste paper, NRAIL's core input.
- **KUANTUM** is a writing/printing (Maplitho/copier/specialty) paper maker on wood
  and agro (wheat-straw) fibre. It states outright across multiple calls that it is
  "not in the paperboard segment at all" (Q2/H1 FY26 call [10-Nov-2025], p.7) and
  "I will reserve my comments for the only white paper because we are in this
  industry. Paperboards is an entirely different segment" (Q4 FY26 call [29-May-2026],
  p.5). Its four calls (Sep 2025–Jun 2026) are the most current window available but
  speak to a different product and a different fibre stream than NRAIL.

This mismatch is treated as informative throughout: where a peer is structurally
unable to speak to a claim, the verdict is UNVERIFIABLE rather than inferred, per
protocol rule 3.

One methodological note: the JKPAPER file labelled "May_2022" (Q4 FY22 results call)
was retrieved after a PDF-rendering tool failure (pdftoppm unavailable) required a
retry; its content was independently confirmed genuine on the second attempt, but
page-number citations for that single file carry slightly lower confidence than the
other seven, which were read and re-verified page-by-page. This is flagged in the
coverage map.

## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

**2A. Demand environment.** Across both peer vintages (JKPAPER FY22-24, Kuantum FY25-26), demand/volume is consistently described as intact — "robust" in the FY22-23 upcycle (JKPAPER, multiple calls) and "steady," with "encouraging improvement in volumes," even as pricing weakens in FY25-26 (Kuantum, Q1 FY26 call p.3; Q3 FY26 call p.2). The consistent peer framing is that the sector's margin problem is import-driven realization pressure, not a demand collapse — directionally consistent with, though not proof of, NRAIL's own decline being supply/import-driven rather than demand-driven.

**2B. Pricing and input costs.** Universal theme across both peers: realizations compressed by cheap imports (Indonesia/China/ASEAN) through FY25-26, with a DGTR anti-dumping investigation into paperboard imports from Indonesia initiated in July 2025 (Kuantum, Q1 FY26 call, 11-Aug-2025, p.3). Simultaneously, both peers report significant own-fibre cost inflation (JKPAPER wood +30-35% YoY FY24; Kuantum wheat straw +50-60% YoY into FY26, driven partly by Punjab flood aftermath). This "price down, cost up" squeeze pattern matches NRAIL's own described dynamic in shape (falling realization + rising input cost simultaneously), even though the specific inputs differ.

**2C. Capex cycle.** Genuinely industry-wide and concurrent with the downturn — not a lone-expander situation (see Q5). Concentrated in tissue and packaging-board sub-segments; writing/printing sees comparatively less new capacity per Kuantum's own account.

**2D. Competitive mentions.** No peer transcript, across all eight calls, mentions N R Agarwal Industries or NRAIL by name or by unmistakable description. This silence is noted but not itself informative given NRAIL's smaller scale relative to JKPAPER/Kuantum's typical peer set.

**2E. Risks peers discuss that NRAIL disclosures (as seen in this pipeline to date) do not appear to name:**
- GST/inverted-duty-structure risk: paper/board GST raised from 12% to 18% while converted products (mono-cartons, corrugated boxes) were cut to 5% and notebook paper made nil-rated, creating input-tax-credit blockage and higher working capital needs; this forced Kuantum to structurally exit the notebook segment, cutting its share from ~22% to ~7-8% (Kuantum, Q2/H1 FY26 call p.3; Q4 FY26 call p.13, 17).
- Regulatory timing risk on trade protection: both peers' anti-dumping/anti-subsidy/MIP applications remain pending 1.5-2+ years after filing, i.e. protection is not fast or guaranteed (Kuantum, Q4 FY26 call, 29-May-2026, p.9-10; JKPAPER, Q4 FY24 call, 21-May-2024, p.8).
- West Asia geopolitical conflict and freight disruption, with an explicit risk of Chinese/Indonesian import surplus being diverted to India at "predatory pricing" (Kuantum, Q4 FY26 call, p.2) — a live, dated 2026 macro risk.
- Structural competition for wood/agro fibre from adjacent industries (MDF, plywood, pulp-board sector) inflating input costs independent of paper-industry demand (JKPAPER, Q4 FY23 call p.7; Kuantum, Q1 FY26 call p.9).
- China's domestic "anti-involution" (overcapacity-reduction) policy as an unconfirmed potential tailwind — management explicitly skeptical of any near-term Chinese capacity reduction (Kuantum, Q3 FY26 call, 9-Feb-2026, p.16-17).

## PART 4: TRIANGULATION SUMMARY

- **Claims verified:** 1 of 6 (Q5, industry-wide capex cycle)
- **Claims partially verified:** 2 of 6 (Q1, realization decline direction but not FY25 turn; Q6, India-growth link for JKPAPER only)
- **Claims contradicted:** 2 of 6 (Q3, coated-board import-substitution framing; Q4, consolidation narrative) — **these go to synthesis as priority items**
- **Claims unverifiable:** 1 of 6 (Q2, waste-paper cost inflation — wrong fibre stream for both selected peers)

**Single most consequential contradiction:** JK Paper's own president, asked directly whether import pressure is driving industry consolidation, explicitly rejects the causal chain and attributes smaller-mill closures to individual mismanagement rather than dumping-driven distress (Q4 FY24 call, 21-May-2024, p.12). Any NRAIL thesis component that leans on "distress-driven consolidation benefits organized players" as a tailwind should discount this specific mechanism.

**Single strongest independent confirmation:** Both peers, plus three additional peers named by Kuantum (West Coast, Andhra Paper, Ruchira Paper) alongside JKPAPER, confirm a genuine industry-wide capex cycle running concurrent with the margin downturn (Q3 FY26 call, 9-Feb-2026, p.5). This validates that NRAIL's second greenfield unit is consistent with sector-wide behaviour rather than an outlier bet, though it also means NRAIL is one of several capacity adders into an already import-pressured market.

**Overall assessment.** The peer set neither cleanly supports nor cleanly undercuts NRAIL's management narrative — it complicates it. The demand backdrop (steady/growing, price-not-demand-driven margin pressure) and the capex-cycle framing are corroborated and lend the sector-level parts of NRAIL's story credibility. But the two most flag-worthy, differentiated claims in NRAIL's own narrative — that industry consolidation favours organized players like itself, and that coated/value-added board demand reflects import substitution — are directly contradicted by the peer with the closest product overlap (JKPAPER). The waste-paper cost claim, meanwhile, simply cannot be checked against this peer set at all, a real evidentiary gap that Stage 5's peer selection did not anticipate given both chosen peers are virgin/agro-fibre players.

## PART 5: CROSS-PEER HYPOTHESIS

Combining (a) JKPAPER's explicit disavowal of dumping-driven consolidation as the primary mechanism behind smaller-mill exits; (b) both peers' large, simultaneous, counter-cyclical capex programmes concentrated specifically in tissue, packaging-board and specialty grades rather than commodity capacity; (c) Kuantum's explicit strategic exit from the low-margin, GST-penalized notebook segment toward copier/Maplitho/specialty grades; and (d) JKPAPER's own diversification into corrugated packaging as a margin-defensive move —

**Hypothesis:** the organized/listed segment of the Indian paper industry is not consolidating primarily by acquiring or absorbing distressed unorganized capacity (the data suggests such exits happen for idiosyncratic reasons, not price-driven ones), but rather by structurally re-mixing AWAY from commodity grades most exposed to import price competition (notebook paper, plain packaging board, uncoated commodity grades) and TOWARD higher-value, harder-to-import-substitute grades (specialty/food-grade paper, tissue, corrugated packaging, coated/branded board). In other words, organized players appear to be differentiating their way out of import competition rather than out-competing on price or directly absorbing smaller mills' market share. No single peer states this explicitly — it only emerges from reading JKPAPER's corrugation pivot, Kuantum's specialty/tissue pivot and notebook exit, and both companies' counter-cyclical capex allocation together as one pattern.

This is testable: if the hypothesis holds, commodity-grade (plain writing/printing, notebook paper, base packaging board) pricing and utilization should continue to lag import-exposed benchmarks, while specialty/tissue/corrugated-packaging segment margins and volumes should outperform over the next several quarters, largely independent of whether the pending anti-dumping/anti-subsidy/MIP measures are eventually imposed.
