## SECTION 1: THE BUSINESS MODEL IN PLAIN ENGLISH

### 1A. One-line description

D. P. Abhushan buys gold, silver, diamonds and platinum, has outside goldsmiths turn them
into jewellery, and sells the finished pieces to families in 12 showrooms across Madhya
Pradesh and Rajasthan, mostly for weddings and festivals (AR p.4, p.8, printed p.02, p.10).

### 1B. Money flow chain, by revenue stream

**Stream 1 — Retail showroom sales (the core business, 87.2% of FY26 revenue, see 1C)**

Input: gold/silver/diamond bullion, sourced from bullion dealers, DTC sight holders, and
customer gold-exchange, plus Gold Metal Loan (GML) and leased-gold facilities newly used in
FY26 (AR p.8 printed p.10; Note 12, AR p.93-94 printed p.180-183)
→ What the company does: centralised buying and inventory allocation from Ratlam, jewellery
manufacturing outsourced to a nationwide goldsmith/vendor network within a ~300 km radius
(AR p.8, printed p.10-11)
→ What it delivers: hallmarked gold/diamond/silver/platinum jewellery displayed and sold
through 12 Company-Owned-Company-Operated (COCO) showrooms (AR p.6, printed p.06-07; AR p.8
printed p.11)
→ Who pays: the walk-in retail consumer, overwhelmingly individual households in Tier 2/3
towns (AR p.4, printed p.02-03)
→ How they pay: cash/card/UPI at point of sale (cash-and-carry; trade receivables are 0.04%
of FY26 revenue, Note 8, AR p.92 printed p.178-179), or via the DP Swarn Plus scheme — a
10-month, Rs 5,000+/month gold-accumulation plan that pre-funds part of the purchase before
mandatory redemption into jewellery (Q1 deck p.14; AR p.13, printed p.20-21). Old-gold exchange
funded roughly 25% of Q1 FY27 sales (Q1 deck p.6-7, management commentary).

**Stream 2 — "Non-Retail Operations" (12.3% of FY26 revenue, composition undisclosed)**

Input, process and delivery: NOT FOUND. Note 20 (AR p.95, printed p.184-185) discloses only
the rupee total, split as "Revenue from Retail Operations" vs "Revenue from Non-Retail
Operations," with no narrative anywhere in the AR or MD&A explaining what the non-retail line
is (bullion/wholesale trading is the most likely reading given the industry context, but this
is an inference, not a filed fact). It fell 5.96% YoY (Rs 530.98 Cr to Rs 499.32 Cr, Note 20)
in a year retail grew 27.6%, a divergence the company does not explain (also flagged in B02
rank 11 / B03).
→ Who pays / how: NOT FOUND.

**Stream 3 — Other Operating Income (0.46% of FY26 revenue): commission income plus a
"Profit Th. MCX Hedging" line of Rs 18.60 Cr (nil in FY25), both booked inside Revenue from
Operations rather than shown as a separate financial-income line (Note 21, AR p.95-96,
printed p.186-187).** This is a gold/silver hedging profit, not a sale of jewellery to a
customer; it is included here because the company includes it in "Revenue from Operations,"
not because it fits the plain-English "who pays / how they pay" chain of a retail sale.

### 1E. The chai-stall-uncle version

Picture the family jeweller you have trusted for three generations, except it grew into 12
shops across Madhya Pradesh and Rajasthan. The shop does not melt or mine its own gold: it
buys bullion, sends the design out to goldsmiths it trusts, and sells the finished piece over
the counter, mostly for weddings. Four-fifths of what it earns is really the price of the gold
itself, marked up a little for the making charge; the shop makes its real money on that small
markup and on getting people to choose its showroom over the informal jeweller down the road.
When gold prices shoot up, the shop's rupee sales look great even if it actually sold fewer
grams — which is exactly what happened in FY26. It just opened its first online store and is
about to try its first "franchise-like" outlet, but almost everything else is still the
family running its own showrooms with its own money on the shelf.

### 2D. Industry lifecycle stage

The Indian retail gems & jewellery industry is mid-formalisation: organised share was ~37% of
the CY25 gold-jewellery market and is projected to keep rising through CY29-30 (Q4 deck p.17,
p.25-26), with the overall market growing at a projected ~12.2% CAGR CY25-CY30P (MD&A p.143,
printed p.143). D. P. Abhushan itself sits earlier on its OWN company lifecycle than the
industry's formalisation curve: it only opened its first e-commerce channel in FY26, has not
yet opened a single franchise-format (FOCO) store, and remains concentrated in two adjoining
states, whereas larger organised peers already run national multi-format networks. The company
is a regional-scale-up story riding an industry-level formalisation tailwind, not yet a
national platform.

### 3D. Unit economics — the physics of the business

**Unit chosen: one gram of gold jewellery sold.** Gold dominates both revenue (~90-91% of
FY26 revenue by the deck's segment split) and cost (over 90-95% of total cost, CARE PR p.2),
so gram-level economics are the load-bearing physics of this business, more so than
per-store or per-transaction economics (for which the corpus lacks a clean, comparable
breakdown — see caveat below).

| Item | FY26 | FY25 | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gold grams sold (sales/consumption) | 55,30,535.03 g (~5,530.5 kg) | 63,80,585.86 g (~6,380.6 kg) | AR Note 33.5, p.101 printed p.197; FY25 AR Note 33.5, printed p.~205 |
| Gold segment revenue (deck) | Rs 3,702 Cr | Rs 3,071 Cr | Q4 deck p.9 |
| Implied revenue per gram | ~Rs 6,693/gram | ~Rs 4,812/gram | Calculated: gold revenue (deck) ÷ gold grams sold (AR Note 33.5) |
| YoY change | Grams sold: **-13.3%**. Revenue per gram: **+39%**. Gold segment revenue: +21% | | This is the single clearest evidence on LBF1: FY26 gold revenue growth is a price story, not a volume story |

- Revenue per unit: ~Rs 6,693/gram FY26 (calculated above; NOT an audited per-gram disclosure,
  it combines an audited quantity note with a non-audited deck segment revenue figure — flagged
  as a calculated cross-source figure, not a single-source anchor).
- Cost per unit: NOT FOUND at gram level. Cost of Material Consumed (Note 23, AR p.96, printed
  p.185: Rs 777.96 Cr FY26 vs Rs 703.80 Cr FY25) is company-wide and blended across gold,
  silver, diamond and platinum; it cannot be divided by gold grams alone without assuming a
  cost allocation the company does not disclose.
- Volume driver: footfall x conversion % x grams per ticket. Conversion held 81-87% across
  FY24-FY26 (Q4 deck p.10); grams per ticket is not separately disclosed.
- Price driver: the global gold price (pure pass-through on the bullion-value component) plus
  the making-charge rate and the diamond/studded mix (the only two levers management actually
  controls).
- Cost driver: bullion purchase price (>90-95% of total cost, CARE PR p.2), GML/gold-lease
  financing cost (new in FY26, Note 12), outsourced job-work/making charges paid to the
  goldsmith network, store rent (low, Tier 2/3 cities, mostly rented), and a thin employee cost
  base (Rs 40.01 Cr FY26, 0.98% of revenue).
- Incremental margin / operating leverage: real fixed-cost absorption is visible — Employee
  benefit expense (0.98% of revenue) and Other Expenses (1.80% of revenue) both grew far slower
  than the 22.9% revenue increase, consistent with FY26's 234 bps EBITDA margin expansion
  (AR p.10, printed p.14-15). But this stage cannot cleanly separate that structural operating
  leverage from the price/inventory-valuation effect described above (gold appreciating on a
  large, debt-funded inventory book) — the two are mixed in the same reported EBITDA line, and
  disentangling them is explicitly LBF1, carried to stage 11.
- Per-store economics caveat: Company-wide average revenue/store (Rs 339 Cr, AR p.4) blends
  mature flagship stores (Indore: 55,798 FY26 walk-ins) with a store open only weeks at
  year-end (Dhar: 133 FY26 walk-ins, Q4 deck p.11) — a genuine same-store or store-cohort
  unit-economics view is NOT FOUND in this corpus (only footfall, not revenue, is given by
  store).

**Which cycle stage matters for valuation:** the dominant near-term swing factor is not the
company's own life-cycle stage but the GOLD PRICE CYCLE. FY26 and Q1 FY27 results were earned
at record/near-record gold prices with declining physical volume (Section 3D); any multiple
applied to FY26/Q1 FY27 earnings risks paying peak-cycle prices for a price-driven margin
spike rather than a volume-driven, structural improvement. Stage 11 should test earnings on a
volume-normalized or through-cycle basis, not the as-reported FY26/Q1 FY27 print alone.

### 4C. Quarterly monitoring checklist

1. CFO (quarterly) — good: less negative or positive; trouble: a 4th straight negative year
2. Inventory days — good: trending toward the 75-90 day AR target; trouble: above CARE's own 75-day negative trigger
3. Gold grams sold, YoY — good: flattening or turning positive; trouble: continued double-digit decline
