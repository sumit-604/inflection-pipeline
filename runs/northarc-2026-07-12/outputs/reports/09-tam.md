## SECTION 1: MARKET DEFINITION

### 1A — Precise boundaries

- **Product scope (in):** secured MSME lending (LAP, supply-chain finance, mid-market business loans), unsecured MSME business loans, consumer finance (personal loans + consumer-durable finance, **non-gold**), rural/microfinance (JLG and individual, via Pragati Finserv), delivered D2C and via Intermediate Retail (co-lending/on-lending/FLDG with 368 originator partners); plus the placement/syndication fee pool and the NAIM AIF/PMS fee pool that ride on top of these same underlying credit flows.
- **Product scope (out, explicit exclusion):** gold loans (Northern Arc has no gold-loan product or licence — B04 confirms no such revenue stream), large-corporate/wholesale lending, prime-bank-served salaried personal loans (Tier-1, bank-dominated), international credit markets. Affordable Housing Finance (AHF, ~6% of the ₹6,802 Cr IR book, ≈₹408 Cr) and Vehicle Finance (VF, ~19% of IR book, ≈₹1,292 Cr) are carried only as pass-through IR co-lending/on-lending exposure to partner NBFC/HFCs, not as Northern Arc D2C product lines (D2C AUM is 100% MSME+Consumer+Rural per the Mar-26 AUM waterfall) — these are treated as SAM-expansion optionality (Section 5D), not core TAM.
- **Geographic scope:** India only; no international operations (B04, AR).
- **Customer scope:** underserved/underbanked self-employed individuals and MSMEs, largely non-metro; secured-lending customers screened at CIBIL 650+; ticket sizes ₹5–25 lakh (MSME secured), ₹40,000–5 lakh (consumer), ₹30,000–75,000 (rural/microfinance) — `investor-presentation.txt` pp.11-16.
- **Channel scope:** 432 D2C branches + 57 digital partners + Intermediate Retail co-lending/FLDG/placement network of 368 originator partners + institutional placement/syndication to banks/NBFCs/offshore investors + NAIM fund management (6 funds + 2 PMS).
- **Price segment:** mid-to-high-yield retail/MSME lending (blended yield 15.5–16.9%, B04 unit economics), not prime bank-rate lending.

### 1B — Management's own market-opportunity claim

Northern Arc's AR (FY24-25, MD&A §4.1.3, `annual-report.txt` lines 3929-3936) states: *"India's MSME sector presents a significant lending opportunity, with a credit gap of INR 117 trillion against a total demand of INR 159 trillion – only INR 42 trillion is currently served through formal channels."* Sourced to a CRISIL Intelligence estimate (also independently found via web search — CRISIL: MSME credit gap ≈₹117 trillion as of FY25, up from ₹58.4 trillion in FY17). Date: AR published July 2025, MD&A section dated to CRISIL's 28-June-2025 report.

This is the only explicit, sourced market-opportunity number management attaches to its own business narrative (the ₹82 trillion "India retail credit market" figure on p.11 of the AR is broader macro scene-setting, not a company-specific claim, and lacks a stated segment breakdown — flagged below). Credibility read: **broad**. It sizes the *entire national MSME credit gap*, not the narrow small-ticket (₹5-25 lakh secured / sub-₹5 lakh unsecured), CIBIL-650+-screened slice Northern Arc actually underwrites. It is held for the Section 2 triangulation table.

## SECTION 2: TAM ESTIMATION, MULTIPLE METHODS

All figures ₹ Crore unless stated as ₹ trillion for readability (1 trillion = ₹1,00,000 Cr). TAM is built as a **credit-stock** figure first (total addressable loan book at 100% capture) and then converted to a **revenue-pool** figure (interest + fee income at 100% capture) using representative net yields — standard practice for lender TAM sizing.

### Method 1 — Top-down (segment-level CRISIL/SIDBI credit-market sizing)

Sourced segment sizes, FY24-25 (all `annual-report.txt` MD&A §4.1, CRISIL-dated 28-Jun-2025, cross-checked against independent web search):

| Segment | Formal-served stock FY24-25 | Total demand (formal + gap) | Growth | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| MSME (secured + unsecured) | ₹42 trillion | ₹159 trillion (gap ₹117tn) | 16% CAGR to FY27-28 | AR l.3929-3936; web-confirmed (CRISIL) |
| Consumer finance ex-gold (personal loans ₹14.6tn + durables ₹0.83tn) | ₹15.43 trillion | not disclosed as a gap figure | ~20% CAGR to FY27-28 | AR l.3973-4014 |
| Rural / MFI (total industry portfolio) | ₹3.8 trillion | not disclosed as a gap figure | 8-10% CAGR to FY27-28 (AR) — **STALE, see flag** | AR l.4029-4039 |
| **Sum — formal-served (conservative stock base)** | **₹61.23 trillion** | | | |
| **Sum — total demand (realistic stock ceiling, MSME gap included)** | | **₹178.23 trillion** | | |

Gold loans (₹12.4tn of the ₹27.8tn consumer-finance headline) are explicitly subtracted — Northern Arc has no gold-loan product (1A). Housing (mortgage) and the bulk of vehicle finance are excluded at the outset — they sit outside Northern Arc's D2C product set and are picked up only as small IR pass-through exposure (Section 5D), not core TAM.

**Revenue-pool conversion:**
- Conservative TAM revenue = ₹61,20,000 Cr (stock) × 13% conservative blended yield (weighted toward the lower-risk, lower-price segments that dominate the *formal-served* base) = **₹7,95,600 Cr**
- Realistic TAM revenue = ₹1,78,20,000 Cr (stock, includes the MSME gap) × 16% (Northern Arc's own blended net yield, B04 unit economics — representative of what a lender actually pricing the *underserved* segment earns) = **₹28,51,200 Cr**

### Method 2 — Bottom-up (unit economics, MSME segment)

Addressable unit: one underserved MSME lacking formal credit access.
- Total MSMEs in India: ~70 million; <15% have formal credit access (SIDBI, "Understanding Indian MSME Sector," May 2025, web-confirmed) ⇒ underserved units ≈ 59.5 million (5.95 crore).
- Revenue-per-unit proxy: Northern Arc's own average ticket size for secured MSME lending is ₹11-15 lakh (`investor-presentation.txt` p.15); midpoint ₹13 lakh.
- Bottom-up implied credit gap = 5.95 crore MSMEs × ₹13 lakh = **₹77.35 trillion**.
- Sanity check against Method 1's top-down MSME gap (₹117 trillion): bottom-up lands at **0.66x** of top-down — same order of magnitude, directionally consistent (top-down includes larger-ticket MSMEs beyond Northern Arc's own ATS band, which is expected to push the top-down number higher). **Confidence: Medium** — this method corroborates Method 1 rather than standing fully independent, since it reuses Northern Arc's own ATS as the per-unit multiplier.
- Revenue-pool conversion at 16% yield: ₹77.35tn × 16% = **₹12,37,600 Cr** (MSME-only; excludes Consumer and Rural, for which no reliable underserved-borrower-count data was found — **NOT FOUND**, not estimated, per input_gaps).

### Method 3 — Peer AUM aggregation (directional cross-check, not a headline estimate)

Four listed/large peers operating in overlapping retail/MSME/consumer-NBFC segments, most recent disclosed AUM (all web search, FY26):
- Cholamandalam Investment & Finance: ₹2,42,630 Cr (FY26, +21% YoY)
- IIFL Finance: ₹1,08,180 Cr (FY26, +38% YoY)
- Poonawalla Fincorp: ₹60,348 Cr (FY26, +69.4% YoY)
- Vivriti Capital: ₹9,302 Cr (FY25; group-level incl. VAM >₹13,000 Cr; targeting ₹20,000 Cr)
- **Sum of these four alone: ₹4,20,460 Cr.**
(Five-Star Business Finance and MAS Financial Services figures returned by search were internally inconsistent/unreliable — e.g. one Five-Star figure implied ~₹1.24 lakh Cr AUM, an order of magnitude above its known scale — and are **excluded** rather than used, per the "never estimate" rule.)

These four peers alone (excluding hundreds of smaller listed/unlisted NBFC-MFIs, HFCs, and fintech-NBFCs, and excluding the unorganised/informal-lender segment, conventionally 30-60% of an Indian retail-credit market per pipeline guidance) already sum to ~₹4.2 lakh Cr of *formal, organised* book. Grossing this up even lightly for the long tail of smaller organised players plus a 40-50% unorganised overlay lands comfortably within the ₹40-80 lakh Cr range — the same order of magnitude as Method 1's conservative-to-realistic stock band (₹61-178 lakh Cr). **Directional confirmation, low precision — used to sense-check scale, not as a standalone headline number.**

### Method 5 — Global benchmark (household credit / GDP)

- India nominal GDP FY24-25: ₹188 trillion (AR MD&A §4.1.1, l.3823-3826).
- India retail credit as % of GDP, per the AR: ~25% (l.3866-3872). Per RBI data (web search, Sept-2025): household-sector debt is 45.5% of GDP on a broader definition. China's household debt/GDP: ~59% (web search).
- Applying the RBI 45.5%-of-GDP base and asking "what if India converged toward China's 59%": incremental household-credit headroom = (59%-45.5%) × ₹188tn GDP ≈ **₹25.4 trillion** of additional stock, before even accounting for India's GDP growing to ₹235tn by FY29-30.
- **Caveat, flagged:** the AR's "25% of GDP" and RBI's "45.5% of GDP" use different scope (formal retail credit only vs. all household debt including informal/other), so this method is directional only, low confidence, and used solely to confirm that India is structurally under-penetrated versus regional peers — not blended into the headline TAM number.

### Triangulation table

| Method | Estimate (revenue-pool, ₹ Cr) | Confidence | Staleness |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 — Top-down, conservative (formal stock only) | 7,95,600 | H | Fresh (CRISIL, Jun-2025) |
| 1 — Top-down, realistic (incl. MSME gap) | 28,51,200 | M | Fresh, but Rural/MFI sub-component flagged stale (see below) |
| 2 — Bottom-up (MSME only) | 12,37,600 (MSME-only, not directly comparable to Method 1's 3-segment sum) | M | Fresh |

**Conservative TAM: ₹7,95,600 Cr. Realistic TAM: ₹28,51,200 Cr.** Methods converge on the same order of magnitude (multi-lakh-crore revenue pool); the spread between conservative and realistic is driven almost entirely by whether the ₹117 trillion MSME credit gap is treated as capturable (realistic) or excluded (conservative) — a reasonable, explained divergence, not averaged away.

**Management's claim vs. conservative estimate:** mgmt_claim_cr (₹117tn MSME gap, converted to revenue-pool terms at Northern Arc's own 16% yield) = **₹18,72,000 Cr**. Ratio to conservative TAM (₹7,95,600 Cr) = **23.5x**. Per the standard read (>2x likely inflated), this reads **inflated** — but the inflation is definitional, not dishonest: management's cited figure is an industry-wide MSME statistic covering ticket sizes and enterprise scales well beyond Northern Arc's own product/ATS fit, not a claim that Northern Arc itself addresses ₹117 trillion. Treated as sector context, not as Northern Arc's own TAM.

## SECTION 3: SAM & SOM

### 3A — SAM: five filters applied

Product fit is already embedded in the TAM base (Section 1A/2 excluded gold loans, housing, wholesale/corporate lending at the outset), so no further product-fit discount is applied here (retention = 100%, to avoid double-counting).

| Filter | Retention | Rationale (judgment-based where no precise disclosure exists — flagged) |
|---|---|---|
| Geography | 55% | D2C branch-based sectors (MSME: 90 branches/7 states; Rural: 342 branches/8 states+1UT) cover roughly a third of India's 28 states + 8 UTs directly; IR/placement channel is genuinely pan-India (28 states, 7 UTs, `annual-report.txt` l.4062), which pulls the blended retention above a pure branch-count ratio. **Judgment estimate, not sourced to a specific disclosure.** |
| Channel (applied only to the unmet MSME-gap slice) | 35% | Only a minority of the ₹117tn unmet MSME demand is reachable via any regulated NBFC/fintech channel within a realistic horizon; the rest requires informal-to-formal conversion (credit-bureau onboarding, GST formalisation) that no single lender's channel can force. **Judgment estimate.** |
| Customer | 80% | Excludes thin-file/no-CIBIL-history borrowers (a real subset of the unserved population) and prime salaried-bank customers at the top end. **Judgment estimate.** |
| Capability | 90% | Co-lending/syndication model (368 originator partners) means capital is not a hard volume ceiling — Northern Arc can lay off excess demand via IR/placement rather than being capacity-capped the way a manufacturer is. **Judgment estimate; cross-checked in 3C below.** |

Calculation (applied to the two TAM stock components separately, since the channel filter only bites on the unmet-gap portion):
- Formal-served stock (₹61,20,000 Cr) × 0.55 (geo) × 0.80 (customer) × 0.90 (capability) = **₹24,23,520 Cr**
- Unmet-gap stock (₹1,17,00,000 Cr) × 0.55 (geo) × 0.35 (channel) × 0.80 (customer) × 0.90 (capability) = **₹16,21,620 Cr**
- **SAM stock = ₹40,45,140 Cr (≈₹40.45 trillion)**
- SAM revenue-pool = ₹40,45,140 Cr × 15% blended representative yield = **₹6,06,800 Cr**

**SAM as % of realistic TAM: 6,06,800 / 28,51,200 = 21.3% (≈21%).**

### 3B — SOM at 3 and 5 years

Current lending AUM (Mar-2026): ₹16,594 Cr. Current SAM share (revenue basis) = ₹2,387 Cr / ₹6,06,800 Cr = **0.39%**.

The standard share-gain-in-percentage-points framework (1-2pp normal / 3-5pp aggressive / >5pp only on competitor exit) **does not bind here** — SAM is ~254x current revenue, so even a "trivial" 0.1-0.3 percentage-point gain represents multi-fold company growth. Given this, SOM is anchored directly to management's own stated, credible operating guidance (double AUM by FY2029; sustain 22-25% growth thereafter) rather than mechanically applying the pp-gain rule, and then cross-checked against the implied SAM-share movement.

- **SOM 3-year (FY29) AUM** = ₹16,594 Cr × 2.0 (management's explicit "double AUM by FY2029" target, implying a 26.0% 3-yr CAGR — `results-Q4-FY26.txt` l.2329) = **₹33,188 Cr**
- **SOM 5-year (FY31) AUM** = ₹33,188 Cr × (1.235)² (continuing at the midpoint of the guided 22-25% "sustain" range for two further years) = **₹50,633 Cr**
- Revenue-pool proxy, applying the current total-income/AUM ratio (₹2,387 Cr / ₹16,594 Cr = 14.39%, held constant — **flagged assumption: no margin expansion/compression modelled**):
  - **SOM 3-year revenue = ₹33,188 Cr × 14.39% ≈ ₹4,777 Cr**
  - **SOM 5-year revenue = ₹50,633 Cr × 14.39% ≈ ₹7,286 Cr**

**som_implied_revenue_cagr:**
- yr3: (4,777/2,387)^(1/3) − 1 = **26.0%**
- yr5: (7,286/2,387)^(1/5) − 1 = **25.0%**

Cross-check against SAM: SOM 3yr revenue as % of (SAM grown at ~16%/yr for 3 years ≈ ₹9,22,000 Cr) = 4,777/9,22,000 = **0.52%**, up from today's 0.39% — a **+0.13pp** gain over 3 years, well below even the "normal" 1-2pp band. This confirms the earlier point: management's guidance, while ambitious in company terms (doubling AUM), is a negligible share-gain against the true scale of the addressable market. **The binding constraint on Northern Arc's growth is not TAM/SAM size or competitive share dynamics — it is capital, funding-mix access, and underwriting/credit-cost discipline** (consistent with B04's one-line verdict that earnings live and die on credit cost, not revenue growth).

### 3C — Capacity cross-check

B07_CAPEX_FIGURE = 0 (NBFC, no manufacturing capex; the capex-embedded-growth mechanic does not apply, per injected instructions). The equivalent cross-check for a lender is **balance-sheet/capital capacity**, not physical plant capacity:
- Capital adequacy ratio (CRAR): 22.6% as on 31-Mar-2026 (`results-Q4-FY26.txt` l.2305) — well above the regulatory minimum for an NBFC-ML (~15%).
- Net worth: ₹3,896 Cr, +13% YoY (`results-Q4-FY26.txt` l.2304).
- **capacity_check: sufficient.** At 22.6% CRAR against a ~15% regulatory floor, Northern Arc has meaningful embedded leverage headroom to fund AUM growth toward the doubling-by-FY2029 target without an immediate fresh equity raise, provided credit costs stay controlled (a genuine capability constraint, but not a capital-adequacy one). The real constraint flagged in B04's mgmt_questions — bank-funding pullback from NBFCs and the target DCM/bank/DFI funding mix — is a **funding-access** question, not a capital-adequacy or market-size one. Neither the SOM nor the "capacity" side reads as the optimistic party here; funding mix is the swing factor, and it sits outside this stage's scope (flagged for stage 11/13 attention).

## SECTION 4: GROWTH DRIVERS, RISKS & STRUCTURE

### 4A — TAM growth drivers

| Driver | Impact | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Formalisation of MSME credit | High | MSME credit gap widened from ₹58.4tn (FY17) to ₹117tn (FY25) even as formal MSME credit grew 16% CAGR — formalisation is running but the addressable base is expanding faster than it's being served (AR, web-confirmed CRISIL) |

| Risk | Monitoring signal |

### 4C — Market structure

- Highly fragmented at the borrower level (70mn MSMEs, ~5.5 crore unique microfinance borrowers) but concentrated at the large-NBFC/bank level for organised credit.
- Organised vs. unorganised: MSME formal penetration <15% by count; ₹42tn served vs. ₹159tn total demand (~26% served by value) — unorganised/unmet share is large (>40%), consistent with the pipeline's "faster share-gain possible where unorganised >40% is formalising" rule, though Section 3B shows this doesn't change the conclusion that TAM size is not Northern Arc's binding constraint.
- Consolidating in large-ticket NBFC lending (Cholamandalam ₹2.4 lakh Cr, IIFL ₹1.08 lakh Cr, Poonawalla ₹60k Cr scaling fast at 21-69% YoY) while remaining fragmented in the small-ticket/co-lending niche Northern Arc occupies (368 originator partners, mostly sub-₹2,000 Cr AUM each per the partner-AUM-range chart, `investor-presentation.txt` l.606-610).
- Price vs. differentiation: Northern Arc competes on underwriting-data differentiation (NuScore) and channel/co-lending structuring rather than price, per B04.
- Rural/MFI segment is currently contracting (exits/pullback by weaker players amid the FY25 overleveraging correction) — a segment where competitor stress could open share faster than the "normal" pp-gain rule, but also the segment carrying the highest credit-cost risk.

## SECTION 5: SUMMARY & RUNWAY

### 5A — Funnel

```
TAM (realistic, revenue-pool)  : ₹28,51,200 Cr   [MSME+Consumer(ex-gold)+Rural, total demand incl. MSME gap, @16% yield]
TAM (conservative)             : ₹ 7,95,600 Cr   [formal-served stock only, @13% yield]
        ↓  5 filters (geo × channel × customer × capability)
SAM                             : ₹ 6,06,800 Cr   (21% of realistic TAM)
        ↓  current share 0.39%, guided 22-25% AUM CAGR / double-by-FY29
SOM 3-year                      : ₹    4,777 Cr   (26.0% implied revenue CAGR)
SOM 5-year                      : ₹    7,286 Cr   (25.0% implied revenue CAGR)
Current FY26 total income       : ₹    2,387 Cr
```

### 5B — Runway assessment

- Revenue headroom = SAM ÷ current revenue = 6,06,800 / 2,387 = **254x**.
- TAM growth rate: blended ~17% (MSME 16%, Consumer ~20%, Rural 8-10% per stale AR figure but likely lower/negative near-term per Section 4B) — weighted toward MSME's dominant share of the stock base.
- Company AUM CAGR (22-25% guided, 22% FY26 actual) is running **above** blended TAM growth (~17%) — Northern Arc is gaining share, not merely riding the market, though the absolute share gained (Section 3B: +0.13pp over 3 years) is immaterial against the total market.
- Years to saturate SAM at current growth: **not meaningfully computable / effectively >25-30 years** — SAM itself compounds at ~15-17%/yr in absolute ₹ terms, which outpaces the rate at which Northern Arc's much smaller base can close the gap even at its own faster 22-25% CAGR, for a long stretch of the projection horizon. Functionally unconstrained by TAM within any investable time horizon.

### 5C — Runway classification

**MASSIVE.** Revenue headroom of 254x, blended TAM growth in the mid-to-high teens, and a company nowhere close to saturating even its already-filtered SAM. The standard caveats apply: MASSIVE runway on the market side does not offset the credit-cost and funding-mix execution risks flagged in B04 and Section 3C — this stage measures market size, not execution probability.

### 5E — Final output card

- Market definition: Indian formal + credit-gap retail lending market across MSME, non-gold consumer finance, and rural microfinance, served via D2C branches/digital and Intermediate-Retail co-lending/placement channels.
- TAM: ₹7,95,600 Cr (conservative) to ₹28,51,200 Cr (realistic). SAM: ₹6,06,800 Cr (21% of realistic TAM). SOM 3yr: ₹4,777 Cr. SOM 5yr: ₹7,286 Cr.
- Runway: MASSIVE (254x revenue headroom; not TAM-constrained on any investable horizon).
- **Valuation implication line:** At **25-26%** revenue CAGR implied by SOM, with a margin trajectory of (RoA 2.8% FY26, guided toward 3.7-4.0% per B04's must-track metrics, i.e. margin **expanding**), the earnings growth embedded here is **materially above 25-26%** revenue CAGR once RoA expansion and operating leverage are layered on (exact PAT CAGR is a stage-11 DCF/multiple-driven output, not computed here), which **supports** the operator's 25% CAGR transition-alpha target on a market-size basis — the constraint this stage identifies is not market size but credit-cost/funding-mix execution (B04, Section 3C), which stage 11's exit-multiple discipline (Section 1B v3.3) and stage 13 synthesis should weigh explicitly rather than assume away.
