## Business Understanding Narrative

Kross forges and casts metal parts for trucks, trailers and tractors, and assembles them into trailer
axles. Two revenue streams, split 41% trailer axles and suspension to 59% components in the June 2026
quarter (IP p.6). The component list is long and unglamorous: companion flanges, axle shafts, rear end
spindles, anti-roll bars, differential spiders, inter-axle kits, universal joints and crosses, cam
shafts and tractor hydraulic-lift parts (Rating p.3). Customers are the OEMs: Tata Motors, Ashok
Leyland, TAFE, DANA India, International Tractors and Eaton, most of them for nearly two decades
(Rating p.3). On the trailer side it sells to fabricators and dealers rather than OEMs, and India
Ratings places it among "the top three trailer axle manufacturing players competing with bigger
players such as Tata Motors and York Transport Equipment" (Rating p.3).

The money comes from conversion, not from price. The company buys steel, forges, casts, machines and
ships. Gross margin is 44.8% and EBITDA margin 12.2% in the June quarter, so almost all the value sits
in the conversion step and its overheads (IP p.8). Management is plain about pricing: "Pricing is
always in line with what competitors do" (Concall p.10). A 3% to 5% price rise from 1 April was steel
pass-through, with "another 1% or 2%" to follow, staggered "so that the customers also accept it"
(Concall p.10). India Ratings says the same thing from outside: "The company benefits from a
pass-through mechanism with OEMs for raw material price volatility, which largely compensates for
input cost increases, albeit with a time lag" (Rating p.3).

What is changing is the manufacturing route. Kross spent Rs 100.2 crore of capex in FY26 against
Rs 28.4 crore in FY25 and Rs 27.2 crore in FY24, a three and a half times step up (Rating p.3). It
bought that: India's first axle beam extrusion plant, commissioned February 2026, Rs 25 crore, which
replaces a welded fabricated beam with a single extruded piece; a tipping jack line; multiple
high-tonnage forging presses; a high-pressure moulding line for the foundry; robotic press forging for
axle shafts; and a Rs 167 crore seamless tube plant now under construction.

Why now: the commercial vehicle cycle turned. Revenue rose 32.3% year on year in the June quarter to
Rs 184.3 crore, the best first quarter the company has had, helped by "GST rationalization benefits"
and an M&HCV revival (IP p.5). Q4 FY26 sales "were sort of capped by our capacities" (Concall p.6).
The capacity arriving now meets demand that already exists.

What must be true: the new routes must earn a higher margin than the old ones. The extrusion plant's
claim is lower material cost with no welding, lighter weight and better tyre life (IP p.9). Seamless
tubes replace a bought-in raw material with a made one.

What breaks it: the EBITDA margin has been 13.0%, 13.1% and 13.1% across FY24, FY25 and FY26
(Rating p.3), and 12.2% in the June quarter (IP p.8). Three years of backward integration have moved
it nowhere. And the depreciation from all this capital is already outrunning the profit it produces:
profit after tax grew 24.4% in the June quarter against EBITDA growth of 39.5%, "primarily due to
higher depreciation expenses from our strategic capital investments" (IP p.5, p.7). That gap widens
before it narrows.

## 2. Business model and archetype
Buy steel, add process, ship a safety-critical part against a drawing. Revenue is units times a
converted price. The units are now disclosed: about 9,500 axles and 3,200 to 3,300 suspensions in the
June quarter, mechanical and air together (Concall p.9). Against a stated axle beam capacity of 7,500
units a month, or 22,500 a quarter (IP p.9), the axle line ran near 42% utilisation in the quarter the
new capacity arrived. Forging and casting run "around a 70% utilisation" (Concall p.9). There is a lot
of unused plant here, which is the whole point of the coming two years and also the whole risk.

Archetype: **Build-to-spec component maker** for the 59% component business, where customer capex
cycle, design wins, content per unit and input-cost pass-through are exactly the right dials and all
four are visible in the corpus. The 41% trailer axle business behaves differently: it sells to
fabricators and dealers, competes on price with York and Tata Motors, and is closer to a
**commodity converter**. Declare both. The margin bridge for this company is two bridges.

## 3. Competitive advantages
Real but narrow, and better evidenced than on most cards in this run.
- **Backward integration.** "KL's business is fully backward integrated with the presence of its
  foundry division; it is among the few players with a full product range of axles and suspension
  (both mechanical and air suspension)" (Rating p.3). Management ties this to pricing control:
  "when we are backward integrated with most of our products on the axle and suspension, it is easier
  for us to have a good calculation on that" (Concall p.10).
- **A process advantage that is genuinely first.** The axle beam extrusion plant is described as
  India's first single-piece extrusion route, replacing welding, with lower material cost, lighter
  weight and improved tyre life (IP p.9). If that holds up commercially, it is a spec advantage a
  customer pays for, which is the definition of the rung above.
- **Two decades of OEM relationships**, a Super 8 Supplier recognition from Ashok Leyland in FY25, and
  200-plus customers across three fiscals (IP p.14; Rating p.3). Switching costs in safety-critical
  forged parts are real: requalification is slow.
- **Low import dependency**, "only 12% of total purchases sourced primarily from China" (Rating p.3).

Against all of that sits one sentence from the company: "Pricing is always in line with what
competitors do" (Concall p.10). A business with pricing power does not say that.
**Verdict: a moat forming, not present.** The integration is real; the pricing power it is supposed to
buy has not appeared in three years of margin data.

## 4. The promoters
A Jamshedpur family business, incorporated 1991, four promoters: Sudhir Rai, Anita Rai, Sumeet Rai and
Kunal Rai (RHP p.252). Sudhir Rai, aged 64 at the IPO, is Chairman and Managing Director. Sumeet Rai
and Kunal Rai both answer operating and financial questions on the call, so the second generation is
visibly running the business rather than being introduced.

Pre-IPO the four held 99.26% between them, with Dipak Rai HUF holding a further 0.74% through Sudhir
Rai as karta, so 99.99% in total (RHP p.105). Post-IPO group holding is **NOT FOUND**: the prospectus
leaves the post-offer column blank and the corpus holds no shareholding pattern. Looked for in the
RHP, the decks and the SAST disclosure.

What the corpus does give is better than a percentage. On 30 March 2026, Anita Rai, promoter and whole
time director, **bought 30,000 shares in the open market**, taking her holding from 18.40% to 18.45%
(SAST p.2). The same filing records her encumbered shares, before and after, as nil (SAST p.2). A
promoter adding to a position with her own money, in the market, twelve days before the year end, is
the cleanest skin-in-the-game signal available in this run. Pledge, for this holder, is nil and
evidenced. For the group it is NOT FOUND.

Three readings, in order.

**The three-year line is flat.** Revenue Rs 620.3 crore to Rs 620.4 crore to Rs 673.2 crore. India
Ratings explains FY25: "revenue remained flat in FY25 as increased volumes in the trailer axle
business was offset by muted demand in certain component categories" (Rating p.3). EBITDA margin sat
at 13.0%, 13.1%, 13.1%. Nothing moved.

**The FY26 shape inside that line is a recovery.** First half revenue Rs 270.3 crore with Rs 18.8
crore of profit; second half Rs 402.9 crore with Rs 36.5 crore. Q4 FY26 delivered a 14.9% EBITDA
margin, the best quarter in the table, on capacity-capped sales.

**The June quarter is strong on revenue and soft on margin.** Revenue Rs 184.3 crore, up 32.3% and the
best-ever first quarter (IP p.5). EBITDA margin 12.2%, up 63 basis points year on year but down from
Q4's 14.9% and below the FY26 average. Depreciation rose 46.7% year on year and profit growth of 24.4%
lagged EBITDA growth of 39.5% (IP p.8). Management names the reason itself.

Leverage is modest and rising with purpose. Gross adjusted debt went from Rs 66.4 crore to Rs 88.6
crore in FY26 "due to the additional debt raised primarily for adding capacities for seamless tubes"
(Rating p.3). A Rs 100 crore term loan for the seamless plant is sanctioned and being drawn as
machines arrive; management says there is "no plan on any debt in the immediate timeline" for the
existing business (Concall p.9). IPO proceeds are "utilized fully as per the plan" (Concall p.5).

**Inflection classification: EARNINGS-LED, cycle-assisted, margin not yet participating.** Revenue up
32.3% with the margin roughly flat is volume, not mix. Management attributes part of it to a Q4
spillover and to GST rationalisation (IP p.5; Concall p.6), both of which are real and neither of
which is a transition. Cash conversion is **INDETERMINATE**: no cash flow statement is held, and the
framework caps that at PROCEED WITH CAVEATS with the missing evidence named. Return on capital is
**NOT FOUND**: no balance sheet is held, and for a company in a capex ramp that is the number that
matters most.

## 6. The transition and the quality ladder
FROM **R2 COST-ADVANTAGED CONVERTER.** Margin comes from cost position, not price: a foundry, forging
presses, machining, and a pass-through arrangement with OEMs. Mid-teens EBITDA, flat for three years.
The company's own pricing sentence puts it here.
TO **R3 VALUE-ADDED / SPEC'D SUPPLIER.** Spec-in through a process nobody else in India runs, plus
switching costs in safety-critical parts, earning partial pricing power.
Mechanism: the extruded axle beam. Not a capacity story but a product story. **Management's claim,
quoted once:** "Clear Competitive Edge: Lower material cost (no welding), Lighter weight, Superior
technical performance leading to improved tyre life... Margin Upside: EBITDA margins expected to
improve significantly above 50% utilization" (IP p.9).
**Ladder ruling: one rung, which is the base rate, and the mechanism is specific and physical.** That
is a materially better transition claim than a slogan. What is missing is any margin evidence that it
works, and the first quarter of commercial production only begins in August 2026.

## 8. Proof check
Volumes and revenue move the way the triggers claim. Margin does not, yet.

**What moved.** Axle and suspension volumes rose 34% year on year (IP p.6). Tractor and agri rose
about 40% (IP p.5). Exports went 3.8%, 4.0%, 4.5% across three reporting points, the right direction
though slow against an 8% target. Tipping jacks went from nothing to 220 kits. Capex of Rs 100.2
crore was actually spent and the plants are physically commissioned, which an agency verified
independently (Rating p.3). Five triggers delivered.

**What did not.** The EBITDA margin. Three years at 13.0%, 13.1%, 13.1%, and 12.2% in the June
quarter (Rating p.3; IP p.8). Every backward-integration step so far has been absorbed rather than
banked.

**The company's own caution matters.** Asked whether the 34% axle growth against single-digit
industry growth meant market share gains, Sumeet Rai declined to claim it: "this was sort of like a
spillover of the very strong Q4 of FY26... As far as market share is concerned, I can't really
comment on it right now" (Concall p.6). That is the correct answer and it removes the strongest
reading of the quarter.

**The proof gate has NOT FIRED**, and it is unusually close to a dated test. The extrusion plant
begins commercial production in August 2026; the foundry line and robotic forging land in September
2026. The September and December 2026 quarters are the first that contain them. If the margin does not
move by then, with volumes at these levels, the R2 to R3 claim fails on evidence rather than on
opinion.

## 9. Flags from the documents
- **Margin has not responded to three years of integration.** 13.0%, 13.1%, 13.1%, then 12.2%. The
  central risk on this name, and it is visible in the company's own numbers.
- **Depreciation is running ahead of the profit it funds.** Up 46.7% year on year with Rs 167 crore of
  seamless tube still to capitalise (IP p.8). Profit growth will lag EBITDA growth for several more
  quarters by arithmetic.
- **Management denies pricing power in its own words** (Concall p.10), and the rating agency
  independently describes a pass-through mechanism with a time lag (Rating p.3).
- **The milestone table on deck page 15 is unreliable.** Year labels do not match events: "2019"
  against a listing of 16 September 2024, "2023" against an extrusion plant India Ratings dates to
  February 2026, "2022" against a tipping jack the same deck launches in Q1 FY27.
- **Seamless tube timing differs by source.** Management: commission by Q4 FY27 (Concall p.9). India
  Ratings: "fully operational by FY28" (Rating p.3). A year of difference on a Rs 167 crore project.
- **Capacity utilisation is low where the new money went.** About 9,500 axles a quarter against a
  stated 7,500 a month of beam capacity; and the extrusion margin upside is explicitly conditioned on
  "above 50% utilization" (IP p.9; Concall p.9).
- **Cyclicality is the agency's only named weakness** (Rating p.2). The June quarter was helped by GST
  rationalisation, which is a one-time demand pull.
- **Tipping jacks are early.** 220 kits against 800 a month of capacity, deliberately, "prioritizing
  product validation" (IP p.10). Correct approach, negligible revenue for now.
- **No balance sheet in the corpus.** Not a company failing, a coverage gap, and it means return on
  capital cannot be stated on this card.
- **A reader artefact to check.** Deck pages 13 and 16 returned reader-generated slide descriptions
  including a reference to "KR Crosses". Not treated as company text. See the manifest.

## 10. Independent check: the credit rating
**COMPLETE.** India Ratings & Research, 1 June 2026, affirmed IND A/Stable/IND A1 on Rs 111.5 crore of
existing bank loan facilities and assigned the same to Rs 17.95 crore of additional limits, on a
standalone view (Rating p.1, p.2).

On sustainability: "The ratings reflect the agency's expectation of sustained revenue growth over
FY27-FY28, with healthy profitability and credit metrics" (Rating p.2). The agency expects revenue "to
improve significantly FY27 onwards" on the back of the tipping jack, the extrusion plant, the forging
expansion and the seamless tube shed (Rating p.3).

On leverage: credit metrics "improved significantly as a large part of its external borrowings has
been repaid through the IPO proceeds", with gross adjusted debt rising to Rs 88.6 crore at FYE26 from
Rs 66.4 crore, for the seamless tube build (Rating p.3). The agency believes "KL's deleveraged balance
sheet and healthy cash balance would support its credit profile in case of any downturn in the auto
industry" (Rating p.2).

On the transition, and this is the part that matters most: the agency expects margins to stay at
"13%-13.5% in the near term" (Rating p.3). That is an independent view that the backward integration
buys stability, not a re-rating. It is the single most useful sentence on this card, and it argues
against the R3 claim on the timeframe the deck implies. India Ratings names one weakness only: "Cyclical
nature of automobile industry" (Rating p.2).

A rating is one view, and today's filings win over it. But an agency that holds management's numbers,
expects significant revenue growth and still forecasts a flat margin is telling the operator exactly
where the argument sits.

## 11. Posture: the Transition Decision Matrix
- **Proof gate: NOT FIRED**, with a dated test two quarters away. Step 8.
- **Ugliness: ARTIFACT-OF-CLIMB**, and this is the clearest such case in the run. The June quarter's
  soft margin and lagging profit growth are caused by depreciation on plant that has not yet produced
  commercially. Management says so (IP p.5), and the agency corroborates it as "expansion-related
  drag" (Rating p.3). Three sources agree on the cause. Flat FY25 revenue was an industry pause, not
  decay.
- **Recognition gap: OPEN, narrowly.** Rs 1,455 crore of market value against Rs 55.2 crore of FY26
  profit is about 26 times trailing earnings, and about 2.2 times FY26 revenue. That is not a distressed
  price and it is not a franchise price either. The market appears to be paying for an R2 converter
  having a good cycle, not for an R3 spec'd supplier. If the extrusion and seamless routes do move the
  margin, the gap is real. If the agency is right at 13% to 13.5%, it is already closed.

**Posture: RESEARCH / WATCH.** Proof not fired, ugliness an artifact, gap open. Watch the gate.

## 12. Verdict card

**PROCEED to `/step1`.** The strongest of the seven names read in this run.

Not because the numbers are good today. The margin has been flat for three years and the June quarter
was below it. Kross earns the deep run because it is the one name here where the transition question is
**specific, physical and dated**. An extruded single-piece axle beam either costs less and performs
better than a welded one, or it does not. Commercial production starts in August 2026. A foundry line
and a robotic forging cell land in September 2026. A Rs 167 crore seamless tube plant that makes the
company's own raw material commissions within eighteen months. Each has a date, a rupee figure and a
physical asset, and an independent agency has verified that Rs 100.2 crore was actually spent.

The corpus supports a deep run in a way the other six do not: an annual report for FY25 and FY26 that
Bull AI holds and this shallow read did not open, quarterly transcripts going back two years, a full
rating rationale, and a prospectus. A `/step1` here has material to work with.

State the bear case at the same bar. Three years of backward integration produced no margin gain.
Management denies pricing power in its own words. India Ratings, which has the management numbers,
forecasts 13% to 13.5%, meaning the outside professional view is that this remains an R2 converter.
Depreciation will suppress reported profit for several more quarters. And the whole thing rides an
M&HCV cycle that the agency names as the single weakness. At about 26 times trailing earnings, a
converter having a good cycle is roughly what the price says.

**Load-bearing facts a deep run must verify first:**
1. Return on capital employed, FY24 to FY26, and the incremental return on the Rs 100.2 crore. The
   balance sheet is not in this corpus and it is the number the thesis turns on.
2. Segment margin: is the trailer axle and suspension line genuinely higher-margin than components,
   and by how much? The deck splits revenue and not profit.
3. The extrusion economics in rupees: cost per beam extruded against fabricated, and the price the
   customer pays for each. The claim of lower material cost and better tyre life must be a number.
4. Customer concentration. Tata Motors and Ashok Leyland are named; their share of revenue is not.
5. Cash conversion: operating cash flow against EBITDA across FY24 to FY26, and working capital days
   in a business feeding fabricators and dealers.
6. Post-IPO promoter holding for the group, and any encumbrance beyond Anita Rai's nil.
7. Whether the seamless tube plant commissions in Q4 FY27 as management says or FY28 as the agency
   says, and what the Rs 167 crore earns at each date.

**What would change the view.** To a strong PROCEED: a September or December 2026 quarter with EBITDA
margin above 14% on comparable volumes, with management attributing it to extrusion mix rather than to
price. To WATCH: two more quarters at 12% to 13% with the extrusion line commercial, which would
confirm the agency's read and demote the thesis to a cycle trade. To PASS: an M&HCV downturn arriving
before the seamless tube plant earns, with debt drawn and depreciation running.

**This is not a position.** No valuation, no target price, no entry zone. The shallow read decides only
that a full `/step1` is worth running on this name.
