## PART 1: CLAIM-BY-CLAIM VERIFICATION

### Q1. Is a 23-25% EBITDA margin achievable and durable in greige/grey-fabric weaving, or does it depend on a temporary raw-material cost advantage?

**Verdict: UNVERIFIABLE** (on the specific magnitude; the durability half is
addressed via Q2-Q4 below)

**Peer evidence:** No peer, including SANGAM (the only integrated
yarn-PLUS-fabric weaver in the set), discloses a fabric/weaving-only EBITDA
margin. SANGAM gives a revenue mix by product line (PV Yarn 20%, Cotton Yarn
25%, Denim Fabric 31%, "Woven Fabric with processing" 22%, Garment 2% —
SANGAMIND-Investor_Presentation_Q1FY27, p.10) and a loom count (304 weaving
looms plus 298 denim looms — SANGAMIND-Investor_Presentation_Q4FY26, p.17)
but never breaks out a segment or per-loom margin. Its BLENDED margin
(yarn+fabric+garment+denim combined) ran 7.3%-12.9% across the twelve
quarters covered (SANGAMIND Q4FY26 call, "close to 10%" for Q2FY26, p.9;
SANGAMIND-Investor_Presentation_Q1FY27 p.4 quarterly table), with a stated
FY29 aspiration of ">13%" blended margin and ROCE "inching towards 20%"
(SANGAMIND-Concall_Apr_2026, p.12). FILATEX's FY26 EBITDA margin was 8.33%
(Rs346.50cr/Rs4160.51cr — FILATEX-Concall_May_2026, p.4), its best single
segment being the still-ramping Ecosis recycling subsidiary at a guided
"minimum 30%" (FILATEX-Concall_Aug_2026, p.12-13; FILATEX-Concall_May_2026,
p.12). SANATHAN's standalone margin, even after the Nov-2025 policy shock
fully reversed, topped out at 11.67% in Q1FY27 (SANATHAN-Concall_Aug_2026,
p.6).

**Peers silent:** No peer was asked, or volunteered, an opinion on Borana's
specific margin claim; none was expected to be.

**Net read:** The claim cannot be directly verified or contradicted because
no comparable weaving-only margin exists anywhere in the corpus. But the
peer ceiling is informative on its own: all three peers, operating in the
same input-cost environment Borana cites as its own margin driver (see Q2-Q3),
never get within half of Borana's 23-25% band, even at their best quarters.
That does not prove Borana's number is wrong — a pure fabric weaver on
bought yarn is a genuinely different economic unit from an integrated
polymerizer, which is exactly stage 4's ruling — but it means the
value-chain-step explanation stays an assumption, not something this peer
set can confirm, because none of the three peers discloses the one number
(a true fabric-only or per-loom margin) that would test it directly.

### Q2. What did POY prices and polyester-chain spreads (PTA-MEG-POY) do, quarter by quarter, across FY25 and FY26?

**Verdict: VERIFIED** (direction and rough magnitude, two independent peers)

**Peer evidence:**
- FILATEX: per-kg profitability "around INR9 a Kg" in Q3FY26, dropping
  "significantly" in Q4FY26; management states "if I take March
  independently... there would be hardly any EBITDA" and "today also it is
  almost EBITDA neutral" (FILATEX-Concall_May_2026, p.9). By Q1FY27, "Imports
  have also declined from China, and now the margins have become quite
  reasonably good" (FILATEX-Concall_Aug_2026, p.13).
- SANATHAN: FDY prices "dropped by almost about 10%, 12%" immediately after
  the Nov-2025 QCO removal, with "almost 50% of that we have recovered back"
  by the Feb-2026 call; DTY prices were unaffected because "there was no QCO
  even before that" on DTY (SANATHAN-Concall_Feb_2026, p.9). Standalone
  EBITDA margin fell to 7.3% in Q3FY26 from a 9M run-rate of 8.6%
  (SANATHAN-Concall_Feb_2026, p.4), then recovered to 11.0% in Q4FY26 and
  11.67% in Q1FY27 (SANATHAN-Concall_May_2026, p.6-7; SANATHAN-Concall_Aug_2026,
  p.6).
- Separately, both FILATEX and SANATHAN describe a second, unrelated shock —
  a 40-45% crude-linked spike in petrochemical feedstock costs from the
  Iran-Israel-US conflict disrupting the Strait of Hormuz in Q4FY26/Q1FY27 —
  compressing yarn and fabric-segment margins across the industry
  (FILATEX-Concall_May_2026, p.4-5; FILATEX-Concall_Aug_2026, p.3-4).

**Peers silent:** None of the required peers (FILATEX, SANATHAN) is silent
on this; both give quantified, quarter-anchored figures.

**Net read:** The direction and rough magnitude are corroborated by two
independent producers: a temporary, ~10-12% price compression on FDY/POY-type
products following the Nov-2025 QCO/BIS event, reversing about half by
Feb-2026 and largely normalising by mid-2026. Two distinct drivers are
tangled in FY26 — the QCO/import event (Nov-2025) and the crude/geopolitical
spike (starting ~March-April 2026) — and peers are careful to separate them;
Borana's own account (see Q3) does not.

### Q3. What did the December 2025 removal of the BIS-certification import restriction on Chinese polyester textured/POY yarn do to imported yarn availability and price?

**Verdict: CONTRADICTED** (on the date; corroborated on substance)

**Peer evidence:** Borana's CFO, on the Q3FY26 call (27-Jan-2026), states
twice that the restriction "was now removed in December"
(Concall_Q3FY26_Jan2026_Transcript, p.5-6). Three independent peer sources
place the same event in NOVEMBER 2025, not December:
- FILATEX's Chairman, on the Feb-2026 call: "Q4 will not be as good as Q3
  because government had lifted BIS on November 12 or 13... after that,
  there is a flood of Chinese products coming to India"
  (FILATEX-Concall_Feb_2026, p.6).
- SANATHAN management, on the same-week Feb-2026 call: "the sudden removal of
  the BIS QCO requirements in November, which created temporary margin
  pressure for the industry" (SANATHAN-Concall_Feb_2026, p.3).
- A CRISIL analyst on FILATEX's Aug-2026 call frames it as settled fact:
  "since the revocation of QCO in November have we seen any impact on our
  business for Q4?" (FILATEX-Concall_Aug_2026, p.9-10).

On substance, both peers confirm the mechanism Borana describes: the
restriction was a BIS "Quality Control Order" (QCO) requiring BIS
certification to import certain polyester yarns from China; its removal
triggered "a flood of Chinese products" and materially cheaper Chinese POY,
FDY and PTA (FILATEX-Concall_Feb_2026, p.6; FILATEX-Concall_Feb_2026, p.4:
"withdrawal of QCOs on yarns and PTA has eased supply side constraints...
access to competitively priced imports... from China").

**Peers silent:** none of the required peers.

**Net read:** The mechanism is real, industry-wide, and independently
dated by two companies (plus an analyst) to November 2025 — a
multiply-corroborated, one-month discrepancy against Borana's own
"December" account of the single most load-bearing fact behind its FY26
margin story. The direction and magnitude of the import/price effect are
otherwise well corroborated (see Q2).

### Q4. How does the anti-dumping duty regime on imported polyester textured yarn/PTY reconcile with a simultaneous BIS-restriction removal?

**Verdict: PARTIALLY VERIFIED** (peers resolve the "tension" — it is not a
contradiction, it is two different, separately-timed policy instruments)

**Peer evidence:** Borana's CFO, on the same call, appears to treat the two
as the same thing: "No. Previously, there is an anti-dumping duty. There is
not actually an anti-dumping duty. They have quoted as a BIS..."
(Concall_Q3FY26_Jan2026_Transcript, p.6). Peer evidence shows they are
distinct:
- FILATEX (Feb-2026): "there is one antidumping investigation going on, that
  is on textured yarn... there is an investigation going on in the textured
  yarn in China. I don't know what the outcome will be"
  (FILATEX-Concall_Feb_2026, p.9) — i.e. still an open DGTR investigation,
  not a duty in force, as of Feb-2026.
- SANATHAN (Feb-2026), asked directly about "DGTR's initiation on
  anti-dumping probe around PTY imports from China": "it will only help us if
  it comes through... there are still investigations on and we are yet to
  see the result of that" (SANATHAN-Concall_Feb_2026, p.9) — confirming the
  ADD process on PTY is a live, unresolved, SEPARATE track from the already-
  completed QCO/BIS removal.
- A related, also-separate anti-dumping proposal on MEG (a raw material, not
  a finished yarn) was recommended by DGTR but ultimately NOT accepted by
  the Ministry of Finance (FILATEX-Concall_Nov_2025, p.4, 9-10;
  FILATEX-Concall_Feb_2026, p.4: "non-acceptance by the Ministry of Finance
  of the DGTR recommendation on antidumping duty on MEG").

**Peers silent:** SANGAMIND does not discuss anti-dumping duty, QCO or BIS
in any of its four transcripts (see Part 3).

**Net read:** Peer evidence resolves the apparent tension Borana's call
raises: the BIS/QCO certification requirement (a technical import-quality
gate, removed Nov-2025) and the anti-dumping duty on PTY (a trade-remedy
process, still under DGTR investigation as of Feb-2026, not yet imposed) are
two different regulatory instruments running on different, not simultaneous,
tracks. Borana's CFO conflating them ("there is not actually an anti-dumping
duty... they have quoted as a BIS") is imprecise language for a real
distinction that two peers draw explicitly and correctly.

### Q5. What is a realistic revenue-per-rupee-of-capex ratio for new loom/texturising capacity, against Borana's stated 2x-3x?

**Verdict: CONTRADICTED**

**Peer evidence:** Neither SANGAMIND nor SANATHAN gives a capex-to-revenue
ratio specifically for water-jet looms or texturising machines. The closest
available peer-anchored benchmark is SANATHAN's newest capacity decision
(Aug-2026 call): a Rs400cr investment in 72,500 cotton-yarn spindles at its
Madhya Pradesh site, guided at "an asset turn of about 0.8, 0.85," i.e.
Rs350-375cr of incremental revenue on Rs400cr of capex
(SANATHAN-Concall_Aug_2026, p.9) — an asset turn of roughly 0.9x, not 2x-3x.
SANGAMIND gives only a blanket maintenance-capex figure (Rs50-70cr/year on
its existing gross block — SANGAMIND-Concall_Apr_2026, p.10) with no
revenue-per-rupee figure attached to any specific machine class.

**Peers silent:** Neither peer gives a figure for the specific asset class
(water-jet looms, texturising machines) Borana's claim is about; the
SANATHAN spindle figure is the nearest available proxy from a different
machine class (cotton spinning, not weaving/texturising).

**Net read:** With the caveat that the comparison is cross-machine-class
(spindles vs. looms/texturisers), the only quantified peer capex-efficiency
benchmark in this corpus runs at roughly 1x asset turn, materially below
Borana's claimed 2x-3x. This is the single most consequential quantitative
contradiction in the peer set for the forward capacity-doubling thesis,
because it is a testable, near-term input to any revenue model built on
Borana's Rs350-400cr plan.

### Q8. What share of production does a typical integrated weaver run as value-added, against Borana's stated 20-25%?

**Verdict: PARTIALLY VERIFIED** (direction confirmed; magnitude not
comparable — different definitions)

**Peer evidence:** SANGAMIND reports "53% of our product mix comprises of
value-added products" as of Q4FY24, with a stated ambition to reach 60%
(SANGAMIND-Concall_May_2024, p.3; SANGAMIND-Concall_Aug_2024, p.9), where
"value-added" is defined as the shift of yarn output into fabric and
garment production, not a coated/technical-use category. SANATHAN reports
"more than 40%" value addition at its mature Silvassa facility, versus
"about 10%" at its newly ramping Punjab facility, with "value-added" there
defined as a mix of higher-spec yarn types (FDY/DTY-type products) rather
than fabric downstream (SANATHAN-Concall_May_2026, p.10).

**Peers silent:** neither required peer gives a figure using Borana's
specific "coated/technical-use fabric or yarn" definition.

**Net read:** Both required peers confirm value-added mix is a real,
board-tracked KPI industry-wide, and both report materially HIGHER
value-added shares (40-60%) than Borana's claimed 20-25%. But the
definitions do not match — SANGAM's figure is a yarn-to-fabric/garment
migration metric, SANATHAN's is a yarn-grade mix metric, and neither is
"coated or technical-use" as Borana's own claim specifies. The direction
(value-add is real and rising) is corroborated; the magnitude comparison is
not meaningful across definitions.

## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

**2A Demand environment.** Across all three peers and five quarters
(Nov-2025 through Aug-2026), the consistent language is "cautious,"
"need-based," "selective," "shorter booking cycles," "lean inventories"
(FILATEX-Concall_Aug_2026, p.4) rather than any demand boom. SANGAM alone
uses "healthy demand" (Nov-2025, pre-shock quarter). No peer describes the
kind of unambiguous domestic MMF demand strength that would independently
corroborate a capacity-doubling decision; this neither confirms nor denies
Borana's framing, since Borana's own language ("structurally strong,"
unquantified) is similarly non-specific.

**2B Pricing and input costs.** Two distinct, sequential shocks run through
FY26 for the yarn producers: (i) the Nov-2025 BIS/QCO removal, a one-time
step-change that pressured FDY/POY selling prices ~10-12% before partially
reversing (Q2 above), and (ii) a March-April 2026 crude/geopolitical spike
(Iran-Israel-US conflict, Strait of Hormuz disruption) that pushed
petrochemical feedstock costs up 40-45% and briefly drove FILATEX to
"EBITDA neutral" in March 2026 (FILATEX-Concall_May_2026, p.4-5, 9). Neither
of these appears in the Borana corpus's Q3FY26 call (27-Jan-2026, which
predates the crude shock) or in the FY2026 Annual Report MD&A (per B05's
finding that the margin driver vanished from the AR). The margin narrative
Borana gave live is therefore only half the FY26 industry picture; the
second, larger shock (crude/geopolitics) that hit its input-supplying peers
hardest is absent from Borana's own communications entirely.

**2C Capex cycle.** All three peers are expanding, not holding or cutting:
FILATEX's Rs690cr brownfield PFY/FDY/DTY expansion plus the Ecosis recycling
subsidiary; SANATHAN's Punjab polymerization scale-up to 950 TPD plus a new
Rs400cr cotton-yarn spindle line at MP; SANGAM's renewable-energy buildout
(Rs150-160cr) plus a new garmenting vertical (~Rs300cr EBITDA potential at
full utilisation). This is an industry-wide, moderate investment cycle — but
none of the three peers describes anything close to DOUBLING its core
capacity on Borana's ~2-year timeline. Borana's Rs350-400cr, 1,000-to-2,000-
loom plan by March-2028 is proportionally the most aggressive expansion in
the set, not simply riding a common capacity race.

**2D Competitive mentions.** None. "Borana" does not appear in any of the
twelve peer transcripts or six peer presentations.

**2E Risks peers discuss that Borana does not.**
- The crude/geopolitical (Iran-Israel-US, Strait of Hormuz) shock and its
  40-45% feedstock cost spike (FILATEX-Concall_May_2026, p.4-5) — absent from
  Borana's own communications, which predate it.
- The MEG anti-dumping duty proposal, ultimately not accepted by the Finance
  Ministry but a live cost overhang through FY26 for POY/yarn producers
  (FILATEX-Concall_Nov_2025, p.4; FILATEX-Concall_Feb_2026, p.4) — a risk to
  Borana's own input costs that its one call never raises.
- Migrant-labour shortages disrupting production, linked in one instance to
  an LPG cylinder price spike (FILATEX-Concall_May_2026, p.7-8).
- SANATHAN's own account that the QCO transition was not unambiguously
  positive even for a beneficiary: "there was a pressure on everything that
  we held back on the inventory and so we lost something there"
  (SANATHAN-Concall_Feb_2026, p.6) — an inventory/write-down risk dimension
  during the transition that Borana's account frames as a simple, one-sided
  margin gain.
- Currency/hedging cost on USD-denominated PTA/MEG imports, running
  "INR17.5 crores to INR20 crores every year in the hedging cost" for
  FILATEX at its scale (FILATEX-Concall_May_2026, p.7) — a structural cost
  line with no analogue discussed in the Borana corpus.

## PART 4: TRIANGULATION SUMMARY

- Claims verified: 1 of 8 (Q2)
- Claims partially verified: 2 of 8 (Q4, Q8)
- Claims contradicted: 2 of 8 (Q3, Q5) — priority items for synthesis
- Claims unverifiable: 3 of 8 (Q1, Q6, Q7)

**Most consequential contradiction:** The BIS/QCO timeline. Borana's CFO
told the one available call that the restriction was "removed in December"
and conflated it with anti-dumping duty. Two independent peers (FILATEX,
SANATHAN) and a third party (a CRISIL analyst) date the same event to
November 2025 and treat the QCO removal and the still-open PTY anti-dumping
investigation as clearly separate. The underlying mechanism and its
direction are real; the specific factual account Borana gave of its own
central FY26 margin driver is not precisely right, on the one occasion it
was explained at all.

**Strongest independent confirmation:** The POY/PTA-MEG price-compression
event itself. Two independent yarn producers, unprompted by Borana's
narrative and with no reason to support it, describe the same November-2025
policy shock, the same rough magnitude (10-12% price compression, partially
reversing), and the same directional benefit flowing to buyers of cheap
Chinese yarn (which is Borana's position in the value chain) at the expense
of sellers (which is theirs).

**Overall:** The peer set complicates Borana's narrative rather than
cleanly supporting or undercutting it. The core policy event is real,
industry-wide, and independently dated within a month of Borana's own
account — this is not a fabricated story. But every place this stage could
test a specific number, the number either does not hold up (the December
date, the 2x-3x capex ratio) or cannot be tested at all because no peer
discloses the one figure that would settle it (a fabric-only or per-loom
margin). Symmetrically: nothing in the peer set proves Borana's 23-25%
margin band is wrong, since the value-chain-step explanation is untestable
with this evidence, not falsified by it — but nothing in the peer set
supports the specific magnitude either, and the peer margin ceiling (never
above ~13% even for the integrated fabric weaver) is a real plausibility
concern that the operator should weigh.

## PART 5: CROSS-PEER HYPOTHESIS

All three peers, hit by the same November-2025 China-import shock and (for
FILATEX and SANATHAN) the same March-2026 crude/geopolitical shock, show a
margin trough followed by recovery on a roughly TWO-QUARTER cadence:
SANATHAN standalone margin fell from an 8.6% 9M run-rate to 7.3% in the
shock quarter, then recovered to 11.0% and 11.67% over the following two
quarters; FILATEX went "EBITDA neutral" in March 2026 and was back to
"reasonably good" margins by Q1FY27, also roughly two quarters later. No
single peer states this cadence explicitly — each describes only its own
trough-and-recovery.

The testable hypothesis this suggests: mid-size Indian polyester/synthetic-
textile producers appear to need about two quarters to absorb a raw-material
or policy shock through inventory turnover, pricing resets and product-mix
shifts. If that ~2-quarter absorption cadence is a genuine sector norm, then
Borana's account — a shock landing in the SAME quarter it is reported as an
immediate, one-directional margin benefit (Q3-to-Q4 FY26, the quarter
immediately following the event) — is unusually fast relative to the
sector's own observed absorption speed on the seller side. That does not
make Borana's account wrong (a buyer of cheap yarn plausibly benefits faster
than a seller repricing its own output), but it is a specific, checkable
asymmetry: the next 1-2 quarters should show whether Borana's margin gain
proves as durable as the two-quarter-cadence pattern suggests it should, or
reverts the way a genuinely temporary cost windfall would.
