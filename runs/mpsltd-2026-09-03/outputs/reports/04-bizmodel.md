## SECTION 1: THE BUSINESS MODEL IN PLAIN ENGLISH

### 1A. One-line description
MPS is an "AI-first knowledge company" that gets paid by publishers, universities, ed-tech firms, corporations and (since Feb-2026) hospitals to turn raw research, course, and training content into finished, trusted, deliverable material, using its own content-engineering teams, its own software platforms, and increasingly its own AI tools (AR p.4-5, p.14).

### 1B. Money flow chain, by revenue stream

**Research Solutions (60% of FY26 revenue, INR 463.5 Cr / 46,351.09 lacs) (AR p.15, p.28, Note 43(i) p.305)**
[Input] Author manuscripts, peer-review workflows, and a publisher's own journal/book content →
[What MPS does] Editorial processing, peer review management, research-integrity screening (AI-assisted via the "Rubriq" editing engine, built on 1mn+ expert edits), typesetting/production, and hosting on the MPS-owned HighWire platform (AR p.4, p.14, p.30) →
[What it delivers] Published journal articles, scholarly books, and a publisher-neutral hosting/analytics platform →
[Who pays] Academic and STM (scientific/technical/medical) publishers, university presses, and learned societies (AR p.14, p.28) →
[How they pay] Fee-for-service on manuscripts/titles processed (percentage-of-completion for fixed-price contracts) plus recurring platform/hosting fees; some multi-year volume commitments (AR p.28: "a major multi-year commitment for 900 titles"; Note 2.9 p.254, revenue recognition policy).

**Education Solutions (27% of FY26 revenue, INR 208.9 Cr / 20,890.03 lacs) (AR p.15, p.28, Note 43(i) p.305)**
[Input] Raw course syllabi, textbook manuscripts, ed-tech product requirements, and (from Feb-2026) clinical/medical reference content via the acquired Unbound Medicine business →
[What MPS does] Content development, multi-platform digital conversion, learning-platform engineering (e.g. THINK365 subscription management SaaS), and clinical-content delivery apps (AR p.16, p.31) →
[What it delivers] Digital courseware, K-12/Higher-Ed content, ed-tech platform infrastructure, and (new) point-of-care clinical decision-support content/apps for nurses and clinicians →
[Who pays] Ed-tech companies, academic institutions, learning-content publishers, and now hospitals/healthcare institutions and individual clinician subscribers (AR p.16, p.25, Note 40(a) p.301) →
[How they pay] Project/license fees for content development plus, increasingly, recurring subscription revenue from Unbound Medicine ("recurring, high-renewal healthcare revenue" — Inv. Pres. slide 4, slide 8).

**Corporate Learning (13% of FY26 revenue, INR 96.0 Cr / 9,595.26 lacs) (AR p.15, p.28, Note 43(i) p.305)**
[Input] A corporate or government client's workforce-training need →
[What MPS does] Custom learning-content design, simulation and AR/VR-based immersive training, and AI/technology-led delivery, now unified under one global brand "Liberate Global" (AR p.16, p.28-29) →
[What it delivers] Bespoke corporate training programs and performance-support tools →
[Who pays] Enterprises and government agencies (AR p.16) →
[How they pay] Project/consulting fees, order-book/engagement based, generally lower revenue visibility than the other two segments (AR p.28-29 describes FY26 as "The Turn" following a multi-year restructuring).

### 1E. The chai-stall-uncle version
Think of MPS as the printing-and-proofreading shop that scientific journals, textbook makers, and companies have used for decades to turn messy drafts into finished, trustworthy material. Over 50 years that shop didn't just get better scissors, it bought the printing press too (its own software platforms) and hired AI apprentices trained specifically on its own trade, not general-purpose helpers. Because the shop knows a customer's exact rules (a journal's specific editorial standards, a hospital's clinical protocols), customers keep coming back for 20 years instead of shopping around. Three counters run in that shop: research papers (the biggest and steadiest counter), education content (the fastest-growing, now serving hospitals too), and corporate training (the smallest, most bespoke counter). The tailor's biggest cost is still the tailors' wages, not the cloth.

### 2D. Industry lifecycle stage
- Research Solutions: sits inside a structurally growing but mature scholarly-publishing industry (global research output ~3.5mn science/engineering articles in 2024, AR p.27); MPS's specific niche (research-integrity verification, AI-assisted editorial) is an emerging growth pocket inside that mature industry, driven by a stated ">14,000 retraction notices issued in 2023" integrity crisis (AR p.27).
- Education Solutions: growth industry — global education solutions market cited at ~USD 416bn (2025) growing to ~USD 878bn by 2031 (AR p.27); MPS just entered the healthcare-knowledge adjacency via Unbound Medicine (Feb-2026), an early-stage sub-vertical for the company.
- Corporate Learning: smaller, project-cyclical sub-market (corporate e-learning cited at ~USD 353bn in 2025, growing to ~USD 874bn by 2030, AR p.27); MPS's own segment is described as having just completed a multi-year restructuring ("The Turn," AR p.28-29), i.e. company-specific recovery phase rather than pure market growth.
- Company overall: mid-stage of a consolidation strategy in a fragmented industry (11 acquisitions in 13 years, AR p.15, p.18), now layering AI-embedded platforms atop that consolidated base.

### 3D. Unit economics — the physics of the business
A precise "per unit" revenue/cost figure (e.g., revenue per manuscript, revenue per course module) is NOT FOUND, check investor presentation or concall; MPS does not disclose per-transaction pricing. The best available proxy, given the people-based delivery model, is **revenue and cost per employee**:

- Define one unit: one billable employee (FTE) in the delivery organisation.
- Revenue per employee (proxy): FY26 consolidated revenue INR 768.37 Cr / "3,200+" employees (AR p.3) ≈ INR 0.24 Cr (~INR 24 lacs) per employee per year (computed; AR does not disclose this ratio directly).
- Cost per employee (proxy): FY26 consolidated employee benefits expense INR 319.95 Cr / "3,200+" employees ≈ INR 0.10 Cr (~INR 10 lacs) per employee per year (Note 36, p.297; computed).
- Volume drivers: number of billable employees/delivery capacity, manuscripts/titles/courses processed, and — increasingly — AI throughput that lets the same headcount handle more volume (AR p.3: "200+ AI engineers," 1mn+ manuscripts processed).
- Price drivers: mix shift toward higher-value, recurring platform/subscription work (e.g., Unbound Medicine, THINK365) versus lower-value fee-for-service production; multi-year volume commitments with STAR accounts.
- Cost drivers: wage inflation in Tier-2 Indian delivery hubs; new labour-code provisioning (a one-time step-up absorbed in FY26, AR p.29); integration/restructuring costs from serial acquisitions.
- Incremental margin / operating leverage: explicit management evidence of leverage — Q1 FY27 revenue grew 20.38% YoY while headcount grew only 2.73%, and EBITDA grew 53.03% YoY (Inv. Pres. slide 5); FY26 full-year: EBITDA margin expanded 170bps on 5.71% reported (≈13.3% underlying ex-M&A) revenue growth (AR p.29). This confirms a genuine operating-leverage/AI-productivity story, not just acquisition arithmetic.
