### 2.1 Receivable Under Service Concession Arrangements (RUSCA) — a major balance sheet item Pass 1 never located

Consolidated Note 7 "Receivable Under Service Concession Arrangements"
(sheet 116): Non-Current Rs 14,299.58 million (FY26) vs Rs 10,235.89
million (FY25); Current Rs 279.33m/143.92m; Total Rs 14,578.90 million
(FY26) vs Rs 10,379.80 million (FY25), +40.5% YoY. Disclosed as
"Receivable Under Service Concession Arrangements with NHAI."

Pass 1 only speculated this note might exist, as a hypothesis for the
loans-to-subsidiaries gap (Section 6: "or within Receivable under Service
Concession Arrangements"); it never located, quantified, or flagged the
note itself. 🔴 Red Flag by omission in Pass 1, now corrected: this is the
group's financial-asset receivable for guaranteed HAM annuity income from
NHAI (the "financial asset model" of service concession accounting, used
because HAM annuities are largely fixed/traffic-independent). Standard
accounting treatment for a HAM portfolio, not inherently adverse — but its
SCALE is material and its growth rate compounds the cash-conversion
concern named in the run brief. Combined with Contract Assets (Rs
14,132.38m, +61.8% YoY, Pass 1 finding #1), total non-cash
receivable-type financial assets = Rs 28,711.28 million (FY26) vs Rs
20,613.23 million (FY25), +39.3% YoY — equal to approximately 71.4% of
FY26 consolidated revenue (Rs 40,224.01 million). This is a materially
larger "cash yet to be collected" picture than Pass 1's Contract-Assets-
only framing conveyed.

### 2.2 Assets and liabilities classified as held for sale — first hard evidence of an active divestment

Consolidated Note 19(a) and 19(b) (sheet 117): "Assets classified as held
for sale — Assets associated with investment: Rs 5,431.08 million (FY26)
vs Nil (FY25)." "Liabilities classified as held for sale — Liabilities
associated with investment: Rs 3,302.65 million (FY26) vs Nil (FY25)."
Both lines are new this year; net value approximately Rs 2,128.43 million.

🟡 Watch, new finding, potentially important and constructive: Pass 1 did
not extract this note at all. Under Ind AS 105, held-for-sale
classification requires the sale to be highly probable within 12 months —
this is the first hard accounting evidence that a specific investment
(almost certainly a HAM SPV, given the run context's "stated
capital-recycling strategy") is in an active, near-term divestment
process. No note anywhere in the document reviewed this pass identifies
WHICH investment, the counterparty, expected consideration, or expected
gain/loss. NOT FOUND IN DOCUMENT: entity name and transaction narrative.
This is exactly the kind of disclosure a capital-recycling thesis should
be able to point to, but as filed it is a bare, unexplained balance-sheet
reclassification — a named verification target for the live-web
verification stage, and a direct question for management.

### 2.3 Note 18 "Other Current Assets" contradicts Note 54's IPO-utilisation statement

Consolidated Note 18 (sheet 117): "(D) Share issue expenses recoverable
(Refer note 54) — Rs 60.49 million (FY26) vs Rs 309.96 million (FY25)."
This is a real, non-zero, quoted asset balance as at 31 March 2026.

Pass 1 quoted Note 54 (sheet 118, adjacent) verbatim: the monitoring
agency confirms "there are no outstanding amounts recoverable or
unadjusted balances remaining as at 31st March, 2026," and Pass 1 rated
IPO utilisation 🟢 Clean on that basis. Note 18, a few pages away in the
same consolidated financial statements, states a live Rs 60.49 million
recoverable balance tied explicitly to Note 54 by cross-reference. 🟡
Watch, new finding, a genuine cross-note inconsistency: either Note 18's
balance is not truly "outstanding" in the sense Note 54 means (e.g., it
nets against a payable, or is pending administrative settlement rather
than economically recoverable), or Note 54's "fully accounted for"
language overstates the position. Not explained by any note read this
pass. Downgrades Pass 1's 🟢 Clean rating on this specific IPO-utilisation
line to Watch; does not change the broader "no diversion" conclusion.

### 2.4 Collateral coverage on bank guarantees is falling as the guarantee book grows

Consolidated Note 6 "Non-Current Financial Assets: Others" (sheet 116):
"(A) Deposits with remaining maturity more than 12 months" Rs 555.55
million (FY26) vs Rs 259.15 million (FY25); footnote: "Above deposits
includes lien marked deposit of INR 1,767.40 millions as on March 31,
2026 against bank guarantees given for the projects (March 31, 2025: INR
2,253.93 millions)."

🟡 Watch, new finding, supports and sharpens Pass 1 finding #2: the fixed-
deposit collateral specifically backing the guarantee book FELL 21.6%
(Rs 2,253.93m to Rs 1,767.40m) in the same year gross bank guarantees
issued grew sharply (standalone Rs 8,403.35m to Rs 10,280.34m, +22.3% per
Pass 1). A growing share of the guarantee book is running on lower cash
margin coverage even as the book itself expands — worth naming alongside
the contingent-liability red flag rather than as a standalone item.
