## SECTION 1 — ONE-LINE THESIS

CleanMax is a levered annuity builder. It holds 5.7 GW of contracted capacity on 23-year agreements with investment-grade corporates [MGMT]. It grows operating EBITDA from Rs 1,132 Cr in FY26 [FILED] to roughly Rs 4,100 Cr in FY30 [MGMT/INFERENCE] as the fleet reaches about 7,900 MW [MGMT]. If the market keeps paying 12 to 14 times forward EBITDA, inside the current peer band of 11 to 15 [SECONDARY], the equity compounds at roughly 22 to 42 percent a year to an FY29 exit after dilution [INFERENCE].

The break condition is the multiple, not the operations. The fleet can commission on plan and the thesis still fails if the forward multiple compresses to a mature-yield 8 to 10 times. The measurable trigger is the peer-group forward EV/EBITDA multiple. It is observable every trading day. When it falls one full turn and holds, the thesis has lost about a quarter of its equity value before any operating miss. The per-share values and the return math sit in Section 5.

## SECTION 3 — BULL CASE

The contracted annuity is real, not narrated. CleanMax holds 5.7 GW of capacity on power-purchase agreements that run about 23 years [MGMT]. The counterparties are the strongest set in Indian infrastructure: investment-grade corporates buying power to run their own operations [MGMT]. This is not merchant power exposed to spot prices. It is a contracted cash annuity with a long tail.

The operating fleet works. The state-grid portion runs at 99.35 percent uptime [MGMT]. Young assets are seasoning: Rs 5,339.21 Cr of capital work in progress at FY26 close converts into earning plant as projects commission [FILED, AR Note 3]. The crossover into operating profit has happened once. Operating PBT before other income printed +51.6 Cr in Q1 FY27 [FILED] after FY26 sat below the line.

The cost of debt is falling. CleanMax prepaid Rs 599 Cr of 11.5 percent NCDs on 2-Apr-2026, saving Rs 68.89 Cr a year from FY27 [FILED]. That saving drops straight to pre-tax profit.

Demand has a structural tailwind. Data and AI load is 42 percent of the contracted book [MGMT]. Indian data-centre capacity is growing about 30 percent a year with more than 126 billion dollars committed [SECONDARY]. Newer contracts price at Rs 4.00 per unit against Rs 3.57 on already-commissioned capacity [MGMT]. Pricing is rising as the fleet grows.

The business is de-risking on several axes at once. Customer concentration is falling [MGMT]. The accounting is conservative: interest capitalisation on the filed average CWIP runs 5.1 percent interest-only against an 8.4 percent cost of debt [FILED, extraction 02 D1]. The board is credible and the audit is clean and unmodified for two years [FILED]. Informed capital agrees with the direction: Temasek and Bain entered at Rs 1,053 in filed transactions [FILED]. Koppal's transmission line finishes ahead of the plant, which removes the most common evacuation risk for that block [MGMT].

The bull case in one sentence: a contracted 23-year annuity, funded by the best corporate counterparties in the country, is doubling its fleet into a data-centre demand wave while its cost of debt falls and its realised price rises.

### Reconciliation (one paragraph)

The two methods disagree because they price different things. EV/EBITDA prices what the market pays for a growing contracted fleet. The SOTP FCFF prices what the cash the assets throw off is worth. They diverge because the assets earn below their cost of capital: post-tax return on invested capital near 3.4 percent now, about 5.4 percent at the guided FY28 state, against a cost of capital near 10 percent [FILED/MGMT]. EV/IC of 0.95x says the same thing: the market pays about book for invested capital that does not yet clear its hurdle. The operator has ruled that the market method governs and the SOTP is the recorded dissent. The two are not averaged. The dissent is not deleted: it is the reason the thesis carries a hard break condition on the multiple.

The framework does not authorise entry above the Role 1 zone, and every valuation method places fair value below the price. If the operator overrides to enter on the growth thesis, the rules cap a sub-hurdle, multiple-dependent, leveraged name at a **starter position only**, sized to survive a one-turn de-rating. ## SECTION 9 — PORTFOLIO CONTEXT

The operator's portfolio is concentrated Indian small and mid-cap across EPC, defence, pharma, logistics, BFSI, specialty chemicals, hospitals and data-centre infrastructure.

**Overlap with existing data-centre infrastructure exposure.** CleanMax is a demand-correlated play on the same wave: 42 percent of its contracted book is Data and AI load. A position here doubles the portfolio's bet on Indian data-centre buildout. If the operator already holds data-centre infrastructure, CleanMax is not a diversifier. It is the power-supply leg of the same theme. A shortfall in Indian data-centre demand hits both at once.

**Concentration in leveraged infrastructure.** CleanMax is a highly geared balance sheet (net debt / EBITDA 8.03x). Adding it raises the portfolio's leveraged-infrastructure concentration. In a rate-up or credit-tightening event, the geared names move together. This is a correlation the operator should size against, not a name to add on top of existing leveraged exposure without a haircut.

**Replace or duplicate.** CleanMax does not replace anything in the current set. It duplicates the data-centre theme on the supply side and adds a new leveraged-infrastructure exposure. Its distinct feature is the 23-year contracted annuity, which no other portfolio name provides. That annuity is the reason to hold it and the reason it is not a pure duplicate. The duplication is thematic (data-centre demand), not structural (contracted renewable IPP is a new asset class for the book).

## CLOSING NARRATIVE

CleanMax builds power plants and sells the electricity on 23-year contracts. The buyers are the best corporate names in India. The plants run. The contracts hold. On the surface this is a clean annuity, and the annuity is genuinely there.

Underneath, the company is in a land grab. Grid access is scarce. The firms that lock up corridors and connectivity now will own the next decade of corporate renewable supply. So CleanMax builds as fast as it can raise money. It raises money with debt first and shareholder equity second. Each new gigawatt loads more debt onto the stack and sells a slice of the project to minority partners. The parent shareholder ends up with a thin claim on a large and growing pile of assets.

That is the tension at the centre of this stock. The fleet grows. The equity does not grow as fast, because the debt grows too and the returns sit below the cost of capital. Growth here adds size. It does not yet add value. Post-tax return on capital is about 3 percent today and reaches about 5 percent on the company's own FY28 plan. The cost of capital is near 10 percent. A business that earns 5 and pays 10 destroys value on every rupee it invests, however fast it grows.

So why would anyone own it. Because the market pays 12 to 14 times forward profit for the growth, and if it keeps paying, the equity compounds fast. That is the whole thesis. It is not a bet on the plants. It is a bet on the multiple. The plants can hum along at 99 percent uptime and the stock can still halve if the market decides a builder that must one day become a landlord should trade like a landlord, at 8 to 10 times.

The annuity is strong. The multiple is the weak point. A buyer here is paid to grow the fleet and exposed to the day the market reprices growth it cannot yet earn. Watch one number above all others: the price the market puts on a rupee of forward profit. When that price falls, this thesis falls with it, and the debt makes sure the shareholder feels it first.
