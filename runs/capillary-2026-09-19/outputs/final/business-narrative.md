# CAPILLARY business narrative (phase 1, evidence only)

## Business understanding narrative

Capillary sells a cloud platform that runs loyalty programmes for large consumer brands. Subscriptions to that platform made 89% of FY26 revenue. The core product, Loyalty+, keeps the points ledger itself: every point a shopper earns, spends or loses to expiry sits in Capillary's system, audited like a financial ledger. Engage+, Insights+, Rewards+ and the aiRA AI layer sit on top, and the brand pays per member or per transaction, not per user seat, on contracts of three to seven years. A brand cannot drop it quickly, because the ledger holds its members' live balances and an average customer runs about nine integrations into its tills, online stores and data systems. Installation fees, 10% of revenue, pay once for connecting a new or migrated customer, and campaign services, under 1%, are run as agent. The buyers are about 415 brands in 49 countries, around 20 of them Fortune 500 names, across retail (now under a third of revenue), banking, telecom, travel, hospitality and healthcare. Buying is concentrated: the top 10 customers made 58.71% of FY25 revenue, the company has not disclosed the FY26 figure, and the United States supplied 56% of FY26 revenue. Demand today comes from brands moving money into keeping customers, because the company's cited industry data says winning a new customer costs five times as much as keeping one. The run tracks that demand through "Fortune 500/large-enterprise new-logo referrals via System Integrators and Consulting Partners", which brought about 40% of FY26 new customers. It also tracks "US enterprise MarTech/customer-engagement software budget growth" and "Named large/flagship end-customer renewal and expansion decisions". Growth should come from software taking share in a loyalty market that management says is still over 90% agency spend, a claim no peer transcript could verify. It should also come from buying and migrating fragmented agency rivals, and from privacy rules that push brands toward owned customer data, tracked as "Data-privacy regulatory activity (GDPR enforcement, US state privacy laws, India DPDP Rules rollout)". Two outside signals cut both ways: "Enterprise generative-AI/agentic-AI adoption rate among marketing organisations" can lift aiRA or let brands build their own tools, and "USD/INR and other cross-currency rates" added about 6% to Q1 FY27 growth. The subscription line holds the one clear moat, switching costs from the owned ledger and the integrations. The emerging moat scan landed in the MODEST band, and its strongest row, the acquisition rollup, fails the sacrifice test because a well funded rival could run the same rollup without giving anything up. The security certifications are table stakes, aiRA's usage pricing is an untested counter to seat priced rivals, and the scan scored talent asymmetry and the cannibalisation barrier (Categories 21 and 22) at zero. The installation and campaign lines have no moat.

---

Loyalty ledger rollup, organic growth unresolved

Capillary runs loyalty software for big brands and bills most of it as subscription. FY26 revenue was Rs 735 Cr, up 23%, with 89% recurring. The company listed on 21 Nov 2025. It lost money every year from FY21 to FY24 and turned profitable in FY25 and FY26. Revenue grew from Rs 171 Cr in FY21, about 34% a year compounded.

The transition is from a loss making software vendor to a profitable rollup. Capillary buys small loyalty firms at 0.4 to 3 times revenue and moves their customers onto its own platform. The claimed prize is gross margin rising from about 30% on an acquired book to 65 to 70% at steady state. Three of five deals reached contribution margins of 40 to 45%. Kognitiv (May 2025) and SessionM (USD 20 m, closed 1 May 2026) are still in progress. FY27 guidance is Rs 1,000 to 1,050 Cr, and management later said it "will definitely beat" Rs 1,065 Cr. The organic growth rate under that guide is unresolved. Management claims 17%, but about 6 points of that may be currency. An analyst worked Q1 organic growth out near 11%. Management's own split of the plan implies organic revenue growth of only 3 to 6%, a verifier's inference from call arithmetic. H1 FY27 organic revenue at constant currency is the number that separates these readings.

The backward numbers are weak and the forward ones are young. The Gate 0 scorecard gives 66 of 160 after a verifier fix (the stage scored 68), graded AVERAGE, with the moat class cut from STRONG to MODERATE. Three deal breakers bind, all from the loss years. ROCE reached 8.8% in FY26, the best year on record and still under 10%, and management called it "about 3%" on one call before recasting it as a 22% cash return. Operating EBITDA margin was 12.6% in FY26, below all three listed peers at 17 to 26%. FY26 profit of Rs 52 Cr holds a Rs 25 Cr one time Kognitiv churn indemnity, 49% of pretax profit, plus a net tax credit. Management's own normalised profit was Rs 32 Cr. Q1 FY27 showed a Rs 9.5 Cr net loss after a Rs 33 Cr fraud loss. On the other side, organic revenue compounded 42% a year from FY23 to FY26 and the company has 15 straight quarters of revenue growth.

Cash improved sharply in FY26 but the multi year trend is mixed. Operating cash flow swung from minus Rs 46 Cr in FY25 to plus Rs 150 Cr in FY26, and the one time indemnity was kept out of it. Receivable days fell from 98 to 90, and bad receivables fell by two thirds. Working capital days still rose from 24 in FY23 to 53 in FY26, because payable days fell from 67 to 37. The stage first blamed FY26 cash on a payables squeeze. A verifier showed both receivables and payables helped FY26 cash instead. About Rs 44 Cr of FY26 cash came from a build in payables and other liabilities, probably customer prepayments, which the record has not confirmed. The balance sheet holds Rs 359 Cr of cash and Rs 323 Cr of fixed deposits against Rs 45 Cr of borrowings. Goodwill of Rs 310 Cr is 24% of assets, tested at a 27.7% discount rate with no headroom disclosed.

Management delivers on direction and is loose on numbers. The concall stage graded credibility B; a verifier would grade it B minus to C plus. FY27 guidance held and was sharpened, margins rose, and management declined to raise guidance under pressure. Against that, four material figures moved between calls without comment. aiRA went from "4 to 5% of revenues" to a USD 2 to 2.5 m run rate. SessionM went from a USD 35 m business to USD 32 m of ARR. Platform gross margin went from 69 to 70% to "upwards of 75%" organic. New order value and net retention were both restated by leaving out one large healthcare customer. A EUR 3.0 m cyber banking fraud hit a recently acquired Czech subsidiary in July 2026. KPMG runs a forensic audit, and the Q1 call gave it little detail. The promoter record is clean on law and pledges (0%), graded CAUTION. Institutions voted 55.9% against an ESOP extension. Two senior finance staff resigned in May and July 2026. The promoter holding company sold 4.14% on 10 Aug 2026 to exit Peak XV, and other PE funds still own over 30% of that company.

Peers confirm the cost engine and not the market story. NEWGEN and INTELLECT both show costs growing well below revenue, the same mechanism behind Capillary's margin climb. INTELLECT's own acquisition landed at a single digit operating margin, a different measure from Capillary's gross margin target. No peer supports the claim that software is under 10% of loyalty spend, or that 110% plus net retention is top decile. UNIECOM also reports net retention above 100% only after leaving out one top 10 client, the same adjustment Capillary uses. NEWGEN reports longer enterprise decision cycles while Capillary says it sees no slowdown. The peer stage found no claim that peers contradict outright.

The market is large once measured on the right base. On a software only definition, the total pool is Rs 8,550 to 11,115 Cr, growing about 12% a year. The serviceable slice is Rs 5,835 Cr, and Capillary already holds about 12.6% of it. The run sizes an obtainable market of Rs 1,361 Cr in three years and Rs 1,913 Cr in five, implying 21 to 23% annual growth and 7.9 times revenue headroom, a STRONG runway. Management's USD 18.2 bn figure counts the whole loyalty spend, including agencies and rewards, and runs 18 times the software pool. The FY27 guide of 36 to 43% growth sits above the obtainable market path because SessionM adds a full year of acquired revenue at once.
