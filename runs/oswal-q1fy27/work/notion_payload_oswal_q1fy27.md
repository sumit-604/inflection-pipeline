## PLAIN-LANGUAGE BRIEF

### 1. Summary narrative
Revenue was Rs 474 Cr, down 7.9 percent from a year ago and down 7.1 percent from the March quarter (L9). Reported EBITDA was Rs 82 Cr at a 17.1 percent margin, operating EBITDA Rs 74 Cr at 15.7 percent (L9). PAT was Rs 54 Cr at an 11.2 percent margin (L10). Management guides FY27 to 20 to 25 percent revenue growth, a 15 to 17 percent operating EBITDA margin, and an 11 to 13 percent PAT margin (L10, L33). Every one of those is lower than the FY26 base of plus 44 percent revenue and a 24.9 percent margin, so this is a reset, not a wobble. Management blames the reset almost entirely on outside forces: a roughly 9 percent price cut in the Magel Tyala pump scheme from aggressive bidding, plus 3 to 3.5 percent from higher raw material cost and 1 to 1.5 percent from operating leverage (L67). The bull point is that the promoter was on the call and answered directly, capacity milestones look dated and near, and the backward integration into modules, structures, and now inverters is advancing (L76, L85). The bear point is that the entire FY27 growth number rests on PM Surya Ghar, which contributed almost nothing in Q1 and has to ramp to Rs 800 to 1,000 Cr in the back half off a near-zero base (L129, L103). Cash got worse: receivable days rose to about 229 from 155, cash conversion to about 224 days from 72, and collections this quarter were admitted as very few (L10, L93). Two governance items the thesis cares about went unaddressed: whether the CFO who presented is the permanent successor, and the Doon Infrapower SPV and segment returns (FND-08, FND-10). The market noticed the strain, with five analysts pressing the same margin question and one naming a revenue disconnect out loud (L123). Decision Status stays WATCHLIST. The operational entry gate reads negative on proxies, the governance gate cannot be scored a pass, and three of the four hard-stop legs are met with the fourth pointing negative. No number here is estimated; the concall is the only source and its ASR garble is preserved, not corrected.

### 2. Sector intelligence
Provenance: this quarter's call unless marked (Notion). The solar water pumping sector is B2G and policy-driven. PM-KUSUM 2.0 is delayed and management can only say it hopes for August with no guarantee (L22 to L24, this call). Because the big central scheme is late, national bidders crowded into single-state schemes like Maharashtra's Magel Tyala, and price competition cut realization about 9 percent (L18, L30, this call). Management's own install history shows why: roughly 1.5 lakh pumps installed FY23-24, 3.5 lakh FY24-25, and 5 lakh FY25-26, and rising volumes attracted more players (L112, this call). The company frames current bid prices as unsustainable and expects vendor-side and demand-supply relief when PM-KUSUM 2.0 restores volume and choice (L55, L56, this call). Payer mix is government, which management calls fully secure but slow, and that shows in the receivable blowout (L10, L93, this call). Sector context from prior work (Notion): a 63 MW Bihar RESCO award on 14-Jun-26 splits into Rs 247 Cr install plus Rs 257 Cr of 10-year annuity, and Bihar DISCOM slow-pay makes RESCO a 10-year cash recovery, which is the working-capital tension the thesis flags. Structural tailwind is real government demand; structural headwind is bidding-driven margin compression plus long government payment cycles.

### 3. Business-model intelligence
Provenance: this quarter's call unless marked (Notion). The company makes money by manufacturing and installing solar pumps for government schemes, and it is backward integrated into modules, BOS, and structures, with inverters targeted in-house within about six months (L76, this call). That backward integration is the margin defence: in PM Surya Ghar it captures both the module-manufacturing margin and the EPC and installation margin, and uses its existing pump channel partners for distribution (L138, this call). Unit economics this quarter: volume was about 43,000 pumps versus 56,000 a year ago, down 23 percent, while revenue fell only 7.9 percent, which implies mix moved toward higher-value modules and EPC even as pump prices fell (L43, L50, L9, this call, reconciliation derived). Model drift is the story of the quarter: from a pure-play pump maker the company is opening PM Surya Ghar as a dedicated vertical, entering solar EPC and IPP aggressively, adding wires and cables at Rs 70 to 100 Cr for entry purposes, and taking some projects where profit is deliberately spread over five years (L9, L71, L105, L133, this call). The bull read is diversification away from one delayed scheme; the bear read is four or five new lines launched at once with no segment ROCE and no capital-allocation envelope disclosed (FND-05, FND-10). Cash conversion is the model's weak point, and it worsened this quarter (Notion cash multiplier 0.65x, structural drag; L10, L93 this call confirm).

### 4. Competition intelligence
Provenance: this quarter's call unless marked (Notion). The company competes in government pump tenders where more players entered as scheme volumes rose, and some bid aggressively to win entry while some older players bid low to defend falling volumes (L112, this call). Where the company wins: backward integration means less dependence on external suppliers and a claimed ability to recover through value engineering that competitors without in-house modules cannot match (L18, L76, this call). Management asserts that if copper or input prices rise the hit lands on the whole industry and not on Oswal specifically, and that peers face the same pressure (L98, L99, this call). Where it is structurally weaker: it competes in commoditized single-state tenders where price, not product, decides, so its realization is exposed to the least disciplined bidder in the room (L30, L56, this call). A competitor publicly guided PM-KUSUM 2.0 to August, which management could only match with hope, so it is not an information leader on the key catalyst (L22, this call). Competitive risk to watch: if PM-KUSUM 2.0 slips further, the single-state price war continues and the 15 to 17 percent margin floor is tested toward its lower bound.
