# AEQUS (Aequs Ltd), business narrative

Run: runs/aequs-2026-09-05. Phase 1 (evidence) only. Stages 10 and 11 did not
run, so this file carries no valuation, no entry zone and no decision.

## BUSINESS UNDERSTANDING NARRATIVE

Aequs makes certified precision parts and assemblies for commercial aircraft,
built to a customer's own approved drawing. The range covers engine parts,
structures, actuation, landing systems, turned parts, interiors, cargo items and
finished assemblies. It held 5,654 aerospace part numbers at FY26 year end, and
forges, machines, surface treats and assembles them inside one special economic
zone at Belagavi, plus sites in France and the United States. Aerospace is 85
percent of revenue, and a customer cannot move the work easily, because each part
number is qualified to one program on one approved line. The second line is
consumer goods built to a brand owner's specification: toys, cookware, small
durables and plastic enclosures for consumer electronics, molded and machined at
Koppal and Hubballi. Consumer is 15 percent of revenue and carries much lower
switching cost, because toy and enclosure work moves between contract
manufacturers with little friction. The named aerospace buyers are Airbus,
Boeing, Safran, Collins Aerospace, Honeywell, SAAB, GKN Aerospace, Eaton and
Bombardier. Four customers, anonymised in the notes, were 58 percent of FY26
revenue, all four in aerospace, and the top three relationships average 15 years.
The consumer buyers are Hasbro, Mattel, Spin Master, Tramontina, Wonderchef and
Reliance Retail, and Hasbro stopped sourcing entirely during FY26 with no root
cause given. Demand today rides the Airbus and Boeing single aisle build rate
ramp, which two listed peers confirm from their own order books. The disclosed
order book grew from USD 889 mn at 31 March 2026 to USD 1,004 mn at Q1 FY27. The
outside signals that carry that demand are the Airbus and Boeing combined single
aisle and long range delivery and backlog trend, the Safran Landing Systems A320
wheel programme ramp, the Collins Aerospace, Honeywell, GKN Aerospace and Eaton
programme awards, the USD/INR exchange rate, and Mattel and Spin Master global
sourcing guidance. Growth from here rests on the Safran 15 year A320 wheel
agreement, two Farnborough Tier 1 agreements not yet in the order book, the Hosur
aero engine and landing gear site, the India ECMS and PLI 2.0 approvals, and the
Tamil Nadu and Karnataka state MoU milestones. Each of those has an outside
check: Airbus and Boeing monthly delivery reports, Safran investor disclosure,
MeitY and PIB releases, and state government releases. The competitive advantage
sits in aerospace only. Qualification lock in, NADCAP and AS9100 approvals, the
first Airbus and Boeing approved surface treatment line in India, and single site
vertical integration are the durable parts, and the emerging moat scan names
qualification lock in, geographic first mover position and strategic partnerships
as the three strongest forming categories. Two of those capabilities sit inside
joint ventures Aequs does not wholly own: surface treatment with Magellan
Aerospace and forging with Aubert & Duval. Consumer has no moat, the annual
report makes no uniqueness claim for it, and the same scan found the two
financial categories, war chest and working capital, argue against the company
rather than for it.

---

Order book grew, operating cash turned negative

Aequs reported FY26 revenue of Rs 1,230.4 crore, up 33.1 percent. Aerospace
contributed Rs 1,046.4 crore, up 27 percent. Consumer contributed Rs 184.1 crore,
up 84 percent off a small base. The consolidated net loss widened to Rs 113.25
crore from Rs 102.35 crore. The pretax loss actually narrowed, from Rs 94.08
crore to Rs 71.54 crore, and a tax charge five times larger reversed that
improvement. The company listed on 10 December 2025.

The claim is that aerospace scale outruns the consumer drag. Aerospace segment
EBITDA margin reached 26.9 percent in FY26, up from 19.4 percent, and segment
pretax profit more than doubled to Rs 173.98 crore. Consumer ran a segment EBITDA
margin of minus 42.5 percent and a pretax loss of Rs 217.92 crore, 49.4 percent
wider than FY25. Consumer holds Rs 1,286.9 crore of segment assets against
aerospace's Rs 1,378.3 crore, for one sixth the revenue. Management guides
consumer to EBITDA breakeven by Q4 FY27 and the group to PAT breakeven by H1
FY28. Consumer utilisation fell across three quarters, 31 percent to 23 percent
to 22 percent, while the 40 to 50 percent FY27 exit target stayed unchanged.
Management guided FY27 aerospace revenue growth of 25 to 30 percent; the margin
bar was phrased "maintained at 20%" on the Q4 FY26 call and "above 20%" on the Q1
FY27 call, two different bars in two consecutive calls.

The Gate 0 screen scores AVOID. Core score 25, moat score 9, grand total 34, two
moats confirmed. Five deal breakers fired: Block A below 8, Block B at 0, median
ROCE of minus 0.65 percent, cumulative operating cash to profit of 0.25 times,
and negative profit in FY24, FY25 and FY26. The AVOID comes from returns and
cash, not from the aerospace story. Aerospace segment ROCE was 21.69 percent in
Q1 FY27 while consolidated ROCE was 1.56 percent in FY26, because consumer
segment ROCE ran minus 24.77 percent.

Operating cash flow swung from plus Rs 26.14 crore in FY25 to minus Rs 98.75
crore in FY26. Reported FY26 EBITDA of Rs 154.45 crore converted to no operating
cash at all. Free cash flow was minus Rs 441.30 crore in FY26 and minus Rs 239.02
crore in FY25, the only two years this corpus allows to be computed. Working
capital days rose 19.6 days to 151.4. Debtor days rose from 61.8 to 78.5,
receivables grew 69 percent against revenue growth of 33.1 percent, and expected
credit loss coverage on gross receivables fell from 1.91 percent to 0.73 percent
with no fresh charge booked. Q1 FY27 operating cash flow was minus Rs 41.4 crore.
The whole FY26 cash build came from IPO proceeds, not from operations, and
management has flagged a further raise of about USD 150 mn as possibly needed.
Peer working capital days of 120 to 200 put the Aequs range of 120 to 151 inside
the sector norm, which argues the build is growth linked rather than broken.

The concall stage graded management credibility B. The red flag verifier would
grade it C. Q1 FY27 beat both FY27 growth guides, with aerospace revenue up 40
percent and consumer revenue up 190 percent. The margin leg does not survive a
like for like test: the 23 percent Q1 segment margin includes other income and
excludes unallocated cost, while the 20 percent guide was defined on the
exclusive basis. Q4 FY26 headline EBITDA of Rs 32.1 crore contained Rs 27.9 crore
of other income, disclosed only one quarter later, leaving operational EBITDA of
Rs 4.2 crore. The same quarter's reported loss of Rs 54.1 crore contained an
undisclosed Rs 9 crore exceptional gain. New aerospace parts added fell from 433
in Q4 FY26 to 86 in Q1 FY27, an 80 percent drop in management's own performance
proxy, one call after it claimed 100 to 150 parts a month. Four ratios were
restated or left unreconciled between consecutive calls: working capital days,
net debt to equity, FY25 fixed asset turnover, and capacity hours that match no
prior disclosure. Management also said directly that raising margin would reduce
the win rate, and that the company owns no intellectual property.

Nine peer transcripts from AZAD, UNIMECH and DYNAMATECH were read and all nine
were used. They confirm three things: the single aisle build rate ramp, working
capital day norms of 120 to 200, and capex accelerating around order wins funded
by a mix of accrual, debt and equity. No peer contradicted an Aequs claim
outright. Two findings complicate it. AZAD and UNIMECH post blended EBITDA
margins in the 30s percent against the Aequs 20 to 27 percent aerospace band,
which cuts against the vertical integration margin edge management offered when
analysts pushed twice. Both peers also separated non operating income from
operating performance in the same quarter Aequs embedded it in the headline. AZAD
has already qualified two Indian steel mills for critical grades with GE and
Siemens approval, cutting raw material from about 12 percent to about 5 percent
of sales, while Aequs says India qualified raw material is not possible soon and
runs about 99 percent imported input.

The market work puts the addressable market at Rs 16,231 crore to Rs 20,920 crore
and the serviceable slice at Rs 11,673 crore. Aequs holds about 10.5 percent of
that slice today, which leaves 9.5 times revenue headroom, and the runway class
is STRONG. Serviceable capture implies revenue compound growth of 21.7 percent
over three years and 20.4 percent over five. The company's own Vision 2031 target
of 4 to 6 times FY26 revenue implies 32 to 43 percent compound growth, well above
that path. Management's own market sizing comes in at 1.17 times the independent
build, which reads as reasonable. Consumer sizing rests on much thinner evidence:
no India specific contract manufacturing figure exists for export toys,
electronics enclosures or export cookware, so consumer capture was built from a
capacity conversion check instead.

The promoter stage returned CAUTION. No deal breaker fired. Pledge is zero and
stayed zero through listing, the legal and SEBI record is clean over three years,
and three independent directors with checkable credentials joined in April 2025
before the IPO. Against that, both statutory audit opinions carry a Rule 11(g)
audit trail qualification, CARO names adverse remarks across seven group entities
including auditor confirmed cash losses at three, and four further entities had
no CARO report issued at all. CEO total pay rose 111 percent to Rs 9.27 crore in
a year the consolidated loss widened and median employee pay fell 16.82 percent.
Related party purchase and service flows run about Rs 115 crore, or 9.4 percent
of revenue, and include 13 percent a year unsecured promoter loans, a flat Rs 1
crore branding fee and a guarantee fee up 473 percent to Rs 5.85 crore. The CFO
resigned effective 30 June 2026, five weeks after the audit sign off, with no
successor named. Four of six directors, including the CEO, missed the first AGM.

The corpus is thin in named places. There is no prospectus, no results filing, no
rating rationale, no Reg 30 announcement record and no shareholding filing. Every
Q1 FY27 number traces to the investor presentation or the August 2026 transcript,
never to a results filing. Three of the four facts this run was told to verify
first came back part confirmed. The FY27 aerospace guidance framing does not
appear in the annual report at all and was found only in the Q4 FY26 call. The Rs
500 crore of Rs 660 crore consumer capex split appears only in that same call and
turned conditional on utilisation one quarter later. The separate Safran engine
parts agreement named in company memory is not identifiable in any of the three
transcripts. The fourth fact holds: the USD 1,004 mn order book and the 15 year
Safran Landing Systems A320 wheel agreement are both confirmed in the calls and
the presentation. Market cap of Rs 16,242 crore sits above the small and micro cap
remit this strategy works in, which is the operator's call at Halt 1.
