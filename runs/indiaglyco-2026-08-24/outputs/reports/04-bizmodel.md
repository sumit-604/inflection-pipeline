## SECTION 1: THE BUSINESS MODEL IN PLAIN ENGLISH

### 1A. One-line description
India Glycols runs four different businesses under one roof: it makes
bio-based industrial chemicals from sugarcane/grain feedstock (glycols,
glycol ethers, industrial gases), makes and sells branded country liquor
and IMFL, makes ethanol for the government's petrol-blending programme, and
makes plant-based pharma ingredients and nutraceuticals — and from
1 September 2026 the liquor+ethanol business and the pharma-ingredients
business separate into their own listed companies (Inv. Pres. slide 6-7;
AR p.12-13).

### 1B. Money flow chain, one per revenue stream

**1. Bio-based Specialities & Performance Chemicals (BSPC) — glycols, glycol
ethers, specialty chemicals, industrial gases**
[Molasses/grain-based ethanol, in-house ethylene oxide] → [IGL converts to
bio-MEG/DEG/TEG, glycol ethers, specialty derivatives, industrial gases at
Kashipur] → [delivers bulk chemical/gas shipments to industrial buyers] →
[PET/polyester, paints & coatings, oilfield, automotive, textile, food &
animal-nutrition manufacturers pay] → [invoiced sale, priced off global
petro-MEG/glycol-ether benchmarks] (AR p.34, BRSR p.66).

**2. Potable Spirits — Country Liquor, IMFL, ENA**
[Molasses/grain, imported alcohol] → [distilled into ENA, blended/bottled
into branded whisky, vodka, rum at Kashipur & Gorakhpur under state
excise license] → [delivers cased liquor to state-controlled retail
channels, CSD/defence canteens, and ENA to beverage/pharma/perfumery
buyers] → [retail consumers, government canteen stores, and B2B ENA buyers
pay] → [sale through state excise-regulated distribution, State Excise
Duty collected and remitted by the company] (AR p.14-15, BRSR p.66).

**3. Bio-Fuel — fuel-grade ethanol**
[Grain/molasses feedstock, distillery capacity at Kashipur (600 KLPD) and
Gorakhpur (500 KLPD)] → [ferments and distills to fuel-grade ethanol] →
[delivers via tender-won allocation] → [Oil Marketing Companies (OMCs) pay,
under the Ethanol Blending Programme (EBP)] → [government-administered
procurement price, volume fixed by annual allocation, not open-market sale]
(AR p.14, Inv. Pres. slide 37).

**4. Ennature Bio Pharma — plant-based APIs, nutraceuticals, nicotine**
[Plant/botanical raw material] → [extracted via SCFE-CO2/solvent extraction
at the Dehradun EU-GMP facility into APIs (Thiocolchicoside, Colchicine),
branded nutraceutical ingredients (Maxicuma, Xanthogreen), and nicotine
derivatives] → [delivers to pharma companies, nutraceutical formulators,
and NRT/pharma nicotine customers] → [pharma and nutraceutical companies
pay] → [B2B contract sale, global-quality-certified] (AR p.15, Inv. Pres.
slide 40).

### 1E. The chai-stall-uncle version
Think of India Glycols as four separate shops sharing one landlord.
One shop turns sugarcane and grain into industrial chemicals sold to
factories, like a sugar mill that learned chemistry. One shop is a licensed
liquor maker selling bottled whisky, vodka, and country liquor, most of
whose sticker price is actually government tax it collects and hands over.
One shop sells fuel-grade alcohol to oil companies under a government quota,
like a contract farmer who gets told each year how much to supply. One shop
is a small specialty pharma-ingredients maker selling to drug companies.
From 1 September 2026 the landlord is splitting the building: the liquor-
plus-fuel shop gets its own signboard (IGL Spirits), the pharma shop gets
its own signboard (Ennature Bio Pharma), and only the chemicals shop keeps
the original "India Glycols" name. If you own the stock today, you are
buying all four shops; after the split you keep only the chemicals shop
unless you also hold the new shares (AR p.12-13, Inv. Pres. slide 6-7).

### 2D. Industry lifecycle stage
BSPC (bio-MEG/glycol ethers): mature-cyclical global commodity-chemical
market undergoing a "commoditize vs specialize" split — the AR itself notes
"glycol ethers are gradually shifting from commodity status to specialized
applications" (AR p.35), and IGL is trying to migrate up this curve via
Performance Chemicals/Bio-Amines. Global MEG market growing ~3.2% CAGR to
2030, India's domestic MEG market ~7-8% CAGR (AR p.35) — India segment is
growth-stage even while global segment is mature. Potable Spirits: mature,
regionally defended, with a premiumisation sub-cycle underway (management
targets doubling IMFL volumes, Inv. Pres. slide 9). Bio-Fuel: policy-driven
growth stage — India completed its "20% ethanol blending rollout" as of
30 June 2026 (Inv. Pres. slide 37), meaning the tailwind that drove FY26's
+41% segment revenue growth (AR p.14) is now largely realized; incremental
growth depends on further mandate escalation or export markets, not
FOUND in these documents. Ennature: early-growth niche within a mature
global plant-API/nutraceutical industry, constrained by raw-material
availability (Inv. Pres. slide 13).

## SECTION 3: FINANCIAL METRICS THAT MATTER FOR THIS BUSINESS MODEL

### 3A. Ignore-these-track-these

| Commonly tracked ratio | Why misleading/irrelevant here |
|---|---|
| Blended company-wide EBITDA/PAT margin on GROSS revenue | ~57% of FY26 gross revenue (₹5,615 cr of ₹9,826 cr) is State Excise Duty collected and remitted, sitting almost entirely inside Spirits (AR Standalone P&L note). Pres. slide 27) |
| Single blended company-wide P/E or EV/EBITDA multiple | Blends a cyclical commodity-chemical business, a government-quota biofuel business, a licensed consumer-brand business, and a niche specialty-pharma business, each with a structurally different growth/risk/multiple profile — and each is about to be a separately listed stock (Inv. Pres. slide 6-7) |
| Consolidated Debtor/Inventory/Payable days as one number | Masks the divergence between low-WC Spirits (excise-duty-timed cash collection) and higher-WC BSPC/Bio-Fuel (imported alcohol, grain stocking, capex-in-progress inventory) |
| Net capital turnover ratio as reported (standalone, -18.94x FY26) | The negative reading is produced by short-term borrowings sitting inside current liabilities, not by genuine negative trade working capital; taken at face value it wrongly signals extreme WC efficiency (AR Financial Ratios note, p.144) |
| Company-wide gross margin | BSPC (commodity-adjacent) and Ennature (specialty pharma) sit at opposite ends of the margin spectrum; a blended figure conceals both and moves for reasons unrelated to either business's real economics |
| ROCE on FY26 year-end capital employed | FY26 capital employed includes the ₹467 cr preferential-issue proceeds (allotted 24 Nov 2025) not yet fully deployed for a full year, and CARE Ratings is still monitoring utilisation (AR p.12) — near-term ROCE is not yet a clean run-rate number |
