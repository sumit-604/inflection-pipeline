4. **Sector cap row: Recycling / Manufacturing, 25x. This corrects the manifest.** The manifest says "Pharma / CDMO" at 38x, which is a data collector error; GNG makes no pharma product. The best fitting row in the Section 1B table is Recycling / Manufacturing at 25x: refurbishment is remanufacturing of ICT devices, it carries R2v3, EPR and ISO certifications and a circular economy identity, and the company adds real value through a 21 step process, warranty and a brand premium rather than passing product straight through. Both readings: (a) value adding refurbisher and remanufacturer, which is Recycling / Manufacturing at 25x, the row this draft uses; (b) working capital heavy IT hardware distributor and trader, whose closest analogs are the actual peers Redington and Rashi Peripherals trading near 15x to 18x, which argues for a cap nearer 20x given GNG's distribution like 12 to 20 percent gross margins. But surplus cash explains only about 4 of the roughly 14 point reported fall; the other 10 points are the tripled equity being deployed into a low-turnover working-capital-heavy base (inventory 486.57 to 743.11 Cr, receivables 67.62 to 206.85 Cr, results p.17), not idle cash waiting to be spent. Only the ~4 point idle-cash piece is a mechanical unwind; the rest recovers only if working-capital turns improve or margins rise, which is the same open question as the cash-conversion flag. The verdict stays TEMPORARILY DEPRESSED but the recovery is not mostly mechanical. Confidence: `genuinely uncertain` (downgraded from fairly sure). Both readings: temporarily depressed if FY27 operational ROCE climbs back above about 30 percent as the equity earns; declining if it stalls near 28 percent while the working-capital base keeps growing with revenue. The draft uses the temporarily depressed read. Wrong if FY27 operational ROCE fails to rise above about 30 percent.

## THE CATALYST STORY IN PLAIN WORDS

**Revenue.** What makes it keep firing: capacity went from 120,000 to 150,000 units a month, UAE facilities from 3 to 8, headcount from 1,194 to 2,148, all documented on the Q4 call. The memory and component price spike is pushing buyers toward refurbished machines, and Redington and Rashi Peripherals independently confirm that spike and its persistence. What would confirm it: Q1 FY27 revenue beating the 25 percent guide, in line with the FY26 pattern of beating guidance. What would kill it: growth decelerating toward or below the 33 percent industry formalisation rate for two quarters.

**Margin.** What could lift it: operating leverage as scale builds, and a mix shift toward higher value non laptop categories. What would confirm it: gross margin holding above 19 percent and EBITDA margin climbing back above 10.5 percent. What would kill it: component prices normalising, which takes the per unit price gain away, or the Q4 sequential compression continuing.

**Cash.** This is where the catalyst search comes up short. There is no factoring facility, no named working capital programme with a met deadline, and the one specific promise, holding debtor days at 30 to 35, was missed as they drifted to 40 to 45. The deliberate Rs 743 Cr inventory build is a bet on rising memory prices; if the bet pays it converts to cash, if it reverses it becomes a writedown. What would confirm improvement: the first statutory annual report showing operating cash flow turning positive. What would kill the thesis: a third straight year of negative operating cash flow.

**ROCE.** The catalyst is mechanical, the deployment of the roughly Rs 400 Cr of IPO equity into an earning base while EBIT compounds 30 to 60 percent. What would confirm it: FY27 ROCE printing above 26 percent. What would kill it: ROCE stuck at 24 percent as the working capital bulge grows the capital base as fast as profit.
