## NEW FINDINGS

### 1. "Discounts, claims to customers and other selling expenses" more than TRIPLED YoY
Standalone Note 29 (Other Expenses, p.135): ₹61.848 Cr (FY26) vs ₹19.539 Cr (FY25),
**+216.5%** YoY — against revenue growth of only +20.6%. This single line item grew
from 1.27% of revenue to 3.33% of revenue. Consolidated Note 29 (p.216) confirms the
same swing at Group level: ₹62.309 Cr vs ₹19.793 Cr, +214.8%. No sub-analysis breaking
out how much is customer discounts vs claims vs other selling expenses is disclosed —
NOT FOUND IN DOCUMENT. This is the single largest unexplained YoY swing in the entire
cost structure and was not surfaced in Pass 1 (which did not walk through Note 29 line
by line). 🔴 Red Flag.

### 2. Bad debts / irrecoverable advances & receivables written off (net) more than doubled
Standalone and consolidated Note 29 (p.135 / p.216, identical figures): ₹4.895 Cr
(FY26) vs ₹2.161 Cr (FY25), **+126.6%** YoY. This directly reinforces Pass 1's Section
4 receivables-quality finding (rising >6-month overdue bucket, near-doubled ECL
provision) — actual write-offs, not just provisioning, accelerated this year, which
Pass 1 did not separately identify. 🔴 Red Flag (compounds an existing flag).

### 3. Export obligation under Advance License Scheme grew while actual exports fell
Note 42(b) Commitments (p.156): "Export obligation under Advance License Scheme on
duty free import of raw materials, remaining outstanding" grew +31.2% YoY to
₹39.626 Cr (FY26) from ₹30.202 Cr (FY25) — even as ACTUAL export revenue DECLINED
-13.9% YoY (Note 21, p.133; already flagged in Pass 1 Finding #7, consolidated Note 21
p.213 shows a smaller -8.8% consolidated export decline due to the US subsidiary's own
sales). A rising unmet export commitment against falling export performance raises the
risk of customs duty clawback plus interest/penalty if the scheme's export obligation
is not met within its timeline; no maturity schedule or completion percentage for this
obligation is disclosed anywhere in the notes — NOT FOUND IN DOCUMENT. This is a
genuine cross-note risk that Pass 1 did not connect. 🔴 Red Flag (new cross-reference).

### 4. Capital commitments more than doubled
Note 42(a) Commitments (p.156): "Estimated amount of contracts remaining to be
executed on capital account (net of advances)" grew from ₹8.739 Cr (FY25) to
₹17.526 Cr (FY26), **+100.6%**; capital advances paid also surged from ₹0.099 Cr to
₹4.326 Cr. This note (Note 42) was not mentioned at all in Pass 1. 🟡 Watch — signals
a capex ramp-up ahead, consistent with finding #5 below and with "Capital creditors"
(Note 16, current) jumping from ₹2.471 Cr to ₹7.598 Cr (+207.5%).

### 5. Segment capital allocation contradicts the profit-growth segment
Note 39 Segment Information, "Other information — Capital Expenditure" table (p.150),
not extracted in Pass 1: of total FY26 capex of ₹40.536 Cr, **92.8% (₹37.627 Cr) went
to Performance Polymers & Chemicals** — the segment being RETAINED post-demerger —
while P&K Fertilizers, the segment that supplied essentially all of the year's
segment-profit growth (Pass 1's Finding #1, a swing of ₹57.173 Cr) and is being
demerged out, received only ₹2.253 Cr (5.6% of total capex), up from a trivial
₹0.643 Cr in FY25. Management has not been reinvesting behind the fertilizer
segment's earnings success — consistent with (and plausibly informed by) the pending
demerger decision (Note 32). 🟡 Watch, high investor relevance — sharpens Pass 1's
Finding #1 rather than duplicating it.

### 6. Segment assets confirm working-capital buildup sits inside the division being demerged out
Note 39 Segment Information, "Segment Assets/Liabilities" table (p.150), not extracted
in Pass 1: P&K Fertilizers segment assets grew **+63.3% YoY** (₹216.725 Cr →
₹353.864 Cr, driven by the subsidy receivable buildup Pass 1 flagged in Section 4),
while Performance Polymers & Chemicals (the retained segment) assets grew only +9.8%
(₹474.882 Cr → ₹521.657 Cr). Post-demerger, the retained entity's balance sheet and
growth trajectory will look materially different from FY26's consolidated picture —
the working-capital intensity is leaving with the fertilizer business. 🟡 Watch.

### 7. Compensated absences liability grew far faster than the gratuity obligation Pass 1 flagged
Note 34C (Other long-term benefits — compensated absences, p.141): present value of
obligation grew **+63.5% YoY** (₹4.911 Cr → ₹8.028 Cr) on a standalone basis (Group
figure identical, consolidated Note 34C p.222) — materially faster than the Gratuity
PVO growth of +18.1% that Pass 1's Section 9 specifically called out, and faster than
headcount or wage inflation alone would typically explain. No narrative explanation is
given — NOT FOUND IN DOCUMENT. Could reflect reduced leave utilization/encashment,
headcount growth, or a policy change. 🟡 Watch.

### 8. Trade receivables ageing contains a DISPUTED credit-impaired sub-category
Note 36 ageing schedule (pp.144-145), sub-analysis not extracted in Pass 1: within the
credit-impaired bucket, receivables are split "Undisputed" vs "Disputed." The
**Disputed credit-impaired total is ₹5.021 Cr (FY26) vs ₹3.978 Cr (FY25)**, concentrated
in the older buckets (₹2.468 Cr sits in the >3-years-overdue bucket alone, up from
₹2.167 Cr). This is a distinct legal/collection-dispute layer inside the credit-impaired
book, separate from the aggregate >6-months-overdue deterioration Pass 1 already
flagged (2.67% → 7.86% of gross receivables). 🟡 Watch.

### 9. Auditor's Key Audit Matters corroborate the receivables and inventory concerns
Consolidated Independent Auditor's Report (pp.166-167) — technically outside the Notes
proper, but directly relevant and flagged here for completeness per the instruction to
catch what a note-by-note read would otherwise miss. The statutory auditors designate
BOTH **"Valuation of trade receivables"** (citing "long outstanding/disputed
receivables" and "recoverability and provisions of long outstanding/disputed
receivables" as an explicit audit procedure) AND **"Existence and Valuation of
inventory"** as Key Audit Matters for FY26. This is independent, highest-level audit
corroboration of the receivables-quality concerns Pass 1 already raised from the notes
(Section 4) — the auditors' own procedures specifically targeted disputed/long-standing
receivables as requiring extended audit attention. 🟡 Watch / corroborating evidence
(sourced from the Auditor's Report, not a Note — flagged transparently as such).

### 10. Direct taxes paid nearly doubled, compounding the operating cash conversion collapse
Statement of Cash Flows (p.101), not walked through in Pass 1 beyond the headline OCF
figure: "Direct taxes (paid)/refund (net)" was **₹(44.008) Cr FY26 vs ₹(19.546) Cr
FY25, +125.2%** YoY. "Cash generated from operations" (pre-tax) was actually reasonably
resilient at ₹119.322 Cr (FY26) vs ₹150.991 Cr (FY25), but the near-doubling of the tax
cash outflow is a second, independent drag on operating cash conversion alongside the
₹140.029 Cr working-capital drag Pass 1 already identified in Finding #3. 🟡 Watch
(adds a second driver to an existing red flag, not a standalone new flag).

### 11. CWIP / intangible-assets-under-development overrun schedules show an unexplained project drop
Note 4.1/4.2 (CWIP ageing and cost/time overrun schedule, pp.119-121) and Note 6.1/6.2
(Intangible Assets Under Development, p.123) — not extracted in Pass 1 at all. Both
years disclose numerous individual capex projects with cost/time overruns against
original plan (Schedule III mandated disclosure). Notably, the FY25-disclosed
"Proposal for new R&D Centre at Greater Noida" (₹2.417 Cr overrun) does not reappear
anywhere in the FY26 overrun list, while a separately named "New R&D Centre at Savli"
project continues (₹0.360 Cr FY26 overrun, down from a combined ₹9.454 Cr FY25 overrun
for the Savli R&D centre). No narrative cross-reference explains whether the Greater
Noida project was dropped, relocated to Savli, or completed. 🟡 Watch, minor — capex
execution/disclosure discipline.
