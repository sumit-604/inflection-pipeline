### PART A — THE FROM STATE

**A1. ARCHETYPE.**
- Ethanol and ENA (core line, 97.6% of FY26 consolidated revenue, B04 revenue_streams): **Commodity converter.** Output price is fixed by the Government of India per feedstock type per Ethanol Supply Year; margin is the spread against feedstock cost, not pricing power (B04 FLAG-CONVERTER-CLASSIFICATION, prospectus p.233-234). This binds Section 1B Amendment 17 for every downstream stage.
- Compressed biogas (2.4% of consolidated revenue, B04): an early **licence/scarcity-adjacent** line — regulated SATAT procurement price, 10-15 year offtake contracts (B07; business-narrative.md) — but with only one plant operating and three under construction, it does not yet carry enough operating history to sit on the Quality Ladder in its own right.
- SAF and retail fuel: pre-revenue and immaterial respectively (B04 input_gaps; B07). **Fits no known archetype yet** — SAF has a grant and a FEED-stage MoU but no signed EPC contract (B03 guidance_table); retail is seven outlets against a stated Phase 1 target of 100 (AGM outcome filing, 31-Aug-2026, p.4).

**A2. THE SIMPLE ANALOGY.** TruAlt runs five ethanol plants in Karnataka that used to work only during the four-to-five-month sugarcane crushing season, because they could only turn cane juice and molasses into fuel alcohol. The government pays a fixed price for that alcohol so it can blend it into petrol. TruAlt has now rebuilt three of the five plants so they can also run on maize and surplus government rice the rest of the year. The whole business bet is turning a seasonal cane factory into a year-round grain-and-cane factory, on capacity that is already built and mostly paid for.

### PART B — THE TRANSITION

**B1. FROM to TO (ethanol line, the load-bearing line).** FROM **R1 COMMODITY PRICE-TAKER** (a seasonal, cane-only converter with cyclical ROCE around the crush window) TO **R2 COST-ADVANTAGED CONVERTER** (a year-round, dual-feedstock converter with durable mid-teens ROCE from scale and feedstock flexibility, Amendment 17 governs its Section 1B treatment) (CLAUDE.md QUALITY LADDER; B04 archetype classification).

**B2. THE ENGINE.** One mechanical change: 1,300 of 2,000 KLPD of installed capacity converted from mono-feed (cane only) to dual-feed (cane or grain), across three distilleries, delivered on schedule in 3Q FY26, with 93% of the Rs150.68cr IPO capex allocation drawn by 31-Mar-2026 (FY26 AR p.163-164; B03 strengths_top3). This is the physical thing that changes; everything else (CBG, SAF, retail) is optionality sitting beside it, not part of this engine.

**B3. THE PROOF GATE.** Quarterly capacity utilisation, measured on an all-days basis (not the narrower "operating days" basis the AR itself sometimes uses, B03 monitorables), sustained above management's own 80-85% target for two consecutive quarters, together with positive cash flow from operations for at least one full quarter. As of the last filed quarter, neither condition has fired: Q1FY27 utilisation was 60.57% against an 80% promise for that quarter (Investor Presentation Q1FY27 p.6; Concall Feb-2026 p.13), and FY26 CFO was negative (FY26 AR p.247). This is the observation Stage 11 FTTCP tests.

**B4. THE RECOGNITION GAP (open question, resolved at Stage 11).** TruAlt trades at a screener trailing PE of 23.2x (step1-business-brief.md, 18-Sep-2026 basis) while Gate 0 classifies it AVOID on FY26 leverage (B01). Whether that multiple already reflects a market that has priced in the year-round, dual-feed TO state — leaving no re-rating room even if the transition succeeds — or whether it still reflects the R1 crush-season past, is not concluded here. Stage 11 resolves it through the destination-PE gap.

**B5. THE UGLINESS TEST.** The most evidenced reading is **ARTIFACT-OF-CLIMB**: the dual-feed capex arrived on schedule and audited without qualification (B03 strengths_top3); the FY26 revenue fall was a planned three-plant shutdown, not demand loss (B04 irrelevant_ratios); part of the FY26 cash swing is IPO proceeds paying down related-party trade credit that had financed pre-IPO working capital (FY25 working capital -Rs183.1cr, B02 rank 8); and December-weighted sales stretched year-end receivables in a way a rating agency itself frames as timing (India Ratings rationale p.4, quoted in gate-recommendation.md). A rival reading, **STRUCTURAL-FEATURE**, stays live and is not dismissed: national ethanol demand clears at only 50-57% of installed industry capacity, a sector-wide ceiling TruAlt cannot out-execute (B09 capacity_check; B06 peer evidence), and 73.2% of standalone FY26 PBT depends on two government schemes that are policy-contingent (B02/B03). The observation that separates the two readings: consolidated inventory and H1 CFO in the Q2FY27 half-year results, due on BSE by mid-Nov-2026 — inventory below Rs528cr with positive H1 CFO supports ARTIFACT; inventory at or above Rs528cr with negative CFO supports STRUCTURAL (gate-recommendation.md falsification line).

**B6. THE TRANSITION FALSIFIER.** Utilisation stays near 60% (does not climb toward 80-85%) for four consecutive quarters after FY26, AND cash flow from operations stays negative through FY27, AND grain margin per litre does not recover from the Rs6-7 level printed in Jul-2026. Together, this would mean the multi-feed conversion bought flexibility the company cannot monetise, without killing the underlying ethanol-to-OMC business itself (B04 must_track_metrics; gate-recommendation.md falsification line).

**C3. ## SECTION 3: BUSINESS UNDERSTANDING NARRATIVE

*(Drafted at Halt 1 per prompts/13-synthesis-pipeline.md's five-question spec. Stage 13 has already produced a Phase 1 lite copy of this narrative in outputs/final/business-narrative.md; reproduced here rather than re-derived, per the instruction that the spec is defined once. This copy remains subject to update by later stages.)*

TruAlt turns sugarcane juice, syrup, molasses, maize and surplus rice into fuel ethanol at five distilleries in Karnataka. Installed capacity is 2,000 KLPD, and 1,300 KLPD of it can now run on grain as well as on sugar feedstock. Own manufactured ethanol, extra neutral alcohol and by-products such as DDGS, CO2 and dry ice made up 86% of standalone FY26 revenue from operations, traded goods 12%, and a state production incentive about 2%. Oil marketing companies cannot do without the ethanol, because the national blending programme requires it and reached 20% in ESY 2025-26. Compressed biogas from one 10 TPD plant, sold under 10 to 15 year offtake contracts, was 2.4% of consolidated income, and seven company run fuel outlets are too small to show as a separate line.

The buyers are a few public sector oil companies, and four customers took 77% of FY26 standalone revenue. They buy by tender at a price the Government fixes for each feedstock, so the core line has no qualification barrier and no price negotiation. Volume depends on the allocation each tender grants, and peers confirm that FY26 allocations fell short of bids across the sector. On the input side the business depends on the promoter family's own sugar mills, which supplied 72.6% of FY26 purchases.

Present demand comes from the blending mandate, and two public signals track it: the OMC ethanol tender allocation volumes and the national blending percentage in the Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas monthly bulletin. Supply already runs ahead of that demand, because peers put national installed capacity at about 1,995 to 2,300 crore litres against offtake of 1,100 to 1,350 crore litres.

Growth has to come from events a reader can check outside the company: a national blending percentage above 20%, the FCI surplus rice allocation for ethanol, the Karnataka High Court and Supreme Court litigation over a 15 crore litre allocation, and first commercial operation of the Sumitomo and GAIL biogas plants. A national SAF blending mandate notification would open a further line, but the SAF plant is pre revenue and has no signed EPC contract. Maize and FCI rice prices decide how much of any volume growth becomes profit.

The ethanol line has a narrow cost and scale edge from the largest installed capacity in India, 3.6% of the national total, and from running on either feedstock. That edge shrinks to almost nothing when sugar and grain feedstock are dear at the same time, and the line has no pricing power. The emerging moat scan scored the business 17.5, MODEST, with talent asymmetry and cannibalisation barrier both at zero, and a verifier recount puts the score at 14.2 to 15.5 after removing double credit for OMC status. The biogas line has contracted offtake and two credible partners but only one working plant, and the run could not establish a moat for the retail outlets or the SAF project.

## SECTION 5: PLAIN-LANGUAGE SUMMARY

1. TruAlt Bioenergy makes fuel ethanol at five distilleries in Karnataka, with 2,000 KLPD of installed capacity, the largest single ethanol capacity in India.
2. Own-manufactured ethanol and its by-products made up 86% of FY26 standalone revenue; traded goods and a state incentive made up most of the rest.
3. A small compressed-biogas business (2.4% of consolidated revenue) and seven retail fuel outlets sit beside the core ethanol line. Both are too early to size on their own.
4. The buyers are public sector oil marketing companies. Four customers took more than 10% of FY26 revenue each, and the government fixes the price TruAlt is paid, by feedstock type.
5. Demand exists because the national fuel-blending mandate requires ethanol, and reached 20% in the ethanol supply year that just closed.
6. Demand growth is not open-ended. Peer evidence shows the national ethanol industry already has more installed capacity than the market buys, so more plants running does not automatically mean more sales for any one company.
7. TruAlt's one real cost edge comes from scale and from being able to run on two feedstocks instead of one. That edge nearly disappears when both feedstocks cost a lot at the same time.
8. TruAlt has no pricing power on its core product. The government sets the ethanol price, so profit depends only on the gap between that price and feedstock cost.
9. The mental model is a transition: three plants that used to run only in the cane-crushing season can now also run on grain, aiming to turn a seasonal business into a year-round one on capacity that is already built.
10. As filed, that transition has not yet delivered. Utilisation reached only 60.57% against an 80% promise for the same quarter, and grain margin has already fallen from Rs15-16 a litre to Rs6-7 a litre.
11. The evidence base rests on nine outside-the-company variables, from feedstock prices to a pending court order to a lender's covenant decision. Most are independently checkable, but a lender action on the sub-1x debt-service cover is a possible single point of failure. The fragility read is FRAGILE.
12. The corpus could not establish who owns the buyer of TruAlt's Unit 5 plant, or the current court status of two personal legal proceedings against the managing director.
13. The corpus also could not establish the bank's own covenant threshold for TruAlt's debt-service cover ratio, or whether a requested reserve-account waiver has been granted, even though the printed ratio fell below 1.0x in FY26.
14. The biggest open question is whether FY26's negative cash flow was a one-year transition cost that reverses in FY27, or a lasting feature of a business selling into a market that already runs at about half of national installed capacity. The half-year results due by mid-November are the first hard test.
15. A second open question, raised by two competitors but never addressed by TruAlt on any of its four calls, is whether a coming government restriction on sugar-based ethanol will bind on the roughly half of TruAlt's current volume that still runs on sugar.
