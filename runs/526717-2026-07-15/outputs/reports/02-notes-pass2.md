## NEW FINDINGS

### 1. New ₹3,320.29 lakh INTEREST-FREE related-party loan from the dominant customer to the subsidiary, originated this year
Note 33(C)(2) (consolidated, Disclosure of Secured & Unsecured Term Loans & Credit Facilities, Annual_Report.pdf
p.170) discloses, for the first time by name, that the "Unsecured Loans" line inside Short Term Borrowings (Note 19,
"From Body Corporates-Related," ₹3,320.30 lakh) is a loan FROM **Plastene India Limited TO the subsidiary K.P. Woven
Private Limited**, carrying **0% interest**, with a **NIL balance in FY24** (i.e. the loan is new this year). Plastene
India Limited is simultaneously the dominant related-party sales customer (32.96%-34.7% of revenue, per Pass 1), the
largest receivable counterparty (49.2% of group receivables), and — per finding #3 below — a promoter-group equity
shareholder. A zero-interest loan of this size from that same counterparty directly contradicts the company's own
assertion (Note 35.2A standalone, Note 37.2A consolidated) that "transactions entered into with related parties are
made on terms equivalent to those that prevail in arm's length transactions" — a 0% rate on an unsecured ₹33.2 crore
loan is not an arm's-length term by any market benchmark. 🔴 Red Flag — new, high-priority finding; deepens the
already-flagged RPT entanglement into the financing side of the balance sheet, not just sales/purchases/receivables.

### 2. Consolidated (and standalone) operating cash flow was NEGATIVE despite the profit turnaround — profit did not convert to cash
Consolidated Cash Flow Statement (Annual_Report.pdf p.122): Net Cash from Operating Activities = **NEGATIVE
₹(1,852.78) lakh in FY25**, only marginally better than FY24's ₹(1,888.53) lakh — this **despite** consolidated PBT
swinging from ₹19.92 lakh (FY24) to ₹1,800.55 lakh (FY25) and PAT to ₹1,333.08 lakh. "Cash generated from operations"
before tax was actually **worse** in FY25 (₹(1,926.62) lakh) than FY24 (₹(1,664.69) lakh), driven by working-capital
consumption of ₹6,037.80 lakh (receivables ₹3,364.80 lakh + inventory ₹2,052.82 lakh + payables absorption
₹620.17 lakh) that outran the operating profit improvement. The shortfall was funded by ₹3,240.40 lakh of gross new
borrowings (net financing inflow ₹1,777.84 lakh) — i.e. the FY25 growth and profit turnaround was **debt-funded, not
self-funded**. The same pattern holds at standalone level: Net Cash (used in) Operating Activities = ₹(581.08) lakh
in FY25 despite standalone PBT of ₹762.18 lakh (Annual_Report.pdf p.79), funded by ₹1,001.45 lakh of fresh borrowing
proceeds. Pass 1 flagged the receivables and inventory buildups individually but did not cross-reference them to the
cash flow statement's bottom line. 🔴 Red Flag — directly relevant to receivables_trend / cash-conversion assessment;
this is the strongest quantitative evidence that reported profit growth has not yet been matched by cash generation.

### 3. Plastene India Limited — the dominant customer/receivable counterparty — is also a disclosed Promoter Group shareholder
Note 14(d) standalone (Shareholding of Promoters & Promoter Group, Annual_Report.pdf p.101) lists **Plastene India
Limited holding 5,00,000 equity shares (4.68%)** within the "Promoter and Promoter Group" 5%-plus disclosure table,
alongside Prakash Hiralal Parekh, Madhu Parekh, Aman Parekh, Prakash Hiralal Parekh HUF, HCP Enterprise Limited, and
Ayush Anil Kanwar (0.28%, an individual not otherwise named in the KMP/related-party lists). This confirms Plastene
India Limited is not merely an "associate" counterparty in the loose commercial sense but is formally classified as
part of the promoter group by shareholding pattern — the same entity that dominates the company's sales, purchases,
and receivables is also a direct equity holder. 🔴 Red Flag — materially sharpens the related-party concentration
concern already raised in Pass 1's #1 finding; this was not previously connected to the shareholding pattern note.

### 4. The subsidiary itself is a >10%-of-standalone-revenue customer of the parent — combined related-party revenue concentration is higher than Pass 1 stated
Note 38.2(c) standalone (Annual_Report.pdf p.114): the single->10%-of-revenue disclosure lists **K P Woven Private
Limited (the subsidiary) at ₹1,609.38 lakh = 13.6% of FY25 standalone revenue**, in addition to Plastene India
Limited at ₹4,097.66 lakh = 34.7% (already known from Pass 1). Together, just these **two named related
counterparties account for ~48.3% of standalone revenue**, not 34.7% as a reading limited to the single-name
disclosure would suggest. 🔴 Red Flag — upgrades the severity of Pass 1's already-flagged RPT revenue concentration.

### 5. Full related-party universe is wider than the single "Plastene India" name emphasised in Pass 1: seven promoter-influenced enterprises
Note 35.1(C) standalone / Note 37.1(B) consolidated (Annual_Report.pdf p.112, p.169) lists "Enterprises over which
Key Managerial Personnel are able to exercise significant influence" as: **HCP Enterprise Limited, Plastene India
Limited, Plastene Infrastructure Ltd, Cornucopia Tradelink Private Limited, Elite Inks Pvt Ltd, Jain Bulkpack,
Plastene Polyfilms Ltd, Oswal Extrusion Ltd** — an ecosystem of at least seven related enterprises beyond the single
name that dominated Pass 1's RPT discussion. The FY24 comparative RPT transaction table (Note 35.3 standalone,
Annual_Report.pdf p.108) independently confirms material FY24 transaction volumes with four of these by name (HCP
Enterprise Limited, Plastene India Limited, Plastene Polyfilms Limited, Oswal Extrusion Limited), each with
materially different transaction sizes. 🟡 Watch — context-setting finding; the group's related-party trading
footprint is broader and more structurally embedded than a single-counterparty read suggests.

### 6. Cross-guarantee web is wider than Pass 1 captured: a related corporate entity also guarantees subsidiary debt, and a promoter-family member is the subsidiary's Managing Director
Note 17.1 consolidated (Annual_Report.pdf p.155-156) discloses that, beyond the personal guarantees of "Shri Pritesh
Kushalraj Parekh and Smt. Madhu Prakash Parekh" and "Third Party Guarantee of Shri Prakash Hiralal Parekh" (all
already partially known), the subsidiary's Bank of Baroda/Union Bank of India/Karnataka Bank facilities are
**additionally secured by a Corporate Guarantee from Cornucopia Tradelink Private Limited** — one of the seven
related enterprises identified in finding #5, expanding the cross-guarantee network beyond the parent-subsidiary
reciprocal guarantees Pass 1 already flagged. Separately, Note 37.1(C) consolidated (Annual_Report.pdf p.171)
confirms **Mrs Madhu Parekh (a disclosed "Relative of KMP" and 3.69% shareholder) is the Managing Director of the
subsidiary K.P. Woven Private Limited**, and that "Mr Pritesh Parekh" and "Mr Makrand Deorankar" are subsidiary
directors not otherwise named in the parent's standalone KMP table. 🟡 Watch — the family-control and cross-guarantee
structure is more extensive than the parent-only KMP disclosures suggest.

### 7. A brand-new, previously non-existent foreign currency exposure appeared at the standalone/parent level this year
Note 38.4 standalone (Foreign Currency Exposure, Annual_Report.pdf p.109) shows Trade Receivable exposure of **USD
1,319.93 lakh + EUR 138.41 lakh (net ₹1,458.34 lakh unhedged) in FY25**, against **USD 0.00 / EUR 0.00 in FY24** —
i.e. the parent had literally zero foreign currency exposure a year earlier and has now taken on a material unhedged
FX position with no corresponding trade payables to offset it, coincident with the parent's large new export sales
in FY25 (₹5,956.04 lakh, per Pass 1). 🟡 Watch — new risk that did not exist in the comparative year; the 5%
sensitivity table (same note) shows a ₹66.00 lakh PBT impact from a 5% adverse USD move.

### 8. Property, Plant & Equipment note shows a mechanically impossible NEGATIVE net book value in one asset category
Note 4(A) consolidated (Annual_Report.pdf p.151-152) shows **"Plant & Machinery — Continuous Process" net block of
₹(66.42) lakh as at 31 March 2025** — i.e. accumulated depreciation (₹4,980.64 lakh) EXCEEDS gross block (₹4,914.21
lakh) for that category, which is not possible under standard straight-line depreciation to a residual value and
should have triggered a write-down/reclassification stop before this point. Tracing the rollforward: the FY24
opening-to-closing schedule shows a large, unusually labelled **"Transfer from Vehicle to P&M" entry of
₹38,08,39,758 (₹3,808.40 lakh) added to Plant & Machinery — Normal Process and an almost-offsetting ₹(37,97,16,604)
deducted from Plant & Machinery — Continuous Process gross block**, with a matching ₹10,67,23,000 accumulated-
depreciation transfer between the same two categories — a mislabelled entry (the amounts sit within the two P&M
columns, not the small Vehicle column) that appears to be the mechanical source of the subsequent negative balance.
🔴 Red Flag — a genuine arithmetic/classification anomaly in the fixed asset schedule that a verifier or management
should be asked to explain; not previously identified in Pass 1's PPE-adjacent findings (impairment-testing staleness).

### 9. A third instance of stale/unrefreshed template dates and boilerplate surviving into the filed notes

Watch — a pattern of template/copy-paste hygiene issues across at least three separate notes (going concern, PPE
date captions, and Note 42 entity references), worth surfacing as a disclosure-diligence pattern for Pass 3.

### 11. Resolution Plan mechanics: a 32:1 share consolidation and a lender debt-to-equity conversion, not previously detailed
Note 14(a) standalone (Reconciliation of shares outstanding, Annual_Report.pdf p.101) shows historical rollforward

### 12. Export-incentive income embedded in "core" revenue fell sharply even as export sales grew strongly
Note 23 consolidated (Revenue from Operations, Annual_Report.pdf p.132): "Advance Authorisation License and MEIS

### 15. RPT disclosure granularity appears to have regressed year-over-year
The FY25 related-party transaction table (Note 35.3 standalone / 37.3 consolidated) presents current-year figures
aggregated into broad buckets — Subsidiary Company / Joint Venture / Promoter Group / Associate — whereas the same
table's FY24 comparative column headers (visible within the same note, Annual_Report.pdf p.108, p.171) are
structured by **individually named counterparty** (HCP Enterprise Limited, Plastene India Limited, Plastene
Polyfilms Limited, Oswal Extrusion Limited, Subsidiary Company, Joint Venture). A marginal annotation on the
consolidated Note 37 page reads "Format Chang[ed]." This is a reduction in RPT disclosure specificity between the
two years' presentations — a reader relying only on the current year's face table cannot see which specific
promoter-group entity transacted how much, whereas last year's table (as reproduced for comparison) could. 🟡 Watch
— relevant to Pass 3's "disclosure seems deliberately vague or minimal compared to detail given elsewhere" test.

## PASS 2 NEW FINDINGS SUMMARY

Sixteen new findings surfaced on the second read, none of them duplicating Pass 1's top-10. The two most important
are quantitative and both point the same direction: (1) a new ₹3,320.29 lakh, 0%-interest loan from the company's
dominant related-party customer (Plastene India Limited) to the subsidiary, directly contradicting the group's own
arm's-length RPT assertion; and (2) negative operating cash flow at both standalone (₹-581.08 lakh) and consolidated
(₹-1,852.78 lakh) levels in FY25 despite the profit turnaround, with growth funded by fresh borrowings rather than
internally generated cash. Three further findings (the Plastene India shareholding, the subsidiary's own >10%-
revenue status, and the seven-entity related-party universe) collectively raise the severity of Pass 1's already-
flagged related-party concentration finding. A cluster of disclosure-hygiene findings (stale PPE note dates, "Group"
language in standalone notes, RPT disclosure-granularity regression) reinforces the going-concern-boilerplate
pattern Pass 1 identified once, now observed in at least three additional locations — material for Pass 3's
pattern-focused review. One mechanical anomaly (negative PPE net book value in the Continuous Process Plant &
Machinery category, consolidated) and one unreconciled cross-note balance (Promoter Group unsecured loan payable)
are flagged for verification/management query rather than asserted as errors.
