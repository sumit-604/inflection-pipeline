### 2B. Excuse pattern analysis

For every miss above, the stated reason is external (Middle East war disruption, seed/feedstock shortage, nicotine competitor pricing, crude-linked EO cost swings, cash-credit-limit accounting opacity). This is largely plausible and specific rather than vague — management names the mechanism (e.g., "propylene oxide... prohibitively expensive," "Gloriosa seed" shortage) rather than a generic "market conditions." Two patterns stand out:
- **Rolling promises forward rather than naming a miss**: the NSU "doubling" promise was missed in FY26 and, instead of being called a miss in the Q4 FY26 call, was restated as a fresh "doubling" target for FY27 — only in Q1 FY27, when that fresh target was also tracking short, did management use the words "lower than what we had targeted." This is a soft form of deflection: the shortfall becomes visible to the operator only two quarters after it first occurred.
- **Silence/redirection rather than an excuse on data-transparency requests**: when asked twice (Q3 FY26, Q4 FY26 calls) for a segment-wise debt breakup or a specific next-year prepayment number, the CFO did not offer an excuse at all — he deferred to an offline follow-up both times. See 2E.
- Management does raise hard topics proactively in places (Chemicals top-line decline explained unprompted in the opening remarks each quarter, Ennature under-pressure margins flagged before being asked), and does say things close to "we made a mistake" at least once ("lower than what we had targeted," Q1 FY27 call) — this keeps the pattern from being purely external-blame.

**Overall classification: balanced** — specific, plausible external causes for operational misses, combined with genuine proactive disclosure of weak spots, but real avoidance on two recurring transparency requests (segment debt breakup; a clean FY26 term-loan closing confirmation).

### 2D. What they are NOT saying

- **FX/currency hedging: total silence.** Across all three primary transcripts, "hedge" or "hedging" never appears. Management discusses the rupee hitting a record low, crude price spikes, tariff-driven export disruption to the Middle East and US, and volatile freight (5x-20x on westbound cargo) — all currency- and commodity-price-sensitive topics — without once describing a hedging policy, forward cover, or risk-management framework. Given the export exposure (oilfield chemicals to Middle East, US customers) and import exposure (propylene oxide, other petrochemical feedstocks) discussed at length, this is a real gap, not a topic that simply never came up. Matches the prior-stage flag on the FX hedge-book collapse; management addresses none of it in any of the three calls.
- **Bio-glycols realisation (-29.9% per prior-stage flag): not addressed by name.** The narrative on glycols across all three calls is uniformly positive — "our glycols did well" (Q4 FY26), "glycols business, value up 83%" (Q1 FY27) — with no mention of a realisation decline of the magnitude flagged upstream. This is either a segment-mix effect (volume/value up while per-unit realisation is down) that management does not choose to explain, or a genuine tension between the concall narrative and the underlying financial-statement data. Flagged for stage 6/11 to reconcile directly against the results PDF.
- **EBP ethanol allocation down ~22% YoY (per prior-stage flag): never mentioned.** The Bio-Fuels segment narrative in all three calls is uniformly upbeat (FY26 revenue +40.9%, Q1 FY27 EBIT +19% YoY), with OMC allocation dynamics discussed only in the background Q2 FY26 call ("the allocations for next year have largely been done... not complete allocations") and never revisited with a specific allocation-cut figure in any of the three primary calls. If the ~22% YoY allocation decline flagged upstream is accurate, it sits uncomfortably next to the growth narrative management gives on Bio-Fuels; flagged for stage 6/11 verification against OMC data.
- **Debt-breakup by segment**: requested twice on-call (Q3 FY26, Q4 FY26), never delivered on-call either time (see 2E).
- **Royalty terms with Amrut**: explicitly declined as "not in public domain" in the background Q2 FY26 call; never revisited or quantified in any of the three primary calls.

### 3A. Competitor commentary

### 3B. Industry/market intelligence dropped in the calls
- Ethanol blending reached 20% "ahead of schedule" (Q4 FY26 call: "initially scheduled for 2030"); background Q2 FY26 call framed the near-term target as "up to October 2026" for the 20% policy itself — the "originally 2030" framing in the Q4 FY26 call is a more generous read of how far ahead the achievement is than the Q2 FY26 call's own framing suggests. Minor over-promotion candidate; not a hard contradiction, but worth a peer check.
- Ethanol feedstock mix: grain-based feedstock rose from roughly 9% (FY20) to 70-73% (FY26) of national ethanol production, with maize specifically moving from near-zero to ~46% (Q3 FY26 and Q4 FY26 calls, cited with slightly different splits between the two calls — 80%/46% in Q3 FY26 vs 73%/46% in Q4 FY26).
- Payments to the farm sector from the ethanol program: ~Rs40,000cr in FY25; forex savings ~Rs43,000cr in FY25; crude-oil substitution ~24 lakh MT (Q2 FY26 background call).
- DDGS (dried distillers grains, a protein by-product) prices and acceptability rising steadily (Q3 FY26 and Q4 FY26 calls) — supports Bio-Fuels margin.
- UP IMFL industry volume cited as growing from ~15 lakh cases/month to 23-25 lakh cases/month "today" (Q3 FY26 call, Raju Vaziraney) — no specific base-year given for the "15 lakh" figure, a specificity gap.
- UP market value/volume split: Indian Made Indian Liquor (IMIL) is 65% of volume but 52% of value; IMFL is 16% of volume but 34% of value; medium/economy IMFL brands grew 4-7%, premium IMFL brands grew "higher double-digit" (Q4 FY26 call, CEO).
- Industry-wide IMFL CAGR cited as "4% to 5%" over the last two years versus IGL's stated FY27 outlook of "healthy double digits" (Q1 FY27 call, Manoj Rai) — an explicit market-share-gain claim, flagged for peer verification.
- Excess ethanol capacity in the country relative to current blending demand, repeated across all three calls, cited as the reason IGL can substitute in-house ethanol for imports in its Chemicals business.
