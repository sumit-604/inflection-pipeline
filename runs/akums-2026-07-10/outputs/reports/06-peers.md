## PART 1: CLAIM-BY-CLAIM VERIFICATION

### Claim 1
**Claim (verbatim from handoff):** IPM volume growth cited flat to ~1-1.5% (Q2-Q4 FY26) versus Akums' own double-digit-to-25%+ CDMO volume growth same period — genuine share gain or one-off/restocking? Check: INNOVACAP, COHANCE, WINDLAS.

**Verdict: PARTIALLY VERIFIED**

**Peer evidence:**

*IPM flat/low-volume-growth half of the claim — strongly corroborated:*
- WINDLAS management cites IQVIA/AIOCD-sourced IPM data every quarter of FY26 and it tracks Akums' framing almost exactly: Q1 FY26 "Indian pharmaceutical market registered a Y-o-Y growth of 9% in Q1 FY26 with a modest volume growth of 1%" (WINDLAS, Q1 FY26 call, Aug 13 2025, Hitesh Windlass). Q2 FY26: "registered a year-on-year growth of 7.7%... with volume decline of 0.2%" (WINDLAS, Q2 FY26 call, Nov 7 2025, Hitesh Windlass) — volume actually negative. Q3 FY26: "grew 11.8% Y-o-Y... with volume growth of 1.6%" (WINDLAS, Q3 FY26 call, Feb 6 2026, Hitesh Windlass). FY26 full year: "Indian pharma market registered a Y-o-Y volume growth of 2.7% in FY26" (WINDLAS, Q4 FY26 call, May 22 2026, Hitesh Windlass).

*Akums-style CDMO volume surge — only partially corroborated, and directly contradicted in one case:*
- WINDLAS' own Generic Formulations CDMO vertical grew well ahead of IPM every quarter — 17.8% YoY (Q1 FY26, "mostly volume-driven," Komal Gupta), 18% YoY (Q2 FY26), 23% YoY (Q3 FY26), 20% YoY (FY26 full year) — and management repeatedly says this is "primarily volume oriented... we are a cost-plus business and API prices are not increasing" (WINDLAS, Q3 FY26 call, Feb 6 2026, Hitesh Windlass). This is directionally consistent with Akums' claim of a genuine divergence between muted IPM and elevated CDMO volume growth for organized players, but the magnitude (18-23%) sits below Akums' claimed 25%+.
- INNOVACAP's headline CDMO revenue growth was strong (15% Q2 FY26, 29% Q3 FY26, 24% FY26 full year), but management's own disaggregation shows the *pure organic volume* component is much smaller: "our volume growth was around 8% to 10%" ex-Jammu (INNOVACAP, Q2 FY26 call, Nov 10 2025, Lokesh Bhasin) and "our volume growth is around 6% to 10%" (INNOVACAP, Q3 FY26 call, Jan 27 2026, Lokesh Bhasin). A large share of Innovacap's headline growth instead comes from the new Jammu facility ramp-up (INR60cr to INR89cr to INR300cr FY26) and the Sharon Bio acquisition — i.e., capacity/M&A-driven, not a broad organic volume surge.
- COHANCE directly contradicts the premise of an industry-wide 25%+ CDMO volume surge. Its Pharma CDMO revenue *declined* through FY26: Q2 FY26 revenue -8% YoY reported (+14% adjusted for restocking) (COHANCE, Q2 FY26 call, Nov 12 2025, Himanshu Agarwal); Q3 FY26 Pharma CDMO revenue -27% YoY reported (+7% adjusted for destocking) (COHANCE, Q3 FY26 call, Feb 12 2026, Yann D'Herve); and for full FY26, "Adjusting for the destocking impact in the two large commercial molecules, the business delivered an underlying growth of early single digit" (COHANCE, Q4 FY26 call, May 12 2026, Yann D'Herve). Cohance's FY26 revenue guidance was revised down to an outright decline over the year.

**Peers silent:** None of the three checked peers is silent on this topic; all three discuss both IPM/market volume trends and their own CDMO growth composition in detail.

**Net read:** The IPM-flat-to-low-single-digit half of Akums' claim is well corroborated — Windlas' quarterly IQVIA-sourced data lines up almost exactly with Akums' "flat to 1-1.5%" framing. But the "genuine, industry-wide 25%+ CDMO volume surge" half is not corroborated at Akums' scale: Windlas (18-23%, mostly volume) and Innovacap (6-10% pure organic volume, rest from facility ramp/M&A) both show real but materially smaller organic CDMO volume growth than Akums claims, and Cohance shows CDMO revenue outright declining/flat. The peer set suggests organized-player share gain against a weak IPM backdrop is real but Akums' magnitude looks company-specific rather than a uniform sector phenomenon.

### Claim 2
**Claim (verbatim from handoff):** Top-200 API basket prices down ~8% YoY (Q2 FY26) and ~20-25% over FY26, with a single-high-digit early-FY27 rebound blamed on Middle East-linked crude/solvent inflation — corroborate this raw-material trend? Check: PPLPHARMA, COHANCE, INNOVACAP.

**Verdict: VERIFIED** (direction and rough magnitude/timing; precise "top-200 basket" percentage not independently replicated)

**Peer evidence:**
- PPLPHARMA: the file provided (`PPLPHARMA-Concall_Oct_2025_Transcript.pdf`) is **not** a Piramal Pharma Solutions transcript. It is Piramal **Finance** Limited's Q2 FY26 earnings call (Oct 17, 2025) — an NBFC discussing AUM, mortgages, wholesale lending and gold loans. It contains zero pharma/API/CDMO content and cannot be used to verify this claim. See Part 3 for full note.
- INNOVACAP corroborates both the decline phase and the timing/cause of the rebound. Decline phase: "negative price range of 10% to 12%" on CDMO manufacturing (ex-Jammu, Baddi) in Q2 FY26 (INNOVACAP, Q2 FY26 call, Nov 10 2025, Lokesh Bhasin); "year-on-year, if you see, then there is a negative on the prices... We are even losing on the price front" as late as Q3 FY26 (INNOVACAP, Q3 FY26 call, Jan 27 2026, Vinay Lohariwala). Rebound phase, matching Akums' Middle East framing almost verbatim: "due to this going on conflict, there is certain uptick in prices of our raw materials and major ingredients... largely, those increase has been passed to our customers" and on cephalosporin API specifically, "our cephalosporin prices have reduced during quarter 2 and quarter 3... started stabilizing in first half of quarter 4 before this conflict plays out... yes, there is an increase in the prices [April YTD]" (INNOVACAP, Q4 FY26 call, May 8 2026, Lokesh Bhasin and Vinay Lohariwala).
- COHANCE independently corroborates the same Middle East-linked timing and mechanism: "Towards the end of Q4, uncertainties in the Middle East region led to escalation in logistics and freight cost, along with selective inflation in certain raw materials and key starting materials. While Q4 did not witness any material impact from this situation, Q1 will experience impact of nearly 100 to 150 bps on... gross margin levels, largely on account of API Plus business. We are under discussion with some of our customers to share part of this cost inflation" (COHANCE, Q4 FY26 call, May 12 2026, Himanshu Agarwal).
- WINDLAS (not on the original check-list but directly relevant) adds a third independent confirmation: "on the API side... there have been supply disruptions and price increases based on the West Asia crisis, the fuel price surge... API prices are increasing," while noting Windlas' own exposure is muted because it is "not an API-centric manufacturing... very little solvent usage" (WINDLAS, Q4 FY26 call, May 22 2026, Hitesh Windlass).

**Peers silent:** PPLPHARMA effectively silent (wrong company in the file).

**Net read:** Two independent, on-topic peers (Cohance and Innovacap) — plus a third corroborating data point from Windlas — confirm both legs of Akums' claim: an FY26 API/raw-material price decline followed by a Middle East-crisis-linked cost rebound landing in Q4 FY26/Q1 FY27. The precise "top-200 basket down 8% / 20-25%" figures are Akums-specific framing that no peer replicates verbatim, but Innovacap's disclosed 10-12% CDMO price decline is in the same order of magnitude, and the causal narrative (Middle East crude/logistics/solvent inflation, early-FY27 timing) is corroborated almost word-for-word by two separate managements.

### Claim 3
**Claim (verbatim from handoff):** Akums speculates the CDMO volume surge may reflect share shift from smaller/non-compliant manufacturers ahead of Schedule M enforcement, without confirming — do peers report similar acceleration? Check: INNOVACAP, WINDLAS.

**Verdict: PARTIALLY VERIFIED**

**Peer evidence:**
- WINDLAS provides the most direct evidence in the peer set. When asked explicitly why Windlas' CDMO growth is running "much higher than industry volume growth," CEO/CFO Komal Gupta and MD Hitesh Windlass answered: "it's a combination of the things that you mentioned, schedule-M compliance, better delivery, on-time delivery to customers, greater business development team and alignment of that team... hard to break it up into any of these" (WINDLAS, Q3 FY26 call, Feb 6 2026, Hitesh Windlass, in response to Avnish Tiwari). This is a direct — though explicitly multi-causal and unquantified — management confirmation that Schedule M compliance is contributing to organized-player CDMO growth outperformance, echoing Akums' own speculation almost exactly.
- WINDLAS also confirms the structural thesis over multiple quarters: "this was one of our important thesis... given the very highly fragmented nature of the contract manufacturer industry... increasing quality pressures, we definitely expected it should lead to more benefit towards organized players" (WINDLAS, Q1 FY26 call, Aug 13 2025, Hitesh Windlass), and later confirms escalating enforcement intensity: "the government is very serious. Regulator is very serious. A lot of inspections have been done. Some plants have been given observations and closures" and "no more extensions were granted [for MSMEs]. Now everybody is expected to comply" (WINDLAS, Q3 FY26 call, Feb 6 2026, Hitesh Windlass).
- INNOVACAP was asked the same question twice and gave materially weaker, non-committal answers both times. Q2 FY26: asked directly whether Schedule M compliance pressure is "helping our growth," Vinay Lohariwala replied only in general terms about regulatory maturity ("every regulator and industry gets mature... those companies who are complying with the standards... will see a bright and better future") without confirming or quantifying any acceleration (INNOVACAP, Q2 FY26 call, Nov 10 2025). Q4 FY26: asked again, "do you see like crack down on facilities which are not compliant? And is it like helping our growth?" — the answer again stayed at the level of general compliance posture ("our all facilities are well compliant... definitely, those who are complying will be in an advantage phase") without confirming a specific share-shift or acceleration (INNOVACAP, Q4 FY26 call, May 8 2026, Vinay Lohariwala).

**Peers silent:** COHANCE (not on the original check-list for this claim) does not discuss Schedule M at all in any of its four transcripts.

**Net read:** One peer (Windlas) directly and repeatedly corroborates the qualitative direction of Akums' speculation — Schedule M compliance is explicitly named by management as one driver of above-market CDMO growth — but even Windlas declines to isolate or quantify Schedule M's specific contribution versus execution/BD factors. The second peer (Innovacap) is evasive both times it is asked the identical question directly, offering only generic "compliant players benefit" language with no confirmation of acceleration. This matches Akums' own posture of speculating without confirming — the peer set neither proves nor disproves a Schedule M-driven share shift, but Windlas' repeated, unprompted linkage to its own CDMO outperformance is the strongest available corroboration.

### Claim 4
**Claim (verbatim from handoff):** Akums is capex-cycling (FY26 INR222cr actual, FY27 INR300cr target, plus US$45m Zambia JV and EUR35m/yr European contract) — is the broader CDMO sector in a similar capacity-expansion cycle? Check: INNOVACAP, COHANCE.

**Verdict: VERIFIED** (domestic capacity-expansion dimension only) / **UNVERIFIABLE** (international dimension)

**Peer evidence:**
- COHANCE's capex trajectory numerically mirrors Akums almost exactly: "Capex during the year [FY26] was INR2.15 billion [~INR215cr]... We expect capex spend of nearly INR3 billion [~INR300cr] in FY27" (COHANCE, Q4 FY26 call, May 12 2026, Himanshu Agarwal). Both the FY26 actual (~INR215cr vs Akums' INR222cr) and the FY27 target (~INR300cr, identical to Akums') land in the same range, though Cohance's capex is directed at ADC/oligonucleotide niche-technology capability build-out rather than general CDMO/API volume capacity.
- INNOVACAP confirms an active, ongoing capacity-expansion posture, though at a somewhat smaller incremental scale for FY27: cumulative Jammu facility investment of "around INR480 crores" (largely already spent, ramping toward INR1,400cr peak revenue potential) (INNOVACAP, Q2 FY26 call, Nov 10 2025, Lokesh Bhasin), plus a newly disclosed Baddi land acquisition for a general-block expansion with "overall capital outlay... in the range of INR150 crores, INR170 crores" targeted for FY27/FY28 (INNOVACAP, Q4 FY26 call, May 8 2026, Vinay Lohariwala/Lokesh Bhasin).
- WINDLAS (supplementary) is the most conservative of the three: Plant 6 refurbishment capex of INR40-50cr (later revised to INR50-60cr), and management explicitly frames its philosophy as staged and need-based — "we do not want to do a capex which is like 5 years kind of... creating capacity for the next 5 years. We want to do it more like 1.5 to 2.5 years kind of chunk" (WINDLAS, Q3/Q4 FY26 calls, Komal Gupta).
- On the international dimension specifically: none of the three peers discloses anything resembling Akums' Zambia JV or European supply contract. Windlas explicitly states "we have 0 sales to U.S. and 0 sales to Europe" (WINDLAS, Q3 FY26 call, Feb 6 2026, Hitesh Windlass); Innovacap and Cohance's international exposure is limited to export sales and (for Cohance) an existing US subsidiary (NJ Bio) rather than new overseas manufacturing JVs or long-term supply contracts of the kind Akums describes.

**Peers silent:** None of the checked peers discusses anything resembling an international manufacturing JV or a multi-year European supply contract of Akums' scale — this silence is itself informative (see Part 2C).

**Net read:** This is not a lone-expander situation domestically — both Cohance and Innovacap are actively capex-cycling in FY26-FY27, and Cohance's numbers (~INR215cr FY26 actual, ~INR300cr FY27 target) are close enough to Akums' own figures to suggest a genuine sector-wide capacity cycle rather than an Akums-specific outlier. However, Akums' international ambition (Zambia JV, European contract) has no peer analogue in this data set — none of the three domestic-focused peers is pursuing comparable overseas manufacturing commitments, so that specific piece of Akums' capex story sits outside what peer transcripts can confirm or deny.

## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

**2A Demand environment:** The peer set is split, which complicates rather than confirms a uniform "CDMO demand boom" framing. Windlas confirms a muted underlying IPM (flat-to-2.7% volume across FY26) but strong, largely volume-driven outperformance of its own CDMO book. Cohance's FY26 was explicitly framed as a "transition year" dominated by destocking, biotech-funding-winter effects on its NJ Bio subsidiary, and a Nacharam plant disruption — its Pharma CDMO revenue declined or grew only low-single-digit on an underlying basis, the opposite of a boom. Innovacap shows the strongest headline growth (up to 42% YoY by Q4 FY26) but management's own breakdown attributes much of it to the Jammu facility ramp-up and the Sharon Bio acquisition rather than organic demand — i.e., execution/capacity-driven rather than a pure market-wide demand surge. Read together, the peer set does not support a simple "industry-wide CDMO demand boom" story; it supports a bifurcated picture where compliant, capacity-adding, well-executing players are pulling ahead of a flat market, with considerable company-specific variance in how much of that gain is organic volume versus mix/M&A/facility ramp.

**2B Pricing and input costs:** Strong three-way consensus (Cohance, Innovacap, Windlas) that raw-material/API prices declined through most of FY26 and then began rising from Q4 FY26 into Q1 FY27 specifically because of Middle East/West Asia-linked crude, logistics and solvent cost inflation. This matches Akums' cost-plus CDMO/API margin narrative directionally and in rough timing. Cohance explicitly quantified an incoming 100-150bps FY27 gross-margin headwind from this; Innovacap confirmed pass-through to customers on a cost-plus basis; Windlas confirmed the same trend with a caveat that its own low solvent/API-centricity means muted impact. This is the best-corroborated of the four claims.

**2C Capex cycle:** Not a lone-expander situation — Cohance and Innovacap are both actively expanding capacity into FY27, and Cohance's capex trajectory is numerically close to Akums'. This reads as a genuine, if uneven, sector-wide capacity-expansion cycle among organized Indian CDMO/API players, consistent with anticipated Schedule M-driven consolidation (see 2A/Claim 3). The one dimension of Akums' capex story with no peer echo is its international manufacturing/supply ambition (Zambia JV, European contract) — all three peers remain domestic-and-export-focused with no comparable overseas commitments disclosed.

**2D Competitive mentions:** No peer transcript across all 12 files names Akums or refers to it by an unmistakable description. Given Akums is India's largest listed pure-play CDMO, this silence is itself informative — none of Cohance, Innovacap or Windlas frames Akums as a direct competitive reference point on these calls, which neither confirms nor undercuts Akums' narrative but means no peer-sourced competitive read (positive or negative) is available.

**2E Risks peers discuss that Akums' claim set does not:**
- **US FDA quality/compliance risk at a peer CDMO/API site:** Cohance's Nacharam FDF facility moved from OAI status to a formal Warning Letter during FY26, with an approximately INR55cr revenue impact and an ongoing remediation program involving external US FDA consultants (COHANCE, Q2/Q3 FY26 calls). This is a live reminder that USFDA enforcement risk is not hypothetical for Indian CDMO/API manufacturers generally.
- **Leadership/governance instability:** Cohance saw two successive senior leadership departures (Dr. Sudhir Singh, then MD Dr. Prasada Raju) within FY26, prompting a sharp investor rebuke on an earnings call ("the execution has clearly derailed... I have not never seen this level of disarray" — Varun Bang, COHANCE Q2 FY26 call, Nov 12 2025) and a further CEO change (Umang Vohra joining as Group CEO) by the Q4 FY26 call. A reminder that management-continuity risk is a live theme among listed Indian CDMO peers.
- **US biotech-funding-cycle exposure:** Cohance's NJ Bio (US ADC/bioconjugation subsidiary) was repeatedly hit by a "biotech funding winter," with customer order deferrals tied to reduced NIH funding flowing through to biotech clients — a read-across risk for any CDMO with exposure to venture/biotech-funded (rather than large-pharma) demand.
- **Product-specific regulatory market exit:** Windlas' Trade Generics vertical was hit by the exit of codeine-based cough syrups from the Indian market (including two large MNC brands) following patient-level misuse/monitoring concerns, an approximately INR50-60cr annualized impact — illustrating that abrupt, drug-specific regulatory actions are a real risk in the Indian generics/OTC space.
- **GST-linked incentive erosion:** Innovacap's Jammu facility GST incentive was cut from 12% to 5% reimbursement following a GST rate rationalization, a direct, quantified fiscal-policy risk for any manufacturer relying on area-based GST incentives in Northern India (a category that could plausibly extend to other Northern-India manufacturers, Akums included, though this was not independently verified against Akums' own filings in this exercise).

## PART 4: TRIANGULATION SUMMARY

- **Claims verified:** 2 of 4 (Claim 2 — API pricing/Middle East cost trend; Claim 4 — domestic capacity-expansion cycle). Both remaining claims (1 and 3) are PARTIALLY VERIFIED, not fully verified or contradicted outright.
- **Claims contradicted:** 0 of 4 outright, but one important sub-finding under Claim 1 is a direct contradiction: Cohance's Pharma CDMO revenue declined/grew only low-single-digit on an underlying basis through FY26, directly contradicting the notion of an *industry-wide* 25%+ CDMO volume surge (as opposed to an Akums-specific one). This is flagged as a priority item for synthesis.
- **The single most consequential contradiction:** Cohance — a scaled, listed Indian CDMO/API peer — reported Pharma CDMO revenue *declining* 27% YoY in Q3 FY26 (+7% adjusted for destocking) and only "early single digit" underlying growth for full FY26, in the same window Akums claims 25%+ CDMO volume growth. If the CDMO volume surge were a broad, Schedule M-driven, industry-wide phenomenon, Cohance should be showing some of the same lift; instead it shows the opposite. This does not disprove Akums' own growth, but it substantially weakens the "genuine, industry-wide share gain" framing and points toward a more company-specific (or at minimum, highly uneven across peers) explanation.
- **The single strongest independent confirmation:** The Middle East-linked raw-material cost narrative (Claim 2) is corroborated almost word-for-word by two independent managements — Cohance ("uncertainties in the Middle East region led to escalation in logistics and freight cost, along with selective inflation in certain raw materials and key starting materials... Q1 will experience impact of nearly 100 to 150 bps") and Innovacap ("due to this going on conflict, there is certain uptick in prices of our raw materials and major ingredients... largely, those increase has been passed to our customers"), with Windlas adding a third confirming data point. This is the cleanest, most reliable piece of triangulated evidence in this exercise.
- **Overall:** The peer set complicates more than it confirms Akums' management narrative. It clearly supports the macro backdrop Akums cites — a flat-to-low-single-digit IPM, and an FY26 API-price decline followed by a Middle East-driven early-FY27 cost rebound — and it confirms the broader Indian CDMO/API sector is genuinely capex-cycling in a similar timeframe and (in Cohance's case) a similar magnitude to Akums. But on the two claims most central to explaining Akums' outsized FY26 earnings beat — the scale of the CDMO volume surge and its attribution to Schedule M-driven share shift — peer evidence is mixed at best and, in Cohance's case, points the other way. Windlas provides the closest analogue to Akums' story (organized-player CDMO outperformance explicitly linked in part to Schedule M compliance) but at roughly 18-23% growth versus Akums' 25%+, and Innovacap's organic (non-M&A, non-new-facility) CDMO volume growth of only 6-10% is well below Akums' claimed range. Symmetrically: nothing here proves Akums is manufacturing its growth, but nothing here proves the growth is simply "the whole sector is doing this too" either — the peer evidence leaves the single largest unexplained driver behind Akums' FY26 beat only partially explained by industry-wide forces, with a meaningful residual that peers cannot account for.
