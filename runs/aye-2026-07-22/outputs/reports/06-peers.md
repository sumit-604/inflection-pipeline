Overall: directional consensus on improving demand, split on how quickly it converts to approvable/eligible volume.

**2B Pricing and input costs (yield/spread equivalent for lenders).** No peer reports margin compression from competitive pricing pressure that would contradict AYE's NIM narrative. MASFIN's opex increase is "well compensated by our increase in yields" (MASFIN Nov-2025 call, Kamlesh Gandhi, p.13). Northern Arc's NIM improved 25bp YoY to 9.4% for FY26 on funding-cost tailwinds (NORTHARC May-2026 call, p.5). Consistent with (supportive of) AYE's declining-cost-of-borrowing/NIM story; no peer signals sector-wide spread compression.

**2C Capex cycle (distribution/branch build-out equivalent).** All three peers are actively expanding distribution in the same window: MASFIN targeting 20-25 new branches/year on top of 15,000+ pin-code reach already (MASFIN Feb-2026 Investor Day, Darshana Pandya, p.11-12); Northern Arc added 64 rural-finance branches in FY26, taking its rural network to ~342 (NORTHARC May-2026 call, p.3); SBFC guided 20-25 new branches/year and added them steadily through the window (SBFC Nov-2025 call, Mahesh Dayani, p.11). This is an **industry-wide distribution build-out, not a lone-expander situation** — AYE's mortgage-team headcount investment (Trigger 5, B05) sits inside a genuine sector-wide capacity race rather than standing out as unusual capital allocation. This somewhat de-risks the "lone expander" read but also means AYE's distribution investment is not obviously buying it a differentiated edge versus peers investing at a comparable or greater pace.

**2D Competitive mentions.** None. No peer transcript names AYE, Aye Finance, or gives an unmistakable description of it, across all 11 transcripts searched.

**2E Risks peers discuss that AYE's own concall/B05 record does not surface.**

## PART 4: TRIANGULATION SUMMARY

- **Claims verified:** 1 of 7 (Q5 — cost-of-borrowing decline)
- **Claims partially verified:** 4 of 7 (Q1, Q2, Q4, Q6-TAM-context)
- **Claims contradicted (directionally):** 2 of 7 (Q3 — Bihar-insulation framing, using the Karnataka
  analogue; Q7 — sub-2% mortgage credit-cost aspiration, using SBFC's micro-ticket secured
  commentary)
- **Claims unverifiable at the precise-number level:** Q3 (Bihar specifically), Q6 (67m TAM figure
  specifically), Q7 (precise micro-LAP credit-cost percentage)

**The single most consequential contradiction:** SBFC's account of the Karnataka MFI ordinance —
"the ordinance, which had nothing to do with us, brought our collection numbers down sharply,"
with damage taking a quarter and repair taking four more, and management explicitly arguing the
contagion channel is "not about geography... more about ticket sizes" — directly undercuts AYE's
implicit framing that its 15.5% Bihar AUM concentration can stay insulated from a state-level MFI
regulatory shock. This is the closest available peer analogue to AYE's own regulatory-containment
claim (Trigger 7 / Flag 3 in B05), and it points the other way: contagion travelling through
borrower leverage and money velocity, not staying geographically or product-contained, with a
year-plus healing cycle for the peer that experienced it.

**The single strongest independent confirmation:** all three rated peers show materially declining
cost of borrowing over the same window (roughly 35-90bp, MASFIN/Northern Arc/SBFC each
independently), driven by system-wide repo transmission and liquidity easing. This is a clean,
three-for-three corroboration that AYE's funding-cost tailwind (Trigger 6 in B05) is a genuine
sector-wide dynamic, not solely a function of AYE's own post-IPO capitalisation or rating story.

**Overall:** the peer set neither cleanly supports nor cleanly undercuts AYE's narrative — it
complicates it. The funding-cost tailwind (Q5) and, more loosely, the general demand-improvement
backdrop (2A) and industry-wide distribution build-out (2C) are genuinely sector-wide and
corroborate parts of AYE's story. But the two claims most load-bearing for AYE's near-term
thesis — a clean, sector-wide credit-cost normalisation (Q1) and a contained, geographically
insulated regulatory shock (Q3) — are not well supported by the peer set: credit-cost trends are
split three ways across peers rather than uniformly improving, and the closest available regulatory
analogue (Karnataka) shows deeper, longer, and less geographically contained spillover than AYE's
own framing implies. The approval-rate reversal timing (Q2) is similarly split, with one peer
already recovering and another still worsening in the same window. Symmetrically: none of this
proves AYE's own numbers wrong (peers operate in adjacent, not identical, segments, and AYE's
anchored asset-quality ratios did genuinely improve in the two anchored post-call filings per
B05), but the peer evidence does not independently validate AYE's "sector tailwind" framing for
its most consequential near-term triggers, and it surfaces one real, evidenced downside risk
(regulatory-shock contagion beyond the directly affected state/product) that AYE's own record does
not address.

## PART 5: CROSS-PEER HYPOTHESIS

Across the three peers, credit-cost and approval-rate trajectories in the same FY26 window are
inversely related to **secured-collateral intensity and average ticket size**, not to sector
membership as such. Northern Arc's most-secured/most-institutional segments (LAP, rural finance
with CGFMU credit-guarantee cover) are the fastest to recover; MASFIN, a mixed secured/unsecured
mid-ticket lender, is flat; and SBFC — nominally the "safest" peer on paper (100% collateralised
by property and gold, no unsecured book at all) — has the *worst* trajectory of the three, with
credit cost still rising and approval rates still falling through the most recent quarter
collected. The common thread SBFC itself names is ticket size, not collateral type: its own CIBIL
data shows industry-wide MSME NPAs *improving* above Rs50 lakh and *deteriorating* below Rs10
lakh over the same year. This suggests the sector is not bifurcating cleanly along the
secured-versus-unsecured axis that headline asset-quality disclosures imply, but along a
ticket-size/borrower-density axis that cuts across collateral status — the smallest-ticket segment
of every lender in this peer set, secured or not, is the one still under stress in mid-FY26,
regardless of how "safe" that lender's book looks on a collateral basis. If this holds, AYE's own
book — concentrated at the small end of the ticket-size spectrum by design — sits in the segment
that is, on this peer evidence, still the last to normalise across the sector, which is a
materially different read than "AYE is riding a sector-wide MSME recovery." This is inferable only
from combining SBFC's own segment-and-ticket-size breakdown with Northern Arc's segment-level
recovery data and MASFIN's flat aggregate number — no single peer states this cross-cutting pattern
itself.
