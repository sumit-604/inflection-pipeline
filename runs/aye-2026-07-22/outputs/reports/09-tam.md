# STAGE 9 — TAM / SAM / SOM MARKET SIZING
## Aye Finance Limited (AYE) | Run date: 2026-07-22 | Mode: full (no prior run)

## SECTION 1: MARKET DEFINITION

### 1A. Precise boundaries

**Product scope.** Formal credit to informal micro and small enterprises via three
product lines Aye itself offers: secured hypothecation loans (movable/working-asset
collateral, avg ticket ~₹1.5 lakh), secured mortgage/LAP loans ("Saral" property
loans, avg ticket ~₹4.9 lakh), and unsecured business/hypothecation loans (Q1FY27
mix: hypothecation 76.7%, LAP/mortgage 21.8%, Saral 1.5% — operator digest,
corroborated by results filing 2246e44a per digest cross-check note). CRISIL's own
taxonomy (Prospectus p.184-196/614) buckets this as "MSME loans with ticket size
less than ₹1.0 million" across its Secured MSME, MSME Hypothecation and Unsecured
MSME categories — this ticket-size cut is used throughout Section 2 as the
scoping proxy because it is the only ticket-level breakdown CRISIL discloses and it
brackets Aye's actual average ticket sizes on both products.

**Geographic scope.** India only (no cross-border operations). Aye operates 571
branches across 18 states + 3 union territories = 21 states/UTs (operator digest,
Q1FY27 deck) of India's 36 states/UTs — i.e. national in intent but not yet in
footprint, and management has explicitly said it intends to **slow** further branch/
geography expansion, having reached "adequate geographic diversification"
(Prospectus p.237/614, cited in B07 §2D). Within states served, Aye is semi-urban/
rural weighted (branch-based, cluster-underwriting model).

**Customer scope.** Micro-scale enterprises (98% of India's ~57.7 million MSMEs by
Ministry of MSME's own count, Annual Report 2024-25, cited at Prospectus p.181/614)
with disclosed target annual turnover of ₹20 lakh to ₹1 crore (Prospectus p.26/614,
"Primary business of our Company"), operating in manufacturing, trading, services
and allied agriculture, predominantly thin-file / new-to-credit borrowers.

**Channel scope.** Branch-based direct origination with cluster-based underwriting
(32% AI/ML-assisted, 68% cluster-based per Q3FY26 deck) plus 100% paperless
origination / 100% cashless disbursement (operator digest, anchored via collected
Q3FY26 concall). Excludes pure-digital-lending, DSA-only and supply-chain-finance
channels at any scale (SwitchPe, Aye's own SCF platform, is immaterial — ₹11.14mn
NII in H1FY26 per B07 §"active_categories" evidence, vs ₹490cr quarterly total
income).

**Price segment.** Sub-₹10 lakh ticket business credit, i.e. the smallest, most
underserved end of the MSME lending spectrum — explicitly NOT the larger-ticket LAP
or working-capital-line segment banks and larger NBFCs compete for.

**Explicit inclusions.** Formal (bureau-reportable) secured and unsecured business
credit to micro-enterprises, sub-₹1 million ticket bracket, delivered by any lender
type (banks, NBFCs, SFBs) — because Aye's realistic competitive set spans all these,
not NBFCs alone.

**Explicit exclusions.** Medium/large enterprise credit, working-capital/trade
finance ticket sizes above ~₹25 lakh, consumer/retail non-business credit (personal
loans, housing loans, vehicle loans), equity/quasi-equity MSME finance, and
international MSME lending markets.

### 1B. Management's own TAM claim

The Prospectus contains a section literally titled **"Growth Runway and Large
Target Addressable Market ('TAM')"** (Prospectus p.225/614, "Our Business"): "In
India, 98% of MSMEs are classified as micro enterprises, highlighting a substantial
TAM for financial services... As of Fiscal 2025, MSME credit demand is estimated to
be around ₹159 trillion, of which 27% to 28% of demand is met through formal
financing... total addressable credit demand is estimated at approximately ₹76
trillion, out of which current formal financing stands at approximately ₹42
trillion taking the total addressable MSME credit gap to around ₹34 trillion, which
needs to be met by financial institutions." (Source cited throughout: CRISIL
Report, "Report on Loans and Financial Services Industry in India," dated
November 2025, exclusively commissioned and paid for by Aye — Prospectus
p.223/614.) The identical ₹103tn/₹76tn/₹34tn framing also appears in the Prospectus
Summary (p.27/614, printed p.22).

**Definition used by management**: the entire India-wide MSME formal-credit gap,
across all ticket sizes, all enterprise sizes (micro/small/medium) and all lender
types — i.e. not scoped to Aye's own product, ticket, geography or customer
boundaries at all. This is a whole-of-industry number presented as Aye's "growth
runway," not a company-specific SAM.

**Independent corroboration (web, this stage)**: SIDBI's own report ("Understanding
Indian MSME Sector: Progress and Challenges," May 2025, not commissioned by Aye)
independently pegs the MSME credit gap at **~₹30 lakh crore** (₹30 trillion), out of
total debt demand of ₹92 lakh crore — a different methodology but the same order of
magnitude as CRISIL's ₹34tn. Northern Arc Capital's own investor material
(multibagg.ai / company disclosures, FY26) separately cites "an addressable credit
gap of nearly 24%... about ₹30 lakh crore." Three independent sources (CRISIL/Aye,
SIDBI, Northern Arc) converge on ₹30-34 lakh crore for the national headline
number, which raises confidence in the scale of the *unscoped* figure even though it
remains far broader than Aye's addressable market. By contrast, the RBI's own U.K.
Sinha Committee (2019) pegged the gap at ₹20-25 lakh crore — **STALE** (7 years old,
informs direction only: the gap estimate has grown roughly 20-40% in nominal terms
over that period, consistent with nominal MSME-credit-demand growth, not treated as
a headline number here).

**Credibility read: broad.** The ₹34tn figure is directionally corroborated and not
implausible for India as a whole, but it is not Aye's addressable market by any
reasonable product/geography/customer scoping — see the >2x divergence from the
conservative TAM estimate in Section 2's triangulation table, and the explicit
mgmt_claim_ratio computed there.

## SECTION 2: TAM ESTIMATION, MULTIPLE METHODS

All figures ₹ Crore unless noted. India-only market (no global comparison
required).

### Method 1 — Top-down (CRISIL industry gap, ticket-scoped)

Starting point: CRISIL's own FY25 addressable-demand/gap decomposition (Prospectus
p.183/614, current — Nov-2025 report, FY25 data, **not stale**):
- Total addressable MSME credit demand, FY25 = ₹76,00,000cr (₹76 trillion)
- Current formal financing = ₹42,00,000cr
- **Total addressable MSME credit gap, FY25 = ₹34,00,000cr**

This is national, all-ticket-size, all-enterprise-size. To scope it to Aye's actual
product/ticket range, apply CRISIL's own disclosed ticket-size proxy: "the majority
of micro scale businesses require loans with a ticket size of less than ₹1.0
million, representing approximately **12%** of overall MSME credit outstanding
(reported to Commercial bureau)" (Prospectus p.184/614). No demand-side (as opposed
to outstanding-credit-side) breakdown by ticket size was found in the extracted
material — **NOT FOUND** — so the 12% outstanding-credit share is used as an
analyst proxy for the demand-side split too. This is the single approximation step
in Method 1; it is flagged as the main source of estimation risk in this method.

- Ticket-scoped addressable demand = 12% × ₹76,00,000cr = **₹9,12,000cr**
- Ticket-scoped addressable gap = 12% × ₹34,00,000cr = **₹4,08,000cr**

Confidence: **M** (CRISIL-direct on both base numbers; one analyst proxy step for
ticket-scoping).

### Method 2 — Bottom-up (unit economics)

- Total micro-enterprises in India ≈ 56.5 million (98% of 57.7 million MSMEs,
  Ministry of MSME Annual Report 2024-25 via CRISIL, Prospectus p.181/614).
- Share of these enterprises inside Aye's disclosed target turnover band (₹20
  lakh-₹1 crore annual turnover, Prospectus p.26/614) that are realistically
  formal-credit-addressable: **NOT FOUND** in any collected source. Applied
  estimate, clearly labelled: **25%** (≈14.1 million enterprises) — a judgement
  call reflecting that most of the 56.5 million are sub-scale/subsistence and not
  realistically bankable even under full formalisation.
- Blended average ticket size, weighted by Aye's own Q1FY27 product mix
  (hypothecation 76.7% @ ₹1.5L, LAP/mortgage 21.8% @ ₹4.9L, Saral 1.5% @ ~₹2L est.
  — operator digest, non-anchored, cross-checked against results filing per digest
  provenance note): ≈ **₹2.25 lakh**.
- Full-penetration bottom-up TAM = 14.125 million × ₹2,25,000 = **₹3,17,800cr**

Confidence: **L** (two stacked, unsourced assumptions — the 25% addressability
share and the digest-sourced blended ticket size — versus Method 1's single proxy
step).

### Method 3 — Peer aggregation + unorganised-sector estimate

Current organised-NBFC book in Aye's exact ticket bracket = ₹53,500cr as of FY25
("MSME credit for NBFCs with smaller ticket size of less than ₹1.0 million reached
₹535 billion as of Fiscal 2025," Prospectus p.184/614), corroborated by summing the
six CRISIL-named peer AUMs as of H1FY26 (Prospectus p.206/614): Aye ₹6,028cr +
Five-Star ₹12,847cr + SBFC ₹9,938cr + Veritas ₹7,746cr + Vistaar ₹5,047cr + Finova
₹3,554cr = **₹45,159cr**. Adding two web-sourced peers outside CRISIL's named set —
MAS Financial (₹15,304cr consolidated AUM, Mar-2026) and Northern Arc's MSME-book
specifically (₹3,691cr, Mar-2026, +43% YoY) — brings the broader organised
competitive set to **≈₹64,150cr**, still well short of Method 1/2's TAM range,
confirming the organised-NBFC layer is a small fraction of the addressable pool.

Grossing this up: formal institutions meet only ~27-28% of total MSME credit
demand nationally (CRISIL, Prospectus p.181/614). Applying that same formal-ratio
to the ticket-scoped *outstanding* credit base (₹3,66,000cr = 12% × ₹30,50,000cr
overall MSME credit outstanding FY25, Prospectus p.184-185/614):

Total demand for the ticket-scoped segment ≈ ₹3,66,000cr ÷ 0.275 ≈ **₹13,31,000cr**

This is the highest of the three methods and is **flagged as likely inflated**: it
conflates CRISIL's own "total demand" (₹159tn, includes a large unbankable/informal
tail) with "addressable demand" (₹76tn, the financially-viable subset) — CRISIL's
own framework treats these as materially different (₹76tn is only ~48% of ₹159tn).
Used here for directional upper-bound context only, excluded from the conservative/
realistic average below.

### Triangulation table

| Method | Estimate (₹Cr) | Confidence | Staleness |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 — Top-down, ticket-scoped, gap-only | 4,08,000 | M | None (Nov-2025 CRISIL, FY25 data) |
| 1 — Top-down, ticket-scoped, total demand | 9,12,000 | M | None |
| 2 — Bottom-up, unit economics | 3,17,800 | L | Base data current; two unsourced assumption layers |
| 3 — Peer + unorganised gross-up | 13,31,000 | L | **Flagged inflated** — excluded from headline |

**Method divergence, explained, not averaged blindly**: Method 2 (₹3,17,800cr)
sits below Method 1's gap-only figure (₹4,08,000cr) despite both nominally scoping
to the same ticket bracket. This is not treated as the new conservative headline
because Method 2's gap comes entirely from a stacked, unsourced 25%-addressability
assumption — a genuinely lower-confidence number, not a "more conservative but
equally reliable" one. **Conservative TAM = ₹4,08,000cr** (Method 1, gap-only —
the CRISIL-direct, least-assumption-laden lower bound). **Realistic TAM =
₹9,12,000cr** (Method 1, total addressable demand). Method 2 is retained in the
report as a directional sanity check (same order of magnitude, ~78% of the
conservative figure) and Method 3 as a flagged upper bound only.

**Management's claim vs the conservative estimate**: ₹34,00,000cr (unscoped,
Section 1B) ÷ ₹4,08,000cr (conservative, ticket-scoped) = **8.3x**. Standard read:
>2x → **inflated**. This is the expected result of comparing a whole-of-India,
unscoped market-context number to a product/ticket-scoped company estimate — it is
not evidence Aye is overstating its own opportunity so much as evidence that the
Prospectus's "Growth Runway and Large TAM" framing (Section 1B) does the scoping
work that this stage does instead.

## SECTION 3: SAM & SOM

### 3A. SAM — five filters applied to conservative TAM (₹4,08,000cr)

Applied to the ticket-scoped Method-1 gap figure (₹4,08,000cr) rather than to the
bottom-up conservative headline (₹3,17,800cr), because Method 2 already embeds an
implicit customer-turnover-band assumption; using it as the SAM base here would
double-count that filter. Method 1's ₹4,08,000cr is national, all-turnover, all-
geography, all-channel — the correct un-filtered starting point for this cascade.

| Filter | Retention | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Product fit | 95% | Minor exclusion: supply-chain/invoice-finance-style products (SwitchPe-equivalent) not addressed by Aye's core hypothecation/LAP/unsecured line-up. Estimate. |
| Geography | 58% | 21 of 36 states/UTs served (571 branches, 18 states + 3 UTs, operator digest Q1FY27; "already PAN-India across 21 states/UTs," B07 §2D, Prospectus p.237/614). Ratio computed, not CRISIL-sourced. |
| Channel | 85% | Branch-based + cluster-underwriting model excludes purely digital-native / DSA-only originated volume. Estimate. |
| Customer (turnover band) | 70% | Target turnover ₹20L-1Cr (Prospectus p.26/614) is narrower than "all micro-enterprises needing sub-₹1mn credit." Exact proportion **NOT FOUND** in any collected source — labelled estimate. |
| Capability | 90% | Cluster-based underwriting requires mapped clusters; not all served-state semi-urban/rural pockets are cluster-mapped. Estimate. |

Combined retention = 0.95 × 0.58 × 0.85 × 0.70 × 0.90 = **29.5%**

**SAM = ₹4,08,000cr × 29.5% = ₹1,20,400cr**

### 3B. SOM at 3 and 5 years

Current Aye AUM (Q1FY27, 30-Jun-2026) = **₹7,324cr** (operator digest, corroborated
by the collected 2246e44a results filing per digest cross-check note).

Current share of SAM = ₹7,324cr ÷ ₹1,20,400cr = **6.1%**

Share-gain trajectory: informal/unmet credit is >70% of total MSME demand
nationally (100% − 27-28% formal-financing ratio, CRISIL, Prospectus p.181/614),
well above the 40% threshold at which the framework's share-gain rules permit
faster-than-normal gains. Execution evidence for the "aggressive" (3-5pp/3yr) band:
Aye posted the **highest** 2.5-year AUM CAGR (37.4%, FY23-H1FY26) among its six
named CRISIL peers (Prospectus p.206/614), and B07 computes ~84% capital-headroom
before the rating agency's own gearing sensitivity ceiling binds. No competitor
exit or acquisition evidenced, so the >5pp-in-3-years band is not used.

- **3-year**: +4pp (mid-aggressive) → share = 10.1% → **SOM_3yr = ₹12,140cr**
- **5-year**: +6pp cumulative (extending at a decelerating pace, consistent with
  S-curve share-gain logic — no >5pp-per-3yr rule violation on a 5-year base) →
  share = 12.1% → **SOM_5yr = ₹14,540cr**

Implied revenue CAGR (AUM used as the revenue-proxy base, since Aye's income is
loan-book-linked at a roughly stable yield — FY26 total revenue ÷ FY26 AUM ≈ 25.8%,
results p.6/26 & Investor Presentation slide 6; treated as a labelled
approximation, not a precise revenue build):

- 3yr: (12,140 ÷ 7,324)^(1/3) − 1 = **18.3%**
- 5yr: (14,540 ÷ 7,324)^(1/5) − 1 = **14.7%**

**Cross-check vs management's own aspiration**: management's medium-term AUM CAGR
aspiration is **~28-33%** (operator digest, NON-ANCHORED — Q3FY26 deck "medium-term
AUM aspiration ~30% CAGR"; Q4FY26 "3-yr vision: AUM CAGR 28-33%"). The SOM-implied
3yr CAGR (18.3%) is **materially below** this — management's own aspiration runs
roughly **1.5-1.8x faster** than the disciplined SAM/share-gain-rule ceiling used
here. Two readings, both plausible and neither provable from available evidence:
(a) management is guiding to share gains beyond even the "aggressive" 3-5pp/3yr
band, which the >70% informal-share formalisation tailwind could support but which
this stage's rules do not pre-authorise without more direct evidence; or (b)
management's guidance is optimistic relative to a conservatively-scoped SAM. This
is flagged for Stage 11 as the explicit handoff use of som_implied_revenue_cagr.

### 3C. Capacity cross-check

Using B07's capex-embedded-growth analog (B07.capex_embedded_growth_pct = **84%**
— capital-adequacy headroom to ICRA's own 4.5x gearing sensitivity ceiling, on
Aye's 30-Jun-2026 net worth, before any further equity raise; B07 §2C):

Static capital ceiling AUM = ₹7,324cr × 1.84 = **₹13,476cr**

- **SOM_3yr (₹12,140cr) < capacity ceiling (₹13,476cr)** → **sufficient**, with
  ₹1,336cr (~10%) of headroom to spare on the current capital base alone.
- **SOM_5yr (₹14,540cr) > capacity ceiling (₹13,476cr)** → a **gap of ₹1,064cr**
  by year 5. On a *static* capital base (no further raise, no retained-earnings
  accretion) the SOM_5yr trajectory is the optimistic side of this specific
  cross-check. In practice this gap is plausibly closed by organic net-worth
  accretion at the guided 17-20% RoE (operator digest, FY27 guidance) over the
  intervening years, or by the ₹4,000cr NCD programme the board has already
  approved (22-Jul-26 announcement, operator digest) — but that programme is debt,
  which does not by itself relieve the gearing ceiling on which this 84% figure is
  built. **Flagged, not treated as a hard capacity failure.**

### 4C. Market structure

- **Competitor count**: at minimum 8 identifiable MSME-focused NBFC peers across
  CRISIL's named set (Five-Star, SBFC, Veritas, Vistaar, Finova, Kinara — Prospectus
  p.65/614) plus two web-sourced additions (MAS Financial, Northern Arc), against a
  much larger unnamed universe of banks and smaller NBFCs.
- **Concentration among organised peers**: top-3 of the six CRISIL-named peers
  (Five-Star + SBFC + Veritas) = (₹12,847+₹9,938+₹7,746)cr ÷ ₹45,159cr ≈ **68%** of
  that named-peer AUM pool — moderately concentrated at the organised-NBFC layer.
  But that entire organised-NBFC layer (₹53,500cr, FY25, Prospectus p.184/614) is
  only ~15% of the ticket-scoped formal segment (₹3,66,000cr) and a low single-digit
  percentage of the ₹4,08,000-9,12,000cr TAM range — i.e. **the overall market
  remains highly fragmented and informal**, not concentrated.
- **Organised vs unorganised**: formal institutions (banks + NBFCs + SFBs combined)
  meet only ~27-28% of total MSME credit demand nationally (Prospectus p.181/614) —
  over 70% remains informal/unmet, the structural basis for the "faster" SOM
  share-gain band used in 3B.
- **Consolidating or fragmenting**: fragmenting at the entrant level (new NBFC/
  fintech entrants drawn by rising NBFC share), while formalisation is
  simultaneously shrinking the addressable-informal pool over time (Udyam
  registration trend).
- **Competition basis**: differentiation-led (cluster underwriting, branch trust,
  turnaround time), not primarily price-led — CRISIL explicitly cites high
  operational cost-to-serve and underwriting data scarcity as the structural
  barriers protecting incumbents (Prospectus p.184-185/614).
- **Entries/exits**: no named exits found in the collected material; entries
  implied by the NBFC segment-share trend.
- **Import share**: not applicable (domestic financial-services market).

## SECTION 5: SUMMARY & RUNWAY

### 5A. Funnel

```
TAM (conservative, ticket-scoped)  ₹4,08,000cr   [Method 1, CRISIL gap x 12% ticket proxy]
  └─ TAM (realistic)                ₹9,12,000cr   [Method 1, CRISIL total demand x 12%]
       └─ SAM (5 filters, 29.5%)    ₹1,20,400cr
            └─ Current AUM (6.1% of SAM)  ₹7,324cr
            └─ SOM 3yr (10.1% of SAM)     ₹12,140cr   → implied AUM CAGR 18.3%
            └─ SOM 5yr (12.1% of SAM)     ₹14,540cr   → implied AUM CAGR 14.7%
```

### 5B. Runway assessment

- **Revenue headroom (literal, SAM ÷ current revenue)**: SAM ₹1,20,400cr ÷ FY26
  total revenue ₹1,814.73cr (results p.6/26, per 01-gate0.md sourcing) = **66.3x**.
  Flagged as a unit mismatch — SAM is a loan-book (stock) measure while revenue is
  an income (flow) measure at Aye's ~25.8% AUM yield. The more economically
  meaningful like-for-like measure is **loan-book headroom**: SAM ÷ current AUM =
  ₹1,20,400cr ÷ ₹7,324cr = **16.4x**, used below for classification.
- **TAM growth rate**: NBFC sub-₹1mn-ticket segment grew at 19% CAGR, FY19-25
  (Prospectus p.185/614) — the most directly ticket-scoped growth figure available;
  broadly consistent with CRISIL's projected 17-19% CAGR for overall MSME credit
  outstanding, FY25-27 (Prospectus p.184/614).
- **Company CAGR vs TAM**: Aye's own 2.5-year AUM CAGR (37.4%, FY23-H1FY26,
  Prospectus p.206/614) is roughly **2x** the segment's 19% CAGR — Aye is gaining
  share, not merely riding the market.
- **Years to saturate SAM at current growth**: solving ₹7,324cr × (1.265)^n =
  ₹1,20,400cr (using FY26's actual delivered 26-27% YoY AUM growth, digest+results)
  gives **n ≈ 11.9 years** — well beyond the 3-5 year SOM horizon.

### 5C. Runway classification

**STRONG.** Basis (explicit convention applied, since no numeric thresholds were
supplied to this stage): loan-book headroom of 16.4x and ~12 years to saturate SAM
at the current delivered growth rate sit in the "strong, not yet massive" band —
below the kind of >20x/>15-year profile that would justify MASSIVE, but well above
the thresholds for GOOD/MODERATE/LIMITED. The literal income-based headroom (66.3x)
would on its own suggest MASSIVE, but that figure is not treated as decisive here
given the stock/flow unit mismatch flagged in 5B.

### 5D. SAM expansion levers actually being pursued

- **Mortgage/LAP mix shift** (from ~22% toward the 30-35% target over 2-3 years,
  operator digest + Prospectus p.237/614) is the primary lever the company is
  actively pursuing that could expand SAM. It moves Aye slightly up the ticket-size
  curve into the growing sub-₹1.5mn LAP bracket (16%→20% share of the LAP book,
  Prospectus p.195/614). Rough, labelled estimate: broadening the ticket-scope
  filter modestly (from <₹1mn toward <₹1.5mn on the mortgage leg only) could add on
  the order of **10-15%** to SAM — revised SAM ≈ **₹1,32,000-1,38,000cr**. Not
  CRISIL-sourced directly; presented as a directional estimate only.
- **Generative-AI underwriting pilot** (digest-only, NON-ANCHORED) could expand the
  capability filter (currently 90%) if it demonstrably widens thin-file/cluster
  underwriting reach, but has no quantified evidence yet.
- **Geographic expansion is explicitly NOT being pursued** (Prospectus p.237/614) —
  this lever is closed by management's own stated intent, capping the geography
