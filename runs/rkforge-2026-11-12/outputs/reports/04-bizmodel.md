## SECTION 1: THE BUSINESS MODEL IN PLAIN ENGLISH

### 1A One line
Ramkrishna Forgings buys steel bars and billets, hammers and presses them red-hot into shaped metal parts (forgings), machines many of them to finished size, and sells them mainly to truck and bus makers in India, the USA and Europe. It is the largest forging company in Eastern India and among the top nationwide (AR26 p.43). Casting (poured metal parts) and aluminium forging are new, small add-ons (IP slides 9, 10; IndRa p.2).

### 1E The chai-stall-uncle version
Think of a blacksmith's shop that grew to the size of a small town. Truck makers hand him a drawing. He heats a steel bar until it glows, hammers it into shape, then files it to exact size. He sells the finished piece. The steel price is passed on to the buyer, so his profit is the hammering and finishing work, not the steel. He keeps the fire busy by working for a few big buyers, and one buyer (Tata Motors) gives him about a third of his work (IndRa p.2). Money gets stuck for a long time. He pays for steel, then waits about 190 days to be fully paid before bank help is counted (IndRa p.3; B03). When trucks sell well and his fire is full, profit jumps. When trucks sell badly or America adds a tax, profit drops hard.

### 2D Industry lifecycle
Auto forging in India is mature and cyclical, tied to CV production. FY26 CV production +13% to 11,70,150 and M&HCV +16% to 4,58,506 (AR26 p.4). ICRA expects CV volumes +4-6% in FY27 off a high base (AR26 p.5). Metal forging in India is forecast at an 8.8% CAGR 2026-2033 from USD 11,346.9 million in 2025 (Grand View, cited in AR26 p.35). US Class 8 is described as at the "bottoming" phase after a 13-14% fall (AR26 p.4). The company sits late-mature in its core (CV forgings) and early-growth in its adds (casting, aluminium, rail bogies, PV).
