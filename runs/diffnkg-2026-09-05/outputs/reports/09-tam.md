## SECTION 1: MARKET DEFINITION

### 1A Precise boundaries

Diffusion Engineers is NOT a general welding-consumables company. Its order
book is 81.6% Heavy Engineering vs 5.7% Welding Consumables (Mar-26, per B04
note), and its product description is anti-wear/hardfacing focused, not
general fabrication welding. The market this stage sizes is therefore a
NICHE inside three adjacent industries, not the industries themselves.

- Product scope (IN): anti-wear/hardfacing welding consumables (coated
  electrodes, flux-cored wires, thermal-spray/PTA powders, cold-repair
  compounds); wear plates and wear parts (AR/CCO overlay plates, liners,
  crusher/chute parts); heavy-engineering repair and reconditioning
  services (kiln-shell replacement, roller reconditioning, in-situ
  machining) sold to steel, cement, power, mining, sugar, railway and
  defence customers.
- Product scope (OUT): general/commodity welding consumables for
  automotive, shipbuilding and general fabrication (plain MIG/TIG wire,
  shielding gas, standard stick electrodes) — this is ESAB India's and
  Ador Welding's larger core market, not Diffusion's; full heavy
  construction-equipment OEM manufacture (excavators, cranes, bulldozers)
  — Diffusion services and wear-protects this equipment, it does not
  build it.
- Geographic scope: India core (~92% of consolidated revenue is
  domestic/export mix implies ~92% non-export); direct exports ~7.8% of
  revenue (UAE — plant yet to commence, Turkey subsidiary, ad hoc exports
  such as the Russia rotor assembly and kiln-shell exports cited in the
  AR MD&A).
- Customer scope: steel, cement, power (thermal and renewable), mining
  (coal and critical minerals), sugar/ethanol, railways, defence. EXCLUDE
  automotive OEM and shipbuilding, which the AR names as adjacent
  opportunity but where Diffusion has no disclosed installed base.
- Channel scope: direct + distributor (95 domestic, 9 overseas
  distributors, AR2026 [PAGE 23/445]).
- Price/segment scope: technical/differentiated segment (NABL-accredited
  testing, proprietary alloy formulations), competing against
  commoditised low end where Chinese imports dominate (AR2026 [PAGE
  47/2544], [PAGE 48/2680]).

A wrong definition makes every later number useless. The definition above
deliberately EXCLUDES the two largest numbers the company's own investor
presentation and AR cite (global/India welding consumables at large, and
heavy construction equipment at large), because Diffusion does not compete
for most of that revenue. Section 2 and this narrowing are the load-bearing
judgment of this report.

### 1B Management's own TAM claim

Diffusion's AR2026 MD&A and investor presentation do NOT state a single
numeric TAM or addressable-market figure for the company itself. Instead
they cite three adjacent industry sizes as demand-driver backdrop:
- Global welding consumables: USD 18.7 bn (2025) -> USD 31.6 bn (2035),
  5.4% CAGR (Market Business Insights, cited AR2026 [PAGE 46]).
- India welding consumables: USD 1.66 bn (2026) -> USD 2.1 bn (2033), 6%
  CAGR (Fortune Business Insights, cited AR2026 [PAGE 46] and Investor
  Presentation [PAGE 25]).
- Global wear plate market: USD 3.7 bn (2024) -> USD 5.5 bn (2030), 6.7%
  CAGR (Grand View Research, AR2026 [PAGE 48]).
- India wear plates: USD 122.8 mn (2024) -> USD 186.8 mn (2030), 7.2% CAGR
  (Grand View Research / Verified Market Reports, AR2026 [PAGE 48],
  Investor Presentation [PAGE 26]).
- Global heavy construction equipment: USD 160.11 bn (2026) -> USD 226.91
  bn (2033), 5.1% CAGR; broader mining/material-handling definition USD
  209-224 bn (2025) (MarketsandMarkets/Fortune Business Insights, AR2026
  [PAGE 49]).
- India construction equipment market: Rs 69,046 cr / USD 7.91 bn (2025)
  -> Rs 1,02,827 cr / USD 11.78 bn (2030) (IBEF, AR2026 [PAGE 49-50]).

Taking the India-specific figures at face value and summing them (as the
MD&A's own narrative structure invites a reader to do, by presenting them
back-to-back as "the opportunity landscape" for Diffusion): Rs 14,940 cr
(welding consumables, USD 1.66bn x 90) + Rs 1,105 cr (wear plates, USD
122.8mn x 90, 2024 base) + Rs 69,046 cr (construction equipment, already in
INR) = Rs 85,091 cr.

Credibility read: BROAD. This is a textbook TAM=SAM conflation. Two of the
three components (general welding consumables market at large, and the full
construction-equipment OEM market) are markets Diffusion does not directly
address per the 1A scope. Section 2 below carries this Rs 85,091 cr figure
forward as mgmt_claim_cr for the ratio test, with the caveat that it is an
inferred sum, not a single stated management number.

## SECTION 2: TAM ESTIMATION, MULTIPLE METHODS

### Method 1 — Top-down (narrowed)

Base: India welding consumables market. Two figures found, materially
divergent:
- AR2026-cited (Fortune Business Insights): USD 1.66 bn (2026) = Rs 14,940
  cr at Rs 90/USD [AR2026 PAGE 46].
- Web cross-check (IMARC Group): USD 1.32 bn (2026) = Rs 11,880 cr
  (searched 2026-09-05).
- A third web-cited Fortune figure of USD 1.8 bn (2026) was also found in
  the same search result, internally inconsistent with the AR's own USD
  1.66 bn citation of the identical source — flagged as a source-naming
  inconsistency, not resolved here.

Per the conservative-bias rule, the lower figure (IMARC, USD 1.32 bn = Rs
11,880 cr) anchors the conservative case; the AR-cited USD 1.66 bn = Rs
14,940 cr anchors the realistic case.

Narrowing step — general welding consumables to hardfacing/anti-wear
niche: no India-specific split was found (NOT FOUND). Proxy used: the
global hardfacing welding market was sized at USD 1.73 bn (2023, Grand View
Research, web search 2026-09-05) against the global welding consumables
market of USD 18.7 bn (2025, same AR source as above) = ~9.25% of the total
market. This is a cross-market proxy (different year, different geography
mix) and carries LOW-MODERATE confidence; applied here for lack of a
better anchor.
- Conservative: 9.25% x Rs 11,880 cr = Rs 1,099 cr
- Realistic: 9.25% x Rs 14,940 cr = Rs 1,382 cr

Add wear plates (already scoped, no further narrowing needed): India wear
plates market USD 122.8 mn (2024) = Rs 1,105 cr [AR2026 PAGE 48].
- Conservative = Realistic = Rs 1,105 cr (single sourced figure; the 2030
  forecast of Rs 1,681 cr is a future point, not used for the current-year
  TAM).

Heavy-engineering repair/reconditioning services (kiln shell, roller
reconditioning, in-situ machining): NO external market-size figure exists
for this specific MRO niche (NOT FOUND). The AR cites capital-goods and
construction-equipment OEM totals (Rs 69,046 cr, Rs 4,29,001 cr) but these
measure equipment MANUFACTURE, not the repair/reconditioning service
Diffusion sells into that installed base — explicitly excluded per 1A.
This component is carried forward via Method 3 (peer/company revenue) only,
not added here, to avoid inventing an unsourced number.

Method 1 TAM: Conservative Rs 1,099 + 1,105 = Rs 2,204 cr. Realistic Rs
1,382 + 1,105 = Rs 2,487 cr. Confidence: M (wear plates figure is
single-sourced and specific; the hardfacing-share proxy is the weak link).

### Method 2 — Bottom-up

Not usable to a defensible standard. A per-unit approach would need
average annual wear-consumable/hardfacing spend per steel plant, cement
plant, thermal power plant, sugar mill etc. No such disclosed benchmark
was found in the corpus or via web search (NOT FOUND). Rather than invent
a number, this method is not populated; Methods 1, 3 and 5 carry the
triangulation.

### Method 3 — Peer revenue aggregation (incl. unorganised estimate)

Listed peers with overlapping product lines, FY26 consolidated revenue
(all web search, 2026-09-05):
- Diffusion Engineers: Rs 406.6 cr (consol FY26, own figure, anchor:
  AR2026 P&L, work/txt/annual-report/Annual_Report_2026.txt).
- Ador Welding: Rs 1,140 cr FY26 consolidated (web search).
- ESAB India: Rs 1,514.18 cr FY26 (up 9.6% YoY; web search, Whalesbook/
  scanx.trade, corroborated across 3 sources).
- GEE Ltd: Rs 393 cr (most recent revenue cited; web search).

Raw sum of full company revenues = Rs 3,453.6 cr. This overstates
Diffusion's addressable niche because ESAB India and Ador Welding sell
mostly general welding equipment and consumables, cutting and automation
products — not primarily anti-wear/hardfacing. Applying a niche-relevance
discount of 15-20% to ESAB and Ador's revenue (an assumption, NOT sourced
to a disclosed segment split — flagged) and taking GEE and Diffusion at
full value (both are hardfacing/wear-oriented specialists by product
description):
- ESAB niche-relevant: Rs 227-303 cr
- Ador niche-relevant: Rs 171-228 cr
- GEE (full): Rs 393 cr
- Diffusion (full): Rs 406.6 cr
Organized-sector niche revenue range: Rs 1,197.6 cr - Rs 1,330.6 cr.

Unorganised-sector share: web search returned "close to 50 to 55 per cent"
for the broader India welding equipment market (source not date-stamped
or tier-identified in the search result — MODERATE-LOW confidence, applied
cautiously). Grossing up the organized-sector estimate at a 45-50%
organized share:
- Conservative (higher organized share assumption, 50%): Rs 1,197.6 cr /
  0.50 = Rs 2,395 cr
- Realistic (55% unorganised, 45% organized): Rs 1,330.6 cr / 0.45 = Rs
  2,957 cr

Method 3 TAM: Conservative Rs 2,395 cr. Realistic Rs 2,957 cr. Confidence:
M (two stacked assumptions — niche-relevance discount and unorganised
share — each unsourced to a India-specific, dated report).

### Method 4 — Import substitution (directional only, not counted in headline TAM)

Wear plate imports grew 23% YoY in 2023-24, China the dominant supplier of
CCO and ceramic-composite variants (AR2026 [PAGE 48/2690]). No absolute
Rs cr import value was disclosed (NOT FOUND). This is used only as a
growth-driver signal in Section 4A, not added to the TAM to avoid double
counting against Methods 1 and 3, which already price the served Indian
market inclusive of import share.

### Method 5 — Global benchmark (directional only)

Global wear plate market USD 3.7 bn (2024) against a global population of
~8 bn = ~USD 0.46 per-capita spend. India wear plate market USD 122.8 mn
(2024) against ~1.45 bn population = ~USD 0.085 per-capita, roughly 18% of
the global average despite India being among the top-3 global steel and
top-2 cement producers. At global per-capita parity the India wear-plate
market alone would be ~USD 650 mn (~5.3x the current USD 122.8 mn). This is
a LOW-confidence, directional upside signal only (population-weighted
averages ignore industrial-mix differences); not used for the headline
TAM number.

### Triangulation table

| Method | Conservative (Rs cr) | Realistic (Rs cr) | Confidence | Staleness |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Top-down (narrowed) | 2,204 | 2,487 | M | Hardfacing-share proxy uses 2023 global data — 3 yrs old at run date, STALE per >2yr rule |
| 3. Peer aggregation + unorganised | 2,395 | 2,957 | M | Peer FY26 figures current; unorganised-share source undated, treat as directional |
| 5. Global benchmark | n/a (directional) | n/a | L | 2024 base, borderline 2yr |

Conservative estimate (lower of the two quantified methods): Rs 2,200 cr
(rounded from Method 1's Rs 2,204 cr).
Realistic estimate: Rs 2,600 cr (midpoint-and-down of Method 1's Rs 2,487
cr and Method 3's Rs 2,957 cr, rounded down per conservative bias rather
than averaged straight, since Method 3 carries two stacked unsourced
assumptions).

Management's implied claim (Section 1B) vs conservative estimate:
Rs 85,091 cr / Rs 2,200 cr = 38.7x. Standard read: FAR beyond the >2x
"likely inflated" threshold. Read: INFLATED — driven almost entirely by
including the full construction-equipment OEM market (Rs 69,046 cr),
which Diffusion does not serve.

## SECTION 3: SAM & SOM

### 3A SAM

Applying the five filters to the realistic TAM (Rs 2,600 cr):
1. Product fit (Diffusion doesn't yet cover the most demanding CCO/ceramic
   composite import-substitution niche): retain 85% -> Rs 2,210 cr
2. Geography (distributor network is real but not uniform nationwide;
   ~92% domestic revenue mix suggests near-full domestic reach but with
   pockets uncovered): retain 90% -> Rs 1,989 cr
3. Channel (direct + 95 domestic distributors, minimal further loss):
   retain 95% -> Rs 1,890 cr
4. Customer (core scope is steel/cement/power/mining/sugar/rail/defence;
   excludes automotive and shipbuilding, which the AR itself flags as
   adjacent but unaddressed): retain 80% -> Rs 1,512 cr
5. Capability (technical/differentiated segment only, excludes the
   commoditised low-price segment Diffusion does not compete for on
   price): retain 75% -> Rs 1,134 cr

SAM (realistic) = Rs 1,130 cr (rounded). SAM % of TAM = 1,130 / 2,600 =
43.5%.
SAM (conservative, same cumulative filter of 0.3919 applied to the Rs
2,200 cr conservative TAM) = Rs 862 cr.

### 3B SOM at 3 and 5 years

Current SAM share: Diffusion's niche-relevant revenue is taken as
consolidated FY26 revenue less the Trading revenue stream (7.4%, per B04,
assumed non-core to the anti-wear/wear-plate/heavy-engineering scope):
Rs 406.6 cr x (1 - 0.074) = Rs 376.5 cr.
Current SAM share = 376.5 / 1,130 = 33.3%.

This is already a high share of its own SAM — a function of SAM being
defined tightly around Diffusion's actual served niche after five rounds
of filtering (near-tautological by construction). The real expansion room
is in the filters loosening (product, geography, customer), covered in
5D, not in SAM share gain alone.

Share-gain trajectory: capacity is being roughly doubled (Nimji facility
9,000 -> 18,000 MT welding consumables, ~Rs 70 cr of a ~Rs 100 cr program),
which supports an AGGRESSIVE share-gain assumption (3-5pp in 3 years per
the standard rule) rather than the normal 1-2pp:
- 3yr SAM share: 33.3% + 4pp = 37.3%
- 5yr SAM share: 33.3% + 7pp (aggressive, cumulative; also supported by
  unorganised-share formalisation per the >40% unorganised exception,
  since Method 3 estimates 45-55% of this market is unorganised) = 40.3%

SAM grown forward at the realistic TAM growth rate (~6%/yr, blended from
the 6% India welding-consumables CAGR and 7.2% India wear-plates CAGR):
- SAM_3yr = Rs 1,130 cr x 1.06^3 = Rs 1,346 cr
- SAM_5yr = Rs 1,130 cr x 1.06^5 = Rs 1,512 cr

SOM:
- SOM_3yr = 37.3% x Rs 1,346 cr = Rs 502 cr
- SOM_5yr = 40.3% x Rs 1,512 cr = Rs 609 cr

Implied revenue CAGR from current niche revenue (Rs 376.5 cr):
- 3yr: (502 / 376.5)^(1/3) - 1 = 10.1%
- 5yr: (609 / 376.5)^(1/5) - 1 = 10.1%

Cross-check against management's own aspiration ("Rs 650 cr+ consolidated
revenue platform," B07, unstated horizon): if read as a 5-year horizon
from the Rs 406.6 cr FY26 base, that implies a 9.8% CAGR — closely
matching the SOM-implied 10.1% 5yr figure above. This is a useful,
independently-derived consistency check: the company's own aspiration is
NOT wildly more aggressive than a bottom-up SAM-share-gain calculation
would support, given the capacity now being committed. It is not proof the
aspiration will be hit; it shows the aspiration is not obviously fantasy
either.

### 3C Capacity cross-check

Nimji facility capacity doubling (9,000 -> 18,000 MT welding consumables,
+100% volume) against the SOM_5yr-implied niche-revenue growth of ~62%
(609 / 376.5 = 1.62x). No disclosed Rs-per-MT realisation rate was found
to convert tonnage to revenue precisely (NOT FOUND), so this is a
directional check, not an exact one. Directionally: installed-plus-
committed capacity growth (100%) EXCEEDS the SOM-implied revenue growth
(62%) over the same multi-year window. Capacity is not the binding
constraint on the SOM case; the capex plan is sized for a MORE OPTIMISTIC
scenario than this report's conservative SOM math, consistent with
management's Rs 650 cr+ aspiration sitting slightly above the SOM_5yr
figure of Rs 609 cr (niche revenue; total company revenue including
Trading would be somewhat higher). Capacity check: SUFFICIENT, with the
capex plan being the more optimistic side of the two, not the SOM.

## SECTION 4: GROWTH DRIVERS, RISKS & STRUCTURE

### 4A TAM growth drivers

- Formalisation: unorganised share estimated 45-55% (Method 3); GST-linked
  formalisation and Make-in-India tender preferences favour organised,
  NABL-accredited players like Diffusion (AR2026 [PAGE 47]). Impact:
  medium, evidence: moderate (undated web source).
- Import substitution: wear-plate imports (China, Germany, US) grew 23%
  YoY in 2023-24 (AR2026 [PAGE 48/2690]); "significant opportunity for
  domestic manufacturers with advanced overlay and bonding capabilities"
  (AR2026 [PAGE 48]). Impact: medium-high, evidence: specific but dated
  (2023-24 import data).
- Regulatory/infrastructure tailwind: government capex up 89% FY22-FY26
  to Rs 12.22 lakh cr allocation for FY26-27 (AR2026 [PAGE 43, 2554]);
  cement capacity +40 MT/yr by FY26-27; coal output +42% to 1.5 bn tonnes
  by 2030; power-sector Rs 25 lakh cr 5-year capex plan (AR2026 [PAGE
  49-50]). Impact: high, evidence: specific, sourced to PIB/government
  data.
- New applications: nuclear capacity near-tripling to 22,480 MW by 2031
  (ASME-grade/nuclear-qualified product approval pathway cited, AR2026
  [PAGE 50]); EV/battery-enclosure welding, robotic/automated welding
  compatible flux-cored wire (AR2026 [PAGE 44, 2481]). Impact: medium,
  evidence: specific but early-stage (approvals not yet secured per the
  extract).
- Geographic expansion: UAE plant (not yet commenced) and Turkey
  subsidiary target export growth on top of the India-domestic core
  (injected input). Impact: low-medium currently (plant not operating),
  evidence: specific.

### 4B TAM risks

- Import competition: China's cost-competitiveness on standardised SKUs
  and CCO/ceramic variants continues to pressure the commoditised segment
  (AR2026 [PAGE 47, 2544]). Monitoring signal: DGCI&S/Ministry of Commerce
  import volumes (see Section 6).
- Cyclicality: demand tied to capex/maintenance cycles in steel, cement,
  power, mining; a downturn in any could defer shutdowns and off-take
  (AR2026 [PAGE 51/2875]), partly cushioned by sector diversification and
  maintenance-agreement minimum call-out levels.
- Input-cost volatility: nickel moved ~18% in Q1 2025 alone; fixed-price
  project contracts can see margin compression (AR2026 [PAGE 51]).
- Saturation risk: none evident at the India welding-consumables/wear-
  plate level (mid-single-digit-to-high-single-digit CAGRs across all
  cited sub-markets); risk is competitive share, not market saturation.

### 4C Market structure

- Organised welding consumables in India is effectively a duopoly at the
  top (ESAB India, Ador Welding), per web search characterisation,
  alongside smaller specialists (GEE, Diffusion) — top-2/3 concentration
  is high within the organised tier, but organised tier itself is only
  ~45-55% of the total market (Method 3).
- Unorganised share: 45-55% (web search, undated — flagged low-confidence
  on the precise number, directionally consistent with a fragmented,
  import-exposed commodity tail).
- Price vs differentiation: the AR explicitly frames the competitive axis
  as differentiated/technical-service players vs commoditised, import-
  pressured SKUs (AR2026 [PAGE 47]) — consistent with Diffusion's stated
  NABL/proprietary-alloy positioning.
- Import share trend: rising (wear plates +23% YoY 2023-24); a headwind
  for the commodity tail, a substitution opportunity for differentiated
  domestic players.

## SECTION 5: SUMMARY & RUNWAY

### 5A Funnel

TAM (India, niche-scoped): conservative Rs 2,200 cr / realistic Rs 2,600 cr
-> SAM (five filters applied): conservative Rs 862 cr / realistic Rs 1,130
cr (43.5% of realistic TAM) -> current niche revenue Rs 376.5 cr (33.3% of
realistic SAM) -> SOM_3yr Rs 502 cr / SOM_5yr Rs 609 cr.

### 5B Runway assessment

- Revenue headroom = SAM (realistic, Rs 1,130 cr) / current consolidated
  revenue (Rs 406.6 cr) = 2.78x.
- TAM growth rate: ~6%/yr (blended, realistic case).
- Company CAGR vs TAM: Diffusion's 5-year consolidated revenue CAGR is
  21% (web search), far above the ~6% TAM growth — the company has been
  GAINING SHARE, not riding the market. SOM-implied forward CAGR (10.1%)
  is markedly lower than the trailing 21%, implying either deceleration
  from a smaller base or that the SOM math here is conservative relative
  to the company's demonstrated execution.
- Years to saturate SAM at current growth: using the trailing 21% CAGR
  against Rs 1,130 cr SAM from a Rs 376.5 cr niche-revenue base: (1,130 /
  376.5) = 3.0x headroom; at 21%/yr that saturates in ln(3.0)/ln(1.21) =
  ~5.8 years. At the SOM-implied 10.1%/yr, saturation is ~11.6 years (SAM
  itself is also growing at ~6%/yr, so true saturation is later than
  either static calculation implies).

### 5C Runway classification

MODERATE. Revenue headroom (2.78x) sits below the multiples typically
associated with STRONG/GOOD runway, TAM itself grows only mid-single
digits, and the company's current SAM share is already high (33%) by
construction of a tightly-scoped SAM. Runway exists but is share-gain
dependent, not market-size dependent — consistent with a niche, already
partly-penetrated market rather than a large greenfield opportunity.

### 5D SAM expansion levers actually being pursued

- UAE plant and Turkey subsidiary (export geography expansion, currently
  not operating/limited scale) — potential addition NOT sized (plant not
  yet commenced; NOT FOUND for a disclosed target revenue contribution).
- Nimji capacity doubling (9,000 -> 18,000 MT) supports both deeper
  penetration of the existing SAM AND, if paired with product
  qualification in adjacent segments (nuclear, EV), a loosening of the
  "product fit" filter — not yet reflected in the SAM figure above because
  approvals are not yet secured.
- Revised headroom if UAE/Turkey materialise plus one adjacent-segment
  qualification succeeds: illustratively +10-15% to SAM, i.e. Rs 1,240-
  1,300 cr — NOT separately verified, shown as a directional sensitivity
  only.

### 5E Final output card

TAM (conservative / realistic): Rs 2,200 cr / Rs 2,600 cr
SAM: Rs 1,130 cr (43.5% of realistic TAM)
SOM 3yr / 5yr: Rs 502 cr / Rs 609 cr
SOM-implied revenue CAGR: 10.1% (3yr) / 10.1% (5yr)
Runway class: MODERATE
Management claim vs conservative TAM: 38.7x — INFLATED (driven by
inclusion of the full construction-equipment OEM market, which Diffusion

Valuation implication line: At 10% revenue CAGR implied by SOM, with
