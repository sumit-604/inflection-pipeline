## SECTION 1: THE BUSINESS MODEL IN PLAIN ENGLISH

### 1A. One-line description

GNG buys used laptops, desktops and other IT hardware in bulk from corporates, OEMs and leasing companies, puts them through a 21-step repair-and-cosmetic-restoration process at its own facilities, and resells them as warrantied "as good as new" devices at roughly one-third to one-half the price of a new device, worldwide (DRHP p.174, p.180).

### 1E. The chai-stall-uncle version

Imagine a shop that buys second-hand phones and laptops that companies and banks no longer want, because they've upgraded. Instead of just cleaning them up and reselling them "as-is" like the roadside second-hand shop, GNG takes each device through a proper 21-step repair line — fixing the screen, replacing the battery, repainting the shell, wiping the old data securely — so it looks and works almost like new. Then it sells these devices, backed by a one-to-three-year warranty, at roughly one-third the price of a brand-new laptop, mostly to resellers and big buyers rather than directly to you and me. It's basically a "buy old, fix well, sell trusted" business — closer to a large, organised, warrantied version of the refurbished-phone shop, but running factories in Mumbai, Dallas and Dubai instead of one counter. The catch: buying and holding all that used inventory before it's fixed and sold ties up a lot of cash, so even though the shop is profitable on paper, the cash in the till doesn't always keep pace.

## SECTION 2: INDUSTRY DYNAMICS & COMPETITIVE POSITION

### 2A. Five forces, plainly

| Force | Assessment | Helps / Hurts / Neutral |

**Reading the map:** GNG is scale-comparable to Close the Loop and MusicMagpie and growing faster than both, with the best ROE and PAT margin of the four (GNG's superior asset-light, low-capex structure shows up in ROE despite the lowest gross margin). But GNG's **gross margin is the lowest of the four by a wide margin** — 12.31% vs 27.70%-46.67% for peers. This is the single most important competitive-position finding: GNG appears to be winning on volume and B2B/wholesale scale (laptop-heavy mix, thinner unit economics) rather than on price/margin per unit, unlike New Jaisa's much richer margin on a fraction of the revenue base.

### 2D. Industry lifecycle stage

The global refurbished-electronics market is in **early-formalization growth** — organized players are displacing both unorganized/mom-and-pop refurbishers and "as-is used" sales. Indian organized-market share: 5% (FY19) → 11% (FY24) → projected 32% (FY29) at a 42% CAGR (DRHP p.174). GNG, as the disclosed largest India laptop/desktop refurbisher by value, sits at the **formalization leader** position within an industry that is itself still early in its S-curve — i.e., company-specific execution risk is compounded by market-structure risk (the "organized" category itself is young and could see new capital-backed entrants, especially post-GNG's own IPO having demonstrated the economics are investable).

### 3D. Unit economics — the physics of the business

**Unit definition:** one refurbished ICT device sold (blended across laptops and "others").

| Metric | FY22 | FY23 | FY24 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Volume (devices refurbished) | 195,651 | 248,135 | 369,320 |
| Revenue from operations (₹mn) | 5,204.95 | 6,595.42 | 11,381.38 |
| **Revenue per unit (derived, ₹)** | ≈26,604 | ≈26,581 | ≈30,822 |
| Gross margin (₹mn) | 616.51 | 1,011.45 | 1,401.52 |
| **Gross margin per unit (derived, ₹)** | ≈3,151 | ≈4,076 | ≈3,795 |

(Volumes and revenue: DRHP p.176; per-unit figures derived by dividing revenue/gross margin by volume — not company-disclosed directly)

- **Volume drivers:** growth in the procurement network (157→447 suppliers, DRHP p.175) and OEM/leasing-company relationships (HP, Lenovo, banks, Tata Capital) feeding inbound used-device supply; growth in the sales network (1,263→3,265 customers) absorbing outbound supply.
- **Price drivers:** device configuration/grade (screening produces a condition report, DRHP p.187), category mix (laptop vs. higher-ASP "others"), and the new-device reference price the refurbished unit is priced against (typically 33-65% discount, DRHP p.187-188).
- **Cost drivers:** procurement price of the used device (by far the largest cost element — materials/purchases run 80-88% of revenue), spare parts (HDD/SSD/RAM/keyboard/battery/adapters, DRHP p.188), refurbishment labour (894 technicians), and logistics/freight.
- **Incremental margin / operating leverage:** EBITDA margin has expanded from 6.66% (FY22) to ~10.60% (FY26, derived) as revenue scaled ~3.6x over the same period, evidencing real operating leverage on the fixed cost base (facilities, corporate overhead, sales/marketing team of 95, DRHP p.193). However, this operating leverage is **not yet flowing through to cash** — the same growth period saw operating cash flow turn and stay negative, because each incremental unit of revenue requires financing an incremental unit of inventory before it is sold. The core physics: **GNG's P&L operating leverage is real, but its cash-flow leverage is currently negative** — growth consumes cash faster than it throws it off.

### 4D. Highest-value questions for management

1. **Why does GNG's gross margin (12-20%) trail all three named peers (New Jaisa 32-47%, Close the Loop 30-38%, MusicMagpie 27-30%) despite superior scale and growth?**
   - Reassuring answer: it is mix-driven — the laptop-heavy B2B/wholesale channel is inherently thinner-margin, and the faster-growing, higher-margin "others" category (24-32% of revenue) is steadily lifting the blend (evidenced by GM rising from 12.31% FY24 to ~20.14% FY26, derived).
   - Worrying answer: it reflects a structural inability to price above cost-plus in a fragmented, undifferentiated market, and the margin gap persists even as the "others" mix grows.

2. **Why was operating cash flow negative in both FY25 and FY26 despite consistently positive and growing PAT, and what is the targeted steady-state working-capital cycle once growth normalizes?**
   - Reassuring answer: this is a temporary growth-investment phase; NWC days are guided back toward the FY22-24 historical range (42-61 days) as inventory management matures post-IPO.
   - Worrying answer: working-capital intensity is structural to the buy-hold-refurbish-sell model and will keep consuming cash pro-rata with growth indefinitely, capping free cash flow generation for the foreseeable future.

3. **What is the sustainable steady-state debt-to-equity given the FY26 IPO-driven deleveraging (2.53x pre-IPO down to ~0.53x post-IPO), and how quickly does leverage rebuild as inventory-funded growth resumes?**
   - Reassuring answer: management targets sub-1.0x leverage funded increasingly from internal accruals as gross/EBITDA margin expands.
   - Worrying answer: leverage rebuilds quickly once the IPO cash cushion is absorbed by inventory growth, reverting to historical reliance on working-capital loans.

4. **With top-10 supplier concentration at 69.60% of FY24 inventory cost (several related parties) and no disclosed long-term supply contracts, what protects GNG from losing a top-2 supplier (a combined 38.6% of FY24 inventory cost)?**
   - Reassuring answer: multi-year informal relationships plus a diversified base of 447+ procurement partners (Sep-2024) provide practical redundancy even without formal contracts.
   - Worrying answer: no contractual protection exists, and related-party suppliers could renegotiate terms unfavourably with limited recourse.

5. **Given the corporate guarantee structure (GNG parent guaranteeing Electronics Bazaar FZC's bank facilities, now up to AED 20mn across multiple UAE banks per the FY26 and Q3FY26 board resolutions), what is the parent's contingent exposure to FZC-level credit stress?**
   - Reassuring answer: guarantees are arm's length and FZC self-funds against its own receivables/inventory with no cross-default triggers on the parent.
   - Worrying answer: the parent balance sheet is contingently exposed to FZC's own leverage, and an FZC-level credit event could force parent-level cash support (relevant given EB FZC generated 62.50%-49.60% of consolidated revenue across recent periods, DRHP p.307).

6. **Why does the company not maintain a formal FX hedging policy despite 75.65% of H1FY25 revenue originating outside India, relying instead on a "natural hedge"?**
   - Reassuring answer: procurement is also largely denominated in foreign currency, so the natural hedge has held historically (realized FX losses <0.3% of revenue in every disclosed period, DRHP p.45).
   - Worrying answer: the natural hedge breaks down in sharp INR/AED/USD moves, and unhedged residual exposure could swing a single quarter's margin materially.

7. **Is GNG's revenue growth (48% CAGR FY22-24) a genuine organized-segment share gain, or simply riding the industry-wide formalization tailwind (organized segment itself growing at a 33% CAGR over the same period)?**
   - Reassuring answer: GNG's growth rate is well ahead of even the fast-growing organized segment, evidencing real share gains within the formalizing category.
   - Worrying answer: growth is largely a rising-tide effect that will decelerate sharply once formalization normalizes, especially as new capital-backed entrants (drawn in by GNG's own successful IPO) compress category economics.
