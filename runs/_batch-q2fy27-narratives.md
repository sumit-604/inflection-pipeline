# Aurum Proptech: Q2 FY27 in plain words

Source: the committed review, the A5 audit and work/notion-thesis.md (run aurum-q2fy27, commit 7918b8fa). No agent was re-run.

1. **What the company does.** Aurum sells software and leads to property developers. It also runs co-living homes and rental platforms. A small third arm wants to launch a small REIT. In September it bought Housing.com, a large property portal, and paid in its own shares.

2. **How this compares with our earlier view.** We first looked at Aurum on 14 July 2026. We said AVOID, because the price was far above our value, and set an entry zone of Rs 71 to 89. Our main tripwire was simple: two quarters in a row of losses in the core business, once one-off income is taken out. Q1 was the first strike. This quarter was the second strike. So that tripwire has now fired.

3. **What happened this quarter.** Sales rose about 64% on a year ago. Most of the rise came from the developer business, and most of that likely came from PropTiger, which Aurum bought last year. The company does not split out how much is real organic growth.

4. **What improved.** The core loss is much smaller than a year ago. It went from about Rs 11 Cr to about half a crore. The rental business is now near breakeven before lease interest. Reported profit was positive for the second quarter running.

5. **What got worse, or stayed bad.** The core business still lost money once other income is removed. The company also moved Rs 3.7 Cr of its own staff cost onto the balance sheet. If that cost were counted as an expense, the loss would be about Rs 4 Cr. The developer arm looks profitable, but its margin falls below our 12% line once head office cost is shared out. Co-living beds fell 14%.

6. **Cash and balance sheet.** Cash from operations looks better, but part of it was a tax refund. Most of the rise in cash came from selling the old building and from cash that came with Housing.com. Goodwill and other intangibles now equal almost all of shareholders' equity. That is a lot of value resting on accounting judgement.

7. **What management said.** The press release calls this a fourth straight quarter of profit. We can confirm only two of those quarters, and both are losses on the core test. Management talks about an AI operating system and about joining Housing.com to the group. It gives no dates and no numbers for either. Housing.com will appear in the results from next quarter.

8. **The red flags in plain words.** No document tells us whether Housing.com makes money, so we cannot tell if the price was fair. One loan of about Rs 9.5 Cr earns about 42% a year, and the borrower is not named. This matters, because our record has a standing worry about loans to the promoter company. The promoter's warrants are priced near the market, but the promoter has paid only a quarter of the money. It can walk away from the rest. The REIT still has no first scheme, more than three years after its licence.

9. **Open items.** The audit passed after two rounds of fixes. Only small wording notes remain. Three of your rulings are still open: whether the warrant price passes your tracker test, whether to rebase the house case to the larger share count, and what monitoring item 9 covers. Our goodwill baseline also needs fixing. Notion says Rs 174 Cr, but the audited March figure is Rs 207 Cr. Both auditor ID numbers were unreadable in the scan, so they need a check.

10. **What this means for the thesis.** The AVOID view holds and is firmer. The core business has now lost money for two quarters in a row. The balance sheet now rests on a large purchase whose profit we cannot see. Nothing here moves Aurum toward a buy. Your decision status stays yours to set.

11. **What to watch next quarter.** The Q3 result is due by about mid-February 2027. Look at one number: core profit with other income removed, shown with and without Housing.com. It should also be shown with the capitalised staff cost counted as an expense. A third loss in a row confirms the bear case. Before that, the half-year related-party filing should show whether any money went to the promoter company. Zero is good. Anything above zero confirms the worry.

# Cyient DLM: Q2 FY27 in plain words

Source: the committed review, the A5 audit and work/notion-thesis.md (run cyientdlm-q2fy27, commit 1e6f9f35). No agent was re-run.

1. **What the company does.** Cyient DLM builds electronic parts for aircraft, medical machines, defence and industrial customers. The customer gives the drawing, and Cyient DLM builds it to that drawing. Almost all of its work is this kind of build-to-print work. It owns a US unit, the old Altek, which it bought to reach customers abroad.

2. **How this compares with our earlier view.** There is earlier work, but no signed thesis. Our first pass in September found a slow, cash-hungry business that the market had just priced as a fast one. The mental model is unsigned, Halt 1 is pending, and there is no entry price. Before this quarter we named six things to test. This review checks each of them, so treat it as a first read against that list.

3. **What happened this quarter.** Sales grew about 26% on a year ago. The order book hit a record of about Rs 2,800 Cr. Aerospace was the engine, and aerospace plus industrial gave more than all of the growth. The rest of the business shrank.

4. **What improved.** The order book keeps rising, so that test is green. The India business grew its sales by about a quarter. Stock days came down a little.

5. **What got worse.** The company earns less on each rupee of sales. The gross margin fell almost four points, and the operating margin fell to under 9%. Year on year, the cause is the cost of materials. Quarter on quarter, overheads caused most of the fall. Management calls the material jump a one-off. Yet the same deck sells the shift to full assemblies, which use more bought-in material, as the strategy.

6. **The profit is not what it looks like.** More than half of reported profit is a one-off gain. The company cut what it owes the sellers of the US unit, because that unit missed its targets. This is the second year in a row this has happened in the same quarter. Without it, profit still grew, but almost all of that growth was a currency swing. Take out both, and pre-tax profit was nearly flat. The US unit lost money before that gain.

7. **Cash and balance sheet.** Cash is the weak spot. Over the half year, the business turned only a small part of its profit into cash. The India unit took new short-term debt to pay for more stock. Group cash fell. US debt is now mostly due within a year.

8. **What management said.** Management says the outlook for the year is positive, but gives no number. The deck targets a margin of 11% to 13% over FY27 to FY29. The company is moving away from that band, not toward it. It also names new areas, such as chip equipment, robotics and AI data centres. None of them shows any revenue yet.

9. **The red flags in plain words.** A shrinking payout to the sellers of the US unit usually means it missed its plan. So the goodwill on that unit may need a write-down at the year-end test. The stated growth in new orders does not match the order book figures. The filing also has a printing error in last year's staff cost, and one earnings-per-share figure that cannot be right.

10. **Open items.** The audit ran two rounds of fixes, and one small item stays open. In one question, our imagined good answer says the new borrowing was only US refinancing. The filing shows the opposite: the new debt is in India. It changes no number or verdict, and it is flagged in the payload. You also need to rule on how to count working capital days. The filing basis gives 189 days, the deck says 153, and our earlier 169 matches neither.

11. **What this means for the view.** Three of the six tests read red: gross margin, the margin part of the proof gate, and the US unit. The order book is green. Cash waits for your basis ruling. Related-party flows to the parent cannot be seen yet. Nothing here supports the claim that the company is climbing to better work. The decision stays with you at Halt 1.

12. **What to watch next quarter.** The Q3 result is due around mid-January 2027. Watch the gross margin. A move back to about 40% says the drop was a one-off. A margin at or below this quarter's level, with full assemblies still growing, says the strategy itself squeezes margin. Also watch the overhead ratio, the US unit's profit, and whether the India debt is repaid. Run the concall review on the 15 October call first. It may answer the margin question.

# Fedbank Financial Services (FEDFINA): Q2 FY27 in plain words

Built only from the committed review (work/review_fedfina_q2fy27.md), the A5 audit (work/audit_fedfina_q2fy27.md) and the live Notion copy (work/notion-thesis.md). No agent was re-run.

1. **What the company does.** Fedbank lends money against gold jewellery and against property. Gold loans are now a little over half the book. Loans against property and small home loans make up the rest. Federal Bank owns about 60% of it. That parent gives it a strong credit rating and cheap borrowing.

2. **What we thought before.** Our earlier work said this is a decent lender that is getting better, but the share price already pays for the recovery. Notion holds it as WATCHLIST / AVOID, deep watch. We would only buy at Rs 68 to 77, about half of the last recorded price. Three things would break the thesis: credit losses above 1.1%, return on equity failing to pass 14% by FY28, and loan-loss cover (PCR) falling below 32%.

3. **What happened this quarter.** Profit rose 63% from a year ago, to about Rs 131 Cr. The loan book under management grew 37%. Return on equity reached 17%. Costs grew slower than income. On the surface this was a strong quarter.

4. **What improved.** Return on equity and return on assets are now above the levels we hoped to see by FY28. Credit cost fell to 0.6%, inside the company's own "below 1%" promise. The gap between what it earns on loans and what it pays to borrow widened a little. None of the three thesis-breaking tripwires fired.

5. **What got worse.** Bad loans rose while money set aside for them fell. The share of bad loans went up, but the provision charge went down. Loans in early trouble (Stage 2) grew by about a third in one quarter. These new problem loans got very little new provision. If the company had kept last quarter's cover, most of this quarter's profit gain would disappear. This is an inference, but it is the main question of the quarter.

6. **Growth went off the balance sheet.** Almost all the growth came from loans the company made and then sold to banks. Loans kept on its own books barely moved. It sold about twice as much as last quarter. The deck and the filing do not agree on how much was sold, and the route for the difference is not stated. The sales also made a small loss for the second quarter in a row.

7. **Gold growth is mostly the gold price.** The gold book grew mainly because gold got more expensive, not because customers pledged more gold. In this quarter, about 70% of gold growth was price. The company also lends more per gram than before, so there is little room left to grow that way. If gold prices stop rising, the gold growth target is hard to reach unless far more gold comes in.

8. **Capital and borrowing.** Capital is adequate and well above the lenders' minimums. Debt compared with equity is the same as in March, so there was no real reduction in leverage this half year. About half of the better return on equity comes from using more debt, not from better lending. At this growth pace the company will need more loan sales, more partner lending or new equity.

9. **What management said or promised.** The deck and press release give no new guidance for the year. A call was held, but its transcript is not in this run. Earlier promises from the Q1 call still stand on record: credit cost below 1%, 200 new branches, faster gold growth. Branch openings and gold tonnage are running behind those promises. The deck blames the rise in gold bad loans on "industry changes" without saying what they are.

10. **Red flags in plain words.** The printed quarterly earnings per share is wrong; the half-year figure gives a lower number. Provisions fell while bad loans rose. Two ratios in the filing do not match their own labels. A new fee to the parent bank appears. The text layer of the filing was poor, and two numbers had to be checked against the page images. Collection efficiency and write-offs are not disclosed, so we cannot run the real cash test for a lender.

11. **Open items for you.** First, a ruling: our Notion rule says the asset-quality score drops if loan-loss cover "thins further". It thinned against last quarter but not against March. You must say which baseline counts. Second, one small wording fix in the review stays open after two loops; it changes no number or verdict. Third, Notion needs tidying: the stock price, the gold share of the book, and the credit-cost basis are stale or mixed. One old question quotes a loss ten times too large.

12. **What it means and what to watch.** The thesis holds and no tripwire fired, but loan-loss cover moved the wrong way. The tripwire most at risk is still loan-loss cover. The decision does not change: watch, do not buy at this price. Our entry zone now sits below the company's book value, so the next valuation should revisit it. The one number to watch is credit cost in the December quarter, due in mid-January. At 0.8% or lower, this quarter was clean. At 1.0% or higher, this quarter's profit was borrowed from the next.

# Tata Communications: Q2 FY27 in plain words

Source: the committed review, the A5 audits (run 1 and loop 1) and work/notion-thesis.md (run tatacomm-q2fy27, commit 4c53546e). No agent was re-run.

1. **What the company does.** Tata Communications runs a global data network for large companies. Most of its revenue comes from data links and digital platforms such as messaging and customer engagement tools. A small US business checks text-message campaigns for mobile operators. Voice calls are a shrinking old business.

2. **How this compares with our earlier view.** First look, no earlier analysis on record. There is no Notion page, no thesis, no entry zone and no decision status. So this quarter has nothing earlier to be measured against.

3. **What happened this quarter.** Sales grew about 10% on a year ago. Operating profit grew about 21%, and the margin rose about two points. Profit for shareholders rose 86%. On the surface, this was a strong quarter.

4. **What improved.** The operating margin reached its best level in five quarters. The digital platforms still lose money, but the loss is shrinking. Cash from operations rose sharply over the half year.

5. **What got worse.** The cost of running the network grew almost twice as fast as sales. So the company earns less on each rupee of network sales. The margin gain came from lower staff and other costs, not from better prices. The parent company itself barely grew. Voice kept shrinking, and so did one services business.

6. **Where the profit came from.** About 90% of the jump in profit from last quarter came from subsidiaries, not the parent. The filing does not say why. The smaller digital loss explains about half of it at most. The rest may be one-off cost credits. The small US campaign business now earns about a quarter of segment profit on 4% of sales. Its big jump this year also looks large because last year's quarter was weak.

7. **Cash and balance sheet.** Part of the cash gain came from lower tax payments. Building new capacity took almost all of the operating cash. After lease and interest payments, cash flow was negative. The dividend was larger than the half-year profit. Net debt rose by about Rs 1,600 Cr in six months, and the new debt sits in subsidiaries. The parent's own debt ratios improved, which makes the group look safer than it is.

8. **What management said.** The CEO says the company enters the second half with momentum. The deck says the team is in place and the goal is growth in operating profit. Management sees a large opportunity in links between data centres. It gives no date for when the digital platforms stop losing money. It also gives no margin target and no figure for its AI revenue.

9. **The red flags in plain words.** There is a long dispute with the telecom department worth about Rs 7,500 Cr. That is more than twice the group's equity, and the auditor points to it. A foreign VAT case of about Rs 366 Cr had its court date on 13 October, and the filing does not give the outcome. A staff cut charge is called one-time, but it appears every quarter. Bad debts written off rose sharply at the parent. The company also put more money into an associate that earns less than 1% on its book value.

10. **Open items.** The audit passed after one round of fixes. Only small wording notes remain. The concall on 15 October still needs its own review, because its transcript was not in this run. Any full pipeline run first needs the Step 1 intake, since there is no spear on record.

11. **What this means for the thesis.** There is no thesis yet. This quarter looks better than it is. The profit jump rests on subsidiaries and on cost cuts we cannot explain, while debt rises and free cash is thin. The verdict is PROCEED WITH FLAGS. Whether to look further is your call.

12. **What to watch next quarter.** Watch one number in the Q3 result: the operating margin. At or above 21%, the Q2 step looks real. At or below about 19.5%, Q2 held one-off credits. Also watch the VAT ruling, the net debt path, and whether management names the one-off cost credits on the concall.

# Heritage Foods Q2 FY27: the story in plain words

Built from the committed review (work/review_heritgfood_q2fy27.md), the A5 audit (work/audit_heritgfood_q2fy27.md, verdict COMPLETE) and work/notion-thesis.md. No agent was re-run.

1. **What the company does.** Heritage Foods is a dairy company based in Hyderabad. It buys milk from over 3 lakh farmers in 9 states. It sells packaged milk, and it makes curd, paneer, lassi, buttermilk, ice cream and yogurt. These made products are its "value-added products". They earn more per litre than plain milk. It also owns a cattle feed business and two smaller dairy businesses it bought recently.

2. **First look.** This is a first look. No earlier analysis is on record. There is no Notion page, no company memory file and no spear line. So there is no thesis to test and no tripwire to check. This review is a starting point, not a check against a plan.

3. **What happened this quarter.** Sales grew well, but profit fell. Revenue rose about 19% to Rs 1,320 Cr. Profit after tax fell about 40% to Rs 31 Cr. Part of that fall is a one-off: last year's quarter had a Rs 9 Cr one-off GST refund. The cleaner measure, profit from operations alone, still fell about 26%.

4. **Why profit fell.** Milk got expensive. The company paid about 11% more for each litre of milk. It raised its own milk price only about 6%. So it kept less on every litre it sold. Management says this cost pressure has not ended yet.

5. **What improved.** The made products grew fast, up 32% from last year. Lassi, buttermilk, ice cream and curd all sold more. Against the June quarter, margins also improved a little. But the same seasonal lift happened last year, and margins then fell in the second half. Also, part of the made-product growth came from higher prices, not more volume.

6. **What got worse.** The new plant costs money before it earns money. Depreciation rose about a third and interest costs nearly doubled. The bought businesses and the feed unit now add almost nothing to group profit. A year ago they added a clear amount. The two bought dairy businesses lose money in every period shown.

7. **Cash and balance sheet.** Cash from operations looks strong on paper, but most of it is depreciation and a normal seasonal fall in stock. Measured against operating profit, cash came in at roughly one to one. Debt doubled last year to fund the build. Spending on unfinished projects nearly tripled in six months to Rs 129 Cr. No note says what the project is or when it starts.

8. **What management said and promised.** Management says it will expand capacity for made products, push better margins through premium products and cost control, and keep investing for growth. None of these promises has a date or an amount. A slide shows targets for 2028 but prints no numbers on them. New products did launch this quarter, such as a high protein curd and organic curd.

9. **Red flags in plain words.** The investor slides are kinder than the filed numbers. A ratio labelled "net debt" subtracts no cash. A slide titled "Strengthening Financial Health" shows every ratio getting weaker. Two slides in the same deck disagree on how fast milk buying grew. The same quote is credited to two different people. In the accounts, Rs 7 Cr sent to subsidiaries does not show where it went. One subsidiary's numbers were not checked by its auditor, and its loss jumped. The company has not said what it paid for its acquisitions.

10. **Open items still flagged.** The earnings call was held on 16 October, but we do not have the transcript. So management's spoken answers are not in this review. One chart does not say which line is cow milk. That decides whether cow milk inflation is easing or still running near 15%. We also do not know if the made-product figure includes the subsidiaries, which changes how big the mix gain really is. The Notion save waits for your decision, because no Heritage page exists.

11. **What this means.** The growth story in made products is real, but this quarter it lost to the milk cost cycle. The company is building and buying ahead of profit, and the bought businesses are not yet paying. The verdict is to proceed with flags. The numbers are clean and audited, but the quality questions are serious. Cash conversion is not yet proven either way.

12. **What to watch next quarter.** Watch the December quarter gross margin against the same quarter last year, due by 14 February 2027. If the gap narrows, pricing is catching up with milk cost. If it widens, the squeeze is deepening. Also watch for a named project and start date, a price increase on milk, and whether the bought businesses cut their losses. First, get the call transcript and see how management answered the 34 open questions.

# Indo Rama Synthetics: Q2 FY27 in plain words

Sources: the committed review (work/review_indorama_q2fy27.md, after loop 2), the A5 audit (COMPLETE) and work/notion-thesis.md. No agent was re-run.

1. **What the company does.** Indo Rama makes polyester at one large site in Nagpur. It buys oil linked raw materials and turns them into fibre, yarn and plastic resin for bottles. It earns the gap between what it pays for inputs and what it gets for its products. Revenue can rise just because oil prices rise, so the gap matters more than sales.

2. **What happened this quarter.** Profit was Rs 74 cr, three times last year's Rs 25 cr. Sales rose about 10% to Rs 1,341 cr. That sounds very strong, but last year's quarter was a weak one. Against an ordinary quarter, profit before tax rose about 41%. That is good, not dramatic.

3. **What improved.** Costs outside raw materials fell, and the currency loss shrank. Interest cost came down a little because the company owes less. These cost savings, not better selling prices, drove most of the profit gain.

4. **What got worse.** The gap between product prices and raw material costs did not widen. It was slightly worse than a year ago and clearly worse than last quarter. The first half of the year was weak overall: sales in the first quarter fell by more than a quarter, and management blames trouble in the Middle East. In the parent company alone, gross profit fell even though sales rose.

5. **Cash and the balance sheet.** Net debt fell by about Rs 261 cr in six months. That is real progress. About half came from cash the business earned. The other half came from collecting money from customers faster and paying suppliers slower. That second half can reverse. The company now collects from customers in about a week but pays suppliers in about five months. The filing does not say if it sells its bills to banks, which can make debt look smaller than it is.

6. **The capex decision.** The board approved about Rs 200 cr to build capacity for special fibre and yarn at the same Nagpur site. Output should start between October 2027 and April 2028. It will be paid for by a bank loan plus the company's own cash, but the filing does not say how much of each. It also does not say how the money splits between the company and its subsidiary.

7. **What management said or promised.** The chairman said Middle East events pushed up oil and raw material costs. Management says the new plant will widen the product range and move the company into higher value products. It also promises to keep improving results, but gives no numbers, no capacity figure and no expected sales from the new plant. The press release calls total income "revenue" and counts other income inside its profit figure, which makes the headline look better.

8. **Forensic red flags in plain words.** First, the company pays no tax. A normal tax rate would cut profit by about a quarter, and nobody outside knows how long this tax shield lasts. Second, the subsidiaries made between a quarter and half of group profit, but the filing gives no split. They seem to do a lot of trading, and they owe the parent at least Rs 183 cr. Third, a tax credit carried on the books makes up about 40% of the company's net worth. If that credit is worth less, the balance sheet is weaker than it looks.

9. **Flagged open items.** Cash quality cannot be judged yet, because the filings leave out factoring, the make-up of supplier credit and a large block of other assets. The audit closed every error after two correction rounds. Seven small wording notes remain, and none changes a number or the verdict. The verdict is "proceed with flags".

10. **What this means for the thesis.** This is a first look, with no earlier analysis on record. There is no Notion page, no thesis, no entry price and no tripwires to compare against. So no tripwire moved and no thesis is tested. The quarter shows a cheaper, less indebted business, but not yet a better one. The step up the quality ladder needs a wider price gap, and that has not shown up.

11. **What to watch next quarter.** The single best test is the gross margin in the December quarter, due by mid February 2027. Above about 24% would say the gap is widening. Below about 22% would say this quarter's gain was mostly cost cutting. Also watch whether tax starts to appear, whether customer collections stay this fast, and whether the bank loan for the new plant is signed and for how much.

12. **Before any deeper work.** The name has no spear line, so the full pipeline cannot run on it yet. A Step 1 intake would come first. The 20 questions for management, led by the tax shield, the price gap per tonne and profit by subsidiary, are the fastest way to settle the open points.

# Supreme Power Equipment: H1 FY27 in plain words

Source: committed review, A5 audit and work/notion-thesis.md for runs/supremepwr-h1fy27. No agent was re-run.

1. **What the company does.** Supreme Power is a small company listed on the NSE SME board. The filing says it has one business segment and gives no more detail. Our background knowledge says it makes transformers in Tamil Nadu for utilities and renewable projects. That is not yet checked against a live source. It owns 90% of one partnership firm, Danya Electric Company.

2. **What happened this half.** The business grew fast. Group sales rose about 80% on the same half last year, to Rs 135 Cr. Profit after tax rose about 45%, to Rs 14 Cr. Profit grew, but slower than sales.

3. **What improved.** Growth is real at the profit level. It does not come from one-off income. The parent company's own margin also went up a little.

4. **What got worse.** The group margin went down, from about 18.8% to 17.3%. Depreciation and interest grew much faster than sales, because the company is building new assets with borrowed money. The parent margin rose only because of how profit is split between the parent and Danya. The group as a whole earned a thinner margin.

5. **Cash and balance sheet.** This is the weak spot. The business used cash in the half, about Rs 6 Cr, while it reported Rs 14 Cr of profit. Stock and customer dues both grew fast. Cash fell to almost nothing. Net debt rose about 42%, to Rs 57 Cr. Suppliers now fund the company more than banks do. A large part of the money owed is to small suppliers, and the payments are close to the legal 45-day limit.

6. **What management said or promised.** There was no earnings call and no presentation. The filing gives no guidance. It says one thing about the future: investors who bought warrants have paid 25%. The other 75%, about Rs 16 Cr, comes in only if they choose to convert, by about February 2027. Nobody has converted yet. The company also says it has used the warrant money as planned. It reports that money under one combined purpose, so we cannot test that claim.

7. **Red flags in plain words.** Danya's numbers are not audited, and the filing leaves a blank where it should say who checked them. The managing director owns part of Danya. The parent put more money into Danya this half while the other partners took money out. Goods move both ways between the parent and Danya, and no profit was removed for stock still sitting inside the group. All raw material is labelled as goods bought for resale, yet the company is building a factory. So we do not yet know if it is mainly a maker or a trader.

8. **The open items.** Our final check found two errors that we did not fix. First, the review says this half's profit is not boosted by goods held inside Danya. The check showed that claim cannot be proven. The boost could be anything from zero to a large share of profit. Second, the review says sales booked outside the parent were not collected. That is true only if the parent sold nothing to Danya in the half, which the filing does not show. Some extracted numbers were also hard to read in the scanned PDF and need a re-read.

9. **What this means for the thesis.** First look, no earlier analysis on record. There is no Notion page, no thesis, no entry price and no tripwires to compare against. Our verdict is "proceed with flags": the growth is real, but cash, the related subsidiary and the unclear business type all need answers first. Whether to take the name further, through the step 1 intake, is your decision.

10. **What would change our view.** The most important answer is how much of sales the company makes itself versus buys and resells. That decides what kind of business this is and what it is worth. The second is how much bank credit is still unused. The third is whether the auditor checked Danya.

11. **What to watch next.** About February 2027: do the warrant holders pay the Rs 16 Cr? About May 2027: the full-year results. The one number to watch is the cash cycle, meaning how many days money is tied up in stock and customer dues. If it falls back to about 104 days, the growth is healthy. If it stays above about 121 days, the strain is real. By September 2027: the annual report should show Danya's audit status and the trade between the parent and Danya.

12. **Next step for you.** We drafted 21 questions for management, to send by investor-relations letter. The Notion record is saved as a draft only. Nothing was written to Notion.

# Karnika Industries: H1 FY27 in plain words (17 Oct 2026)

Sources: the committed review, the three A5 audits and work/notion-thesis.md. No agent was re-run.

1. **First look.** There is no Notion page, no company file and no spear on record. This is a first look, with no earlier analysis to compare against.

2. **What the company does.** Karnika makes children's clothes in Howrah. It sells them in bulk to a few large buying houses, which pass them on to shops. It also owns 75% of Kidcity, a smaller arm that sells through store counters and online partners.

3. **What happened this half.** Management said sales grew 52% in the September quarter. That figure compares the whole group this year with Karnika alone last year, before Kidcity joined. On a fair basis, Karnika's own sales grew about 17%. Part of that is Karnika selling to its own subsidiary. Sales to outside customers may have grown only about 6%, though this is our estimate.

4. **What improved.** Profit after tax rose about 15% in the quarter and about 46% for the half. Kidcity reported a strong profit margin. The company has almost no factory assets, because outside workshops do the processing.

5. **What got worse.** The core business grew profit only 7% in the quarter. About half of the profit gain came from other income, such as interest and the sale of old machines. The operating margin fell by about two points. Costs for outside processing and staff rose.

6. **Cash and balance sheet.** This is the weak spot. The business used cash in the half, even though it reported Rs 18 Cr of profit. Customers now take about 142 days to pay, up from about 110. Debt rose, and the company also took Rs 12 Cr of warrant money. At the same time it doubled its loans to outside companies to Rs 31 Cr. The filing does not name the borrowers.

7. **Side bets.** The company bought 40% of Elementa Lifespaces and agreed to buy 26% of a sports academy. Neither is a clothing business. Elementa already shows a loss.

8. **What management promised.** Management targets 30 to 35% yearly growth to FY28, a margin of 17 to 20%, and a new chain of airport and mall stores. Today it has one store. Debt will stay at this level, and new equity will fund the stores. The call gave few hard numbers. The launch date of the first store moved within the same call, and the airport story changed three times. Three statements on the call conflict with the filing. One example: the CFO said Kidcity has no customers who owe it money, but the accounts suggest they owe at least Rs 35 Cr. We rate the guidance as evasive.

9. **Red flags in plain words.** The accounts carry careless errors. The minority holders' share of Kidcity's profit was worked out on the wrong number in the first quarter. Last March's group balance sheet seems to leave out an investment loss. Earnings per share were not adjusted for the bonus issue, so they look like they halved when profit rose. Kidcity was bought cheaply, for about one year of its profit. Its margin doubled after Karnika began supplying it, so prices between the two companies may decide where profit lands. A quarter of Kidcity's profit goes to an owner nobody has named.

10. **Open items.** Our final check left three points unfixed. None of them changes the verdict. First, last year's base already included a full half of sales to Kidcity. Second, the investment loss error may sit only in this filing's comparison column, which makes it less serious. Third, Kidcity's small positive cash flow rests on paying its own suppliers late. Some of that unpaid money may be owed to Karnika itself.

11. **What it means.** The verdict is to proceed with flags. Sales really are growing, but part of the growth is sales to its own subsidiary, and profit is not turning into cash. Money is going into unnamed loans and side bets. There is no thesis, no position and no valuation. The name needs answers before anyone puts a value on it.

12. **What to watch next.** The most important question is who borrowed the Rs 31 Cr and whether they are linked to the promoters. Next comes how much of the money owed by customers was collected in October. The decisive reading is the FY27 annual result, due by about end May 2027. If customers pay within about 110 days and cash follows profit, the story improves. If payment slips past about 142 days, or cash flow stays negative, the bear case holds. We have 37 questions ready for the company.

# Eimco Elecon Q2 FY27: the story in plain words

Source: the committed review (work/review_eimcoeleco_q2fy27.md), the A5 audit (work/audit_eimcoeleco_q2fy27.md) and work/notion-thesis.md. No agent was re-run. All money is in Rs crore.

**Earlier analysis:** first look, no earlier analysis on record. There is no Notion page, no company file, no thesis, no Decision Status and no tripwires to compare against.

1. **What the company does.** Eimco Elecon makes heavy machines that work underground in coal and metal mines. These include loaders, continuous miner sets and chair lifts that carry miners. It also makes piling rigs for construction, and it has started to sell battery vehicles. About 1,065 of its machines are in use today, and they need spare parts. The spares business is the steady part of the company.

2. **What happened this quarter.** Sales were about 57 crore, up 71% from a year ago. That sounds strong, but the same quarter last year was very weak. Against the June quarter, sales fell 27%. This company always sells more in the second half of the year. One quarter alone tells you little.

3. **What improved.** Profit from operations rose a lot against last year. The operating margin rose from about 16% to about 19%. The order book is 133 crore, three times what it was a year ago. If those orders are firm, they cover about six months of sales.

4. **Why the improvement is less than it looks.** The rise in profit starts from a bad quarter. The fair test is June against June, and there profit grew about 15%. Profit over the last twelve months is still below the year before last. Also, almost all of the margin gain comes from one cost line. The company paid its distributors much less as a share of sales than before, and nobody explains why. If that payment goes back to normal, the margin falls back to about 13% to 15%.

5. **What got worse.** Gross margin, which is what is left after the cost of materials, fell a lot against last year. Against the June quarter it actually rose. The review reads this as a shift toward more machines and fewer spares. The auditor pushed back: mix explains only part of the fall. The rest may be lower prices, higher input costs or a different machine mix, and the filing cannot tell which. Profit after tax grew less than operating profit, because income from investments fell.

6. **Cash: the weakest point.** In six months the company booked about 23 crore of profit, but only about 2 crore came in as cash. Even that 2 crore depends on one line of money it owes to others. Without it, cash from operations was negative. Customers owe the company more each month: unpaid bills rose by a quarter in six months. Over the last four years, only about half of profit turned into cash. This looks like a habit, not a one time problem.

7. **The balance sheet.** The company holds about 213 crore of investments, more than a third of everything it owns. Almost all of it is long term, and the company does not say what it holds. The short term, easy to sell part shrank, and the company borrowed for the first time in years. Most of that shrink was investments moved from short term to long term, not cash spent. So the borrowing may show a cash need, or it may be a choice to borrow rather than sell. Plant and equipment also jumped by about 47 crore with no note on what was bought.

8. **What management said and promised.** Management says it targets an operating margin of 18% to 20%, with no date. That target sits below what the company earned two years ago. It says the order book gives "strong visibility" for the months ahead. It says it will keep investing in its capabilities, with no amount given. It launched new products, but gave no orders or sales for them.

9. **Forensic red flags in plain words.** The chief financial officer leaves on 30 October and the company names no successor. His letter is dated 5 September, but the company told the market 38 days later. The 133 crore is called an "order book" in one place and an "open order pipeline" in another, and those are not the same thing. The press release says margins improved, while profit margin after tax actually fell. Some ratios on the slides do not match the balance sheet. None of these proves wrongdoing. Together they say the disclosure is loose.

10. **Open items the auditor flagged at close.** Three points were still not fixed when the work closed. First, the claim that the margin fall is only about mix is too strong, as point 5 explains. Second, the CFO letter went to an unnamed executive director, and the board has two. We do not know who held it for those 38 days. Third, the review should say both reasons the liquid cash shrank, as point 7 does. None of these changes the overall verdict.

11. **What this means for the thesis.** There is no thesis yet. This is a first look. The verdict is "proceed with flags": the numbers are filed cleanly and the auditor raised no concern, but cash, the unexplained cost line, the missing asset note and the CFO exit all need answers. Nothing here forces a decision. The real question is whether to start a full intake on this name.

12. **What to watch next quarter.** Watch the December quarter results, due by mid February. One number matters most: operating profit, which should reach about 10 crore if the business is truly back. Also watch four things. Does depreciation jump once the new asset starts to count? Does the distributor payment stay low, and does the company explain it? Is a new CFO named? Do the 133 crore of orders turn into sales and cash, or into more unpaid bills?

The next step is the Step 1 intake, if the operator wants to go further.

For the full half year, profit grew about 27%. The half looks better than the quarter because the same period last year started weak.

Full year cash flow is not in this filing. These answers sit in the annual report and in management replies. We drafted 14 questions for the company secretary. A first step would be a spear pass to decide whether this name deserves deeper work.

# Poonawalla Fincorp, Q2 FY27: the story in plain words

Sources: the committed review (work/review_poonawalla_q2fy27.md), the two A5 audits, and work/notion-thesis.md. No agent was re-run.

1. **First look, no earlier analysis on record.** There is no Notion page, no Decision Status, no entry zone and no tripwires for this company. So we cannot compare this quarter with any earlier view. This is a first read, not a thesis check.

2. **What the company does.** Poonawalla Fincorp is a lender. It lends to people and small businesses. Its main loans are against property, small fast consumer loans, loans to mid-sized firms, business loans, personal loans, used cars and gold. About half the book has security behind it. The other half does not.

3. **What happened this quarter.** Profit after tax was about 375 crore. That is 22% more than last quarter and about five times the same quarter last year. Last quarter had a one-off gain from selling loans. This quarter had none, so the profit is cleaner than it looks.

4. **Loan growth.** The loan book is about 74,000 crore. It grew 10% in three months and 55% in one year. That is very fast. Management's own target is 35 to 40% a year, and it is running ahead of that.

5. **Asset quality.** The share of bad loans fell to 1.2%. The cost of bad loans fell to 2.19% of the book. But in rupees, the losses did not fall. They stayed flat at about 375 crore. The ratios look better mainly because the book grew bigger. Also, the money set aside against weak loans fell for four quarters in a row. And in this same quarter the company sold about 160 crore of stressed loans to recovery firms. The filing does not say if those loans were already counted as bad.

6. **Margins.** Income grew well, and costs per rupee of income are far lower than a year ago. But this quarter, costs grew a little faster than income. The deck claims "strong operating leverage". This quarter does not show it. Borrowing costs also started to rise. Fees are an important part of profit, and a proposed insurance rule could cut some of them.

7. **Capital.** The company raised 2,500 crore of new equity in April. In one quarter, it used about half the room that raise created. Debt is now 4.3 times equity. Profit alone cannot fund this speed of growth. Expect another raise, or slower growth, within about a year.

8. **What management said, and how believable it is.** Management said low credit cost is "structural" and that profit will not suffer as rates rise. They gave no number for write-offs. They gave no level for their June 2028 return target. They gave no number for the insurance fee risk, after three questions on it. The CEO misstated the credit cost drop on the call. The tone is confident, but the claims about the future are mostly adjectives, not numbers. Treat them as unproven.

9. **Forensic red flags.** First, no document gives write-offs. Second, the money received for written-off loans has no clear line in the profit statement. A separate loss on loan sales also does not tie to its own note. Third, one of the two audit firms left after July, with no reason given. Fourth, an employee trust that the auditors do not review sold about 228 crore of company shares in six months. Fifth, two filings give different dates for the April share sale. Sixth, one table in the debt security annexure does not add up.

10. **Flagged open items.** The final check found four points still open. Two concern the stressed loans sold to recovery firms. The evidence suggests they were already bad loans. If so, bad loans before the sale rose a little in rupees, though the ratio still fell. The other two are wording fixes in the review. None of them changes the verdict.

11. **What this means for the thesis.** There is no thesis yet. The verdict is PROCEED WITH FLAGS. Profits are real and growing fast. The open question is whether low losses are real or only look low because the book is young and growing. The operator decides whether to start a full first study (Step 1) on this company.

12. **What to watch next quarter.** The results in mid January 2027 will settle most of this. Watch losses in rupees, not only the ratio. Watch for a write-off number. Watch whether borrowing costs rise faster than loan rates. Watch for any sign of a new equity raise. If rupee losses grow slower than the book, the bull case gets stronger. If the loss ratio goes back to 2.4% or higher, the bear case wins.
