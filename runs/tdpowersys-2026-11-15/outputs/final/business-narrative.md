## BUSINESS UNDERSTANDING NARRATIVE

TD Power Systems builds AC generators. A generator is the machine that turns the spinning shaft of a gas engine, gas turbine, steam turbine or water turbine into electricity, and the company also makes industrial motors and parts for railway traction motors. The filings give no revenue split by product, so the run did not establish the share of any line; management ranks gas engine generators first and puts steam turbine generators at about a quarter of revenue on a call. Gas engine and gas turbine generators go into data centre, distributed power and oil and gas plants, steam turbine generators go into captive, biomass and waste heat plants, and hydro generators are built one project at a time for India, Nepal and Vietnam. The engine or turbine maker cannot ship a power set without a qualified generator, and management says the generator is under 5% of the cost of a data centre project, which is a management claim. The buyers are the makers of prime movers: Siemens, INNIO Jenbacher and Triveni Turbine sit in the top ten, the top ten take 84% of revenue, and one unnamed customer rose from 14% to 23% of consolidated revenue in FY26. These buyers sign long agreements but pay per purchase order, the Siemens Energy ten year framework of August 2026 states no minimum volume, and management says growth comes from deeper share inside existing OEMs, not from new clients. Present demand comes from AI and data centre power in the US, which the tracker follows as "Hyperscaler and data-centre developer capex and behind-the-meter gas procurement" and "Major US gas turbine OEM identified in Q2 FY27 deck". Forward demand ties to "Global gas turbine backlog, slot reservations and OEM capacity additions (GE Vernova, Siemens Energy, MHI)", where backlogs of 116, 69 and 35 GW run delivery slots to 2031, and to "INNIO Jenbacher gas engine bookings, capacity ramp and lead times", with INNIO capacity planned to rise from 3.5 GW to about 10 GW by 2030. "Siemens Energy gas and steam turbine orders and its 10-year build-to-print framework" will show whether the August framework turns into priced orders, and "Nepal and South Asia hydropower awards, plus India CEA hydro and pumped storage commissioning" carries the hydro line. The peers temper this, because Triveni still calls data centre steam demand enquiries and its domestic orders fell 35% in Q1 FY27. Railway demand is shrinking, since management takes no fresh rail orders and will review the line at year end, and "Alstom traction motor stator and rotor supply under the 2017 long-term agreement" tracks it. The moat the run found sits in gas generators, where OEM qualification scores as a moderate lock-in and the Siemens framework as a strong partnership, but no contract shows a volume floor. Core generator designs are licensed from Toyo Denki, Voith and Siemens, licence expiry was not found and R&D is 0.5% of sales, so the design edge is rented, not owned. Hydro, motors and rail show no moat: hydro is project work, motors are 105 of 8,055 units ever supplied, and rail is winding down. The talent test and the competitor filing test scored zero, and the forward moat scan reads MODEST at 18.7 against a line of 25.

Export generator orders outrun cash conversion

TD Power Systems makes AC generators and motors for turbine and engine makers, from two plants in Bengaluru, one in Tumkur and a small one in Izmir. It had supplied 8,055 units by March 2026 and 8,772 by September 2026. FY26 consolidated revenue was Rs 1,856 Cr, up 45%, and profit was Rs 239 Cr. EBITDA margin was about 18%. About 70% of group sales went overseas. Ten customers took 84% of revenue, and the largest took 23%.

The order book has moved abroad and toward gas. Standalone exports rose 135% in FY26 while domestic sales fell 13%. Exports were 88 to 93% of quarterly order inflow, and domestic inflow fell 31% in H1 FY27. The order book grew from Rs 1,973 Cr in March 2026 to Rs 2,319 Cr in September 2026. H1 FY27 revenue was Rs 1,481 Cr, with Rs 841 Cr in Q2. Management set its FY27 revenue guide at Rs 2,200 Cr and raised it three times to Rs 2,900 to 3,000 Cr, beating each step before it raised it. The next leg is large generators of 40 to 200 MW. That leg rests on a Siemens Energy framework with no stated volume and one Rs 192 Cr order, with deliveries from August 2027. Rail goes the other way, with no fresh orders and a review at year end.

The backward scorecard reads AVERAGE, 45 of 100 on the core and 70 of 160 overall. Two things hold it down. FY17 to FY20 returns were poor, with ROCE between minus 0.1% and 8.6%. Capex and payables history before FY25 is missing, and missing data scores zero. The last three years show ROCE of 24%, 27% and 31%, with net cash and interest cover of 44 times. A strict reading of the scoring rule on capital employed gives a core of 39 and AVOID, and that question is open for the operator. The forward moat scan reads MODEST at 18.7, below the line of 25, with six categories active and none yet proven.

Cash is the weak link. Operating cash flow was 23% of profit in FY25 and 54% in FY26. Take out the rise in customer advances and FY26 falls to about 29%. H1 FY27 shows 101%, but only because the other payables and liabilities line added Rs 298 Cr. Without that line, H1 operating cash flow was minus Rs 86 Cr. Receivable days rose from 125 to 146 in FY26, and receivables grew 70% against revenue growth of 45%. The growth reading has support: 86% of receivables were not yet due at March 2026, and about 1% were over six months old. The structural reading has support too: cash conversion over FY21 to FY26 adds up to 47% of profit. The company also raised Rs 675 Cr of equity while it held net cash, and no filing states the use of the Rs 600 Cr QIP. Other current liabilities rose from Rs 190 Cr to Rs 430 Cr in six months, and no filing splits that rise.

Management earns a B for delivery. Of 20 testable promises over four quarters, 11 were delivered, 5 in part and 4 missed. Every revenue and order inflow promise was beaten. The misses were a 35% gross margin target, with Q4 FY26 at 31%, flat employee cost, rail growth and a big new US customer; the verifier reads the last one as unproven, not missed, and the grade holds. The large generator plan was promised within two to three months in May 2026 and then moved to the November call. Management declined, on all three calls, to give the data centre share of orders. The promoter stage rates governance CAUTION. The promoter group sold 99 lakh shares in one block in May 2025 for about Rs 456 Cr, and its holding fell from 34.4% to 26.9% in two years. It then bought Rs 75 Cr of new shares at Rs 600, allotted in September 2026, and three weeks later the QIP priced at Rs 775. The annual report prints the MD's pay at three different figures, and Rs 5.9 Cr of his commission came from subsidiaries. On the other side, an outside CEO from L&T-MHI Power Turbine Generators and GE Power joined in April 2026, no shares are pledged, CRISIL upgraded the rating to AA minus in June 2026, and mutual funds held 25.7% at September 2026.

The peers neither confirm nor refute the core claim, because none of them makes generators. What they show is that contract terms decide who keeps the margin in this upcycle. CG Power, with price variation clauses across almost its whole power order book, widened its margin, partly from price. Azad, with a 5% price band, held EBITDA at 36 to 38%. Triveni, on fixed price contracts, fell to 18% EBITDA from 26%. Management says TD has price variation clauses, but no filing shows how much of the book they cover. On US tariffs, Triveni held back orders and Azad saw no disruption. TD sells to engine and turbine makers as Azad does, and its record Q3 FY26 inflow of Rs 656 Cr shows no disruption so far. Triveni missed its own double digit FY26 guide with 9% growth, so a beaten guide is not a rule of this cycle.

The market is not the limit. Capacity and a few OEM order books are. The only sourced market size that fits the product is the global gas generator market, about USD 5.9 bn or Rs 56,200 Cr in 2026, and FY26 revenue was 3.3% of it. The serviceable slice could not be sized. Management's market claim is about nine times the sourced figure. Capacity is the number that binds: about Rs 3,200 Cr for FY28, and Rs 4,000 Cr by FY31 only if new capacity is built. No capex plan for that is filed, and capital commitments stand at Rs 40 Cr. One call transcript prints FY28 capacity as both Rs 2,200 Cr and Rs 3,200 Cr. The new guide also implies H2 FY27 revenue of Rs 709 to 759 Cr a quarter, below the Q2 figure of Rs 841 Cr.

Two readings fit the same facts. In the first, TD is a qualified supplier to a few prime mover makers that are short of capacity, and the Siemens Energy framework and a US company's Rs 192 Cr order for a TD design show the qualification is real. In the second, the growth is OEM capex passing through one vendor, with no volume floors, a top customer rising to 23%, cash funded by advances and a flat gross margin. The first Siemens purchase order with a value, and a lower top customer share in the FY27 annual report, would separate them. The nearer test is Q3 FY27 revenue against Rs 709 to 759 Cr, with order inflow back above revenue after Q2 came in at 0.98.
