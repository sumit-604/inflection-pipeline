# PVR INOX (PVRINOX): business narrative, phase 1 lite, 2026-11-12

Phase 1 lite. No valuation has run. This file carries no entry zone, no decision and no exit multiple. Units: annual report, results and presentation figures are in Rs million as printed; call and rating figures are in Rs Cr as spoken or printed. Each figure states its basis: audited (Ind AS 116 reported) or company adjusted (Ind AS 116 adjusted, unaudited).

## BUSINESS UNDERSTANDING NARRATIVE

PVR INOX runs about 1,800 cinema screens in 114 Indian cities, and two products carry the business. Movie tickets bring 53% of FY26 revenue: the company sells a seat for about two hours to watch a film it does not own, and the film owner takes about 45% of the box office. Food and beverage brings 31%, with popcorn, drinks and snacks sold at the counter and in the app to the same viewer, and this line earns the best profit per rupee. Advertising at 7% and online convenience fees at about 4% ride on the same seated audience. A viewer can do without any one cinema, because switching costs nothing, so the pull is the big screen and the premium formats, which make up about 285 screens or 16% of the network. The customers are moviegoers, no single customer brings more than 10% of revenue, and they are price sensitive: about 15% of FY26 tickets went through discount programmes such as Blockbuster Tuesdays, and the company now sells Rs 99, Rs 149 and Rs 79 value seats. Advertisers form the second customer class and buy film by film more than by category, while mall developers act as landlords and, in the asset light and FOCO (franchise owned, company operated) models, as joint investors. Present demand comes from the film slate, and three series track it: the Ormax monthly India gross box office and admissions, the Hollywood and pan India tentpole release calendar, and the film release count in UFO Moviez network release data. That demand is hit driven: industry gross box office rose 23% in Q2 FY27 with English films up 87%, while UFO counted 386 releases against 462 a year earlier. Demand should grow because India has about 7 screens per million people, and multiplexes keep replacing single screens: multiplex screens rose from 3,400 to 4,800 between 2019 and 2025 while all screens rose only from 9,527 to 10,003. The forward signals a reader can check outside the company are the developer and mall pipeline behind 138 signed capital light screens, competitor screen additions at Cinepolis India, Miraj and Mukta A2, and state ticket price caps, licence timelines and the CCI virtual print fee probe, which can slow that growth. Industry box office grew 4.9% a year over CY15 to CY25 on the annual report's own chart, against the 7% to 8% its text claims, so the run did not establish a faster growth rate for the market. In tickets and food the company holds a scale and location position of medium strength, at about 37% of multiplex screens, but its leases run 5 to 7 firm years and its premium brand is partial, so neither line shows a durable moat. The emerging moat scan scored 9.4 and classed NONE, and only two rows reached Moderate: the repaired balance sheet, which is a delivered result and not a forming moat, and the digital booking platform, with 72% of tickets sold online and convenience fee income up 25% in Q2 FY27. Talent asymmetry and the cannibalization barrier, categories 21 and 22, scored zero, because a rival destroys nothing to copy the scale, the formats or the online fees. Advertising shows no moat, since its income grew 1.3% in H1 FY27 while UFO's in cinema ad revenue grew 33% in Q1, and the film distribution arm is project led with no moat evidenced. The SMART format for Tier 3 and 4 towns is the one attempt at counter positioning, and with one cinema of four screens open, the run did not establish whether it works.

Cinema cash repaired, footfall and screens lag

PVR INOX is India's largest multiplex chain. It reported 1,798 screens in 359 properties for March 2026, and the same annual report also prints 1,789 and 1,782, a 16 screen gap nobody has explained. FY26 audited revenue was about Rs 66,460 million. Rent and other fixed costs make up about 57% of costs, so profit swings with occupancy. On the company's adjusted standalone figures, each admission brought Rs 432 of income and Rs 62 of EBITDA in FY26. On that arithmetic, profit before tax breaks even near 139 million admissions, against 150 million sold.

Three things are changing. First, borrowings less cash fell from Rs 9,522 million in March 2025 to Rs 1,619 million in March 2026. Rs 5,707 million of free cash after leases, interest and capex funded it, with Rs 2,221 million net from selling the 4700BC popcorn brand to Marico. The company states net cash of Rs 1,407 million at September 2026 after a Rs 300 Cr buyback. The balance sheet gives Rs 132 Cr, and Rs 8 Cr stays unreconciled. That net cash excludes Rs 58,079 million of lease liabilities. Second, new screens are moving to capital light models: 55% of FY26 additions and 77% of H1 FY27 openings. Third, a small SMART cinema format targets Tier 3 and 4 towns, with one cinema of four screens open since 4 September 2026.

The scorecard says AVOID on the reported basis, with a core score of 37 of 100 and a total of 57 of 160. The lease and merger years drive it: losses from FY21 to FY25 and a balance sheet that carries the leases. The sensitivity has four readings, and all four belong together. Reported, the core is 37, AVOID. Lease rebased, it is 44, AVERAGE, but only with the Rs 195 Cr gain from the 4700BC sale inside FY26 profit. Without that gain it is 27, AVOID. Lease rebased and without the gain it is 35, still AVOID. Profit has three bases. Audited FY26 profit was Rs 3,328 million in total and Rs 1,761 million from continuing operations. The company's adjusted figures are Rs 3,868 million and Rs 2,306 million. A Rs 871 million lease write back made up 31.6% of profit before tax and exceptional items. Goodwill of Rs 57,367 million is 77.7% of equity and 3.76 times tangible equity, tested as one unit with no headroom shown.

Cash is the strongest part of the record, and leases are its first claim. Operating cash flow was Rs 21,597 million in FY26, and receivables grew 4% against revenue growth of 17%. Lease cash paid was Rs 11,847 million, and Rs 12,904 million falls due in FY27. Volume has not grown with price. FY26 admissions of 150 million matched FY24's 151 million, so two years of growth came from price. In Q2 FY27 admissions rose 4% while ticket price and food spend per head each rose 14%. Company income grew 17% against industry box office growth of 23%. Audited ROCE was about 7.1% for FY26. The company's own measure, which excludes leases and merger goodwill, gives 10.2% for FY26 and 16.5% for the year to September 2026.

Management keeps its financial promises and misses its expansion promises. Across four calls it delivered 11 of 19 scored promises, part delivered 3 and missed 5. Gross debt fell from about Rs 760 Cr to about Rs 501 Cr as promised. Net cash arrived one quarter early. Margin reached 22.3% at 30.4% occupancy in Q2 FY27 on the adjusted basis. The FY27 screen plan fell from about 150 in February to 90 to 100 in October, and no call named the cut. SMART delivered four screens late against 28 to 30 promised. The calls grade B on credibility. Verifier B puts it at the B and C boundary for two reasons. The pre-COVID ROCE benchmark moved from 18% in February to about 13% in October. The Rs 790 Cr FY26 free cash headline likely includes Rs 227 Cr of sale proceeds, against about Rs 563 Cr from operations.

Governance carries the main open question. In early April 2026 the two promoters received anonymous letters alleging impropriety by certain employees, with developers named as the source of kickbacks. The company hired outside experts for a preliminary review, and it says that review did not show evidence of kickbacks. The executive in charge of growth, Pramod Arora, resigned on 4 May for personal reasons. The company set out the matter only on 7 September, in reply to exchange queries after a news report and an 8% share fall, and no call raised it. One reading is a closed personnel matter. The other is an unscoped problem with developers, who are the same counterparties as the capital light pipeline. A named final report or an audit committee statement would separate the two. The promoter review grades CAUTION, not CONCERN. Ajay Bijli had 2,944,444 shares pledged at September 2026, cut to 2,122,344 on 2 November. The Chairman draws a Rs 190 million brand fee whose basis is not disclosed. Executive pay is 561 times the median, and the audit committee chair resigned on 24 July. That review ran without its regulator database and court searches.

The peers are thin, and what they show complicates the story. No listed exhibitor peer has transcripts, so nothing tests PVR INOX's share, admissions or screen build. UFO, the cinema advertising vendor, grew ad revenue 33% in Q1 FY27 while PVR INOX's fell about 2%, which suggests part of the ad weakness is its own. UFO's base was low and UFO credits one film for much of it, so the contrast rests on one peer in a different business. Wonderla, the park operator, held or cut ticket price, 2% up in Q1 and 3% down in Q2, and grew footfall 6% to 7%, the opposite trade. Its Q2 price includes a new park priced lower, and in Q3 FY26 it also grew on price with flat footfall. Wonderla's spend per guest outside the ticket rose 11% in Q2, which supports the food spend direction.

The market is moderate in size and growth. The exhibition revenue pool is about Rs 17,840 Cr to Rs 18,410 Cr for FY26, and PVR INOX holds about 38% of the multiplex part. Holding that share flat gives revenue of about Rs 7,660 Cr in three years and Rs 8,430 Cr in five, about 6.6% and 5.9% a year. Gaining share needs the 150 to 175 screens a year now guided from FY28, which the company has not yet shown it can open. Management's 45,000 screen claim implies a pool 3.4 times the conservative estimate. H1 FY27 already shows a share slip of about 1.8 points. The market sizing ran with some web searches skipped, so the multiplex share of box office and industry FY26 admissions stay NOT FOUND.
