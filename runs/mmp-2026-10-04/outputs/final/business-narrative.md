# MMP Industries Ltd (NSE: MMP): business narrative, phase 1

Run 2026-10-05. Phase 1 lite synthesis. Stage 11 has not run, so this file holds no valuation, entry zone, decision or destination multiple.

## Business understanding narrative

MMP turns bought aluminium into powder, foil and wire at five plants near Nagpur. Powders made 61% of FY26 sales and about 90% of segment profit, and they are atomised, pyro, flake and leafing grades that explosives makers use as a sensitiser, AAC (autoclaved aerated concrete) block makers use as a foaming agent, and pesticide, paint and fireworks makers use in their products. Foils made 26% of sales and go into pharma strip and blister packs, food foil and printed packaging, while conductors made 12% of sales as bare aluminium wire and cable for overhead power lines. A buyer cannot easily drop a qualified powder grade that comes out of a hazardous process, but the run found no margin evidence of the same switching cost for foil or conductor buyers. The buyers are explosives makers, AAC block makers, pesticide makers, pharma packagers, state utilities and EPC contractors, plus AVL Metal Powders in Belgium under a long term sales contract. The company does not disclose customer names or concentration, so the run did not establish how concentrated its sales are. Pharma foil and utility insulator buyers qualify a vendor over 1.5 to 2 years by management's account, while conductor buyers order on tender and pay late. Present demand follows mining, housing, pharma and grid spending, which the tracker reads through India monthly coal production and explosives offtake, AAC block output and PMAY-U 2.0 housing approvals, India pharma exports and domestic production, and state utility payments and RDSS disbursement. The aluminium ingot price on LME and MCX sets revenue per tonne and buyer mood in every line. Forward demand rests first on exports, which grew 67% in FY26 and which management guides up 40% to 50% in FY27, a claim the AR27 export figure will test. The DGTR and CBIC order on Chinese foil, read with China foil import volumes, should lift domestic foil use, and listed explosives makers' volume commentary leads powder orders. PGCIL vendor registration and BIS licences gate the insulator, LT cable and AL59 conductor revenue that management plans. Powders are the only line with a plausible edge: four decades of process know how and the AVL and Toyo links, which the emerging moat scan rates Moderate as partnerships but which the margin does not yet confirm. Foils earned a 2.0% segment margin and conductors 4.9% in FY26, so neither line has a moat. Insulators and LT cable are startups with no moat yet, and the scan scored zero on talent asymmetry and cannibalization barrier (Categories 21 and 22), for a total of 11.6, just under the 12.0 floor.

## Powder engine funds three unproven startups

MMP Industries is a Nagpur aluminium converter with ₹824 Cr of FY26 revenue, up 19%. EBITDA margin fell about 1.3 points to 8.0%, and PAT fell 20% to ₹31 Cr. Powders gave 61% of sales at an 11.9% segment margin. Foils gave 26% at 2.0%, and conductors 12% at 4.9%. The promoter family holds 74.5% with no pledge. Q1 FY27 revenue was ₹233 Cr, up 27% on a quarter the Umred fire had depressed, with EBITDA at 9.0% and PAT at ₹13.7 Cr.

The transition is a move from bulk conversion toward integrated products that need customer approval. Three bets carry it. MMP Electricals makes polymer insulators. It sold ₹2.3 Cr in FY26 at a ₹3.3 Cr segment loss and ₹0.5 Cr in Q1 FY27, against a FY27 guide of ₹18 to 20 Cr. MMP Cables is building an 18,000 MTPA wire rod plant, with trials due by end Q3 FY27, and its budget rose from ₹13 to 15 Cr to ₹20 to 25 Cr in 11 weeks. An LT power cable pilot started production on 7 July 2026 at 100 MT a month and waits for BIS approval. The ₹85 to 90 Cr Umred cable greenfield moved from H2 FY27 to H1 FY28. Lidding foil and a 7 MW captive solar park are due in Q3 FY27. The plants get built: four of four completed plants met or beat their annual report dates. The commercial ramps slip: PGCIL registration, the insulator ramp, the LT cable launch, Umred and solar each moved at least once. On the filed record, no proof gate has fired.

Gate 0 scores 66 of 160 and classifies AVERAGE on a core score of 54 of 100. Two moat marks pass: scale rank among three listed peers and a gross margin gap over them. ROCE fell to 12.9% in FY26 from 16.3%. Of that 3.4 point fall, 2.4 points is the one time fire loss, and without it FY26 ROCE reads 15.3%. Net debt is 2.8 times EBITDA. Over nine years PAT grew 6.8% a year against 16.8% for revenue, and that gap is old, not a fire rebase. Associates gave 20.5% of FY26 PBT, and without them FY26 ROCE is about 10.9%. Two open framework readings turn the class to AVOID: a full window rule with the ROCE proxy rejected (core 38), and a confidence tier keyed to the three year window. Verifier C's two open readings cut the core to 50 or the moat to THIN, and AVERAGE holds under both. The emerging moat scan scored 11.6, class NONE, with three Moderate categories and none Strong.

Cash is where the record splits. FY26 operating cash flow was ₹53 Cr. Of that, ₹17 Cr was short term borrowing booked inside operations and ₹19 Cr was extra supplier credit. Net of both, operating cash was ₹17 Cr, about a quarter of EBITDA, against about ₹56 Cr of capex. Free cash flow was negative ₹2.3 Cr. Parent cash was ₹0.6 Cr at year end. Of ₹185 Cr group debt, 77% falls due within a year. Parent guarantees for subsidiaries reach ₹67 Cr, 22% of standalone net worth. The insurer has paid nothing yet on a ₹7.9 Cr claim, which is 47% of a fire loss the FY25 annual report called fully insured. The other side is real. Standalone trade receivables fell 1.4% while standalone revenue rose 19%. Receivable days dropped to 39 from 47, and 96% of receivables were not yet due. CRISIL rates the bank lines BBB+/Stable, calls working capital management efficient, and rated ₹135 Cr of lines, up from ₹80 Cr. The run cannot yet say whether the weak cash conversion comes from growth or from the business itself.

Management earns a C on credibility. Of 18 closed promises, 7 were delivered, 2 partly and 9 missed, and Verifier B would move one more to partial. The core held. Powders grew 15% against a single digit guide, and H2 FY26 powders rose 25% against a 20 to 25% guide. The new and tender lines missed. Conductors missed three guides in a row, and Q1 FY27 conductor revenue fell 42% to ₹17 Cr. The FY27 revenue guide fell from about 20 to 25% to 15 to 18% within 11 weeks. Older pledges lapsed too. The July 2022 call promised no external borrowing, a 60 to 70 day working capital cycle, a gross margin near 27% and five times asset turn. FY26 shows ₹185 Cr of debt, a cycle of 89 to 103 days by basis, and a 20.5% gross margin. Foil conversion utilisation was 45 to 50% in 2022 and about 45% in 2026.

The promoter read is CAUTION, not CONCERN. There is no pledge, no promoter selling and no SEBI finding. The April 2025 Umred fire killed seven workers and stopped the powder plant for about 50 days. Police booked three officials. One is a general manager whose name and age match the whole time director in the annual report. That link stays an inference until the FIR text or a company reply settles it. Both long serving independent directors left on 10 August 2026 at the end of their terms, and the executive chairman joined the audit committee. Associate profit of ₹8.2 Cr came with a ₹0.1 Cr cash dividend from Star Circlips, an unlisted promoter group company that also holds 4.6% of MMP. Against that, two new independents with outside careers joined, and CRISIL lifted its issuer not cooperating tag once management shared information.

Peers confirm the direction and dispute the size. Apar and Maan agree that high metal prices made buyers defer orders and that approvals are slow. Apar's Q1 FY27 conductor revenue rose 20%, with volume down 7%, while MMP's fell 42%. Much of MMP's conductor weakness therefore looks company specific. No peer blames state utility or RDSS payment delays, which is MMP's main explanation. Both peers hedge metal back to back and report no metal inventory gain. MMP booked a ₹2 to 2.5 Cr inventory gain in Q4 FY26, which points to open metal exposure. Maan and Apar report Gulf shipments halted and freight up 5 to 10 times, which cuts against the 40 to 50% export guide. Verifier D notes those peers sell extrusions and conductors, not powder. No listed peer makes aluminium powder or pharma foil, so those claims rest on MMP's own word. Peers run working capital at 45 to 60 days against MMP's 102.5 on a purchase basis.

The market is smaller than the headline. The run sizes the serviceable market at ₹19,760 Cr, but 91% of it is conductors and insulators, where MMP is tender bound or new. The core powder and foil market is about ₹1,850 Cr against ₹719 Cr of FY26 revenue in those lines, so headroom is 2.6 times. MMP already holds about 42% of domestic powder by value. Share rules and capacity give a 9.6% three year revenue CAGR, below the 15 to 18% FY27 guide. At constant prices, powders and foil need about ₹23 Cr more capacity by FY29 and ₹138 Cr by FY31, and management plans no powder or foil capex for two years. Runway is MODERATE. Management's only market figure covers the whole Indian aluminium industry, 5.3 times the run's conservative TAM. The insulator peak of ₹130 to 140 Cr would need a 7.0 to 7.5% share of a ₹1,865 Cr market, above the 5 point share gain ceiling.
