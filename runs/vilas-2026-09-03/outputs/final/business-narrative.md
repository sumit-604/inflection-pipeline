# Vilas Transcore (VILAS): Business Understanding

## Business Understanding Narrative

Vilas Transcore cuts and stacks cold-rolled grain-oriented electrical steel, called CRGO, into laminations and cores. These parts form the magnetic core of a transformer. The core carries the magnetic flux, and low-loss grain-oriented steel keeps the transformer from wasting energy as heat, so a transformer maker cannot skip it. CRGO work is 98% of revenue. Four newer lines sit at or near zero revenue: nanocrystalline cores, radiators, copper conductors, and high-voltage bushings.

Customers are transformer makers. Named buyers include Voltamp, Shilchar, Electrotherm, ECE Industries, and Kirloskar Electric. One related buyer, Atlas Transformers, is majority-owned by the managing director and trades on both sides of the book at about 12.5% of FY25 revenue. Buyers qualify a processor over years, so switching is slow once a supplier is in. The rating agency puts the single largest customer at 40 to 55% of revenue, but the annual report discloses no customer split, so the run could not confirm the figure.

Demand comes from transmission and distribution build-out. Each gigawatt of new solar or wind needs step-up and evacuation transformers, which pulls CRGO core demand. The trackable drivers are named: PGCIL vendor approval, CEA National Electricity Plan transmission data, marquee OEM order books, the DGTR anti-dumping decision, and monthly renewable-capacity bulletins. This demand is externally verifiable.

Forward growth rests on the tripled capacity filling and on the four new lines converting. Unit-3 lifted CRGO nameplate from 12,000 to 36,000 MTPA in July 2025. The new lines carry higher value-add in theory but no earnings history yet.

The moat sits only in the core CRGO line, and it is moderate: OEM qualification history and processing scale among the few qualified Indian CRGO processors. The Emerging Moat scan scored 11 and classified NONE. The four new lines have no moat established. Capacity build is growth, not a moat.

## CRGO converter tripling capacity into unproven lines

Vilas processes imported CRGO steel for transformer makers, from a base in Vadodara. It imports almost all its CRGO steel, so a landed-cost swing hits margin directly. It is a price-taker converter, not a brand.

Capacity tripled in July 2025, from 12,000 to 36,000 MTPA. Utilisation sat near 55% in FY26 at about 19,856 tonnes. Four new lines aim to lift the mix above plain conversion: nanocrystalline cores, radiators, copper conductors, and HV bushings. Three of the four have no commercial revenue yet.

Gate 0 scored 74 of 160, an AVERAGE reading. The core score was 59 of 100. A STRONG four-moat profile could not lift the grade, because the core sat below the 60 threshold the moat overlay needs. One block did the damage: cash generation quality scored 3 of 20. Growth and balance sheet both scored strongly.

FY25 operating cash flow was minus Rs 35.46 Cr while PAT rose. Operating profit before working-capital changes rose 56% in the same year, from Rs 33.92 Cr to Rs 52.97 Cr. The cash gap is a working-capital build ahead of the Unit-3 ramp. Inventory was 100% raw material; receivables were 90% non-related and 99.5% current by ageing. It is a timing story, not a margin collapse, but it is still a real cash-conversion miss for the year. Payables fell while receivables and inventory grew, and that part does not explain itself.

The credibility grade is C, and it splits cleanly. The physical build earns trust: all four plants delivered, and the two old units run above nameplate. The rupee guidance does not: FY26 revenue landed near Rs 470 Cr against a Rs 600 Cr target, and the FY27 target was cut from Rs 1,000 Cr to Rs 750 to 780 Cr. The sharpest gap is older. The FY24 annual report guided 35 to 40% FY25 revenue growth; actual was 14%; management never named the miss. PGCIL approval and the export ambition both fell out of the calls with no walk-back.

Peers confirm the two facts that hurt most. Three independent peers report the same CRGO price crash and the same war-driven transformer-oil shock. JAYBEE, the one true CRGO peer supplied, names Vilas twice as the consistently better-margin processor. JAYBEE also contradicts two Vilas claims: it says there is no CRGO safeguard duty, and it names NLMK among mills that faced price pressure, against Vilas's claim that NLMK held firm. JAYBEE fears an industry oversupply price-war back to 5 to 7% EBITDA margins, and names Vilas's own expansion as part of that race.

The core CRGO market is Rs 9,000 to 10,500 Cr. Filling the tripled capacity implies about 23% revenue CAGR over three years from the core alone. The blended figure reaches about 26% only if the unproven new lines carry weight. Management's cited transformer-market TAM is inflated about 4.7 times, because it counts the whole transformer industry, not the CRGO-processing slice.
