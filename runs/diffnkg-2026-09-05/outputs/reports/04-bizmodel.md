## SECTION 1: THE BUSINESS MODEL IN PLAIN ENGLISH

### 1A. One-line description
Diffusion Engineers makes the metal alloys and equipment that stop industrial machines wearing out, and it
fixes them when they do (AR p.18-19, "Company Overview").

### 1B. Money flow chain, one chain per revenue stream

**Stream 1: Welding consumables (electrodes, flux-cored wires, thermal spray powders, cold-repair compounds)**
[Ferro-alloys, powdered metals, steel plates, metal wire — AR p.14] → [Diffusion melts, coats, and shapes these
into welding rods and wires with proprietary alloy formulas — AR p.31] → [it delivers finished electrodes and
wires to a factory, dealer, or job site] → [the buyer is an OEM, a dealer, or the plant's own maintenance
team — AR p.11-12] → [the buyer pays on standard trade terms per invoice, repeat and recurring — AR p.163
revenue note].

**Stream 2: Wear plates and wear parts**
[Diffusion's own flux-cored wire, plus forgings and castings — AR p.31] → [an open-arc welding process
overlays a hard, abrasion-resistant surface onto steel plate, or builds a ready-to-fit part such as a crusher
liner or roller — AR p.31] → [it delivers a bolted-on plate or a swap-in part] → [buyer is a cement, steel,
mining, or power plant, direct or via OEM] → [paid per order; a rental model for wear parts was introduced in
FY25, adding a recurring lease-style payment on top of the one-time sale — AR p.31].

**Stream 3: Superconditioning / reconditioning services (in-situ job work)**
[A worn kiln shell, roller, or crusher part already on the customer's site — AR p.29-30] → [Diffusion's
technicians travel to the site and rebuild the worn surface using its own consumables, restoring or exceeding
original spec — AR p.21-22, "Superconditioning Edge"] → [it delivers a repaired, operating asset without a
capex replacement] → [buyer is the plant operator, often under a standing maintenance agreement] → [paid per
service call or under an annual maintenance contract with minimum call-out levels — AR p.51].

**Stream 4: Heavy engineering equipment (kilns, rollers, fans, tipplers)**
[Steel plate, castings, forgings, precision-machined components — AR p.31] → [Unit IV in Khapri fabricates and
machines large, bespoke equipment (10 to 500 tonnes) to a customer's engineering spec — AR p.32] → [it
delivers a finished piece of heavy equipment, e.g. a Tandem Tippler or Compound Cast Roller — AR p.51] →
[buyer is a cement, steel, mining, or power plant, often government or PSU-linked] → [paid against milestone
billing over the project's execution period — AR p.52 mitigation note].

**Trading (allied products)**: Diffusion also trades third-party welding equipment, powders, and sealants
(Rs262.53 Mn standalone FY26, AR p.163) as a complementary revenue line that deepens the customer relationship
without needing its own manufacturing capacity (AR p.24).

### 1E. The chai-stall-uncle version
Think of Diffusion like the uncle who does not just sell you a new tyre when your bike tyre wears out. He also
sells the rubber compound the tyre is made from, he can re-tread your old tyre so it lasts longer, and if your
whole wheel assembly is worn out, he can build you a new one from scratch. Big factories that crush rock,
grind cement, or roll steel wear out their machine parts every day from friction and heat, the same way a
bike tyre wears from the road. Diffusion sells the metal rods and wires used to build tough surfaces onto
those parts, sells ready-made wear plates and parts, builds whole heavy machines, and sends its own
technicians to repair worn parts on site instead of asking the factory to buy new ones. It makes money four
different ways from the same "wear and tear" problem, and because it makes its own raw wire, it saves a step
compared to a plate maker who buys wire from someone else.

### 3D. Unit economics — the physics of the business

Because Diffusion sells four structurally different products (consumables by weight/volume, wear plates by
area, wear parts by piece, and heavy engineering by project), there is no single clean "unit". The closest
disclosed unit-level datapoint is capacity in the wear-plate/hardfacing line:

- **Unit**: one square metre of wear-overlay/cladding surface produced (the metric the company itself
  discloses for the Nimji capacity expansion).
- **Capacity added in FY26**: 18 torches (three machines) added 16,848 m²/year of hardfacing capacity,
  lifting total wear-overlay/cladding throughput to more than 70,000 m²/year (AR p.49).
- **Revenue per unit**: NOT FOUND (no Rs/m² realisation disclosed).
- **Cost per unit**: NOT FOUND (no per-m² cost breakdown disclosed).
- **Volume drivers**: capacity expansion (Nimji doubling under way), order inflow from cement/steel/mining
  capacity additions (AR p.47-48).
- **Price drivers**: input-cost pass-through via quarterly repricing clauses on long contracts (AR p.48);
  premium for CCO/ceramic composite over commodity AR-steel wear plates (AR p.45-46).
- **Cost drivers**: nickel/chromium/ferro-alloy prices (globally traded, volatile — nickel moved ~18% in Q1
  2025 alone, AR p.48); electricity/fuel tariffs at heat-intensive plants (AR p.48, mitigated by 1.4MW solar
  at Nimji); employee cost (Rs483.88 Mn consolidated FY26, AR p.163-adjacent notes).
- **Incremental margin / operating leverage**: management explicitly attributes FY26/Q1FY27 EBITDA margin
  gains partly to "operating leverage" from higher volumes and better absorption of fixed costs (Inv. Pres.
  slides 30, 34: standalone EBITDA margin rose to 12.67% in Q1FY27 from 11.79% in Q1FY26 on 30.7% volume/
  revenue growth); this points to real but moderate operating leverage, since a fixed-cost-heavy, five-plant
  manufacturing base should show more margin expansion per unit of revenue growth than the ~90 bps seen.

PRIMARY method: P/E on normalised, one-time-adjusted consolidated earnings, cross-checked against listed
Indian welding-consumables/capital-goods peers (Esab India, Ador Welding, GEE Ltd).
SECONDARY method: DCF, explicitly modelling the working-capital drag (rising receivable/NWC days) and the
ongoing Nimji/Unit IV capacity-expansion capex.
TERTIARY method: EV/EBITDA cross-check against capital-goods/engineering peers, particularly relevant while
the company is mid-capex-cycle and reported net profit carries IPO-related base-effect noise.
Cycle stage that matters for valuation: early-to-mid capacity-expansion cycle, post-IPO (listed Oct-2024),
with capacity roughly doubling; near-term earnings should be read against this capex ramp, not treated as a
steady-state run-rate.

6. **Q**: What proportion of FY26 revenue was one-time/project-driven (Heavy Engineering milestone billing)

   Reassuring: recurring share is large and growing.

7. **Q**: What is the status and expected commercial contribution of the UAE (Middle East) operation and the

   Reassuring: near-term revenue contribution with named contracts.
