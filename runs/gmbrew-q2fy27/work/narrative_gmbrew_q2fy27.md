# G M Breweries, Q2 FY27: the story in plain words

Source: the committed review (work/review_gmbrew_q2fy27.md), the A5 audit (work/audit_gmbrew_q2fy27.md) and work/notion-thesis.md. No agent was re-run.

1. **What the company does.** G M Breweries makes country liquor at one plant in Maharashtra. It buys spirit, bottles it and sells it. The state takes about three quarters of every rupee of sales as duty and tax. So the useful sales figure is what is left after duty. This report uses that figure.

2. **Earlier analysis.** First look, no earlier analysis on record. There is no Notion page, no company memory file and no spear pass for this name. So this is a first read of the business, not a check against a thesis.

3. **What happened this quarter.** Sales after duty grew about 18% from a year ago. Profit after tax grew about 13%, to Rs 39 Cr. Profit grew slower than sales. The numbers for the parent company and the group are the same, because the only subsidiary is tiny.

4. **What improved.** Sales are at the highest level of any quarter the filing shows on its own. Most of the profit gain came from the liquor business, not from interest or investment income. For the full half year, profit grew about 27%. The half looks better than the quarter because the same period last year started weak.

5. **What got worse.** The profit margin fell this quarter. The cause is one line: other expenses rose by about 63%. The filing does not say what those expenses are. Material cost was steady this quarter. But last year it moved a lot from quarter to quarter, so it can swing the margin again.

6. **Cash.** The business turned more than all of its profit into cash this half. Last year it turned only about two thirds into cash. The gain did not come from collecting money faster. It came from paying some bills more slowly and from smaller payouts on other dues. Without the slower supplier payments, cash would roughly equal profit. That is still good, but less strong than the headline.

7. **Balance sheet and where the money goes.** The company has no debt. Customers pay in about two days. The plant itself is small. Most of the assets are not part of the liquor business: about two thirds of the assets are investments and investment property. This half the company put almost all its operating cash into more investments. That money earns only about 3% a year before tax.

8. **What the filing tells us about plans.** Very little. There is no guidance, no conference call and only one item on the board agenda, the results. Two quiet signals exist. A dormant subsidiary with a real estate name started to receive money from the parent. And a large capital project sits unfinished, bigger than the plant itself, with no date given. Neither is explained.

9. **Forensic red flags in plain words.** The auditor signed the review about an hour before the board meeting began. Yet the review says the board had already approved the results. The auditor's report also names only the quarter, while the company says the review covers the half year. The chairman rents property to the company and earns more as landlord than as managing director. That lease does not show on the balance sheet. The group accounts seem to count the money sent to the subsidiary twice. A tax payment and a capex figure do not match, and the gap sits between them.

10. **Open items still flagged.** We do not know what is in other expenses, in some large asset lines, or in the unfinished project. We do not know what kind of investments the company holds. Full year cash flow is not in this filing. These answers sit in the annual report and in management replies. We drafted 14 questions for the company secretary. Nothing has been sent and nothing was written to Notion.

11. **What this means for the thesis.** There is no thesis yet. The verdict is to proceed with flags, which means the numbers can be used but the open points must be weighed. The picture is a steady, cash rich liquor business wrapped in a growing pile of low yield investments. Any value work must split the two parts. A first step would be a spear pass to decide whether this name deserves deeper work.

12. **What to watch next quarter.** The key test is the Q3 margin after duty, due by mid February 2027. If it climbs back to last year's level, the cost rise was a one time event. If it slips further, the higher cost is here to stay. Also watch the shareholding pattern due this month, whether the auditor signs after the board next time, whether more money goes to the subsidiary, and whether supplier payments return to normal.
