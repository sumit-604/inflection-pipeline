# B09 — TAM / SAM / SOM Market Sizing: Sical Logistics Ltd (SICALLOG)
Run date: 2026-07-28 | Model: claude-sonnet-5 | Stage: B09-tam

Sical operates three structurally distinct businesses (per B04): Mining Logistics
(overburden removal / coal transport, 43% of FY26 revenue), Terminals (CFS +
MMLP, 36%), and Warehousing & 3PL (21%). Each has a different market, different
peer set, and different growth ceiling. This report sizes each segment
separately (Section 2), then aggregates to one company-relevant TAM/SAM/SOM
(Section 3 onward), per the operator's instruction. All figures ₹ Crore unless
stated. FY26 consolidated revenue base = ₹385.68 Cr (screener Data_Sheet FY26 /
FY26 audited results, ₹3,857 mn).

---

## SECTION 1: MARKET DEFINITION

### 1A — Precise boundaries, by segment

**Segment 1 — Mining Logistics (overburden removal & coal-handling services)**
- Product scope: overburden (waste-rock) removal and coal handling/transport
  under multi-year contracts at open-cast coal projects (OCPs). Excludes: full
  Mine Developer-cum-Operator (MDO) contracts that bundle mine-development
  capex (Adani/Thriveni's model); underground mining; non-coal mineral OB
  removal (Sical's own capability line references "minerals and overburden"
  historically, but FY26 revenue and growth strategy are 100% coal-focused per
  the deck).
- Geographic scope: India; specifically the coalfields where Sical holds
  contracts — Madhya Pradesh and Odisha (AR 2024-25 Board's Report, "State of
  affairs," p.5, standalone) plus Chhattisgarh (new SECL Porda-Chimtapani,
  Investor Presentation p.11-12).
- Customer scope: PSU coal miners (Coal India subsidiaries — SECL, NCL) and
  the wider MDO/outsourced-contract-mining channel.
- Channel scope: direct multi-year tendered contracts, not spot/open market.
- Price segment: large fixed multi-year unit-rate contracts.

**Segment 2 — Terminals (Container Freight Stations + Multi-Modal Logistics
Parks)**
- Product scope: off-dock container handling, customs clearance support,
  storage (CFS) + rail-linked freight-village handling — rail siding,
  container yard, warehousing (MMLP). Excludes on-dock port terminal
  operations (Sical does not operate the ports themselves) and non-rail-linked
  ICDs outside its footprint.
- Geographic scope: Chennai, Tuticorin, Vizag (CFS, South/East coast) +
  Chennai and (upcoming) Bengaluru MMLP.
- Customer scope: EXIM importers/exporters, shipping lines; domestic
  rail-freight users (automobiles, food grain, iron & steel, cement — Investor
  Presentation p.15).
- Channel scope: customs-bonded, licensed off-dock facility, land-parcel
  based — a structural entry barrier (B04).
- Exclusions: North India (46% of national ICD/CFS count) and West India (29%)
  — outside Sical's footprint (Investor Presentation p.36).

**Segment 3 — Warehousing & 3PL**
- Product scope: contract warehousing, inbound/outbound stock handling, order
  processing, kitting/repair/shrink-wrap value-add, and (new, FY26) a
  pharmaceutical "national super-stockist" distribution model.
- Geographic scope: at least 4 states disclosed for the PVLPL entity as of
  FY25 AR (Maharashtra, Goa, Chhattisgarh, Uttar Pradesh); full FY26 state list
  for the now-20-warehouse network NOT FOUND.
- Customer scope: electricals & electronics, paints, renewable energy,
  beverages, pharmaceuticals — mid-market B2B accounts.
- Exclusions: e-commerce-fulfilment-scale warehousing, Grade-A
  institutional/REIT-backed warehousing (DP World/ESR/Prologis-type
  developers), pure cold-chain specialists, cross-border freight forwarding.

A wrong definition makes every later number useless, and the biggest risk here
is conflating "India's total warehousing market" (all verticals, all
operators, including e-commerce giants' captive facilities Sical will never
touch) with Sical's addressable slice. That conflation is flagged explicitly
in Section 2/3 below and is the single largest driver of the mgmt_claim_ratio.

## SECTION 2: TAM ESTIMATION, MULTIPLE METHODS

### Segment 1 — Mining Logistics

**Method 2, bottom-up (primary — no independent top-down figure could be
sourced):**
- India coal production, FY25 actual: 1,047.6 Mt (Ministry of Coal / PIB,
  "coal crossed 1 billion tonne milestone in FY25" — vs 997.8 Mt FY24).
- Opencast share of production: 94.98% (multiple industry/academic sources,
  consistent with an 83-95% range cited across sources) → opencast tonnage
  ≈ 1,047.6 × 0.9498 = **995.2 Mt**.
- Per-tonne OB-removal/coal-handling contract rate: NOT disclosed by Sical or
  any peer (B04 flag). Derived from Sical's own 7 disclosed OCP contracts
  (Investor Presentation p.12, contract value ÷ contract quantity):
  - Lajkura 2014: ₹580 mn / 14.2 Mt = ₹40.8/tonne
  - Bharatpur 2014/2017: ₹2,500 mn / 42 Mt = ₹59.5/tonne
  - Samleshwari 2016: ₹2,630 mn / 37 Mt = ₹71.1/tonne
  - Lajkura 2016: ₹3,850 mn / 53 Mt = ₹72.6/tonne
  - Jhingurda 2017: ₹3,040 mn / 28 Mt = ₹108.6/tonne
  - Amlohri 2018: ₹15,820 mn / 145 Mt = ₹109.1/tonne
  - Nigahi 2023: ₹26,560 mn / 472.5 Mt = ₹56.2/tonne
  - Range ₹40.8-109.1/tonne; simple average of the 7 = **₹74.0/tonne**;
    most-recent large contract (Nigahi, 2023) = **₹56.2/tonne** (used as the
    conservative anchor — recent and large-scale, vs. the 2014 figure which
    may reflect decade-old pricing).
  - This rate is a **derived, non-disclosed** estimate — flagged, not an
    audited or company-confirmed figure.
- Addressable channel filter: 54% of India's coal production is already
  produced via the outsourced Mine Developer-cum-Operator (MDO) route (over
  40 mines, >500 Mt annual capacity outsourced — thePrint/Mondaq, web search).
  This 54% figure is applied at the **SAM** stage (Section 3), not TAM — TAM
  below assumes 100% of opencast tonnage is theoretically monetizable as an
  outsourced service.

**TAM (Mining), conservative** = 995.2 Mt × ₹56.2/tonne = **₹55,930 Cr** ≈ **₹55,900 Cr**
**TAM (Mining), realistic** = 995.2 Mt × ₹74.0/tonne = **₹73,645 Cr** ≈ **₹73,600 Cr**

**Method 3, peer aggregation (corroborating, not headline):**
- Adani Enterprises Mining Services segment revenue, FY25: ₹1,233.67 Cr (web
  search, Adani FY25 results commentary).
- Thriveni Earthmovers consolidated turnover: ~₹9,250 Cr (FY23 estimate, press
  source) — **STALE** (>2 years old from run date; still within the 4-year
  usable window, so directional only, lower confidence).
- Sical Mining Logistics: ₹164.8 Cr (FY26).
- Named organized players sum ≈ ₹10,650 Cr from just 3 disclosed players
  (materially incomplete — misses SECL/NCL in-house crews, and unnamed
  regional OB contractors). If this ₹10,650 Cr represents ~40-70% of the
  organized (outsourced) segment, implied organized market ≈ ₹15,000-26,500 Cr
  — **this triangulates below the bottom-up SAM figure derived in Section 3**
  (₹30,200 Cr), consistent with taking the lower, more conservative estimate
  and flagging peer data as thin (B06 also found **no peer transcript
  coverage of the mining/coal-logistics segment**).

**B06 cross-check, explicitly weighed:** B06 found no peer-transcript
coverage of this segment and flagged that ALLCARGO's own macro commentary
shows FY26 demand **decelerating**, not accelerating. That finding is
diversified-logistics-wide commentary (not mining-specific), but it argues
against reading the OB-removal/coal-production growth charts as an
unambiguous runway-boom story. This tension is carried into Sections 4 and 5.

### Segment 3 — Warehousing & 3PL

**Method 1, top-down (broad, flagged for over-breadth):**
- India warehousing market: ₹1.4-1.5 trillion FY25 (chart point: ₹1,400 bn) →
  ₹2.3-2.5 trillion FY30 (chart point: ₹2,359 bn), 10-13% CAGR (Investor
  Presentation p.37). Independently corroborated in order of magnitude by
  IMARC/GrandView/others (dispersion 2-3x across providers by methodology —
  itself a confidence flag).
- 3PL + e-commerce together are stated to be 55-60% of total warehousing
  demand (same source) — **but e-commerce and 3PL are not split out**, so this
  cannot cleanly isolate Sical's addressable (non-e-commerce, mid-market 3PL)
  slice (input gap, flagged below).
- Applying the 55-60% (midpoint 57.5%) factor as a rough 3PL-relevant filter:
  0.575 × ₹1,45,000 Cr (FY25 midpoint) = **₹83,375 Cr ≈ ₹83,400 Cr** — used as
  the **realistic** figure, with the caveat that this still likely includes
  e-commerce-fulfilment demand Sical does not compete for.

**Method 2, bottom-up (conservative, used as headline):**
- Organized warehouse space, India: 450-500 mn sq ft, midpoint 475 mn sq ft
  (Investor Presentation p.37).
- Sical's own realized yield: Warehousing & 3PL revenue ₹81.5 Cr (FY26) ÷ 1.2
  mn sq ft (Investor Presentation p.16) = **₹679/sq ft/year**.
- This is far below the implied all-India average yield (₹1,45,000 Cr ÷ 475
  mn sq ft ≈ ₹3,050/sq ft/year), which strongly suggests the ₹1.4-1.5 trillion
  headline figure captures a broader definition (real-estate/leasing value,
  in-house corporate warehousing, e-commerce mega-facilities) than the
  fee-for-service 3PL model Sical actually runs.
- **TAM (Warehousing), conservative** = 475 mn sq ft × ₹679/sq ft =
  **₹32,253 Cr ≈ ₹32,300 Cr**.
- **TAM (Warehousing), realistic = ₹83,400 Cr** (from Method 1 above).

## SECTION 3: SAM & SOM

### 3A — SAM (five filters applied to TAM, by segment)

**Mining:** Product fit and customer fit are already baked into the TAM
definition (coal-only, contract OB-removal services). The dominant remaining
filter is **channel**: only the outsourced/MDO-route portion of coal
production is realistically contestable by a private contractor like Sical;
Coal India's own departmental crews will not tender out. Applying the 54%
outsourced-route factor (thePrint/Mondaq, web search):
- SAM conservative = ₹55,900 × 0.54 = **₹30,186 Cr ≈ ₹30,200 Cr**
- SAM realistic = ₹73,600 × 0.54 = **₹39,744 Cr ≈ ₹39,700 Cr**
- A further geography/capability filter (Sical's contracts sit in only 3 of
  ~5 major coal-bearing states — Chhattisgarh, Odisha, MP, but not Jharkhand
  or Maharashtra) would narrow this further, but no data source allows a
  precise haircut — **NOT FOUND, flagged, not applied** (directional risk
  only, SAM here should be read as an upper bound within the MDO-route pool).

**Terminals:** Geography filter — Sical's 3 CFS sit in the South/East region,
which per the national ICD count split is South 17% + East 8% = 25% of
national facility count (used as a rough value proxy, Investor Presentation
p.36):
- SAM conservative = ₹6,000 × 0.25 = **₹1,500 Cr**
- SAM realistic = ₹7,000 × 0.25 = **₹1,750 Cr**

**Warehousing:** Already narrowly defined bottom-up (Sical's own realized
yield already reflects its specific vertical/geography mix), so:
- SAM conservative = **₹32,300 Cr** (= TAM conservative, no further haircut)
- SAM realistic = **₹83,400 Cr** (= TAM realistic; flagged that this may still
  include inaccessible e-commerce-dominated demand — input gap)

**Aggregate SAM, conservative = ₹30,200 + ₹1,500 + ₹32,300 = ₹64,000 Cr**
**Aggregate SAM, realistic = ₹39,700 + ₹1,750 + ₹83,400 = ₹1,24,850 Cr**

**SAM as % of TAM (conservative pairing, used as headline per conservative
bias):** 64,000 / 94,200 = **67.9%**

### 3B — SOM at 3 and 5 years

Current SAM share = current revenue ÷ SAM (conservative) = 385.68 / 64,000 =
**0.60%**.

Sical's current SAM share is extremely low (well under 1%). This matters for
how the standard share-gain rules (1-2pp normal / 3-5pp aggressive) should be
applied: at a ~5-10% starting share, a 1-2pp gain is a modest relative move;
at a 0.6% starting share, even a 1pp gain nearly triples the company's
relative position. **Flagged explicitly**: the pp framework, applied
literally, mechanically produces high implied CAGRs off this tiny base. Given
no competitor-exit/acquisition catalyst is identified (ruling out the >5pp
bucket) and B06's finding of decelerating peer demand (arguing against the
aggressive 3-5pp bucket), this stage uses the **low end of the "normal" 1-2pp
range**:

- **3yr: +1.0pp** → SAM share 0.60% + 1.0% = 1.60% of SAM
  - SOM_3yr = 64,000 × 0.0160 = **₹1,026 Cr**
  - Implied CAGR: (1,026 / 385.68)^(1/3) − 1 = (2.660)^0.333 − 1 = **38.5%**
- **5yr: +2.0pp** (cumulative, still within the "normal" band extended to a
  longer window, not invoking the aggressive bucket) → SAM share 0.60% +
  2.0% = 2.60% of SAM
  - SOM_5yr = 64,000 × 0.0260 = **₹1,666 Cr**
  - Implied CAGR: (1,666 / 385.68)^(1/5) − 1 = (4.320)^0.20 − 1 = **34.0%**

Both figures **exceed the 25% CAGR house strategy target** and are a
mechanical artifact of the tiny starting base, not a forecast to be taken at
face value — **this is the FORMAL handoff to stage 11**: use these as an
upper-bound sanity check on revenue-growth assumptions, not as the base case,
and weigh against B06's decelerating-demand finding and the absence of
mining-segment peer coverage.

### 3C — Capacity cross-check

B07's capex-embedded-growth figure is **0 / NOT FOUND**: the one large
quantifiable capex programme (Chennai MMLP CWIP ~₹52 Cr) is already
substantially commissioned into the FY26 base, and Bengaluru MMLP carries no
disclosed capex figure. A precise ₹ Cr capacity-gap calculation is therefore
**not possible** with the data available. Qualitative cross-check by segment:

- **Mining**: the disclosed order book (₹4,600 Cr, Mining-specific, as of
  1-Apr-2026) already implies multi-year revenue visibility. SECL
  Porda-Chimtapani alone (₹3,422 Cr / 140 months) implies an average run-rate
  of ~₹293 Cr/year once ramped — on its own, roughly comparable to the entire
  segment's FY26 revenue (₹164.8 Cr) and consistent with, or ahead of, this
  stage's conservative SOM trajectory. Physical capacity (fleet of excavators/
  dumpers/dozers) is described only qualitatively as "capital-efficient" /
  "limited incremental capex" (Investor Presentation p.10) — no fleet-capacity
  ceiling is disclosed to test against.
- **Terminals**: Chennai MMLP (82 acres) commissioned Dec-2025, so its
  physical capacity is now in the base; Bengaluru MMLP (~65+ acres) is
  "upcoming" with no disclosed capex figure or commissioning date.
- **Warehousing**: explicitly an "asset-light" leased model (Investor
  Presentation p.21) — scalable without large disclosed capex, though B04
  flagged an unexplained +779% jump in capital advances with no stated
  capacity/date attached.

**capacity_check = "sufficient for Mining (order book exceeds implied SOM
revenue need); Terminals/Warehousing capacity adequacy not independently
quantifiable — capex figure NOT FOUND per B07."** No side is clearly the
"optimistic one" on hard numbers; the qualitative evidence leans toward
capacity not being the binding constraint on the SOM figures above, but this
cannot be confirmed to a ₹ Cr precision.

### 4C — Market structure

- **Mining/OB removal**: concentrated at the top (Adani largest MDO
  nationally); Thriveni-Sainik a large established OB contractor (~₹9,250 Cr
  turnover, stale FY23 estimate) dwarfing Sical (₹164.8 Cr); the market is

  services-only" player like Sical if the mix shifts further toward

- **Terminals/CFS**: ~250 operational ICD/CFS facilities nationally

  Distriparks (~₹1,860 Cr FY25, multi-port, CFS revenue itself declined 5.9%
  YoY) are both larger and structurally different (CONCOR govt-linked/rail
  focus; Gateway multi-port diversified).
- **Warehousing/3PL**: highly fragmented from small unorganized operators to
  large institutional developers; organised/unorganised split for this
  specific 3PL sub-vertical NOT FOUND (broad stat only: 450-500 mn sq ft
  organized nationally). Sical/PVLPL is a small niche player (19-20
  warehouses, 1.15-1.2 mn sq ft).
