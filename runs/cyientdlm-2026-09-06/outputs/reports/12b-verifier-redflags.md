## PART 2: PEER TEST OF MANAGEMENT'S EXPLANATIONS

I used the twelve peer transcripts to separate industry conditions from
company-specific ones. Three results change how the company record reads.

**P1. The tariff position was settled two and a half months before management
said there was "no clarity".** Avalon, Feb-2026 call p.2: "U.S. tariffs have
reduced from 50% to 18%, benefiting our U.S. export business", repeated at
p.3 ("the long-awaited trade deal has been announced with U.S. tariffs on
imports from India reduced from 50% to 18%") and treated as settled in
May-2026 call p.3. Cyient DLM, Apr-2026 call p.13 (RMS): "the tariff overhang
still continues to be there. There's no clarity in terms of how -- which
direction is going, which I think we are all aware of"; Apr-2026 call p.17
(Rajendra): "there was some mis-clarity, but still the cloud has not gone
away... we still don't know... probably we can just clear that cloud probably
the next one quarter or so, once we get full clarity from the U.S. tariffs."
The rate had been public since early February. Syrma's Jan-2026 call p.3 is
consistent with the pre-deal uncertainty ("We have been hoping for the last
quarter on 4, 5 months that it would settle"), which dates the change
precisely to the window between 30-Jan-2026 and 5-Feb-2026.

**P2. The component and PCB cost shock was live, industry-wide and openly
disclosed by both other peers in the same window — and is entirely absent
from Cyient DLM's Q1 FY27 call.** Kaynes, Aug-2026 call p.15 (Ramesh
Kunhikannan): "this component industry is gone back to COVID times... the PCB
prices have gone up 3 times. When it comes to components, availability has
become a big problem. Prices are also going up... in the range of around
10%-12% only"; earlier on the same page N. Muthukumar puts other component
prices up "30%-35%" with "6 to 8 months even for order booking". Kaynes,
Aug-2026 call p.19: "It is a global problem, the entire industry is aware of
it. This has been going on for last 3-4 months. So I'm also surprised if
nobody have addressed this." Syrma, Aug-2026 call p.4 (Gujral): "supply chain
is a cause of concern... supply of some specialty chemicals which go into the
PCB making, lead time of components going up... We have formed a war room in
the company." Syrma, Aug-2026 call p.9, walks an analyst through exactly the
mechanic Cyient DLM was asked about and did not give: "if my chip is Rs. 60
and I am selling it at Rs. 100... The chip goes from 60 to 100. I sell it at
140 to the customer. 140 is not 40%. So... the percentage margin would undergo
a change." Cyient DLM's call is dated 21-Jul-2026, inside the same window
Kaynes dates from roughly April 2026. Its answer to the identical
gross-margin question was "investments what we have made into our
organization" (Jul-2026 call p.14).

**P3. The West Asia material-supply excuse is corroborated in kind but not in
magnitude.** Kaynes, May-2026 call p.3, names the same cause: "geopolitical
disruption, especially the West Asia conflict, which led to last-minute
customer deferment, supply chain delays and product timing shift" — and still
grew Q4 FY26 revenue 26% YoY (Kaynes May-2026 call p.5). Avalon, asked
directly on 7-May-2026 about "the conflict in the Middle East has disrupted
the supplies of raw materials at PCBs", answered that it is a pass-through
and "at the moment, nothing that we see is critical" (Avalon May-2026 call
p.14). So the disruption was real, and the -13.8% YoY outcome was not.

## PART 6: CREDIBILITY GRADE

B05 grades management **C — Mixed**. I would grade **lower**.

B05's grade is built on two axes: promise-versus-delivery on guidance, and
working-capital discipline. On both it is right and well anchored. But three
further axes it does not weigh all cut the same way:

1. **Internal consistency of disclosed figures.** Exports stated as "60-plus
   percent" and "over 90%" in one call (F3). One order-book number described
   as "highest ever", "highest in 10 quarters" and "highest since 2.5 years"
   in one call, with the base year flipped between calls (F4). A product mix
   that sums to 80% (F10). An order-book bridge that does not tie in any FY26
   quarter (F2). A one-off expense stated in two currencies (A13).

   EBITDA for the full year, for the first time" and "double-digit EBITDA for
   four consecutive quarters" — both depend on normalization that is never
   named when the claim is made (F6), and the FY26 profit-growth headline is
   produced by an acquisition failure (F16).
3. **Governance.** An unexplained CFO change inside a two-quarter window that
   also contains an earn-out reversal, a written-off acquisition and the
   introduction of normalized reporting (F5).

Add F1: on the one input-cost question that both peers answered in detail in
the same window, this management deflected once and then gave an answer that

disclosure choice or a command-of-the-numbers problem, and either reading is
worse than "C — Mixed".

in Q1 FY27 is real on a YoY basis and argues against a clean D, but F7 shows

carries less weight than B05 assigns it.
