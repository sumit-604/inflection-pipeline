## SECTION G — DRAFT MENTAL MODEL DECLARATION (unsigned)

Per-line archetype, drawn only from what the documents support.

**1. Archetype per business line.**
- Digital Service (JK Technosoft, ~78% of revenue): Outsourcing partner /
  IT services. Watch client concentration, wallet share, capacity fill,
  contract stickiness, price per unit.
- Defence & Aerospace (Allen composites, JK Defence, Devanahalli):
  Build-to-spec component maker plus Order-book business. Watch design-win
  pipeline, content per unit, order inflow, book-to-bill, execution pace.
- Digital Manufacturing & Advanced Systems (3D printing, medical implants):
  Build-to-spec component maker with a Licence/scarcity option on the
  medical side (CDSCO gating). Early stage; treat as optionality.
- Parent (Jaykay Enterprises): Holding company / capital allocator. Reported
  profit is dominated by investment gains; the parent functions
  substantially as an investment-holding vehicle that funds its subsidiaries
  through rights issues.

**2. The 3-4 dominant variables the archetypes imply.**
- Organic growth and margin of JK Technosoft IT services (the cash engine).
- Defence order inflow and execution: BrahMos conversion and Devanahalli
  commissioning turning into segment revenue.
- Earnings mix: recurring operating PBT versus exceptional/investment gains.
- Capital cadence: fresh rights raised versus cash consumed by loss-making
  subsidiaries (Neumesh) and defence capex.

**3. One-paragraph analogy.**
A family holding company owns three things: a steady IT-services business
that pays the bills, a young defence-parts workshop that just won its first
big missile-parts order, and a science-project unit doing 3D printing and
medical implants. Most of last year's headline profit came from booking a
gain on an investment, not from the workshops. The holding company keeps
returning to shareholders for fresh money through rights issues to fund the
young units, one of which has already burned through its net worth.

**4. What the model rejects as noise.**
- Headline PAT growth that is really exceptional or investment gains.
- Segment revenue multiples off tiny bases (Digital Manufacturing 19x).
- Press-release optimism ("robust," "exciting") without a matching filing.
- QoQ EBITDA "turnarounds" driven by a single lumpy quarter.

**5. The model's own falsifier.**
The AVOID read weakens if, across two clean quarters, consolidated
operating PBT (before exceptional and share of associates) both funds the
group's cash needs without a fresh raise AND the Neumesh / balance-
reconciliation qualifications resolve without a write-off. It holds if the
next raise again funds subsidiary losses and operating PBT stays a fraction
of headline PAT.

## SECTION I — PLAIN-LANGUAGE SUMMARY

**Reported profit is mostly investment gains.**

Jaykay Enterprises is a Kanpur holding company with three parts. The big
part is JK Technosoft, an IT-services business. It made about 78% of Q1
FY27 revenue and most of the profit. The second part is Defence and
Aerospace, built around Allen Reinforced Plastics, which makes composite
parts and just won a Rs 60 Cr BrahMos order. The third part is Digital
Manufacturing, a young 3D-printing and medical-implant unit. Last year's
headline profit of Rs 215 Cr was 82% an unexplained exceptional item. The
operating businesses earned only about Rs 44 Cr before that item. The
corpus does not say what the exceptional gain was. The auditor flags a
subsidiary, Neumesh Labs, whose net worth is gone (negative Rs 44 Cr) yet
is carried at Rs 20 Cr with no write-down. The auditor also disputes a Rs 4
Cr deferred-tax asset. The company funds itself by asking shareholders for
money: a new rights issue of up to Rs 154 Cr opens on 7 Sep 2026, after a
Rs 146 Cr rights issue in 2024. Both top executives work without pay. There
is no cash-flow statement in the corpus, so cash conversion cannot be
checked. The one thing that decides the thesis is whether the operating
businesses can fund the group on their own, or whether the pattern of
investment-gain profit plus repeated raises to cover subsidiary losses
continues.
