## SECTION 1: THE BUSINESS MODEL IN PLAIN ENGLISH

### 1A. One-line description
VOEPL is a contract manufacturer (OEM, moving to ODM) of room air conditioners, commercial
refrigeration, EMS electronics (LED/PCBA/controllers/remotes), washing machines and — as of
FY26 — its own compressors, building the products that sit inside other companies' brand
boxes, and getting paid per unit shipped, not per brand sold (AR MD&A p.64-65; Inv. Pres.
slide 14).

### 1E. The chai-stall-uncle version

Think of VOEPL as the guy who builds the actual samosas that a big snack brand sells under
its own name — the brand puts its logo on the box, but VOEPL is the one buying the flour and
oil, running the kitchen, and shipping the trays out on time. Right now that "kitchen" makes
mostly one dish — air conditioners — for a small number of big buyers, which is risky if any
one buyer walks away. So VOEPL is doing two things at once: making more dishes (fridges,
washing machines, its own compressors) so no single buyer or product controls its fate, and
making more of its own ingredients in-house (plastics, copper tubing, and now compressors)
so it keeps a bigger slice of every rupee it earns instead of buying that slice from someone
else. It just spent a lot of money building new kitchens (48.6% growth in gross fixed assets,
a near-7x jump in construction-in-progress), so the near-term question is whether those new
kitchens get filled with enough orders to pay for themselves.

### 2B. Competitive positioning map

sustained 35-40% CAGR (Inv. Pres. slide 30). This is self-selected aspirational benchmarking,
not a named-competitor comparison, and should be read as management's framing rather than
an independent assessment.

Sector peers referenced in the orchestrator's operational digest (AMBER, ELIN, EPACK, PGEL)
are NOT named in the AR or the Investor Presentation and are not anchored here; they are

### 2D. Industry lifecycle stage

Room ACs (VOEPL's core, still ~60% of revenue) sit in a growth phase in India — AC
penetration is cited at just 8% in India versus 30% globally, described as signaling "huge
potential for growth" (Inv. Pres. slide 33), and the AR cites an ~16-17% CAGR market growth
estimate to 2029 (AR MD&A, Industry Outlook, p.65). VOEPL itself, however, is in an early/
scaling stage of its OWN lifecycle within that market: it is mid-capex, mid-diversification,
and mid-ODM-transition, i.e., a company trying to move from single-product vendor to
multi-vertical platform status while the underlying end-market is still growing — a
double-layered growth story where company-specific execution risk (capacity utilization,
ODM traction, compressor ramp) is at least as important as end-market growth.
