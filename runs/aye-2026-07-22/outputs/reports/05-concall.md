### 2B. Excuse pattern analysis

The call had almost no "misses" to explain in real time — it is a maiden, largely
forward-looking presentation — so the excuse-pattern read comes from how management framed the
one area they explicitly conceded was below plan: **credit cost above their own preferred
range**. Two causes were given: (1) the mortgage-team build-out cost (1,300-1,400 people
added over ~18 months) — framed candidly as **management's own strategic choice**, not blamed
on anyone; (2) elevated credit cost itself, framed partly against the sector-wide
"demonetization, COVID pandemic, over-lending" cycle (external/environmental framing) while
also disclosing that **AYE itself had tightened credit policies**, cutting hypothecation
approval rates by 10-15pp over 18 months — an admission of a self-imposed, not externally
forced, growth trade-off. On Bihar, management attributed collection risk to the **MFI**
segment specifically while positioning AYE's business-loan customer as insulated — a
differentiation claim, not blame-shifting, though it is asserted rather than evidenced with
Bihar-specific data beyond collection-efficiency percentages.

Pattern check: management did not use "we made a mistake" language anywhere in the transcript.
Hard topics (elevated credit cost, Bihar concentration, mortgage competition) were raised both
proactively (in the prepared remarks) and under analyst pressure (Q&A) — a reasonably balanced
mix, not purely reactive.

`excuse_pattern: "balanced"` — genuine self-attribution on the credit-policy tightening and the
mortgage-team cost sits alongside some environmental/sector framing on the credit-cost cycle
and Bihar/MFI narrative.

## SECTION 3: COMPETITIVE INTELLIGENCE FROM CONCALLS

### 3A. What management says about competitors

Management asserted category leadership without naming specific competitors: "in our target
segment of unorganized micro-scale businesses, we are the leading pan-India provider"
(Sanjay Sharma, p.5) and described hypothecation lending as having "almost no alternate
supplier of this product." In mortgage/LAP, management was more candid about competitive
intensity: "there are many providers in the market and honestly speaking there's a fair
amount of supply in the market for micro-lap product... sometimes we do also come across a
competitor trying to also make an offer" (Sanjay Sharma responding to Sameer Bhise, p.12).
**Credibility check:** the hypothecation "no competition" claim is asserted with no supporting
market data; the mortgage competitive-intensity admission is more credible because it is
volunteered candidly and is directionally consistent with a segment (micro-LAP) known to have
multiple NBFC entrants.

### 3B. Industry and market intelligence

- Addressable market claim: "over 67 million unorganized micro-scale enterprises across India"
  underserved by banks/NBFCs, contributing "as much as 90% of non-agri employment" (Sanjay
  Sharma, p.3) — company-sourced estimate, not independently cited.
- Sector-wide credit tightening: hypothecation approval rates fell from ~55% historically to
  40-43% over the last 18 months as AYE (and, implicitly, the sector) tightened underwriting
  post over-lending (Sovan Satyaprakash, p.11; Sanjay Sharma, p.14).
- Regulatory pipeline: draft MFI bill and Bihar-specific ordinance raised by two analysts
  (Viral Shah, Rohitash Arora); management drew a direct parallel to how Karnataka and Tamil
  Nadu regulatory action played out for non-MFI NBFCs (minimal effect) (Sanjay Sharma, p.9-11).
- Funding environment: incremental cost of borrowing falling (10.31% in Q3 FY26 vs 10.96%
  blended), attributed to improved credit profile and post-IPO capitalisation (Sanjay Sharma,
  p.6).

### 3D. Customer and order-book signals

41,015 new borrowers added in Q3 FY26; granular book of 5.23 lakh loans as of Dec-2025 across
18 states and 3 union territories; low foreclosure rate of 3.33% over 9M FY26; growth
composition skewed to existing branches (60% from AUM-per-branch deepening, 39% from repeat
loans, only 1% from new-branch additions) — a genuine, specific operating signal rather than a
generic growth claim (Sanjay Sharma, p.6-7). Geographic concentration: Bihar at 15.5% of AUM
against 8.5% of branches (Sovan Satyaprakash confirming Viral Shah's observation, p.9) — a
concentration point worth tracking given the regulatory overhang in that state.
