## PASS 3: PATTERN RE-READ

Re-read the notes a third time hunting specifically for contradictions,
mismatches against the main statements, deliberately thin disclosure next
to detailed disclosure elsewhere, restatements, post-balance-sheet events,
and going-concern language. Cross-checked Pass 1 and Pass 2's four flagged
contradictions directly against source text and confirmed all four:

1. **CARO Annexure A (vii)(b) vs Note 36.** CARO discloses a disputed
   ₹266.85 lakh Madhya Pradesh Municipal Corporation Act property tax claim
   (MP High Court, Jabalpur, FY2003-04 to FY2025-26) plus a ₹0.61 lakh
   TDS dispute (CIT(A), AY2016-17) — combined ₹267.46 lakh. Note 36
   "Contingent Liabilities" shows only ₹20.85 lakh, with no cross-reference
   to CARO or the municipal dispute anywhere in its text (Note 36, p.101,
   vs CARO Annexure A (vii)(b), p.69-70). 12.8x understatement in the notes
   relative to the audit-report annexure disclosing the same company's
   litigation. Confirmed, unresolved.
2. **Sub-1.0x DSCR both years, verified against the source ratio table.**
   Note 49(f) standalone, p.110-111 (extracted-text line ~5638): "Debt
   Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR) ... 0.39 [FY26] 0.40 [FY25] -2.65%."
   Verbatim confirmation, no transcription error. This sits alongside the
   FY26 operating cash flow swing from +₹113.81 Cr (FY25) to -₹20.91 Cr
   (FY26) despite PAT more than tripling (Cash Flow Statement, p.76) — two
   independent disclosures (a ratio-note metric and the primary cash flow
   statement) both confirming the same structural cash-conversion failure.
   Confirmed, and the two data points reinforce rather than merely repeat
   each other: DSCR is a full-year average metric while the cash flow
   statement shows the FY26 point-in-time deterioration — consistent
   direction, different lenses.
3. **Gratuity past-service-cost table (₹191.31 lakh) vs narrative
   (₹223.54 lakh), same Labour Codes event, same note, four pages apart**
   (Note 38(i)/(iv) vs Note 38(xi) standalone, p.99-101; Note 37 equivalent
   consolidated, p.137-140). ₹32.23 lakh gap, no reconciliation offered in
   either standalone or consolidated notes. Confirmed.
4. **Standalone vs consolidated FY25 gratuity DBO mismatch** (₹422.74 lakh
   standalone vs ₹427.71 lakh consolidated, Note 38 vs Note 37, p.99-101 /
   p.137-139) — a ₹4.97 lakh gap in a number that consolidation should not
   change at all, since the sole subsidiary (DMCC, UAE) carries no Indian
   gratuity scheme. The gap is exactly offset in the FY26 "Benefits Paid"
   line, so the FY26 closing DBO reconciles to the same ₹473.20 lakh figure
   in both note sets — meaning the comparative-year base was silently
   adjusted between standalone and consolidated presentations with no
   disclosed reason. Confirmed.

No fifth contradiction of comparable weight surfaced on this pass. Two
additional pattern observations, both sub-material, noted for completeness:

5. **Deliberately thin disclosure next to detailed disclosure elsewhere.**
   Note 17's ₹1,500 lakh (15.00 Cr) "Unsecured Loans from Bodies Corporate"
   carries a rate (8.85%-9.00%) and maturity (Nov-2028 to Mar-2029) but no
   lender name, while every other borrowing line in the same note
   (Note 17/21, p.92-94) is tied to a named bank or NBFC facility type. The
   asymmetry in disclosure granularity within the same note, for the one
   line item that is both unsecured and below prevailing secured bank
   rates, is the kind of selective thinness this pass is designed to catch.
   Already flagged 🟡 in Pass 1; re-confirmed as a genuine pattern (not an
   isolated omission) on this read, since every other funding line in the
   same table names its counterparty type.
6. **Note 51 standalone / Note 48 consolidated "regrouped/rearranged"

   Already flagged in Pass 2; on this read, confirmed there is no

### D. FIVE QUESTIONS FOR MANAGEMENT

1. The CARO Annexure A discloses a ₹2.67 Cr disputed municipal tax matter (MP High Court, since 2003) that does not appear in Note 36's contingent liability table. Why is this item excluded from Note 36, and what is the Company's current assessment of the probability and quantum of loss?
2. What is the identity of the overseas customer behind the ₹24.79 Cr stale export receivable that has now rolled forward unresolved for 2-3+ years, and what specific steps, beyond "confirmation/reconciliation," support continuing to carry it at full value with no provision?
3. DSCR was below 1.0x in both FY25 and FY26, and 62.3% of borrowings are now payable on demand. Given the pending merger into VTL, what near-term steps, if any, ensure continuity of these facilities through and after the scheme's effectiveness?
4. The Labour Codes past-service-cost impact is disclosed as ₹191.31 lakh in the gratuity movement table but as ₹223.54 lakh in the accompanying narrative text (Note 38(xi)). Which figure is correct, and can the two be reconciled?
5. What is the corporate guarantee fee, if any, paid or payable to Vindhya Telelinks Limited for the credit support VTL provides on the Company's secured borrowings, and how will this arrangement be treated once VTL becomes the surviving entity post-merger?

### E. NOTES-BASED RED FLAGS

- **Contingent liability disclosure gap (earnings management adjacent, understatement risk)**: Note 36 omits a CARO-disclosed ₹2.67 Cr municipal tax dispute, a 12.8x understatement of what the notes convey versus what the audit report annexure discloses about the same company.
- **Cash-conversion divergence from reported profit**: PAT more than tripled while operating cash flow turned negative (-₹20.91 Cr) and DSCR sits below 1.0x in both years — a classic earnings-quality warning sign where reported income is not translating into cash, driven by working-capital absorption (receivables + inventory) rather than one-off items.
- **Unresolved, unnamed, aged receivable carried at full value**: ₹24.79 Cr overseas receivable, 2-3+ years outstanding, no name, no independent evidence beyond management representation — a classic "aged receivable held on faith" pattern the sole Key Audit Matter also targets (via the broader ECL trend).
- **Undisclosed-value related-party credit support**: VTL corporate guarantee on Company borrowings with no fee disclosed, alongside an unnamed related-sounding unsecured lender ("Bodies Corporate") at a below-market rate — not proven non-arm's-length, but the opacity itself is a governance disclosure gap, more consequential given VTL absorbs BCL under the pending merger.
- **Internal note contradictions left unreconciled**: two separate ₹-value mismatches within the gratuity disclosures of the same annual report (₹32.23 lakh table-vs-narrative gap; ₹4.97 lakh standalone-vs-consolidated FY25 comparative gap), neither explained.

### F. ONE-LINE NOTES VERDICT

The notes reveal moderate accounting practices with concentrated
concerns in specific areas rather than pervasive weakness. Key concern:
reported profit is not converting to cash, evidenced independently by a
negative FY26 operating cash flow, a sub-1.0x DSCR in both years, and a
contingent liability table that understates true tax exposure by 12.8x
against the company's own audit report annexure. Key strength: revenue
recognition, tax accounting, and dilution disclosures are clean and
conventional, with an unqualified audit opinion and no going concern
qualification. Overall accounting quality: 6/10.
