NEW FINDING 2 — the inventory build is not just a balance-sheet flag, it
is a large, quantified P&L credit this year (Note 23, "Changes in
Inventories", CONSOLIDATED p.163-164, STANDALONE p.128-129)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

🔴 RED FLAG. Pass 1 flagged the inventory build (Section 5) as a
balance-sheet red flag and, separately, the Altek fair-value gain and the
ESOP-forfeiture reversal (Section 12) as items flattering reported PBT.
Pass 1 did not connect a third, and in fact the LARGEST, such item: the
"changes in inventories of finished goods, stock-in-trade and work in
progress" line in the P&L is itself a direct function of the same
inventory build.

CONSOLIDATED: net (increase)/decrease in FG+WIP = (325.79) FY26 vs
212.54 FY25 — i.e. FY26 shows a Rs325.79mn INVENTORY BUILD, which is
booked as a Rs325.79mn REDUCTION to cost of goods sold (a P&L credit);
FY25 showed a Rs212.54mn inventory DRAW-DOWN, which added Rs212.54mn to
cost of goods sold (a P&L debit). The YoY swing in this single line is
Rs325.79 - (-212.54) = Rs538.33mn of favourable P&L movement, purely from
building unsold stock instead of running it down.

STANDALONE: net (increase)/decrease = (299.92) FY26 vs 122.45 FY25 — a
YoY swing of Rs422.37mn.

This is not an accounting violation; it is the correct mechanical
consequence of production continuing to run ahead of falling shipments
(matching principle). But from a quality-of-earnings standpoint it means:
the reported PBT number is flattered, this year, by MORE from unsold
production sitting in the warehouse (Rs538.33mn consolidated) than from
the Altek earn-out fair-value gain Pass 1 already flagged (Rs195.75mn)
and the ESOP-forfeiture reversal swing (roughly Rs91mn) combined. If this
inventory does not sell through, it converts to future write-downs or
cash-tied-up losses rather than profit already booked. This is the
single most useful NEW number in Pass 2: it turns Pass 1's separate
inventory-build flag and profit-quality flag into one connected,
quantified story.

NEW FINDING 4 — standalone employee costs fell materially, a genuine
quantified real-cost-reduction Pass 1 flagged only qualitatively via
gratuity/leave provisions (Note 24, EMPLOYEE BENEFITS EXPENSE,
STANDALONE p.129, CONSOLIDATED p.164)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

🟡 Watch — corroborates the masking pattern with a new metric.
STANDALONE: total employee benefits expense fell from Rs1,294.20mn (FY25)
to Rs1,051.60mn (FY26), -18.8%; salaries and wages alone fell from
Rs1,120.09mn to Rs944.89mn, -15.6%. This decline happened DESPITE
absorbing the one-time Rs17.27mn Labour Code catch-up charge (Rs12.82mn
gratuity + Rs4.45mn leave, Pass 1 Section 1/9) that pushed costs UP —
meaning the underlying, like-for-like reduction in the India cost base is
larger than the headline -18.8% suggests. Headcount itself is still not
disclosed anywhere in the notes (Pass 1 already flagged this gap), so the
reduction cannot be split between headcount cuts, pay cuts, or mix
change, but the magnitude (a swing of over Rs240mn) is a real cash-cost
reduction consistent with "reduction in operations."

CONSOLIDATED: total employee benefits expense ROSE from Rs1,862.18mn
(FY25) to Rs2,267.78mn (FY26), +21.8%; salaries and wages rose from
Rs1,580.40mn to Rs1,939.33mn, +22.7% — driven by a full year of Altek
headcount (Altek was only in scope from 04-Oct-2024) rather than any
India-side growth. Staff welfare expenses alone jumped from Rs147.09mn
to Rs284.02mn, +93.1%.

This divergence (standalone employee cost down ~19%, consolidated
employee cost up ~22%) is a second, independent metric — beyond revenue,
which Pass 1 already used — showing the consolidated income statement
structurally masks a shrinking India operation behind a newly
consolidated US subsidiary.

NEW FINDING 5 — two of the three Key Audit Matters are UNCHANGED for a

🟡 Watch (persistence, upgrades Pass 1's two "thin disclosure" flags).
The Independent Auditor's Report to the standalone financial statements
(p.104-106) names three Key Audit Matters: revenue recognition
(percentage-of-completion judgement), inventory obsolescence (Note 9),
and valuation of investments (Note 6A — the Stuam Technologies FVTOCI

disclosure" (Pass 1's framing) to "structural, recurring judgement risk
area the company itself has not been able to resolve or shrink over two
annual cycles" — worth naming explicitly for anyone modelling forward
provisioning risk.

NEW FINDING 6 — liquidity note gives two facts Pass 1 did not extract:
a large cash drawdown AND a stable credit-line cushion (CONSOLIDATED
Note 33.2, Liquidity risk, p.169)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

🟡 Watch, mixed direction — one reinforcing, one mitigating.
- Total cash and bank balances (full basis, including then-unutilised
  IPO proceeds) fell from Rs2,877.82mn (FY25) to Rs1,258.00mn (FY26), a
  56.3% decline. This reconciles with Pass 1's Section 12 finding that
  the Rs6,631.54mn IPO proceeds, partly parked in FDs (Rs1,559.81mn
  unutilised at FY25-end), were fully utilised by FY26-end — this note
  supplies the actual cash-balance mechanics behind that utilisation and
  shows the cash cushion the company carried into FY26 has now largely
  been spent (on debt repayment and, per the 08-Mar-2026 reallocation
  Pass 1 flagged, on working capital).
- Against this, unutilised bank credit limits stood at Rs4,172.60mn
  (FY26) vs Rs4,412.80mn (FY25) — essentially stable and substantial.
  This is a genuine liquidity mitigant not mentioned anywhere in Pass 1,
  and relevant directly to the Gate 0 cash-conversion deal-breaker
  (cumulative FY2023-26 CFO of -Rs25.07cr): even with weak operating cash
  conversion, the group retains a large unused credit cushion as at
  FY26-end. This does not resolve the cash-conversion flag but it is a
  material, quantified mitigant that should sit alongside it.
Standalone corroboration (Note 21, p.128): interest income on bank
deposits fell from Rs247.72mn to Rs168.82mn, -31.8%, consistent with a
shrinking average FD balance as IPO cash was drawn down through the
year, not just at a point-in-time balance-sheet date.

NEW FINDING 7 — capex pulled back harder in cash terms than the capital-
commitments figure alone showed (Note 3A/3C, PPE and CWIP, STANDALONE
pp.119-120)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

🟡 Watch (reinforcement with harder numbers). Pass 1 flagged (Section 3)
that capital commitments fell from Rs293.97mn to Rs78.25mn. The actual
cash capex movement is a sharper story:
- Gross PPE additions: Rs385.33mn (FY25) to Rs174.82mn (FY26), -54.6%.
- CWIP additions: Rs431.42mn (FY25) to Rs164.30mn (FY26), -61.9%; CWIP
  capitalised into PPE also fell (Rs385.33mn to Rs198.74mn).
- Standalone Right-of-use assets (leasehold land + buildings, Note 3B,
  p.119) show ZERO new lease additions in BOTH FY25 and FY26 — the
  entire India lease footprint is running down through amortisation
  alone (Rs494.14mn opening FY25 to Rs365.59mn at FY26-end), with no
  reinvestment in new leased space in either year.
Together these confirm the capex slowdown is a two-year pattern, not a
one-year swing, and is broader than the commitments note alone signalled
(commitments only capture contractually locked, not-yet-executed spend;
actual cash additions fell by more in percentage terms).

NEW FINDING 8 — minor cross-document tension: a GST dispute exists in
the CARO annexure that Note 28's blanket "NIL contingent liabilities"
statement does not surface (Auditor's CARO Annexure 1, standalone, p.107)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

🟡 Watch (minor, immaterial in size, flagged for completeness/pattern
scan). Technically this sits in the statutory auditor's CARO annexure,
not in the Notes to Financial Statements proper, so it falls slightly
outside this stage's core scope — but Pass 2's instructions specifically
ask for cross-references and internal contradictions, and this is one.
CARO Annexure 1 (p.107) discloses: "Goods and Services Tax, 2017...
Amount involved Rs2.08... Amount paid under protest Rs0.12... Period FY
2021-22... Forum: Deputy Commissioner of commercial taxes." Note 28
(Contingent Liabilities, both standalone and consolidated, cited by Pass
1 as "NIL... no tax disputes... disclosed anywhere in the notes") does
not mention this GST dispute at all. The amount is immaterial (Rs2.08mn

not a materiality-threshold breach, but it is a genuine instance of a
disputed statutory due existing in one part of the filed document set
while the note that is supposed to be the complete register of disputes
states none exist. Worth naming as a disclosure-completeness point, not
a red flag on its own.

PASS 2 NEW FINDINGS SUMMARY
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

The single most important catch in this pass is Finding 1: CONSOLIDATED
Note 35 (Schedule III entity-wise net-assets/profit table, p.171), which
Pass 1 missed in full. It proves, with the company's own numbers, that
100% of consolidated PAT growth this year came from the US subsidiary
structure while the Indian parent's own PAT fell 26.6%, and it gives the
first direct, quantified look at Altek's actual full-year profit
(Rs31.32mn), which is LOWER than Altek's part-year profit the prior year
— hard evidence behind Pass 1's inference that Altek is missing its
earn-out targets. Finding 2 is a close second: it shows the inventory
build Pass 1 already flagged as a balance-sheet red flag is also the
single largest profit-flattening item in the P&L this year (Rs538.33mn
consolidated), larger than the Altek fair-value gain and the ESOP
reversal Pass 1 flagged, and not previously connected to them. Findings
3-4 add standalone-level (India-only) numbers on inventory and employee
cost that sharpen Pass 1's "consolidated masks India stress" thesis with
two new, independent metrics. Findings 5-10 are smaller confirmatory or
minor-watch items (KAM persistence, liquidity mechanics, capex cash
numbers, a minor CARO/Note-28 cross-reference gap, component-auditor
structure, one unexplained finance-cost sub-line).

No restatements of prior-year figures were found anywhere in the
financial notes (only unrelated BRSR/sustainability-data restatements
appear elsewhere in the AR, out of scope for this note). No going-concern
language, qualified/adverse/disclaimer audit opinion, or emphasis-of-
matter paragraph was found in either auditor's report, confirming Pass
1's "NONE" finding on going concern.
