# AVANA (Avana Electrosystems Ltd): business narrative

Run: avana-2026-10-05. Stage 13, PHASE 1 LITE. Valuation has not run, so this file carries no entry zone, no decision and no destination PE. Units: Rs Cr unless stated; the annual report, prospectus and results report in INR lakh (100 lakh = Rs 1 Cr). No earnings calls exist (NO-CONCALL MODE).

## BUSINESS UNDERSTANDING NARRATIVE

Avana makes two products for electricity substations. The first is the control and relay panel, a steel cabinet built to the customer's drawing, from which operators watch, switch and protect a substation at 11 kV to 220 kV, with SCADA and automation work that the annual report puts at up to 400 kV. Panels were 60% of sales in the first half of FY26, up from 49% in FY25. The second is the protection relay, an electronic unit that senses a fault and trips the breaker before the fault damages a transformer or a line, and Avana designs its hardware and firmware in house; relays were the other 40% of sales. Protection functions have no close substitute, and the move from electromechanical to numerical relays is an upgrade inside Avana's own range. The buyers are mostly EPC contractors and private panel builders, at 76% to 82% of revenue, with state utilities buying by tender for 16% to 23% and six dealers for about 1%. The utility approves the vendor and the contractor places the order, so each new state needs a new approval, such as the MPPTCL registration for 400 kV panels and the CSPTCL approval for SCADA up to 220 kV in February 2026. Repeat customers gave 71% of FY25 revenue, but no contract locks them in, and the top five customers rose from 22% of revenue in FY25 to 39% in the first half of FY26. Present demand comes from substation building for transmission and for renewable evacuation. The run follows it through POWERGRID capex and TBCB awards, CEA monthly transformation capacity, MPPTCL, CSPTCL and KPTCL tenders, renewable and BESS additions, and EPC contractor order wins. India added 113,013 MVA of transformation capacity in FY26, and the CEA target for FY27 is 158,339 MVA, 40% more. Solar added 44.6 GW and wind 6.1 GW in FY26, and each evacuation substation needs panels and relays. Forward demand also depends on CERC and CEA transmission planning orders and RDSS sanctions, all public, while vendor reports put this niche's market growth at only 6% to 8% a year, below the grid unit growth. The panel line has a moat of medium durability from utility approvals and type tests, but approvals are granted state by state and project orders are rebid. The relay line has no evidenced moat: design is in house, but the run found no patents and no listed relay pure play to test it against. The Emerging Moat scan found 2 of 22 categories at moderate strength, utility qualification lock in and the IPO cash pile, for a score of 6.7 and class NONE. Talent asymmetry and cannibalisation barrier, categories 21 and 22, scored zero, because nothing stops a large switchgear maker from adding a 400 kV panel line.

Growing substation panel maker, new factory unbuilt

Avana Electrosystems was set up in July 2010 by four engineers who still run it as executive directors and together own 73.64%. It listed on NSE Emerge on 20 January 2026. FY26 revenue was Rs 84 Cr and profit after tax Rs 12 Cr. Revenue grew from Rs 28 Cr in FY23, a compound rate of 43% a year, and rose 36% in FY26 alone. The first half of FY26 brought Rs 35.75 Cr of revenue. The company reports one segment, so the FY26 split between panels and relays is not disclosed.

The change underway is a move from two rented units to one owned plant on KIADB land in Karnataka. The prospectus plans capacity of 1,75,000 relays and 1,500 panels, 150% above the stated base of 70,000 and 600. The board first guided commercial production for mid May 2026 and now guides end of October 2026. The prospectus gives a KIADB deadline of 26 October 2026 with no further extension, while another page reads as three years from May 2025. That conflict is open, and the earlier date is 21 days away. By March 2026 the company had spent Rs 2.76 Cr of its Rs 11.55 Cr IPO capex budget, with Rs 4.67 Cr in work in progress and Rs 10.95 Cr more committed. The prospectus shows both rented units on leases ending in July and August 2026, and the run found no renewal. Panel output of 886 in FY26 already ran past the stated capacity of 600, so the stated capacity figures are not reliable.

Gate 0 rates the business GOOD+, at 100 of 160, with core 69 of 100 and 7 of 12 moat tests passed. Verifier C recomputed ROCE on the fixed formula and got core 76; the class does not change. Four of the seven moat scores are formula effects of the margin jump and a three company peer set. ROCE was 27% in FY26 on the exact formula, after 42% in FY24 and 46% in FY25. Gross margin fell from 47.8% in FY25 to 40.6% in FY26, and to 36.6% in the second half. EBITDA margin excluding other income was 19.5% for the year, but it fell from 21.3% in the first half to 18.1% in the second. Lower other expenses held it up. The largest swing is the warranty provision: FY26 additions were 0.50% of revenue against 3.72% in FY25, and the notes give no reason. At the FY25 rate the gap would be about 17% of profit before tax, as an illustration only.

Profit is not turning into cash at the same pace. Operating cash flow was Rs 7.95 Cr in FY26, 68% of profit and 49% of EBITDA, the same 68% of profit as in FY25. After Rs 7.50 Cr of capex, free cash flow was Rs 0.45 Cr. Inventory rose from Rs 14.71 Cr to Rs 22.43 Cr, and finished goods more than doubled to Rs 6.90 Cr. Receivable days improved from 126 to 97, but dues older than two years rose from Rs 0.46 Cr to Rs 0.86 Cr with no provision. Bank stock statements showed receivables above the books in every FY26 quarter. On paper the balance sheet holds Rs 27 Cr of net cash against Rs 0.76 Cr of debt, but most of that cash is IPO money and Rs 21 Cr of it is unspent. Only Rs 1.23 Cr of the Rs 8.60 Cr IPO working capital budget was used, so operations and supplier credit funded the stock build. No credit rating document exists in the record, so no lender view on working capital is available.

With no earnings calls, credibility rests on prospectus promises against filed results. Of seven scored promises, four missed, two were delivered and one was partial, and no miss came with a stated cause. The grade is C, the floor for this mode, and Verifier B argues for one notch lower. The annual report contradicts its own numbers in places. It calls the margin change not significant, prints interest cover of 0.05x against about 19x derived, and disagrees with the auditor's CARO report on cost records and CSR. NSE asked the company to refile its FY26 results over four defects, and the company secretary left 69 days after listing. The promoters show no pledge, no litigation and no regulatory action, and the promoter verdict is CAUTION. The four promoters drew Rs 2.89 Cr, 25% of FY26 profit, and five relatives received Rs 0.78 Cr in fees with no stated scope, together 31% of profit. Shareholders passed FY27 pay of Rs 3.42 Cr, above the Section 197 limits, with 97.6% of votes cast; the promoters hold 73.64%. Independent directors attended 9 of 19 board meetings.

Peer evidence is thin. Only Danish Power holds calls, and it sells transformers beside panels; Shivalic Power Control and Marine Electricals have one investor deck each. No listed relay pure play exists. Danish grew 22% in FY26 and gave up 1 to 2 points of margin. Marine grew 14% and Shivalic 17%, with FY26 cost ratios flat or better. No peer explains Avana's 7 point fall in gross margin. The peer stage placed Avana's working capital beside Shivalic's cash drain. Verifier B found that reading unsupported: on a sales basis Avana's receivable days of 97 sit nearer Danish's 78, and Avana cut its debt while Shivalic raised its own. No peer confirmed or contradicted a company claim outright.

The run sizes the addressable market at Rs 6,055 Cr to Rs 7,592 Cr and the serviceable part at Rs 2,422 Cr. The serviceable figure is a proxy that applies a 40% transmission and distribution share to vendor market sizes. Avana holds about 3.5% of it, with 29 times revenue headroom, and the runway class is STRONG. Management cites a narrower Rs 4,315 Cr control panel market, 0.71 times the run's lower figure, so its claim reads as conservative. The share gain path gives Rs 165 Cr of revenue in three years and Rs 227 Cr in five. The five year figure exceeds stated capacity after expansion, worth Rs 170 Cr to Rs 177 Cr at fixed unit prices, though the stated capacity itself is unreliable. Relay market estimates run from USD 166 million to USD 3.2 billion across vendors, so the run carries USD 196 million with low confidence.
