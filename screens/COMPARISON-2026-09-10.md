## What the operator's filter returns

The filter: cheap or overlooked, PLUS a strong transition, PLUS strong growth.

Six of eight fall out.

- **MRPL and GRAPHITE fail on transition.** Cheapest on growth, climbing nothing.
  Their P/E to growth ratios of 0.34 and 0.28 are the most misleading numbers
  here, because that growth is one year of cycle recovery and it reverses.
- **CORONA fails on cheap.** Best transition of the eight, delivered and priced.
- **MOLBIO and TITANBIO fail on knowability.** A transition cannot be scored when
  no document was readable.
- **INDNIPPON fails on the gate.** Real transition, decent growth, but no EFI
  revenue exists. Narrative until the gate fires.

**Two pass all three: TAALTECH and DELTON.** They are the same two the shallow
run gave PROCEED. The framework verdicts and the arithmetic agree independently.

### TAALTECH, the cleaner of the two
A holding company amalgamated its operating arm and renamed itself in November
2025. Completed and dated, not promised. It has never filed an investor
presentation or held an earnings call in any year Bull AI indexes, so no
mechanism exists by which the market could have repriced it. That is a genuine
recognition gap rather than merely a low multiple. Risks: the unverified 28% net
margin, and two CFO resignations inside eleven months (2022-07-01, 2023-05-30).

### DELTON, the higher variance one
Working capital fell from 218 days in FY22 to 74 in FY26, which is four years of
real operating work and hard to fake across audited balance sheets. Customers are
Siemens, Siemens Energy India, BHEL, Honeywell Automation and Adani Power. Trades
near 0.36 times sales. Risks: 1.7% net margin, profit growing at half the rate of
revenue, and book value per share jumping from Rs 105 to Rs 397 on a land
revaluation where nothing was earned.

**One caution on the whole exercise.** Cheap plus strong transition plus strong
growth is rare, and when it appears the usual explanation is that one of the
three is not what it looks like. Here the suspect leg is the same in both cases:
the margin.

## Priority order for the deep dig

**1. TAALTECH.** Highest value per hour of the eight.
One document settles the thesis: the FY2026 annual report under Reg. 34(1),
uploaded 2026-08-07. Questions, in order:
- Post amalgamation revenue and EBIT margin FY26, against the standalone
  engineering business FY25. Is the 27.9% net margin real or an accounting
  artefact of the first merged year?
- Client concentration. Revenue share of top one, three and five clients. In
  engineering services one lost client can remove a quarter of revenue.
- Amalgamation terms: swap ratio, shares issued, dilution, effective date.
- Why two CFOs resigned, 2022-07-01 and 2023-05-30.
- Promoter identity, current holding, pledge.
- **Market cap dispute to settle:** Bull AI reported Rs 1,410 cr, screener showed
  about Rs 901 cr on the same day. One is wrong. Establish the true figure and
  the share count before any per share work.

**2. DELTON.** Second priority, higher variance.
- The land revaluation. Quantum, valuer, location, route through reserves. Web
  search indicated roughly Rs 240 cr under the Ind AS 16 revaluation model with
  the surplus in OCI. **Unverified. Confirm from the annual report, not news.**
  Then ask whether the land is surplus and saleable or under the factory.
- The bridge from EBITDA to PAT for FY26. Interest, debt quantum, depreciation,
  tax rate, against FY25. Profit grew 21.49% while revenue grew 39.07%. Find
  where the gain went.
- Whether 74 working capital days holds at the half year, not only at 31 March.
- Q1FY27 result. Web indicated it carries an order book figure, which was NOT

  and Adani Power.
