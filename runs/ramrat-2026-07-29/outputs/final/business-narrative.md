Record profit, broken cash, deeply overvalued stock

Ram Ratna Wires converts copper into enamelled winding wires and strips, about 76% of sales, copper tubes and pipes, about 22%, and a small contract fabrication remainder. It sells roughly 70% to 75% of output to large OEMs in power, auto, consumer durables, transformers and construction, and the balance through dealers. The company sits inside the Mumbai based RR Global group, is run by the Kabra family, listed on the BSE in 1995, and runs three plants at Dadra Nagar Haveli plus the new copper tube plant at Bhiwadi. FY26 revenue was Rs 5,076 Cr standalone and Rs 5,177 Cr consolidated, PAT Rs 108 Cr standalone and Rs 107 Cr consolidated, against a market value of Rs 4,249 Cr at Rs 454 a share. Both bases are now anchored to the audited results, and the stock trades near 36 times forward earnings. Materials run about 93% of revenue, so this is a thin spread conversion business, not a branded one.

The change underway is a push into copper tubes. Bhiwadi added 24,000 MTPA of copper tube capacity and started production in June 2025, copper tubes now run about 22% of revenue and grew about 115% in the year, and management frames this as import substitution against a claimed 70% import dependence. A Rs 86 Cr Silvassa expansion was board approved in March 2026, CTC and HVDC entry is targeted for the second calendar quarter of 2026, the Global Copper merger completed under NCLT order in June 2025, and the Tefabo wind tower stake rose to 64%. Revenue compounded 30% and PAT 52% over five years.

Gate 0 screens AVERAGE, 40 of 80 on the core, and the emerging moat scan grades MODEST at 21, so the combined read is AVERAGE rather than a confirmed transition setup. Two things pull Gate 0 down, a real FY19 to FY21 return trough of 8% to 11% ROCE over the COVID window that drags the ten year medians, and blocks that scored zero for missing shareholding and capex disclosure. Cash is the break. FY26 operating cash flow swung to negative Rs 96 Cr standalone and negative Rs 93 Cr consolidated, from positive Rs 225 Cr and Rs 227 Cr the year before, in the same year PAT hit a record. Receivables rose 64% to Rs 640 Cr and inventory rose 108% to Rs 486 Cr against roughly 40% revenue growth, with zero inventory write downs, and cash was negative even before tax. Off balance sheet factoring more than doubled to Rs 187 Cr, so the reported debtor position understates the true trade credit. The cash determination is INDETERMINATE, and the CARE rating from September 2025 still describes a working capital cycle that shortened to 23 days on FY25 data, a picture the FY26 numbers contradict.

Credibility grades B, and governance grades a CONCERN. Three commitments were delivered as narrated, Bhiwadi, the Global Copper merger, and Tefabo to 64%, with no misses, but the MD&A never names the cash flow break and the dividend was held flat at Rs 2.50 despite PAT rising more than 50%. A Section 132 tax search from November 2023 plus a Section 148 reassessment on the merged Global Copper leave about Rs 104 Cr of contingent tax exposure, roughly a fifth of net worth, under an auditor emphasis of matter. Independent director Ankit Kedia attended 33% of board and 40% of audit committee meetings, the family holds 5 of 10 board seats, and sister listed R R Kabel owns 6.05% and shares an independent director. Against that, a professional CFO joined in April 2026, promoter personal guarantees were released in May 2026, and pledge is zero.

Two listed peers, Bhagyanagar and Vidya Wires, confirm the parts of the story that touch winding wire but not the parts that matter most here. Both run the same capex cycle, both confirm copper price pass through, and both confirm that rising copper prices inflate working capital even under full pass through, which supports the cash reading. Neither makes copper tubes, so the Bhiwadi import substitution claim and the copper tube demand claim cannot be checked against this set. Vidya Wires named Ram Ratna as a peer with 50% to 60% product overlap, and Bhagyanagar's own analyst flagged overcapacity risk from new entrants including Adani and Hindalco.

The runway is real but the sizing is stretched. The realistic domestic market is about Rs 37,862 Cr, the addressable slice about Rs 28,260 Cr, current share about 18%, revenue headroom about 5.5x, and the runway class is STRONG. Management's copper tube growth claim of 16% to 17% CAGR projects to about 2.9x an independent conservative estimate, which reads inflated, and the 14% capex embedded growth sits about 3 points above the 11% share implied rate.

The four pillar destination multiple lands at 15.4 times on the operator approved additive track and 11.7 times on the conservative return model track, built on a 23.55% ROCE, an INDETERMINATE cash multiplier of 0.80, no growth premium, and a 25 times sector cap. At Rs 454 the stock trades near 36 times forward, roughly three to four times that destination, and even bull case FY29 earnings valued at 15.4 times give Rs 389, a loss from here. The hurdle fails on both tracks and even on bull earnings, so the decision is AVOID. The entry zone is Rs 134 to Rs 153, the margin of safety price Rs 114, and the probability weighted three year return is negative 15%. That zone sits about 66% below the current price.

The adversary review left the AVOID standing and unassailable, since earnings would need to roughly triple to clear the hurdle, and it weakened the growth triggers while pressing one harder point. The cash may be structural rather than merely unresolved, because even the negative reported cash flow was flattered by factoring that doubled to Rs 187 Cr and supplier finance of Rs 647 Cr. If the drag is structural, the honest entry zone is nearer Rs 80 to Rs 116, below the zone the operator elected by taking the additive track over the conservative one.
