# Fee monopoly on power, regulator redesigning it

IEX | Indian Energy Exchange Ltd | Run date 2026-09-08 | Phase 1, evidence only

Phase 1 lite. Stages 10 and 11 have not run. This file carries no valuation,
no entry zone and no decision.

---

## BUSINESS UNDERSTANDING NARRATIVE

IEX runs India's power exchange and never owns an electron. The product is a
matched trade. A generator or a distribution utility puts a bid on the screen,
the matching engine clears one price for that time block, the load despatch
centre confirms the schedule, and IEX invoices a fee of about 4 paise per unit
traded. Electricity transaction fees are 91.7 percent of standalone revenue,
certificate fees on renewable energy certificates are 4.2 percent, and annual
membership and subscription fees are 3.9 percent, so the electricity line is
the only stream above 10 percent and is the business. Treasury income is a
further 20.4 percent of standalone total revenue, and the company labels it
treasury income in its own metrics table; it is a return on the investment
book, not a second product line. The customers are state and private
distribution utilities, generators, traders and commercial and industrial open
access buyers, about 9,000 registered participants in all. They stay because
settlement, margining and back office systems are wired into the venue, with 72
percent of cleared day ahead volume arriving through bidding APIs, though one
unnamed customer was 16.2 percent of FY26 revenue against 15.65 percent the
year before. Demand today comes from India's short term power market, the slice
of electricity bought outside long term power purchase agreements, and two
external series report it every month: the CERC Short Term Power Market report
and the CEA generation and demand met data. Exchange traded volume is about 8
percent of national consumption, and IEX cleared 141 BU of electricity in FY26,
up 16.9 percent. Demand should grow for two reasons an outsider can check
without the company. India added 55 GW of non fossil capacity in FY26, and
intermittent output needs a short term market to balance it, which is what the
real time segment does. Procurement is also shifting off long term power
purchase agreements toward exchange buying, and the same two monthly reports
track that shift. Three pools sit outside the core electricity fee pool: gas
through IGX, coal through the Indian Coal Exchange, and carbon under the Carbon
Credit Trading Scheme, whose external markers are Coal India's monthly
electronic auction allocation data and a Ministry of Coal licence award
notification that has not yet been issued to IEX. The competitive advantage is
not the same on every line. The real time line holds the strongest position,
with customer ecosystem, digital platform lock in through APIs, regulatory
first mover status and the war chest all scoring strong in the emerging moat
scan. The day ahead line is the one under attack, because coupling moves price
discovery to a shared operator and the liquidity network effect that built day
ahead is the exact thing coupling neutralises. The certificate line has no moat
worth naming: REC volume fell 81.4 percent year on year in Q1FY27 and 86.6
percent in August 2026. The execution category scored zero, not for want of
data but as a negative finding against management's own promise record, and
categories 21 and 22 were both addressed and both scored zero.

## THE BUSINESS

IEX clears roughly 80 to 85 percent of India's exchange traded electricity and
earns about 4 paise per kWh on it. FY26 consolidated revenue was Rs 616 crore
and profit Rs 493 crore, on an operating margin near 87 percent. The cost base
is almost entirely fixed: employee cost Rs 48 crore and technology cost Rs 14
crore against a standalone operating cost of Rs 85 crore. Net block is Rs 97
crore against total assets of Rs 2,436 crore. Net cash and investments are
about Rs 2,087 crore. Every extra unit traded drops through to profit at close
to the full fee rate, and any cut in the fee rate drops through the same way,
because there is no variable cost to trim in offset.

## WHAT IS CHANGING

CERC has ordered day ahead market coupling with Grid India as the coupling
operator. Coupling takes price discovery off the exchange for the day ahead
segment and leaves IEX collecting bids. APTEL declined to set the order aside
on 13 February 2026. The Supreme Court admitted IEX's appeal on 11 May 2026 and
refused an interim stay on 27 July 2026, so CERC may proceed. Day ahead is 39
percent of FY26 volume, down from 95 percent in FY16, and real time is 34
percent and grew 41 percent in FY26. That mix shift is the offset management
points to. Against it, day ahead volume fell 6.6 percent in June 2026 and 7.7
percent in July 2026, the exact segment coupling threatens. A second and
separate threat sits on the fee side: a CERC staff paper of December 2025
proposes a fixed 1.5 paise per unit transaction fee against the current 2 paise
ceiling. Stage 9 found that paper by web search. It is not in the corpus, and
nothing in the corpus mentions it.

## WHAT THE NUMBERS SAY

Gate 0 scores 115 of 160, EXCELLENT, with 7 of 12 moats confirmed, a FORTRESS
moat class and no deal breakers. The emerging moat scan adds 28 points and
STRENGTHENING, for a combined EXCELLENT+. Cash conversion is clean: standalone
cash from operations is 89.6 percent of profit, and the gap is treasury income
sitting under investing rather than operating, not accrual quality. Trade
receivables are Rs 1.22 crore and 100 percent under six months, with nothing
disputed or credit impaired. Working capital days are slightly negative.
Member settlement balances of Rs 951 crore swing reported operating cash flow
year to year and are members' money, not IEX capital. The one number that
moves against the quality read is the non operating share. Other income of Rs
131.30 crore plus the IGX equity pickup of Rs 19.80 crore is Rs 151.10 crore,
23.4 percent of FY26 pre tax profit of Rs 645.56 crore, rising to 29.8 percent
in Q1FY27. Fee realisation fell from 4.33 to 4.16 paise per unit between Q1FY26
and Q1FY27, and revenue growth trails volume growth in every period on record.

## WHAT THE ANNUAL REPORT SHOWS

Accounting quality scores 6 of 10. The audit opinion is unqualified on both
statements, the fraud focused key audit matter found nothing, and no
restatement appears. Three internal contradictions sit inside one board
approved annual report: the whistleblower investigation is concluded in the
Board's Report and ongoing in Notes 46 and 49 and in CARO, both signed the same
day; MSME dues rise 462 percent in one note and fall 42 percent in another for
the same balance sheet date; and the real time volume share is stated at 34
percent in three places and near 39 to 40 percent in two others. The materiality
call is the sharper item. Market coupling, the largest disclosed business risk,
carries no rupee quantification anywhere in the audited notes, while a Rs 5.04
crore GST dispute worth 0.37 percent of net worth gets a full management
assessment.

## WHAT MANAGEMENT SAYS AGAINST WHAT IT DOES

Management credibility grades D. The promise record is four delivered, two
partial and four missed. Delivered: 15 to 20 percent volume guidance, met at
15.9 percent in Q1FY27; the dividend policy, paid as stated; and the chief
financial officer's treasury income recovery, verified from the filed results
at Rs 22.11 crore in Q4FY26 to Rs 44.92 crore in Q1FY27. Missed: real time
growth guided at 25 to 30 percent and printed at 10.2 percent in July and 10.6
percent in August; the REC recovery, still down 86.6 percent in August; the
buyback, restated as future consideration across two quarters; and "things will
definitely go in our favour" on the APTEL case, falsified 14 days later when
APTEL dismissed the appeal for want of standing. Three patterns matter more
than the tally. The REC excuse names buyer confusion, while the company's own
power market update filed three weeks earlier shows sell bids down 86.1 percent
with clearing prices rising, which is a seller side failure. Market share
disclosure loses precision every quarter as the coupling threat sharpens, from
an 84 percent full product split to a bare 80 to 85 percent range with no
product detail. The fee competition question has been evaded twice across two
quarters, and the pricing half of it was never answered by anyone in the most
recent session.

## OWNERSHIP AND CONDUCT

There is no promoter. Public shareholders hold 99.73 percent and employee
trusts 0.27 percent. Stage 8 returns TRUSTWORTHY with no deal breakers, a
scorecard of six clean, four caution and zero red, zero pledge, and a clean
regulatory, criminal and litigation record for every current director and key
manager. A SEBI interim order of 15 October 2025 barred eight individuals and
impounded about Rs 173 crore for insider trading in IEX securities ahead of the
coupling order. The leak traces to a CERC Economics Division official. No IEX
director, key manager or promoter group entity is named. The verdict sits at
TRUSTWORTHY rather than higher for two reasons: the unresolved whistleblower
status conflict, and two SAST disclosures from April and June 2026 that are
scanned images with no readable text, leaving a 2 percent shareholding move
unattributed.

## WHAT THE PEERS SHOW

The peer set complicates the read rather than settling it. The customer loyalty
defence fails its own test at CDSL: the depository claimed loyalty and
commitment in May 2026, in the middle of an erosion already visible from
November 2025, and disclosed a further 420 basis points of incremental share
loss one quarter later. The one real precedent for a regulator forced market
redesign runs the other way. BSE's expiry day reallocation was expansionary,
not compressive, and BSE explains its own stuck cash equity share as a smart
order routing bottleneck rather than loyalty. On disclosure, the peers are less
forthcoming than IEX, not more: CDSL and MCX both refuse concentration
questions outright across four instances, and IEX is the only one of the four
names to have put a quantified concentration figure on the record. Two MCX
investors, discussing a different company, named IEX unprompted and described
coupling as having derailed the situation.

## WHERE THE OPPORTUNITY SITS

Stage 9 sizes the fee pool, not the value of electricity traded, and holds the
identity of revenue equal to volume times fee against FY26 actuals. The
serviceable market is Rs 924 crore against a total pool of Rs 1,087 to 1,196
crore, IEX already holds about 60 percent of it, and revenue headroom is 1.65
times. Runway class is LIMITED. The coupling downside case implies 2.9 percent
revenue growth per year at year three and 5.3 percent at year five; the base
case implies 9.2 to 9.5 percent. The strategy hurdle is 25 percent. The core
electricity fee pool does not reach it on any case tested. Any bull case has to
be built on the three option pools sized separately. Gas has the clearest
arithmetic, with IGX at 1.94 BCM of FY26 volume against a cited 16.40 BCM
market, 8.45 times headroom, reaching IEX only through an equity stake falling
from 47.28 percent to the PNGRB ceiling of 25 percent. Coal is real in volume,
100 to 120 million tonnes in year one against Coal India's own electronic
auction run rate of 110 to 150 million tonnes, but no per tonne fee is
disclosed anywhere, so no rupee figure exists, and the licence has not been
awarded to IEX. Carbon is not sized at all: trading has not started and no fee
schedule exists.
