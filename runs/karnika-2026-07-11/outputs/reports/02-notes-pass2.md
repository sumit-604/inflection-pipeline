### Note 2(t) vs Note 18 — Cash equivalents classification inconsistency (NEW)
Note 2(t), p.76 states the accounting policy: cash and cash equivalents comprise "cash at banks and on hand and short-term deposits with an **original maturity of twelve months or less**." Note 18, p.83, however, includes a line item "Fixed Deposit with banks (with maturity more than 12 months): 56.71 (FY25) vs 53.23 (FY24)" classified *inside* Cash and Cash Equivalents — directly contradicting the maturity threshold stated in the policy note itself. 🟡 Watch — minor but genuine classification/disclosure inconsistency between Note 2(t) and Note 18; not raised in Pass 1, which took the Note 18 total at face value without checking the sub-line composition against the stated policy.

### Note 18 — Cash decline composition (NEW supporting detail, not in Pass 1)
Pass 1 flagged the 90.5% cash decline (Rank 4) but did not extract its composition. Full breakdown: Balances with banks 546.49→19.76 (-96.4%); Cheque in Hand 26.23→0 (fully drawn down); Fixed Deposit <12mo maturity 526.52→27.97 (-94.7%); Fixed Deposit >12mo maturity 53.23→56.71 (+6.5%, essentially untouched — see classification point above); Cash on hand 0.95→5.60. 🔴 (feeds existing Rank 4 flag) — every liquid, near-term component of the FY24 cash cushion was drawn down to near-zero except the longer-tenor FD, which is consistent with active near-term deployment into the securities book (Note 12) and the inter-corporate loan (Note 19) rather than a broad-based liquidity drawdown (Note 18, p.83).

### Note 27 vs Note 33 — Unidentified unsecured loan, cross-reference gap (NEW)

### Note 34 — CORRECTION to Pass 1 Rank 9 finding (IMPORTANT)
Re-reading Note 34, Table 1 (Components of Employer Expense, p.90) against Pass 1's characterization: Pass 1 described FY24 as an "actuarial LOSS of 917.89 thousand" that flipped to an "actuarial GAIN of 1,383.08 thousand" in FY25. On re-check, **both years' Actuarial Losses/(Gains) figures are shown in parentheses in the source table — FY24 (917.89) and FY25 (1,383.08)** — and under the table's own "(Gains)" sign convention, a parenthesized value is a GAIN, not a loss. This is confirmed by the arithmetic: FY24 Current Service Cost 1,459.87 + Interest Cost 88.36 = 1,548.23; to reach the disclosed Total Employer Expense of 630.34, the actuarial item of 917.89 must be *subtracted* (i.e., it is a gain, not an addition/loss — adding it would produce 2,466.12, which does not match the disclosed total). **Correct reading: the Company booked an actuarial GAIN in both years — 917.89 thousand (₹9.18L) in FY24, rising to a larger gain of 1,383.08 thousand (₹13.83L) in FY25** — not a loss-to-gain reversal. 🟡 Watch, correction — the underlying question for management is still valid and arguably sharper: why did the actuarial gain *grow* by ~51% YoY despite the discount rate falling from 7.30% to 7.00% (which mechanically increases the DBO, all else equal), in *both* years rather than just one; other assumptions (salary escalation, attrition, mortality) remain undisclosed — NOT FOUND IN DOCUMENT (Note 34, Table 1, p.90; Note 26, p.84).
