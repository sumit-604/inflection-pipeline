## Business Understanding Narrative

**What does it do, and how does it make money?** Filatex makes polyester yarn
at Dahej in Gujarat and Dadra. It buys PTA and MEG, polymerises them, and spins
partially oriented yarn, fully drawn yarn and draw texturised yarn. It also
makes polyester chips, polypropylene and narrow woven fabric. It reports one
segment, manufacturing and trading of polyester goods (Q1 FY27 results,
page 9). FY26 sales volume was 388,800 tonnes (FY26 results call, page 4). The
money is the conversion spread per tonne.

**What is changing?** Two things, ten years apart. The first shift is done. In
2016 polyester chips were 61% of revenue and DTY plus FDY were 18%. By 2021
chips were 5% and DTY plus FDY were 76% (Investor Presentation FY2022 Q3,
page 16). Blended margin rose with the mix. The second shift is live. Filatex
is spending Rs 690 crore. Part expands FDY, POY and DTY at Dahej. Part builds a
greenfield textile-to-textile chemical recycling plant through the subsidiary
Ecosis Limited (Q1 FY27 call, page 5).

**Why now?** The capex lands this quarter. Management said the brownfield
expansion would be 50% complete by September 2026 and the balance by October
2026. Ecosis has signed a memorandum of understanding with Indica Sporting
Goods Private Limited, a Decathlon Group entity, which is running structural
trials of Ecosis chips and yarn across its Indian manufacturing partners.
Filatex has also collaborated with American and Efird Global LLC for industrial
threads (Q1 FY27 call, page 5).

**What must be true for the thesis to work?** Recycled polyester must sell at a
premium that a commodity converter cannot earn. Management guided the recycling
plant to add roughly Rs 80 crore of EBITDA on Rs 300 crore of capex, and the
brownfield expansion to add roughly Rs 70 crore on Rs 235 crore. It guided a
blended EBITDA margin of 11% to 12% (Q1 FY26 call, pages 6 and 7). FY26
delivered 8.3%.

**What breaks it?** Chemical recycling is hard. Management itself says other
companies have spent millions of dollars on research and still have no large
plant, and ends with "Let us hope it works well" (FY26 results call, page 13).
The plant is 75 tonnes a day. First-six-month utilisation was guided at 65% to
70%. If brands do not pay the premium, Rs 300 crore has bought more commodity
capacity.

## 2. Business model and archetype

One segment, two plants, one economic engine. Revenue equals tonnes multiplied
by realisation. Q1 FY27 revenue rose 16.3% sequentially on flat volume, which
management attributed to higher raw material prices passed into price (Q1 FY27
call, page 3). The margin sits in the mix. FDY and DTY earn more per tonne than
POY, and chips earn least.

**Archetype: commodity converter, moving toward outsourcing partner and
value-added supplier.** Section 1B v3.7 Amendment 17 binds the converter slice
of any later valuation.

## 3. Competitive advantages

Partial and forming. Scale is real: 389,027 tonnes produced in FY26 (FY26
results call, page 4). Integration from polymer to yarn is real. Neither gives
pricing power in POY. The recycling platform is the one place a moat could
form, because it needs a qualified product and a customer committed to recycled
content. Moat: absent today, forming in one product line.

## 4. The promoters

The Bhageria family. Madhu Sudhan Bhageria is Chairman and Managing Director.
Purrshottam Bhaggeria and Madhav Bhageria are promoters. Vedansh Bhageria is a
Director of Ecosis and leads the recycling venture (Q1 FY27 call, page 2). As

Skin in the game is visible. The promoters bought shares in the market in

## 6. The transition and the quality ladder

FROM R2 cost-advantaged converter. TO R3 value-added and spec'd supplier, in
the recycled line only. The mechanism is circularity. Global brands have
announced recycled-content commitments, and a qualified supplier of virgin-like
recycled chips can charge for the qualification rather than the tonne.

Management's claim, quoted once: "Filatex will become one of the first
integrated polyester manufacturers in India to establish a circular polyester
platform capable of converting end-of-life textile waste into virgin-like
polyester chips suitable for manufacturing premium polyester yarns" (Q1 FY27
call, page 5).

This is one rung. The framework's base rate allows one rung in two to three
years. The claim is inside the base rate.

## 8. Proof check

The triggers claim margin expansion from mix. The last two quarters show margin
expansion. FY26 EBITDA margin was 8.3% against 6.1% in FY25. Profit before tax
rose from Rs 53.30 crore in Q4 FY26 to Rs 65.25 crore in Q1 FY27 on a 16.3%
revenue rise.

The proof point that matters has not arrived. The recycling plant has not
been reported as commissioned in any document held. The brownfield expansion is
half done. Every rupee of the guided Rs 150 crore EBITDA uplift is still a
forecast. Name the absence plainly: no delivered volume from either project
appears in this corpus.

## 9. Flags from the documents

- Volume is falling while revenue rises. Production down 11.5% year on year in
  Q1 FY27. Flag.
- Management quoted Q1 FY27 profit before tax of Rs 65.87 crore and profit
  after tax of Rs 49.1 crore on the call. The consolidated filing shows
  Rs 65.25 crore and Rs 48.52 crore. The gap is the standalone-to-consolidated
  difference. Small, but the call did not say which basis it used. Flag.
- Cumulative investment in Ecosis reached Rs 64.98 crore after a further
  Rs 10 crore rights issue in Q1 FY27 (Q1 FY27 results, page 5). The Rs 300
  crore recycling project is far from funded through this route. Flag the
  funding path.
- India imports MEG, which management calls a structural risk (Q1 FY27 call,
  page 4). Promoter-group total and pledge NOT FOUND.

## 10. Independent check: the credit rating

India Ratings and Research reaffirmed IND AA-/Stable and IND A1+ on Rs 998
crore of bank facilities on 12 May 2026 (Credit Rating intimation, page 1). AA
minus is a strong rating for a mid-cap converter. The agency reaffirmed it
after FY26, so it saw the margin expansion and the Rs 690 crore capex plan and
did not flinch. The rationale itself is NOT FOUND, so the agency's view on the
recycling project is unknown.

## 11. Posture: the Transition Decision Matrix

- **Proof gate: NOT FIRED.** The recycling plant is not commissioned. The
  brownfield is half built. The margin gain so far is mix and price inside the
  existing asset base.
- **Ugliness: ARTIFACT-OF-CLIMB.** Falling volume and a thin 8.3% margin sit
  next to a Rs 690 crore build. The optic is temporary if the capex lands.
- **Recognition gap: CLOSED on delivered earnings, OPEN on guided earnings.**
  Price basis, not a corpus document: Bull AI company record, market
  capitalisation Rs 4,174 crore. Paid-up capital of Rs 44.41 crore at Re 1 face
  value gives 44.41 crore shares (Q1 FY27 results, page 8). FY26 earnings per
  share were Rs 4.13. The market pays a full converter multiple for what has
  been delivered. It does not obviously pay for the Rs 150 crore of guided
  EBITDA.

**Posture: RESEARCH / WATCH, one print away from RE-RATING LIVE.** The gate
fires or fails in Q2 and Q3 FY27.

## 12. Verdict card

**PROCEED to `/step1`.**

The case for spending a full run is timing, not price. The proof gate is weeks
away, the triggers carry dates and rupee numbers, and the operator should hold
a signed mental model before the print rather than after it. The company has
already executed one mix shift over a decade, it carries an AA minus rating,
its finance cost is trivial against its revenue, and its promoters bought stock
in the market. That is a base worth building on.

**Load-bearing facts a `/step1` must verify first**
1. Ecosis commissioning. Is the 75 tonne a day plant running, and at what
   utilisation.
2. The Decathlon and A&E arrangements. Are they trials or are they offtake.
   The corpus says trials.
3. Realisation per tonne on recycled chips against virgin chips. The whole
   rung climb lives in that spread.
4. How the Rs 690 crore is funded. Cumulative equity into Ecosis is Rs 64.98
   crore. Find the debt.
5. Why volume fell 11.5% while revenue rose.

**What would change the view**
- A commissioning announcement with volume, not a commissioning plan.
- A named offtake contract with a recycled-content price.
- A second quarter of falling volume without the price offset, which would
  turn the ugliness structural.

No valuation was run. No target price. This verdict decides only whether the
name earns a full `/step1`, and it is not a position.
