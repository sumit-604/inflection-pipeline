- Demand runway is large and confirmed by the deck's F&S data (this quarter's deck slide 16, in ₹ Bn): organised CCR ₹98 Bn → ₹227 Bn by 2030 (12.7% CAGR), organised ETS ₹78 Bn → ₹198 Bn (14.2% CAGR), GCC count 1,580 (2023) → 2,400 (2030). Ecos at ~₹808 Cr FY26 revenue is a small share of a growing organised market.
- The structural sector risk is pricing power: cars and drivers are vendor-sourced, so input-cost inflation passes through to margins with limited ability to reprice (Notion/prior-work). This quarter's flat operating EBITDA on 16.7% revenue growth and the 8% fall in revenue per trip are that risk showing up in the numbers (this quarter's press release + deck).
- Regulation/payer mix: demand is tied to corporate travel and outsourced-transport budgets; no regulatory catalyst is disclosed. Single reportable segment (Ind AS-108), so no sub-sector P&L breakout; a board-approved event-management line could create a second segment later (this quarter's results filing, Note 4).

### 8.3 Business-model intelligence
- How it makes money: asset-light corporate car rental. Cars and drivers are sourced from third-party vendors; Ecos holds the customer relationship and takes a spread. The 5% owned / 95% vendor GROUP fleet mix (this quarter's deck slide 22) shows the model is intact at group level and MITIGATES-PENDING the results-review worry that the new Ecos Fleet Management subsidiary signalled a drift to owned assets (the subsidiary's own gross block is still undisclosed, so the concern is eased, not closed).
- Unit economics this quarter: operating EBITDA margin 10.3% consolidated, reported ~12.1%, PAT margin ~6.9% — all below the guided 13-15% EBITDA and 8.5-10% PAT bands (guidance = Notion/prior-work; actuals = this quarter's filing). Cost of services alone is 75% of revenue (₹158.7 Cr / ₹211.4 Cr, this quarter's filing). Revenue per trip ~₹1,428, down ~8% YoY (this quarter's press release, derived from trips and revenue).
- Balance-sheet model: near-zero leverage confirmed for the first time from the FY26 balance sheet (this quarter's deck slide 12): net cash ~₹137.6 Cr, borrowings ₹0.11 Cr, debtor days ~48, building to ₹155.8 Cr cash by 30 June. - Model-drift signals: revenue mix shifting to lower-yield ETS (59%); long-standing (>5yr) customer revenue share drifting down 61→55→51% (this quarter's deck + press release); tech and leadership-bandwidth spend expensed (zero capitalised intangibles) and adding to near-term margin pressure (this quarter's deck balance sheet).

### 8.4 Competition intelligence
- Where it wins: scale and breadth in organised corporate mobility (151 cities, 70+ Fortune 500 and 75+ BSE 500 clients, ~1,400 active clients up 18% — this quarter's press release), a genuinely asset-light near-zero-debt balance sheet with ₹137.6 Cr net cash (this quarter's deck), a customer-satisfaction rating of 4.8 (this quarter's deck), and a promoter judged TRUSTWORTHY with Management Grade B (Notion/prior-work).

The new SIXT India-GSA (leisure/inbound) and the board-approved event-management line take it into adjacencies where it has no demonstrated moat, potentially diluting returns while the core margin is already soft (this quarter's press release + results filing). Named peer unit economics were not disclosed in any of the three documents (metric not disclosed).
- Competitive risk to watch: top-10 CLIENT concentration is still not disclosed (the deck gives city concentration, top-3 cities ~51%, not client — this quarter's deck); it resolves at the FY26 Annual Report. City mix is metro/GCC-hub concentrated (Bangalore 22.3%, Delhi 14.6%, Gurgaon 14.1%), so a slowdown in GCC hiring would hit revenue directly (this quarter's deck slide on city mix).
