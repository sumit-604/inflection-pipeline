## NEW FINDING 1 — Annexure VI restatement: FY23 audited PAT cut 25.9% by a tax-expense restatement;
## recurring tax-expense restatement adjustments in 4 of 5 periods presented — MISSED ENTIRELY in Pass 1

Pass 1 stated it read "Annexure VI — Statement of Restatement Adjustments (extract pages 404-405/614)" but
did not extract its contents. Re-reading it now:

**Annexure VI, Reconciliation between audited PAT and restated PAT (p.404/614), ₹mn:**

| | Sep-25 (6mo) | Sep-24 (6mo) | FY25 | FY24 | FY23 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| PAT per Audited Financial Statements | 651.18 | 1,078.00 | 1,712.72 | 1,611.27 | 537.96 |
| Restatement adjustment — Tax Expense | (5.21) | 0.00 | +39.80 | +105.52 | **(139.23)** |
| PAT per Restated Financial Information | 645.97 | 1,078.00 | 1,752.52 | 1,716.79 | **398.73** |

FY23 audited PAT of ₹537.96mn was restated DOWN to ₹398.73mn — a **₹139.23mn, -25.9% cut**, entirely a
"Tax Expense" line adjustment. The explanatory note (p.404/614) states: "The company had identified
short/excess provision for income tax in accounting of earlier year tax adjustments and had accounted as
prior period items in the year in which the short/excess of provision was identified. Further, the
necessary adjustments related to the computation and effect of assessment orders have also been made in
the relevant financial year/period."

The equivalent equity reconciliation (same page) confirms the same pattern at the balance-sheet level:
Total Equity as per Audited FS at March 31, 2023 was ₹7,685.04mn; the restated figure is ₹7,544.93mn
(-₹140.11mn) — consistent with the PAT-level cut carried into opening reserves. The April-01-2022 opening
equity also carries a smaller -₹0.88mn tax adjustment.

**This directly explains a Pass-1-flagged mystery.** Pass 1 §10 flagged: "the FY23 'Others' tax
reconciling item of ₹174.05mn (about 24% of pre-tax profit) is unexplained in the note" (Note 40(b),
p.358/614). Cross-referencing now: the FY23 tax-expense reconciliation "Others" line of ₹174.05mn is
substantially the SAME restatement event as the ₹139.23mn Annexure VI adjustment (the ₹174.05mn is the
gross P&L reconciling figure; ₹139.23mn is its net-of-related-effects PAT impact). Pass 1 had the two
numbers in two different notes and did not connect them; they are the same underlying prior-period tax
true-up.

**What is new and did not appear in Pass 1 at all**: the restatement pattern is not confined to FY23. Tax
expense restatement adjustments appear in **4 of the 5 periods presented** (all except H1FY25), and they
run in BOTH directions:
- FY23: tax expense revised UP (unfavourable), cutting PAT -25.9%.
- FY24: tax expense revised DOWN (favourable), raising PAT +₹105.52mn (+6.5% vs audited ₹1,611.27mn).
- FY25: tax expense revised DOWN (favourable), raising PAT +₹39.80mn (+2.3% vs audited ₹1,712.72mn).
- H1FY26: tax expense revised UP marginally (-₹5.21mn, -0.8%).

🔴 **Red flag, upgraded from Pass 1's "watch" framing on the FY23 item alone.** A single large unfavourable
correction is one thing; a recurring pattern of material tax-provision corrections — one of them equal to
over a quarter of a full year's profit — across nearly the entire restated track record used for this IPO
is a genuine tax-provisioning-process concern. It is fully disclosed, audited, and standard under SEBI
restatement rules (so not itself an integrity issue), but investors relying on FY23-FY25 PAT/EPS as a
"clean" three-year growth base should note that none of the three full years' tax lines were free of
material prior-period correction, and the FY23 base year specifically is 26% lower than what the company
originally reported to its own board and auditors at the time.
(Annexure VI, p.404/614; cross-ref Note 40(b), p.358/614)

## NEW FINDING 2 — CRAR fell over 5 points in the most recent 12 months; Tier I capital roughly flat
## through H1FY26 despite a profitable half — connects to the DTA/ECL note Pass 1 flagged separately

**Note 48, Capital management (p.365/614), regulatory capital, ₹mn:**

| | Sep-25 | Sep-24 | FY25 | FY24 | FY23 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tier I capital | 14,262.45 | 13,976.85 | 14,295.19 | 10,587.63 | 6,563.76 |
| Tier II capital | Nil | Nil | Nil | Nil | Nil |
| Risk weighted assets | 44,201.26 | 37,163.92 | 40,940.80 | 32,292.76 | 21,124.92 |
| CRAR (%) | 32.27% | 37.61% | 34.92% | 32.79% | 31.07% |

Not covered by Pass 1 at all (Note 48 was not extracted). Two things stand out:

1. CRAR fell from **37.61% (Sep-24, immediately after the ~₹1,858mn Sep-2024 equity raise flagged in Pass
   1 §12) to 32.27% (Sep-25)** — a drop of over 5 percentage points in twelve months — because risk-weighted
   assets grew +19.0% (₹37,163.92mn → ₹44,201.26mn) while Tier I capital grew only +2.0%
   (₹13,976.85mn → ₹14,262.45mn).
2. Within H1FY26 alone, Tier I capital actually **declined slightly**, ₹14,295.19mn (Mar-25) to
   ₹14,262.45mn (Sep-25), a -0.23% fall, despite ₹645.97mn of profit earned in the same half-year (per
   Annexure II P&L). Since Tier I capital for an Ind-AS reporter is broadly retained earnings plus paid-up
   capital less regulatory deductions (intangibles, certain DTA), a period of positive PAT producing flat-to
   -negative Tier I growth implies regulatory-capital deductions grew faster than retained profit added
   capital. The most plausible driver, cross-referencing Pass 1 §10, is the growing net DTA balance
   (₹609.78mn at FY25 → declining slightly to ₹582.05mn at Sep-25 per Note 9, p.328/614, but still
   substantially collateralised by ECL timing differences per Pass 1's own finding) combined with rising
   intangible assets under development (Note 37, ₹41.47mn Sep-25) — both of which are commonly deducted or
   capped in RBI Tier I computations.

🟡 Watch, new pattern connecting two Pass-1-separate notes. CRAR at 32.27% remains comfortably above the
RBI 15% regulatory minimum for NBFCs, and the ₹7,100mn fresh-issue IPO proceeds will materially replenish
capital post-listing, so this is not a compliance risk. But the trajectory — a five-point CRAR fall in one
year on a zero-Tier-II capital structure, with Tier I capital stalling in the very half-year that saw the
sharpest Stage 3/write-off deterioration (Pass 1 §6c) — is a genuine capital-consumption signal that
belongs alongside the credit-quality flags, not read in isolation.
(Note 48, p.365/614; cross-ref Note 9, p.328/614; Pass 1 §10)

## NEW FINDING 3 — ARC-sale footnote reveals the FY25 transfer was already-written-off paper; this
## meaningfully tempers (not eliminates) Pass 1's Finding #5 on NPA sales understating GNPA

Pass 1 Finding #5 read the FY25 ₹2,593.70mn NPA transfer to ARCs as loans being "moved off-book" in a way
that "understates" the headline GNPA ratio. Re-reading Note 53.27.1(d)(i) in full, including its footnote
(which Pass 1 did not extract), changes this:

"*Including written off loans amounting to Rs. 2,593.7 millions in March, 2025 and Rs. 516.5 millions in
March, 2023." (footnote to the ARC-transfer table, p.393-394/614)

- **FY25**: the entire ₹2,593.70mn aggregate principal outstanding transferred to ARC was ALREADY WRITTEN
  OFF before the transfer (the footnote figure exactly equals the full transferred amount). Once a loan is
  written off, it is removed from gross carrying value/Stage 3 GNPA under Note 52/53.13.4 — so this
  transfer, by construction, could not have "understated" the FY25 GNPA ratio; the loans were already
  excluded from the on-book gross-loan denominator before the sale. It reads more accurately as a recovery
  /monetisation event on an already-provided-for pool (generating security receipts) than as a mechanism to
  avoid GNPA recognition.
- **FY23**: only ₹516.50mn of the ₹837.60mn transferred (61.7%) was previously written off; the remaining
  **₹321.10mn was sold while still on-book** (not yet fully written off) — this smaller, FY23-vintage
  portion is the part that genuinely fits Pass 1's original "moved off-book before recognition" framing.

🟡 Revision, not a new red flag — but a material correction to how Finding #5 should be weighted. Pass 1's
framing implied the FY25 ₹259 crore ARC transaction was compounding the GNPA-understatement concern; the
underlying note shows the opposite for FY25 specifically (it is post-write-off recycling, economically a
recovery), while the smaller FY23 transaction (₹32.1 crore of live NPA) is the genuine instance of the
concern Pass 1 described. Net effect: the ARC-sale-related GNPA-distortion risk is real but much smaller in
absolute terms (~₹32 crore, FY23 only) than Pass 1's original framing suggested.
(Note 53.27.1(d)(i), footnote, p.393-394/614)

Pass 1 §6b flagged qualitatively that ARCIL security receipts were "93% impaired" but did not quantify or

- **Note 28, Impairment on financial instruments (p.347/614)** — the P&L note that Pass 1 relied on for the
  ₹2,888.26mn FY25 impairment charge — does NOT itemise this ₹290.51mn; its four sub-lines (ECL on

disclosed P&L caption in Notes 21-30 discloses it as a distinct expense line; it is visible only by

🔴 Flag — new. This is a disclosure-transparency gap, not evidence of misstatement (the amount is

write-down of retained ARC interests Pass 1 already identified). But for an amount this size, ahead of an
IPO, the absence of a clean P&L trail is a fair question for management and a genuine minus on the
disclosure-transparency dimension of the accounting-quality score.
(Note 6, p.329/614; Annexure III, p.315/614; Note 28, p.347/614; Note 30, p.348/614)

## NEW FINDING 5 — Restructured book nearly doubled again WITHIN H1FY26 itself, a materially sharper
## move than the FY23-FY25 trend Pass 1 captured

Pass 1 §6c reported the restructured book grew from 262 borrowers/₹24.30mn (FY24 opening) to 401
borrowers/₹40.93mn (FY25 closing), +53%/+68%. Re-reading Note 46.1 (six months ended September 30, 2025,
p.361/614) in full — which Pass 1 read but did not carry the closing H1FY26 figures out of — shows the book
kept moving sharply within the very next half-year:

**Note 46.1, restructured-accounts rollforward, H1FY26 (₹mn, borrower count):**

| | Borrowers | Amount | Provision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opening, April 1, 2025 (= Pass 1's "FY25 closing") | 401 | 40.93 | 28.77 |
| Fresh restructuring during H1FY26 | +317 | +36.20 | +25.23 |
| Down-gradations | (15) | (4.77) | (3.11) |
| Write-offs of restructured accounts | (38) | (4.86) | (3.46) |
| **Closing, September 30, 2025** | **665** | **67.50** | **47.42** |

(Reconciles exactly: 401+317-15-38 = 665 borrowers; 40.93+36.20-4.77-4.86 = 67.50; provision ties similarly.)

That is **+65.8% in borrower count and +64.9% in amount within a single six-month period**, driven by 317
fresh restructurings (₹36.20mn) in H1FY26 alone — a volume comparable to the entire FY23-FY25 restructuring
build-up Pass 1 already flagged. The book remains small in absolute AUM terms (₹67.50mn / ₹56,202.39mn
gross loans = 0.12%), but the rate of acceleration is materially sharper than the trend Pass 1 characterized
as "notable... still tiny relative to AUM."

🔴 Flag, new — reinforces (with sharper, more recent evidence) the credit-stress pattern already identified
around Stage 2/3 migration, write-offs, and GNPA. The most recent half-year is the worst point in the
restructuring trend, not merely a continuation of it.
(Note 46.1, p.361/614)

## NEW FINDING 8 (minor, clarifying, no new concern) — The H1FY26 "Net loss on fair value changes" spike is
## fully explained by an equal-and-opposite hedge gain in a different note

Note 27 (p.346/614) shows "Net loss on fair value changes" jumping to ₹307.53mn in H1FY26 from ₹62.59mn in
H1FY25 — a number large enough that, read alone, might look like a new market-risk concern. Cross-referencing
Note 24 (p.346/614), the "Gain on fair value of cross currency swap" line is ₹314.14mn in the SAME period —
i.e., the ₹307.53mn "loss" in Note 27 is the currency-translation leg of the cross-currency swap, and its
economic offset (the swap's own fair-value gain) sits in a different revenue-side note. Net P&L effect is a
small ₹6.61mn gain, not a ₹307.53mn loss. This is consistent with, and confirms, Pass 1's finding that FX
borrowings are fully hedged (Note 49.3.1) — it is a gross-versus-net presentation point, not a new risk.
🟢 no incremental concern; included because Pass 1 did not connect these two notes and a reader relying on
Note 27 alone would misread the size of the swing.
(Note 27, p.346/614; Note 24, p.346/614)
