Profit tripled, cash turned negative, merger pending

Birla Cable makes telecom cable at one plant in Rewa, Madhya Pradesh. Two product lines carry the whole company. Structured copper LAN cable was 86% of FY26 revenue. Optical fibre cable was 14%. FY26 revenue was Rs 771 Cr and net profit was Rs 17 Cr. Raw material eats 82% of revenue, so this is a converter, not a brand. One customer bought 45.0% of FY26 revenue, up from 39.6% the year before. The rating agency puts the top ten customers at 94% of FY25 revenue and the largest at 47%. The company itself never publishes either number.

The dominant fact is a merger, not an operating story. The board approved a scheme of amalgamation on 21 March 2026 that folds Birla Cable into its parent, Vindhya Telelinks. Shareholders receive 10 VTL shares for every 115 Birla Cable shares. The appointed date is 1 April 2026. The exchanges gave their letters in August 2026 and the NCLT approval is still pending. The rating agency expects completion about ten to twelve months from the board date, so roughly January to March 2027. If the court sanctions the scheme, Birla Cable is dissolved without winding up and stops existing as a listed company.

The four year record is contraction, not growth. Revenue compounded at minus 0.89% from FY23 to FY26 and profit at minus 19.94%. Revenue fell in two of the three year on year steps. Median return on capital was 7.55% across the two years the formula could be computed cleanly. The Gate 0 screen scored 39 out of 100 on the core blocks and classified the company AVOID. A stricter recomputation by the framework verifier cuts that core score to 27 and leaves the classification unchanged. Zero of twelve moat tests scored a pass, so the moat block is 0 out of 60.

FY26 growth came from one segment and one buyer. Copper LAN cable revenue rose 28.7%. Optical fibre revenue fell 8.3% in the same year. Earnings per share went from 1.68 to 5.62 on that mix shift, with no exceptional items, no fresh equity and no goodwill behind it. Q1 FY27 then printed revenue up 51.1% year on year with profit up more than twenty times. No results filing splits copper from fibre, so the claimed fibre recovery is still unproven at company level.

The cash did not follow the profit. Operating cash flow was minus Rs 21 Cr in FY26 against net profit of Rs 17 Cr, after plus Rs 112 Cr in FY25. Receivables absorbed Rs 33 Cr and inventory Rs 28 Cr. Receivable days went from 92 to 96 and working capital days from 104 to 119. The debt service coverage ratio, the cash available against debt due, was 0.39 times in FY26 and 0.40 times in FY25. Both years sit below one. Borrowings payable on demand went from 22% to 62% of a Rs 131 Cr debt book. A Rs 25 Cr export receivable has been outstanding two to three years with no named counterparty, carried as good on management representation. Capex was Rs 1.9 Cr, about 12% of depreciation, and the fixed asset base shrank 14%, while two sections of the annual report describe the year's spending as capacity expansion.

Management said one useful thing and delivered it once. The annual report signed on 22 May 2026 said fibre demand had revived since late March and would strengthen in FY27. Q1 FY27 landed three months later and validated the direction. That is a single cycle, with the mechanism unconfirmed, and there are no earnings calls to test anything further. Set against it are four disclosure defects inside the same document set. The Directors' Report claims effective cash flow and working capital management in the year cash flow went negative. The Directors' Report says fibre order flow was broadly in line with last year while the management discussion in the same report says demand was subdued and revenue fell 8.3%. The management discussion carries a profit figure that matches neither the standalone nor the consolidated audited number. And Note 36 shows contingent liabilities of Rs 0.21 Cr while the audit annexure in the same document discloses a Rs 2.67 Cr municipal tax dispute running since 2003.

Promoter conduct is stable but the merger is a related party deal. Promoter holding is 66.35% and has not moved since September 2023. No promoter sold. The only pledge is 12.5 lakh Birla Cable shares, 4.17% of equity, pledged by Vindhya Telelinks with SBI for its own working capital, unchanged year on year. Against that, Vindhya Telelinks owns 19.33% of Birla Cable and Universal Cables owns 13.00%, so promoter entities sit on both sides of the swap. Vindhya Telelinks also guarantees Birla Cable's secured borrowings with no fee disclosed anywhere. No proxy adviser published a view on the ratio. The chairmanship itself rests on a 22 year old Birla estate dispute with Supreme Court appeals still pending. The chief executive of 21 years resigned in January 2026 with no reason given, and the finance head was promoted into a combined manager and finance role four months later. A director aged 87 attended one of five board meetings and is up for re appointment by special resolution.

Peers confirm the demand story and complicate the price story. Twelve quarters of calls from HFCL, Sterlite Technologies and Paramount Communications all describe an AI and data centre led fibre upcycle, with BharatNet, 5G and home broadband behind it. Two peer statements cut against the company. HFCL's managing director said on 30 April 2026 that fibre prices had reached almost their final level and would not rise further. The Birla Cable board signed the opposite framing three weeks later. Sterlite calls fibre pricing stable with no uptick and refuses to disclose realisation, so only one peer actually supports the record pricing claim. Peers also describe an input squeeze the company never mentions: bare fibre from Rs 250 to above Rs 300 per km, preform costs up 20 to 25%, and germanium supply controlled from China. HFCL is spending Rs 580 Cr on its own preform plant. Birla Cable makes no fibre and buys it from a group joint venture. All three peers quantified US tariff damage in FY26. Birla Cable exports about 74% of its fibre cable revenue and its entire record says nothing about tariffs.

The market is large and the company's share of it is capped by its own capex. The conservative addressable market is Rs 1.39 lakh Cr, covering global standard fibre cable plus India copper and LAN cable content. The serviceable slice is Rs 6,060 Cr and the company already holds about 12.7% of it, which leaves revenue headroom of 7.9 times and a runway class of GOOD. But with no capacity expansion in flight, the three and five year realistic share implies revenue growth of only 2.5 to 2.9% a year. A separate check on capex embedded growth landed at 3%, so two methods converge on a low single digit ceiling. Management's implied market claim runs about eleven times the conservative cable market because it cites India's telecom services market rather than the cable market. The disclosed order book was Rs 24 Cr at 31 December 2025, against a quarterly revenue run rate above Rs 200 Cr. The market sizing work is recorded as partial, with one method and peer segment revenue left unresolved.
