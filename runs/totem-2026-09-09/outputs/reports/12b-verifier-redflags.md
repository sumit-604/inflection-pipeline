## PART A — MY INDEPENDENT RED-FLAG LIST (47 items)

### A1. Claims contradicted by a number in the same document

**1. FY26 exports called a "stable trend" while FOB exports fell 15.9%.**
MD&A: "The business has also seen a stable trend in the export business
performance while the domestic market saw reasonable growth" (AR2026 p.25).
Annexure IV, sixteen pages later in the same report: export of goods on FOB
basis Rs 3,189.46 lakh (AR2026 p.42), against Rs 3,791.05 lakh the prior year
(AR2025 p.33). That is minus 15.9 per cent. Exports fell from 16.3 per cent of
revenue from operations to 12.7 per cent. Every rupee of FY26 growth came from
domestic (implied domestic Rs 19,475 lakh to Rs 21,912 lakh, plus 12.5 per cent).
The negative appears only where s.134(3)(m) forces the number out.

**2. The same misdescription, one year earlier.** AR2025 MD&A: "The business has
also seen an improving trend in the export business performance" (AR2025 p.19).
FY25 FOB Rs 3,791.05 lakh against FY24 Rs 3,834.10 lakh (AR2024 p.29) — a
decline. Two consecutive years in which the direction of the export line is
stated backwards in the narrative and correctly in the forced annexure. **This
is a repeated pattern across two reporting periods, not a one-off slip.**

**3. Interest-coverage explanation contradicted by the auditor in the same
annual report.** MD&A ratio table: interest coverage fell 33.46 per cent
"mainly due to the Company availed borrowing facilities to meet its working
capital requirements" (AR2026 p.26). CARO Annexure A, clause (ii)(b), signed by
Sharp and Tannan on the same date: "During the year the company has not been
sanctioned a working capital facility" (AR2026 p.63). A third filed number
breaks the MD&A version outright: total borrowings **fell** from Rs 1,968.29
lakh to Rs 1,487.07 lakh (AR2026 p.27). The real cause is visible in the FY25
cash flow — Rs 1,700 lakh of long-term borrowing drawn part-way through FY25
(AR2026 p.72) carrying a full year of interest in FY26, finance cost Rs 112.79
lakh to Rs 166.07 lakh (AR2026 note 25, p.103). The MD&A gives a reason the
company's own auditor says did not happen.

**4. Statutory managerial-remuneration disclosure understates the rise by
roughly ten times.** Annexure II, the mandated s.197(12) disclosure: "The
percentage increase in remuneration of Key Managerial Personnel are average
7.5%" and "There are no exceptional circumstances for an increase in managerial
remuneration" (AR2026 p.37). Note 30 in the audited accounts: KMP remuneration
Rs 361.34 lakh to Rs 566.11 lakh, plus 56.7 per cent (AR2026 p.110). The
Managing Director alone went from Rs 265.52 lakh to Rs 464.00 lakh, plus 74.7
per cent (AR2026 note 30 p.108 and p.110; the Rs 464.00 lakh breakdown is at
p.48). That is 105.65 times the median employee, and 16.1 per cent of PAT, in a
year PAT rose 0.1 per cent, headcount fell 12.7 per cent, and median employee
pay rose 7.2 per cent (AR2026 p.37). The 7.5 per cent figure is an affirmative
misstatement in a disclosure the Act requires.

**5. "Relentless focus on ... inventories" printed on top of a 76.7 per cent
inventory build.** AR2026 Finance section: "Relentless focus on receivables,
inventories, strict cost control and use of alternative borrowing instruments,
where possible, has helped in keeping the borrowings and effective interest
cost under control" (AR2026 p.29) — the identical sentence appears in AR2025
p.20. In the year it was reprinted: inventories Rs 3,193.07 lakh to Rs 5,642.15
lakh, plus 76.7 per cent (AR2026 p.68); effective interest cost plus 47.2 per
cent (p.103). Two of the three objects the sentence names moved against it.
Recycled text kept unchanged when the facts changed.

**6. Board's Report says there were no material related-party transactions; the
related-party note shows roughly Rs 724 lakh of FY26 expense flowing to the
promoter group.** "There were no material related party transactions made by the
Company with Promoters and Directors" (AR2026 p.29). Note 30 (AR2026 p.108):
rent Rs 145.73 lakh, travelling and conveyance Rs 236.13 lakh (Rs 223.27 lakh of
it to Sterling and Wilson Renewable Energy), legal and professional Rs 93.04
lakh to the holding company, repairs and maintenance Rs 125.30 lakh (Rs 122.95
lakh of it to the holding company), other reimbursements paid Rs 124.51 lakh.
That is about 18 per cent of PBT, before the Rs 1,904.86 lakh dividend. A
manufacturer paying its holding company Rs 122.95 lakh for "repairs and
maintenance" is a line that invites a question no forum exists to ask.

**7. Three mutually inconsistent counts of how long the business has operated.**
MD's Message: "we completed our second full year as an integrated precision
round cutting tool" (AR2026 p.7). MD&A, eighteen pages later: "this was the
third year full operations as a Precision Tool business entity" (AR2026 p.25).
AR2025 MD&A: "this was the first year of full operations" (AR2025 p.19), which
makes FY26 the second. Two of the three statements cannot be right.

**8. CSR annexure item 7 is filled "Not Applicable" when it applies.** Annexure
I item 7, "Details of Unspent CSR amount for the preceding three financial
years": **Not Applicable** (AR2026 p.35). The same annual report's own note
shows Rs 39.78 lakh transferred to an Unspent CSR Account for FY2024-25 and Rs
39.78 lakh spent out of that account during FY26 (AR2026 note 27, Note 1,
p.105). Item 7 exists precisely to disclose that fact. This is a stronger defect
than the framing point about the Board's Report sentence: it is a false entry,
not an omission.

**9. The auditor-report template error the corrigendum did not fix.** The
3-Jun-2025 corrigendum re-issued the FY25 annual report for "certain inadvertent
typographical errors ... in the Independent Auditors Report on Page no. 42, 43
and 46" without saying what any of them were. Comparing the two versions, one
correction is visible: the "other information" paragraph in the uncorrected
AR2025 ends "We have nothing to report in this regard" in the wrong place
(AR2025 p.48); the corrected text places it correctly (corrigendum file,
line 3373 region). But a plainer error survives the correction in both versions:
in a standalone-only auditor's report for a company with no subsidiary, the
key-audit-matters paragraph reads "those matters that were of most significance
in the audit of the **consolidated** financial statements" (AR2025 p.50 region;
same wording retained in the corrected file). The corrigendum fixed some things
and left that one.

### A2. Material facts absent from the narrative

**10. Headcount fell 12.7 per cent.** Permanent employees 481 to 420, a fall of
61 (AR2026 p.37 against AR2025 Annexure II). Against the 485 employees
transferred in from Forbes and Company on the effective date (AR2024 p.14
region), the workforce is down 13.4 per cent. The Board's Report section headed
"Material Development in Human Resources and Industrial Relations" (AR2026 p.28)
never states the number; it says relations were "very cordial" and that the
Board "specifically thanks its employees, its union and the Management". The
number appears only in the s.197(12) annexure, nine pages later, where the Act
forces it. Revenue rose 7.9 per cent on 12.7 per cent fewer people, which is
also the most likely mechanical driver of the FY27 margin move that both stage
reports attribute to capex.

**11. Research and development expenditure: Nil.** Annexure IV, item (v): "the
expenditure incurred on Research and Development: **Nil**" (AR2026 p.42). It
sits directly beneath two paragraphs describing thread-mill software, Simufact
simulation, aerospace machining solutions for titanium and superalloys, and
computer-aided engineering benchmarking (AR2026 p.41-42), and beneath an MD&A
claim of "strengthening our product development capabilities" (p.26). The peer
contrast is stark: Wendt discloses R&D rising from 0.9 per cent of sales in FY22
to about 2.6 per cent in FY25 with a DSIR-recognised R&D centre (WENDT p.10 and
p.21). For a thesis that rests on a climb toward a spec'd, value-added supplier
position, a filed zero on R&D spend is a first-order fact.

**12. The inventory-turnover ratio is missing from the ratio table in the year
inventory rose 77 per cent.** SEBI LODR Schedule V Part B(1) requires nine
ratios with an explanation for any change of 25 per cent or more. The MD&A gives
four — debtors turnover, interest coverage, operating profit margin, return on
net worth (AR2026 p.26; identically in AR2025 p.20). Inventory turnover, current
ratio, debt-equity ratio and net profit margin are all absent. Inventory
turnover is the one ratio whose disclosure would have forced a 25-per-cent
explanation of the FY26 build. The Corporate Governance compliance certificate
(AR2026 p.56) and the Annual Secretarial Compliance Report both certify full
LODR compliance over this gap.

**13. MSME trade payables nearly doubled.** Total outstanding dues of micro and
small enterprises Rs 741.30 lakh to Rs 1,431.62 lakh, plus 93.1 per cent
(AR2026 p.69; MSMED note p.100). MSME share of total trade payables rose from
31.7 per cent to 44.2 per cent. In the same year inventories rose Rs 2,449
lakh. This is filed evidence bearing directly on how the inventory build was
funded, and it is unmentioned anywhere in the narrative.

**14. Operating cash flow halved and the maiden dividend was about 2.8 times
free cash flow.** Net cash from operations Rs 5,131.62 lakh to Rs 2,755.29
lakh, minus 46.3 per cent (AR2026 p.71). Capex payments Rs 1,839.18 lakh, so
free cash flow is about Rs 917 lakh against Rs 2,531.03 lakh of dividend
actually paid (p.72) — 2.76 times. The gap was bridged by Rs 4,522.62 lakh of
mutual-fund sales (p.71); current investments fell Rs 4,395.86 lakh to Rs
2,322.59 lakh (p.68). Cash conversion: CFO to EBITDA 92.5 per cent in FY25,
48.6 per cent in FY26; CFO to PAT 1.79 times to 0.96 times. None of this
appears in the MD's Message, the Board's Report, or the MD&A, which says only
that the company "remains Net Debt Free" (p.29). **The clean cash-flow statement
is on AR2026 p.71-72**; the OCR problem is confined to the results filing.

**15. No geographic revenue split and no major-customer disclosure.** Note 38:
"the company has only one operating segment ... The principal geographical area
in which the company operates is India" (AR2026 p.126), while FOB exports are
12.7 per cent of revenue and the MD&A devotes four paragraphs to export markets.
Note 21 disaggregates revenue only into finished goods, traded goods, services,
export incentives and scrap (p.101). Both peers do better: Wendt gives revenue
**and EBIT margin by vertical** — super abrasives 22 per cent, machine tools
about 20 per cent, precision products about 12 per cent (WENDT p.9); Kennametal
reports segment results and discusses hard metals separately from machines.

**16. The promoter's entire stake has been pledged for the whole corpus
window.** SP and Company Private Limited: 35,967,172 shares, 100 per cent of its
own holding, 69.71 per cent of the company, pledged at 30-Jun-2025, at
31-Mar-2026 and at 30-Jun-2026 (declaration item 7 and Table II in all three
shareholding files). Forbes Campbell Finance's 2,135,592 shares carry no
pledge. This is a **standing** condition, not a FY26 event. It is not mentioned
anywhere in the FY26 annual report.

**17. Credit rating IVR BBB/Stable, assigned 5-Dec-2024, unreviewed for about
seventeen months.** AR2026 p.54 confirms the assignment date and says the
ratings "continue to remain valid as on the date of this report". BBB is the
lowest investment grade.

**18. Net loss on foreign-currency transactions and translations rose 498 per
cent**, Rs 20.71 lakh to Rs 123.87 lakh (AR2026 note 27, p.104), while the
Corporate Governance Report states the company "has managed foreign exchange
risk and hedged in compliance with its extant foreign exchange risk management
policy" (p.54).

**19. Minority dissent on the only pay resolution minorities could vote on.**
Postal ballot on payment of commission to non-executive directors: promoters
voted 38,102,764 shares 100 per cent in favour; public non-institutional
holders cast 17.46 per cent of their polled votes against (AR2026 p.50). The
commission that resolution enabled is the Rs 125 lakh of non-executive
commission inside the Rs 143.50 lakh directors' fee line.

### A3. Estimates restated and guidance walked back

**20. The Labour Codes charge was walked from "cannot be quantified" to Rs 387
lakh to Rs 590 lakh in six months, with identical boilerplate each time and no
explanation.** H1 FY26 (30-Oct-2025), note 5: "The effective date of the code is
yet to be notified and the rules for quantifying the financial impact are also
yet to be issued. In view of this, the impact of change, if any, will be
assessed and recognized post notification." Q3 FY26 (22-Jan-2026), note 5:
"resulting in an incremental impact of Rs 387 lakhs (comprising gratuity and
compensated absences) which has been recognized as an employee benefit expense
in the current reporting period." FY26 audited results and AR2026 note 39
(p.126): the same sentence, word for word, with **Rs 590 lakhs**. A 52.5 per
cent upward revision inside one quarter on a charge equal to 14.9 per cent of
PBT. The quarterly arithmetic supports it: Q4 employee cost Rs 1,277 lakh
against Rs 1,032 lakh in Q4 FY25, a roughly Rs 203 lakh top-up. Nothing anywhere
explains the revision. (The Q3 figure reads `={387` in the OCR; the surrounding
sentence and the Q4 arithmetic both confirm 387.)

**21. The capex payoff claim was quietly demoted from returns to quality, and
the dependency claim it carried is falsified.** AR2025 p.19: "Our capital
investment strategies yielded substantial **returns** and **reduced our
dependencies on third parties supplies**, particularly evident in the doubling
of our High-Speed Drills portfolio in the last fiscal year." AR2026 p.26, the
same sentence opening: "Our capital investment strategies yielded substantial
**benefits on the quality front**, which results in better product quality. **We
expect** the market to respond better to this quality and **reward us with
larger volumes in the near future**." Financial returns became quality, and the
volume payoff moved to the future. The dependency half is directly falsified in
the same report: processing charges paid to third parties rose from Rs 1,365.46
lakh to Rs 1,703.94 lakh, plus 24.8 per cent, against revenue plus 7.9 per cent
(AR2026 note 27, p.104). Outsourcing grew three times faster than sales.

**22. The pass-through success claim was excised, not softened.** AR2024 p.16:
"In response to inflated input costs, we implemented price increases across our
portfolio, which we were able to successfully pass on to our customers." The
sentences immediately before and after it ("Successful demonstrations of product
superiority enabled us to secure long term relationships with focus key
accounts. Strong measures in supply chain management and volume-based
negotiations further contributed to the improved profitability.") survive
word-for-word into AR2026 p.26. The pass-through sentence is deleted from that
paragraph in both FY25 and FY26. A specific success claim was surgically removed
from otherwise unchanged boilerplate.

**23. A forward promise was transplanted from one product line to another.**
AR2025 p.19, on HSS Drills: "We are confident, this portfolio will grow
substantially over time. Consequent to the above, we have made substantial
capacity investments in this segment and we expect high rate of growth in this
segment." AR2026 p.26, on Taps: "We are confident, this portfolio will further
grow substantially over time. Consequent to the above, we have made substantial
investments in this segment and we expect high rate of growth in this segment."
The same promise sentence, moved to a different product, with no closing
statement on the first.

**24. Consumer-goods boilerplate in an industrial risk section, unchanged across
years.** The "Market Development" risk paragraph speaks of "consumer insights",
"category and brand strategies", and translating "trends in consumer preference
and taste" (AR2025 p.21, AR2026 p.27, verbatim). TOTEM sells round cutting tools
to machine shops. The only word that changed between the two years is "Electric
initiatives" to "EV initiatives".

**25. "Enhancing market share domestically" claimed three years running with no
number, no competitor named, and no evidence** (AR2024 p.16, AR2025 p.19,
AR2026 p.26). Kennametal, asked the same question directly, declines to answer
it: "That's a question I can't answer for sure. And this is a question we have
not answered for years now" (KENNAMET Mar-2024). TOTEM asserts the answer
without being asked.

**26. Persistent EPS presentation defect, three years running.** The Board's
Report results table reports the identical figure on two lines, "Earnings Per
Share ... (Continuing operation)" and "... (Discontinued operations)": Rs 110.63
and Rs 110.63 (AR2024 p.14), Rs 5.57 and Rs 5.57 (AR2025 p.18), Rs 5.58 and Rs
5.58 (AR2026 p.24). The company has no discontinued operations. Three years,
uncorrected.

### A4. The Q1 FY27 quarter

**27. The Q1 FY27 margin improvement is a soft-base artifact, and sequentially
the margin fell.** PBT margin by quarter: Q1 FY26 9.7 per cent (508 on 5,241),
Q2 17.5 per cent (1,105 on 6,327), Q3 12.5 per cent (804 on 6,437, carrying the
Rs 387 lakh charge), Q4 21.6 per cent (1,533 on 7,096), Q1 FY27 17.6 per cent
(1,190 on 6,755). Q1 FY26 was the weakest quarter of FY26 by a wide margin, and
Q1 FY27's PBT margin is **four percentage points below Q4 FY26's**. On a
consistent EBITDA-excluding-other-income basis the FY26 full-year figure is 21.1
per cent against Q1 FY27's 22.9 per cent — a 1.8-point gain, not the 6.8-point
gain the Q1-on-Q1 comparison implies. (Sources: Q1 FY27 filing rendered p.3;
Q3 FY26 filing p.4; H1 FY26 filing p.4.)

**28. Q1 FY27's cost mix moved the wrong way on materials and inexplicably on
other expenses.** Net material cost (cost of materials consumed plus the
inventory change) was Rs 2,440 lakh on Rs 6,755 lakh of revenue, 36.1 per cent,
against 32.2 per cent for FY26 as a whole and 31.8 per cent in Q1 FY26 — a
430-basis-point deterioration. Other expenses **fell** in absolute terms, Rs
1,603 lakh to Rs 1,522 lakh, minus 5 per cent, on revenue up 28.9 per cent. That
single line is the arithmetic driver of the year-on-year margin gain, and the
only note that touches it is boilerplate note 4, "Figures for the previous
periods are re-classified/re-arranged/regrouped, wherever necessary" (Q1 FY27
rendered p.4). No explanation, no quantification of the regrouping.

**29. The Q1 FY27 filing carries no Labour Codes note and no closure
statement** (rendered p.4, notes 1-4 only), despite the FY26 note undertaking to
"account for any further impact ... in the period in which such developments
occur".

**30. A corrigendum was needed to correct the date on the Q1 FY27 board-meeting
intimation** — "the date 'April 12, 2026' was mentioned ... due to an
inadvertent typographical error" (BM Outcome 12-Aug-2026). Second corrigendum in
fourteen months.

### A5. People and governance

separately announced.** The 1-Aug-2025 Regulation 30 filing designating Mr

**33. Mr P. Shetty, appointed SVP on 1-Aug-2025 with stated "turnaround"

**34. Directors' sitting fees and commission rose 676 per cent**, Rs 18.50 lakh

per cent and the CSR obligation went entirely unspent. Unexplained anywhere in

**35. The FY26 CSR obligation of Rs 53.83 lakh was 100 per cent unspent**, the
second consecutive year of a full shortfall, while the Board's Report states

(AR2026 p.30) — which was the FY25 carry-forward, not the FY26 duty (Annexure I
p.35-36; note 27 Note 1, p.105).

**36. The maiden dividend paid out 89.7 per cent of PAT** (Rs 2,579.72 lakh
declared against PAT of Rs 2,877.30 lakh; AR2026 p.73 and p.70), against a
capex programme the MD&A describes as ongoing.

**37. The FY26 inventory build is unexplained anywhere in the corpus, and its
composition points at raw material, not finished goods.** Raw materials Rs
738.71 lakh to Rs 1,993.71 lakh, plus 170 per cent; WIP plus 84 per cent;
finished goods plus 43 per cent; stores and spares minus 27 per cent (AR2026
note 8, p.92). Raw-material days of consumption roughly doubled, 36 to 78. At
30-Sep-2025 inventory was still only Rs 3,776 lakh (H1 FY26 filing p.6), so
about Rs 1,866 lakh of the build landed in H2.

**38. The FY26 "abnormal commodity prices" claim is unevidenced.** "commodity
prices (namely Solid Carbide, Steel and other related commodities) increased
abnormally in very high proportion in the Financial Year in consideration"
(AR2026 p.25). No price index, no supplier quote, no percentage is given
anywhere in three annual reports, and the ratio table that would carry a margin
consequence is incomplete (item 12 above).

### A6. Peer-side items

**39. Wendt's exports GREW 19 per cent year on year in Q1 FY26 (Apr-Jun 2025)**,
Rs 10.78 crore against the prior year (WENDT p.6) — a peer export datapoint
squarely inside TOTEM's FY26 window, moving in the opposite direction to
TOTEM's minus 15.9 per cent, in the exact quarter TOTEM's US and Mexico tariff
attribution would first bite. Different products and different geographies, so
it is directional and not conclusive, but it is the only in-window peer export
evidence in the corpus.

**40. Kennametal states the exact accounting point behind the withdrawn
pass-through finding.** Suresh Reddy, asked whether Kennametal had failed to
pass on a 28 per cent raw-material rise: "No, we have recovered the price
increase so, but you must understand as a percentage the raw material would
still look as a percentage higher because in the top line it would be only to
compensate the raw material increase that would have gained" (KENNAMET
Mar-2023). This is the peer authority for the proposition that a rising
raw-material percentage does not prove a failed pass-through.

**41. Tungsten input cost cannot be a source of cost advantage.**
Vijaykrishnan Venkatesan: "it's so transparent, right ... All are customers
also track the prices, so it's a very transparent system, right? It's not that
one will edge out the other because everybody buys at the same price"
(KENNAMET Mar-2024). Suresh Reddy in the same exchange confirms the symmetry —
when tungsten falls, "customers may come up and ask for additional discounts ...
hence we may also have to offer discounts."

**42. Kennametal qualifies the aerospace story TOTEM has run for three years.**
"aerospace continues to grow very fast as a sector while base is very, very,
very small in the country" (KENNAMET Mar-2024). Also: about 50 per cent of
Kennametal's inputs including all tungsten are imported — "It's all imported"
(KENNAMET Jun-2023).

**43. Kennametal explained its own inventory swings, repeatedly and causally,
across three separate calls.** "we had built up some inventory to just ensure
that, during the transition into the new facility, there is no impact to the
customer"; "we had been building inventory for almost six months before the
transition happened. So even the reduction also will happen in a phased manner"
(KENNAMET May-2023); "we had a large capex cycle inventory which was again
communicated ... four quarters back" (Mar-2024). This is the disclosure standard
against which TOTEM's silent 77 per cent build should be read.

**44. Wendt spent its full CSR obligation with nothing unspent** — "I'm pleased
to share that there were no unspent amounts at the end of the year" (WENDT p.6)
— against TOTEM's two consecutive 100 per cent shortfalls.

**45. Both peers restrict investor engagement, which is genuine mitigating
context.** Wendt's Chairman refuses a quarterly call on the record: "we are not
considered any kind of a quarterly investor meet or other things at this point
of time" (WENDT p.22). Kennametal opens every meeting with "We will not be
making any forward-looking statements on this call, and we would be restricting
our talk to publicly available information only" (KENNAMET Mar-2024), and
declines a three-to-five-year target outright: "I can't do that Amber."

**46. But both peers still submit to a Q&A record, and TOTEM does not.** Wendt's
AGM produced a shareholder questioning DSO moving 80 to 101 days, a shareholder
challenging a 420 per cent capex jump on ROE grounds, and a shareholder asking
about attrition and tariffs. TOTEM has no forum in which any of items 1 to 38
above could be put to management. The FY26 AGM notice does invite written
questions by 6-Aug-2026 (AR2026 p.13), but no answers are published.

**47. Wendt's CEO departure was named, dated and explained at the AGM** — Ninad
Gadgil "will be stepping down from the Board effective 15th September 2025 to
pursue opportunities outside the Company", with the Chairman confirming a search
was under way (WENDT p.7 and p.22). TOTEM's HR-head exits carry no reason at
all.

## PART C — PIPELINE CLAIMS I DID NOT FIND, OR FOUND UNSUPPORTED

**NS-1. Stage 5, the pledge-and-dividend chain, "Reading A ... the more likely
reading". NOT SUPPORTED — and the corpus resolves it the other way.**
Stage 5 builds an unresolved two-reading construct on whether the 1,470,000-share
transfer to Forbes Campbell Finance preceded the 2-May-2025 record date, prefers
Reading A, and computes Rs 1,871.86 lakh to SP and Co. The audited related-party
note on the page stage 5 itself cites settles it: dividend paid, Shapoorji
Pallonji and Company Private Limited **Rs 1,798.36 lakh**, Forbes Campbell
Finance **Rs 106.50 lakh**, total Rs 1,904.86 lakh (AR2026 note 30, p.108). Rs
106.50 lakh at Rs 5 per share is 2,130,000 shares. Forbes Campbell held 665,592
shares at 31-Mar-2025 (AR2026 note 11, p.95). It could only have collected a
dividend on about 2.13 million shares if the transfer completed **before** the
2-May-2025 record date. Reading B is confirmed; Reading A is excluded. Stage 5
quotes the Rs 1,798.36 lakh figure without recognising that it is the answer,
and the reason it offers for preferring Reading A — that AR2025 still shows the
pre-transfer holding — is a non-sequitur, because AR2025 reports the position at
31-Mar-2025 and is silent about April 2025 by construction. **MAJOR.** The chain
survives; the arithmetic and the preferred reading do not.

**NS-2. Stage 5, the Wendt "commodity prices stayed largely stable" citation.
NOT SUPPORTED.** Stage 5 calls it "the nearest in-window peer datapoint" against
TOTEM's FY26 abnormal-commodity claim, and admits "I did not re-read the Wendt
transcript myself". I read it. The sentence sits in the Chairman's opening
scene-setter: "During 2024, the global economy grew at an uneven pace across
regions ... While inflation eased in most countries, prices in the service
sector remained elevated. Commodity prices stayed largely stable, though risks
of renewed price increase persist" (WENDT p.3). It describes **calendar 2024**,
which is TOTEM's FY25, not TOTEM's FY26 (April 2025 to March 2026). It is a
global macro remark, not a statement about carbide, tungsten or steel. Wrong
period, wrong subject, unread by the citing stage, and used to support a HIGH
red flag. **MAJOR.**

**NS-3. Stage 5 and Stage 6, the causal attribution of Q1 FY27 margin to the
drills capex. NOT SUPPORTED.** Stage 6: "TOTEM's own Q1 FY27 margin jump (16.1%
to 22.9%) follows directly from its own capex delivery (Drills CNC capacity,
**confirmed** in the FY26 AR)". Stage 5: "capacity investment genuinely
delivered into Q1 FY27 growth" (4C) and trigger 1 "already showing in Q1 FY27".
Nothing in the corpus links the two. The Q1 FY27 filing has four boilerplate
notes, no commentary, no segment data, no capacity or utilisation figure
(rendered p.3-4). Three untested alternatives sit in the same numbers: Q1 FY26
was the weakest quarter of FY26 on every measure (item 27); headcount fell 61
during FY26 (item 10); and other expenses fell in absolute terms on revenue up
29 per cent with only a regrouping note against it (item 28). "Confirmed" is not
earned, and stage 6's Part 5 hypothesis rests on it. **MAJOR.**
(The 16.1 to 22.9 pair is internally consistent as Q1 FY26 against Q1 FY27 on an
EBITDA-excluding-other-income basis. Neither the basis nor the two periods is
stated, which is a separate presentational defect.)

**NS-4. Stage 5, "the auditor is right ... that sentence does not survive the
three-year series." OVERSTATED.** The CIF series is real and I rebuilt it
