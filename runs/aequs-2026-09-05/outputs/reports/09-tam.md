Run date: 2026-09-05. Figures in Rs Crore unless marked $. FX used is named
at each figure: the FY26 Annual Report's own implicit rate (Rs 84.02/$,
back-solved from the AR's own Rs257.09 Bn = $3.06 Bn conversion, AR p.39) for

## SECTION 1: MARKET DEFINITION

### 1A Precise boundaries

**Primary market — Commercial-aerospace precision components (85% of FY26
revenue, Rs 1,046.4 Cr).**
- Product scope: precision-machined and forged components, assemblies and
  special-process services (surface treatment, forging) across seven
  categories Aequs itself reports — engine systems, landing systems,
  actuation systems, structures, turnings, interiors & cargo, assemblies,
  plus doors (AR p.19, 5,654 SKUs).
- Aircraft scope: commercial single-aisle (A320, A220, B737) and long-range
  (A330, A350, B767, B777, B787) platforms only (AR p.18). Excludes
  business jets, helicopters, spacecraft and defence platforms.
- Geographic scope: manufacturing performed in India (Belagavi SEZ, Hosur
  under construction), France (Cholet) and the US (Paris, Texas), sold into
  the global OEM/Tier-1 supply chain (AR p.15-16).
- Customer scope: direct long-term build-to-print/build-to-spec contracts
  with OEMs and Tier-1s (Airbus, Boeing, Safran, Collins, Honeywell, GKN,
  Eaton, Bombardier, SAAB, Dynamatic, Triumph, Mubea — AR p.18). Excludes
  MRO/spares aftermarket (Aequs is OEM-programme supply, not MRO) and
  defence-platform manufacturing (HAL-type programmes — Aequs's new Ajna
  JV enters this separately, treated as optionality in 5D, not core TAM).
- Channel scope: direct program contracts, not distribution/retail.

**Secondary market — Consumer contract manufacturing (15% of FY26 revenue,
Rs 184.0 Cr).**
- Product scope: toys (outdoor toys, toy vehicles, dolls, sets, STEM toys
  — Mattel, Spin Master; Hasbro discontinued FY26), consumer-electronics
  precision mechanical enclosures (portable computers, smart wearables —
  unnamed "largest global technology brand", AR p.17), consumer durables/
  cookware (Tramontina JV) (AR p.17).
- Scope: India-manufactured, B2B2C contract manufacturing for GLOBAL brand
  owners' own distribution — explicitly EXCLUDES Aequs's own retail sales
  (none exist) and India's domestic-retail consumer markets, which are
  served overwhelmingly by other manufacturers Aequs has no relationship
  with.

## SECTION 2: TAM ESTIMATION, MULTIPLE METHODS

### AEROSPACE (primary; full multi-method treatment)

**Method 1 — Top-down, India commercial-aerospace-component manufacturing
market.**
AR-cited (company-commissioned) 2030 target: Rs 257.09 Bn = $3.06 Bn (AR
p.39). Itemised breakdown (Rs Cr, converted at the AR's own Rs84.02/$):
Aerostructures 10,458; Landing systems 3,984; Actuation 2,241; Turnings
2,117; Engine 1,245; Interiors 996; Doors 747. Sum of itemised categories =
Rs 21,788 Cr (85% of the stated Rs 25,709 Cr total); Rs 3,921 Cr unassigned
(other/unnamed categories).
This is a 2030 target, not a current-year figure, and no base year is
disclosed. Back-solving to a current (FY26/27) figure needs a CAGR: the
closest independently-sourced CAGR for this exact scope is 6.8% (Grand View
Research, "India aerospace parts manufacturing market", 6.8% CAGR 2024-2030,
and IMARC's implied 6.8% CAGR between its 2025 ($17.3Bn) and 2034 ($31.3Bn)
figures for "India aircraft components market" — see cross-check below).
Using 7% (rounded) over 4.5 years (mid-2026 to 2030):
Current TAM = Rs 25,709 Cr ÷ (1.07)^4.5 = Rs 25,709 ÷ 1.352 = **Rs 19,020
Cr**.
Cross-check (third party, likely BROADER scope): IMARC "India aircraft
components market" $17.3 Bn (2025) → $31.3 Bn (2034); Grand View Research
"India aerospace parts manufacturing market" $21.48 Bn by 2030 (6.8% CAGR,
2024-2030, implying a 2024 base of ~$14.48 Bn). These are **7-10x LARGER**
than the AR's own $3.06 Bn 2030 figure for an apparently similar label.
Most likely explanation: the third-party figures include defence-platform
manufacturing (HAL and India's indigenous defence-aerospace base), MRO/
spares, and possibly raw-material/tooling value — a materially broader
scope than Aequs's build-to-print commercial-component niche, which the
AR's own itemised category list (matching Aequs's actual SKU mix) does not
include. Per the stage's conservative-bias rule, methods that diverge
materially are flagged, not averaged: **the AR/company figure is used as
Method 1's number precisely because it is both the lower and the more
narrowly-matched-to-scope estimate**, with the third-party range carried
only as upside context (Section 4A/5D).

**Method 2 — Bottom-up, global aerostructures market x India's stated
share.**
Global aerostructures market, lowest of three third-party 2025 estimates:
$66.53 Bn (Research and Markets, CY2025; vs $75.2Bn Allied Market Research
and $100.5Bn Grand View Research — conservative bias takes the lowest).
Applying management's own (peer-unverified, B06) claim that India is "about
5% of the global market": 5% × $66.53 Bn = $3.33 Bn = Rs 279.4 Bn (at
Rs84.02/$) = **Rs 27,943 Cr** — a CURRENT-year figure (built off current
global data and management's stated CURRENT share).
This is HIGHER than Method 1's both current (Rs19,020cr) and 2030-target
(Rs25,709cr) figures. That is a flag in itself: applying management's own
qualitative share claim to independent data yields a bigger number than
management's own quantified forecast — most likely because the "5%" claim's
denominator ("the global market") is vaguer and probably broader than
"aerostructures" alone, or because the claim is rounded up. Given B06
explicitly could not verify this claim against peer disclosure, Method 2 is
carried as a LOW-confidence directional cross-check, not the anchor.

**Method 3 — Peer revenue aggregation.**
Known listed India commercial-aerospace-component peers, aerospace-segment
FY26 revenue:
- Aequs (aerospace segment): Rs 1,046.4 Cr (AR p.11, Note 36)
- AZAD Engineering (consolidated, predominantly aerospace/energy precision
  forged & machined components): Rs 602.98 Cr (FY26, +31.8% YoY; company
  results release, 2026)
- Unimech Aerospace: Rs 240.5 Cr (FY26, -1% YoY; company results, 2026)
- Dynamatic Technologies, aerospace segment only: Rs 775.61 Cr (FY26,
  +27.6% YoY, 48% of Rs1,621.34cr consolidated total; company results/
  presentation, 2026)
Known-listed sum = **Rs 2,665.5 Cr**.
Unlisted/private capacity not captured here is material: Tata Advanced
Systems, Bharat Forge Aerospace, Godrej Aerospace, Maini Precision, Rossell
Techsys, and captive India units of Safran/Collins/Honeywell/GE Aerospace
are all sizeable India aerospace-component manufacturers with no separate
public revenue disclosure. Applying a judgment-call multiplier (ANALYST
ESTIMATE, not sourced) that known-listed players represent roughly 15-20%
of the true organised-plus-captive India market:
Rs 2,665.5 Cr ÷ 0.175 (midpoint) = **Rs 15,231 Cr**.

**Triangulation table (Aerospace, current-year):**

| Method | Estimate (Rs Cr) | Confidence | Staleness |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Top-down (AR/company, back-solved to current) | 19,020 | M (itemised, but self-commissioned + back-solve assumption) | Current, <1yr |
| 2. Bottom-up (global aerostructures x India share claim) | 27,943 | L (input claim UNVERIFIABLE per B06) | Current |
| 3. Peer aggregation + judgment multiplier | 15,231 | L-M (peer sub-total H-confidence; multiplier is judgment) | Current |

Conservative estimate (Aerospace) = **Rs 15,231 Cr** (Method 3, lowest).
Realistic estimate (Aerospace) = **Rs 19,020 Cr** (Method 1, best scope
match).

### CONSUMER (secondary; lighter but still multi-sourced treatment)

Consumer-segment India-specific market data is genuinely thin — this is a
named data-quality gap, not an invented number.

**Method 1 — Top-down, India toy-export trade data (toys sub-piece only).**
India's toy exports for FY 2025-26 are reported inconsistently across two
government-linked sources: $186 Mn (Commerce Ministry, via Sitharaman
office statement, Jul-2026) vs $384.7 Mn (India Briefing, citing the same
FY, Jul-2026) — a >2x divergence in the SAME government data lineage,
flagged. Conservative bias takes the lower: $186 Mn = Rs 1,757.7 Cr (at
current FX Rs94.4/$: 186 × 94.4 ÷ 10 = 1,755.8, ≈ **Rs 1,758 Cr**). This is
current INDIA-WIDE toy-export value (all manufacturers, not just Aequs) —
the appropriate current TAM proxy for Aequs's toy-manufacturing-for-export
niche.

**Method 5 — Global benchmark cross-check (toys).**
Global toy retail market $117.83 Bn (CY25, AR p.41); toy-industry
manufacturing/COGS value typically ~30% of retail ≈ $35.35 Bn globally. If
India captures 3-5% of global toy-manufacturing value within a decade
(mirroring the "China+1" trajectory seen in electronics) = $1.06-1.77 Bn =
Rs 1,001-1,672 Cr (at Rs94.4/$) — consistent order of magnitude with Method
1's Rs 1,758 Cr, cross-validating the toys estimate.

Cookware and Consumer-Electronics enclosures: **NOT FOUND** at the specific
scope needed (an "India contract-manufacturing wallet for cookware/CE
enclosures serving global brands"). Only broad end-market retail sizes
exist (India cookware $1.87-6.55Bn domestic consumption, wrong scope;
global EMS too broad), and none isolate the mechanical-enclosure or
export-cookware slice Aequs actually competes in. These are named upside,
NOT quantified into the headline Consumer TAM.

**Consumer TAM: conservative = Rs 1,000 Cr, realistic = Rs 1,900 Cr**
(toys-only, both methods; cookware/CE flagged as unquantified additional
upside, not included).

### BLENDED TAM (headline)

| | Conservative | Realistic |
|---|---|---|
| Aerospace | 15,231 | 19,020 |
| Consumer | 1,000 | 1,900 |
| **Blended TAM** | **16,231** | **20,920** |

**mgmt_claim vs conservative:** Management's only explicit, sourced,
quantified market-size claim is the AR's Rs 25,709 Cr (2030, aerospace-only).
Comparing like-for-like (2030 vs 2030): grow the blended conservative
estimate (Rs16,231cr) forward at 7%/yr for 4.5 years = Rs16,231 × 1.352 =
Rs 21,944 Cr. Ratio = 25,709 ÷ 21,944 = **1.17x** → REASONABLE (within
1.5x). Naively comparing the 2030 claim against today's conservative
estimate without time-adjustment gives 25,709 ÷ 16,231 = 1.58x, which would
misleadingly look elevated — this is a timing artefact, not inflation, and
the 1.17x like-for-like reading is used as the reported ratio.

## SECTION 3: SAM & SOM

### 3A SAM

**Aerospace SAM** (five filters applied to the realistic TAM, Rs 19,020 Cr,
because its itemised categories map onto Aequs's own SKU mix):
- Product fit: 100% — Aequs's seven reported SKU categories match the
  itemised TAM breakdown almost exactly (AR p.19 vs AR p.39).
- Geography: 90% — Aequs manufactures in India, France and the US, which
  covers most but not all of the India-sourced-or-adjacent supply chain
  (ANALYST JUDGMENT; minor haircut for programmes with local-content rules
  Aequs's non-India sites cannot satisfy).
- Channel: 100% — direct OEM/Tier-1 contracts already match the TAM's
  channel scope (MRO already excluded upstream).
- Customer: 85% — Aequs's current OEM/Tier-1 roster (Airbus, Boeing,
  Safran, Collins, Honeywell, GKN, Eaton, Bombardier, SAAB) is broad but not
  exhaustive (excludes e.g. Embraer, Dassault, and some Tier-1s) (ANALYST
  JUDGMENT).
- Capability: 75% — Engine and Landing Systems categories (Rs1,245cr +
  Rs3,984cr = Rs5,229cr, ~24% of the itemised total) are explicitly areas
  Aequs is still "deepening participation... extending beyond
  aerostructures into more complex applications" (MD&A message, AR p.8-9),
  i.e. capability still building, not yet fully mature (ANALYST JUDGMENT).

Combined = 1.00 × 0.90 × 1.00 × 0.85 × 0.75 = 0.574.
Aerospace SAM = Rs 19,020 Cr × 0.574 = **Rs 10,913 Cr**.

**Consumer SAM:** Aequs serves 2 of many global toy brands (Mattel, Spin
Master) with a fairly broad in-category product fit; applying a combined,
blunter judgment-call filter of ~40% (reflecting large theoretical TAM but
narrow realistic accessibility given the small number of qualified brand
relationships Aequs actually holds) (ANALYST JUDGMENT):
Consumer SAM = Rs 1,900 Cr × 0.40 = **Rs 760 Cr**.

**Blended SAM = Rs 10,913 Cr + Rs 760 Cr = Rs 11,673 Cr** (55.8% of the
realistic blended TAM of Rs20,920cr).

### 3B SOM at 3 and 5 years

**Aerospace.** Current share of SAM = Rs1,046.4cr ÷ Rs10,913cr = **9.59%**.
Aequs has (a) an order book crossing $1Bn with a new 15-year Safran wheel
programme, (b) FY26 aerospace revenue growth of 27% and ROCE improving from
14.3% (FY25) to 18.5% (9M FY26), and (c) Rs1,900cr (Hosur, 10yr) plus
Rs2,856cr (Karnataka, 5yr) of committed capacity expansion — this combination
of demonstrated execution and funded capacity justifies the AGGRESSIVE
share-gain band (3-5pp in 3 years), not the normal band.
- Yr3: +4pp → share 13.6%. SAM grows with TAM at ~7%/yr: SAM_yr3 = Rs10,913
  × (1.07)^3 = Rs 13,368 Cr. SOM_3yr = 13.6% × 13,368 = **Rs 1,818 Cr**.
- Yr5: gain decelerates to +1.5pp more in years 4-5 (a normal-band pace,
  since a second consecutive aggressive 3yr window would breach the >5pp
  cap without a competitor exit/acquisition, which is not in evidence) →
  cumulative +5.5pp, share 15.1%. SAM_yr5 = Rs10,913 × (1.07)^5 = Rs15,311
  Cr. SOM_5yr = 15.1% × 15,311 = **Rs 2,312 Cr**.

Implied revenue CAGR (Aerospace, from Rs1,046.4cr current):
- 3yr: (1,818/1,046.4)^(1/3) - 1 = (1.738)^(0.333) - 1 ≈ **20.2%**
- 5yr: (2,312/1,046.4)^(1/5) - 1 = (2.210)^(0.2) - 1 ≈ **16.9%**
This lands almost exactly on management's own qualitative framing on the
Q3 FY26 call ("opportunity to grow north of 20% in our aerospace business"
— Aravind Melligeri), and sits BELOW the formal FY27 guidance of aerospace
+25-30% — a defensible, conservative-but-not-implausible SOM.

**Consumer.** The toys-market-share approach is unreliable here: Aequs's
disclosed Rs184cr Consumer revenue spans toys + CE + cookware combined,
while the Rs760cr SAM built above is toys-only — dividing one by the other
would overstate share. Given this scope mismatch and the data gaps named
in Section 2, Consumer SOM instead uses the CAPACITY-conversion cross-check
from Stage 7 (B07 2C): the FY27 Rs500cr Consumer-earmarked capex slice, at
management's own disclosed 1.5x consumer asset-turn target, implies Rs750cr
of incremental Consumer revenue potential once fully utilised — i.e.
Consumer could reach roughly 4-5x its current Rs184cr size "once this single
capex layer is fully utilised (a multi-year outcome)" (B07 finding, itself
sourced to the Q4 FY26/Q1 FY27 calls).
- Yr3 (partial utilisation of this capex layer, consistent with
  management's own FY27 guide of +125-150% Consumer revenue growth,
  extended one more year): Consumer SOM_3yr ≈ **Rs 400 Cr** (~2.2x FY26).
- Yr5 (this capex layer substantially utilised): Consumer SOM_5yr ≈
  **Rs 800 Cr** (~4.3x FY26, within the B07 4-5x range).

**Blended SOM_3yr = Rs 1,818 Cr + Rs 400 Cr = Rs 2,218 Cr.**
**Blended SOM_5yr = Rs 2,312 Cr + Rs 800 Cr = Rs 3,112 Cr.**

Blended implied revenue CAGR (from Rs1,230.44cr current, FORMAL handoff to
Stage 11):
- Yr3: (2,218/1,230.44)^(1/3) - 1 = (1.803)^(0.333) - 1 ≈ **21.7%**
- Yr5: (3,112/1,230.44)^(1/5) - 1 = (2.529)^(0.2) - 1 ≈ **20.4%**

This is materially BELOW management's Vision 2031 revenue target (4-6x FY26
revenue by FY31 = 32.0-43.1% CAGR over 5 years). A 12-23pp gap between a
disciplined share-gain SOM and management's own long-range target is a
genuine, flagged divergence, not an error — see Section 5E.

### 3C Capacity cross-check

Using B07's capex-embedded-growth figure (63%, blended company-wide,
FY26-turnover basis; aerospace-only/consumer-only fixed-asset-turnover NOT
FOUND per B07):

**Aerospace.** Q1 FY27 aerospace revenue Rs322.2cr annualised = Rs1,288.8cr,
at 70% blended (India+France+US) capacity utilisation (Inv. Pres. slide 8).
Utilisation catch-up alone (70%→100%, illustrative ceiling): Rs1,288.8cr ÷
0.70 = Rs1,841.1cr — already close to SOM_3yr (Rs1,818cr) from EXISTING
capacity alone, before any new capex. At a more realistic ~90% practical
ceiling: Rs1,288.8 ÷ 0.70 × 0.90 = Rs1,657cr, a Rs161cr shortfall against
SOM_3yr — comfortably inside the disclosed Rs1,900cr (Hosur) and Rs2,856cr
(Karnataka) capex programmes. **Capacity check: sufficient** for both
SOM_3yr and SOM_5yr.

**Consumer.** SOM_3yr (Rs400cr) and SOM_5yr (Rs800cr) sit inside the B07-
derived "4-5x current size" ceiling (Rs736-920cr) from the single disclosed
FY27 Consumer capex layer — **sufficient**, though this is a company-wide
(not consumer-isolated) turnover-based estimate, so precision is limited
(NOT FOUND: segment-specific fixed asset turnover).

**Order-book reconciliation (Load-bearing fact 4).** Aerospace order book =
$1,004 Mn (Q1 FY27, Inv. Pres. slide 8) = Rs9,478 Cr (at current FX
Rs94.4/$). Order-book conversion cadence (book-to-bill, average
programme-to-revenue timeline) is **NOT FOUND** — no such metric is
disclosed. Bracketing two assumptions:
- If the backlog converts over 5 years: Rs9,478 ÷ 5 = Rs1,896cr/yr —
  close to, and broadly consistent with, SOM_3yr (Rs1,818cr) and just
  below SOM_5yr (Rs2,312cr).
- If the backlog converts over 3 years: Rs9,478 ÷ 3 = Rs3,159cr/yr — well
  ABOVE both SOM_3yr and SOM_5yr (+74% and +37% respectively).
The order book has been GROWING faster than it could plausibly be drawn
down (up 13% sequentially in one quarter, Q1FY27), which is more consistent
with a longer (5yr+) effective coverage period, in line with Aequs's own
"15-year average relationship with top-3 customers" framing. On that
reading, this SAM/SOM framework is NOT more optimistic than the order book;
if the true conversion cadence is faster (3yr), the order book — a
demand-side, already-contracted signal — is the more optimistic of the two,
not this TAM/SAM/SOM build.

### 4C Market structure

**Aerospace:** Among known LISTED peers, Aequs's aerospace-segment revenue
(Rs1,046cr) is the largest (AZAD Rs603cr, Dynamatic aerospace Rs776cr,
Unimech Rs241cr), but several large UNLISTED/private players (Tata Advanced
Systems, Bharat Forge Aerospace, Godrej Aerospace, Maini Precision, Rossell
Techsys) and MNC captive India units are not in this count — true top-3
concentration is **NOT FOUND**. The segment is overwhelmingly ORGANISED
(NADCAP/AS9100 certification barriers) — the "unorganised sector" concept
used elsewhere in Indian manufacturing barely applies here. Consolidating:
OEMs are actively "seeking to simplify fragmented supply chains" (AR p.38),
favouring larger vertically-integrated suppliers like Aequs. Competition is
differentiation-led (qualification, single/dual-sourcing), not price-led —
yet Aequs's 20-24% EBITDA margin sits BELOW AZAD/Unimech's 30s% (B06 flag),
suggesting Aequs has not fully captured available pricing power despite
scale.

**Consumer:** Toys — moderate competitive intensity, MNC + regional
players, formalising rapidly under government policy (AR p.42). Consumer
Electronics — dominated by giant EMS players (Dixon Technologies alone
Rs48,893cr FY26 revenue) — Aequs is a small niche enclosure-only entrant.

## SECTION 5: SUMMARY & RUNWAY

### 5A Funnel

```
Blended TAM (realistic): Rs 20,920 Cr  [Aerospace 19,020 + Consumer 1,900]
        ↓ (SAM filters: product/geo/channel/customer/capability)
Blended SAM: Rs 11,673 Cr  (55.8% of TAM)  [Aerospace 10,913 + Consumer 760]
        ↓ (current share 10.5%; aggressive-band share gain)
SOM 3yr: Rs 2,218 Cr   [Aerospace 1,818 + Consumer 400]
SOM 5yr: Rs 3,112 Cr   [Aerospace 2,312 + Consumer 800]
Current revenue: Rs 1,230.4 Cr (FY26)
```

### 5B Runway assessment

- Revenue headroom = SAM ÷ current revenue = 11,673 ÷ 1,230.44 = **9.5x**.
- Blended TAM growth ≈ **7.3%/yr** (weighted: Aerospace ~6.8-7% CAGR per
  IMARC/GVR, ~91% weight; Consumer toys proxy ~12% CAGR, ~9% weight).
- Company CAGR vs TAM: Aequs grew consolidated revenue 33% in FY26
