## NARRATIVE (plain English)

Aarti Surfactants makes surfactant ingredients and sells them to a handful of very large FMCG buyers. The numbers have gone the wrong way for two years: margins more than halved, returns on capital fell to single digits, and a credit-rating agency downgraded the company twice while it borrows to build a new plant. There is one hopeful print — a single quarter of recovered margin and a burst of revenue growth — but the peer group did not see the same tailwind that quarter, and the cash that looked strong last year was a one-time squeeze of inventory and supplier payments, not durable earning power.

The valuation math is decisive and does not depend on any of the soft judgments. The stock changes hands at roughly 24 times trailing earnings. On the four-pillar framework the business earns a 12.2 times forward multiple. Even if earnings grow along the base path, the fall from 24 to 12 swamps the growth, so the three-year return is slightly negative, and the downside if margins stay flat is severe. The Hurdle Ratio says STOP. The right price to look again is roughly Rs 202 to Rs 252, less than half of today's Rs 523.

The action is to wait. Re-open the file only if two things happen together: the price comes down into that Rs 202–252 band, and the next two quarters show operating margin holding at or above 7.5 percent — proof that the single good quarter was a turn and not noise. Until both are true, this is an AVOID.
