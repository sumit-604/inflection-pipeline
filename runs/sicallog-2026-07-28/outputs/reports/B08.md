## SECTION 3: BUSINESS CONDUCT & ETHICAL TRACK RECORD

### 3A. Related-party transaction history (anchored to AR FY24-25 Note 28
and AOC-2, cross-checked against the operator's B02 lead)

- **Equipment lease, Pristine Malwa -> Sical (Rs 67.48 cr / Rs 6,748
  lakh, FY25 addition):** AOC-2 (Annexure to Board's Report, p.32) lists
  this as a **material related-party contract "at arm's length"** —
  leasing of 42 + 17 commercial equipment units over 5-year terms
  starting FY24-25 and FY25-26. **Confirmed finding on the B02 lead:**
  the Rs 6,748 lakh right-of-use addition appears in Note 29 (Leases,
  p.123) **without related-party attribution in that note**, and the
  Note 28 related-party transaction summary table (which itemises
  loans, interest, guarantees, rentals) **does not carry a matching line
  item for this lease**. The transaction is disclosed (AOC-2), but its
  cross-reference within the RPT note itself is incomplete — a
  **disclosure-fragmentation gap**, not proven evidence of a
  non-arm's-length term (AOC-2 explicitly checks "arm's length," and
  "details of contracts... not at arm's length: NIL"). Flagged CAUTION:
  disclosure completeness, not pricing.
- **Kanpur Logistics Park Pvt Ltd (KLPPL, fellow subsidiary) loan, Rs 130
  cr (Rs 13,000 lakh) outstanding at FY25-end, at 12.5% p.a.:** ✅
  VERIFIED (AR Note 28(v), p.121; Note 12.1(j)/CARO, confirming "loan
  from related party represents loan from Holding company & other group
  entities at an interest rate of 12.5%"). Total related-party
  borrowings rose from Rs 4,730 lakh (FY24) to **Rs 18,430 lakh (Rs
  184.3 cr, FY25)** — a **~290% YoY increase in reliance on group-entity
  debt** funding Sical's working capital/growth, alongside bank debt of
  Rs 6,238 lakh. 12.5% is within the plausible band for unsecured
  related-party lending in Indian mid-market logistics (not per se
  evidence of an off-market rate), but the **speed of increase and
  concentration** (holding company + KLPPL + PLIL together = nearly all
  of related-party debt) is a capital-structure dependency worth
  watching. CAUTION.
- **SMART (step-down subsidiary via SIAL) support:** Corporate guarantee
  given by Sical to SMART rose to **Rs 10,000 lakh (Rs 100 cr)
  outstanding** at FY25-end (Rs 2,500 lakh given during the year; ✅
  VERIFIED, CARO clause, AR p.80). Advances to SMART: Rs 843.3 lakh (Rs
  8.43 cr) outstanding at FY25-end, up from Rs 256.7 lakh (FY24). **Note
  a correction to the operator's B02 lead:** the AR records **interest
  income on this inter-corporate deposit to SMART** (Rs 88 lakh FY25,
  interest receivable Rs 93 lakh) — this is an **interest-bearing loan**,
  not the "interest-free advance" characterised in the digest. This is
  a favourable correction (less generous to the subsidiary than
  originally flagged), anchored directly to AR Note 28(iv)/(v), p.119-121.
- **Postal ballot, 9-Mar-2026 (approved 10-Apr-2026), Rs 45 cr material
  RPT:** (i) unconditional corporate guarantee + first pari-passu
  mortgage on SMART's Anuppampattu (Chennai MMLP) land, securing SMART's
  Rs 20 cr CSB Bank facilities; (ii) separately, Sical's own Rs 25 cr
  HDFC Bank facility (Rs 10 cr CC + Rs 15 cr BG) is secured by an
  **exclusive mortgage over PMIPL's (fellow subsidiary) 23.78-acre Bihta,
  Patna land + PMIPL corporate guarantee.** ✅ VERIFIED for the amounts
  and structure (digest cross-checked against AR-consistent related-party
  categories) + 📰 MEDIA REPORTED for the postal-ballot mechanics
  (scanx.trade, whalesbook, Mar-Apr 2026). This is a **cross-collateralised
  web**: Sical's own bank facility is secured by a sister subsidiary's
  land, while Sical in turn guarantees a different subsidiary's debt.
  It was put to a shareholder vote (as SEBI LODR requires for material
  RPTs) rather than executed unilaterally — a **process positive** — but
  the underlying entanglement of balance sheets across the Pristine group
  is a structural RPT concern that should be watched each time SMART or
  PMIPL draws on these facilities. CAUTION.

### 3B. Capital allocation behaviour

- **Rights issue, 11:5, Rs 930.3 mn (~Rs 93 cr), 2026:** executed for
  minimum public shareholding (MPS) compliance; **the promoter forwent
  its own rights entitlement**, which is the mechanism that cut promoter
  holding from ~90% to 73.5% (see shareholding table below) — this is a
  **compliance-driven dilution of the promoter's own stake**, not a
  value-destructive issuance to outside parties, and proceeds were
  directed to debt repayment and working capital (✅ VERIFIED via the
  operator's digest, cross-checked against FY26 results commentary).
  This is a rare instance of a promoter accepting dilution rather than
  imposing it on minorities — a mild positive signal.
- Debt/equity improved **4.1x (FY25) -> 1.6x (FY26)** per the operator's
  digest, aided by the rights issue and non-core asset sales (e.g., sale
  of Madhavaram, Chennai land/building for Rs 18 cr, 📰 MEDIA REPORTED,
  scanx.trade). Directionally disciplined.
- **Countervailing:** Sical remains a **high-leverage, loss-making**
  turnaround story by several independent analytics services — MarketsMojo
  flags a debt-equity ratio in the low-to-mid single digits to high
  double digits depending on the metric base used, negative ROE, and a
  **loss-making Q4 FY26** despite EBITDA growth, attributed to volatile
  "other income" (📰 MEDIA REPORTED, marketsmojo.com, downgraded to
  "Strong Sell" 12-May-2026, further downgrade 13-Jul-2026). This
  reflects operating/financial risk more than promoter ethics, but it
  frames whether the aggressive related-party and bank funding of growth
  (mining logistics capex, MMLP build-out) is being matched by
  cash-generative execution — a fair question for stage 11 valuation and
  for the FTTCP overlay, not resolved here. No promoter salary excess or
  dividend-vs-hoarding issue was found (whole-time director remuneration
  Rs 54.11 lakh FY25, +7.5% YoY — modest for a company this size,
  ✅ VERIFIED, AR Annexure IV).
- No promoter warrants issued/lapsed, no buyback, found in this pass.

### 3C. Promoter share transactions (pledge, sales, encumbrance)

**This is the most material adverse finding in this report.**

- Pledge was **created 16-17 Jun-2025**: Pristine Malwa Logistics Park
  Pvt Ltd (HoldCo) pledged **3,28,43,780 shares = 50.34% of Sical's total
  equity** in favour of Catalyst Trusteeship Ltd (security trustee for
  lenders), plus a non-disposal undertaking covering a total of **51% of
  share capital** (📰 MEDIA REPORTED, TradingView/Moneycontrol,
  Jun-2025).
- By **March 2026, pledge stood at ~56.01%**; by **June 2026, ~56.75%**
  of promoter holding, per independent data aggregators (Trendlyne,
  TipRanks) — a **rising trend, and materially above the 40% pledge
  deal-breaker threshold** specified in this stage's operating rules.
  📰 MEDIA REPORTED (multiple independent aggregators converge on the
  56%+ figure, satisfying the "never rest on a single source" rule).
- Separately (and consistent with the operator-supplied shareholding
  screener), the fall in headline promoter holding from 89.87% (Dec-2025)
  to 73.50% (Mar-2026) is the **MPS-compliance rights-issue dilution**,
  not a promoter sell-down — confirmed by the AR's own account of an
  **earlier, smaller compliance sale** of 24,74,514 shares that took
  promoter holding from 93.79% to 90% in FY24-25 (✅ VERIFIED, AR p.69),
  plus a disclosed sale of 80,000 shares (18-Jun-2025) and a further
  planned sale of 0.15% of paid-up capital (Sep-2025) — all explicitly
  framed by the company as MPS-driven, not conviction-driven.
- **Read together:** the promoter is simultaneously (a) diluting its own
  stake for regulatory compliance (a positive-conduct signal) and (b)
  pledging a rising majority of its remaining stake to lenders (a
  liquidity/leverage risk signal). These are two different mechanisms and
  should not be netted against each other. The pledge is the one that
  matters for the deal-breaker check: **it is now triggered.**
