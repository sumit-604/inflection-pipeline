## Where the market stands

Think of the market as about 2,250 stocks, each with three trend lines under its price: the 20 day, 50 day and 200 day averages. A stock above a line trends up on that clock. On Wednesday 720 stocks sat above the long line, about one in three. 408 sat above the 50 day line, about one in five. Only 319 sat above the 20 day line, about one in seven. The short count fell by about 250 stocks in one session. It has not been this low since 30 March.

The story of the year frames the number. In late March the market hit its low. On 23 March only 291 stocks held the long line. Then came two great buying days. On 1 April more than 1,100 stocks rose four and a half percent in a single session, and on 8 April 880 did. Those are thrust days, broad bursts of buying that start real advances. The long count climbed through spring and summer. It peaked near 1,370 on 7 August, the high of this whole record.

Then the autumn slide began. The count fell through August and early September. On 15 September about 350 stocks fell hard, and a repair followed that peaked at 1,186. A smaller repair peaked at 980 on 6 October. On 8 October about 310 stocks fell hard, and the count hit 838. A third repair reached 939 on 16 October. Monday took it back to 853. Wednesday took it to 720. The long count last sat this low on 13 April. It stands where it stood two weeks into the spring recovery.

So the staircase has changed shape. Until Monday the market had lower peaks on top and a flat floor underneath. Wednesday stepped the floor down by more than a hundred stocks. Lower highs over a lower low is the plain definition of a downtrend in breadth.

Inside the day, sellers owned every hour. About half of all stocks traded four percent below their prior close at some point. About half of those closed near their low. About one stock in eleven traded four percent above its prior close. Only one in seven of those held the gain. That is the weakest close for early risers in this whole record. Buyers who stepped in during the day lost.

The volume changed character, and that matters most tonight. Turnover was about 558 crore, against about 457 crore a year ago and about 514 crore on Monday. The share of stocks trading on heavy volume, against those on light volume, more than doubled from Monday. Monday's brief named heavy volume on a down day as the sign that a markdown had turned into forced selling. Wednesday delivered that sign.

Deep damage has started to spread, though the very deep end is still thin. About one stock in eleven is down ten percent or more over five days, one of the highest shares in this history. Only six stocks are down twenty percent or more over five days, and nine are up that much. New yearly highs fell to 23, the fewest since 7 April. New yearly lows rose to 237, the most since 30 March.

Fear has now crossed the line Monday's brief named. The India VIX measures what traders pay to protect against big index swings. It rose about six percent to about 15.7. It is about a third higher than three months ago. Gold funds did not help either. They are down about seven percent over the month, and the Nifty is down about four. The usual shelter fell harder than the stock index.

## What would change this read

The read is a broken floor on heavy volume, under a staircase of lower peaks. It turns worse if the long count falls under about 600. That would put the market below its mid April level. New yearly lows above about 400 would say the same thing. A second shock day with 300 or more hard fallers inside a week would turn a break into a slide. A VIX close above 17, with the Nifty finally falling as hard as the small names, would say the large company shelter has given way. If IT turns down while breadth stays this weak, the index loses its last prop.

The cautious read fails if buying comes back broad and fast. The first test is a thrust day, with about 250 or more stocks up four and a half percent on heavy turnover. The last one came on 12 June. The second test is the long count back above about 840, the old floor, within a few sessions. That would make Wednesday a washout and not a breakdown. The third test is new lows back under about 100 while new highs climb past about 50. A VIX close back under 14 would support all three. State banks holding their weekly gain and the sugar mills holding their highs are the only signs of that kind of buying tonight. Two small groups do not make the case.
