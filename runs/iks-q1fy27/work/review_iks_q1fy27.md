IKS makes money by charging health systems for technology-plus-labour services across the patient journey — pre-visit scheduling and financial clearance, autonomous coding, billing/denial management, care management, clinical documentation [deck slide 7]. It is a single reportable segment ("Care enablement platform", Note 4 L415). Revenue quality is high (85%+ from repeat clients, average top-client vintage 5.5–7.4 years [deck slide 3, Tbl3]).

What this quarter says about model drift: (i) **Growth is increasingly acquisition-led** — TruBridge (US$557 Mn), WWMG and ARAI all reshape the group; the model is shifting from organic platform growth toward a buy-and-integrate strategy, which raises integration and PPA/goodwill risk (three provisional PPAs open [Notes 8/9/10]) and makes "one-time" acquisition costs a likely recurring line — a point that also bears on the ~590bps YoY ROE decline (F-13). (iii) **The standalone revenue spike is a one-off** — a pre-acquisition TruBridge software-license sale inflated the parent this quarter and eliminates as intercompany from Q2 [F-02], a reminder that reported standalone growth is not a clean run-rate. (iv) **Cash conversion needs watching** — the Rs 1,430 Mn "economic value add" payment to a customer [deck L370] hints the model may sometimes pay to win/retain large clients, which if recurring changes the true free-cash economics [F16c]. Metrics the filing did NOT disclose this quarter: no balance sheet, no cash-flow statement (Q1 not mandated), no ROCE, no segment split, no TruBridge financials, no split of the standalone license revenue — all ND.
