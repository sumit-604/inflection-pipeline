## Business Understanding Narrative

India Nippon makes the part that lights the spark in a small engine. Its core
product is the flywheel magneto, the rotating magnet assembly that generates
electricity on a motorcycle and fires the ignition. It also makes the ignition
control units around it. The company says it holds the number one market
position in flywheel magnetos (doc LP2iax p.14). It sells to TVS Motor, Hero
MotoCorp and Bajaj Auto (doc lURfqq p.130). An annual report calls TVS Motor
"the Company's most important customer right from inception".

That is a good business and a captive one. Three customers make most of India's
motorcycles, and they are hard buyers. Revenue is units shipped times content
per vehicle, and the content is small. FY26 revenue was Rs 10,685 Mn with an
EBITDA margin of 11.44% (doc LP2iax p.14, p.16). Eleven percent is what a tier
one supplier earns when the customer knows the cost of a magnet and a coil.

What is changing is the content, not the customer. The company signed a
technical licence with BorgWarner "which will enable to enter a new product
segment of the EFI system and serve customers for two and three-wheeler
applications" (doc LP2iax p.11). It also says it will expand into "sensors,
controllers, converters and engine control units" (doc LP2iax p.4). An engine
control unit is worth many times a magneto on the same motorcycle. This is a
content per vehicle story, and it is the only real transition on the card.

Why now has two parts. Ownership settled first. Across 2022 and 2023 the Mahle
group sold its entire stake to Lucas Indian Service, which took its holding to
70.32% (doc Y3OaRp p.7). The foreign technical partner left, and a licence from
BorgWarner arrived to fill the gap [INFERENCE, from the sequence of the two
filings]. Second, the company is winning share now. Q4FY26 sales grew 28%
against two wheeler industry growth of 19% (doc LP2iax p.14).

What must be true is that electronic fuel injection actually converts to
revenue. Nothing in the reachable record shows a rupee of EFI sales, a design
win or a start of production date.

What breaks it is the quality of the earnings. FY26 EBITDA was Rs 1,222 Mn and
net profit was Rs 1,112 Mn (doc LP2iax p.16). Profit is 91% of EBITDA. A company
with depreciation and tax cannot reach that from operations alone. Non operating
income is doing heavy work, and Rs 15.2 cr of it is a one off land compensation
from the Haryana government (doc LP2iax p.14).

## 2. Business model and archetype

India Nippon designs and makes ignition and electrical components for two and
three wheeler engines. Flywheel magnetos are the lead product. The stated
portfolio direction is sensors, controllers, converters and engine control units
(doc LP2iax p.4).

Archetype: build to spec component maker. The library's markers all fit.
Customer capex cycle: revenue tracks two wheeler production. Design win
pipeline: parts are approved model by model. Content per unit: the whole
transition thesis. Input cost pass through: copper and magnets, bought through
suppliers including Ram Ratna Wires for copper wire and terminals (doc Q2HwDs
p.62) and Shivam Autotech (doc gXo8xf p.14).

Segment and product revenue split: NOT FOUND.

The one economic engine is content per vehicle on a captive customer base. The
company cannot grow faster than its three customers unless it sells each of them
more per motorcycle. That is why the EFI licence matters more than the volume
figure.

Two customers outside the two wheeler business appear, Generac and Polaris,
described as newly acquired (doc Q6E7V2 p.8). Generac makes generators and
Polaris makes off road vehicles. Both are engine makers, so the same
competence travels. Revenue from either: NOT FOUND.

Manufacturing includes a Rewari facility, where a third phase flywheel magneto
line was installed (doc LP2iax p.14).

## 3. Competitive advantages

Present in the legacy product and unproven in the new one.

Switching cost: strong, and the best evidence on the card. An ignition component
is approved into a specific engine and stays there for the model's life.
Supplying TVS "right from inception" (doc lURfqq p.130) is a relationship
measured in decades, not contracts.

Scale: real within the niche. The company claims the number one position in
flywheel magnetos (doc LP2iax p.14). That is a share statement without a share
number, so treat it as a claim.

Pricing power: no. An 11.44% EBITDA margin against three of India's toughest
buyers is the answer.

Cost advantage: not evidenced. No cost curve position, no capacity figure and no
realisation per unit was reachable.

Scarcity: none.

The new advantage being built is licensed, not owned. The EFI capability comes
from BorgWarner under a technical licensing partnership (doc LP2iax p.11).
Licensed technology is a faster route and a weaker moat. The licence terms,
exclusivity and duration are all NOT FOUND, and they decide how much of the
advantage the company keeps.

Verdict: moat present on switching cost, absent on price, and licensed where it
is new.

## 6. The transition and the quality ladder

FROM R2, cost advantaged converter. TO R3, value added and specified supplier,
with an R4 case if electronics scale.

The mechanism is content per vehicle. A flywheel magneto is electromechanical
and cheap. An engine control unit is electronics, is specified into the engine
management system, and carries both a higher price and a higher margin. Moving
from one to the other on the same motorcycle, sold to the same three customers,
is a rung climb that needs no new customer.

Management's claim, quoted once: the BorgWarner partnership "will enable to
enter a new product segment of the EFI system and serve customers for two and
three-wheeler applications" (doc LP2iax p.11).

Against the base rate this is well shaped. One rung in two to three years is
normal, and the company is claiming one rung. Nothing here requires a leap.

The problem is that the climb has not started in any number that is held. The
11.44% margin is the FROM rung. There is no EFI revenue, no design win and no
start of production date in the reachable record.

## 11. Posture: the Transition Decision Matrix

- **Ugliness: STRUCTURAL-FEATURE.** The ugly optics are an 11.44% margin, three

## 12. Verdict card

**WATCH.** Do not spend a full `/step1` yet.

The reasoning is about what a deep run would buy. `/step1` would open the
earnings quality question and size the customer concentration, and both are
worth knowing. But it would not resolve the thing the thesis rests on. Whether
electronic fuel injection converts to revenue is not in any document filed so
far, because it has not happened. A full run today would cost the deep budget
and end where this card ends, waiting for the same gate.

The name deserves the watch. A licensed route into engine control units, sold
into three captive customers who already buy the incumbent part, is a genuine
content per vehicle climb with a short path to revenue. The parent owns 70.32%
and is an industrial group in the same trade. The balance sheet carries no debt.
The company is gaining share in its existing product. This is a well positioned
business waiting for one thing to start.

Load-bearing facts to verify first, when the run happens:
1. The EFI gate. A named design win, a customer, a start of production date, or
   first EFI revenue as a separate line.
2. The bridge from EBITDA of Rs 1,222 Mn to net profit of Rs 1,112 Mn.
   Depreciation, other income, treasury income and tax, each separately.
3. Revenue share of TVS, Hero and Bajaj individually.
4. The BorgWarner licence terms. Exclusivity, duration, royalty and territory.
5. Current promoter holding and free float, from a recent shareholding pattern.

What would change the view to PROCEED:
- Any disclosed EFI or ECU order, design win or production date. This single
  item flips the verdict.
- EBITDA margin moving above 13% with an electronics mix stated behind it.
- The company beginning to hold earnings calls, which would make every later
  proof point cheaper to verify.
- Evidence that Generac and Polaris are material, which would loosen the three
  customer concentration.

No valuation, no target price and no position is set here.
