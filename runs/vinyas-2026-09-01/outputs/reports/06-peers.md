Q1 FY27 EBITDA margin realized: 10.5%; FY26 normalized: 10.3%.
- AVALON (Q1 FY27, Aug 2026): EBITDA margin 12.0% (up from 9.2% a year earlier, 11.8% prior quarter); FY26 full year 10.8%; India-manufacturing-specific segment margin 16.7%. Comfortably inside the 9-13% band.
- CENTUM (bonus corroboration, not in named list but directly on point, May 2026 call, Sundararajan): "EMS segment… fairly pegged at between 9% and 10%… will not substantially exceed 10%" — explicit third-peer confirmation of the same band for the EMS-specific (not BTS) segment.

Different business model, not a like-for-like comparator. DATAPATTNS (bonus, same caveat): 27% Q1 FY27 EBITDA, guided 35-40% full year — also an IP-owning company, not EMS.

Net read: Two-plus independent peers (Cyient DLM explicitly, Avalon by realized results, Centum explicitly by segment guidance) corroborate the 9-13% band as the correct benchmark for EMS/subsystem players with defence/aerospace complexity but no owned IP. Astra Microwave and Data Patterns sit far above this band, but both are IP-owning product companies — their margins reflect IP ownership premium, not manufacturing complexity, and should not be used to argue VINYAS (a subsystem/EMS integrator) deserves similarly high margins.

### Claim 3 — What receivable-days/DSO do peers report, and do they attribute long DSO to milestone/project timelines as VINYAS does (~161d)?
Suggested peers: CENTUM, DATAPATTNS, ASTRAMICRO, CYIENTDLM

**Verdict: PARTIALLY VERIFIED (attribution logic corroborated; magnitude contradicted by closest analog)**

Peer evidence:
- CYIENTDLM (Q1 FY27, Jul 2026): discloses days sales outstanding (DSO) explicitly at 77 days (vs 74 days Q4 FY26), attributed to "continued discipline in collections." Separately, its blended net working capital days was 161 days in Q1 FY27 — a coincidental numeric match to VINYAS's headline figure, but this NWC number is driven mainly by inventory (162 days) and customer advances, not receivables. CYIENTDLM's own DSO component is less than half of VINYAS's claimed 161-day figure. (Apr 2026 call, CFO R.M. Subramanian, additionally: "DSO has improved steadily moving from the low 90s to the mid-70s, driven by stronger collection and billing discipline.")
- CENTUM (May 2026 call): "adjusted net working capital days improved to 142 days in FY26 from 159 days" — a blended figure, still below VINYAS's 161d, and improving, not stretching.
- DATAPATTNS (Aug 2026 call, Hardik Rawat Q): management explicitly attributes long-standing receivables to delayed customer inspection/approval cycles — qualitative support for the "milestone/project timeline" logic VINYAS uses, though no specific day-count is given.
- ASTRAMICRO (Jun 2026 call): references debtors "held up for various technical reasons" tied to program milestone completion and government payment cycles — again qualitative support for the attribution logic, no magnitude comparator.
- KAYNES (bonus, not in named list, Aug 2026 and May 2026 calls): overall working capital days 163 days (12-month rolling, Aug 2026) — very close in magnitude to VINYAS's claim, but Kaynes attributes this primarily to its metering business and strategic inventory build, NOT to defence/project milestone timelines; its core EMS-only working capital days is just 53 days (May 2026 call), sharply lower.

Net read: The qualitative logic (milestone/government payment cycles drive long receivables) IS corroborated directionally by Data Patterns and Astra Microwave. But on magnitude, the cleanest apples-to-apples analog (Cyient DLM, similar aerospace/defence/industrial EMS mix) discloses a pure DSO of 77 days — less than half VINYAS's 161-day claim — with its 161-day figure only matching at the blended (inventory-inflated) working-capital level, not at receivables specifically. This is a material distinction: if VINYAS's 161 days is genuinely DSO (not blended NWC), it looks elevated relative to peers running comparable milestone-billing businesses, not simply an industry-standard consequence of milestone billing.

### Claim 5 — What capex-to-capacity ratios do peers report for SMT/system-integration expansions vs VINYAS's Rs 500-600 Cr/SMT-line and 2-3 month online claim?
Suggested peers: KAYNES, SYRMA, CYIENTDLM, AVALON

**Verdict: CONTRADICTED**

Peer evidence:
- AVALON (Aug 2026 and May 2026 calls, Balasubramanian Q / Suresh V.R.): explicit — "we are doing INR50 crores to INR60 crores annual capex with 10x of asset turn… every year INR500 crores to INR600 crores additional revenue." Avalon's ENTIRE company-wide ANNUAL capex (Rs50-60cr) is roughly one-tenth of VINYAS's claimed cost for a SINGLE SMT line (Rs500-600cr). FY25 capex Rs58cr, FY26 capex Rs56cr, against a Rs1,603cr FY26 revenue base (asset turns ~9.9x).
- SYRMA (Aug 2026 call, Bijay Agrawal): a complete new greenfield multi-layer PCB manufacturing PLANT (buildings, equipment, full facility) costs ~Rs400cr total across all phases (Rs130cr funded so far); ex-PCB capex for the entire company is guided at Rs100-150cr for the full year. Even a whole new plant costs less than VINYAS's claimed cost for one SMT line. Timeline: equipment arrives from October, power-on targeted Jan-Mar, commercial production targeted April 2027 — over 18 months from capex start to commercial output, not 2-3 months.
- KAYNES (Aug 2026 call): OSAT capex Rs473cr FY26, PCB capex Rs324cr FY26 (entire multi-line facilities); FY27 guidance ~Rs300cr OSAT + Rs300cr PCB + Rs250cr EMS. These are whole-facility, multi-year capex figures in the low-hundreds-of-crores range — comparable to or below VINYAS's claimed cost for a SINGLE line — and Kaynes's facilities take multiple quarters to become operational, not 2-3 months.
- CYIENTDLM: no specific SMT-line capex figures disclosed (mentioned only a lab-space expansion from 6,000 to 15,000 sq ft for B2S product development, not capex-quantified).

Net read: This is the single clearest contradiction in the peer set. All three peers with disclosed capex-to-capacity data (Avalon, Syrma, Kaynes) show capex intensity for comparable or larger capacity additions that is an order of magnitude below VINYAS's implied Rs500-600cr-per-single-SMT-line figure, and none describes a 2-3 month capex-to-commercial-production timeline — the peer range for facility ramp-up runs from multiple quarters (Kaynes, Avalon incremental lines) to 18+ months (Syrma's PCB plant, start to commercial production).

## PART 5: CROSS-PEER HYPOTHESIS

Across the sampled peer set, inventory days are rising or being deliberately held elevated (Syrma explicitly: "we will not treat inventory as a working capital… Inventory will be treated as a strategic asset"; Cyient DLM's inventory at 162 days, explicitly linked to advance stocking against memory shortages and West Asia logistics delays; Kaynes's strategic inventory build) at the same time that receivables/DSO discipline is IMPROVING at the same companies (Cyient DLM's DSO down to mid-70s from low-90s; Avalon's receivable days down to 72-74; Centum's working capital days down from 159 to 142). No single peer frames this as a sector pattern — each describes its own inventory build and its own DSO improvement as separate, locally-motivated management actions.

Hypothesis: the Indian defence/aerospace-adjacent EMS sector's working-capital story in this period is asymmetric by design — companies are collectively trading AWAY receivables efficiency gains (tightening DSO through active collection) to fund a DELIBERATE inventory buffer against semiconductor/memory shortages and West Asia supply risk, rather than showing genuine overall balance-sheet discipline. This is testable: for any peer (or VINYAS) whose total working-capital days are elevated, the diagnostic question is which component is driving it. If DSO/receivables specifically is the driver (not inventory), that peer does NOT fit the sector's current pattern and represents a different, less benign story — receivables stretch reflects customer payment behaviour the company does not control, while the sector's inventory build is a chosen strategic hedge. This bears directly on how VINYAS's 161-day working-capital figure should be read: the claim-by-claim analysis above (Claim 3) found evidence that CYIENTDLM's matching 161-day NWC figure is inventory-driven, not receivables-driven — VINYAS's own figure needs the same decomposition before it can be judged against the sector pattern.
