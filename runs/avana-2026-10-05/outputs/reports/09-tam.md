## SECTION 1: MARKET DEFINITION

1A Scope.
- Product: customised control and relay (C&R) panels, protection relays (numerical and electromechanical), SCADA/substation automation sold inside panels. SCADA is not reported separately (B04).
- Geography: India. Exports were Rs 14.20 lakh of 3,574.71 lakh in H1 FY26 = 0.4% (RHP p153). Excluded.
- Customers: EPC contractors, private renewable and generation developers, state transmission utilities (B04; RHP p153).
- Channel: direct to EPC after utility vendor approval (Reg 30, 10-Feb-2026).
- Voltage: 11 kV to 220 kV panels per RHP p153; SCADA to 400 kV and MPPTCL 400 kV registration per AR p68 and Reg 30. Conflict carried from B04.
- Excluded: MCC and LV industrial panels, GIS/switchgear, transformers, non-power SCADA.

1B Management claim (AR p70-71, MD&A, FY26). Management gives no TAM for Avana. It cites four third-party figures:
- India electrical equipment consumption about USD 59B in 2025, 11% CAGR 2020-25, T&D nearly 40% (AR p70). Rs 5,23,861 Cr (59,000 x 88.79 / 10). Credibility: broad.
- India electric control panel market USD 486M in 2025 to USD 790M by 2031, 8.4% CAGR (AR p70). Rs 4,315 Cr to Rs 7,014 Cr. Credibility: specific, but covers all control panels (the Ken report names MCCs as the largest type).
- Electrical equipment production could pass USD 195B by 2035 (McKinsey/IEEMA, AR p71). Broad.
- Transmission 220 kV and above to grow to about 6.48 lakh ckm and about 2,345 GVA by 2031-32 (AR p72).
Held for Section 2: the USD 486M figure (Rs 4,315 Cr) is the closest to a claim.

## SECTION 3: SAM AND SOM

3A SAM. The five filters:
- Product fit: control and relay in power systems only. Needed share of Ken panel market: NOT FOUND.
- Customer: T&D and generation. Proxy used: T&D is about 40% of electrical equipment (AR p70). This is the one sourced ratio I have, applied to panels and relays by assumption.
- Geography: India only (exports 0.4%). No cut.
- Channel: EPC and utility approval route. No sourced cut.
- Capability: 11-220 kV panels plus relays; the 400 kV share is NOT FOUND. No cut.
SAM = 40% x 6,055 = Rs 2,422 Cr. SAM is 40% of conservative TAM. This is a proxy, not a measurement (FLAG-SAM-PROXY). Reading A: true utility C&R share is below 40%, which shrinks SAM and lifts Avana's share. Reading B: generation and renewable evacuation panels add to the T&D share. Separating observation: Avana FY26 customer-type mix (NOT FOUND).

3B SOM. Current share = 83.86 / 2,422 = 3.46%.
SAM growth 7.7%, weighted: (4,315 x 8.4% + 1,740 x 5.9%) / 6,055 = 7.68%.
- SAM yr3 = 2,422 x 1.077^3 = 3,026. SAM yr5 = 2,422 x 1.077^5 = 3,510.
- Share path (share-gain rules: 1-2pp normal over 3 years; 3-5pp aggressive with capacity and execution). Avana has capacity coming and a 36% growth record. Yr3: +2.0pp to 5.46%. Yr5: +3.0pp to 6.46%.
- SOM yr3 = 5.46% x 3,026 = Rs 165 Cr. SOM yr5 = 6.46% x 3,510 = Rs 227 Cr.
- CAGR yr3 = (165.2 / 83.86)^(1/3) - 1 = 1.970^(0.3333) - 1 = 25.4%.
- CAGR yr5 = (226.7 / 83.86)^(1/5) - 1 = 2.703^(0.2) - 1 = 22.0%.
Historical FY23-26 CAGR is 43.5%; the SOM path is below it. Forward evidence: order book Rs 52.24 Cr at 30-Nov-2025 (RHP), about 62% of FY26 revenue (5,223.65 / 8,385.88). Order book after that date NOT FOUND.

No shading. The SOM is the share-gain path. A higher reading exists: CEA targets 158,339 MVA of transformation additions in FY27 against 113,013 MVA in FY26 (+40.1%, loktej.com, 24-Sep-2026). If panel demand follows grid additions, Avana can outgrow SOM with no share gain. The one separating observation: CEA FY27 additions actually delivered, plus Avana H1 FY27 order book.

3C Capacity cross-check (B07 figure not available; used capex facts).
- Installed (RHP p105): panels 600 and relays 70,000 now. After KIADB: 1,500 panels and 1,75,000 relays (+150%).
- Prices (B04, produced-unit basis): FY25 panel Rs 5.75 lakh (3,008.96 / 523) and relay Rs 4,769; H1 FY26 panel Rs 6.88 lakh and relay Rs 4,221.
- Full capacity, FY25 prices: 1,500 x 5.75 lakh = 86.25 Cr; 1,75,000 x 4,769 = 83.46 Cr; total 169.7 Cr.
- Full capacity, H1 FY26 prices: 1,500 x 6.88 lakh = 103.2 Cr; 1,75,000 x 4,221 = 73.9 Cr; total 177.1 Cr.
- Yr3 SOM 165 Cr = 93% to 97% of that capacity: fits, but with no slack. Yr5 SOM 227 Cr is a gap of Rs 50 to 57 Cr (226.7 - 177.1 = 49.6; 226.7 - 169.7 = 57.0).
- Which side is optimistic? At fixed prices the capex plan is the tighter side. But stated capacity is unreliable: FY26 panels produced 886 vs 600 stated (148%, AR p74), relays 62,034 vs 70,000 (88.6%). The RHP also says no major machinery is needed, only space (RHP p105). So I do not call the SOM optimistic. Relays need demand of 2.8x FY26 volume to fill 1,75,000 (175,000 / 62,034), which is the real test.
- Status of the capex: KIADB plan Rs 1,155.38 lakh funded by fresh issue; AR CWIP 466.79 lakh and commitments 1,094.99 lakh (AR Note 25); commercial production guided by end-Oct-2026 (board outcome 19-Aug-2026). Unbuilt as of the filings (LBF1).
