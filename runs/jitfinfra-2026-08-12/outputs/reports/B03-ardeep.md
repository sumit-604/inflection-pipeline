## 6E Quiet Abandonment Check (mandatory)

**Item 1 — "diversified funding" and "balance-sheet discipline," hedged retreat.**
Opening/strategy language (MD&A p.82, p.86) repeatedly asserts "capital-allocation
discipline" and, specifically under Financing risk mitigation, "diversified funding,
promoter support, and balance-sheet discipline." The operational section that should
show this — the borrowings and RPT notes (Notes 20, 42.14) — instead shows funding
concentrated in two related-party lenders (~45% of finance cost), collateral
concentrated in promoter-pledged listed shares of group companies, and a personal
guarantee from a named individual. The claim is not silently dropped (funding is
discussed at length in the Notes) but it is materially **hedge-retreated**: "diversified
funding" in the opening becomes, in the operational section, funding that is
*undiversified and related-party-concentrated*, without the MD&A ever naming the scale
of that concentration or reconciling its own "diversified" characterisation against
it. Classification: **(c) hedged retreat.** Materiality: **changes the thesis** — a
reader relying on the MD&A's funding-risk framing would materially misjudge the
Group's actual funding-concentration risk.

**Item 2 — "financial discipline: strengthening... working-capital and receivables
management" as a stated FY27 priority, silent drop of the FY26 baseline.** The
strategic-priorities section (p.82-83) lists, as a forward-looking priority: "Advancing
operating cash generation, tightening working-capital and receivables management" (JWIL
Infra specific: "Accelerate completion of the 11 FY27-targeted projects to convert
order book into revenue and cash," "Strengthen collections to improve cash conversion
and reduce working-capital intensity"). This forward statement is the only place in the
entire front matter that gestures at a working-capital problem — but it never states
what the FY26 problem actually was (receivables +45% vs revenue +24%, >1yr overdue
nearly tripling, ECL coverage flat at 0.12%, retention money +33.9%, net working-capital
cash drag of ~₹27,983 lakh). The Key Financial Highlights table immediately preceding
this priority (p.83-84) touts Revenue +21%, EBITDA +29%, Order Book +123% with no
mention of working capital at all. Classification: **(b) silent drop** — the magnitude
of the underlying problem is never named anywhere in the narrative sections, only
recoverable from the Notes. Materiality: **changes the thesis** for anyone reading the
MD&A without also reading Phase 2/3's Notes-level analysis — the stated priority reads
as routine operational hygiene, not as a response to a genuine, quantified
deterioration.

No other quiet abandonments of comparable materiality were identified; the WtE
capacity-expansion narrative (Item covered in 4C/6A) is a credibility/pacing watch item
rather than a retraction, since the not-yet-commissioned status of three projects is
disclosed (just not reconciled against the "220 MW" framing) elsewhere in the same
document.

## Phase 6 Verdict

🔴 **Red Flag** — the front matter is well-written and well-sourced on growth, but two
of its central claims (diversified/disciplined funding, and an unstated working-capital
problem) do not survive contact with the operational sections of the same document,
and this pattern is consistent with, not incidental to, the Phase 4B omission findings.
