### 2C. Growth embedded in capex — the arithmetic

Per the prescribed formula: total capex under execution x historical fixed asset turnover =
implied incremental revenue.

- Capex under execution = Rs61.0325cr (machinery only; quotations, no POs, RHP p.88)
- Historical FAT (FY26) = Revenue Rs148.767cr / Net Fixed Assets Rs25.08cr = **5.93x** (RHP p.55,
  per B01 M3)
- Implied incremental revenue = 61.0325 x 5.93 = **Rs361.9cr**, i.e. **+243.3%** above FY26 revenue
  (361.9 / 148.767)

**This number should not be trusted at face value and is flagged as such.** FY26's FAT of 5.93x is
itself an artefact of a company whose revenue grew 573% in one year while net fixed assets grew far
more slowly — it is not a steady-state ratio, and applying it to brand-new, not-yet-ordered
machinery overstates the implied revenue by a wide margin. A cross-check using the company's own
disclosed capacity-hours math is more grounded: installed capacity hours rise 3,83,019 ->
6,71,499, **+75.3%**. Applying that ratio to FY26 revenue (which itself assumes hours are the
binding constraint, not customer demand — a strong assumption given Units 1-3 are already at
73-77% utilisation, not 100%) gives a rough incremental-revenue signal closer to **+75%**, roughly
a third of the FAT-method figure. Neither number is a reliable forecast; both are shown because the
protocol requires the arithmetic. The YAML `capex_embedded_growth_pct` field carries the
prescribed-formula result (243%) with this caveat attached.

### 4C. Regulatory moat assessment
This is a real, large, structural tailwind that is *already* showing up in Millworks' own numbers —
Defence rose from 6.81% (FY25) to 69.43% (FY26) of revenue (RHP p.131) largely on the back of the
Quick Pay/drone-kit relationship, which MD&A links (without naming Quick Pay directly) to "global
geopolitical changes" increasing Defence spending (RHP p.208). But because none of the specific
mechanisms (PLI enrolment, iDEX participation, a named defence-corridor incentive) is confirmed as
company-specific, and the tailwind is explicitly non-exclusive (every AS9100D-certified competitor
in the same segment benefits equally), this is classified **emerging/active-but-shared**, not a
company moat. Time to kick in: already kicking in. Sustainability: durable for years at the
sector level (multi-year government targets through 2030+); not sustainable as a *company-specific*
advantage unless Millworks converts scale/certification into disproportionate share gain, which the
concentration data (Section 3, C2) does not yet show.
