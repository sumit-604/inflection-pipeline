Real oilfield turnaround, valuation says avoid today

Rs791 Cr consolidated sales in FY26, up 70% on the year, is what Asian Energy Services shows after buying Kuiper Group, a Middle East offshore services firm, in September 2025. The company runs Indian oilfield and upstream energy services: seismic, operations and maintenance, integrated field development, and crude production through the Indrora participatory interest, alongside a mineral and coal handling business. Standalone sales were Rs492 Cr, up only 6% organically once Kuiper is stripped out. FY26 EBITDA was Rs99 Cr at a 12.5% margin, down from 15.6% the year before, and net profit was Rs51 Cr. The promoter is Oilmax Energy, the managing director is Kapil Garg, and the shares trade at Rs340 for a market value of Rs1,654 Cr.

Rs305 Cr to Rs465 Cr to Rs791 Cr is the three year sales path from FY24 to FY26, a real recovery from a business that had collapsed and lost money in FY23. Two structural moves define the change. Kuiper is already consolidating and nearly doubled reported size. The promoter's private company Oilmax is being reverse merged into the listed entity, pending the NCLT and guided for September or October 2026, which would add roughly 70 million barrels of reserves. The forward moat read is strengthening and the combined verdict is turnaround.

37 out of 100 on the backward looking Gate 0 core score classifies the company AVOID, but that number looks backward and carries a data hole. It is driven by the FY23 revenue collapse to Rs110 Cr with a Rs44 Cr loss, an earlier FY17 loss year, and a shareholding block that scored zero only because promoter holding data was missing from the Gate 0 inputs. Promoter holding is actually 60.83% with no pledge. The FY24 to FY26 window is a fully anchored recovery. AVOID here does not cap the decision.

Negative operating cash flow in both FY24 at minus Rs44 Cr and FY25 at minus Rs33 Cr sat against rising reported profit, all four working capital ratios worsened at once, unbilled contract assets grew 234% against 52% revenue growth, and borrowings rose roughly 6.7 times into FY26. The FY25 cash build was funded by a Rs157 Cr equity raise, not by operations. FY26 is the counterweight: operating cash flow turned positive at plus Rs53 Cr and CFO to PAT printed 1.02 times during the growth ramp. That turn reads as a growth effect in a percentage of completion business rather than a structural drain, but half the standalone revenue and more than half the receivables sit inside related party JVs, so the read is not yet settled.

Grade C credibility comes from a delivery record of 5 promises kept, 2 partial, and 4 missed, with the misses blamed on outside events. FY26 standalone guidance of Rs650 to Rs700 Cr was reaffirmed and then missed, blamed first on monsoon and then on the West Asia conflict. Field production dates at Duarmara, Tiphuk and Amguri slipped in every one of the three calls, with Duarmara ending with no committable date. The order book fell from over Rs2,000 Cr to Rs1,750 Cr even as FY27 growth guidance was raised to 30 to 40%. An analyst challenge on the roughly 50 times valuation of the Oilmax merger went unanswered across all three calls.

Both peers contradict the monsoon excuse. Deep Industries grew revenue 69% in the same quarter with no weather mention anywhere, at a 46.6% EBITDA margin. The West Asia disruption claim is partly corroborated by one peer only. The net effect of the peer read is that it complicates the company's account rather than clearing it. Good runway sits under the story with 14.8 times revenue headroom, but management's $100 billion by 2030 framing reads inflated at 41.8 times the independent market estimate because that figure is sector capex commitment, not services demand the company can capture.

Half of standalone revenue, about 50.8%, is related party, concentrated in newly formed majority owned JVs, and the Oilmax reverse merger is flagged rich by outside commentary at roughly 53 times FY25 earnings. The professional CEO was replaced by the promoter as managing director in 2023, MD remuneration is disclosed at both Rs277 Lakh and Rs157 Lakh in the same annual report, and the auditor notes recovery steps were not taken on a fully impaired related party loan. On the other side, the pledge is zero, the board carries credentialed former PSU heads, and the public shareholders passed the merger vote 99.99% in favour. The promoter verdict is CONCERN, not a deal breaker.

Rs249 base fair value on the additive track and Rs182 on the more conservative RRM track sit against Rs340 today, so the valuation decision is AVOID. The destination multiple comes out at 13.2 times on the additive four pillar build (range 12.0 to 14.0) and 9.3 times on the reverse rate method (range 8.5 to 10.0); on a 30% divergence the more conservative reverse rate method governs. ROCE enters STAGNANT at 13.71%, the cash multiplier is set at 0.85 times for the growth induced conversion, the Emerging Moat scores 28, and the sector cap is 20 times. The Hurdle check returns STOP at a base ratio of 0.76, and the probability weighted three year return works out to minus 12.1%. The entry range is Rs75 to Rs93 with a margin of safety price of Rs75. UA was not applied because the DII share for the ownership test is unresolved and the conservative call was to decline it.

The devil's advocate review keeps the overall thesis alive: growth, moat and management trust all survive, and only valuation safety weakens, on magnitude not direction. The honest concession is that a straight anchored sum of the parts lands near Rs225 to Rs277, and around Rs290 to Rs320 on generous inputs, which is only 18 to 34% below Rs340. The Rs75 to Rs93 entry is punitive because the conservatism stacks: STAGNANT rather than recovering ROCE, a 0.85 times multiplier on a 1.02 times FY26 cash print, UA declined, and a 0.70 reverse rate floor. The AVOID direction itself is robust, triple bound by the Gate 0 AVOID, the promoter CONCERN, and the Hurdle STOP. The pivotal unverified fact is the Oilmax merger accounting basis: if it pools at book with audited accretion, ROCE flips to recovering and the dilution ladder flips to accretion in one event.

The operator reads the same facts as a bull case, seeing roughly 50% upside on valuation alone and not overpaying at Rs340 because the standalone business plus Kuiper roughly cover today's price, with the Oilmax merger and the exploration book as the largely unpriced upside and Duarmara production as the medium term high gear. That upside rests on forward EBITDA and sum of the parts on EV to EBITDA plus merger accretion, not on a low current multiple, since the shares already trade near 30 times earnings against a 20 times sector cap. The framework AVOID and the operator bull case diverge on the entry price, and both are on the record here.
