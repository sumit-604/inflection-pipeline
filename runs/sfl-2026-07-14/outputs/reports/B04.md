## SECTION 1: THE BUSINESS MODEL IN PLAIN ENGLISH

### 1A. One-line description
Sheela Foam manufactures polyurethane (PU) foam and sells it two ways: as branded mattresses and home-comfort products to households under Sleepwell (and, since the 2024 merger, Kurlon), and as unbranded technical/industrial foam to other businesses (auto, furniture, filtration), with additional B2B foam manufacturing in Australia and Spain through wholly owned subsidiaries (AR p.2-3, 30; Inv. Pres. slide 15).

### 1E. The chai-stall-uncle version
Imagine a bakery that makes bread two ways: fancy branded loaves it sells in its own shops with its name on the wrapper (that's Sleepwell mattresses, sold to you and me), and plain flour-dough blocks it sells in bulk to other bakeries and factories (that's technical foam sold to car-seat makers and furniture factories). The "flour" here is a chemical called Polyol and TDI, which SFL buys mostly from abroad, so when that price jumps, the bakery's costs jump too, and it can only pass some of that on to customers after a lag. SFL has its own mini-bakeries doing the same "bulk block" business in Australia and Spain. Since 2024 it also bought a second big branded-loaf competitor, Kurlon, so now it sells two competing bread brands from the same ovens. And there's a tiny software-services side-shop (Staqo) that isn't really about bread at all.

### 2D. Industry lifecycle stage
The organized Indian mattress industry is described as still shifting share from unorganized to organized players ("clear shift from unorganized to the organized market with the implementation of GST and formalisation of the economy") (AR p.28) — this places the *organized segment* in a growth phase riding a structural formalization tailwind, even though mattresses as a product category are mature. SFL's position: incumbent market leader in the organized segment, using economy sub-brands (Starlite, Feather Foam) specifically to capture unorganized-to-organized conversion (AR p.30-31).
