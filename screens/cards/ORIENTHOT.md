## Business Understanding Narrative

**What does it do, and how does it make money?** OHL owns or leases seven hotels in South India with 825 rooms, 59 of them suites (screener/Annual_Report_2026.pdf, p.4). Three are freehold: Taj Coromandel and Taj Fisherman's Cove in Chennai, and Gateway Coonoor. Four are long leases: Taj Malabar Cochin, Vivanta Coimbatore, Vivanta Mangalore and Gateway Madurai (filings/TAJORIENTAL_24082026091305_SEPressRelease.pdf, p.2). IHCL runs every hotel under its brands and takes fees. OHL earns room revenue and food and banqueting revenue. In FY26 rooms brought Rs 262.83 crore and food, beverage and banqueting Rs 196.08 crore (Annual_Report_2026.pdf, p.38). The two Chennai hotels earn 55% to 60% of revenue (credit-ratings/02-ICRA.pdf, p.2).

**What is changing?** Two things. First, a renovation cycle of several years ended. Taj Malabar reopened in early 2024 after a full rebuild (Annual_Report_2025.pdf, p.7). Management calls the upgrades complete (filings/TAJORIENTAL_04052026150853_Press_release.pdf, p.2). Second, and decisive: on 24 August 2026 the boards of OHL and IHCL approved a scheme to amalgamate OHL into IHCL. The swap ratio is 25 IHCL shares for every 117 OHL shares, all stock. The appointed date is 1 April 2027 and completion is targeted in H2 FY28 (SEPressRelease 24-Aug-2026, p.3).

**Why now?** The price already moved. The 52-week range is Rs 80 to Rs 154 and the price is Rs 152 (market-data-2026-10-18.md, screener.in service record dated 2026-10-18, not a filed document). FII holding rose from 0.36% in June 2026 to 2.18% in September 2026 (same service record). That is the pattern of merger arbitrage, not of a rerating on OHL's own earnings.

**What must be true for the thesis to work?** For a standalone transition thesis: margins must climb back toward the 27% to 30% the rating agency expects (credit-ratings/01-CARE.pdf, p.2). For the stock as it trades today: the scheme must pass NCLT, the shareholders of both companies and SEBI. After that, the OHL share is 0.2137 of an IHCL share, and only IHCL's price matters.

**What breaks it?** A failed or revised scheme. If the scheme falls away, OHL trades on its own earnings, and those earnings are flat (step 5). The price would then rest on an unproven margin recovery.

## 2. Business model and archetype

One segment: hoteliering, all in India (Annual_Report_2026.pdf, p.97). The economic engine is room rate times occupancy on a fixed key count. Average room rate rose from Rs 10,155 in FY24 to Rs 10,837 in FY25 (Annual_Report_2025.pdf, p.31) and to Rs 11,628 in FY26. Occupancy went from 71% to 73% to 75% (Annual_Report_2026.pdf, p.38). Food and banqueting is a large second line. The key count has not changed in years (01-CARE.pdf, p.2).

The margin comes from owning scarce urban and resort land under a brand OHL does not own. IHCL charges a base fee of 3% to 5% of gross income plus 4.5% of gross sales for marketing and other services (Annual_Report_2026.pdf, p.21).

**Archetype: licence/scarcity business, with a rented brand.** The fit is imperfect. The scarcity is the land and the capacity cap of 825 keys. The pricing power belongs to the Taj brand, which IHCL owns.

## 3. Competitive advantages

Present, but borrowed. Taj Coromandel and Taj Fisherman's Cove are long established in their Chennai micro-markets (02-ICRA.pdf, p.2). The brand, the loyalty programme and the reservation network all sit with IHCL (Annual_Report_2026.pdf, p.17). OHL cannot take them elsewhere. The moat is real at the asset level and absent at the company level.

## 4. The promoters

Three groups. At 31 March 2026 the Taj group held 39.09% and the other promoters 29.15% (Annual_Report_2026.pdf, p.49). IHCL holds 28.54% directly; with its subsidiaries the figure is 37.09% (same, p.23). Tata Chemicals holds 1.41%. The DPS Reddy family holds about 29.4% (02-ICRA.pdf, p.3). Pramod Ranjan, the Managing Director and CEO, is himself a promoter with 8.00% (Annual_Report_2026.pdf, p.49). That is real skin in the game. His pay is 55.64 times the median employee (same, p.32).

Pledge: none. IHCL, Pramod Ranjan, D. Vijayagopal Reddy and Ramesh D Hariani each filed a Regulation 31(4) declaration of no encumbrance in April 2026 (filings/team_sandeshc_01062026121145_OHL.pdf, p.1; _150459_24.pdf, p.1; _150845_22.pdf, p.1; _150353_25.pdf, p.1). One member of the promoter group, Chilamilika Lalini Hariani, raised her holding from 8,07,692 to 20,38,271 shares in FY26 (Annual_Report_2026.pdf, p.103).

Dependence on related parties is total. OHL paid IHCL Rs 20.86 crore in operating and licence fees and Rs 20.63 crore for sales, marketing and reservations in FY26 (Annual_Report_2026.pdf, p.20).

Three readings come out of this table.

1. **FY26 PAT carries a one-off.** The move to the new 25.168% tax regime cut deferred tax by Rs 11.40 crore (Annual_Report_2026.pdf, p.92). That is why Q4 FY26 shows a tax credit. Without it, FY26 PAT is Rs 59.37 crore, about level with FY23's Rs 58.47 crore.
2. **Margin has not climbed.** FY23 printed the peak, 30.90%. FY26 printed 27.77%. H1 FY27 operating EBITDA was Rs 49.37 crore against Rs 50.80 crore a year earlier, down 2.8% on revenue up 5.1% (computed from q-2026-09.pdf, p.2). CARE names menu spending, headcount and advertising as the Q1 drag (01-CARE.pdf, p.2).
3. **The balance sheet is the real delivery.** Gross debt fell from Rs 150.39 crore to Rs 96.99 crore in FY26 (Annual_Report_2026.pdf, p.39), then to Rs 79.38 crore at September 2026 (q-2026-09.pdf, p.3). Operating cash flow was Rs 125.78 crore in FY26 (Annual_Report_2026.pdf, p.39).

**Classification: an asset event dominates.** The underlying earnings line is flat since FY23. The headline FY26 jump is a tax one-off. The share price is now set by the merger.

Seasonality: H2 carries the year. In FY26 H1 earned Rs 32.13 crore of PBT before exceptional items out of Rs 91.75 crore, so 35% (q-2026-09.pdf, p.2).

## 6. The transition and the quality ladder

FROM the R2/R3 boundary. TO no higher rung on any claim the corpus holds.

Standalone ROCE ran 19.29%, 16.04%, 13.66% and 17.01% over FY23 to FY26 (Annual_Report_2026.pdf, p.6). That is in the mid teens, short of R3's 20% to 25%. Pricing power is real but borrowed: room rate rose 7% a year for two years under the Taj brand. The business sits between rungs.

Management's claim, once: "the company is poised to deliver strong performance in the upcoming fiscal year" (TAJORIENTAL_04052026150853_Press_release.pdf, p.2). H1 FY27 did not deliver it.

The real transition is corporate, not operational. OHL shareholders move into IHCL. In the IHCL CFO's words, the merger will "simplify the group's holding structure" (SEPressRelease 24-Aug-2026, p.3). IHCL's rung is outside this corpus.

## 8. Proof check

The triggers claim that finished renovations lift margins. The last two quarters do not show it. Q1 FY27 operating margin was 20.7% against 23.5% a year earlier. Q2 FY27 was 22.6% against 23.4% (computed from q-2026-09.pdf, p.2). Revenue grew 6.5% in Q2 on RevPAR up 9% (TAJORIENTAL_15102026165413_Pressrelease.pdf, p.2). Costs grew faster.

The proof point is absent. The renovation story finished its spending and has not yet paid in margin.

## 9. Flags from the documents

- **The merger fixes the price.** At Rs 152 and 25 for 117, the market implies an IHCL price near Rs 711. IHCL's own price: NOT FOUND in the corpus. A screen on OHL's earnings now screens nothing.
- **HMA timing.** Shareholders approved new 20 year management agreements with IHCL on 30 July 2026 (filings/TAJORIENTAL_31072026193147_SEVotingResults.pdf, p.11). The merger followed on 24 August. The FY27 approval value for fees and reimbursements is Rs 66.00 crore (Annual_Report_2026.pdf, p.24). The matching FY26 heads totalled Rs 46.38 crore (same, p.20). The FY27 figure is an approval ceiling, not an actual. Whether the valuers used the new fee load to value OHL: NOT FOUND, as the valuation reports are not held.
- **Institutions voted 14.43% against the HMA resolution.** Only 1.37% of shares were polled; promoter votes were excluded (SEVotingResults, p.11).
- **NSE surveillance queries** on price (24 June 2026) and volume (20 July 2026) came before the merger announcement. The company replied that all price-sensitive information was disclosed (TAJORIENTAL_25062026112721_NSEReplyJune2026.pdf, p.1; TAJORIENTAL_21072026111704_NSEReply.pdf, p.1). Surfaced, not investigated.
- **Concentration.** Two Chennai hotels earn 55% to 60% of revenue (02-ICRA.pdf, p.2).
- **Joint venture losses.** The TAL joint venture (Maldives and Sri Lanka hotels) cost Rs 7.62 crore in FY26 and Rs 7.09 crore in H1 FY27 alone (q-2026-09.pdf, p.7).
- **Audit.** Clean opinion; the only key audit matter is revenue recognition (Annual_Report_2026.pdf, p.69). The audit trail did not log privileged-user data changes until 26 June 2025 (same, p.71). Disputed dues and contingent claims total about Rs 14.26 crore (same, p.95).

## 10. Independent check: the credit rating

Both agencies upgraded in the last year and both put the rating on watch for the merger.

- CARE moved the long-term rating to AA- from A+ on 1 December 2025 (credit-ratings/04-CARE.pdf, p.1; history at 01-CARE.pdf, p.5). On 2 September 2026 it placed AA- on Rating Watch with Positive Implications (01-CARE.pdf, p.1). It calls the financial profile comfortable: interest cover 9.75 times and net debt including leases at 0.87 times PBILDT in FY26 (same, p.2).
- ICRA moved to AA- from A+ on 3 March 2026 and placed it on watch positive on 2 September 2026 (02-ICRA.pdf, p.4). ICRA calls DSCR "relatively moderate" at 1.2 times (same, p.2). It expects capex of only Rs 20 to 25 crore in FY27, all maintenance (same, p.3).

On the transition, CARE says the portfolio "has remained stagnant at seven hotels and 825 operating keys" (01-CARE.pdf, p.2). Both agencies lean on IHCL's support. They confirm credit strength, not an earnings climb.

## 11. Posture: the Transition Decision Matrix

- **Proof gate: NOT FIRED.** H1 FY27 operating EBITDA fell 2.8% and margins fell in both quarters (step 8).
- **Ugliness: ARTIFACT-OF-CLIMB.** The ugly years, FY24 and FY25, carried renovation write-offs and the Taj Malabar closure (Annual_Report_2026.pdf, p.38; Annual_Report_2025.pdf, p.31). Those are finished.
- **Recognition gap: CLOSED.** The price is set by the swap ratio. At Rs 2,711 crore of market cap (market-data-2026-10-18.md, service record) the stock trades near 40 times trailing consolidated PAT and about 44 times FY27E (step 13).

**Posture: PRICED NARRATIVE (TRAP), as a merger stub.** The narrative here is the merger, and the merger is already priced. The two readings: either the margin recovery arrives and IHCL captures it, or it does not and OHL holders still receive IHCL shares. In both, OHL's own earnings stop deciding the outcome. The observation that would separate a standalone case from a stub is the scheme's fate at NCLT.

## 12. Verdict card

**PASS.**

The strategy hunts small and micro cap climbs that the market has not repriced. OHL fails on both counts. Its earnings have not climbed since FY23 on a like for like tax basis. Its price now follows IHCL, a group of about Rs 1 lakh crore market cap (01-CARE.pdf, p.2), which is outside the mandate.

**Load-bearing facts, if the operator wants to reopen it**
1. Scheme status at NCLT, SEBI and both shareholder meetings.
2. The two valuation reports and fairness opinions: did they price the new HMA fee load into OHL?
3. H2 FY27 operating margin against the 27% to 30% agency expectation.

**What would change the view**
- Scheme withdrawn or rejected. OHL then re-screens as a standalone hotel owner with little debt.
- A revised swap ratio that opens a gap against IHCL's price.
- An operating margin back above 27% in a full year, which would make a standalone case.

No Role 1 valuation was run and no target price is set. This verdict decides only whether the name earns a full `/step1`, and it is not a position.
