1. 🔴 MSSTL's own statutory auditor's CARO report (Annexure A to the consolidated Independent
   Auditor's Report, p.185-186) discloses three items Pass 1 did not extract:
   - Para 3(9)(d): MSSTL "used funds raised on short term basis aggregating to ₹97.68 Crores for
     long-term purposes due to the bank term loan disbursement was delayed." This figure is the same
     magnitude as the Limitless Contracting Private Ltd promoter-entity loan Pass 1 flagged (₹97.68cr
     closing balance, Note 36 consolidated / Note 15(b) consolidated). Read together, this is the
     connective finding Pass 1 missed: a promoter-controlled lender appears to have bridged MSSTL's
     capex/construction spend on a short-term basis while its bank term loan disbursement was delayed,
     at undisclosed terms, now sitting as the largest unsecured current borrowing in the consolidated
     book.
   - Para 3(13): MSSTL's related-party transactions during the year were NOT approved by its Audit
     Committee, because the committee was formed only on 13-Oct-2024. A table lists specific
     transactions and the portion subsequently ratified: Man Industries (India) Ltd service expense
     ₹270.00 lakh (only ₹90.00 lakh ratified), Man Offshore and Drilling rent income ₹1.50 lakh (₹0.75
     lakh ratified), Man Finance Pvt Ltd rent expense ₹18.00 lakh (fully ratified). This is a subsidiary-
     level RPT governance control gap, distinct from and additional to the parent-level RPT items Pass
     1 covered.
   - Para 3(17): MSSTL incurred CASH losses of ₹205.64 lakh in FY25 and ₹83.02 lakh in FY24 — two
     consecutive years of cash losses (worse than the accounting loss of ₹(264.23) lakh Pass 1 already
     flagged), disclosed only in the subsidiary auditor's own CARO clause, not narrated anywhere in the
     parent's or group's notes.

2. 🟡 Consolidated Note 44, Segment Information, opening sentence (p.247): "The Group's operations
   primarily relates to manufacturing of steel products and plastic products, which has been acquired
   during the previous year." No "plastic products" business, acquisition, or entity appears anywhere
   else in the notes, the RPT list, the subsidiary list (Note 42 consolidated), or the segment table
   itself (which reports only two segments: Manufacturing & trading in Steel Products, and Real
   Estate). "Plastic" appears nowhere else in the financial statements. This reads as a copy-paste
   drafting error (template language from an unrelated filing) rather than a disclosed fact, but it is
   an unresolved internal inconsistency the way the Note 44/Note 49 segment-count contradiction Pass 1
   found is.

3. 🟡 Consolidated Note 14B, Other Equity (p.219) shows a SECOND recurring line item credited directly
   to Retained Earnings, bypassing P&L, beyond the "Consolidation restatement impact" (₹4,058.80 lakh)
   Pass 1 already flagged: "Changes in Ownership interest" of ₹441.16 lakh (FY25) and ₹191.03 lakh
   (FY24) — present in BOTH years, unlike the one-off restatement item. No note anywhere names the
   entity or transaction behind either year's figure. (Ind AS 110 does permit direct-to-equity
   treatment for ownership-interest changes that do not cause loss of control, so the mechanism itself
   may be compliant; the gap is that no entity or percentage change is ever named.) Combined with the
   already-flagged item, total FY25 credits to retained earnings that bypass P&L = ₹4,499.96 lakh, or
   29.4% of consolidated FY25 profit (₹15,317.11 lakh), not the 26.5% Pass 1 computed from the single
   item alone. (Consolidated Statement of Changes in Equity p.190; Note 14B p.219.)

4. 🔴 Debt Service Coverage Ratio is below 1.0x at both entity levels, both years, and Pass 1 did not
   surface this despite citing Note 40/41 for other ratios: standalone 0.59x (FY25) vs 0.63x (FY24);
   consolidated 0.52x (FY25) vs 0.61x (FY24) (Note 40 standalone p.176-177; Note 41 consolidated
   p.244). Earnings available for debt service do not cover contractual interest-plus-principal
   obligations in either year at either entity level, and the ratio deteriorated further at the
   consolidated level (-15.71%). This sits alongside the net-debt swing Pass 1 flagged as a second,
   independent leverage-stress indicator from the same note.

5. 🟡 The consolidated Key Financial Ratio table (Note 41, p.244) selectively drops the mandatory
   "reason for variance" narrative for three ratios that exceed the 25% SEBI threshold: Inventory
   turnover -45.20%, Trade receivables turnover -49.25%, Trade payables turnover -58.26% — all blank
   in the "Reason" column. The IDENTICAL standalone-level ratios (Note 40, p.176), with comparable or
   larger variances, DO carry explicit reasons ("Increase in Inventory," "Increase in Trade
   Receivables," "Increase in Trade Payables"). Conversely, the consolidated table adds reasons for
   Current ratio (-28.11%, "Increase in current liabilities") and Debt-equity ratio (+29.20%,
   "Increase in Debt") that the standalone table omits because those two ratios stayed under 25% at
   standalone level. The same disclosure requirement is applied inconsistently to the same underlying
   figures within one document.

6. 🟡 Cross-note inconsistency between Note 10(a) and Note 35(D)(8), both standalone: Note 10(a) "Other
   non-current assets" (p.145) shows total "Advance for property" of ₹7,026.18 lakh at 31-Mar-2024,
   while Note 35(D)(8), the related-party closing-balance disclosure (p.164), shows "Advance for
   purchase of property — Merino Shelters Private Limited" of ₹5,692.20 lakh at the same date (the

   reconciled or explained; the balance-sheet note gives no breakdown of what the non-related-party
   portion of "Advance for property" relates to.

12. 🟢 Note 5(b), Capital Work-in-Progress ageing (standalone, p.137): CWIP grew from ₹3,059.30 lakh to
