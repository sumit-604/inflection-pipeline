Premium IVF brand where receivables outpace revenue

FY26 revenue was Rs 104 Cr, up 48%, with PAT of Rs 24 Cr and an EBITDA margin near 36%. Gaudium IVF and Women Health runs fertility treatment across 7 hubs and 28 spokes in 9 states, built and led by Dr Manika Khanna. The company listed in February 2026 at Rs 79 and trades near Rs 137, a market value close to Rs 997 Cr. It performed 2,255 egg pickups in FY26 at an average revenue per patient of about Rs 2.98 lakh.

IVF is 64% of revenue, pharmacy trading is now 31%, and one hospital is the remaining 4%. Pharmacy trading was under 1% of revenue three years ago. That shift pulled the consolidated EBITDA margin down from 45% to 36% across FY23 to FY26, because drug resale earns far less than the core clinic. The standalone IVF margin near 51% in the March quarter confirms the dilution comes from pharmacy, not from the treatment business.

The plan is to go from 7 hubs to 26 by FY29, adding 19 hubs over three years, funded by roughly Rs 50 Cr of IPO proceeds. Management also put AI embryology tools live from April 2026, signed patient financing tie-ups, and named international spokes in Nigeria, Sydney and Paris. None of the IPO hub money had been spent as of 31 March 2026, so the entire rollout is still on paper as of the last audited date.

The quality screen graded the company AVERAGE, not GOOD. Two things pulled it down: only four years of audited history since a recent listing, and a cash generation score of 5 out of 20. Revenue and profit grew in each of those four years, so the downgrade is about short history and cash quality, not decline.

Trade receivable days rose from 7 in FY23 to about 156 in FY26, and receivables now sit at 51% of revenue. Cash from operations was only 45% of profit in FY25, free cash flow turned negative in FY26 even as profit grew, and the company booked its first ever credit loss provision. Every headline FY26 figure the CFO gave on the maiden call checks out against the audited accounts, with one exception: management claimed zero bad debt a day after the audited cash flow recorded a Rs 32 lakh credit loss allowance. Management ties the long collection cycle to a deliberate affordability model, patients paying across the treatment cycle, and has doubled the upfront payment to Rs 80,000 and added a Rs 40,000 late transfer fee to correct it, but no forward quarter exists yet to prove the fix. The credibility grade is C.

Peers contradict two of the growth claims directly. HCG collects in 105 to 115 days and Kaya collects prepaid, against Gaudium's roughly 150 days, so the long cycle is not a sector norm. Gaudium says 25 to 30% of its patients are international, while HCG runs about 3.5% and Rainbow about 1.7% of revenue from abroad. The AI embryo edge, the 13% market growth figure, and the 58% success rate each stand unverified, with zero corroboration from any of the twelve peer calls.

The market is not the binding constraint. India's IVF market runs Rs 14,560 to 19,665 Cr, growing about 13% a year, and Gaudium holds about 3.5% of its addressable slice with roughly 28 times revenue headroom. The share it could realistically win implies a 31% revenue growth rate, above its own 25% target, which means capacity and cash collection bind the thesis long before demand does. Only 5 embryologists run the whole network, each capped near 120 cycles a month, and no hiring plan is tied to the 19 hub rollout.

Governance carries a real overhang. The company and Dr Khanna personally face about Rs 50 Cr of contingent tax claims, close to 85% of net worth, from a 2022 survey alleging unaccounted cash from surrogacy patients, all under appeal. Rs 9 Cr went into two unrelated Mumbai entities with no stated business reason, and a subsidiary was moved to the promoters personally months before listing. Against that, the company brought in an external CFO and company secretary, rebuilt the independent board with credentialed appointees, and has run a philanthropy arm since 2014.
