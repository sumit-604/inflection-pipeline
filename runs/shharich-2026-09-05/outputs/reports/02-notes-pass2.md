## NEW FINDING 1 — Note 35.15 "Fair Values": a materially broken disclosure table, identical defect in both
STANDALONE (p.133) and CONSOLIDATED (p.172) statements 🔴

Note 35.15 presents a four-column comparison ("Carrying Value" and, implicitly, "Fair Value," each for FY26
and FY25) for financial assets and liabilities not automatically fair-valued. For financial assets measured
**at fair value through profit or loss (FVTPL)** — quoted equity, unquoted equity, mutual funds — carrying
value and fair value are definitionally the SAME number under Ind AS 109 (there is no separate fair-value
step; the FVTPL carrying amount IS the fair value). The note nonetheless shows two DIFFERENT sets of numbers
for the same FVTPL investments, in both standalone and consolidated statements, e.g.:
- "Investment in equity instruments (quoted)" — Rs 12.96 lakh / Rs 12.96 lakh (identical across FY26 and
  FY25, i.e. static) in the first two columns, against the correct, moving Note 3 carrying values of Rs 2.34
  lakh (FY26) / Rs 2.93 lakh (FY25) in the last two columns.
- "Investment in equity instruments (unquoted)" — Rs 29.56 lakh / Rs 29.56 lakh (static) against the correct
  Note 3 CETP Co-op Society carrying values of Rs 105.30 lakh (FY26) / Rs 91.73 lakh (FY25).
- "Investment in Mutual Funds (quoted)" — Rs 120.00 lakh / Nil against the correct Rs 109.35 lakh (FY26) /
  Nil.

The consolidated version of the same note additionally shows entire lines that are blank, zero, or
transposed against the wrong row: "Bank Deposits with original maturity more than 12 months" shows Rs 13.34
lakh in the FY26 first column (that figure is actually the Security Deposits line directly above it) against
the correct Rs 1,099.53 lakh; "Cash and Cash Equivalents" shows a blank/nil FY26 first-column figure against
the correct Rs 460.94 lakh; "Other Financial Assets" shows blank/nil against the correct Rs 479.45 lakh;
"Long Term Borrowings" shows blank/nil against the correct Rs 727.88 lakh.

Why this is new and matters beyond Pass 1's finding #6/#7 (cross-reference errors, copy-pasted "Group"
language): those were reference/wording slips. This is a **numbers-wrong** disclosure, required under Ind AS
107, that is internally impossible for the FVTPL lines (carrying value cannot differ from fair value for a
FVTPL instrument) and materially incomplete for several balance-sheet lines — and the SAME broken table
appears in both the standalone and the consolidated financial statements, meaning the error propagated
through a shared template rather than being an isolated one-off. Both sets of accounts carry the same
statutory auditor's unqualified report.

## NEW FINDING 2 — Gratuity P&L charge does not reconcile between Note 32 and Note 35.11(E), and the gap
widened sharply this year, coinciding with the unexplained Past Service Cost 🔴

Note 32 (Employee Benefit Expenses, standalone p.123 / consolidated p.162) shows a "Gratuity" P&L expense of
**Rs 49.04 lakh (FY26)** vs **Rs 14.76 lakh (FY25)**. Note 35.11 Section E (standalone p.131 / consolidated
p.169), which is the Ind AS 19 actuarial note's own reconciliation of "(Income)/Expenses recognized in the
... Profit & Loss Account Statement," shows "Net Benefit or (expenses)" of **Rs 24.03 lakh (FY26)** vs **Rs
10.56 lakh (FY25)** for the identical gratuity plan. These two supposedly-parallel figures do not tie in
either year (FY25 gap Rs 4.20 lakh; FY26 gap Rs 25.01 lakh), and the gap grew nearly 6x this year. Note
35.11 Section E itself shows both "Plan Amendments" and "Prior Year Charge" as nil in both years, even though
Section A of the same note shows a **Rs 27.26 lakh Past Service Cost** newly added to the defined benefit
obligation this year (Pass 1 finding #9) — meaning the note's own P&L reconciliation does not show where, or
whether, that Rs 27.26 lakh actually hit profit and loss. This is a genuine cross-note quantitative
inconsistency, not merely a missing qualitative explanation (which is all Pass 1 flagged): the audited P&L
gratuity charge and the audited actuarial-note P&L reconciliation, for the same plan and the same year,
disagree by Rs 25.01 lakh, and no note bridges the two.

## NEW FINDING 3 — Promoter/related-party ownership concentration rose from 54.21% to 59.25% of the company
in one year, entirely via CCD conversion 🟡

Note 35.1(iii) (consolidated p.165; the identical table sits in standalone at p.122/126 but its "TOTAL" row
did not extract cleanly there) gives an explicit total: promoter-group holding was **32,81,396 shares /
59.25%** at 31-Mar-2026 versus **26,75,902 shares / 54.21%** at 31-Mar-2025 — a **+5.04 percentage point**
rise in one year. Cross-checked against Note 35.1(ii)'s >5% shareholder table, the entire increase traces to
this year's four CCD-conversion beneficiaries: Suman Agarwal 7.21%→8.16%, Gayatri Devi Agarwal 7.16%→8.68%,
Kanta Agarwal 7.42%→8.34%, Shubhalaxmi Polyesters Ltd 9.86%→13.93%. Every new share issued in FY26 (6,05,494
shares, entirely from CCD conversion at Rs 79 vs CMP Rs 248) went to promoter-group or promoter-linked
holders; non-promoter/public shareholders' proportional stake fell correspondingly from 45.79% to 40.75%.
Pass 1's finding #5 flagged the CCD dilution as a future overhang; this finding shows the mechanism has
already visibly shifted control concentration in the year just reported, not only in the pending balance.

## NEW FINDING 4 — Consolidated-only export-incentive receivables appear despite Group revenue being
identical, rupee for rupee, to standalone revenue 🔴

Consolidated Note 14 (Other Current Assets, p.157) shows a new **RoDTEP Receivable of Rs 15.42 lakh** (RoDTEP
= Remission of Duties and Taxes on Exported Products, an incentive that accrues only against a company's own
external exports) and a higher "Balances with Government Authorities" figure (Rs 78.27 lakh vs standalone Rs
36.09 lakh, a Rs 42.18 lakh gap) that are both absent, or smaller, at standalone level. Yet the consolidated
Revenue from Operations note (Note 27, p.161) is **identical to the rupee** with the standalone note — Rs
18,450.48 lakh in total, and every sub-line (Sale of Manufactured Goods Rs 14,702.27 lakh, Sale of Traded
Goods Rs 3,349.83 lakh, etc.) matches exactly — meaning the subsidiary contributed zero NET external revenue
to the Group this year (any subsidiary sales would have to have been wholly intercompany and eliminated).
A RoDTEP receivable cannot arise without the claiming entity having made an external export. This is an
unreconciled inconsistency the Notes do not explain: either the subsidiary has undisclosed external export
activity not reflected in the (identical) consolidated revenue total, or these are legacy/administrative
balances unrelated to current-year exports. Not resolvable from the Notes alone; a direct question for
management.

## NEW FINDING 5 — Consolidated CWIP ageing shows Rs 73.55 lakh aged 1-2 years, entirely subsidiary-level,
proving the SDPL capex programme predates FY26 🟡

Standalone Note 2(ii)(a) (p.115) shows 100% of the Rs 764.88 lakh closing CWIP as "less than 1 year" in both
FY26 and FY25 disclosures. The consolidated equivalent (p.154) for FY26 instead shows **Rs 1,056.87 lakh
"less than 1 year" plus Rs 73.55 lakh "1-2 years"** (total Rs 1,130.42 lakh). Since the standalone (parent)
CWIP carries no aged component at all, the entire Rs 73.55 lakh of 1-2-year-aged CWIP must sit at the
subsidiary. This means the subsidiary's capex programme has been running for more than a year without any
asset going live or being capitalised — it is not a fresh FY26 initiative, as the CWIP-doubling narrative in
Pass 1 might read in isolation, but a multi-year-old, still-wholly-uncompleted build. This bears directly on
the company-memory question of SDPL's capex/product-mix timeline.

## NEW FINDING 6 — The subsidiary (or subsidiaries) is currently loss-making at the group level, and the
loss widened this year 🟡

Note 35.8 (Earnings Per Share) discloses Net Profit After Tax on TWO different bases in the standalone and
consolidated notes: **Rs 422.22 lakh (standalone, FY26)** vs **Rs 414.95 lakh (consolidated, FY26)** — a Rs
7.27 lakh gap — and **Rs 512.40 lakh (standalone, FY25)** vs **Rs 509.75 lakh (consolidated, FY25)** — a Rs
2.65 lakh gap. Since both named subsidiaries are wholly owned (no non-controlling interest line appears
anywhere in the consolidated statements), consolidated PAT = standalone PAT + subsidiary-level net
profit/(loss); the gap therefore implies an aggregate subsidiary net LOSS of approximately Rs 7.27 lakh in
FY26, up from approximately Rs 2.65 lakh in FY25 (+174% YoY). Small in absolute terms (under 2% of group
PAT), but it directly confirms the subsidiary is currently a pure cost centre — consistent with Finding 5's
evidence of a still-uncompleted, multi-year capex build with no revenue (Finding 4's revenue identity).

## NEW FINDING 7 — The subsidiary is sitting on roughly Rs 700 lakh (Rs 7 cr) of cash/deposits not yet
deployed into capex, and this is what flatters the consolidated working-capital ratios 🟡

Consolidated Note 10 (Cash & Cash Equivalents, p.157) shows Rs 460.94 lakh (FY26) vs standalone's Rs 55.37
lakh — a Rs 405.57 lakh gap — including a new Rs 400.00 lakh fixed deposit (up to 3 months) that does not
appear standalone. Consolidated Note 11 (Bank Balance Other Than Cash, p.157) shows a new Rs 300.00 lakh
deposit (3-12 month maturity), lien-marked against an overdraft facility, versus Nil at standalone in both
years. Together the subsidiary is holding roughly Rs 700 lakh of cash/FDs — capital that has been raised
(consistent with company memory's Rs 4.95 cr SDPL rights subscription and the broader Rs 9.95 cr FY26
investment increase) but not yet spent on the capex it was raised for. This liquidity is also the direct
explanation for why the consolidated current ratio (0.95, Note 35.22) is materially healthier than standalone
(0.81) and why the consolidated "Net capital turnover ratio" shows a much smaller working-capital deficit
in magnitude (Sale/WC = -73.25x) than standalone (-18.42x) despite identical Group revenue (Finding 4) — the
apparent group-level liquidity improvement is subsidiary cash sitting idle pre-capex, not a structural
parent-level working-capital improvement.

## NEW FINDING 8 — Correction to Pass 1 Finding #10: the Notes DO admit a related-party loan exists;
only the quantification is missing 🟡 (correction)

Pass 1 stated: "no note in either standalone or consolidated financial statements discloses a loan or
inter-corporate deposit to the subsidiary... This is a disclosure gap within the Notes to Financial
Statements specifically." This needs correction. Note 35.23(c), standalone only, p.137 ("Other Disclosures")
states verbatim: **"The Company has granted loans to related parties (as defined under Companies Act, 2013)
and comply with the provisions of Companies Act, 2013."** This IS a disclosure within the Notes to Financial
Statements (not only CARO, as Pass 1's framing implied) that acknowledges a related-party loan exists.
Pass 1's substantive conclusion stands and is not weakened: this sentence gives no amount, no counterparty
name, no rate, no tenure — the quantification gap is real — but the existence of the loan is admitted
in-Notes, not only in CARO. Separately noted: this specific item does not appear at all in the consolidated
Note 35.23 equivalent (p.176), which is unsurprising since an intra-group loan would eliminate on
consolidation and the Group cannot "loan to a related party" that is itself part of the Group — not treated
as a fresh inconsistency.

## NEW FINDING 9 (minor) — Parent-level land purchase, not previously highlighted 🟢

Standalone/consolidated PPE Note 2(i) gross block shows "Land" rising from Rs 80.51 lakh (FY25 close) to Rs

separate from and additional to the subsidiary's capex programme discussed in Pass 1's CWIP section —
capacity expansion appears to be happening at the parent level too, not solely at SDPL. Informational, not a
red flag.

## NEW FINDING 10 (minor) — Parent-level supplier advances for capital goods fell even as capex additions
rose 🟢

Standalone Note 6 (Other Non-Current Assets) — "Advance to suppliers against capital goods" — fell from Rs
125.11 lakh (FY25) to Rs 53.16 lakh (FY26) at the parent level, even as parent CWIP additions of Rs 928.91
lakh and the Rs 73.32 lakh land addition (Finding 9) were made this year. This is the opposite pattern to the
subsidiary, whose capital-goods advances rose sharply (Pass 1: consolidated Rs 372.83 lakh vs standalone Rs
53.16 lakh, i.e. c.Rs 319.67 lakh sits at SDPL). Minor working-capital-quality colour: parent capex appears to
be settled more directly this year rather than through rising supplier advances.

## NEW FINDING 11 (minor) — FVTPL peer-equity holdings mostly lost value this year, a small
quality-of-earnings note on Other Income 🟢

Note 3's quoted-equity FVTPL peer holdings (small stakes in listed chemical companies) mostly declined in
FY26: Epigral Ltd -59% (Rs 0.17 lakh → Rs 0.07 lakh), Kiri Industries -45% (Rs 0.62 lakh → Rs 0.34 lakh),
Bhageria Industries -11%, Bodal Chemicals -17%, Meghmani Organics -33%; only Shree Pushkar Chemicals rose
(+11%). Correspondingly, Other Income's "Revaluation of Investment as per Ind AS" line (Note 28) fell from a
Rs 20.64 lakh gain (FY25) to a Rs 2.34 lakh gain (FY26) — a c.Rs 18.3 lakh smaller mark-to-market tailwind
this year (under 4% of PBT, immaterial in isolation, but relevant colour given Other Income overall rose
sharply on the new FX gain that Pass 1 already flagged).

## Calibration note (not a new finding) — the Financial Ratios note's "reason" column is not uniformly wrong

Pass 1 flagged the DSCR "reason" (Note 35.22) as factually contradicting the company's own borrowings
figures. Re-checking the other reason entries: the Debt-Equity Ratio reason ("Increase in Equity due to
issue of CCD," consolidated) and the ROE reason ("Increase in Equity due to conversion of CCD into Equity,"
both statements) are directionally correct and consistent with Note 15/16's equity movements. The DSCR
reason error is specific to that one row (and the associated ROE "2.48" transcription Pass 1 already
identified), not evidence that the whole ratios note is unreliable. Included so the accounting-quality score
does not over-generalise from the one row.
