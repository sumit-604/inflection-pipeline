## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

### 2A. Demand environment

ALLCARGO's own quarter-by-quarter macro citations show **deceleration, not an accelerating boom**,
across the exact period Sical's FY26 results cover: e-way bill YoY growth cited by Allcargo management
was +21% (Sept-2025, Nov-2025 call) → +23.6% (Dec-2025, Feb-2026 call) → +13% (Mar-2026, May-2026 call).
GDP growth forecasts cited by the same management also stepped down from ~7.3% (Feb-2026 call, citing
FY27 budget-era optimism) to ~6.5% IMF FY27 forecast (May-2026 call), with explicit "cautious on the
near-term outlook due to the current geopolitical scenario" language in the Q4 FY26 call. On the
demand side specifically, Allcargo's Consultative Logistics growth went from "muted" in Q3 FY26 because
"certain e-commerce customers deferred their expansion plans" (Ketan Kulkarni, Feb-2026) to recovering
in Q4. None of this amounts to an industry-wide demand boom narrative matching Sical's buoyant framing
of mining/coal, rail, and warehousing demand; if anything the closest available current-period peer
shows softening macro momentum and at least one instance of a peer's own customers deferring capex
during the same period Sical claims strong secular tailwinds. This complicates, rather than confirms,
Sical's demand framing — noted as informative peer silence/divergence per the protocol rule.

### 2B. Pricing and input costs

Both peers show margin expansion being driven predominantly by **cost discipline and selective
customer/price management, not pure volume-led operating leverage** — Allcargo explicitly: SG&A
"has actually shrunk instead of growing" even as revenue grows (Ravi Jakhar, Nov-2025); Express
segment EBITDA improved despite declining/flat revenue via "cost optimization... rationalize margins
and costs" and deliberately "let go a few customers" (Aug-2025, Feb-2026 calls); a 10.2% Express price
hike effective Jan-2026. GDL similarly took calibrated, below-competitor price increases (Rs 1,000/TEU
NCR, smaller than CONCOR's hike) to protect volume/margin balance rather than maximize either alone.
This is directionally consistent with Sical's own FY26 pattern of EBITDA margin expansion amid a
markedly weaker pre-exceptional profit picture (per B05) — suggesting margin-management-via-cost-
discipline, rather than genuine demand-led pricing power, may be a sector-wide phenomenon in FY25-26
Indian logistics, not something unique to Sical's execution.

### 2C. Capex cycle

Allcargo (current) is explicitly **retrenching toward an asset-light, deleveraging capex posture**:
warehouse space under management actually declined slightly over the period (8.4 mn sq ft Sept-2025 →
8.1 mn Dec-2025 → 8.0 mn Mar-2026); FY27 tech budget capped at Rs 12 cr; OU modernization capex (Rs
10-15 cr) explicitly deferred; management states "we are not a CAPEX-led model, we are working on an
OPEX model" and prioritizes deleveraging alongside modest network expansion. GDL (2021, stale) was in
an expansion phase (satellite terminals ~Rs 200 cr, Snowman ~Rs 400 cr pallet-capacity program) but
that reflects a different macro/rate environment five years removed and cannot be used as a current
read. Taken together with 2A/2B, the one genuinely current-period peer in this set is a **cautious
capex actor in a demand-decelerating environment**, while Sical is simultaneously executing a large,
long-duration capex-heavy order book (SECL Rs 3,422 cr / 140 months) and MMLP terminal construction.
This reads as a **lone-expander situation** for Sical relative to its closest current peer — though the
comparison is only partial given Allcargo (asset-light 3PL/express) and Sical (asset-heavy mining
logistics/terminal infrastructure) are not directly comparable business models, so this should be
weighted as a general capex-cycle divergence signal, not a precise peer benchmark.

### 2D. Competitive mentions

No peer mentions Sical Logistics by name or by unmistakable description in any of the six transcripts
reviewed. Confirmed absent across all four Allcargo calls and both GDL calls.

### 2E. Risks peers discuss that Sical does not

- **Customer-concentration disclosure**: Allcargo voluntarily discloses top-10 customer concentration
  by segment (~50%+ in Consultative Logistics, ~20% in Express — Ketan Kulkarni, Nov-2025). Sical's own
  B05 flags PSU/SECL order-book concentration as a risk but Sical itself does not publish a comparable
  quantified concentration metric for any segment — a disclosure-quality gap by contrast.
- **Customer capex-deferral risk on the demand side**: Allcargo explicitly experienced e-commerce
  customers deferring expansion plans in Q3 FY26 (Feb-2026 call) — a demand-side risk to warehousing/3PL
  growth that Sical's own materials, per B05, do not address for its newest growth segment.
- **Diesel/fuel pass-through failure specifically in CFS**: GDL's 2021 CFS-segment experience (2E/Q6
  above) shows escalation clauses can fail to protect margins in the CFS sub-segment specifically —
  Sical's AR names diesel/power as a general risk but (per B05) does not disclose CFS-segment-specific
  pass-through mechanics or performance.
- **Working-capital drag from upfront rail-linked-infrastructure payment terms**: GDL notes rail
  operators pay Indian Railways haulage charges upfront while extending customer credit of 15-30 days,
  creating structural working-capital drag as rail volumes scale (Sachin Bhanushali, Oct-2021) — a
  capital-intensity risk relevant to Sical's rail-linked MMLP ambitions that is not addressed in Sical's
  own materials per B05.
- **Explicit refusal of margin-dilutive volume ("bad business")**: GDL's management explicitly refuses
  tenders/customers who "go for the lowest price in market," accepting slower volume growth to protect
  margin (Prem Kishan Gupta, Oct-2021) — a direct analog to Sical's own AR-stated aggressive-bidding
  risk, framed here as something peers actively manage against rather than passively disclose.

## PART 5: CROSS-PEER HYPOTHESIS

**Hypothesis**: Across the combined peer set — despite covering different sub-segments (express/3PL vs.
rail/ICD/cold-chain) and different five-year-apart periods — both Allcargo and GDL independently show
the same underlying playbook: EBITDA/margin expansion is being achieved by *substituting* selective
cost/customer/price discipline for genuine volume-led operating leverage, precisely because top-line
volume growth has structurally slowed or plateaued (Allcargo: Express tonnage "hovering around the 3
lakh tonnes mark since... many years" per an analyst's own observation, Feb-2026 call, with management
not disputing it; GDL: explicit refusal of "bad business" volume to protect per-TEU margin, Oct-2021).
Neither peer frames this as a sector-wide phenomenon — each discusses only their own numbers — but the
combined pattern suggests Indian organized logistics operators, when volume growth decelerates
(consistent with Allcargo's own e-way-bill deceleration citations across FY26, 2A above), pivot to
margin-per-unit/mix management as the primary lever, rather than genuine demand-led expansion. This is
testable against Sical specifically: if Sical's own FY26 EBITDA margin expansion is similarly a
mix/cost-discipline story rather than a genuine new-volume story, its underlying segment volumes
(mining-logistics tonnage, CFS TEU throughput, MMLP rake traffic) should show deceleration or plateauing
even as blended margin metrics improve — a pattern that would mirror both peers' own disclosed dynamics
and that stage 11/13 synthesis should check against Sical's segment-level volume disclosures (not just
its headline EBITDA margin) before crediting FY26 margin expansion as durable, demand-led improvement.
