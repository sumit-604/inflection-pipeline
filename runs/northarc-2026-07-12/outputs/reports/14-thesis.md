## NARRATIVE (plain English)

Northern Arc is a genuinely improving lender priced as if the improvement is already banked. The
business is doing the right things: loan book up 22%, margins widening, return on assets back above the
NBFC benchmark, and net bad loans under one percent through four credit cycles. The FTTCP scored it +4
of 8, a deep watch that leans toward buying on dips. But leaning toward buying on dips is not the same
as buying now. At Rs 325 the stock trades at 1.35 times book while the math says a fair multiple for a
lender earning 13.5% on equity against a 14.5% cost of equity is below one times book. Put plainly, the
market is paying about 40% more than the recovery is worth, and every valuation method — theoretical
P/B, the RRM-adjusted exit PE, even the generous additive PE — lands below the 25% return hurdle. The
probability-weighted return from here is roughly plus one percent a year.

The single thing that decides this stock is credit cost, and it is a shared catalyst: the same
normalisation that would lift return on equity is the same thing that props up the asset-quality band.
If it re-accelerates above 3.2% with bad loans past 1.5%, both supports fail together. Provision
coverage has already slipped to about 44%, which means part of the recent improvement was buffer
drawdown rather than genuine healing, and management has quietly diluted its FY27 return targets across
three straight calls. None of this makes the company a reject — it is a quality-watch, an AA- lender
the market has already found. It simply is not a buy at this price. The disciplined move is to wait for
the Rs 153-173 entry zone and to re-check the next two quarters of asset quality before acting, and to
withdraw the zone entirely if the price only fell because credit cost broke.
