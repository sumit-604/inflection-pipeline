Real growth built on one customer

Millworks makes precision machined components for defence, railways, semiconductor equipment and aerospace customers. It is a contract manufacturer, build to print and build to spec. Revenue was Rs 22 Cr in FY25 and Rs 149 Cr in FY26. PAT reached Rs 37 Cr in FY26. The company listed on the SME board in July 2026. Only three restated years exist, FY24 to FY26, because the founders carved it out of a 2003 vintage family firm in 2021. The manifest tagged the company Pharma and CDMO. That tag is wrong. This is a precision components maker, and the honest peer set is Unimech Aerospace and Azad Engineering.

One customer, Quick Pay, was 47% of FY26 revenue. The top 5 customers were 81% and the top 10 were 92%. Defence rose to 69% of revenue in FY26 from single digits two years earlier. Millworks holds a Rs 5.75 Cr equity stake in Quick Pay, funded by converting a loan into shares. The two share common shareholders. The RHP keeps Quick Pay outside the related party rules. The growth is real. It is one customer deep.

The emerging moat scan found nothing durable. Two of 21 categories reached moderate, and both are non exclusive: a manufacturing certification any AS9100D peer can hold, and a defence indigenisation tailwind every peer shares. Unit IV springs sat at trial run at year end, zero commercial output. The Rs 61 Cr machinery capex had no purchase orders placed at the RHP date.

Gate 0 scored 81 of 160 and classified the company AVOID. That is a backward looking quality score, not an investment call. Cash caps the score. Operating cash flow was plus Rs 0.65 Cr in FY24, minus Rs 2.92 Cr in FY25, and minus Rs 10.76 Cr in FY26. PBT was plus Rs 50 Cr in FY26 the same year cash flow was minus Rs 11 Cr. Trade receivables reached Rs 139 Cr, 93% of FY26 revenue. Days sales outstanding ran about 340 on the ageing table. The company held zero doubtful debt provision across all three years.

The cash gap is specific to this company, not a growth tax every fast grower pays. Collection on part of the FY26 receivables depends on Quick Pay receiving its own funding first. The 20 times receivables jump tracks the Quick Pay concentration. The company never discloses the Quick Pay specific receivable balance, so the exact link is inferred, not confirmed.

Management credibility graded C. One guidance item delivered, two partial, zero missed, on a thin first filing base. The excuse pattern leans on outside blame: the 93% receivables figure is explained by geopolitics, which does not address the Quick Pay collection dependency in the same document. The one quantified forward number, an FY27 working capital table, implies two different revenue growth rates by method, 133% and 89%, unreconciled. Governance was rebuilt in the eight months before listing, with a new external company secretary and two new independent directors. The compliance lapses those hires should prevent kept worsening into calendar 2026.

Accounting quality graded 3 of 10. A 13 line restatement cut net worth 21.4% in FY24. A Section 185 self dealing loan to director promoters repeated across FY24 and FY25, with a compounding application filed. V3 Technologies, a promoter partnership, is at once landlord, vendor, customer, lender, guarantor and a disclosed competitor. The MSME payment disallowance rose about 10 times a year to Rs 10 Cr in FY26. Bank drawing power discrepancies recurred across three years and two lenders.

Peers confirm the macro and contradict the company story. All three peers describe a real sector capex and demand upcycle through FY26 and FY27. None matches the receivable spike: Apsis collects in about 45 days and cut its top customer from 52% to 35%, Airfloa improved collection by 24 days. Millworks moved the opposite way. The market runway is large. India outsourced precision components sit near Rs 86,000 Cr on the conservative TAM, with a five year reachable market near Rs 319 Cr. Management's FY27 implied revenue of about Rs 346 Cr exceeds even that five year ceiling, and the capex cannot physically support it in one year.
