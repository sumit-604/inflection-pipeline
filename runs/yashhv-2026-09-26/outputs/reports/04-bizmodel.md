## SECTION 1: THE BUSINESS MODEL IN PLAIN ENGLISH

### 1A. One-line description
Yash Highvoltage makes transformer bushings, the small but irreplaceable insulated
exit-and-entry sleeve every power transformer needs to carry high-voltage current
safely in and out of its tank, and sells them, built to each customer's spec, to
transformer OEMs, utilities and industrial power users in India and 60+ countries
(AR p.9, p.68; Inv. Pres. p.23-24).

### 1B. Money flow chain, by revenue stream

### 1E. The chai-stall-uncle version
Think of a power line like a highway for electricity. Where the highway has to pass
through a wall, a tollbooth or a tunnel mouth, you need a very carefully built gate
that lets the traffic through without letting the wall catch fire. That gate is the
bushing. Yash makes those gates, custom-built for each transformer, in a few
different designs (oil-filled, resin-filled, extra-heavy-duty for huge currents).
Big customers like PGCIL and NTPC only let a supplier make these gates for them
after years of testing, so once Yash is approved, it is hard for a newcomer to
elbow in. But Yash still has to import the most advanced resin cores from Europe,
like a sweet-shop importing a key ingredient it cannot yet make at home — the new
Vadodara plant is built to fix exactly that.

### 2D. Industry lifecycle stage
Growth, policy-driven. India transformer market projected to grow from US$3bn (2025)
to US$4.47bn (2030), 8.33% CAGR; global transformer-bushing market from US$1.8bn
(2023) to US$3bn (2033) (Inv. Pres. p.36). The industry's own narrative is a
technology-shift sub-cycle within growth: OIP (mature, "slowing growth") to RIP
("fastest growing segment", global RIP share of installations projected 30%→45% by
2030, Inv. Pres. p.37). Yash sits early-to-mid in that shift, having localised RIP
technology in India over a decade ago (AR p.14) but still importing the RIP/RIS
core itself.

### 3D. Unit economics — the physics of the business

- **Define one unit**: one transformer bushing (the physical, tested, ready-to-ship
  component). AR and presentation report volumes in "units" (e.g., 10,000
  units/annum OIP capacity, AR p.68; 200 High Current Bushings executed FY26, AR
  p.68).
- **Revenue per unit**: NOT FOUND at company-wide blended level (AR discloses
  category-level revenue and select unit counts but not a blended average
  realisation across all ~15,000 combined annual units). Partial data points exist:
  High Current Bushings averaged roughly Rs10cr/200 units ≈ Rs5 lakh/unit in FY26
  vs roughly Rs6cr/80 units ≈ Rs7.5 lakh/unit in FY25 (AR p.68, calculated), showing
  realisation is not stable per unit and depends heavily on voltage/current class
  and customisation.
- **Cost per unit**: NOT FOUND at unit level (only aggregate COGS/gross margin
  disclosed).
- **Volume drivers**: capacity additions (OIP capacity doubled 6,000→10,000
  units/annum within FY26, AR p.68; Vadodara adds 6,000 RIP/RIS units/annum,
  AR p.9); order-book conversion (Rs400cr+ book, Inv. Pres. p.14).
- **Price drivers**: voltage/current class and customisation level (a 25,000A
  High Current bushing commands a materially different price than a standard OIP
  unit); export mix (US/EU customers pay for IEEE/CENELEC-compliant premium
  variants, AR p.10).
- **Cost drivers**: imported RIP/RIS core cost and lead time (12-16 week import
  cycle, Inv. Pres. p.37); resin, paper, copper/aluminium, silicone input costs;
  labour (Halol/Vadodara plants).
- **Incremental margin / operating leverage**: EBITDA margin expanded from 20.4%
  (FY24) to 25.7% (FY26) as revenue grew from Rs108.5cr to Rs235.1cr (Inv. Pres.
  p.40) — consistent with positive operating leverage on the existing asset base,
  though this expansion happened before Vadodara's fixed-cost base (depreciation,
  interest) is fully in the P&L; post-commissioning incremental margin is
  NOT FOUND (forward-looking, not in this AR).

### 4B. Valuation method applicability

| Method | Applicable? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| P/E on trailing/forward earnings | Applicable, SECONDARY | PAT is clean (B03) but mid-capex-cycle; use only as cross-check, not primary, given the ROCE/margin the company is trying to grow into |

post-Vadodara unit economics are disclosed. Cycle stage that matters for
valuation: mid-capex-cycle, pre-commissioning of the single largest capacity
addition in company history — the multiple should be assessed against the
capacity and margin profile the company is building toward, not only its FY26
trailing numbers.**
