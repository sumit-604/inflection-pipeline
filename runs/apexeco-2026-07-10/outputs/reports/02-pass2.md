## NEW FINDING 1 — Note 20(b) rupee figures do not match Annexure III's disclosed remuneration
## increase percentages for the same three individuals

Note 20(b) (p.58, Related Party Disclosure — Director's Remuneration table) discloses, in rupees:
- Mr. Anuj Dosajh: ₹32.41 lakh (FY24) → ₹87.27 lakh (FY25)
- Mr. Ramakrishnan Balasundaram Aiyer: ₹32.41 lakh (FY24) → ₹87.27 lakh (FY25)
- Mr. Ajay Raina: ₹37.49 lakh (FY24) → ₹47.59 lakh (FY25)

Computed from these note figures: Dosajh and Ramakrishnan each increased **169.3%** YoY; Raina
increased **26.9%** YoY. (Sums tie to Note 17's Director's Remuneration total of ₹222.14 lakh FY25 /
₹116.35 lakh FY24, so the underlying rupee figures in Note 20(b) are internally consistent.)

Annexure III (p.31, Section 197(12) disclosure — outside the notes but the only place the increase
percentage is stated in words) discloses the increase for the *same three individuals* as: Ajay Raina
53.35%, Ramakrishnan Balasundaram Aiyer 37.02%, Anuj Dosajh 37.02%.

Neither pairing reconciles: the two directors whose Note 20(b) rupee figures show a 169% increase
are reported in Annexure III at 37.02%, while the director whose Note 20(b) figures show a 27%
increase is reported at 53.35% — the higher percentage attaches to the smaller actual increase and
vice versa. No note anywhere explains a different remuneration base (e.g., perquisites, employer PF
contribution) that would reconcile the two figures for the same named individuals.

This materially amplifies Pass 1's Finding #5 (disproportionate managerial pay growth in the IPO
year): the true increase for the MD and one Executive Director, per the company's own related-party
note, may be closer to 169% rather than the 37-39% average cited in the statutory remuneration
annexure. 🔴 Red Flag — cross-document numerical inconsistency (Note 20(b), p.58 vs Annexure
III, p.31); a specific, quantified question for management on which figure is correct and why.

## NEW FINDING 2 — Undisclosed Bank of India CC account debit balance of ₹665.43 lakh with no
## matching borrowings-note entry

Note 11 (p.55, Cash and Bank Balances) lists, only for FY25 with a blank FY24 comparative: "Bank of
India CC Account Debit Balance 665.43." This is ~11% of total assets (₹5,968.68 lakh) and roughly a
quarter of total cash and cash equivalents (₹1,843.96 lakh). No cash credit/overdraft facility appears
anywhere in Note 4 (Borrowings), which lists only five individually-secured vehicle loans and shows
Debt-Equity of 0.00. There is no disclosure of the CC account's sanctioned limit, security, interest
rate, or bank relationship terms. Separately within the same note, "Balances with banks — in current
accounts" fell from ₹179.41 lakh (FY24) to ₹17.78 lakh (FY25), -90%, even as total cash rose 2.8x on
IPO proceeds — i.e., operating cash shifted almost entirely into fixed deposits and this undisclosed CC
account rather than remaining in ordinary current accounts. 🟡 Watch — NOT FOUND IN DOCUMENT
(CC account limit, security, terms); a facility of this size with no corresponding Note 4 entry is a
disclosure gap (Note 11, p.55; no cross-reference in Note 4, p.52).

## NEW FINDING 3 — "Factory Building" carried at nil in the PPE schedule; Plant & Machinery net
## block immaterial for a stated manufacturer

Note 8 (p.54) lists "Factory Building" as a named asset category in the PPE roll-forward, with blank/
nil values across gross carrying value, accumulated depreciation, and net block in BOTH years.
Plant & Machinery net block is ₹0.35 lakh (FY25) / ₹0.40 lakh (FY24) — immaterial. The company is
described in the Boards' Report and MDA as engaged in "manufacturing, commissioning and O&M
of Water Treatment Plant... Effluent Treatment Plant... Zero Liquid Damage" systems, and the Boards'
Report's Technology Absorption section refers to updating "its factory for new technology." The
notes disclose no owned factory building asset and negligible plant & machinery, which is consistent
with Pass 1's broader observation of an asset-light EPC execution model (Note 8, capex of only
₹10.08 lakh against ₹7,095.53 lakh revenue) but the explicit "Factory Building" line item carried at nil
sharpens the question of whether the company owns, leases, or subcontracts its manufacturing
premises — a point the notes do not address (no leasehold improvement or right-of-use disclosure,
consistent with IGAAP exemption from Ind AS 116, but a leased-factory rent line is also not separately
identified within Note 19's generic "Rent, Rates & Taxes" of ₹31.51 lakh). 🟡 Watch (Note 8, p.54;
cross-ref Boards' Report Technology Absorption, p.27).

## NEW FINDING 4 — Disputed DLF receivable balance moved unexplained between years, and Note
## 25's principal figure does not match Note 30's ageing table to the rupee

Note 30's Trade Receivables ageing (p.64) shows the ">3 Year" "Disputed Trade Receivables
considered good" bucket (the DLF Home Developers matter, per Note 25 item 2) at **₹11.89 lakh**
(FY25) versus **₹15.51 lakh** (FY24) — a ₹3.62 lakh decrease in an actively litigated, unresolved
receivable where Note 25 states the matter "is pending as on 31 March 2025" with no settlement,
partial recovery, or write-off disclosed anywhere. Separately, Note 25's own narrative states "the
above amount of Rs. 11.88 Lakhs is included in Trade Receivable 'Others'" — but Note 30's ageing
table shows ₹11.89 lakh, a ₹0.01 lakh rupee mismatch between the two notes describing the same
balance, and the amount sits in the ">3 Year" disputed bucket rather than the "Others" line item
Note 25 says it belongs to (Note 10's "Others" total is only ₹13.39 lakh, which is close to but not
reconciled against the ₹11.89/11.88 lakh figure either). No note explains the FY24-to-FY25 movement
or the inter-note rupee difference. 🟡 Watch — unexplained receivable movement and note-to-note
tie-out gap on a disputed, litigated asset (Note 30, p.64; Note 25 item 2, p.60; Note 10, p.55).

## NEW FINDING 5 — Note 1(i)'s deferred tax asset narrative does not match Note 23's table

Note 1(i) (Material Accounting Policies — Deferred Taxes, p.50) states: "deferred tax asset is created
having closing balance of Rs. 12.20 Lakhs (Rs. 12.05 Lakhs in previous year) on account [of] effect of
WDV of Property, Plant and Equipment, disallowed items and carried forward of losses." The FY24
comparative (₹12.05 lakh) ties correctly to Note 23's Fixed Assets DTA closing balance for FY24. But
the FY25 closing figure in Note 1(i) — ₹12.20 lakh — does not match Note 23's detailed table, which
shows the Fixed Assets DTA closing balance at **₹12.63 lakh** for FY25 (before the separately-
disclosed ₹20.41 lakh gratuity DTA component that brings the balance-sheet total to ₹33.04 lakh).
A ₹0.43 lakh unreconciled difference between the accounting-policy note's narrative and the detailed
note's table — immaterial in size but a genuine cross-note tie-out failure and a minor drafting/
proofing quality signal. 🟡 Watch (Note 1i, p.50 vs Note 23, p.59).

## NEW FINDING 6 — Gratuity expense is embedded in "Salaries, wages and bonus" with no separate
## P&L disclosure, compounding the actuarial-assumptions gap already flagged in Pass 1

Note 17 (p.57) labels the line item "Salaries, wages and bonus (Incl. gratuity)" — meaning the annual
gratuity charge (service cost, interest cost, and any actuarial gain/loss under the AS-15 valuation
referenced in Note 1l) is folded into the aggregate salaries line and never separately quantified
anywhere in the Statement of Profit and Loss. Combined with Pass 1's finding that no actuarial
assumptions (discount rate, salary escalation, mortality table, funded status) are disclosed in Note 5
or Note 1(l), this means a reader cannot isolate the current year's true gratuity cost or actuarial
movement from the financial statements at all — only the balance-sheet provision roll-forward
(₹66.16 lakh → ₹81.08 lakh, +22.5%, per Note 5) is inferable, and that roll-forward itself conflates
service cost, interest cost, actuarial losses, and benefits paid into a single net movement. 🟡 Watch
— extends Pass 1's Finding #9 with a more specific P&L-disclosure gap (Note 17, p.57; cross-ref Note
5, p.52 and Note 1l, p.50).

## NEW FINDING 7 — Company Secretary turnover: three CS changes within roughly 17 months
## spanning the IPO window

Note 20(a) (p.58, Related Party Disclosure — KMP list) discloses: "Company Secretary (w.e.f.
03/04/2024) Ms. Kirti Jain" and "Company Secretary (From 02/03/2024 to 02/04/2024) Ms. Nidhi
Sharma" — i.e., Ms. Sharma held the role for barely one month immediately before the IPO
preparation period, replaced by Ms. Jain. Cross-referencing Corporate Information (p.4, outside the
notes but naming the same role), Ms. Jain herself resigned effective 30 June 2025, replaced by Ms.
Vishakha Rani w.e.f. 25 August 2025. That is three Company Secretaries in approximately 17 months
bracketing the IPO (December 2024) and the first post-listing AGM cycle. The notes do not comment
on the reason for either change. 🟡 Watch — governance/compliance-continuity observation not
raised in Pass 1 (Note 20a, p.58).
