## NEW FINDING 1 — Non-operating "Other income" is now a material share of profit; includes an unflagged one-time step-acquisition gain

Consolidated Other income (Note 26, consolidated, p.285) rose to ₹26.77 Cr (267.73 Mn) in FY25 from ₹11.27 Cr (112.71 Mn) in FY24, +137.4% YoY — materially outpacing the 62.6% consolidated PBT growth. This is now **22.6% of consolidated PBT** (₹26.77 Cr / ₹118.42 Cr, up from 18.9% of FY24 PBT). Two components drive this, neither examined in Pass 1:

- **Interest income on bank deposits** ₹23.55 Cr (235.54 Mn), up 150.8% YoY (₹9.39 Cr in FY24) — mechanically explained by IPO/QIP proceeds parked in fixed deposits (per Notes 14(b)/35/36 standalone, Notes 14(b)/36/37 consolidated), i.e. non-operating and expected to shrink as proceeds are deployed or run down.
- **"Gain on re-measurement of investment in Associate" ₹2.275 Cr (22.75 Mn), FY25 only, nil FY24** (Note 26, consolidated, p.285) — this is the Ind AS 103 step-acquisition remeasurement gain on the Company's pre-existing 45% Span Across stake when it was stepped up to fair value on obtaining control (30-Sep-2024). It is a genuine one-off, non-cash gain (also visible as a deduction in the Cash Flow Statement adjustments, p.256) and is not broken out as an "exceptional item" anywhere in the P&L presentation.

Standalone Other income (Note 25, p.227) is ₹24.51 Cr (245.11 Mn) vs ₹11.27 Cr FY24 — the ₹2.26 Cr gap to the consolidated figure is almost entirely this one-time associate remeasurement gain, confirming it sits only at the consolidated level. 🟡 Watch — when normalising FY25 profit growth, roughly a fifth of consolidated PBT is either non-operating interest income (temporary, tied to unutilised IPO/QIP cash) or a non-recurring fair-value gain; Pass 1's profit-growth discussion did not net this out.

## NEW FINDING 2 — Incentive/cash-back expense is not in "Consumption of cards"; Pass 1 mis-located it

Pass 1 stated that cashback/incentive costs are "recorded as a distinct expense line ('Consumption of cards', Note 26 standalone p.227 / Note 27 consolidated)." This is incorrect. **Note 26 standalone / Note 27 consolidated "Consumption of cards" is only ₹1.70 Cr (17.00 Mn)** — physical prepaid-card stock consumption (opening 3.58 + purchases 16.73 − closing 3.31), immaterial to the P&L.

The actual incentive/cashback expense line is **"Incentive / cash back" inside Other Expenses**: ₹359.87 Cr (3,598.72 Mn) standalone FY25 vs ₹216.83 Cr (2,168.27 Mn) FY24, +65.9% YoY (Note 30, standalone, p.227; Note 31, consolidated, p.287 — identical figures both bases). This is **27.6% of standalone revenue (₹1,302.65 Cr)** and by far the largest single expense line in the P&L (larger than total employee costs of ₹65.60 Cr). Growth of 65.9% is broadly in line with Propel/gift-card revenue growth (+70.9%), so the trend itself is not anomalous, but the correct location and true scale of this cost matters for anyone modelling the gross-vs-net revenue presentation Pass 1 flagged in Section 1. Total standalone Other Expenses grew to ₹442.02 Cr (4,420.15 Mn) from ₹272.78 Cr (2,727.82 Mn), +62.0% YoY — also not separately sized in Pass 1. 🟡 Watch (correction to Pass 1, material for margin analysis).

The **"technology and product development" budget is only 35.6% deployed after roughly 2.5 years** — the slowest-utilised category and directly relevant to the transition-alpha/product-investment thesis Pass 1 flagged as a margin-mix concern (Section 11 finding).

**QIP proceeds (Note 36, standalone, p.232 / Note 37, consolidated, p.292)** — raised Dec-2024, net ₹574.14 Cr (5,741.37 Mn) at ₹523.20/share. As at Mar-25 (only ~3 months later): **₹83.68 Cr (836.78 Mn, 14.6%) utilised** — explicitly stated as "towards repayment of certain borrowings, strategic investment (acquisition of shares in associate), general corporate purpose"; **₹490.46 Cr (4,904.60 Mn, 85.4%) unutilised**, parked in deposits and a monitoring account. This is the first explicit documentary confirmation that QIP proceeds — not operating cash flow — directly funded part of the Span Across/Mobileware stake purchases (the "strategic investment (acquisition of shares in associate)" line), reinforcing the Pass 1 point that the Span Across acquisition sits alongside a rapid, capital-raise-funded deleveraging rather than being self-funded from the core business. 🟡 Watch — slow deployment of the technology-development IPO budget specifically, and confirmation that recent M&A was equity-funded rather than organically funded.

## NEW FINDING 4 — Fair value hierarchy: unquoted subsidiary/associate equity stakes classified as "Level 2," not "Level 3"

In the Financial Instruments notes (Note 39, standalone, p.235; Note 39, consolidated, p.295), the unquoted equity investments — Span Across (98.32% subsidiary stake, standalone-only since eliminated on consolidation) and Mobileware Technologies (38.91% associate, both bases) — are classified as **"Level 2"** fair-value inputs (₹79.69 Cr standalone / ₹22.90 Cr consolidated as at Mar-25). Level 2 requires observable market inputs (directly or indirectly); unquoted, closely-held private-company shares with no active market would ordinarily be expected to sit in **Level 3** (unobservable inputs, e.g. DCF or comparable-multiple valuations) unless the disclosed fair value is simply recent transaction cost used as a practical-expedient proxy — no methodology is disclosed either way. This is a disclosure-quality point not raised in Pass 1. 🟡 Watch — worth a management question on what observable inputs support the Level 2 classification.

## NEW FINDING 5 — A dormant foreign subsidiary present in the FY24 group structure has silently disappeared from the FY25 group structure with no explanation anywhere in the notes

The **FY24 comparative** Schedule III disclosure (Note 47, consolidated, p.305) lists: "Subsidiary incorporated outside India: **Zaggle Technologies Limited**" with 0.00% / nil across net assets, profit/(loss), OCI and total comprehensive income for FY24 — i.e., a wholly dormant/shell foreign subsidiary existed as part of the group as at 31-Mar-2024.

The **FY25** Schedule III table (Note 47, consolidated, p.305) contains **no such line** — only "Parent," "Subsidiary incorporated in India: Span Across," "Non-controlling interest," and "Associates: Mobileware." Corroborating this, **Note 1, Corporate Information (consolidated, p.259)** explicitly states: "The Parent Company together with its subsidiary and its associate... hereinafter, collectively referred to as the 'Group' and **it has one subsidiary and one associate entity as of Reporting date March 31, 2025**." No mention of Zaggle Technologies Limited, its disposal, strike-off, or deconsolidation appears anywhere else in the standalone or consolidated notes read (investments, RPT, subsequent events, or elsewhere). Financially immaterial given nil FY24 balances, but this is an unexplained change in group structure with zero narrative disclosure — a governance/transparency gap worth a direct management question (what happened to this entity, and why no disclosure of its exit). 🟡 Watch.

## NEW FINDING 6 — A consolidated-only contingent liability item (subsidiary-specific GST interest demand) exists that Pass 1 assumed away

Pass 1 stated: "Consolidated contingent liabilities note not separately re-extracted line-by-line in this pass... expected to be substantively identical to standalone since the tax disputes relate to the parent entity." This assumption is **not fully correct**. Consolidated Note 32(a)(iv) (p.288) discloses an item specific to the subsidiary: **"Subsidiary company received demand orders for interest on GST for FY 2017-18 and FY 2018-19 amounting to ₹0.57 Mn (₹0.057 Cr) from Assistant Commissioner, Department of Trade and Taxes. The same is currently pending with Department of Trade and Taxes."** This explains the small, previously unreconciled gap between the consolidated GST contingent liability total (₹34.96 Mn) and the standalone figure (₹34.39 Mn) — a pre-acquisition-vintage tax exposure inherited with Span Across. Immaterial in quantum but a factual correction to a Pass 1 assumption. 🟢 Clean/minor.

## NEW FINDING 7 — Minor correction: which ESOP tranche was actually repriced

Pass 1 described the ESOP tranches as "Type I re-priced to ₹1, Type II ₹164." Re-reading Note 46 (standalone, p.242-243) / Note 46 (consolidated, p.301-302) in full: **Type I options were granted at ₹1.00 from inception in FY 2022-23 and were never repriced.** It was **Type II that was originally granted at ₹271.00 and was subsequently repriced down to ₹164.00** (the top of the IPO price band) via shareholder approval dated 11-Dec-2023, generating an incremental Ind AS 102 fair-value charge of ₹51.31 recognised in FY24. Pass 1's phrasing reversed which tranche was repriced. 🟢 Clean/minor, factual correction only.
