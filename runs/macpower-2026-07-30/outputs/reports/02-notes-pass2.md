## NEW FINDINGS BY NOTE

**Statement of Cash Flow (p.132) — capex acceleration not quantified in Pass 1**
Purchase of fixed assets: ₹1,597.77 lakh (FY25) vs ₹1,054.70 lakh (FY24), **+51.5% YoY** (Cash Flow Statement,
p.132). Pass 1 discussed the CWIP balance (₹485.84 lakh, Note 3) but did not quantify the cash capex trend. This
is consistent with — and a fresh confirming anchor for — the factory-building CWIP build (₹475.53 lakh in
progress, Note 3 p.158) and plausibly the post-fire Unit-2 rebuild, though the report does not itself link the two.
🟡 Watch — capex growing far faster than revenue, worth tracking against the fire-related capex needs into FY26.

**Statement of Cash Flow (p.132) — vehicle borrowing gross flows exceed net balance**
Proceeds from Borrowings ₹123.96 lakh and Repayment of Borrowings ₹(102.37) lakh in FY25 (Cash Flow
Statement, p.132), net ₹21.59 lakh, reconciling to the Note 15 Vehicle Loan closing balance of ₹21.58 lakh. The
gross proceeds/repayment figures (not shown in Note 15 itself) imply more than one vehicle loan was drawn
and substantially repaid within the year — a minor observation, immaterial in quantum. 🟢 Clean/informational.

**Note 23, Other Income (p.159) — two items Pass 1 did not extract**
1. Total Other Income collapsed **-73.9% YoY**: ₹35.55 lakh (FY25) vs ₹136.36 lakh (FY24) (Note 23, p.159). Driver:
   Interest Income on Other Deposits fell from ₹58.64 to ₹16.38 lakh (-72.1%); Profit on sale of Investments fell
   from ₹46.65 to ₹1.35 lakh (-97.1%); Cash Discount fell from ₹18.15 to ₹0.37 lakh (-98%). This is the direct P&L
   mirror of the investment-portfolio liquidation Pass 1 flagged as Finding #5 (Note 8) but Pass 1 did not quantify
   the Other Income impact itself. 🟡 Watch — a recurring income stream has been permanently reduced now that
   the ₹516.16 lakh investment book stands at zero.
2. "Notional Interest on Interest Free loan to employee at EIR" jumped to **₹5.16 lakh (FY25) from ₹0.95 lakh
   (FY24)**, a 5.4x increase (Note 23, p.159). This is the accounting recognition of imputed interest income on
   interest-free staff loans/advances (Note 12 shows Loans to Employees + Advances to Employees growing
   ₹72.19 lakh vs ₹46.78 lakh, +54.3%, see below). It is not possible from the document to isolate how much of
   this notional interest specifically relates to the CFO's ₹10.10 lakh "Loan Given" balance disclosed in Note 35
   (p.169), but the growth is directionally consistent with and adds a fresh anchor to Pass 1's Finding #4 (the
   Note 12/Note 35 loans-to-KMP contradiction). 🟡 Watch.

**Note 12, Loans (p.151) — aggregate employee lending growth not previously quantified**
Total Loans & Advances to employees ₹72.19 lakh (FY25) vs ₹46.78 lakh (FY24), **+54.3%** (Loans to Employees
₹44.56 vs ₹27.41, +62.6%; Advance to Employees ₹15.88 vs ₹3.79, +319%). Pass 1 only referenced this note to
establish the Nil related-party-loan contradiction (Finding #4); it did not flag the broader employee-loan-book
growth trend, which is a supporting data point for that same governance question. 🟡 Watch.

**Note 26, Employee Benefit Expenses (p.160) — cost growth far outpacing revenue, not discussed in Pass 1**
Total Employee Benefit Expenses ₹2,981.07 lakh (FY25) vs ₹2,298.64 lakh (FY24), **+29.7% YoY**, against revenue
growth of only +8.6% (Note 26, p.160; Note 22, p.159). Sub-lines: Salaries and wages +28.9% (₹2,693.05 vs
₹2,089.77), Contribution to PF and other funds +38.6% (₹178.74 vs ₹128.96), Staff welfare expenses +36.7%
(₹109.28 vs ₹79.91). Pass 1 covered the RPT-specific KMP salary/fee movements (Kishor Kikani +43.8%, Nikesh
Mehta professional fees +20%) but did not surface that this pattern extends to the entire employee base. 🟡
Watch — a genuine new margin-quality question: staff cost inflation running more than 3x revenue growth.

**Note 28, Other Expenses (p.160) — opex growth far outpacing revenue, not discussed in Pass 1**
Total Other Expenses ₹2,731.14 lakh (FY25) vs ₹1,978.03 lakh (FY24), **+38.1% YoY** vs revenue +8.6% (Note 28,
p.160). Largest unexplained sub-line movements: Miscellaneous Expense ₹587.41 vs ₹298.11 lakh (**+97.0%**,
nearly doubled, no breakdown given); Job Work Exp. ₹946.55 vs ₹724.09 lakh (+30.7%); Transportation Exp.
₹387.34 vs ₹264.96 lakh (+46.2%); Brokerage & Commission Exp. ₹110.31 vs ₹89.35 lakh (+23.5%). None of these
line-item movements were in Pass 1. 🔴 Red Flag / 🟡 Watch — Miscellaneous Expense nearly doubling with zero
narrative explanation, alongside a >30% total opex growth against 8.6% revenue growth, is a genuine cost-
structure concern for margin durability that Pass 1 missed entirely (Pass 1's margin discussion was limited to
the ratio table's headline net-margin figure).

**Cross-reference: Note 24 (Cost of Material Consumed) vs Note 7 (Inventory) — mechanical driver of margin
improvement, not identified as a cross-note linkage in Pass 1**
Cost of Material Consumed **fell -15.2%** (₹14,743.67 lakh FY25 vs ₹17,396.12 lakh FY24, Note 24, p.159) even
as revenue grew +8.6% — the arithmetic driver is not efficiency but the ₹10,096.04 lakh closing raw-material
stock (up from ₹6,690.88 lakh, Note 7, already flagged by Pass 1 as a standalone working-capital item, but not
linked to the P&L margin line). Net purchases were roughly flat YoY (₹18,148.83 lakh vs ₹18,238.53 lakh, -0.5%).
When Cost of Material Consumed is combined with the Changes in Inventories line (Note 25, p.160: expense of
₹1,571.22 lakh FY25 vs an income/credit of ₹(1,101.86) lakh FY24, driven mostly by the fire-related finished-
goods collapse), the combined materials + inventory-movement charge is **₹16,314.89 lakh (FY25) vs
₹16,294.26 lakh (FY24), essentially flat (+0.13%)** against revenue growth of +8.6% — i.e., the underlying
materials cost-to-revenue relationship is genuinely improving once both lines are read together, but the
standalone "Cost of Material Consumed" figure on its own overstates the improvement because it is mechanically
depressed by the raw-material build sitting on the balance sheet. 🟡 Watch — a disclosure-literacy point:
reading Note 24 in isolation (as most ratio analyses would) creates a materially rosier gross-margin picture than
the fuller cross-note reconciliation supports.

**Note 37, Financial Instrument by Category / Fair Value Hierarchy (pp.178–180) — non-recurring FVTPL gains
embedded in profit, not identified in Pass 1**
Note 37 classifies the FY24 "Investment in 11% Debenture" (₹211.04 lakh) and "Investment in Mutual Funds"
(₹305.12 lakh) as **"At Fair Value through P&L" (FVTPL)**, not amortised cost (p.180). Cross-referencing the
Cash Flow Statement (p.132), which adds back "Fair Value Loss/(Gain) on Financial Instrument" of ₹(23.13) lakh
(FY25) and ₹(9.11) lakh (FY24) as a non-cash item within operating profit, indicates that **₹23.13 lakh (FY25)
and ₹9.11 lakh (FY24) of reported profit before tax derived from unrealised/realised fair-value gains on now-
fully-liquidated investments** (Note 8 confirms the investment book is Nil at both FVTPL lines as of 31-Mar-25).
Pass 1's Section 6 stated "zero unrealised gain/loss disclosed" for investments based on Note 8's own
impairment-only disclosure line; it did not surface this FVTPL gain via the cross-reference to Note 37 and the
Cash Flow Statement. 🟡 Watch — a modest but genuine earnings-quality point: this ₹23.13 lakh non-recurring
gain source (0.9% of PBT of ₹3,463.38 lakh) is now extinguished since the investment portfolio stands at zero,
and will not repeat in FY26 absent redeployment of cash into similar instruments.

**Note 33, Lease (p.162) — maturity profile not previously extracted**
Future minimum lease rentals: within 1 year ₹35.08 lakh; 1–5 years ₹135.05 lakh; **more than 5 years ₹117.95
lakh** (FY25 total ₹288.08 lakh) vs FY24 (₹12.63 / ₹34.92 / Nil = ₹47.55 lakh). Pass 1 reported the lease liability

**Note 36, Financial Risk Management (p.170) — a second boilerplate/data inconsistency, distinct from the one
Pass 1 flagged**
The qualitative Market Risk narrative states: "The Company transacts business primarily in USD and Euro. The
Company has foreign currency trade payables **and receivables** and is therefore exposed to foreign exchange
risk" (Note 36, p.170). The quantitative Foreign Exchange Risk table immediately below (p.171) shows **Financial
Assets: Euro Nil, USD Nil in both FY25 and FY24** — i.e., no foreign-currency receivables exist in either year,
directly contradicting the qualitative claim. This is a distinct inconsistency from the Net Exposure
transposition Pass 1 already flagged (Finding #10, Pass 1 Section 12); together the two issues suggest Note 36's
narrative text was not updated to match the underlying data tables. 🟡 Watch — disclosure-quality flag,
consistent with the "unedited boilerplate" pattern Pass 1 separately identified in the gratuity Parameter-risk
note (Note 34).

**Note 36(f), Capital Risk Management / Gearing Ratio table (p.177) — not extracted in Pass 1**
Net Debt is negative in both years (net cash position): **-₹284.17 lakh (FY25) vs -₹884.17 lakh (FY24)**; Gearing
Ratio -2.03% (FY25) vs -8.02% (FY24) (Note 36(f), p.177). The net-cash cushion contracted by roughly ₹600 lakh
year over year. This is a precise, table-sourced quantification of the same liquidity-consumption trend Pass 1

citing as the company's own formal capital-management metric, not previously anchored to this specific note.
🟡 Watch.

**Note 38(vi), Ratios (p.185) — one additional ratio not discussed in Pass 1**

**Note 13(f), Promoter shareholding (p.153) — sharper detail on WHO received the CEO's transferred shares**
Pass 1 characterised the Nikesh J. Mehta stake reduction (13.41% → 8.61%, -480,006 shares) as "an intra-family
transfer" with total promoter group flat at 73.18%. The underlying allottee detail (Note 13(f), p.153) shows the
480,006 shares released were absorbed by **Reyna Rupeshbhai Mehta (+200,000 shares, 0.75% → 2.75%)** and
a **new entrant, Vincy Rupeshbhai Mehta (+280,000 shares, 0% → 2.80%)** — both identified by association
with Rupeshbhai Mehta (the Managing Director), not with Nikesh Mehta himself. Nikesh's own recorded family
holding, Mehta Nimishaben Nikeshbhai, stayed flat at 7.77% throughout. In other words, the WTD & CEO's
individually-held stake moved specifically toward the Managing Director's family branch, not toward his own
spouse's holding. This is a materially sharper framing than Pass 1's generic "intra-family transfer" characterisation
and strengthens the case for a direct question to management on rationale, timing, and whether it reflects a
succession/control realignment within the promoter group rather than routine estate planning. 🟡 Watch —
governance question sharpened, not new in kind but new in the specificity that changes its interpretation.
