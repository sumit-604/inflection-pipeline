=== OPENING REMARKS — Rajesh Loomba, Chairman & Managing Director ===
Good morning and a very warm welcome. ECOS is India's leading organized corporate managed mobility solutions provider anchored around two core segments: Employee Transportation Services (ETS) and chauffeur-driven car rentals (CCR). We serve Fortune 500, BSE 500 companies, global capability centers, IT/ITES companies, consulting firms, manufacturing and pharma companies, and a growing base of Indian enterprises and SMEs.

Q1 was a healthy quarter with operating growth. Revenue grew 16.7%, volumes increased 27%, and we added 61 new clients. At the same time, EBITDA margins were softer, reflecting higher operating costs and business mix. This has been because, in addition to our higher business mix in favor of ETS, the dynamics of the market and the pricing pressure are more intense than we anticipated, especially in ETS. We are taking all steps to keep growing profitably with the highest quality of revenue and minimum credit risk, while cutting costs and bringing in more efficiencies through technology.

We ended Q1 with presence across 151 cities in India, adding 20 new cities during the quarter, taking the domestic footprint meaningfully beyond the 130-plus cities we had reached by end of FY26. Our international network now covers more than 100 countries. We completed approximately 1.48 million trips during the quarter, up 27% year-on-year and close to 7% sequentially. ETS contributed 59% of revenue, higher than last quarter, with CCR at 41%. ETS continued to benefit from stable demand from IT/ITES customers and GCCs, while CCR saw a healthy recovery in corporate and travel related mobility demand, even though inbound international travelers are still on the lesser side.

We onboarded 61 new clients compared to 53 in Q1 FY26. Our active client base stood at 1,400 enterprise organizations during the quarter, a growth of almost 18% year-on-year. Around 51% of our revenue continues to come from customers who have been with ECOS for more than 5 years. Our owned and vendor operated vehicle network together stood at approximately 19,500 vehicles as of June 30, 2026. The vast majority of the fleet continues to come from vendor partners; we build supply in line with the requirements of the business rather than ahead of demand. Our EV fleet increased to 460 vehicles compared to 390 at the end of Q4 FY26.

In Q4 we launched our direct web booking portal, and during this quarter we completed a major upgrade of the company's proprietary technology platform designed to enhance scalability, operating efficiency, and customer experience. Our SIXT partnership is progressing as planned and we continue to build its distribution across corporate and leisure channels. We are also expecting to launch our B2C app this quarter to address the growing B2C demand for premium CCR services. We are strengthening leadership bandwidth in selected areas — deliberate investments ahead of the next phase of growth.

As we look at the rest of FY27, our priorities remain: adding high-quality enterprise relationships, increasing share of wallet with existing customers, expanding into geographies with a clear business case, strengthening leadership bandwidth, and using technology to improve efficiency. Alongside growth, our focus is firmly on improving the quality of that growth. As volume scales and the investments we have named mature, our objective is to translate the strength of the platform into better operating leverage and improving returns. Corporate mobility in India remains highly fragmented while large enterprises increasingly require partners who can deliver reliability, compliance, technology and consistent execution across locations.

=== CFO REMARKS — Hem Kumar Upadhyay, Chief Financial Officer ===
Revenue from operations for the quarter stood at INR 2,113.72 million, up 16.7% year-on-year and approximately 2.2% over Q4 FY26. EBITDA for the quarter was INR 218.47 million compared with INR 219.18 million in Q1 FY26 and INR 241.53 million in Q4 FY26. Our EBITDA margin for the quarter was 9.3% [as transcribed; reconciles to reported 10.34%] compared with 12.0% in Q1 FY26 and 11.7% in Q4 FY26.

EBITDA margin for the quarter was below the 11% to 13% which we had initially indicated for FY27. This is primarily due to higher than anticipated operating cost and pricing pressure. Given the current business dynamic, we now expect EBITDA margin for FY27 to be around 10%. We remain confident in the underlying strength of the business and expect margins to improve as volume scales and operating efficiencies begin to come through.

Employee benefit expenses for the quarter stood at INR 237.63 million, up around 20% year-on-year; for FY27 we expect employee cost to grow by around 20%, reflecting continuous strengthening of talent and leadership. Other expenses were INR 70.32 million compared to INR 81.54 million in Q1 FY26; the reduction was largely because a one-time provision for doubtful debts recorded in the previous year did not recur this quarter. Cost of service increased 20.7% year-on-year, a factor leading to lower EBITDA. Profit before tax was INR 191.64 million compared with INR 186.68 million in Q1 FY26. Profit after tax came in at INR 145.50 million compared with INR 132.87 million in Q1 FY26.

From a balance sheet perspective we continue to remain in a strong financial position with healthy cash flow, low leverage and disciplined capital allocation. As of June 30, 2026, our cash and investments stood at INR 1,558 million. The board has recommended a final dividend of INR 2.38 per equity share for FY26, subject to shareholder approval at the upcoming AGM. Our focus for the coming quarters will be on improving operating efficiency and delivering better operating leverage.

Let's address the elephant in the room, the margins. What changed drastically from Q4/Q1? We were fairly confident of 11-13% margins and have dropped below the bottom end. We had to revisit our pricing strategies keeping in mind quality of business and credibility of names we are onboarding. Internally we have decided on a certain threshold below which we will not do business. We are looking at various ways to cut cost and increase operating efficiency, from automating processes to improving productivity. We are hopeful of maintaining these margins going ahead this year, and at some point the operating leverage will kick in.

CMD: That depends market to market and client to client; these are internal thresholds. As a company overall (mix of CCR and ETS) we hope to maintain the same margins now.

Q3 — Kesha Gag, Counter Cyclical PMS:
In this industry with no entry barriers, where cost of operations (driver salaries, fuel) is the same for everybody, and realizations are competitive (especially ETS where nobody pays extra), ultimately return on capital will come around the cost of capital. What gives us an edge over competition?
CMD: While there are no entry barriers and anybody can buy 10 cars, we look at our right to win in three parts: (1) the quality of clients and the deep relationships enjoyed over decades; (2) a huge supply base, ever-available and ever-reliable, built over decades, so we don't depend on our own capital to sustain supply and growth; (3) in the middle, our processes and people, constantly evolving to bring efficiencies and maintain a strong culture. This combination is the entry barrier — an organization that can achieve scale and deliver predictable, consistent quality trip after trip, more than 5 million trips a year. It is still a fragmented industry, only 15-20% organized; the other 80% is the future opportunity we are aiming for.
