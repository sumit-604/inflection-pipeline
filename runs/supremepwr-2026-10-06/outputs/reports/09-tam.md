## Section 1: Market definition
1A. Product: oil-immersed power and distribution transformers up to 200 MVA / 220 kV, including inverter duty, solar and windmill units (AR p.18; presentation). Excluded: HVDC, 330-765 kV, dry-type, furnace and rectifier transformers. Geography: India, SAM on the southern region (TN, Karnataka, Kerala, Telangana/AP) with Maharashtra entering. Customers: state utilities, EPC firms, renewable developers, industrial buyers (B04). Channel: direct tender and EPC. Government exposure 30.1% of the Rs 590.06 Cr book (B05).
1B. Management claims (AR p.17-18, FY2025-26): (a) Mordor Indian transformer market about USD 3.25bn in 2026, USD 4.82bn by 2031, 8.2% CAGR. Specific, reasonable. (b) National Electricity Plan transmission investment over Rs 9.15 lakh Cr to 2032 and 1,274 GVA. This is an investment programme, broad, and not a transformer TAM. (c) Peak revenue Rs 550-650 Cr FY29 (B05). Credibility read: (a) reasonable, (b) broad, used as a driver only.

## Section 2: TAM, multiple methods
Method 1, top-down.
- CRISIL, 12-Aug-2025 (ianslive): market about Rs 33,000 Cr last fiscal (FY25); sales to grow 10-11% a year; cumulative Rs 70-75,000 Cr over two fiscals. FY26 = 33,000 x 1.105 = 36,465 (DERIVED, midpoint). Confidence M.
- Mordor, 5-Aug-2026: USD 3.25bn 2026. RBI reference about 96 (MSEI, 28-Sep to 01-Oct-2026). 3.25 x 96 = Rs 31,200 Cr. Confidence M, scope undisclosed.
- Both agree on structure: distribution 58.92%, power 41.08% (Mordor); utilities 46.85%.
Method 2, bottom-up (transmission class). CEA added 113,013 MVA of AC transformation in FY26, 90% of the 126,007 MVA target; FY27 target 158,339 MVA (Outlook Business, 26-Sep-2026). Price per MVA anchored on SPEL's own orders: 160 MVA/220 kV Rs 23.30 Cr = Rs 14.6 lakh/MVA; 112.5 MVA/330 kV Rs 13.50 Cr = Rs 12.0 lakh/MVA (presentation p.5). 113,013 x 12.0 lakh = Rs 13,562 Cr; x 14.6 lakh = Rs 16,500 Cr. Cross-check: non-distribution share of top-down = 41.08% x 31,200 = 12,817 and x 36,465 = 14,979. The bottom-up sits at the realistic end. Distribution bottom-up not built: 696,302 distribution transformers installed 2014-2026 (Powerline, 5-May-2026) but rating and price NOT FOUND. Confidence M for transmission class.
Method 3, peer aggregation. FY26 revenue: TARIL 2,508.80 consolidated (electricalmirror, FY26 results); Voltamp 2,153.69 (Business Standard/trendlyne); Indo Tech 782.08; Shilchar 651.94; Danish 520.90; SPEL 181.64 (screener Data_Sheets in inputs). Sum six = 6,799. Hitachi Energy India, Siemens Energy, GE Vernova T&D, CG Power, BHEL transformer revenue NOT FOUND. Unorganised estimate (illustrative): about 60% of distribution (enggpro, undated) x 58.92% = 35.4% of TAM = 12,892 on 36,465. Organised then 23,573; the six listed peers are 28.8% of that. This method gives a floor of 6,799 and no independent TAM. Confidence L.
Method 4, import substitution: not run. AR p.18 says CRGO is 60-90% imported; transformer imports NOT FOUND.
Method 5, global benchmark: not run (skipped).

## Section 3: SAM and SOM
3A SAM (applied to realistic TAM 36,465):
1. Product fit: 10-100 MVA medium units are 40.78% of the market (Mordor). 36,465 x 0.4078 = 14,870. The 100-200 MVA slice SPEL can now build and the sub-10 MVA slice NOT FOUND, so this is a floor.
2. Geography: southern region holds 36.2% of substation capacity under construction (tndindia, undated; L). 14,870 x 0.362 = 5,383. Maharashtra not sized.
3. Channel: no subtraction (SPEL sells direct and via EPC; no evidence for a cut).
4. Customer: no subtraction; ISTS/PGCIL qualification is unknown (the AR says most 2027-32 NEP spend is ISTS), a risk to SAM, not sized.
5. Capability: 220 kV is stated capability, type test Dec-2026/Jan-2027. Not subtracted; flagged.
SAM = 5,383 = 14.8% of realistic TAM (17.2% of conservative: on 31,200, 31,200 x 0.4078 x 0.362 = 4,606). TAM > SAM > SOM holds.
3B SOM. Current share: 181.64 / 5,383 = 3.4%. SAM growth 8.2%: FY29 = 5,383 x 1.082^3 = 6,819; FY31 = 5,383 x 1.082^5 = 7,983. Growth check: NEP GVA 1,251 to 2,342 over 2024-32 = (2,342/1,251)^(1/8) = 8.2%. SPEL FY23-26 CAGR = (181.64/99.76)^(1/3) = 22.1%, so it already gains share against 8-11% market growth.
- Normal (+1.5pp, FY29): share 4.87% x 6,819 = 332; CAGR (332/181.64)^(1/3) = 22.3%.
- Central (+3.0pp, low end of aggressive tier, FY29): share 6.37% x 6,819 = 434; CAGR (434/181.64)^(1/3) = 33.7%.
- Yr5 (+4.0pp, FY31): 7.37% x 7,983 = 588; CAGR (588/181.64)^(1/5) = 26.5%.
- Management peak Rs 550-650 Cr in FY29 = 8.1-9.5% of FY29 SAM, +4.7 to +6.1pp. The top of this is above the 5pp rule, which needs competitor exit or acquisition. Not carried.
Why +3.0pp is central: capacity is physically built (9,000 MVA), book is 3.2x revenue (590.06 / 181.64), and the FY23-26 record is 22.1% CAGR in a market growing about 10%. Why not higher: credibility grade C, Kannur at 20-25% six months in, 220 kV unproven. The normal reading (22.3%) stands if the one separating observation fails: Kannur utilisation disclosed with basis at 30% or more on the Q2 FY27 call plus the CPRI certificate. Conservatism goes to position size, not to the input.
3C capacity cross-check (B05, B07). Kannur potential Rs 500-550 Cr and Unit 1 Rs 100-110 Cr (B05). FY29 434 needs (434 - 105) / 525 = 63% of Kannur potential. FY31 588 needs (588 - 105) / 525 = 92%, at the ceiling; beyond that needs new land (not bought, B05). Capacity-derived FY27 is 225-275 (B05, DERIVED) against a 250-300 guide: the guide tops capacity by up to Rs 25 Cr. The optimistic side is the guide and the ramp, not the capex plan. Stated peak Rs 550-650 equals the 90% ceiling, not a market limit. Rs 6.1 lakh/MVA at peak vs Rs 10.9 lakh/MVA in FY26 (B04) shows MVA does not map to revenue one for one.

## Section 4: Drivers, risks, structure
4A Drivers (evidence).
- Transmission build-out: NEP Rs 9.15 lakh Cr, 1,274 GVA by FY32 (CEA, 15-Oct-2024; 1.98 years old, at the limit). High. Execution seen: PGCIL capex Rs 35,540 Cr FY26 (AR p.17, Kotak Neo).
- Physical pull: 113,013 MVA added FY26; FY27 target 158,339 MVA; 220 kV delivery 11-15 months, 400 kV 15 months, 765 kV 20-24 months (Outlook Business, 26-Sep-2026). High.
- Distribution reform: RDSS Rs 3,03,758 Cr outlay, Rs 2.78 lakh Cr sanctioned, extended to 31-Mar-2028 (impriindia / Rajya Sabha reply). Tamil Nadu plans 40,000 transformers for Rs 2,000 Cr and 178 substations for Rs 1,543 Cr (solarquarter, 17-Jun-2026). Medium.
- Renewables: about 43 GW added FY26 (AR p.18); Green Energy Corridor Phase-II 24,488 MVA and Phase-III 229 GVA (Powerline, 5-May-2026). Medium.
- Data centres: 2.2 GW 2025 to 12 GW 2030 (energetica); SPEL has none and management says none in FY27 (B05). Low for this hold.
- Formalisation/efficiency: BEE star labels from 1-Jan-2025 (AR p.17). Low-medium.
4B Risks and monitors. Peer capacity race: Voltamp +6,000 MVA EHV from Oct-2026, Shilchar +6,500 MVA by Apr-2027, Danish +12-15k MVA up to 400 kV, TARIL +15,000 MVA Q2 FY27 and 22,000 planned (tndindia, NSE, Trendlyne). Watch peer order inflow and pricing. CRGO and copper supply; BIS licensing (AR p.18). Uneven awards: right-of-way and land (Kotak Neo). CRISIL expects industry operating margin 8-10%, far below SPEL's guide: watch gross margin below 24% (B04).
4C Structure. Over 300 manufacturers, about 20 organised (enggpro, undated, L). Unorganised about 60% of distribution (undated). Mordor says moderate concentration. Consolidating at the top: TARIL 33,000 MVA FY26 output, Voltamp 14,000 MVA capacity, Danish 11,000, Shilchar 7,500 (14,000 FY28), SPEL 2,500 to 9,000. West and south generate about 60% of demand (Mordor). Entry barrier is qualification, not capital (AR p.18), but four peers are now spending to reach 220 kV.

## Section 5: Summary and runway
5A Funnel: TAM 36,465 (conservative 31,200) > SAM 5,383 (14.8%) > SOM FY29 434 (6.4% of FY29 SAM 6,819) > SOM FY31 588 (7.4% of 7,983) > today 181.64 (3.4% of SAM).
5B Headroom 5,383 / 181.64 = 29.6x. TAM growth 8.2% vs SPEL 22.1%: gaining share. Years to saturate SAM at current growth: ln(29.64) / ln(1.221/1.082) = 3.389 / 0.121 = 28 years. Not binding.
5C Class: STRONG. The stage file gives no cut-offs; headroom of about 30x points to MASSIVE, 8.2% TAM growth and thin SAM filters hold it at STRONG. The constraint is qualification and execution, not market size.
5D SAM levers: Maharashtra and west (not sized); 220 kV qualification (unlocks the top slice); data centres (none before FY28); 300-500 MVA next capacity (land not bought); exports ("let us see"). Revised headroom not quantified because no data supports a number.
5E Output card. At 33.7% revenue CAGR implied by SOM (FY26 to FY29), with PAT margin of 10% (Q1 FY27 10.1%; guide 9-12%, B05), the earnings growth embedded here is 28.5% CAGR (43.4 / 20.44 FY26 attributable PAT, 3 years), which supports the current valuation of 29.3x trailing P/E (price 239.45, market cap 598.41, FY26 profit 20.44; screener export, snapshot date not on file) only on the +3pp path. On the normal path, 22.3% revenue gives 17.5% earnings growth, which does not support 29x. Margin bridge and dilution are stage 11's.
