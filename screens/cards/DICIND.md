## Business Understanding Narrative

**What does it do, and how does it make money?** DIC India makes printing inks.
Its results state that operations "are predominantly manufacture of 'Printing
Inks' and according to the management this is the single segment" (Financial
Results, FY2024, Q4, page 9). The product range covers sheetfed offset, UV
packaging inks, low-migration low-odour inks for food and pharmaceutical
packaging, aqueous and UV coatings, and mineral-oil-free inks (Reg. 34(1) Annual
Report, FY2024, Q4, page 11). It is a subsidiary of DIC Corporation of Japan.

**What is changing?** The management, completely, and the product mix, slowly.
The MD&A describes an industry where "competitive differentiation is increasingly
driven by technology depth, regulatory capability, and execution discipline
rather than price alone", with demand from flexible packaging and growing
adoption of toluene-free and low-VOC systems (Reg. 34(1) Annual Report, FY2025,
Q4, page 49).

**Why now?** There is no "now" in the numbers. CY2025 revenue was Rs 891.79
crore against Rs 881.53 crore, up 1.2%. Profit for the year was Rs 17.38 crore
against Rs 19.54 crore, **down 11.1%** (Reg. 34(1) Annual Report, FY2025, Q4,
pages 105 and 106).

**What must be true for the thesis to work?** The premium portfolio must
actually re-price. Operating profit margin improved from 1.97% to 2.41%
(Reg. 34(1) Annual Report, FY2025, Q4, page 51), so the mix shift is doing
something. It is doing it on margins so thin that the improvement is invisible
at the bottom line.

**What breaks it?** It is already broken, structurally. A 2.4% operating margin
and a 1.95% net margin on Rs 892 crore of revenue means the business converts
almost nothing. The company's own FY2022 MD&A says brand owners press to "hold
or reduce price points" while input costs rise (Reg. 34(1) Annual Report,
FY2022, Q4, page 44). That squeeze has not lifted in four years.

## 2. Business model and archetype

One engine: buy pigment, resin and solvent, formulate ink, sell to printers and
converters. The cost structure is almost entirely pass-through (Reg. 34(1)
Annual Report, FY2025, Q4, page 105), Rs lakh for the year to 31 December 2025:

| | CY2025 | CY2024 |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue from operations | 89,178.85 | 88,152.89 |
| Cost of materials consumed | 59,453.24 | 59,646.60 |
| Purchases of stock-in-trade | 6,827.46 | 7,245.25 |
| Employee benefits | 7,510.25 | 6,978.67 |
| Other expenses | 12,011.88 | 11,761.02 |
| Finance costs | 141.71 | 175.83 |
| Depreciation | 1,836.61 | 1,908.74 |
| **PBT before exceptional items** | **2,601.11** | **2,515.06** |

Materials plus traded goods are 74.3% of revenue. Everything depends on the
spread between pigment cost and ink price, and the customer sets the price.

**Archetype: commodity converter.** Section 1B v3.7 Amendment 17 binds any later
valuation of this name: spot-year ROCE and rupee-denominated working-capital
trends must not be fed into Section 1B or FTTCP. Spread, utilisation, cycle
position and cost-curve rank are the variables.

## 3. Competitive advantages

Weak but not absent, and the parent is the reason. Three points.

First, technology transfer. The company "continues to focus on expanding its
product range, with DIC Corporation's support and building a more premium and
sustainable portfolio" (Reg. 34(1) Annual Report, FY2022, Q4, page 44). Access
to a Japanese parent's formulation library is something a local competitor
cannot buy.

Second, regulatory capability. Low-migration, low-odour and mineral-oil-free
inks for food, pharmaceutical and tobacco packaging must meet EFSA global
migration limits. A converter printing food packaging cannot switch ink supplier
casually, because the packaging itself must be requalified. That is a genuine
switching cost and it is the best argument on this card.

Third, and against the first two: it is not reaching the margin. Net margin fell
from 2.22% to 1.95% while the premium portfolio expanded. Whatever advantage
exists is being competed away or given to customers.

The FY2025 MD&A is candid that organised players invest in sustainability "under
increasing pressure from brand owners to hold or reduce price points". **Moat
present in theory, not visible in the accounts.**

## 6. The transition and the quality ladder

FROM R1 COMMODITY PRICE-TAKER. TO R2 COST-ADVANTAGED CONVERTER, at most.

The MD&A argues that differentiation now comes from "technology depth,
regulatory capability, and execution discipline rather than price alone". If
true and if it reached the accounts, that is the R1 to R2 step: margin from
position rather than price.

It has not reached the accounts. R2 on this ladder requires durable mid-teens
ROCE. DIC India earns a 2.41% operating margin and a 1.95% net margin. Net worth
of Rs 428.65 crore producing Rs 17.38 crore of profit is a **return on equity of
4.05%**, far below any cost of capital.

Management's claim, quoted once: "competitive differentiation is increasingly
driven by technology depth, regulatory capability, and execution discipline
rather than price alone" (Reg. 34(1) Annual Report, FY2025, Q4, page 49).

That is a claim about the industry, not about this company's climb. **No rung
migration is claimed for the company itself, and none is evidenced.**

## 8. Proof check

**The proof check fails on its own terms.** The restructuring triggers delivered:
Kolkata closed, both leasehold parcels handed back, the premium portfolio
expanded. Those are real, completed actions.

The last two observable years then move the wrong way. CY2024 profit Rs 19.54
crore; CY2025 profit Rs 17.38 crore. Revenue up 1.2%, profit down 11.1%, with
every working-capital ratio deteriorating.

A plant closure and a mix upgrade that together produce falling profit two years
later is the definition of a proof point that did not fire.

The named absence: **no CY2026 interim result is indexed.** The company reports
half-yearly to June and the H1 CY2026 result should exist. It is not in this
corpus, so the newest management's first period is unobserved.

## 12. Verdict card

**PASS for `/step1`. Retain on a NAV watch-list, not a transition watch-list.**

A 33 times multiple on falling earnings, a 4% return on equity and a
five-year-flat revenue line is not transition alpha. The framework's machinery
has nothing to work on: no claimed climb, no fired gate, no forward number
anywhere.

The one genuinely interesting thing about this company is the balance sheet, not
the profit and loss. Net worth of Rs 428.65 crore with debt-equity of 0.01,
against a market capitalisation of Rs 572.13 crore, is 1.33 times book for a
debt-free subsidiary of a Japanese multinational. **If there is a case here it
is an asset and parent-action case**, and the new parent-nominated Managing
Director from 1 September 2026 is the event that might unlock it. That is a
special-situation thesis, not a GARP transition thesis, and it should not be
routed through `/step1`.

Load-bearing facts a deep run would verify first, should the operator override:
1. **The H1 CY2026 result**, the first period under the new management, not in
   this corpus.
2. **The warrant allotment filing of 14 May 2026.** A debt-free company issuing
   warrants needs explaining.
3. **The litigation stream**, seven filings in one quarter, none read.
4. **The CY2025 cash flow statement** and receivable ageing behind the debtor
   turnover fall.
5. **What DIC Corporation intends.** A parent installing its own MD after four
   flat years is the only live variable on this card.

What would change the view. Two consecutive half-years with net margin above 4%,
which would show the premium portfolio finally re-pricing rather than being
competed away.

No price. No position.
