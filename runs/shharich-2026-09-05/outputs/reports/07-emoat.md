## SECTION 4: REGULATORY & POLICY TAILWINDS (R1)

**4A. Regulatory approvals in pipeline.** SEIAA (State Environment Impact
Assessment Authority) granted Environmental Clearance to SDPL for
"manufacturing of Synthetic Organic Chemicals and Dye Intermediates"
(Chairman's Message, AR FY26, p.7). Status: GRANTED. Timeline for
commissioning: NOT FOUND (undated "near term" claim). What it unlocks:
SDPL's manufacturing facility construction, currently reflected as
consolidated CWIP of Rs 11.30 cr (Note 2(ii), p.154), of which Rs 0.74 cr
is already aged 1-2 years. Which competitors have it: NOT FOUND — an EC is
a standard prerequisite for any Indian chemicals manufacturer at this
scale, so its exclusivity value (if any) is unclear.

**4B. Government policy tailwinds.** Standard export incentives only:
duty drawback and GST-refund receivable of Rs 318.20 lakh appear in Other
Financial Assets (Note 12, p.118 area). This is a scheme available to all
exporters equally, not a differentiated tailwind. No PLI scheme, sector-
specific tax incentive, or import-substitution measure is disclosed for
dye intermediates in this AR.

**4C. Regulatory moat assessment.** The SDPL clearance is genuinely
emerging (SDPL has zero revenue to date) but the time to kick in is
uncertain and already running long: SDPL was acquired/incorporated
24-Apr-2024 (AOC-1, p.74) and has produced nothing in two full reporting
years. Sustainability, if the plant is eventually commissioned, would be
ordinary rather than exclusive, since equivalent clearances are a routine
industry requirement, not a scarce asset. Classified active vs emerging:
emerging, not yet active. **Strength: Weak**, reflecting the stalled
track record more than the clearance itself.

**Combined assessment: AVOID.**

transition setups the strategy hunts. The backward score already sits at
AVOID (even though B01 itself notes the underlying profile reads closer to

a single cost line, not the company's pricing power, customer relationships,

two years, and the capital funding that bet (warrants, CCDs) is raising
promoter-family control from 54.21% toward a prospective 73.75%

Nothing in this scan overrides the backward verdict.
