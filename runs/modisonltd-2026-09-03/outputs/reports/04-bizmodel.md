## SECTION 1: THE BUSINESS MODEL IN PLAIN ENGLISH

### 1A. One-line description
Modison melts silver, copper and tungsten into the small metal pads ("contacts") that touch and separate inside industrial switches (switchgear), selling mostly to Indian and export switchgear makers on a raw-material-cost-passed-through basis (AR p.77, "About us"; Inv. Pres. slide 6).

### 1B. Money flow chain, by revenue stream

**Stream 1 — Domestic sale of electrical contacts and contact materials (LV/MV/HV, blended, one reported segment)**
[Silver, copper, tungsten bought on the open market] → [Modison refines, alloys, presses, brazes and machines these metals into contacts, wires, plates, rivets, tulips and disconnector parts to customer spec] → [delivers finished contacts to Indian switchgear OEMs] → [OEM pays, ~75-day average credit period, with silver-price risk largely pre-agreed via long-term supply/price-sharing contracts] → [payment via bank transfer against invoice; for high-impact customers, Modison additionally takes an advance against the silver content booked] (AR note 12, p.166; AR Risk Management table, p.78; AR note 42 Financial Risk Management, p.166-167).

**Stream 2 — Export sale of electrical contacts and contact materials**
[Same manufacturing process] → [shipped to switchgear/component buyers in ~20+ countries across Europe, North America, the Middle East and Asia] → [buyer pays in USD/EUR] → [Modison nets this against USD/EUR raw-material imports as a natural hedge, plus forward contracts for the residual exposure] (AR note 47, Segment Reporting, p.177; AR note 45, Forward Contracts, p.176; Inv. Pres. slide 9-10).

Both streams are reported as ONE accounting segment: "Manufacturing of Electrical Contacts." The company does not disclose a revenue split by voltage class (LV/MV/HV) or by product type (semis, finished contacts, precious-metal compounds, B2C bullion/cutlery) anywhere in the AR or the Investor Presentation provided — **NOT FOUND**, check concall or a future investor presentation (AR note 47, p.177: "The Company's business activity falls within a single Primary segment").

### 1E. The chai-stall-uncle version
Every time a big industrial switch turns power on or off, two tiny metal pads touch and pull apart inside it. Modison makes those pads, for the giant switches used in power plants and factories, not for your bedroom light switch. It melts silver, copper and tungsten and shapes them into these pads to the customer's exact design. Silver is costly, so Modison and its big customers agree in advance: if silver's price goes up, the customer pays more, much like a chai stall raising the price of tea the day milk gets dearer. Modison's own earnings come from the "making charge" it adds on top of the metal cost, and that making charge has swung a lot over the years, from about 7 paise to 16 paise on every rupee of sales (AR p.2, ten-year table).
