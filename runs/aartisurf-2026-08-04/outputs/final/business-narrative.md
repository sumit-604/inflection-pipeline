Contract surfactant maker priced above earned value

Aarti Surfactants makes surfactants and specialty home and personal care ingredients for large FMCG buyers. HUL, P&G, Colgate-Palmolive and Dabur sit among the named customers. It runs one reportable segment on about 113,000 tonnes of nameplate capacity, carved out of Aarti Industries in the 2019 demerger, and carries a market value near Rs 443 Cr. Two customers alone are 71% of product sales, up from 64% a year earlier, and the top five are about 92%. FY26 revenue was Rs 859 Cr, PAT Rs 13 Cr, and the shares trade near Rs 523, about 24 times trailing earnings and about 35 times FY26 reported earnings.

The company is mid way through a capacity expansion. CARE puts the programme at about Rs 85 Cr, roughly Rs 60 Cr of it funded by debt, with the Pithampur leg due to commission by December 2026. The company itself has not confirmed that date or the capex size in its own filings. A Rs 18.50 Cr preference share redemption falls due on 19 August 2026.

Gate 0 lands AVERAGE, a core of 55 out of 100 and a moderate moat, three of twelve tests present. Return on capital fell from 17.4% in FY24 to 9.7% in FY26, return on equity from 11.5% to 5.3%, and operating margin compressed from about 11% to 5.5% over the same window. CARE downgraded the company in December 2025 on that margin fall, bank facilities from A minus to BBB plus and instruments from BBB plus to BBB. Cash flow looks clean on the surface, FY26 operating cash of Rs 77 Cr against PAT of Rs 13 Cr, but that came from a one time working capital release, inventory run down about Rs 16 Cr and payables stretched about Rs 33 Cr, not from better trading. Revenue growth is the one genuine bright spot, up 30% in FY26 and about 26% in Q1 FY27, though cost of materials rose 33% against revenue up 30%, so part of it is raw material price passing through at a thinner margin.

Management says more than the audited numbers support. The Chairman's Letter reports EBITDA of Rs 49.8 Cr, about 9.2% above the audited Rs 45.6 Cr, inflated by a one time insurance gain in the most read section of the report. The management discussion prints return on net worth of 12.28% for FY25 as an increase, while the audited ratio note in the same document shows return on equity of 6.61%, a 42.5% fall off the same base. The stated priority to protect margin was missed for the full year, and the explanations lean external. The credibility grade is C. Set against that, revenue growth and the dividend were both delivered, and Q1 FY27 margin recovered to 7.49% from 5.22%, one real data point.

Peers do not corroborate the recovery story. Rossari and Galaxy show margins flat to down in the same June 2026 quarter and both describe a fresh West Asia driven cost and freight spike moving into it, the opposite of a sector tailwind behind Aarti's Q1 FY27 bounce. Aarti is expanding capacity on debt while Rossari cuts capex and targets debt free status inside 18 months and Galaxy has paused growth capex since August 2025, leaving Aarti the lone debt funded expander. Rossari reports no single customer above 2% of sales and top ten at 12% to 13%, which places Aarti's 71% top two and 92% top five outside disclosed peer practice.

The market runway is real but oversold by management. Addressable Indian surfactant ingredient demand sits near Rs 19,000 Cr to Rs 21,000 Cr, the serviceable slice near Rs 5,280 Cr, and Aarti holds about 13% of that slice, so revenue headroom is roughly 6 times before saturation. The company's own cited market figure is 5.38 times the independently sourced base, drawn from a longer horizon and a higher source, so the size claim reads inflated even where the underlying runway is genuine.

Valuation says avoid at this price. The earned destination multiple is 12.2 times forward earnings on the operator approved additive track, or 8.5 times on the more conservative reverse method, against about 24 times trailing today. Base three year fair value is Rs 493 on the additive track and Rs 343 on the reverse track, both below the Rs 523 price. The return hurdle comes out at 0.94 against a pass bar near 1.95, and it still fails on bull earnings at 1.06. Probability weighted three year return is minus 7%. The entry zone is Rs 202 to Rs 252, margin of safety at Rs 202. The lowest price the stock has closed at since listing is about Rs 321, so the entry top sits about 21% below any level the market has yet tested. It reaches entry only through a thesis relevant shock or a broad de rating, which makes it deep watch, not an actionable buy.

The thesis and the challenge land in the same place. The devil's advocate attacked the avoid from growth, moat, management and valuation and it held on all four. Rebuilt with no margin recovery at all, the hurdle falls to 0.36, so the avoid does not depend on crediting a recovery twice. The one print that would reopen the case is Q2 and Q3 FY27 operating margin both holding at or above 7.5%; a quarter back below 6% confirms the trough.
