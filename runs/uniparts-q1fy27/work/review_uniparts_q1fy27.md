# PLAIN-LANGUAGE BRIEF (mandatory standing deliverable)

## 1. SUMMARY NARRATIVE

Uniparts India, a maker of precision linkage parts and machined components for off-highway (mostly agricultural and construction) vehicles, had a strong first quarter of FY27 [this quarter's filing]. Consolidated revenue rose 26.9% year on year to Rs 347.4 crore, operating profit margin widened by about 4.5 percentage points to 23.6%, and the number that matters most, core operating profit before other income, grew 70.5%, faster than reported net profit growth of 64%. That tells us the growth is real operating improvement, not one-off treasury or dividend income. Reported net profit was Rs 56.6 crore versus Rs 34.5 crore a year ago [this quarter's filing]. The company is sitting on roughly Rs 190 crore of net cash, is effectively debt-free, and declared a Rs 9 per share interim dividend. Auditors gave a clean limited-review conclusion.

The earnings call adds two things the filing did not. First, on cash: Q1 filings carry no cash-flow statement, so last review the quarterly cash conversion was undeterminable. On the call the CFO said operating cash was Rs 44 crore and capex Rs 12 crore [management, concall]. Against Rs 57 crore of profit that is a cash conversion of about 0.77 times, which is below 1.0 and near the level a bear would watch. It is a spoken, unaudited figure, weaker than a filed statement, and most likely reflects working-capital build behind 27% growth rather than a real problem, but it must be confirmed by the audited September-half. Second, management upgraded guidance: FY26 grew 21%, and they now expect FY27 to be a couple of percentage points better, with Q2 similar to Q1 and the second half better than the first [management, concall]. The chairman closed saying they will improve on earlier guidance.

The qualifications are about structure, margin quality, and disclosure, not the headline numbers. About 46% of group profit and 65% of revenue come from three foreign subsidiaries reviewed by other auditors, not the main auditor, and this was never raised on the call. The standalone parent accounts are distorted by intra-group dividends. Part of this quarter's margin came from an unquantified currency gain of roughly Rs 6.8 crore that management declined to break out when asked directly, and the highest-margin sales channel (warehousing) is already at the top of its guided range, so the margin tailwind is near its ceiling. Management has also been talking about acquisitions since 2023, has looked at about a dozen targets, and has closed none. And the single sharpest question on the call, whether the earlier diversification into construction actually reduced the company's cyclicality, was lost to an audio problem and never answered. There is no existing investment thesis here, this is a first look, so there is no buy or sell. The verdict is PROCEED WITH FLAGS: encouraging operating quality and a fortress balance sheet, offset by a real audit-scope flag, margin that leans on currency and mix, and a below-1.0 unaudited cash conversion. The next step is a full workup and pointed questions to management, not a position.

## 2. SECTOR INTELLIGENCE

The company sells into the global off-highway vehicle market: small agriculture, large agriculture, construction equipment, and aftermarket [this quarter's filing, slides 6-7]. Demand is sharply divergent. Construction equipment is in an up-cycle, helped by US infrastructure and technology-linked spending and European government spending, and management says it is now about 45% of revenue [management, concall L8]. Small agriculture is strong in India (subsidy programmes, higher-horsepower adoption) but only "measured" in Western markets; management believes the Western small-ag bottom is behind after three years of declines [management, concall L3]. Large agriculture is still in a cyclical trough; management, citing leading OEMs, expects the industry to shrink about 15-16% in CY2026 and recover only from CY2027 / FY2028 [management, concall L43/L124, attributed to third-party OEM views, not company-guaranteed]. Aftermarket (~12% of revenue, down from ~20% in FY25) was hit by tariff-driven price volatility that management says is easing [management, concall L112]. The structural tailwind for this kind of supplier is farm-equipment mechanisation and OEM outsourcing of complex, low-volume, high-SKU components (general industry knowledge). The headwind flagged is input-cost and supply-chain pressure from the "West Asia situation" [this quarter's filing / concall]. No prior peer/sector Notion work exists; all sector context is either this quarter's filing, labelled management claim, or labelled general knowledge.

## 3. BUSINESS-MODEL INTELLIGENCE

Uniparts makes money by manufacturing engineered systems, three-point-linkage (~47% of revenue) and precision machined parts (~51%), plus smaller power-take-off, hydraulic and fabrication lines, and selling them to global OHV OEMs under long-standing relationships (four of the top five customers for 10+ years; management says the top-five global ag OEMs and top-three construction OEMs are all customers) [this quarter's filing, slides 14-16; management, concall L120]. The model is dual-shore: manufacture in low-cost India, warehouse close to customers in the US, Europe and now Mexico. The economics: gross margin is high (material cost was 33.3% of sales this quarter, so gross margin ~66%) because these are complex made-to-spec parts, but the business carries heavy working capital (~139 days of trailing revenue) [management, concall L4], so cash conversion, not margin, is the variable to watch. That is exactly why the spoken 0.77x Q1 cash conversion matters. Margin quality this quarter has three soft spots management itself surfaced: the 33.3% material cost is below their own "typical 34-37%," implying normalisation up in cost (down in margin); the highest-margin warehouse channel is already ~56%, the top of its 52-56% band; and part of the margin came from an unquantified currency gain [management, concall L35/L95/L130]. Management guides through-cycle EBITDA margin of 20%, below the current ~25% reported, so the honest read is that current margin is above trend. Capex is light and steady at 2.5-3.5% of revenue, the balance sheet is net cash Rs 190 crore, and management is openly hunting acquisitions but has closed none since 2023 [management, concall L27/L58/L70]. No model drift this quarter, but the FX/mix-dependence of the margin is the thing to watch.

## 4. COMPETITION INTELLIGENCE

Uniparts positions itself as the global market leader in linkage systems for small (below 70 HP) tractors, where it says it has a "dominant" global share, and a challenger in above-70HP linkage where it is at "single-digit" share and sees its biggest growth runway; in precision machined parts it says it has a strong share of what it currently supplies to the top-three construction OEMs but a small share of their total relevant sourcing spend, i.e. room to grow via wallet expansion [management, concall L120]. Where it wins: deep decade-plus OEM relationships, design-stage involvement, a dual-shore delivery model that de-risks customer supply chains, and a hard-to-enter low-volume high-SKU niche (company framing; no independent peer benchmark exists for this new name, treat as claim plus general knowledge). Where it is structurally weaker or exposed: customer concentration is high (top five ~78% of sales per the deck), it is a single-segment OHV supplier with construction now ~45% of revenue, itself cyclical, so the "diversification" reduced ag exposure by adding another cyclical end-market, which is precisely the concern in the call's one unanswered question [management, concall L104]; and roughly two-thirds of revenue runs through foreign subsidiaries not reviewed by the principal auditor, a verification and transparency vulnerability [this quarter's filing]. The competitive risk to watch is cyclical timing: in the large-ag down-cycle management says its growth is "entirely" new-business wins, not market-driven, so if wins slow there is little market tailwind until CY2027/FY2028 [management, concall L3/L43]. No named public peers are analysed in any Notion universe (none exists) and no peer concall was available for cross-check, so a like-for-like competitive and credibility comparison must be built at the full-workup stage.
