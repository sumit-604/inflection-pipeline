## Narrative

Paramount makes power and communication cables — a capital-hungry, working-capital-heavy business in a crowded field of far stronger players. It is not a bad little company: it has niche products and grew revenue 21% as its new MP plant came online. But FY26 is the year the US tariff shock landed in full — operating margins halved to \~3.5%, ROCE fell to \~6%, and most damagingly it torched cash: receivables doubled to ₹413 Cr, CFO went negative ₹38 Cr, and it flipped from net cash to ₹90 Cr net debt. Reported profit only looks "down 31%" because a one-time ₹28 Cr keyman insurance payout flattered the year; strip it and core profit roughly halved.

The problem isn't the business — it's the price. The market re-rated the stock \~33% in six months (₹38 → ₹54) and now pays \~28x reported (\~42x normalised) earnings for a company whose own quality justifies 9–12x. You're paying a quality-compounder multiple for a trough-margin, cash-negative, low-ROCE cyclical, just as it prepares to dilute via a preferential issue. The recovery may be real — Q4 margins already bounced — but it is more than fully priced. Buying here bets on both a strong recovery AND a premium multiple the fundamentals don't support. Poor risk-reward at the 25% hurdle.
