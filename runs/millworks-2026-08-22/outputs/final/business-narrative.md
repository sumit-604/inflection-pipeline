# Business Understanding Narrative

Millworks makes precision metal components to a customer's own drawings and specifications. It is a build to print and build to spec job shop. The parts go into four end sectors: defence at 69% of FY26 revenue, railways at 24%, semiconductor machinery at 6%, and aerospace at 1%. In rail the parts are brake, door, coupler and pantograph components. In semiconductor the work is precision fixtures and frames. In aerospace it is engine components. The customer cannot switch easily because each part is qualified to aerospace standards like AS9100D, and qualification runs long. That qualification lock stands as the clearest source of stickiness in the business.

The customers are named and few. Quik Pay Private Limited is the single largest at 47% of FY26 revenue, all inside the defence line. Big Bang Boom Solutions sits in the same drone component chain. Faiveley Transport, owned by Wabtec, is the rail counterparty, and the company's own SWOT names loss of Faiveley business as a risk. The top 10 customers make up 92% of revenue and the top 5 make up 81%. Buying runs through long qualification cycles and standards like NADCAP, which raise switching cost but also concentrate the book.

Present demand ties to named external signals. Defence pull comes from the Union Budget defence capital outlay and the domestic procurement earmark. Quik Pay's drone joint venture and its AASHVAST army pilot feed the largest revenue line. Rail demand tracks Indian Railways and RDSO vendor approvals and tender awards, plus Faiveley India order continuity. Semiconductor demand links to India Semiconductor Mission 2.0. Indigenisation listings on the MoD iDEX and SRIJAN portals sit behind the defence order flow.

Forward growth rests on the railway budget and Make in India, and two peers give an external read. Airfloa reports rail order inflow up 66% year on year, which supports the rail capex to order cycle. Apsis reports domestic defence up about 50% year on year. The market grows near 14% a year, and the run implies Millworks revenue compounding 15% to 18%, so the company rides the sector more than it takes share.

The competitive advantage is uneven across the lines and modest overall. The emerging moat scan scored 21, in the modest band. Qualification lock in the defence and aerospace work is real but early. The two strongest documented moat rows, the embedded customer relationship and the strategic partnership, both rest on the same Quik Pay tie, so they are not independent evidence. The rail line has no disclosed RDSO approval yet, which leaves its moat weaker. The semiconductor and aerospace lines are small, low in predictability, and carry no established moat the run could confirm.

# Precision components maker gated by one customer

Millworks Technologies is a precision engineering job shop, listed on the BSE SME board around 21 July 2026. It was incorporated in November 2021. FY26 revenue was Rs 149 Cr, up from Rs 22 Cr the year before and Rs 9 Cr the year before that. PAT was Rs 37 Cr. The manifest tag called this a pharma CDMO business. That tag is wrong. Millworks makes aerospace, defence, rail and semiconductor components.

One relationship runs through the whole business. Quik Pay Private Limited is the top customer at 47% of FY26 revenue. It is also the receivables counterparty whose own delayed collections the company blames for part of its cash problem. It is also a related equity investment, Rs 5.75 Cr held by Millworks. Revenue, cash conversion and related party risk all point at the same name. The entire defence line, 69% of revenue, is substantially one counterparty dressed as a four sector business.

The cash statement contradicts the profit statement. FY26 operating cash flow was minus Rs 10.76 Cr while PAT was plus Rs 37.06 Cr. Operating cash flow was negative in the prior year too. Trade receivables grew 20 times while revenue grew under 7 times. Closing receivables reached 93% of FY26 revenue. The company set aside nothing for doubtful debts against a book that grew that fast. Gate 0 scored the business AVOID, with the cash generation block at zero out of twenty.

Management frames the receivables stretch as geopolitics. The evidence points at concentration in one related counterparty instead. There is no post listing quarter yet, so there is no delivery record to test. The IPO restatement admits sales were recognised in the wrong period in both FY24 and FY25, which is a candid disclosure and a governance caution at the same time. The credibility grade is C, the no concall default, five weeks into listed life.

The promoter record is mixed, and it does not reach a kill. There is no share pledge, no offer for sale, and no SEBI or criminal action. Against that sit three items. The promoter firm V3 Technologies is landlord, supplier and same line competitor at once. The auditor named a Section 185 breach for loans to director promoters. EPF and ESIC statutory filings ran years late, worsening into the listing window. The board and CFO were all appointed within seven months of the RHP, so there is no pre listing oversight track record.

The peers confirm the discipline the company lacks. Unimech, Apsis and Airfloa all commit further capex only near full utilisation and fund it before ordering machines, never after. Millworks plans Rs 61 Cr of machinery it had not yet ordered at RHP date, funded from the IPO. On receivables the peers run 45 to 160 days. Millworks runs about 178 days on average and 93% at close, well outside that range. No peer cites geopolitics or customer testing delays as a driver, and no peer has an investee as its top customer at this scale.

The market runway is genuine. The addressable market spans Rs 3,200 Cr to Rs 10,300 Cr, and the two methods diverge more than twofold, so the range stays wide on purpose. Revenue headroom against the served market is about 21 times. The three year and five year revenue paths sit close to the 14% market growth rate, so this is share riding more than share taking. Capacity covers the plan, with a ceiling near Rs 340 Cr against a five year served target of Rs 306 Cr.

Two searches came back partial. The promoter background stage lost three corporate database sites and one blog to the egress proxy, and no blocked source was load bearing. The market sizing stage could not open the IMARC precision engineering report or find a per vendor revenue figure. The run says so rather than filling the gaps.
