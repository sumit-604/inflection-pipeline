## SECTION 1: MARKET DEFINITION

### 1A. Precise boundaries

**Product scope (IN):** rail-coach interior components and turnkey furnishing — AC ducting, roller blinds, lighting systems, seating, interior panels, door systems (via the Janatics MOU), FRP/aluminium composite structures, coach-shell exterior work; coach refurbishment/mid-life rehabilitation content; metro-car lighting and interior/structural modules; defence/aerospace structures and ground simulators (AMCA ground simulators, Jaguar cockpit structures, artillery/T90 hull assemblies, missile-wagon structures).

**Product scope (OUT):** full rolling-stock OEM manufacture at Titagarh/Jupiter Wagons/BEML scale (wagons, locomotives, complete trainsets); traction/propulsion systems; signalling systems (the "Kavach-adjacent" product is in trial, not commercial — carried as optionality, not TAM, per Section 5D); civil/track infrastructure and electrification; full aircraft or engine manufacture (Airfloa makes structures and simulators, not airframes or propulsion); the BBBS JV's electronic-warfare/AI-defence-platform line (pre-revenue, not yet incorporated as of the 14-Jul-2026 Q1 FY27 update per B07 — carried as optionality, excluded from TAM).

**Geographic scope:** India-primary. Historical exports (Sri Lanka DEMU, Mozambique Cape Gauge, ICF's broader 650+ coach export history to 14 countries, Prospectus p.111-114) are real but small and not evidenced as a growing channel (B07 Section 2D: "NO EVIDENCE FOUND of new geographic entries in either concall"). TAM below is sized India-only; export upside is noted as a driver, not counted.

**Customer scope:** Indian Railways production units (ICF, RCF, MCF and other coach factories) and zonal railways; metro rail corporations and their primes (e.g., BEML for Chennai Metro); private turnkey/component intermediaries (Acme India Industries); defence/aerospace primes and PSUs where Airfloa holds Approved Vendor status (HAL, CVRDE, Defence Avionics Research Establishment) — explicitly NOT the wider DPSU ecosystem (BEL, BDL, Mazagon Dock, ordnance factories generically) where no qualification is evidenced.

**Channel scope:** direct IREPS/government e-tendering, turnkey project contracts, subcontracting to metro primes, and an emerging (unproven, no disclosed metrics) channel-partner model for zonal-railway and refurbishment bids (B07 Section 2D / B3).

**Price segment:** qualified, regulated, mid-to-high-value component and systems supply — not a commodity/unqualified fabrication segment. Entry is gated by multi-year vendor qualification (IRIS/AS9100D/ISO 9001/EN 15085-2 certifications, B07 A1), which is itself part of what defines the addressable pool (unqualified unorganised fabricators are only partly counted, via the standard unorganised-sector allowance in Method 3).

## SECTION 2: TAM ESTIMATION, MULTIPLE METHODS

All figures ₹ Crores, India, unless stated. FX used where needed: USD/INR 96.36 as of 15-Jul-2026 (x-rates.com / exchangerates.org.uk, 15-Jul-2026) — flagged because this is a materially weaker rupee than the ~83-85 rate embedded in older (2024) source reports; conversions of USD-denominated 2024 estimates therefore carry FX-translation uncertainty in addition to base-year staleness.

### Method 1 — Top-down, segment by segment

**(a) New-build railway coach components.** Indian Railways' FY26 rolling-stock expenditure (wagons + coaches + locomotives budgetary support) is capped at ₹57,693 crore (PRS India, "Demand for Grants Analysis 2026-27, Ministry of Railways," accessed 15-Jul-2026). No single source disclosed the wagon/coach/locomotive split. Cross-check via known unit economics: ~30,000 wagons/year at an implied ~₹40-45 lakh/wagon (from the ₹40,000cr / ~100,000-wagon tender reported May-2026, "Indian Railways Plans INR 40,000 Crore Wagon Tender," ssmb.in, 26-May-2026) ≈ ₹12,000cr; ~1,500 locomotives/year (Inv. Pres. slide 35) at an estimated ₹15-20cr/unit ≈ ₹22,500-30,000cr; leaving a residual coach-value pool of roughly ₹14,000-18,000cr for ~7,133 coaches produced in FY24-25 (PIB press release, 11.2% growth YoY, cited via search) — i.e., ≈₹2cr/coach average, in line with publicly reported LHB/ICF coach costs. **This split is an analyst-derived estimate, not a disclosed figure — flagged.**

Within a ~₹2cr coach, interiors/AC/lighting/doors/seating (Airfloa's addressable content) is estimated at 20-30% of coach value (the balance being shell/underframe/bogie/electricals) — again an analyst estimate, cross-checked in Method 2 against disclosed partial per-coach order values. Applying 20-25% to the ~₹14,000-18,000cr coach-value pool: **₹3,500-4,000cr addressable new-build component/interiors segment.**

**(b) Coach refurbishment programme.** Management states (Jun-2026 call, operator summary) a government-sanctioned ~₹26,000cr programme covering ~25,000 coaches over 3-4 years. **This figure could not be independently corroborated via web search in this run** (search for "Indian Railways coach refurbishment programme 26000 crore" returned only a smaller, differently-scoped "mid-life rehabilitation" pilot at Bhopal's Carriage Rehabilitation Workshop with no disclosed budget). It is carried here only as a company-relayed, concall-sourced figure — flagged, and treated as the single largest and least-externally-verified input to this TAM. Annualised over 3.5 years: ~₹7,429cr/year. Applying a 55-65% addressable fraction (refurbishment is interiors/amenity/fire-safety-heavy, but also includes structural and mechanical work outside Airfloa's scope): **₹4,200-4,800cr/year.**

**(c) Metro rail components.** India's annual metro rail budget grew from ₹5,798cr (2013-14) to ₹29,550cr (2025-26) (search result, "How India's metro expansion is transforming city travel," organiser.org, Mar-2026). Rolling stock/systems (as opposed to civil works/tunnelling/stations) is estimated at 15-20% of total metro capex, of which lighting/interior/component content (Airfloa's scope, cf. the BEML Chennai Metro lighting order) is a further 20-25%: ₹29,550cr × 17.5% × 22.5% ≈ **₹1,000-1,150cr/year.** Both fractions are analyst estimates — flagged.

**(d) Defence/aerospace structures & simulators.** Management's own aerospace components manufacturing market claim: $2bn current / $4bn by 2030 (Inv. Pres. slide 36). At USD/INR 96.36: ₹19,270cr current / ₹38,540cr by 2030. Structures and simulators (Airfloa's actual scope, excluding propulsion/avionics/engine-part manufacturing) are estimated at 15-18% of this broader components market: **₹2,800-3,200cr current.** Cross-check via the government's own defence-production target — ₹1.5 lakh crore FY25 actual, ₹3 lakh crore FY29 target (Inv. Pres. slide 36) — implies the same ~doubling trajectory (~17-19% CAGR to 2029-30) applied independently to structures/simulators, which is internally consistent with the $2bn→$4bn claim.

**Method 1 total:** ₹3,500-4,000 (a) + ₹4,200-4,800 (b) + ₹1,000-1,150 (c) + ₹2,800-3,200 (d) = **₹11,500cr conservative / ₹13,150cr realistic.**

### Method 2 — Bottom-up, unit economics

Addressable unit: one railway coach (new-build or refurbished) or metro car requiring interior/component fit-out.

Units/year: ~7,133 new-build coaches (FY24-25, PIB) + ~7,143 refurbished-coach-equivalents (25,000/3.5 years, company-claimed) ≈ **14,276 coach-equivalents/year.** Metro car counts are NOT FOUND (no disclosed annual metro-car procurement figure); metro is carried forward from Method 1(c) rather than unit-priced here.

Revenue per unit: no clean "per coach" figure is disclosed. The closest available data points are partial-scope order values: the 21-Jan-2026 ICF order (Rs6.00cr for one 16-car rake of modified AC duct + one 16-car rake of "other equipment," operator summary) implies ≈₹18.75 lakh/coach for that partial (2-item) scope. Scaling this conservatively to Airfloa's full addressable content per coach (seating, AC ducting, lighting, doors, panels, roller blinds — roughly 4-5x the scope of the 2-item reference order) gives an estimated **₹25-35 lakh/coach** addressable content — an analyst estimate anchored to one disclosed data point, flagged as directional only.

TAM (rail-only) = 14,276 units × ₹30 lakh (midpoint) ≈ **₹4,283cr.** Adding back metro (₹1,000-1,150cr) and defence/aerospace (₹2,800-3,200cr) from Method 1: **≈₹8,100-8,600cr.**

This is meaningfully **lower** than Method 1's rail-components sub-total, because Method 2's per-coach content estimate (built from one partial-scope order) is more conservative than Method 1's top-down percentage-of-coach-value approach. Per the conservative-bias rule, this divergence is flagged rather than averaged away.

Named competitors (per B07 Section 6B, cited by management on the Jun-2026 call): Kineco, Hindustan Fiberglass Works, DTL Ancillaries, Vibgyor, Universal Engineers, and Tata/Fainsa (seating).

- **Kineco**: consolidated revenue ₹245cr FY25 (up ~40% YoY), across rail + aerospace + defence + telecom composites; order book recently crossed ₹3,000cr, increasingly rail-interiors-driven per a Vande Bharat Sleeper contract win (CompositesWorld, "Kineco Group order buildup highlights rail interior ambitions," accessed 15-Jul-2026). Rail-attributable revenue estimated at ~50% ≈ **₹123cr.**
- **DTL Ancillaries**: operating revenue disclosed only as a **₹100-500cr range** for FY24 (cbinsights.com, via search, accessed 15-Jul-2026); supplies coach-shell kits and turnkey interior furnishing to rail coach factories and metro — a closer product-scope match to Airfloa than Kineco. Midpoint used: **₹300cr** (wide range, flagged).
- **Hindustan Fiberglass Works, Vibgyor, Universal Engineers**: revenue **NOT FOUND** — private/unlisted, no public disclosure located.
- **Tata/Fainsa** (seating): part of a large diversified group; seating-vertical-specific revenue **NOT FOUND**.

Named organised revenue with disclosed figures: Airfloa (₹319.6cr) + Kineco rail-portion (₹123cr) + DTL Ancillaries (₹300cr) ≈ **₹743cr**. This is explicitly a **floor**, not a market estimate — three named competitors and one seating sub-vertical have no disclosed revenue. Allowing a conservative ₹400-600cr for the undisclosed named peers, plus the standard unorganised-sector allowance (30-60% additional, use 40%): **₹1,600-1,900cr.**

**Read:** Method 3 sits well below Methods 1 and 2, almost entirely because of data gaps (3 of 6 named competitors have no public financials), not because the underlying market is actually that small — it is flagged Low confidence and used only as a sanity floor (it confirms the rail-components segment is at minimum ~₹1,700-1,900cr, consistent with, not contradicting, Methods 1/2's ₹3,500-4,300cr range once the undisclosed-peer gap is accounted for).

An independent "India rolling stock market" estimate: **~$5bn (~₹37,000cr, using a ~74 rate implicit in the 2025 source) by 2025** (railmarketresearch.com, "Rolling Stock Market in India 2024-2030," accessed 15-Jul-2026). **SAM = ₹13,150cr × 65.4% ≈ ₹8,600cr.**
**SAM as % of TAM (realistic): 65.4%.**

### 3B. SOM at 3 and 5 years

Current SAM share = FY26 revenue ÷ SAM = ₹319.6cr ÷ ₹8,600cr = **3.7%.**

Share-gain trajectory: the standard rule allows 1-2pp over 3 years (normal), 3-5pp (aggressive, requires capacity+execution), >5pp only on competitor exit/acquisition, and faster where unorganised share >40% is formalising. Two forces pull in opposite directions here: (i) the competitive set is genuinely fragmented with a real formalisation tailwind (qualification barriers, Kineco's own expansion into rail interiors, DTL Ancillaries' comparable but undisclosed scale) which could support faster share capture; (ii) B07's F2 (execution moat) is a **documented negative** — IPO capex only ~21% deployed, new facility construction not started, the ₹1,000cr Mar-2026 order-book target missed by ~53%, FY27 guidance already cut once. Per the conservative-bias rule, this analysis uses **1.5pp over 3 years (normal band, lower-middle)** rather than an aggressive assumption, extending to **3.0pp cumulative by year 5** only on the assumption that the new-facility ramp and/or channel-partner model materialise on a delayed but eventual basis.

SAM is grown at the blended TAM growth rate (8.3%, derived in Section 5B) for the 3-yr/5-yr windows, holding the SAM filter percentage constant:

- **SAM at yr3** = ₹8,600cr × (1.083)³ ≈ **₹10,925cr**
- **SAM at yr5** = ₹8,600cr × (1.083)⁵ ≈ **₹12,820cr**

- **Share at yr3** = 3.7% + 1.5pp = 5.2% → **SOM_3yr = 5.2% × ₹10,925cr ≈ ₹568cr**
- **Share at yr5** = 3.7% + 3.0pp = 6.7% → **SOM_5yr = 6.7% × ₹12,820cr ≈ ₹859cr**

**Implied revenue CAGR, shown arithmetic:**
- Yr3: (₹568cr ÷ ₹319.6cr)^(1/3) − 1 = (1.777)^0.333 − 1 ≈ **21.1%**
- Yr5: (₹859cr ÷ ₹319.6cr)^(1/5) − 1 = (2.688)^0.20 − 1 ≈ **21.9%**

This is the formal handoff figure for Stage 11's cross-check on revenue-growth assumptions: **som_implied_revenue_cagr = {yr3: 21.1%, yr5: 21.9%}.**

**Important divergence to flag for Stage 11:** management's own FY27 guidance (₹500cr, i.e., ~56.5% single-year growth off ₹319.6cr FY26) is far above this 3-5 year SOM-implied CAGR. That single-year number is largely order-book-execution-driven (70-75% of FY27 revenue already covered by the existing order book per management, operator summary) and is not inconsistent with a slower 3-5 year structural CAGR once the current order-book bulge is worked through — but stage 11 should not treat the guidance growth rate as the sustainable trend rate; the SOM-implied 21-22% CAGR is the more conservative, structurally-grounded figure.

### 3C. Capacity cross-check

Using B07's capex_embedded_growth_pct (40.7%, management's own "~4x" capex-turnover claim on the new 14-acre facility, explicitly flagged in B07 as an unverified management assertion): applying this to FY26 revenue (₹319.6cr) implies a near-term revenue ceiling of **₹319.6cr × 1.407 ≈ ₹450cr** — this matches management's own separately-stated claim that two-shift operations plus the new facility can reach "₹400-450cr" organically, with the balance to the ₹500cr FY27 guidance coming from the unproven channel-partner model (operator summary, Section 4).

**SOM_3yr (₹568cr) exceeds this stated capacity ceiling by ≈₹118cr.** SOM_5yr (₹859cr) is not directly comparable to the near-term ceiling but sits within the envelope of management's own longer-run channel-partner ambition ("₹1,000-2,000cr revenue potential… without building plants near every workshop," operator summary Section 4) — though that model has zero disclosed partner count, revenue share, or margin-sharing metrics (B07 B3, Weak strength).

**capacity_check: gap of ~₹118cr by year 3.** Given B07's F2 finding (execution moat scored a documented negative: IPO capex only ~21% deployed, 14-acre facility construction not started as of the Jun-2026 call, order-book and revenue guidance both already walked back once), **the capex/capacity plan is the more optimistic-and-unproven side of this equation**, not the SOM share-gain math itself — i.e., the physical capacity to deliver even the conservative 1.5pp-share SOM by year 3 is not yet demonstrated, and closing the gap depends on an asset-light channel-partner model that today has no disclosed operating metrics.
