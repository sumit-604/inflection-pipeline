## PASS 2 NEW FINDINGS

### 1. 🔴 A THIRD unrelated-party ICD counterparty missed in Pass 1: a ₹6.00 Cr loan to a football
club, at 18% p.a., round-tripped within the year.
Note 12 standalone (AR FY26 p.176) / Note 11 consolidated (p.247): "Sreenidi-Deccan Football Club
Private Limited" received an ICD of ₹600.00 lakhs (₹6.00 Cr) during FY26 at 18% p.a. interest,
"Repaid within due date" — fully repaid, not impaired, so it does not appear in Pass 1's ₹7.00 Cr
write-off total (Bhagyanagar Hotels + Galvanizz). But it is a THIRD instance this year of the
company placing meaningful ICD money with a non-operating, unrelated counterparty (a football
club, alongside a hotel and a "projects" company already flagged), at a rate (18%) higher than
either of the two written-off ICDs (12%) and far above the company's own borrowing cost
(7.25-9.15%). Cross-checked and confirmed in the consolidated cash flow statement (AR FY26 p.226):
"Loan given to parties" ₹(600.00) lakhs and "Loan repaid by parties" ₹600.00 lakhs, both FY26 —
same-year round trip. Combined with the Chairman's ₹13.03 Cr round-trip loan and the Vigilance
Security Services ₹21.00 Cr ICD (both already flagged in Pass 1), this is now the THIRD same-year
round-trip financing arrangement in FY26 (gross: ₹40.03 Cr received / ₹34.78 Cr repaid across the
three). Pattern, not one-off: this is a capital-allocation/treasury-discipline question for
management, sharper than Pass 1's framing of the ICD write-off as an isolated incident. 🔴 Watch.

### 2. 🔴 The Atum Life ICD terms were LOOSENED during the year, not tightened, despite the
subsidiary's net worth collapsing.
Note 12 standalone (AR FY26 p.176), footnote: "During the year the due date of payment for loan
given to Atum Life Private Limited is modified from repayable on demand to fixed term wherein
principal and interest are repayable by March 31, 2028." The company extended a THREE-YEAR runway
on its ₹2.80 Cr receivable from Atum Life in the same year that Atum Life's own net worth fell from
₹1.12 Cr to ₹0.32 Cr (see Finding #4 below) — the opposite of what a lender tightening credit terms
to a deteriorating counterparty would do. No loss allowance is held against this receivable at
either date. This sharpens Pass 1's Red Flag #2 (unimpaired subsidiary investment): management is
not only declining to impair the equity investment, it is actively extending more time to a
subsidiary whose ability to repay is getting weaker, not stronger.

### 3. 🟡 A new paid executive-adjacent role for a promoter-family member, not previously in the RPT
table.
Note 40 standalone (AR FY26 p.201) / Note 39 consolidated (p.269): Mrs. G. Vritika — daughter of MD
Mrs. G. Saroja Vivekanand, listed as a KMP relative — drew Remuneration of ₹25.88 lakhs (₹0.26 Cr)
FY26 in her role as "Chief Business Strategist and Advisor to the Chairman," a role that did not
exist (nil remuneration) in FY25. She already receives dividends, public-deposit interest, and has
a public-deposit balance outstanding (₹60.34 lakhs) as a related party; this adds a new paid
executive role to that list within the same reporting year two other CXOs exited (Pass 1's Finding
on management-stability). Amount is small in absolute terms but the pattern (family member moving
into a newly created, named strategy/advisory role) is a governance-quality data point Pass 1's RPT
table did not capture — Pass 1's table covered only MD/JMD/CEO remuneration lines.

### 4. 🔴 Subsidiary net worth is eroding FASTER, single-year, than Pass 1's framing suggested.
Note 44 "Other disclosures" consolidated (AR FY26 p.276-277), which also carries FY25 net-asset
figures Pass 1 did not have (Pass 1 only had the FY26 AOC-1 snapshot):
| Subsidiary | Net worth FY25 (Cr) | Net worth FY26 (Cr) | Single-year change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Visaka Green | 6.88 | 5.20 | -24.4% |
| Atum Life | 1.12 | 0.32 | -71.4% |
Atum Life's net worth fell by nearly three-quarters in ONE year alone (not "96% erosion of paid-up
capital accumulated over time" as Pass 1's framing implied — the erosion is accelerating, and most
of the cumulative 96% erosion against the ₹7.795 Cr cost happened before FY26, but the FY26 single
year alone destroyed 71% of what net worth remained). This raises the bar on how long "recoverable"
can credibly be asserted as an unimpaired judgement (Note 2, critical judgement #4 standalone).

### 5. 🟡 The standalone Note 44 Financial Ratios table carries a mandated "Return on Investment
(ROI)" line that independently corroborates the subsidiary-drag finding, not cited in Pass 1.
Note 44 standalone (AR FY26 p.206): "Return on investment (ROI) (%) = Earnings before interest and
tax of investee / Total Assets" is DISCLOSED AS NEGATIVE in both years: -16.03% (FY26) vs -18.74%
(FY25), variance -14% (below the 25% threshold requiring explanation, so the company gives none).
This is a Schedule III-mandated ratio, not an informal cross-reference — it is the company's own
formally disclosed metric confirming the investee (subsidiary) business is persistently
value-destructive relative to the assets invested, reinforcing Finding #4 and Pass 1's Red Flag #2
from a different, independently-sourced disclosure line.

### 6. 🟡 Note 2 (Critical estimates and judgements) names "Impairment of loans — inter corporate
deposits" as critical judgement #6 (standalone) / #5 (consolidated) — a direct textual anchor Pass 1
did not cite for the ICD write-off.
Pass 1's Red Flag #3 (the ₹7.00 Cr ICD write-off) and Red Flag #2 (unimpaired subsidiary investment)
were treated as two separate items anchored to different notes. Note 2 itself (AR FY26 p.161
standalone / p.226 consolidated) lists BOTH "Recoverability of investments in subsidiaries" (#4
standalone) AND "Impairment of loans-inter corporate deposits" (#6 standalone / #5 consolidated) as
critical judgement areas in the SAME list — meaning management flagged the ICD-impairment risk as a
named critical judgement before the ₹7.00 Cr write-off happened. This is worth stating as a single
textual anchor tying both red flags to the same critical-judgements disclosure, useful for a
management question: "of the two critical judgements you name in this area, one you wrote off in
full and one you have not moved on at all — what is the difference in your assessment?"

### 7. 🟢 Consolidated operating cash flow rose sharply and CLEANLY EXCLUDES the exceptional gain —
a materially important confirming data point Pass 1 did not quantify.
Consolidated Statement of Cash Flows (AR FY26 p.226): Net cash inflow from operating activities
₹183.14 Cr (₹18,313.81 lakhs) FY26 vs ₹114.05 Cr (₹11,404.71 lakhs) FY25, +60.6% YoY. Critically,
the reconciliation from PBT to operating cash flow explicitly REMOVES the exceptional gain as a
non-cash/non-operating adjustment: "Net profit on sale of assets - exceptional items ₹(5,970.33)
lakhs" is subtracted out, and the actual cash proceeds from the land/building sales appear correctly
in INVESTING activities instead ("Proceeds from sale of PPE" ₹30.56 Cr + "Proceeds from sale of
assets held for sale" ₹39.09 Cr, both already noted by Pass 1 but not tied back to this exclusion
mechanism). This means the ₹183.14 Cr FY26 operating cash flow figure is entirely free of the
one-time gain's influence — a clean, cash-based corroboration of Pass 1's "genuine operating
recovery" reading (Finding #10), independent of and stronger than the segment-profit evidence Pass 1
already cited, because operating cash flow cannot be manipulated by asset-sale timing the way
reported operating profit nominally could be. This is the single most useful NEW positive data point
from this pass and should be elevated in the consolidated Top 15.

### 8. 🟡 Segment-level granularity: Synthetic Yarn delivered a disproportionate share of the
segment-profit recovery relative to its revenue share — not previously decomposed.
Note 37 standalone (AR FY26 p.202): Building products segment profit ₹128.28 Cr FY26 vs ₹93.73 Cr
FY25 (+₹34.55 Cr); Synthetic yarn segment profit ₹14.08 Cr FY26 vs ₹1.36 Cr FY25 (+₹12.72 Cr, a
+934% increase). Synthetic yarn is only 15.6% of revenue but contributed 27% (₹12.72 Cr of ₹47.27
Cr) of the total segment-profit improvement. Pass 1 discussed segment revenue growth and the
consolidated segment-profit aggregate (₹142.36 Cr vs ₹95.09 Cr) but did not decompose which segment
drove the improvement. This changes the operating story materially: the yarn business, historically
the smaller and weaker segment, appears to be turning around faster (on a margin basis) than the
core building-products business — worth a management question on what specifically drove the yarn
margin inflection (cost, pricing, mix) and whether it repeats.

### 9. 🟡 Company's own mandated ratio-variance disclosures self-attribute several headline ratio
improvements to the exceptional item — a direct management admission reinforcing Pass 1's
normalisation thesis (Finding #7 in Pass 1).
Note 44 standalone (AR FY26 p.205-206), "Reasons for variance of above 25%" column: Debt Service
Coverage Ratio +85% "primarily on account of increase in earnings available due to exceptional
items"; Return on Equity +55,250% "primarily on account of increase in net profits after taxes due
to exceptional items"; Net Profit Ratio +52,800% "due to exceptional items... and increase in
margins"; Return on Capital Employed +243% "due to decrease in borrowings, increase in earnings on
account of exceptional items and increase in margins." This is the company's own Schedule
III-mandated disclosure conceding that most (not all — margin improvement gets partial credit too)
of the headline ratio improvement is exceptional-item-driven. Useful as a direct citable admission
rather than an analyst inference, strengthening (not contradicting) Pass 1's Finding #1/#7.

### 10. 🟢 Undrawn borrowing headroom nearly doubled — additional evidence for the deleveraging
finding (Pass 1 Finding #4), from a different note.
Note 35(C)(i) standalone (AR FY26 p.196): undrawn borrowing facilities expiring within one year rose
to ₹264.88 Cr (₹26,488.00 lakhs) FY26 from ₹139.28 Cr (₹13,928.00 lakhs) FY25, +90.2%. Same note
also discloses forward guidance: "Management expects finance cost to be incurred for the year ending
31 March 2027 of ₹27.00 Cr" (₹2,700.00 lakhs) — a management estimate of continued finance-cost
decline versus FY26's actual ₹32.96 Cr, consistent with the deleveraging trend and a useful modelling
anchor not previously captured.
