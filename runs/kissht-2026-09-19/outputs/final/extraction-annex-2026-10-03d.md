## 3. The six lines asked about, side by side

Rs million. FY23 to FY25 and 9M FY26 from the RHP; FY26 from the AR. The Q4 FY26
column is my subtraction of the RHP 9M figure from the AR full-year figure. The 9M
figure is restated, the FY26 figure audited; same line names, same consolidated basis.

| Line | FY23 | FY24 | FY25 | 9M FY26 | FY26 | Q4 FY26 (derived) | 9M FY26 quarterly average (derived) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Branding and marketing | 576.07 | 1,076.49 | 957.74 | 1,763.64 | 2,586.96 | 823.32 | 587.88 |
| Business support services | 257.84 | 113.31 | 64.76 | 21.95 | 62.00 | 40.05 | 7.32 |

| Customer incentive cost | 1.28 | 69.67 | nil | nil | no row | – | – |

COMMENT.
- Branding and marketing rose 2.70x from FY25 to FY26 (my division), to 2,586.96. Q4
  FY26 alone carried 823.32, 40% above the 9M quarterly run rate (my arithmetic). The
  FY26 AR gives no line-level explanation. NOT DISCLOSED as to cause. The RHP's
  stated context is the Sachin Tendulkar campaign (section 5).
- Outsourcing and back office rose 1.53x from FY25 to FY26 (my division). Q4 FY26 alone
  carried 887.88, nearly twice the 9M quarterly average of 467.55 (my arithmetic). This
  is the line that pays contract collection staff (section 4). Its Q4 spike comes one
  quarter BEFORE Kissht's Stage 2 rose from 2.4% to 3.15% and GNPA from 2.12% to 2.25%
  in Q1 FY27 (phase 1 findings). The AR does not explain the Q4 jump. NOT DISCLOSED as to cause.
- The RHP names this line's content once. QUOTE (RHP PDF p.358 / printed p.352, MD&A,
  Fiscal 2025 compared to Fiscal 2024): "decrease in outsourcing and collection expenses
  by 31.92% from ₹2,205.42 million in Fiscal 2024 to ₹1,501.45 million in Fiscal 2025.
  This decrease was primarily on account of improved asset quality and customer
  repayment behavior which significantly reduced the requirement of outsourcing field
  agents and tele-calling staff for collections and recovery processes." The MD&A calls
  it "outsourcing and collection expenses"; the note calls it "Outsourcing and back
  office expenses"; the figures are identical. So by the company's own description,
  this line moves with collection effort, and collection effort was outsourced.
- Business support services fell from 257.84 (FY23) to 21.95 (9M FY26), then 40.05 in
  Q4 FY26 alone, more than five times the 9M quarterly average (my arithmetic). No
  explanation. NOT DISCLOSED.
- CIBIL and verification grew steadily and its Q4 FY26 figure sits just below the 9M
  run rate: a volume cost, consistent with disbursement pacing.
- Customer incentive cost existed only in FY23 (1.28) and FY24 (69.67), and has no row
  in FY25, 9M FY26 or FY26. Whether it was discontinued or reclassified into another
  line (branding, for example) is NOT DISCLOSED.
- Credit Guarantee fees (19.93) appear for the first time in the FY26 AR and are absent
  from the RHP's 9M FY26 column, so the line arose in Q4 FY26 or was reclassified then.
  NOT DISCLOSED which.

QUOTE (AR PDF p.59): "There were 283 permanent employees on the rolls of the Company as

COMMENT, on payroll versus outsourced.
- Field agents are NOT on the permanent payroll, and this follows from the filed
  numbers alone. The collections team is 8,291 field agents and 1,074 tele-callers at
  31-Dec-2025. The ENTIRE permanent workforce on the same date is 1,958, of whom 291
  sit in "Customer service and collection". So at least 9,074 of the 9,365 field agents
  and tele-callers (8,291 plus 1,074 is 9,365; minus 291 is 9,074, my arithmetic) cannot
  be permanent employees. The 13,192 contract workers the RHP discloses are where they
  sit. The RHP never states in one sentence that the field agents are contractors; the
  arithmetic forces it.
- "Managed internally" and "managed in-house" therefore mean Kissht directs the
  collection work through its own systems and app, not that collectors are employees.
  Both claims are compatible with an outsourced workforce, and the MD&A's own words
  ("the requirement of outsourcing field agents and tele-calling staff", RHP PDF p.358)
  confirm the field agents and tele-callers are outsourced.
- Field agents fell from 8,291 (31-Dec-2025, RHP) to "over 7,000" (AR, FY26 narrative),
  while tele-callers stayed "more than 1,000". The AR gives no exact count and no date
  beyond the report itself. A decline of up to about 1,291 field agents is implied
  (my arithmetic, on a rounded floor).
- Collections headcount on the permanent roll: 291 in "Customer service and
  collection" plus 260 "supervision staff" in the collections table. Whether the 260
  supervisors are among the 291, among the 1,958 at all, or contract staff is NOT
  DISCLOSED.
- Technology headcount: 331 permanent employees in "Technology and product", 16.91% of
  the total (RHP PDF p.194 / printed p.188, and p.128 / printed p.122).
- LAP headcount: 970 permanent employees, 49.5% of all permanent staff (my division),
  against LAP at 5.77% of AUM at 31-Dec-2025 (extraction annex 1). Branch count for LAP
  at that date was 82 (RHP PDF p.198). Per-branch headcount: NOT DISCLOSED.
- Permanent attrition was 53.03% in 9M FY26 (RHP PDF p.211), the highest in the series.
- FY26 group headcount by function: NOT DISCLOSED. The FY26 AR gives only the listed
  parent's own headcount of 283 at 31-Mar-2026; Si Creva's staff and the contract
  workforce are not reported there. The FY26 AR carries no BRSR employee table. Where it
  would sit: a BRSR (Business Responsibility and Sustainability Report) section, which
  this AR does not contain, or Si Creva's own annual report, not in the corpus.

## 5. Branding: the brand ambassador paid partly in shares

QUOTE (RHP PDF p.239-240 / printed p.233-234, "Other material agreements"):

> "Services agreement dated December 16, 2024, entered into between Sachin Ramesh
> Tendulkar ("SRT"), SRT Sports Management Private Limited ("SRT Sports"), and our
> Company ... as amended by the amendment agreement dated July 25, 2025 ... Pursuant to
> the SRT Services Agreement, our Company has availed certain services of the SRT
> including grant of workdays for shooting, recording and creating promotional
> materials for marketing of financial products and services offered or facilitated by
> our Company and our Subsidiary including products and services operated under
> 'PayWithRing' and 'Kissht' ... The SRT Services Agreement has been entered into
> between the Parties for a period of three years ("Term"), for an aggregate
> consideration of ₹60.00 million including taxes as the fixed consideration. Further,
> under the SRT Services Agreement, SRT was entitled to receive 53,778 Series Z4 CCPS of
> our Company during the Term, pursuant to which SRT was allotted 17,926 Series Z4 CCPS
> each on February 17, 2025, June 6, 2025, and June 28, 2025."

QUOTE (AR PDF p.38, Board's Report, paid-up capital):

> "Series Z4 compulsorily convertible cumulative preference shares 06-06-2025 Mr. Sachin
> Ramesh Tendulkar 17,926 2,232 NA Allotment made for consideration other than cash /
> Series Z4 compulsorily convertible cumulative preference shares 28-06-2025 Mr. Sachin
> Ramesh Tendulkar 17,926 2,232 NA Allotment made for consideration other than cash"

QUOTE (RHP PDF p.301 / printed p.295): "The Group has alloted 53,778 shares of face value
of Rs.10, pursuant to service agreement with Mr.Sachin Ramesh Tendulkar against the
services received by the Group."

COMMENT.
- The brand ambassador is paid Rs 60.00 million in cash over three years plus 53,778
  Series Z4 CCPS issued for services. At the stated issue price of Rs 2,232, the two FY26
  allotments of 17,926 each are worth Rs 80.02 million and all three allotments Rs 120.03
  million (my arithmetic on the AR issue price; the February-2025 allotment's price is
  not quoted here).
- Where the share-settled portion is expensed: NOT DISCLOSED. It could sit in
  "Branding and marketing expenses" or in share-based payments; neither note says.
- Tendulkar is also a shareholder under the shareholders' agreement (deed of adherence
  dated 14-Feb-2025, RHP PDF p.235). The ambassador is therefore an investor and a
  supplier at once. The services agreement carries an exclusivity in both directions
  (RHP PDF p.36 / printed p.30).
- 'PayWithRing' is named in the agreement as a Kissht product. That confirms the "Ring"
  app listed on SMFG's partner page is Kissht's own (counterparty annex, document 8,
  corrected in this commit).
