## FLAG-CASH: determination STRUCTURAL

Evidence:
- FY26 CFO minus Rs 10.76 Cr against PBT plus Rs 50.23 Cr (B01/B02; RHP p.57, Annexure III p.199).
- Trade receivables Rs 138.69 Cr, 93% of FY26 revenue; days sales outstanding about 340 on the ageing table (B02; Annexure XXXVI p.224, Annexure XX p.212).
- Zero doubtful debt provision across FY24, FY25 and FY26, while the over six month bucket rose to 20.4% of receivables (B02 receivables_trend).
- The driver is Quick Pay, a 47.02% customer the promoters part own, whose collection depends on Quick Pay receiving its own funding first (B04/B02; RHP p.25, p.90, p.93).
- Peers rule out a sector cause: Apsis collects in about 45 days and cut its top customer from 52% to 35%; Airfloa improved collection by 24 days (B06 Q2 CONTRADICTED; Apsis Jun 2026 p.5, Airfloa Jun 2026 p.4-5).

Why STRUCTURAL, not GROWTH-INDUCED: fast growth alone does not explain the gap, because peers grew and improved collection in the same window. The cash gap tracks a related party dependency, with collection gated by a third party's funding, plus zero provisioning against a 340 day book. That is a feature of who Millworks sells to, not a temporary working capital build that converts on its own.

Missing evidence to close (needed to size the structural gap; it does not revive the growth induced case): no credit rating rationale on file; no Quick Pay specific receivable balance disclosed, so the Quick Pay to receivables link is inferred, not confirmed (B02/B03); no ageing beyond the single RHP over six month bucket.

Single next quarter falsification metric: Q1 FY27 operating cash flow. If CFO prints positive with the over six month bucket below 20.4% and Quick Pay share below 40%, the structural read weakens. If CFO stays negative with receivables above 90% of revenue, the structural read holds.
