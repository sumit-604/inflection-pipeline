## NEW FINDINGS

### 1. Subsidiary-level related-party fund flow with "Excellent Fincap Pvt
Ltd" is invisible in the standalone Notes and only surfaces on
consolidation — the single largest new finding this pass 🔴

The standalone Note 32(ii)/(iii) "Others" column (Excellent Fincap Pvt
Ltd, the KMP-influence-only related party) shows just loans taken/repaid
(≤Rs 1.50 Cr, already in Pass 1). But the **consolidated** Note 32(ii)/
(iii) "Others" column carries a materially larger, entirely separate set
of transactions that must originate at a subsidiary (almost certainly
GDAL, the only operating one) — none of which appear anywhere in the
parent's own standalone note:

| Item | FY26 Rs Cr | FY25 Rs Cr | Consolidated Note anchor |
|---|---|---|---|
| Advance given to Others | 11.17 | 33.05 | Note 32(ii), p.219 |
| Advance received back from Others | 0 | 46.55 | Note 32(ii), p.219 |
| Interest paid to Others | 0.31 | 0.25 | Note 32(ii), p.219 |
| Interest received from Others | 1.35 | 1.35 | Note 32(ii), p.219 |
| **Outstanding receivable from Others (closing)** | **12.40** | **0.95** | Note 32(iii), p.219 |

The outstanding receivable grew **+1,211% YoY**. The subsidiary
simultaneously pays and receives interest to/from the same counterparty,
and Interest Received is identical to the rupee in both years (Rs 1.35 Cr),
consistent with a fixed, unchanged principal generating flat income — a
detail with no supporting note. No nature, rate, tenure, or business
purpose for any of these flows is disclosed anywhere in the standalone or
consolidated notes. This is a genuine disclosure gap: an investor reading
only the parent's Note 32 (the natural place to look for RPT exposure)
would never see this ~Rs 11-33 Cr/year flow. 🔴 Halt-1 verification
priority, LBF2/LBF4.

### 2. GDAL has never generated positive operating cash flow in either
year disclosed 🔴

Consolidated Note 39 ("Financial information of non-controlling
interest," pre-elimination GDAL figures) shows:

| | FY26 Rs Cr | FY25 Rs Cr |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 45.41 | 6.99 |
| Net cash from/(used in) operating activities | **(17.44)** | **(26.66)** |
| Net cash used in investing activities | (112.05) | (201.17) |
| Net cash from financing activities | 128.62 | 124.65 |

(Note 39, p.221-222). GDAL's revenue grew +550.1% YoY (Rs 6.99 Cr to
Rs 45.41 Cr) — the ramp is real — but operating cash flow was negative
both years and financing (external debt, the parent loan, and the FY25
NCI infusion) has funded 100% of both operations and capex so far. This
is the cash-mechanics confirmation behind Pass 1's finding #3 (the
Rs 284.70 Cr incremental consolidated capital commitment) and directly
informs LBF3 at the subsidiary level.

### 3. GDAL's non-current liabilities went from near-zero to Rs 68.36 Cr
in one year 🔴

Same Note 39 table: GDAL non-current liabilities Rs 6,836.28 lakh (FY26)
vs Rs 4.76 lakh (FY25) — a jump from effectively nil to Rs 68.36 Cr.
Current liabilities Rs 25,218.85 lakh (FY26) vs Rs 15,218.48 lakh (FY25),
+65.7%. Non-current assets grew Rs 229.30 Cr to Rs 342.95 Cr (+49.6%),
current assets Rs 96.95 Cr to Rs 160.67 Cr (+65.7%) (Note 39, p.221).
This is the subsidiary-level detail behind Pass 1's aggregate finding
that consolidated debt exceeds standalone by Rs 117.30 Cr — GDAL took on
essentially all of its long-term debt in FY26 alone.

### 4. CARO Annexure resolves the unexplained Rs 13.43 Cr excise/tax
jump Pass 1 flagged 🟡 (downgrades Pass 1's "zero narrative" concern,
does not remove it)

CARO Annexure B, clause 3(c) (p.141-142) itemises disputed statutory dues
by forum and financial year:

| Statute | Forum | FY(s) | Rs Cr |
|---|---|---|---|
| Central Excise Act | CESTAT | 2013-14 to 2014-15 | 0.02 |
| Goods & Service Tax Act | GST (Appeal) | 2021-22 | 0.29 |
| Goods & Service Tax Act | GST (Appeal) | 2019-20 to 2023-24 | **13.09** |
| Goods & Service Tax Act | GST (Appeal) | 2019-20 | 0.03 |
| Income Tax Act | ITAT | 2021-22 | 0.99 |

Sum of the first four rows = Rs 13.43 Cr, ties exactly to Note 33's
"Disputed demand under Central Excise & Commercial Tax U.P." line. So the
259x YoY jump is overwhelmingly (97.4%) one multi-year GST appeal
spanning FY2019-20 through FY2023-24, not excise. The forum (GST Appeal)
is now known; the underlying tax position and probability of success are
still NOT FOUND IN DOCUMENT.

### 5. Note 31.2's classification of "Non-current investment" as
amortised-cost contradicts the stated fair-value policy, and its figure
does not reconcile to Note 5 🟡 — a second disclosure-integrity item in
the same family as Pass 1's Note 8 duplicate-ageing-table flag

Note 31.2 "Categories of financial instruments" (p.171) lists "Non-current
investment" under "Financial assets measured at amortised cost," carrying
value Rs 4,334.00 lakh, **unchanged in both FY26 and FY25** despite the
new Rs 175.00 lakh Isharays Energy Two investment made during FY26 (Note
5). The "Fair Value" column shown alongside (Rs 5,523.33 Cr FY26 /
Rs 5,348.33 Cr FY25) does tie exactly to Note 5's investment-in-subsidiaries
plus unquoted-investment total for each year — but Note 2I.1.III states
investments in subsidiaries are carried at FAIR VALUE, not amortised
cost, so the "amortised cost" classification itself is internally
inconsistent with the accounting policy note. The Rs 43.34 Cr carrying
value in Note 31.2 does not match any figure in Note 5 for either year.

### 6. CARO Annexure discloses an unexplained ~Rs 11 Cr loan to a
non-subsidiary "Other" party 🟡

CARO Annexure B clause 3(a) (p.141): "Aggregate amount granted/provided
during the year — Other [Loans]: 500.00" (Rs 5.00 Cr) and "Balance
outstanding — Other [Loans]: 1,097.99" (Rs 10.98 Cr). This is separate
from the "Other [Investment]" figure (which ties cleanly to Note 5's
unquoted investments). No loan to a non-subsidiary counterparty is
separately identified anywhere in the standalone Notes (Note 11 Other
Current Assets carries only "Loan to Subsidiaries" as a named line). The
Reg 34(3) Corporate Governance certificate (p.84, Certificate of
Non-Disqualification / loans disclosure) confirms loans to
director-interested firms/companies are NIL, so this is not that. Nature
and counterparty NOT FOUND IN DOCUMENT.

### 7. True combined employee-benefit provision is Rs 13.34 Cr, not the
Rs 11.65 Cr Pass 1 cited — a current-portion piece sits in a different
note 🟡

Pass 1's Section 9/finding #10 used only Note 15 (long-term provisions):
Gratuity Rs 10.25 Cr + Compensated absences Rs 1.40 Cr = Rs 11.65 Cr.
Note 20 "Other Current Liabilities" (p.167) separately carries the
current portion: Provision for Gratuity Rs 1.55 Cr (FY26) / Rs 1.09 Cr
(FY25), Provision for Compensated Absences Rs 0.14 Cr / Rs 0.13 Cr. True
combined total: **Rs 13.34 Cr (FY26) vs Rs 11.74 Cr (FY25), +13.7% YoY**.
The no-actuarial-assumption-note gap Pass 1 flagged still stands and now
covers a larger balance.

### 8. Unhedged commodity price risk is quantified at ~65% of FY26 PBT 🟡

Note 31.6 (p.173-174): a 5% move in key input prices (HR Coil, Angle
shape and section, Ingot, Zinc) would swing profit by **Rs 151.12 Cr
(FY26) vs Rs 143.41 Cr (FY25)** — against FY26 PBT of Rs 232.91 Cr, that
is 64.9% of PBT for a single 5% commodity move. The note confirms: "the
Company as a matter of policy has not hedged the commodity risk." Pass 1
covered the forex hedge book (Note 31.5) but not this larger, entirely
unhedged exposure.

### 9. Key Audit Matter is the same single item in both years and both
reports — reinforces, and gives an authoritative second source for,
Pass 1's capitalisation-threshold gap 🟡

Both the standalone (p.135) and consolidated (p.181) Independent
Auditors' Reports name exactly one Key Audit Matter: "Property, Plant &
Equipment and Capital Work in Progress — valuation and existence...
including assessment of useful lives and residual value," citing
capitalisation judgement explicitly. No GDAL-specific KAM (goodwill,
NCI measurement, the Rs 284.70 Cr incremental capital commitment) was
raised despite GDAL's scale — worth noting as an absence, not asserted
as an audit deficiency.

### 10. Note 22 "Other Income" — precision correction to Pass 1's
finding #8, plus a new interest-income growth data point 🟡

Pass 1's Rs 4.51 Cr (FY26) / Rs 23.23 Cr (FY25) figures are the "Other
Income" SUB-LINE within Note 22 (p.167), not the note's total. Total
Other Income (as shown on the face of the P&L, p.146) is **Rs 22.85 Cr
(FY26) vs Rs 29.93 Cr (FY25), -23.6% YoY** — a smaller fall than the
sub-line alone suggests, because the "Interest" sub-line within the same
note grew **+169.6% YoY (Rs 6.70 Cr to Rs 18.06 Cr)**, largely driven by
the GDAL loan interest (Rs 12.41 Cr of the Rs 18.06 Cr total, per Note
32). The Rs 14.00 Cr FY25 one-off gain-on-investment non-recurrence
finding from Pass 1 stands; this note refines the surrounding numbers so
a downstream stage does not mis-cite "Rs 4.51 Cr" as the total other
income line.

### 13. Four of five subsidiaries keep manual (non-audit-trail) books 🟢
minor

Consolidated Note 43 (p.224): "In respect of 4 subsidiaries, books of
accounts are maintained and written up manually, therefore reporting
under Rule 11(g) is not applicable for these subsidiaries." The four
dormant shells (Goodluck Infrapower, GLS Steel, GLS Engineering, GLS
Metallics) are outside the MCA audit-trail (edit-log) regime that covers
the Parent and, by implication, GDAL. Low materiality given these
entities are dormant.

### 14. Promoter-family remuneration and payables are larger at
consolidated level than standalone — a subsidiary-level RPT layer Pass 1
did not see, nuancing (not reversing) its constructive finding #9 🟡

Consolidated Note 32(ii) Remuneration Paid (p.219): Relatives of KMP
Rs 3.385 Cr (consol) vs Rs 2.885 Cr (standalone, Pass 1's figure) —
Rs 0.50 Cr higher; Persons belonging to Promoter Group Rs 8.711 Cr
(consol) vs Rs 7.311 Cr (standalone) — Rs 1.40 Cr higher. Combined,
GDAL (or another subsidiary) pays an incremental ~Rs 1.90 Cr in
remuneration to promoter-family individuals that is invisible in the
parent's own Note 32. Consolidated "Other Liabilities" to relatives/
promoter group (Rs 0.20 Cr / Rs 0.52 Cr) similarly exceed the standalone
figures (Rs 0.12 Cr / Rs 0.32 Cr) by a combined Rs 0.27 Cr. Since GDAL's
own operating cash flow is negative (finding #2 above), promoter-family
members drawing incremental pay from that subsidiary is a governance
nuance worth carrying forward, even though the parent-level 45.1% pay cut
(Pass 1 finding #9) remains the dominant, and genuinely constructive,
signal.

### 15. Non-audit fees to the statutory auditor now exceed the audit fee
itself 🟢 minor

Note 29 (p.170): Statutory Audit Fees Rs 10.00 lakh (FY26) vs Rs 9.00
lakh (FY25); Other Services Rs 11.21 lakh (FY26) vs Rs 6.02 lakh (FY25),
+86.2% YoY. Non-audit fees now exceed the audit fee (Rs 11.21 lakh vs
Rs 10.00 lakh). Immaterial in absolute Rupee terms (Rs 21.21 lakh total);
named as a mild auditor-independence trend, not a red flag at this size.
