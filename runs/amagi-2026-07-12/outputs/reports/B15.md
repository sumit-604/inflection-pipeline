## 3. VALUE TRAP TEST

What makes AMAGI LOOK investable: 30% headline growth, 69% gross margin, 126% NRR, Rs 1,664 Cr net cash, debt-free, MASSIVE TAM, TRUSTWORTHY promoter. What is hiding behind it:

- **The cash pile disguises a low-return operating core.** The entire "investable" verdict is manufactured by excluding Rs 1,664 Cr from the ROCE denominator (deliberation override 1). Strip the accounting construct and the shareholder owns a business returning ~4-5% (B10 roe_pct 4.08) with no deployment plan and a 2-for-2 M&A write-off record (B02 rank 6). That is a melting-ice-cube risk dressed as a compounder: the "20-30% operating ROCE" has never once been the actual return on equity the owner earns.
- **Growth quality is deteriorating beneath a rising headline.** cc growth 23% < guided 25% floor, NRR 127→126, logo adds 67→29, concentration 33.7→40.2% (B05, B10). Faster reported growth from a narrowing, FX-flattered, price-renegotiated base is exactly what a late-stage-VC revenue curve looks like before it plateaus.
- **The numbers themselves are auditor-modified.** Three straight years of Rule 11(g) audit-trail deficiency with tampering not ruled out (B02 rank 1). Every quality metric the bull cites inherits that reliability discount.
- **The Kernex parallel is live.** Reported OCF negative for a full profitable year with receivables outrunning revenue (B10) is the textbook working-capital trap the FTTCP cash-DECLINING/Kernex cap is designed to catch (deliberation line 24). The thesis labels it INDETERMINATE; the FY26 full-year print already leans DECLINING.

**Value-trap verdict: elevated.** Not confirmed (growth and NRR are genuinely high), but the reasons it looks cheap-on-quality are precisely the reasons it may not be.

## 4. VARIANT PERCEPTION CHECK

**My variant perception (the thesis's):** the market prices AMAGI as a ~40x FY30 low-cash-return name; I claim the operating business is a 20-30% ROCE compounder worth 21x, so there is a real business here and a real buy price far below.

**Argue the market is right:** the market is NOT netting out the cash — it is telling you the cash is worth what idle cash is worth, and that the operating return the owner actually captures is ~5% (B10). The market also sees 23% cc growth decelerating into concentration, a flat 10% margin guide, a full year of negative OCF, three years of audit-trail modifications, and a related-party Rs 25 stake stacked six months before a Rs 361 IPO. A rational buyer pays a high multiple on today's small PAT ONLY because it trusts the forward compounding — and simultaneously discounts the reliability of the forward numbers because of the audit trail and the cash it cannot verify will ever convert. The market's "expensive on trailing, skeptical on forward" stance is internally consistent. The variant perception assumes the operating-ROCE-ex-cash construct is real economic return; the market's implicit view is that until the cash is deployed and OCF turns durably positive, it is not.

## 7. PRE-MORTEM (2 years out, down 40%)

It is mid-2028. The stock is well below any Rs 109-155 entry an aggressive buyer took. What went wrong: the first two "clean" quarters of FY27 never printed clean. ESOP-settlement cash and a step-up in the AWS take-or-pay ramp (Rs 24,176 mn commitment, only Rs 5,500 mn IPO-funded, B02 rank 2) kept operating cash flow negative, and trade receivables — already 98 DSO and growing 44% (B10) — climbed further as the concentrated top-10 (now past 45%) stretched payment terms. The FTTCP cash verdict resolved DECLINING, the Kernex cap engaged, and the operating-ROCE-RECOVERING premise collapsed because operating capital inflated exactly as the deliberation's own falsifier warned (line 22: "receivables keep outpacing revenue and stall operating ROCE").

Meanwhile growth normalized. cc growth, already 23% and below guidance (B05), drifted toward the mid-teens as the streaming/FAST land-grab matured and CPM pressure bit the Monetization line. NRR slipped through 120%. The market, which had been paying ~40x FY30 for a compounding story, re-rated toward the earned 21.0x/14.7x (B11) as the compounding story became a 10%-margin, low-cash-conversion services story.

The governance overhang finally priced in. A slow Rule 11(g) remediation and an adverse turn in the Rs 1,175 mn TP matter (B08) forced a discount on the reliability of the reported book, and the Vinculum Rs 25 episode (B02 rank 7) resurfaced in proxy commentary. None of it was a fraud; all of it was visible in January 2026. The loss came from paying for a forward that was never disclosed clearly enough to underwrite, on numbers an auditor had modified for three years running.
