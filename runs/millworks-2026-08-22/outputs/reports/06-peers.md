## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

**2A Demand environment.** FY26 was bifurcated by export exposure. Unimech (export-heavy aero tooling, 95% export) had a weak FY26 due to US tariffs, recovering only from Q4 FY26/Q1 FY27 (Q1 FY27 revenue +71% YoY, largely Hobel-driven) (UNIMECH, Q1 FY27 call, p.4). Apsis (defence/aerospace, ~90% components delivered within India even where OEMs are foreign) grew revenue 49.56% YoY in FY26 (APSISAERO, Jun 2026, p.4). Airfloa (rail, largely domestic) grew revenue 66% YoY in FY26 (544516, Jun 2026, p.3). Consensus: India-domestic-facing defence/rail demand was strong and undisturbed through FY26; export-facing aero tooling was the volatile leg, disrupted by US tariff policy, not by underlying industry demand.

**2B Pricing and input costs.** Airfloa explicitly flags severe raw-material inflation — aluminium up "more than 80%," stainless steel up "60% to 65%" YoY — attributed to "the war," only ~50-60% offset by contractual price-variation clauses, compressing EBITDA margin from 25% (implied prior) to 20.1% in FY26 despite strong revenue growth (544516, Jun 2026, p.4, p.9). Apsis separately cites "raw-material costs increased due to geopolitical uncertainties, supply-chain disruptions and foreign-exchange fluctuations" as one of three drivers behind its EBITDA margin moderation from 49% to 37% (APSISAERO, Jun 2026, p.4). Unimech does not flag RM inflation in these four calls; its gross margins stayed at 65-73% throughout, with EBITDA volatility driven by revenue/fixed-cost absorption, not input costs. This is a live, sector-relevant risk (RM/FX cost pressure only partially covered by pass-through) that two of three peers name explicitly.

**2C Capex cycle.** All three peers are expanding simultaneously — this is an industry-wide capacity race, not a lone expander. Unimech: Hobel Bellows acquisition (Rs.450cr EV) plus a $30mn Saudi JV with Kanoo Group. Apsis: Unit 2 (~Rs.60cr, doubling capacity) with Unit 3 already being scoped. Airfloa: new 14-acre facility (50,000-100,000 sq ft), consolidating seven existing units into two. All three either recently IPO'd or hold standing authority to raise capital (Apsis SME IPO already completed; Unimech board resolution to raise up to Rs.750cr via QIP, framed around minimum public shareholding compliance rather than immediate need, UNIMECH Q1 FY27 call, p.16-17; Airfloa using bank debt now, flagging a possible FY2028 equity need). Against this backdrop, Millworks holding capex at 72-77% utilisation without ordering machinery reads as conservative relative to a peer set that is capitalising aggressively into a shared sector tailwind (India aerospace/defence/rail localisation).

**2D Competitive mentions.** No peer transcript names Millworks or gives an unmistakable description of it. Competitor names volunteered: Unimech names MTAR (nuclear, listed) and describes aero tooling as having "not much competitors in India" (UNIMECH, Feb 2026, p.10). Airfloa names Kineco, Hindustan Fiberglass Works, DTL Ancillaries, Vibgyor, Universal Engineers, Tata, and Fainsa (seating segment) (544516, Jun 2026, p.9). None overlaps with Millworks by name in the transcripts reviewed.

**2E Risks peers discuss that Millworks context does not surface.** (1) Unhedged FX/tariff exposure causing a booked currency loss (Unimech, Rs.9.6cr in one quarter). (2) Raw-material inflation (aluminium, steel) only partially covered by price-variation clauses, tied to global conflict (Airfloa, Apsis). (3) China export-control/end-user-certificate delays disrupting precision-machinery procurement timing (Airfloa, Jun 2026, p.19). (4) Long qualification cycles (AS9100, NADCAP) gating entry into higher-value aerospace work, cited as a multi-year barrier by both Unimech (6-9 months for AS9100 alone, 2-3 years for full aerospace qualification, UNIMECH Q4 FY26 call, p.10) and implicitly by Apsis. (5) Defence tender bidding unpredictability — L1/L2 outcomes outside company control even against large bid volumes (Unimech, nuclear tenders). (6) Working capital stretching structurally as project-based/turnkey mix grows, flagged proactively by Unimech as a forward risk to its own receivable cycle, not treated as resolved.

## PART 4: TRIANGULATION SUMMARY

- Claims verified: 1 of 7 (Q4, utilisation threshold and equity/debt-before-machinery sequencing).
- Claims partially verified: 5 of 7 (Q1, Q3, Q5, Q6, Q7).
- Claims contradicted: 0 of 7.
- Claims unverifiable: 1 of 7 (Q2, receivables/testing-delay mechanism — though the contrast itself is informative).
- Single most consequential finding: no peer discloses a receivables cycle anywhere near Millworks' reported 178 days / 48.9% of revenue (peer range: 45-160 days). None of the three peers attributes elongated receivables to geopolitics-versus-counterparty testing delays. This is not a contradiction (no peer denies the mechanism), but the magnitude gap is large enough that it should be treated as a priority item for synthesis: either Millworks' receivables profile is genuinely sector-atypical, or the driver is company/counterparty-specific rather than sector-wide.
- Single strongest independent confirmation: three independent peers (Unimech-Hobel, Apsis, Airfloa) converge on both the utilisation threshold for further capex (80-100%) and the funding sequence (equity or debt secured at or before the machinery order, never after). Millworks holding capex at 72-77% utilisation without ordering machinery is directly consistent with this sector pattern.
- Overall: the peer set complicates rather than cleanly supports or undercuts the Millworks narrative. It supports the capex-discipline and capacity-utilisation framing (Q4) and the railway capex-to-order-inflow thesis (Q5). It complicates the receivables picture (Q2) by showing materially shorter cycles across the sector with no peer citing a comparable driver, and it complicates the "demand environment" framing by showing FY26 was export/tariff-driven volatility for aero tooling but robust, capacity-short growth for domestic defence/rail — a distinction Millworks' own classification needs to sit inside correctly. No peer evidence directly undercuts Millworks; the evidence instead narrows and sharpens where the operator should look next (receivables driver, RM/FX exposure, sector-wide capacity race positioning).

## PART 5: CROSS-PEER HYPOTHESIS

All three peers are simultaneously (a) newly capitalised (Apsis via SME IPO completed FY26; Airfloa listed Sept 2025; Unimech listed with standing QIP authority up to Rs.750cr) and (b) using that capital to add capability inorganically at prices below their own public-market multiples — Unimech acquired Hobel Bellows at roughly 6-7x EBITDA against analyst pushback that "other peers... are trading at roughly around 25 to 30 EV/EBITDA" (UNIMECH, Acquisition update call, p.9), while the seller's stated motive was a succession gap, not distress. Airfloa is independently building a "channel partner" execution network specifically to avoid capital-intensive organic footprint expansion across India's zonal railway system (544516, Jun 2026, p.13-14).

Hypothesis: newly listed micro-cap precision manufacturers in aero/defence/rail are converging on the same playbook — using IPO/QIP-era capital not primarily to build greenfield capacity, but to acquire unlisted, family-owned, succession-constrained component and subsystem makers at a structural multiple discount to their own listed valuation, and to use asset-light partner/channel networks rather than owned facilities to reach geographically dispersed government end-customers. This is a distinct, testable pattern (watch for further sub-scale bolt-on acquisitions of unlisted succession-exit sellers by these three names, and for channel-partner-style execution models spreading to Apsis and Unimech within 12-24 months) that is not stated by any single peer describing only its own transaction, but emerges from comparing all three simultaneously.
