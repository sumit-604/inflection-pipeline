## THE KILL SHOT — THE MULTIPLE IS UNEARNED

The thesis says so itself: everything rests on the growth multiple holding through FY29. So aim there.

**The multiple is unsupported by cash returns.** Post-tax return on invested capital is 3.4 percent now and about 5.4 percent at the guided FY28 state [FILED/MGMT]. The cost of capital is near 10 percent [INFERENCE]. The company earns half its hurdle. The peer group trades at 11 to 15 times forward EBITDA on the same sub-cost-of-capital economics [SECONDARY]. A multiple that is not backed by cash returns is backed by sentiment. Sentiment on a growth infrastructure name survives exactly as long as growth accelerates. The moment growth decelerates, the multiple has nothing under it and it compresses.

**Growth deceleration is unforecastable and arrives without warning.** It is not a line you can watch trend down. It is a guidance cut, a delayed commissioning, a missed contracting quarter, a funding round that does not close. Any one of them reprices the multiple in a day. You cannot hedge a repricing you cannot time.

**Quantify what compression does.** One turn of the multiple is Rs 349 a share, 28 percent of the Rs 1,247 price [INFERENCE, from the Role 2 math]. A de-rating from 14x to 10x takes the FY29 equity from Rs 3,013 to Rs 1,617, a 46 percent fall [INFERENCE]. The debt absorbs the loss first, so the equity falls further than the multiple. The buyer is not long a fleet. The buyer is short volatility on a sentiment multiple, with leverage.

**The builder-to-yield transition is structural, not cyclical.** A mature contracted yield vehicle trades at 8 to 10 times [SECONDARY]. CleanMax must eventually become that vehicle: the fleet stops doubling, the growth optic fades, the market reprices it as the annuity it always was. The thesis assumes the market keeps paying builder multiples for a company whose destiny is to be a landlord. Ask the question the thesis does not: what year does it become a yield vehicle, and what does the equity look like the year the market decides it has arrived. At 8 to 10x on FY30 EBITDA, the FY29 equity is Rs 1,268 to Rs 1,617 after dilution, at or below today's price after a three-year hold. The structural re-rate down is not a risk. It is the base rate for the asset class. The thesis is a bet that it does not happen on the operator's watch.

## THE OPERATIONS

**Bikaner is a permanent impairment, not a temporary hitch.** Sector data puts temporary-connectivity curtailment at 50 to 60 percent in solar hours and close to 100 percent at midday in Rajasthan [SECONDARY]. The corridor fix underdelivered. The next transmission scheme is deadlocked. Nobody in the corpus has diagnosed the severity: the annual report, the RHP and three concalls do not quantify it. The Rs 170 Cr exposure should be treated as permanent, not seasonal [INFERENCE]. Worse: the roughly 800 MW unnamed central-grid pipeline lands in the same corridor [MGMT/INFERENCE]. If it commissions on the same temporary connectivity, the curtailment is not a one-site problem. It is a pipeline-wide problem the company has not disclosed and may not have modelled.

**The product gap is a revenue kill condition.** Data centres run 24 hours. CleanMax sells intermittent solar and wind with batteries measured in tens of MWh [MGMT]. The firm-power tier runs thousands of MWh [SECONDARY]. Serentica sells 55 to 85 percent round-the-clock power at scale [SECONDARY]. The 42 percent Data and AI book was won before the market shifted to firmed supply [INFERENCE]. The next 42 percent goes to whoever can firm the power. That is not CleanMax today. The bull case cites Data and AI demand growing 30 percent a year as a tailwind. It is a tailwind for firmed suppliers and a headwind for intermittent ones. CleanMax is on the wrong side of the shift its own bull case names.

**Serentica does not appear in any CleanMax filing.** Three earnings calls. An 859-page annual report. The most direct, fastest-growing competitor is named zero times [FILED]. Read that silence. Either management does not see the firmed-power competitor as relevant, which is a strategic blind spot, or it sees it and chooses not to disclose it, which is a disclosure problem. Both readings are bad. A company that does not name the competitor eating its next addressable market is either not watching or not telling.

**The build is a plan, not a commitment.** Only Rs 831 Cr of roughly Rs 8,150 Cr of remaining build is contractually committed at year-end [FILED, extraction 02 D4]. The FY28 guidance and the 1.5 GW annual run rate are management intentions backed by 10 percent of the required spend. When funding tightens, the uncommitted 90 percent is the first thing to slip. The growth that the multiple is paying for is the growth that is least locked in.

## THE EQUITY CLAIM

**Dilution moves the break-even before any operational miss.** CleanMax needs about Rs 2,200 Cr of equity a year against about Rs 1,300 Cr of internal generation [MGMT]. The Rs 900 Cr gap is filled with parent shares at whatever price the market offers. That dilution alone moves the break-even multiple from about 11.6x to 12.5 to 13x [INFERENCE]. The thesis needs 12 to 14x to work. Dilution eats the bottom of that band before the plants do anything wrong. The buyer starts the hold already needing the top half of the multiple range just to stand still.

**The leverage web is one event, not three.** The promoter borrowed Rs 670 Cr against 20.02 percent of promoter holding, with cover at 2.17x against a 2.25x requirement on the full facility [FILED]. Rikhab's 8.37 percent is funded by a Brookfield lending entity, with the promoter's son a party [FILED/SECONDARY]. Personal guarantees sit on six sampled SPV facilities [FILED]. These look like three separate risks. They are one. A share-price fall triggers a margin call on the 360 One pledge. A forced sale drops the price further. A lower price stresses the SPV guarantees and the Rikhab funding. The sequence is: price down, margin call, forced promoter sale, price down more, SPV or guarantee stress, control event. The cover is already at 2.17x against 2.25x. The buffer is 0.08x. A modest drawdown starts the chain.

**The supply overhang is a wall.** 33.9m shares freed on 26-Aug-2026 [FILED]. Brookfield's 21.31 percent unlocks around Feb-2027 [FILED]. FII holding fell from 29.8 percent to 11.2 percent in a single quarter [SECONDARY]. Someone large is already selling and has been. Ask the thesis: who is the marginal buyer that absorbs Brookfield's 21 percent, and at what price does the selling stop. The honest answer is a discount to the current price. The float is expanding into a shrinking institutional base.

**The Rs 1,524.80 Cr net-debt gap is unexplained debt until proven otherwise.** Two extractions could not close it [FILED, extraction 02 D2]. Rs 599 Cr may be prepaid NCDs. Rs 925.80 Cr is unexplained. If it is real debt the market is not counting, the equity is worth about Rs 90 a share less on the SOTP and the leverage ratios are worse than reported. An unreconciled gap on the debt line of a name levered 8x is not a rounding item. It is a hole in the number that matters most.

## VALUE TRAP TEST

This looks like a growth story. It is a capital-recycling treadmill that never reaches the harvest phase.

The harvest phase is when the fleet stops growing, the capex stops, the debt amortises, and the contracted cash flows accrue to the equity. That is when a levered annuity pays its owner. CleanMax never gets there on the current plan, because the promoter's incentive is gigawatts and grid corridors, not return on capital. Every rupee of internal cash and every rupee of new equity goes back into the ground as the next gigawatt. The treadmill runs as long as the promoter wants to build, and he wants to build for as long as grid access is scarce and worth grabbing. The harvest is on his timeline, not the shareholder's. A minority shareholder funds the land grab and waits for a harvest the controlling owner has no incentive to call. That is the trap: it never looks like a value trap because the growth optic never fades until the day it does, and by then the multiple has already gone.

## VARIANT PERCEPTION

The operator's belief is that the market multiple holds because peers hold it. Turn that around. The market is right to pay 11 to 15x for NTPC Green and Adani Green. NTPC Green has sovereign funding: it raises debt at rates a mid-cap cannot touch and equity from a government parent. Adani Green has conglomerate funding depth and a group balance sheet behind it. Their sub-cost-of-capital returns are cushioned by a cost of capital the market believes is structurally lower and a funding backstop the market believes is structurally there. CleanMax has neither. It is a promoter-led mid-cap with a leveraged promoter, a pledge at 2.17x cover, a temporary-connectivity curtailment problem, and a Rs 900 Cr annual equity gap it fills at market. The market is correct to extend the multiple to the funded incumbents and wrong to extend it to CleanMax. The peer comparison is the thesis's foundation and it is a category error. CleanMax is not a small NTPC Green. It is a levered mid-cap wearing the peer group's multiple.

## PRE-MORTEM — TWO YEARS ON, THE STOCK IS DOWN 40 PERCENT

It started with a print. Q3 FY27 operating PBT before other income slipped back below zero after the Q1 crossover, as a delayed Koppal block and a soft contracting quarter hit the same statement. The proof gate never fired its second consecutive quarter. The narrative of a company that had turned the corner on operating profit reversed to a company that had printed one good quarter and given it back. The market stopped treating the crossover as a trend.

Then the operations confirmed the fear. The Koppal bay commissioned on temporary connectivity, and the first full quarter of generation showed the Rajasthan-style curtailment the corpus had never quantified. The unnamed central-grid pipeline was in the same corridor after all. Analysts who had modelled the pipeline at full load cut their FY30 EBITDA. The Rs 4,100 Cr number that the whole valuation rested on came down, and every turn of multiple applied to a smaller base.

Then the equity structure did the rest. A block deal cleared at a double-digit discount as Brookfield began its exit into a thin institutional bid. The lower price took the 360 One pledge cover through its trigger and the promoter disclosed a top-up. With growth decelerating, a curtailment overhang, and a pledge in the news, the peer-group forward multiple for the whole sub-sector fell from 13x to 10x in a single quarter as the market reclassified promoter-led renewable mid-caps as yield vehicles with governance risk. Three turns of multiple on a reduced EBITDA base, amplified by 8x leverage, is a 40 percent fall. None of the plants stopped working. The stock still halved from its highs. The annuity was real the whole way down.

**Overall: the thesis is strong on the annuity and weak on the multiple.** It is a genuine 23-year contracted cash machine wrapped in a share price that pays in full for growth the assets cannot yet earn, sitting on a leveraged promoter and an expanding float. The annuity is not the question. The multiple is. At Rs 1,247 the bear case is that the buyer is paid to build gigawatts for the promoter and exposed, with leverage, to the day the market reprices growth to yield. **Let the operator weigh the strong annuity against the weak multiple. The devil's position is that the price already assumes the one thing the thesis admits it cannot control.**
