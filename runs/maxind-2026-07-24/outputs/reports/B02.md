## E. NOTES-BASED RED FLAGS

- **Earnings management**: none identified. No evidence of premature revenue recognition, cookie-jar provisioning, or expense deferral. If anything, DTA non-recognition on large carried-forward losses is conservative rather than aggressive (Standalone Note 20; Consol Note 13(iv)).
- **Aggressive accounting**: none identified. The one provisioning item that could be read as under- rather than over-provisioning is the static ECL allowance against a 4.5x-larger receivable base (Consol Note 8) — a conservatism gap in the opposite direction, not an aggressive-accounting red flag.
- **Undisclosed/under-disclosed risk indicators**:
  - Capital commitments reported as NIL for FY25 despite ongoing large-scale E360/E361/AGEasy capex described in operator context — a disclosure gap worth direct verification (Consol Note 35a, p.272).
  - MSME "nil interest paid/accrued/due" statement (Note 18(i)) sits inconsistently next to an ageing schedule (Note 44) showing MSME balances aged beyond one year — small in rupee terms but an internal-consistency gap between two sub-notes of the same disclosure.
  - Two different figures for the same Max Estates Noida Private Limited equity stake transaction (₹703.38 lakh vs ₹713.23 lakh) across the cash flow statement/RPT note and the investments note.
  - Two similarly-named but differently-scaled "marketing fee(s)" lines in Note 22 (₹22.31 Cr, within revenue) and Note 23 (₹2.39 Cr, within other income) create a real risk of double-counting for a reader reconstructing total marketing-fee income.

## F. ONE-LINE NOTES VERDICT

The notes reveal moderate accounting practices with real, auditor-corroborated weak spots rather than outright manipulation. Key concern: related-party transaction fairness and subsidiary-investment impairment judgment, both independently designated by the statutory auditor as the sole Key Audit Matter at their respective reporting levels, compounded by a widening loss concentrated in Antara Assisted Care Services and a receivables-growth/revenue-decline divergence with static provisioning. Key strength: conservative deferred-tax treatment (no DTA recognised on large carried-forward losses), clean unmodified audit opinions with no going-concern qualification, and a segment-level disclosure granular enough to correctly explain the headline revenue decline as a finance-lease timing artefact rather than demand weakness. Overall accounting quality: 5/10.
