## The picture in one paragraph

Thursday broke the base the market had been building for a week. It was a
heavy selling day. 314 stocks fell four and a half percent or more, against
only 19 that rose that much. Almost nine stocks in ten closed lower. Not one
stock index rose. The Nifty fell about one and a half percent, and the mid
and small indices fell more. The count of stocks above the two-hundred-day
average fell from 965 to 836. That is below the 1 October low near 900, and
the lowest since mid-April. The count above the fifty-day average fell from
758 to 561, well under the washout level near 700. New yearly lows beat new
highs by more than five to one. Turnover ran about a third above a year ago,
so the selling came with real volume. Fear jumped by a tenth. Only about one
stock in three still holds its long trend. The repair failed, and the market
is now making lower lows.

## Where the market stands

Start with what changed in one day. A week ago the market looked like a base.
The long count had fallen to about 900 on 1 October and then climbed back near
980. The short counts were rising. Small companies held their uptrend. On
Thursday all of that gave way at once. The long count fell to 836. The
fifty-day count fell to 561. The twenty-day count fell from 723 to 412. Each
of these sits below its 1 October low. When a market falls through the low
it had just bounced from, the bounce has failed. That is the plain meaning of
Thursday.

Now the longer story, so the size of this is clear. In March the market
crashed. On 23 March almost a thousand stocks fell hard in one session. By
early April only about 420 stocks held the two-hundred-day line. Then the
market healed. Some huge buying days came in April, one with more than 1,100
stocks jumping four and a half percent. By early May about 1,250 stocks held
the long line. On 12 May a shock sent about 500 stocks down hard, and June
repaired it. The count peaked near 1,370 in early August. September turned
bad. On 15 September about 340 stocks fell hard. The count slid to about 900
on 1 October. Thursday took it to 836, the lowest reading since mid-April,
when the market was still climbing out of the March crash.

So Thursday was the third big shock in five months. The 12 May shock sent
about 500 stocks down hard. The 15 September shock sent about 340. Thursday
sent 314. Each shock was a little smaller than the one before. But each one
hit a weaker market. On 12 May the long count fell to about 950. Thursday it
fell to 836, from a much lower start. This is not a March-sized washout. In
March the fifty-day count fell near 210. Today it sits at 561. The damage is
real, and it is still far from the extremes of the spring.

The day itself was wide and heavy, the opposite of Wednesday. On Wednesday
more than four stocks in ten moved less than three percent all day. On
Thursday fewer than one in five did. Prices swung hard. About four stocks in
ten traded four percent below their prior close at some point. More than half
of those closed near their low, so the sellers finished what they started.
Only about one stock in twelve traded four percent above its prior close, and
fewer than a quarter of those held the gain. Turnover ran about 520 crore
against about 380 a year ago, about a third higher. The share of busy stocks
rose. Heavy volume on a down day means sellers who mean it.

The size order still holds, but it narrowed. The microcap index fell almost
three percent. It still sits almost three percent above its thirty-week trend
line, and it is up about one percent over three months. That makes it the
only stock index still up over that span. The smallcap 50 sits about one and a
half percent above its line. The smallcap 250 has slipped just under. The
midcap 150 sits about five percent under its line. The Nifty closed near
22,200. It sits almost seven percent under its thirty-week line and is down
almost nine percent over three months. 28 of 35 indices now sit under their
slow line, up from 25 on Wednesday.

Fear rose sharply and gold still gave no shelter. The India VIX, a gauge of
how much traders pay for protection, jumped about ten percent to near 15.3.
It now sits about eleven percent above its thirty-week average and is up about
thirty percent over three months. Fear is no longer cheap. Gold funds fell
about nine percent over the month, more than the Nifty's fall of about seven.
Investors who left stocks for gold in September did worse than those who
stayed.

## What would change this read

The read tonight is a failed repair and a market making lower lows. It gets
worse if the long count keeps falling below about 800, the mid-April level.
It gets worse if new lows stay above about 200 for several sessions, or if
the deep down tail keeps growing past about 15 names. It also gets worse if the
VIX keeps climbing while turnover stays above last year's. That would mean
heavy, frightened selling. The step after that is a March-style washout,
with the fifty-day count falling toward about 300.

The cautious read fails if buyers return with force, and soon. The first test
is a thrust day of about 300 or more stocks jumping four and a half percent,
on volume above its year-ago level. April showed how fast that can happen
after a shock. The second test is the long count climbing back above about
900, the 1 October low, within a few sessions. The third is new yearly lows
falling back under about 100 while the VIX eases toward 14. Until one of
these shows up, the data favours caution over hope.
