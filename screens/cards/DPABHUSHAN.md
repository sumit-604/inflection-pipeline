## Business Understanding Narrative

D.P. Abhushan runs 12 jewellery showrooms under the D.P. Jewellers brand, based in
Ratlam, Madhya Pradesh, an 86-year family business in its fourth generation (Concall
p.1, p.9; Credit Rating p.4). It buys gold, silver and diamonds and sells them through
its own stores. Revenue is gold-dominated: gold was Rs 781 crore of Rs 854 crore total
in Q1FY27, about 91% (Concall p.6). Profit comes from making charges layered on the
gold price, not the gold itself (Concall p.15, p.18).

What is changing is reach and mix: from an 11-to-12-store MP and Rajasthan chain to a
planned 51-store, multi-state network by FY30, entering Gujarat this year and piloting
its first franchise store in Jabalpur (Concall p.5, p.9). Product mix is shifting
toward studded and diamond jewellery, from 6-7% today to 12-15% by March 2028 (Concall
p.12), alongside a new omnichannel layer (e-commerce and app launched this quarter,
Concall p.5).

Why now: elevated gold prices push demand toward value even as volumes soften
nationally, and 63% of the market remains unorganised (IP p.17), leaving room for a
trusted regional brand to take share. D.P.'s own claim: a single Indore store outsells
14-15 stores of a national brand combined (Concall p.11).

What must be true: volume growth has to move from 1-2% toward the 10% management
target for FY27 and FY28 (Concall p.14, p.20), since almost all revenue growth so far
comes from gold-price appreciation, not more customers. Store rollout has to run
faster than last year, when guidance for eight new stores in FY26 delivered only one
(Guidance Q3FY26; Concall p.9).

What breaks it: if gold prices stop climbing while volume growth stays near 1-2%, the
57-58% revenue growth of recent quarters cannot repeat, since price has done almost
all the work (Concall p.14).

## 2. Business model and archetype
A single economic engine: buy gold near spot, retail it through owned showrooms, earn a
making-charge spread plus a mix uplift from silver, diamond and studded lines.
Archetype: brand/franchise consumer, built on trust and format economics (new stores
break even in six to nine months, mature to Rs 150-400 crore turnover after three to
four years, Concall p.8). Gold is 90-95% of cost of goods (Credit Rating p.4): a
gold-price pass-through with a margin layer on top.

## 3. Competitive advantages
Real but narrow. Brand trust built over 86 years drives outsized single-store revenue
against multi-store national rivals in the same city (Concall p.11); old-gold exchange,
25% of Q1FY27 sales, funds purchases and locks in repeat customers (Concall p.4).
Swarn Plus, a gold-savings SIP launched April 2026, locks discretionary demand into a
forward pipeline (IP p.14). Against that: margin depends on making charges in a
"highly fragmented and competitive" industry (Credit Rating p.2), and 1-2% volume
growth (Concall p.14) says the moat pulls wallet share from existing customers, not new
ones. Moat: forming, not proven at the volume line.

## 4. The promoters
The Kataria family, fourth generation: Santosh Kataria (Chairman and Managing
Director), Anil Kataria (Whole-Time Director), Renu Kataria (Non-Executive Director),
plus promoters Vikas and Sanjay Kataria, active on calls without board seats (AR p.5;
Concall p.2). Founded 1940, listed November 2017 (Credit Rating p.4). Three SEBI
takeover-regulation declarations, filed April 2026, each state no encumbrance of
shares during FY2025-26. No pledge disclosed. Promoter shareholding percentage: NOT

## 5. Financial trajectory
Q1FY27: revenue Rs 854 crore versus Rs 541 crore (+58%). EBITDA Rs 94 crore versus
Rs 55 crore (+70%), margin 11.01% versus 10.21% (Concall p.5). PAT Rs 64 crore versus
Rs 36 crore (+77%), margin 7.55% (Concall p.5-6). Volume growth only 1-2%; 10-15% of
growth came from inventory gains, the rest from higher making charges (Concall p.14).
FY26 (guidance-sourced): revenue Rs 4,070 crore (+23%), EBITDA Rs 310 crore (+77%,
margin 7.61%), PAT Rs 212 crore (+88%). FY25: revenue Rs 3,312 crore (+42%), EBITDA
Rs 175 crore (+74%), PAT Rs 113 crore (+82%) (AR p.7; Credit Rating p.5). PBILDT margin
5.34% FY25 versus 4.38% FY24 (Credit Rating p.3).
Balance sheet (Credit Rating p.3-4): gearing 0.46x FY25 (0.72x FY24), interest coverage
10.37x FY25, inventory turnover 5.63x FY25, operating cycle 54 days flat, current ratio
1.77x.
Operating cash flow negative for three straight years, since 95-98% of assets are
inventory (Concall p.15). ROCE and total debt: NOT FOUND.
Inflection classification: price-led, not volume-led or margin-structural.

## 5A. FY27 forward read
FY26 actual: revenue Rs 4,070 cr, PAT Rs 212 cr, margin 5.2% (guidance). Management
guides 10% volume growth "going forward" with value growth on top from gold (Concall
p.14), no revenue number; basis (b) applies.
- Primary (b): Q1FY27 revenue grew 58% (Concall p.5). FY27E revenue Rs 6,425 cr. PAT at
  Q1's margin of 7.55%: Rs 485 cr, +129%. Forward P/E 6.3x, P/S 0.47x.
- Alternate: same revenue at the FY26 margin of 5.2%, since Q1's margin carried 10 to
  15% of growth from inventory gains (Concall p.14). PAT Rs 335 cr, +58%, P/E 9.1x.
- Separating observation: the gold price path. The 58% is price, not volume (volume
  1 to 2%). A flat gold price from here compresses both the revenue growth and the
  inventory gain in H2FY27; the May 2026 import-duty rise to 15% (Credit Rating p.1
  context) cuts the other way on realisation. Q2FY27 revenue growth below 30% moves
  the year toward Rs 5,500 cr and P/S 0.55x, with the P/E rising in step.

## 6. The transition and the quality ladder
Management's claim, quoted once: "we remain fully committed to our long-term vision" of
51 stores by FY30, 8-8.5% EBITDA margin, and a studded mix at 12-15% (Concall p.9;
IP p.6; guidance).
FROM R1/R2: a regional gold-price pass-through retailer, PBILDT 4.38% in FY24, margin
mostly a function of gold price and inventory timing, not structural advantage.
TO claimed R3: a branded, studded-mix-weighted, multi-state retailer with format
discipline and durable making-charge pricing power. Margin trend supports the
direction, 4.38% to 5.34% to 7.61% across FY24-FY26, but Q1FY27's 11.01% margin is
still mostly gold-price effects, not the studded-mix or volume shift the TO state
needs; diamond revenue fell 7% YoY (Concall p.6). ROCE, the ladder's other defining
variable, is NOT FOUND.

## 8. Proof check
What fired: revenue, EBITDA and PAT growth ran at 58-77%, margin expanded (11.01%
versus 10.21%), same-store sales growth was 52% (Concall p.6), and omnichannel and the
Gujarat entry are real, delivered steps.
What has not fired: volume growth, the metric management says matters most (Concall
p.20), sits at 1-2% against a 10% target. Studded and diamond mix has not moved;
diamond revenue fell. Store rollout missed its FY26 guidance badly, 1 added against 8
guided, alongside a QIP that raised only Rs 67.74 crore of a guided Rs 600 crore
(Credit Rating p.3).
Named proof point: whether FY27 additions reach "five to six" as promised, and whether
volume growth moves off 1-2%. A second rollout miss would be a credibility problem.

## 9. Flags from the documents
Beyond the rollout miss, fundraise shortfall and price-led growth named in step 8: gold
inventory is carried well below spot (book cost ~Rs 1,20,000 versus a prevailing
pure-gold price of Rs 1,51,000-1,52,000, inflating margin when prices rise, Concall
p.15); a management answer on FOCO/COCO mix contradicted itself within the same
response ("one to two will be FOCO," then "five to seven," of the 51-store target,
Concall p.9); and the gold import duty rose 6% to 15% in May 2026, temporarily slowing
expansion (Concall p.4-5, p.10).
Not seen: promoter shareholding percentage, ROCE, total debt, full cash flow
statement, auditor language, related-party schedule, contingent liabilities.

## 10. Independent check: the credit rating
CARE A-; Stable (long-term) / CARE A2+ (short-term), reaffirmed 8 January 2026
(Credit Rating p.1-2). Positive trigger: volume-driven growth plus PBILDT above 6%
sustained. Negative trigger: TOI below Rs 2,000 crore or PBILDT below 3% sustained,
operating cycle beyond 75 days, or gearing beyond 1x (Credit Rating p.2). As of
January 2026 the company sat comfortably inside the positive zone on every metric.
The gap: this rating rests on FY25 and H1FY26 numbers. It predates the May 2026 duty
hike, the Q1FY27 volume softness, and the FY26 store-rollout miss. Today's filings
carry the more current picture, per the framework's own rule.

## 11. Posture: Transition Decision Matrix
Proof gate: PARTIALLY FIRED. Revenue, EBITDA and PAT growth fired repeatedly, but the
two proof points that define the TO claim, volume-led growth and rollout pace, have
NOT: volume sits at 1-2% against a 10% target, and rollout missed its guidance by 7
stores in FY26.
Ugliness: thin, gold-price-dependent margin and three years of negative operating cash
flow. ARTIFACT-OF-CLIMB the studded-mix strategy is curing, or STRUCTURAL-FEATURE of a
making-charge model? The corpus leans STRUCTURAL: the curing levers have not moved,
and one moved backward (diamond revenue down).
Recognition gap: INDETERMINATE, no share price or PE ratio in this corpus.
Proof NOT FIRED on the load-bearing metrics plus STRUCTURAL ugliness maps to VALUE-TRAP
RISK. Classify DEEP WATCH unless disproven by volume and rollout data over the next
one to two quarters.

## 12. Verdict card
**WATCH.** D.P. Abhushan has real brand strength and a coherent transition story: more
stores, better mix, omnichannel, a forward-booked gold scheme. But the two metrics that
would prove the transition, volume growth and rollout pace, have not moved as
management says, and the company already missed its FY26 store guidance by a wide
margin while its guided Rs 600 crore QIP raised barely a tenth of that. Nearly all
recent headline growth is gold-price and inventory-gain arithmetic, not new customers
or a richer mix. Not disqualifying; a name to watch for evidence of execution, not yet
one for the full pipeline.
Load-bearing facts a deep run must verify first:
1. Whether Dahod and Jabalpur opened in Q2FY27, and whether five to six stores open in
   FY27 as promised.
2. Whether volume growth moves off 1-2% toward 10% over the next two quarters.
3. Studded and diamond mix trend; the stated margin lever moved backward.
4. Status of the Rs 600 crore QIP and how any further raise is priced.
5. Exact promoter shareholding percentage and any pledge beyond the declarations here.
6. ROCE and total debt, both NOT FOUND, needed before valuation under Amendment 26.
What would change the view: two quarters of volume growth above 5% with studded-mix
progress moves this toward PROCEED. A second rollout miss, or a raise well short of
guidance, moves it to PASS. No price here.
