## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

### 2A. Demand environment
The peer set does not show a uniform demand picture, which complicates any
reading of MPS's narrative as an "industry-wide AI boom."
- INDGN (closest healthcare/life-sciences read-across) shows accelerating
  demand across the year: revenue growth of +17.1% YoY (Q2 FY26, Oct-2025),
  +30.8% YoY (Q3 FY26, Jan-2026, helped by the BioPharm acquisition; +18.3%
  organic), +32.8% YoY (Q4 FY26, Apr-2026, first ever Rs 1,000 cr+ quarter),
  and +39.7% YoY (Q1 FY27, Jul-2026) — "the strongest quarter-on-quarter
  growth we have delivered in a first quarter in four years" (Manish Gupta).
  Management explicitly attributes this to clients re-engaging after policy
  uncertainty (MFN pricing, tariffs) resolved, and to AI-driven demand for
  centralization.
- DATAMATICS shows the opposite trajectory: growth decelerated across the
  same period, from +18.7% YoY (Q1 FY26, Aug-2025) to +20.5% (Q2 FY26) to
  +19.9% (Q3 FY26) down to only +9.9% YoY in the Q1 FY27 (Aug-2026) call,
  with management citing macro/tariff uncertainty and guiding to "high
  single-digit growth" for FY27 — a clear deceleration, not an AI boom.
- ECLERX stays consistently strong throughout (H1 FY26 USD revenue +20%
  YoY; 9M FY26 INR revenue +22% YoY), citing "cautiously optimistic" pipeline
  commentary and calling out AI-linked large deal wins in Q4 FY26.
- NIITMTS shows soft-to-mixed demand through the earlier quarters (client
  training-budget pressure) with signs of stabilization by Q4 FY26/Q1 FY27.
Net: the AI-driven demand story is real and strong specifically in the
healthcare/life-sciences vertical (INDGN) — the vertical closest to MPS's
Unbound Medicine bet — but is not a uniform, industry-wide pattern; the
broader BPO/publishing peer (Datamatics) shows deceleration over the same
window, complicating any inference that AI tailwinds are lifting the whole
peer set equally.

### 2B. Pricing and input costs
No peer discloses a margin story resembling MPS's without also disclosing
matching wage-inflation drag (see Claim 5b for anchors). eClerx additionally
raises a nuance absent from MPS's own commentary: AI adoption could be a
two-sided force on pricing, potentially deflating contract/booking values
even as it lifts delivery productivity (eClerx, Q4 FY26 call, 14-May-2026,
Kapil Jain). MPS's calls (per B05) frame AI purely as a margin tailwind;
none of the four peers frame it quite that one-sidedly.

### 2C. Capex cycle
This is a mixed situation, not an industry-wide capacity race:
- INDGN is a clear, aggressive expander: three acquisitions in FY26
  (BioPharm ~USD 65m, WARN & Co, Cake Communications) plus heavy GTM/tech
  investment causing a stated ~150bps EBITDA margin compression for 6-8
  quarters (Suhas Prabhu, Q2 FY26 call, 31-Oct-2025).
- DATAMATICS is a modest, opportunistic expander (one bolt-on, TNQTech,
  cash-funded).
- ECLERX is in capital-return mode, not expansion (INR 300 cr buyback,
  bonus issue) alongside organic AI platform investment, not M&A.
- NIITMTS shows no material M&A activity in the four quarters read; focus is
  on organic recovery.
Named situation: this is a "lone/few expander(s) among a mixed peer set,"
not an industry-wide capacity race. MPS's own aggressive M&A pace (Unbound
plus stated appetite for a second Education deal) sits closest to the INDGN
pattern rather than to the peer-set median.

### 2D. Competitive mentions
No peer transcript, across all 16 documents read, mentions MPS Ltd by name
or by unmistakable description. This silence is itself notable: none of
Datamatics, eClerx, Indegene, or NIITMTS treat MPS as a competitor, partner,
or reference point in any call in this window.

### 2E. Risks peers discuss that MPS's own calls do not (per B05 corpus)
- GCC/captive migration risk: eClerx and Datamatics both discuss clients
  moving BPO/KPO work in-house to their own Global Capability Centres as a

- Formal client-concentration risk disclosure: Indegene discloses top-5/

  granular concentration disclosure appears in MPS's calls per the B05
  corpus.
- Currency hedge mark-to-market volatility as a distinct disclosed risk to

  calls, on adoption of cash-flow hedge accounting) treat this as a named
  risk category; not raised as a distinct item in MPS's own calls.

## PART 5: CROSS-PEER HYPOTHESIS

Hypothesis: the AI-tailwind narrative in this peer set is bifurcating along
a specific axis — providers with proprietary domain data and outcome-based
pricing models (Indegene's Cortex/Tandem/Invisage stack; eClerx's Compliance
Manager and Market360 products) are capturing consolidating spend and
describing AI as a moat-widening tailwind, while providers whose business
still leans on generic capacity/time-and-material delivery (Datamatics'
core BPO book, and to a lesser extent NIITMTS's managed training book) show
decelerating growth and, in Datamatics' specific case, explicitly name a
client-side AI-adoption caution (author IP-rights concern in publishing) that
the domain-expert peers do not report facing. This is not stated by any
single peer; it emerges only from comparing eClerx's and Indegene's explicit
"consolidation is happening and we are winning it" language against
Datamatics' explicit "publishing is an AI-adoption laggard" language and its
own decelerating growth print in the same quarter (both from calls held in
the Jan-2026 window).

This is directly testable against MPS. MPS's own transition thesis rests on
proprietary-data, outcome-priced AI capability in STM publishing services (the
52% manuscript acceptance claim) — a segment adjacent to the exact one
Datamatics names as an AI-adoption laggard due to author IP-rights concerns.
If MPS sits in the "domain-expert, outcome-priced" bucket with Indegene and
eClerx, its Research segment margin (45.1% in Q1 FY27) should hold or expand
as the AI-driven consolidation mechanism plays out, similar to those two
peers' trajectories. If instead MPS sits closer to the "generic capacity"
bucket with Datamatics, its Research margin should revert toward the FY26
average (39.9%) as the current renewal cohort re-prices — which is already
named as the stage-06-independent kill-signal for this exact trigger in the
B05 handoff (priority 4). The peer pattern gives that kill-signal a sharper,
falsifiable edge: watch specifically whether MPS's publishing-adjacent
clients show the same IP-rights-driven caution Datamatics reports seeing in
its own publishing book, since that would be the first visible sign MPS is
migrating toward the generic-capacity bucket rather than the domain-expert
one.
