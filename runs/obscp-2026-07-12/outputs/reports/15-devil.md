## 7. PRE-MORTEM

It is July 2028. A re-engagement buyer who bought near Rs 240 in late 2026 (ignoring that the AVOID was really an AVOID) is down ~40% at ~Rs 145. What went wrong.

FY27 operating cash flow printed negative for a second consecutive year. The Rs 100-200 Cr/yr order-book conversion did not close the gap because working capital scaled with it exactly as CRISIL described: export customers took their "long credit periods," inventory built ahead of the Sanand and Supa lines, and gross current assets pushed past 223 days rather than falling below the 210-day upgrade trigger. When a clean annual report finally surfaced, the over-12-month receivables bucket was above the ~15% falsifier. The GROWTH-INDUCED ruling — always resting on unseen FY25-26 ageing and a credit analyst's silence — flipped to STRUCTURAL. The cash multiplier moved from 1.00x to 0.65x and the destination PE dropped from ~20x to ~13x. Fair value re-based below the entry price.

At the same time the order book stopped being a one-way story. Tata AutoComp, which had rocketed from tenth to second customer, normalized its wallet share, and one export program was volume-cut the way RACL's KTM business had been (the peer set had warned this was a live risk). The Rs 1,200 Cr "order book" shrank without any breach or announcement, because it had always been nominations, not firm POs. Pillar 3c's +2x duration premium — credited by operator override on an unaudited figure — came off, Pillar 3a compressed, and the commissioned capex sat underutilized, dragging ROCE from an already-declining 15% toward the low teens. Revenue growth decelerated from 54% to the twenties, which for a stock that had traded at 60x-plus was fatal.

The governance overhang did the rest. With margins visibly promoter-mediated through Omega Bright Steel and the FY25 accounting anomalies (diluted EPS above basic, negative provisions) never cleanly resolved because the annual report kept arriving corrupted, the market applied the discount it had been threatening all along and re-rated the name toward the low-teens PE that a 15% ROCE, cash-negative, related-party-dependent manufacturer actually earns. De-rating swamped what growth remained, exactly the mechanism the valuation had flagged. The "half price" entry turned out to be full price against a fair value that had quietly fallen.
