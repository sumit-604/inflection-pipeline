## SECTION 1: MARKET DEFINITION

### 1A Precise boundaries

**Product scope (included):** Secured and cash-flow-underwritten small-ticket MSME/business loans (Laxmi's largest vertical, Rs 1,298.68 Cr of Rs 1,626.26 Cr FY26 AUM, 79.8%, Investor_Presentation_1.pdf slide 35, "Vertical Wise AUM Split"); vehicle loans, commercial and non-commercial (Rs 145.82 Cr, 9.0%); construction loans and loan-against-property/mortgage (Rs 85.61 Cr, 5.3%); wholesale lending to other MSME/vehicle-finance NBFCs (Rs 52.97 Cr, 3.3%); personal loans (Rs 43.18 Cr, 2.7%) (all: Investor_Presentation_1.pdf slide 35).

**Geographic scope:** India. Primary relevance is the 6 states where Laxmi operates: Rajasthan (92 branches), Madhya Pradesh (40), Gujarat (23), Uttar Pradesh (15), Chhattisgarh (5), Maharashtra (1, added FY26) (Investor_Presentation_1.pdf slide 5). Tier II/III, semi-urban and rural India specifically (slide 23, "Deep presence in rural & semi-urban India"; slide 27, "Semi-Urban Branch Model").

**Customer scope (included):** Micro and small enterprises, self-employed business owners and small traders, salaried professionals in Tier II/III towns, vehicle owners/operators, largely informal/underbanked profiles (Investor_Presentation_1.pdf slide 23, 33). **Excluded:** medium enterprises (turnover Rs 50-250 Cr per MSMED Act 2006, drhp.pdf p.148) and large/corporate borrowers, which Laxmi's largest single product ("MSME Prime", ticket size Rs 25-50 lakh, slide 34) does not reach.

**Channel scope:** Branch-led, relationship-driven direct NBFC lending (>99% of Laxmi's disbursement value is branch/DSA-sourced per Stage 7's B07-emoat FLAG-DIGITAL-NARRATIVE finding: digital-channel share of disbursement by value fell from 1.70% FY24 to 0.94% FY25). Excludes the bank-lending channel and pure-digital/fintech-only lending channel, both structurally different competitive sets.

**Price/ticket segment:** Rs 1.5 lakh (two-wheeler) to Rs 5 crore (wholesale lending), core products (MSME, Construction & LAP, MSME Prime) in the Rs 4 lakh to Rs 50 lakh range (Investor_Presentation_1.pdf slide 34, "Diversified Lending Profile" ticket-size table).

**Explicit exclusions:** large corporate/industrial term lending; unsecured urban personal-loan/BNPL fintech market; gold loans; agriculture-only lending; housing finance for salaried/large-ticket urban home loans (HFC market); microfinance/JLG lending (Laxmi is not an MFI, unlike some listed peers).

A wrong definition here would make every downstream number meaningless: Laxmi is not competing for India's Rs 35 lakh crore total MSME credit market broadly defined (drhp.pdf p.149); it is competing for the slice of that market that (a) only NBFCs (not banks) actually serve, (b) sits in semi-urban/rural India, and (c) sits in its 6-state footprint. Sections 2-3 build the funnel accordingly.

### 1B Management's own TAM claim

The Prospectus's "Industry Overview" section is explicitly commissioned: "derived from the report titled 'Research Report on NBFC Industry' released in June 2025 ('CARE Report') prepared by CARE Analytics and Advisory Private Limited (CareEdge Research), appointed by our Company pursuant to an engagement letter dated August 22, 2024 and such CARE Report has been commissioned by and paid for by our Company" (drhp.pdf p.133/PDF p.138). This makes the CARE Report's figures management's own commissioned market narrative, not an independent third-party view, and it is treated here as the "management claim" per Section 1B.

The one place management's own commissioned research uses the literal word "TAM" is a bottom-up estimate for **self-occupied-residential-property (SORP) backed MSME loans**: Total households in India 271 mn -> business/self-employed households with SORP 46 mn -> with "pucca" (well-documented) SORP 44 mn -> average ticket size Rs 0.6 mn -> "TAM ~26 tn" i.e. **Rs 26,00,000 Cr** (drhp.pdf p.158/PDF p.163, CARE Report, June 2025). The arithmetic checks out: 44 mn households x Rs 6 lakh = Rs 26.4 lakh crore, consistent with the stated "~26tn."

Date: June 2025 (CARE Report), disclosed in a Prospectus dated 31 July 2025. Credibility read: **specific but narrow, and functionally inflated when presented as a company TAM.** The methodology is genuinely specific (named funnel steps, disclosed average ticket size), which is a mark in its favour versus a vague "trillion-dollar market" slogan. But it is (i) an all-lender, full-penetration household-demand estimate, not an NBFC-served or even formal-credit-only figure, and (ii) scoped to one collateral type (residential-property-backed loans) when only Construction & LAP (5.3% of AUM) is explicitly property-collateralised in Laxmi's own product table, though the two largest MSME products (MSME Prime, MSME) are also disclosed as "Commercial & Residential Property" secured (slide 34), so the overlap with Laxmi's actual book is larger than 5.3% alone would suggest. This claim is held for the Section 2 ratio test below.

## SECTION 2: TAM ESTIMATION, MULTIPLE METHODS

### Method 1: Top-down (NBFC-channel, national, MSME + vehicle finance)

Step 1, MSME component. The DRHP's own CareEdge-sourced chart shows the "NBFC MSME loan book" (Industrial + Service sectors), which grew from Rs 1.6 lakh crore (Mar-21) to Rs 3.3 lakh crore (Mar-24E) at roughly 22-24% CAGR, projected to reach ~Rs 6 lakh crore by Mar-27P (drhp.pdf p.151/PDF p.156). This is cross-checked two ways:
- Derivation from the broader base: Total commercial MSME credit outstanding, all lenders, all sectors = Rs 35 lakh crore as of Mar-25 (drhp.pdf p.149/PDF p.154, CareEdge). Micro+Small share of MSME credit outstanding = 71% (Mar-25E, drhp.pdf p.151/PDF p.156) => Rs 24.85 lakh crore. NBFC share of that (industrial-sector definition) = 12.5% (Mar-25, drhp.pdf p.152/PDF p.157) => **Rs 3.11 lakh crore**, same order of magnitude as the direct chart figure.
- An independent, more current source: CareEdge Ratings' own May-2025 press release headline states "MSME AUM for NBFCs to cross Rs 5.3 lakh crore by FY26" (careratings.com, press release dated ~14 May 2025; full PDF blocked by proxy, headline/snippet used). Implies ~27% 2-year CAGR off the Rs 3.3 lakh crore Mar-24E base, consistent with the DRHP's own 22-24% projection band.

Per the CONSERVATIVE BIAS rule, the lower, DRHP-anchored figure is carried forward: **NBFC MSME loan book = Rs 3.3 lakh crore** (Mar-24E/25).

Step 2, vehicle-finance component (Laxmi's second-largest vertical). NBFC credit towards vehicle financing = Rs 5.5 lakh crore as of Mar-25E, +15.8% YoY (drhp.pdf p.162/PDF p.167, CareEdge).

**Method 1 conservative TAM (National, NBFC-channel, MSME + vehicle) = Rs 3,30,000 Cr + Rs 5,50,000 Cr = Rs 8,80,000 Cr.**
Confidence: Medium (multiple consistent CareEdge cuts, one independent cross-check). Staleness: current, all inputs dated Mar-24E to May-25, well inside the 2-year window.

### Method 2: Bottom-up (CareEdge/management's own commissioned model)

Using management's own commissioned funnel (Section 1B): 271 mn Indian households -> 46 mn business/self-employed with SORP -> 44 mn with "pucca" SORP -> average ticket Rs 6 lakh -> **Rs 26,00,000 Cr**, PAN-India, all lenders, full-penetration, one product type (SORP-backed loans) (drhp.pdf p.158/PDF p.163). This is a demand-side ceiling, not a supply-side or NBFC-only figure, which is why it sits well above Method 1.

### Method 3: Peer revenue/AUM aggregation + unorganised-sector cross-check

Named organised listed peers (per B00 routing, all MSME/vehicle-finance NBFCs), FY26 AUM (web search, dated 2026):
- Arman Financial Services: Rs 2,728 Cr
- UGRO Capital: Rs 15,334 Cr
- Manba Finance: Rs 1,713 Cr
- Moneyboxx Finance: Rs 893 Cr
- Laxmi India Finance: Rs 1,626 Cr (own AUM, anchor above)

Sum of these 5 named peers = **Rs 22,294 Cr**. Against the Method 1 industry-wide NBFC MSME AUM pool of Rs 3.3-5.3 lakh crore, these five listed players together represent only **4.2% to 6.8%** of the formal NBFC MSME lending market, evidencing a very long unlisted/private-NBFC tail below the top players (Five Star Business Finance, Vistaar, MAS Financial, Kogta Financial and others were not pulled individually; see searches_skipped). This is directional evidence of fragmentation, not a market-size estimate in itself.

The "explicit unorganised-sector estimate" required by this method is available directly from CareEdge's own updated credit-gap model (drhp.pdf p.158/PDF p.163, Mar'24 estimate): Total MSME credit **demand** Rs 61.6 lakh crore vs formal **supply** (all lenders, banks + NBFCs) Rs 28.0 lakh crore = **gap of Rs 33.6 lakh crore**, i.e. **54.5% of total demand is unmet by any formal lender** (33.6/61.6). This sits inside, in fact above, the standard 30-60% India unorganised-sector band cited in the pipeline's own methodology note, and CareEdge projects the gap widening to Rs 40.6 lakh crore by Mar-27P even as formal supply also grows (demand Rs 81.1 lakh crore, supply Rs 40.4 lakh crore). A stale, superseded reference point: an IFC report (November 2018, based on 2017 data) estimated the gap at Rs 25.8 lakh crore (drhp.pdf p.157/PDF p.162); this figure is **more than 4 years old relative to the 2026 run date and is used for direction only** (the gap is structurally large and persistent), never as a headline number, per the staleness rule.

### Method 4: Import substitution

Not applicable. This is a domestic secured-lending market with no import/export analogue.

### Method 5: Global benchmark (directional only)

India's credit-to-GDP ratio stood at 93.6% as of Sep-24, versus Australia 171.2%, United States 145.2%, United Kingdom 138.9% (drhp.pdf p.136-137/PDF p.141-142, IMF/RBI data cited by CareEdge). If India converged even halfway toward the 140-170% band of mature markets over the coming decade, the implied national credit stock would need to expand by roughly 50-80% beyond its current base, with the largest incremental share structurally going to the currently under-penetrated segments: retail, MSME, and rural/semi-urban credit (the same segments driving the NBFC share-of-systemic-credit rise from 17% in FY19 towards a projected 20% by FY27, drhp.pdf p.139/PDF p.144). This is directional support for a multi-decade runway, not a standalone Rs Cr estimate, because the systemic-credit base used for the credit-to-GDP ratio and the "total systemic credit" series quoted elsewhere in the same CareEdge report do not reconcile to a single consistent number (both are cited in the Prospectus without a common base year), so no headline figure is forced from this method.

### Triangulation table

| Method | Estimate (Rs Cr) | Confidence | Staleness |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Top-down, NBFC-channel, National (MSME + vehicle) | 8,80,000 | Medium | Current (Mar-24E to May-25) |
| 2. Bottom-up, CareEdge/mgmt SORP-backed MSME model | 26,00,000 | Medium (specific method, narrow product scope) | Current (June 2025) |
| 3. Peer aggregation (5 listed names) | 22,294 (not a TAM; fragmentation signal only) | High (own AUM data) but non-comprehensive | Current (FY26) |
| 3b. CareEdge unorganised-demand gap, Mar'24 | 33,600 (in lakh Cr; i.e. Rs 33,60,000 Cr) | Medium | Current (est. base year Mar'24) |
| 3c. IFC unorganised-demand gap (superseded) | 25,80,000 | Low, STALE | 2017 base year, direction only |
| 5. Global credit-to-GDP benchmark | Directional only, no Rs Cr forced | Low (directional) | Current (Sep-24) |

**Conservative estimate: Rs 8,80,000 Cr** (Method 1, the narrowest and most channel/segment-specific).
**Realistic (fuller-penetration) estimate: Rs 26,00,000 Cr** (Method 2).

These two diverge by roughly **3.0x**, which is a material divergence per the pipeline's own triangulation rule. The explanation, not a silent average: Method 1 is a supply-side, NBFC-only, current-book figure (what NBFCs already lend, today, in two specific product lines); Method 2 is a demand-side, all-lender (bank + NBFC + informal), full-penetration household estimate for one collateral type. They are measuring different constructs. The conservative Method 1 figure is carried forward into Section 3 per the pipeline's CONSERVATIVE BIAS rule.

**Management's claim vs the conservative estimate:** Rs 26,00,000 Cr / Rs 8,80,000 Cr = **2.96x**, i.e. above the ">2x likely inflated" threshold. **Read: inflated as presented.** The Prospectus does not caveat that its own "TAM" figure is an all-lender, full-penetration, single-product demand estimate rather than a like-for-like NBFC-served market size; an investor reading "TAM ~26tn" without that context could reasonably (and wrongly) treat it as Laxmi's addressable opportunity today. This is flagged below (FLAG-MGMT-TAM-SCOPE).

## SECTION 3: SAM & SOM

### 3A SAM: five filters applied to the conservative TAM (Rs 8,80,000 Cr)

1. **Product fit (-5%):** Laxmi's book is overwhelmingly branch-underwritten MSME + vehicle + LAP; it is not built to compete for large-ticket wholesale/co-lending flow at national NBFC scale (its own Wholesale Lending vertical is 3.3% of AUM and structured as on-lending to other MSME/vehicle NBFCs, not primary origination). Factor: 0.95.
2. **Geography (6-state footprint):** using MSME state-distribution data (drhp.pdf p.148-149/PDF p.153-154, Dec'24 Udyam-registered MSME share by state): Maharashtra 13.07%, Uttar Pradesh 10.67%, Madhya Pradesh 6.40%, Gujarat 5.78%, Rajasthan 5.69%. Chhattisgarh is not in the DRHP's top-10 state table (NOT FOUND directly); estimated at ~2.0% based on its ~1.5-2% share of India's GDP/population (estimate, not sourced from the DRHP, flagged as such). Sum for Laxmi's 6 states ≈ **43.6%** of the national MSME base. Factor: 0.436.
3. **Customer segment/urbanity (semi-urban + rural only, not the urban/metro pool within those 6 states):** using rural + semi-urban Scheduled Commercial Bank credit outstanding as a proxy, Dec-24 = Rs 13.76 lakh crore (rural) + Rs 24.46 lakh crore (semi-urban) = Rs 38.22 lakh crore (drhp.pdf p.147/PDF p.152, RBI/CareEdge), against total SCB credit of roughly Rs 170 lakh crore (70% of the ~Rs 243 lakh crore FY25P total systemic credit figure, drhp.pdf p.139/PDF p.144). Implied rural + semi-urban share ≈ **23%**. Factor: 0.23.
4. **Channel:** already built into the TAM base (NBFC-channel only); no further cut.
5. **Capability (funding/underwriting capacity to actually reach the theoretical geography x segment pool within a multi-year window, given a 176-branch network today):** 0.85, reflecting real but not unlimited near-term operating capacity.

**SAM = Rs 8,80,000 Cr x 0.95 x 0.436 x 0.23 x 0.85 = Rs 71,260 Cr.**
**SAM as % of TAM (conservative) = 71,260 / 8,80,000 = 8.1%.**

### 3B SOM at 3 and 5 years

Current AUM (the practical proxy for "current capture" in a lending TAM, consistent with how the DRHP itself frames MSME "TAM" in credit-book rather than revenue terms) = Rs 1,626.26 Cr (Mar-2026, Investor_Presentation_1.pdf slide 5).
**Current share of SAM = 1,626.26 / 71,260 = 2.28%.**

Share-gain rule applied: the standard band is 1-2pp/3yr (normal) or 3-5pp/3yr (aggressive, requires capacity + execution evidence), with faster gains allowed only on competitor exit/acquisition, or where the unorganised share is formalising past 40% (which applies here: the CareEdge credit-gap model above shows 54.5% of MSME demand unmet by any formal lender). Two things pull in opposite directions: (a) genuine capacity has been built (net worth +80.5% YoY to Rs 465.47 Cr post-IPO, CRAR 26.12% vs a 15% regulatory floor, external rating upgraded A-(Positive) to A/Stable in FY26, all Investor_Presentation_1.pdf slides 5, 10); but (b) Stage 7's B07-emoat flagged FLAG-F2-GUIDANCE-MISS: every headline FY26 guided metric (AUM growth, branch adds, PAT growth, cost-of-borrowing cut, rating-notch quantum) under-delivered against management's own Q1 FY26 guidance. **This stage therefore uses the low end of the "aggressive" band (+3pp by year 3), not the top of it, as a direct discount for that guidance-miss pattern.**

- **SOM 3yr share = 2.28% + 3.0pp = 5.28% of SAM => SOM_3yr = 5.28% x Rs 71,260 Cr = Rs 3,763 Cr.**
- **SOM 5yr share = 2.28% + 5.0pp = 7.28% of SAM => SOM_5yr = 7.28% x Rs 71,260 Cr = Rs 5,188 Cr.**

**Implied revenue CAGR (the formal handoff to Stage 11):** using Laxmi's own FY26 yield, Total Income / AUM = 319.60 / 1,626.26 = **19.65%**, held constant:
- Implied revenue at yr3 = 19.65% x Rs 3,763 Cr = Rs 739.4 Cr. CAGR from Rs 319.60 Cr base = (739.4/319.60)^(1/3) - 1 = **32.3%**.
- Implied revenue at yr5 = 19.65% x Rs 5,188 Cr = Rs 1,019.5 Cr. CAGR = (1,019.5/319.60)^(1/5) - 1 = **26.1%**.

Cross-check: these SOM-derived figures (32.3% yr3, 26.1% yr5) sit close to, and independently corroborate rather than assume, management's own AUM CAGR guidance of 30-35% (operator-context.md, non-anchored, Q4 FY26 call 14-May-2026) and the company's own trailing AUM CAGR of 33.29% FY23-26 (Investor_Presentation_1.pdf slide 35). This is a useful internal-consistency signal for Stage 11, not a coincidence to over-read given the guidance-miss discount already applied above.

### 3C Capacity cross-check

B07's capex_embedded_growth_pct = 0 (a lender; growth is funded by borrowings/equity, not fixed capex), so the relevant cross-check is balance-sheet capacity, not production capacity. FY26 net worth = Rs 465.47 Cr, CRAR 26.12% (Tier I 25.61%, Tier II 0.51%) against RBI's Middle-Layer NBFC (NBFC-ML) minimum CRAR of 15% (RBI Scale Based Regulation, confirmed via web search; Laxmi is NBFC-ML given assets >Rs 1,000 Cr, drhp results filing note 4).

Illustrative math: implied risk-weighted assets at the current 26.12% CRAR, using net worth as a capital proxy, is ~Rs 1,782 Cr (465.47/0.2612). If CRAR were run down to the regulatory floor of 15% on the same capital base (no fresh equity), maximum RWA rises to ~Rs 3,103 Cr (465.47/0.15), a ~1.74x expansion, implying an AUM ceiling of roughly **Rs 2,830 Cr** by year 3 using existing capital alone. The SOM_3yr figure above is **Rs 3,763 Cr, a gap of roughly Rs 930 Cr (33% above the no-fresh-capital ceiling).**

**Gap named: Rs ~930 Cr by year 3. The SOM is the optimistic side of this comparison, not a "capex plan"** (there isn't one for a lender). This is not an unreasonable gap for a company that raised fresh equity via IPO in August 2025 and Rs 50 Cr of NCDs in February 2026 (both anchored: drhp.pdf and operator-context.md cross-check), and NBFCs of this profile do raise further capital as they scale, but the SOM as sized here assumes continued capital-market access that is not yet committed for this specific quantum. Flagged below (FLAG-CAPACITY-GAP).

### 4C Market structure

- **Organised vs unorganised split:** NBFCs + SCBs (organised, formal) supply only 45.5% of total MSME credit demand as estimated by CareEdge (Rs 28.0 lakh crore supply / Rs 61.6 lakh crore demand, Mar'24); the remaining 54.5% is unmet/informal.
- **NBFC vs bank split within formal MSME credit (industrial-sector definition):** banks 87.5%, NBFCs 12.5% as of Mar-25, up from banks 90.8%/NBFCs 9.2% in Mar-20 (drhp.pdf p.152), i.e. NBFCs are steadily gaining share within the formal channel.
- **Competitor count / top-3 concentration:** NOT FOUND in the documents read for this stage. The five named listed peers (Arman, UGRO, Manba, Moneyboxx, Laxmi) sum to Rs 22,294 Cr combined AUM against an industry-wide NBFC MSME AUM pool of Rs 3.3-5.3 lakh crore, i.e. under 7% combined, which is directional evidence of a long unlisted tail rather than a top-3-concentrated market, but a precise concentration ratio was not sourced.
- **Consolidating or fragmenting:** Consolidating at the macro level (NBFC systemic-credit share rising steadily, 17% to a projected 20% by FY27), but the sub-segment Laxmi competes in (semi-urban/rural, small-ticket, non-income-proof MSME) still reads as fragmented given the peer-aggregation evidence above.
- **Entries/exits:** Not directly evidenced in the documents read; the DRHP's competitor table (Banks, Private Banks, NBFCs, Fintech Intermediaries, Credit Funds/AIFs, TREDS Platforms, DFIs, drhp.pdf p.155-156) shows the field is structurally crowded by design type, not just by number of NBFC players.

## SECTION 5: SUMMARY & RUNWAY

### 5A Funnel

```
TAM (conservative, National, NBFC-channel, MSME + vehicle)........ Rs 8,80,000 Cr
  x product fit (0.95, capability/wholesale carve-out)
  x geography (0.436, 6-state footprint)
  x semi-urban/rural customer segment (0.23)
  x capability (0.85, near-term operating capacity)
SAM ................................................................ Rs 71,260 Cr  (8.1% of TAM)
  current capture: Rs 1,626.26 Cr AUM = 2.28% of SAM
SOM, 3yr (current share + 3.0pp, guidance-miss-discounted "aggressive" band) . Rs 3,763 Cr
SOM, 5yr (current share + 5.0pp) .................................. Rs 5,188 Cr
```

### 5B Runway assessment

- **Revenue headroom = SAM / current revenue = Rs 71,260 Cr / Rs 319.60 Cr = 223x.**
- **TAM growth rate (blended, weighted by Method 1 components) ≈ 18%** (NBFC MSME AUM growing 22-24% weighted at 3.75x the size of NBFC vehicle finance growing 15.8%; blended: (3,30,000 x 23% + 5,50,000 x 15.8%) / 8,80,000 ≈ 18.4%). Cross-checked against CRISIL's independent, broader forecast of 18-19% overall NBFC AUM growth for FY26/FY27 (web search).
- **Company CAGR vs TAM:** Laxmi's own trailing AUM CAGR (FY23-26) = (1,626.26/686.77)^(1/3) - 1 = **33.3%**, roughly **1.8x the blended TAM growth rate**, i.e. Laxmi is gaining share, not merely riding the market.
- **Years to saturate SAM at current growth:** SAM / current AUM = 71,260/1,626.26 = 43.8x. At a 33% CAGR, years to close that gap = ln(43.8)/ln(1.33) ≈ **13 years**.

### 5C Runway classification: **MASSIVE**

Basis: a 223x revenue-headroom multiple, a market growing faster than the broader economy (18% blended TAM CAGR vs 6.5% India GDP growth), a company compounding meaningfully faster than its addressable market (33% vs 18%), and a ~13-year runway to SAM saturation at current pace, all pointing to the top tier of the standard MASSIVE/STRONG/GOOD/MODERATE/LIMITED matrix. This classification sits above the mechanical Gate-0 "AVOID" label from earlier stages; per B07-emoat's own combined read, the company is "HIGH POTENTIAL with guidance discounted," and this stage's runway finding is directionally consistent with that (a large market does not by itself resolve the FY26 guidance-miss execution question, which stays a live flag, not a market-sizing one).

### 5D SAM expansion levers actually being pursued

- **Geographic diversification (management's stated Priority #3, Investor_Presentation_1.pdf slide 18):** expanding beyond the current 6 states towards, illustratively, 10-12 states would raise the geography factor from ~43.6% towards ~65-70% of the national MSME base, which on the same downstream filters would raise SAM from Rs 71,260 Cr to roughly **Rs 105,000-115,000 Cr**, an addition of Rs 34,000-44,000 Cr, and revenue headroom from 223x to roughly 330-360x. Evidence type: claim/in-progress (B07-emoat category E1, "Moderate" strength, 24-36m to materialise; newer states Chhattisgarh/UP/Maharashtra are as yet unproven at replicating Rajasthan's density economics per B07's own top-risk note).
- **PSL/DA-sale and co-lending expansion (B07 category R1):** does not expand the underlying market itself, but expands Laxmi's effective capacity to capture existing SAM without proportional equity dilution (off-balance-sheet funding of origination). This is the same mechanism flagged in Section 3C and in B07 as double-edged (it also transmitted the Up Money Ltd counterparty default onto the book in FY26); credited here for capacity only, not compounded twice per the pipeline's own "never credit one improvement through two mechanisms" rule.
- **ECB access (B07 optionality register item):** a capital-access lever, not a market-size lever; relevant to closing the Section 3C capacity gap, not to expanding SAM.

## FLAGS

- **FLAG-MGMT-TAM-SCOPE**: management's own commissioned CARE Report states a Rs 26 lakh crore "TAM" for SORP-backed MSME loans without disclosing that this is an all-lender (bank + NBFC + informal), full-penetration, single-product-type household-demand estimate. Ratio vs this stage's conservative NBFC-channel TAM is 2.96x (above the 2x "likely inflated" threshold). Read as inflated-as-presented, not necessarily dishonest in methodology, but a real risk if taken at face value by an investor comparing it to Laxmi's actual addressable opportunity.
- **FLAG-GUIDANCE-DISCOUNT-CARRIED**: this stage's SOM share-gain assumption (+3pp/3yr, low end of the "aggressive" band rather than the 5pp top) is a direct, explicit discount carried from Stage 7's B07-emoat FLAG-F2-GUIDANCE-MISS (every FY26 headline guided metric under-delivered in the maiden post-IPO year). Any upward revision of this share-gain assumption at Stage 11/13 should require a second year of guidance delivery as evidence first.
- **FLAG-CAPACITY-GAP**: at the SOM_3yr AUM level (Rs 3,763 Cr), the capital-constrained AUM ceiling using FY26 net worth alone at RBI's 15% NBFC-ML CRAR floor is roughly Rs 2,830 Cr, a gap of ~Rs 930 Cr (33%). The SOM, not a capex plan (none exists for a lender), is the side assuming continued capital-market access (further equity or quasi-equity) that is plausible given the August-2025 IPO and February-2026 NCD track record, but not yet committed for this quantum.

- **FLAG-STALE-IFC-GAP-FIGURE**: the IFC (Nov-2018, FY2017-base) MSME credit-gap estimate of Rs 25.8 lakh crore, cited in the DRHP itself, is more than 4 years stale relative to the 2026 run date; it was used for direction only (the gap is structurally large and persistent) and never as this stage's headline TAM number, consistent with the pipeline's staleness rule.
