### 2C. Growth embedded in capex — the arithmetic

- Historical Fixed Asset Turnover (FY25) = Revenue from Operations ÷ Net Block of Property, Plant & Equipment
  = ₹9,405.18 lakhs ÷ ₹2,761.27 lakhs (RHP Annexure P and Annexure G, Net Block as at 31-03-2025)
  = **3.406x**
- Total capex under execution (Objects of the Issue) = ₹1,668.96 lakhs (RHP p.74)
- Implied incremental revenue = ₹1,668.96 lakhs × 3.406 = **₹5,684.4 lakhs**
- As % of current revenue (₹9,405.18 lakhs FY25 Revenue from Operations): **5,684.4 / 9,405.18 = 60.4%**

**capex_embedded_growth_pct = 60.4%** — carried to Stage 9 Section 3C.

**🔍 Critical caveat (analyst inference, not a company claim):** this arithmetic mechanically applies the company's *existing-business* asset turnover to *all* the new capex, but ~55% of that capex (₹925.56 lakhs) is earmarked for a wholly new, never-before-manufactured product line (conveyor belts) with no established pricing, customer base, or realised turnover ratio, and for which the company has explicitly disclosed it lacks manufacturing experience (RHP Risk Factor 5). The modernisation tranche (₹743.40 lakhs) is more plausibly analogous to historical turnover; the conveyor tranche is not. The 60.4% figure should be read as a **theoretical ceiling under an optimistic transferability assumption**, not a probable outcome, and Stage 9 should treat it accordingly.

## SECTION 3: THE 20-CATEGORY SCAN

### FAMILY A — PRODUCT & TECHNOLOGY

**A1 — Rare manufacturing capability.** RDSO (Research Designs & Standards Organisation, Ministry of Railways) and MoRTH (Ministry of Road Transport & Highways) approvals are a genuine multi-year regulatory barrier. The company holds a documented, currently-active portfolio of product-specific RDSO/DRDO approvals, several renewed or newly obtained within the last two years:
- POT-PTFE Bearings — approved Dec 2, 2020, valid through Nov–Jan 2026 (📄, RHP p.215)
- Elastomeric Bearings — approved Dec 2, 2020, valid through Oct–Dec 2025 (📄, RHP p.215)
- Expansion Joint — approved Apr 12, 2023, valid to Mar 31, 2029 (📄, RHP p.215)
- 6mm NCR GRSP, 10mm Composite CGRSP, 6.2mm NCR GRSP — all approved Feb 27, 2024, valid to Feb 18, 2027 (📄, RHP p.216)
- UIC Rubber Vestibules — approved Feb 2, 2019, valid "till cancelled" (📄, RHP p.216)
- DRDO approval for rubber compounds/moulded products — Mar 1, 2023, valid to Feb 28, 2026 (📄, RHP p.216)
Evidence type: 📄 documented (approval letters with validity dates). Strength: **Moderate** — this barrier is largely *existing*, not newly forming, but the steady cadence of new SKU-specific approvals obtained in 2023–2024 (rather than a single legacy approval) shows continued expansion of regulatory coverage, which is the "forming" element relevant to this scan. Time to materialise: already active; renewal risk within 6–12 months for two certificates (flagged in Section 6).

**A2 — Patent and IP pipeline.** NO EVIDENCE FOUND. The company has 10 trademark applications (2 registered, 8 "Formalities Check Pass" — RHP p.218) covering its logo/brand, but no patents, no lab-partnership disclosures, and no licensing revenue were found anywhere in the RHP or presentation.

**A3 — Process innovation.** NO EVIDENCE FOUND. No yield disclosures, unit-cost trend, or waste-to-value initiative was disclosed. The in-house NABL-accredited laboratory (re-accredited for 4 years, Jan 10, 2025 – Jan 9, 2029, RHP p.216) supports quality control but is not evidence of process innovation per se.

**A4 — Product platform / modular architecture.** NO EVIDENCE FOUND. The company has a broad product catalogue (10+ product families) but no disclosure ties this to a shared modular architecture or accelerating launch cadence.

### FAMILY B — SUPPLY CHAIN

**B1 — Backward integration and RM security.** NO EVIDENCE FOUND — in fact the opposite is explicitly disclosed: the company sources raw materials on a **spot-market basis with no long-term supply agreements** (🎙️/📄 risk disclosure, RHP Risk Factor 2 & 3, p.26–27), and RM purchases from the top 10 suppliers represent 77.80% (FY25), 78.54% (FY24), 84.02% (FY23) of total RM purchases (RHP p.26) — a mild improving trend but still high concentration, not a security-of-supply moat.

**B2 — Qualification lock-in.** RDSO/MoRTH vendor-approved status functions as design-in/sole-source-adjacent lock-in for Railway customers, reinforced by rate contracts — e.g., the Southern Railway rate contract (₹4.20 cr, valid to Mar 31, 2027) disclosed in the operator-supplied Reg 30 announcements (📄, operator provenance, not independently verified against original BSE filings). Strength: **Moderate**. Time to materialise: already active, continuing.

**B3 — Supply chain network effect.** NO EVIDENCE FOUND. The company is a manufacturer, not a platform/aggregator.

### FAMILY C — CUSTOMER

**C1 — Customer ecosystem / embedded relationships.** Weak evidence only. The company sells multiple product families (sole plates, pads, vestibules, crossings, bearings) to the same core customer base (Indian Railways and its zones), which is directionally consistent with cross-sell, but no wallet-share, ERP integration, AMC revenue, or co-development disclosure exists. 🔍 analyst inference only.

**C2 — Customer concentration improving.** This is the strongest documented, quantified trend in the scan. Top-10 customer share of revenue from operations: **64.63% (FY23) → 53.93% (FY24) → 50.11% (FY25)** — a clean three-year declining trend disclosed in the audited financials (📄, RHP p.25 Risk Factor 1; RHP p.113–114 Our Business). Strength: **Strong**. Time to materialise: already occurring, continuing.

No % of total power sourced from solar was disclosed (NOT FOUND). Strength: **Moderate**. Time to materialise: already installed (capex disbursed).
