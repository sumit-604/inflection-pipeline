This **elevates FLAG-CASH** beyond the receivables/cash-collapse framing B02 used — the underlying driver in both years is the same: **trade receivables consumed cash faster than operations generated it** (FY25: -Rs.2,332.46L working-capital use from receivables alone; FY24: -Rs.1,116.40L), and this was **partially offset by trade payables stretching** (+Rs.770.19L FY25, +source of cash — consistent with the 266% MSME-payables growth already flagged as a statutory-disclosure gap in B02/Note 8). Without that payables stretch, cash generated from operations before tax would have been only Rs.259.25L in FY25 (Rs.1,029.44L - Rs.770.19L) — a razor-thin core cash margin propped up, in part, by delaying payments to (increasingly MSME) suppliers.

**CFO quality checks**:
- **One-time inflators**: the ~Rs.173.74L profit on sale of shares/mutual funds is correctly excluded from CFO and reclassified to investing activities (p.70) — no aggressive CFO inflation via this route. However, it **is** included in PBT/Other Income, meaning reported profit growth is partly non-cash-flow-backed non-core gains (see 3C).
- **Interest classification**: interest paid (Rs.446.38L) is classified in Financing Activities; interest received (Rs.100.24L) in Investing Activities — standard, non-aggressive classification for a non-financial company; no evidence of interest being routed through operating activities to flatter CFO.
- **Unsustainable payable stretching**: confirmed as a real, quantifiable contributor to FY25's positive (barely) CFO — see above.
