# Stage 6: Peer Concall Verification, ANAWIL (run 2026-11-12)

Main company: Anawil Wire and Engineering Ltd (ANAWIL), onshore tubular wind towers, NSE SME, listed 2026-08-10.
Evidence base: 12 peer transcripts (KP Green Engineering x4, Inox Wind x4, Suzlon x4). Anchors use (PEER, call quarter, [page N] file marker, speaker where useful). Page numbers are the "[page N]" markers in the .txt files, not the page counters printed inside the transcripts.
Units: Rs Cr unless stated. Anawil figures come from B05 and B00 (derived items are labelled).

Scope warning. The B05 claims come from a Prospectus, press releases and decks, because stage 5 ran in NO-CONCALL MODE (no Anawil transcript). The H1 FY27 call of 2026-11-09 is unfiled. These twelve questions test industry facts, not management tone. No peer is a pure-play tubular tower maker (B00 peer note). KP Green is a diversified steel fabricator (lattice towers, solar structures, telecom, BSNL). Inox Wind and Suzlon are turbine makers. Not one of the 12 transcripts discusses tubular tower prices, tower vendor supply, or tower plant approval. That silence drives four of the verdicts below.

## PART 1: CLAIM-BY-CLAIM VERIFICATION

### Q1. H2 skew of installation and dispatch; size of the H2 share
- Claim: "H2 is 70-80% of annual revenue" (Pres p.22; List p.7). FY27 guidance needs H2 at 66-69% of the year (B05, derived).
- Verdict: VERIFIED (direction and rough magnitude), with one caveat on the top of the range.
- Peer evidence:
  - Inox Wind Q2 FY26 [page 3], Sanjeev Agarwal: "H2 generally being 70% of the annual execution". Q2 FY26 [page 7], Devansh Jain: H1 about 30-35% of the year, achieved about 30%.
  - Inox Wind Q3 FY26 [page 17], Kailash Tarachandani: "It will be always H2 heavy. H1 is many times leaner because of monsoon".
  - Inox Wind Q1 FY27 [page 8]: "70% to 75% of the business is captured in H2". [page 15]: H1 is "almost 25% of the annual operations".
  - Suzlon Q1 FY27 [page 5], Rahul Jain: "about 35, 40 in H1 versus 60, 65 in H2". Suzlon Q2 FY27 [page 7], Ajay Kapur: "H2 is about 1.5x to 1.65x of H1".
  - KP Green H1 FY25 [page 5], Salim Yahoo: H2 "always better" because customer capex lands in H2. KP Green H1 FY26 [page 15]: "40% in H1 and 60% in H2", with last year at about 37/63.
- Peers silent: none.
- Net read: Four of the peer calls agree on the shape. Peer H2 shares run 60% to 75%. Anawil's 70-80% sits at the top of that range, and its 80% end is above every peer number. The guidance needs only 66-69%, which is inside the peer range. So seasonality alone does not make the guidance a stretch. (Consistent with B05's own derivation.)

Inox Q4 FY26 [page 3] and Q1 FY27 [page 4]: "8 to 10 GW" a year over the next few years. Inox Q1 FY27 [page 4]: 1.4 GW commissioned in Q1 FY27, 57.4 GW installed at June 2026.
  - Installed base: Suzlon Q2 FY27 [page 3]: "crossed 59 gigawatts". Anawil used 58.52 GW. Consistent.
  - Pipeline and capacity: Suzlon Q2 FY27 [page 3]: "over 40 gigawatts of projects under various stages of implementation, annual manufacturing capacity exceeding 20 gigawatts". Anawil states 28 GW and about 24 GW. The Suzlon figure for projects under implementation is higher; capacity is in the same range.
  - 100 GW by 2030: Suzlon Q3 FY26 [page 4], Q4 FY26 [page 4], Q1 FY27 [page 3] repeat the near-term 100 GW target.

- Net read: Two turbine makers independently confirm the demand figures. Both are sellers of turbines, so they are not neutral on optimism. Both also say the limit is site readiness, not orders (see 2A).

## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

### 2A Demand environment
Consensus on volume, split on conversion. Suzlon, Inox and the market data agree: FY26 about 6 GW, FY27 about 8 GW, FY28 8-10 GW (see Q2). The same calls say delivery to the site is slower than orders. Suzlon Q3 FY26 [page 14], J.P. Chalasani: "execution is killing us". Suzlon Q2 FY27 [page 16]: about 3,900 MW delivered and not yet installed; about 1,300 MW erected waiting for commissioning. Inox Q3 FY26 [page 3], [page 5]: off-take postponed for site reasons. Anawil's deck uses installation growth and the order book as its demand case and does not mention site delay. Both OEMs also missed H2-heavy plans: Inox dropped its 1,200 MW target for a Rs 5,000 Cr revenue goal (Q3 FY26 [page 4]) and reported about Rs 4,500-4,600 Cr (Q4 FY26 [page 11]); Suzlon delivered 2,456 MW against about 2.5 GW (Q4 FY26 [page 4]). An analyst at Inox Q2 FY26 [page 11] notes shortfalls in FY24 and FY25 too. This is the closest pattern to Anawil's own H2-loaded guidance.

### 2B Pricing and input costs
Steel is mostly passed through (Q7). Other inputs are not: Suzlon cites copper, aluminium, carbon, FX and West Asia effects (Q2 FY27 [page 7]); KP cites fuel and gas for galvanizing (H2 FY26 [page 7]). Mix drives margin percentage down at Suzlon (project and EPC share up; WTG contribution margin 26.9% to 21.2%, Q2 FY27 [page 5]). That matches Anawil's own account that margin percentage falls as Model 3 (material-inclusive) rises. No peer gives a tower price. Turbine prices are stable (Inox Rs 8 Cr per MW turnkey; Suzlon Rs 5.9-6.3 Cr per MW).

### 2C Capex cycle
This is a capacity race with spare capacity, not a lone expansion. Suzlon: 4.5 GW fully operational, capex Rs 600-700 Cr a year, three new blade factories, Puducherry (Q4 FY26 [page 16], Q1 FY27 [page 13]). Inox: capex about Rs 200 Cr a year (Q3 FY26 [page 14]), a new South India blade and tower plant, transformers, cranes. KP Green: 4,00,500 tpa built, 30-34% used (H2 FY26 [page 21]), next steps in rolling mills and onshore tubular tower. Industry capacity is above 20 GW against about 6 GW installed (Suzlon Q2 FY27 [page 3]). Anawil adds Kutch (192 towers a year) into this set. Tower capacity is rising from three directions (Anawil, Inox in-house, KP entry).

### 2D Competitive mentions
No peer mentions Anawil, Koppal, Kutch or any unmistakable description of Anawil. The only competitor-type mentions are KP Green listing onshore tubular towers as a new vertical (H2 FY26 [page 4], [page 10]) and Inox's South India tower plant (Q2 FY26 [page 3]).

### 2E Risks peers discuss that Anawil does not
- Site readiness, right of way, land and grid delays holding back off-take (Inox Q3 FY26 [page 3], [page 5]; Suzlon Q3 FY26 [page 11]).
- Payment delays on PSU contracts and receivables locked until commissioning (Inox Q4 FY26 [page 5]; Inox Q1 FY27 [page 10]; Suzlon Q4 FY26 [page 9]).
- West Asia supply chain, fuel and crane disruption, deferring 10-20% of deliveries (Suzlon Q1 FY27 [page 3]-[page 4]; Inox Q4 FY26 [page 11]; KP H2 FY26 [page 7]).
- OEM insourcing and vertical integration: Inox tower plant, cranes, transformers (Inox Q2 FY26 [page 3], Q4 FY26 [page 9]).
- New OEM entrants with captive supply: Adani, JSW (Suzlon Q2 FY27 [page 7]-[page 8]).
- Related-party and promoter extraction analogues, from the closest SME comp: KP pays its promoter a 2% royalty on turnover (H2 FY25 [page 10]; H1 FY26 [page 6]; H2 FY26 [page 11]) and 22-23% of its Rs 1,800 Cr book is group-company work (H2 FY26 [page 21]; 50% in the H1 FY26 book, [page 7]). Analogy to the CMD loan of Rs 21.68 Cr only; not evidence about Anawil.
- Policy risk on connectivity and hybrid PPAs: Inox Q2 FY26 [page 8]; Suzlon Q3 FY26 [page 15] (standalone wind not affected).
- Chinese 5 MW platforms and platform risk (analysts; Suzlon Q3 FY26 [page 9]).

- Claims verified: 5 of 12 (Q1, Q2, Q7, Q8, Q11). Partially verified: 3 (Q6, Q9, Q10). Unverifiable: 4 (Q3, Q4, Q5, Q12).

## PART 5: CROSS-PEER HYPOTHESIS

[INFERENCE] The binding constraint in FY27-FY28 is cleared sites, not fabrication capacity. That makes tower fabrication a loose-supply, buyer-priced segment, which cuts against Anawil's "limited qualified suppliers" claim.
