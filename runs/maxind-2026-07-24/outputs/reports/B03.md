**DuPont-style read:** the negative and worsening ROE is entirely a **margin/loss** story, not a leverage story — with debt/equity below 1%, none of the ROE deterioration is being amplified by financial leverage. This is a materially different risk profile from a typical distressed small-cap: the balance sheet itself is clean and liquid; the risk is concentrated in the P&L (operating losses) and the pace of treasury drawdown, not in solvency or covenant risk.

Asset walk highlights: Non-current assets fell from ₹34,109.27 lakh to ₹27,724.29 lakh (-18.7%) largely from the Investment Property reclassification (₹6,762.57 lakh moved into "assets held for sale," ₹9,508.45 lakh, alongside other reclassified value — consistent with the Max Towers floor sale process). Current assets fell from ₹34,324.93 lakh to ₹26,052.45 lakh (-24.1%), driven by the cash/bank-balance/current-investment drawdown described in 3A, partially offset by receivables and inventory growth.

### 3C. P&L — line walk, other income, margins

Consolidated Revenue from operations fell -17.2% (₹175.63 Cr → ₹145.49 Cr) — per MD&A and B02, this is a finance-lease revenue-recognition timing artefact (prior-year Antara Purukul apartment-lease wave rolling off), not demand weakness, corroborated by the Chairman's Letter's own explanation (p.23) and by AGEasy/Care segment growth continuing at 43-91%+ per B02.

**Other income:** ₹1,868.51 lakh FY25 = 13.5% of Total Income (₹16,417.11 lakh) — below the 20%-of-PBT flag threshold as a share of revenue, but the PBT-denominator version of this test is not meaningful here since PBT is negative (other income of ₹18.69 Cr against a pre-other-income operating loss makes the loss look smaller than the core operating business, i.e., other income is genuinely cushioning the reported loss, worth watching as treasury shrinks and interest/mutual-fund income shrinks with it in FY26+).

**Margin waterfall (consolidated, ₹ Cr):** Revenue from ops 145.49 → Total income 164.17 → EBITDA loss (99.85) → PBT loss (138.58) → PAT loss (140.39). The gap between EBITDA loss and PBT loss (₹38.7 Cr) is depreciation (₹18.82 Cr) + finance costs (₹8.06 Cr) + exceptional item (₹12.60 Cr) — i.e., the fire settlement alone explains roughly a third of the EBITDA-to-PBT gap.

**Tax rate consistency:** Tax expense of ₹181.05 lakh (positive, i.e., the Group provided for tax) against a loss of ₹13,858.15 lakh before tax — an artefact of the conservative no-DTA-recognition policy (2G above), not an inconsistency.

**Basic vs. diluted EPS:** Consolidated Basic and Diluted EPS are identical at ₹(32.50) (p.221) — no dilution effect shown in a loss year (anti-dilutive ESOPs/warrants are excluded from diluted EPS calculation by convention when the entity is loss-making, which is standard and not a red flag).

### Phase 3 summary + cross-reference with Phases 1-2

The balance sheet is genuinely clean (near-zero debt, no covenant risk, no goodwill risk) — this materially changes the risk framing versus a typical distressed micro-cap: **the risk here is cash-runway risk, not solvency risk.** The cash-flow quality checks pass cleanly except for the already-flagged receivables build. The P&L confirms the auditor's KAM-level concerns feed directly into the bottom line (RPT-heavy revenue base, subsidiary-investment carrying value at risk) without yet showing any sign of that risk having crystallised into a misstatement.

**Phase 3 Verdict: 🟡 Watch.** Cash burn is real, transparent, and funded; balance sheet is not distressed; but the pace of treasury depletion (-45% in one year on the narrow measure) makes the FY26 rights issue and warrant conversion (per operator context) load-bearing for the thesis, not optional upside.

**Kill Switch Assessment (informational):** Based on Phases 1-3, a human reviewer would **not** stop — no solvency risk, no leverage risk, cash burn is disclosed and explicable. The reviewer would flag cash-runway monitoring (treasury balance, burn rate vs. FY26/FY27 breakeven guidance) as the single most important number to track next.

### 6C. Metrics showcased vs. conspicuously absent

Showcased: ARR, NPS, CSAT, collection efficiency, units sold, resident satisfaction, employee engagement score, diversity %.
Conspicuously absent from the front matter: occupancy/utilisation rate for Care Homes (a bed-based business's single most important unit-economics metric), the AACSL/Group-loss concentration figure, the fire-incident settlement, the RPT-KAM designation, and any reference to the rejected Chairman-pay vote (which occurred within this same reporting period, via postal ballot concluding March 29, 2025, i.e., before the AR itself was finalised in late May 2025 — so it was known and disclosable, and indeed is disclosed in the CG Report, just not referenced anywhere in the Chairman's Letter or MD&A front matter).

### 6D. Tone and priority drift vs. prior year

Not independently assessable from this single-year AR without the FY24 annual report for direct textual comparison; the tone read on its face is consistently optimistic/growth-oriented throughout, with no discernible softening despite the EBITDA loss nearly tripling.

### 6E. Quiet Abandonment Check (mandatory)

Reading the Chairman's Letter / MD&A opening (p.22-49) side by side with the operational and financial sections (Phases 1-5):

1. **Silent drop — RPT and impairment risk.** The opening strategic narrative never once mentions related-party transactions, promoter-group dealings, or subsidiary-investment carrying-value risk, despite these being the auditor's own KAMs discussed at length in the audit opinion (p.132-133, 211) just pages later in the same bound document. Classification: **silent drop** (opening does not address the topic at all, not even in softened form). Materiality: **high** — this is precisely the risk an informed reader most needs surfaced before committing capital, and it is the one risk category entirely missing from the front-of-book narrative.

2. **Silent drop — the fire-incident settlement and any care-quality/safety narrative.** The MD&A's "Health, Safety & Quality Assurance" section (p.38-40) describes SHE frameworks, NABH accreditation, and monthly care audits in confident, forward-looking language, with no reference anywhere to the ₹12 Cr settlement paid during the year for a resident's death, nor to the ~2-year gap between the incident (Jan-2023) and the settlement/exceptional-item recognition (Q3 FY25). Classification: **silent drop.** Materiality: **high** for a business whose entire value proposition depends on trust in physical-safety and care quality; the operational section (financial notes) discloses the payment, but the strategic/quality-assurance narrative section does not connect it to the same subject matter at all, even implicitly.

3. **Hedged retreat — "operational profit" language for Antara Purukul.** The Outlook section states Antara Purukul achieved "operational profit of ₹1+ crores ahead of schedule" — a materially smaller and vaguer figure than the ₹8.30 Cr PAT / ₹10.42 Cr PBT the same Annual Report discloses in its own AOC-1 schedule for the identical entity. Classification: **hedged retreat** — the claim is directionally true (profit was achieved, ahead of schedule) but presented at a scale roughly one-eighth of the audited figure, using an undefined term ("operational profit") that reads as more conservative than the underlying number, without naming the actual scale. Materiality: **low-to-moderate** — this cuts in the conservative direction (understating strength), which is a benign form of imprecision rather than a promotional one, but it is still an internal inconsistency a careful reader should catch, and the undefined-term pattern (revenue vs. total income, "net revenue" vs. AOC-1 turnover) recurs often enough in this AR to be a house style rather than a one-off.

4. **No abandonment found on:** the core "Antara 3.0" strategic thesis itself, AGEasy scale-up claims, or the rights-issue capital-deployment narrative — all of these are consistent with, or specifically corroborated by, the operational and financial sections read in Phases 1-5.

Distinct from Phase 4B (which catches coverage the AR never provides anywhere): items 1 and 2 above are things the AR **does** disclose, in full, in the notes and audit opinion — they are simply never connected to, or even mentioned in, the confident opening narrative that a time-pressed reader is most likely to absorb first.

### Phase 6 summary

**Phase 6 Verdict: 🟡 Watch.** The front matter is operationally specific and largely consistent with the financials on growth metrics, but exhibits a clear and material pattern of silently dropping the two risk categories (RPT/impairment, safety/reputational) that the rest of the same document treats as serious enough to be a statutory KAM and a ₹12 Cr exceptional item, respectively.
