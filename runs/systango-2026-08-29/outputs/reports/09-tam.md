## SECTION 1: MARKET DEFINITION

### 1A Precise boundaries

**Product scope.** Offshore/nearshore digital engineering services across the four lines that
make up Systango's actual FY26 revenue mix (Investor Presentation, "FY26 Services Revenue
Breakup", p.4 of deck):
- AI/ML, Data Engineering & Cloud — 46% of FY26 revenue (GenAI development, LLM
  integration, intelligent document processing, custom AI agents, data pipelines, cloud
  migration/DevOps/SRE).
- App Development — 38% of FY26 revenue (mobile/web app development, microservices,
  staff augmentation on client-embedded teams).
- Blockchain & Web3 / BaaS — 16% of FY26 revenue (DeFi platforms, asset tokenisation, NFT
  marketplaces, Layer-2 integrations, Blockchain-as-a-Service).

**Geographic scope.** Delivery from India (Indore HQ, STP-I Crystal IT Park), billing offices in
UK (London) and USA. FY26 geographic revenue split (deck, "FY 2026 Geographical Split"):
UK 65.65%, US 28.04%, Canada 2.92%, ROW 3.39%. UK+US = 93.69% of FY26 revenue — this is
functionally a two-geography company with a long tail across 10+ countries (Nigeria,
Netherlands, Qatar, Australia, UAE, New Zealand, Ireland, Saudi Arabia, South Africa per the
deck's geography map).

**Customer scope.** High-growth startups, scale-ups and select enterprises pursuing digital
transformation/AI adoption (deck, "What Sets Us Apart" — target customers). 600+ clients
(deck snapshot) across FinTech, EdTech, Healthcare, Commerce, AgriTech, Travel, Energy,
SportsTech, Hospitality, Logistics, Fantasy Sports/Gaming (deck, "Revenue by Verticals").
Client concentration is real: FY26 top-1 client = 16% of revenue, top-3 = 42%, top-5 = 55%,
top-10 = 72% (deck, "Revenue by Major Clients").

**Channel scope.** Direct B2B sales led from UK/US (Director-Sales USA/EMEA, VP Sales
Americas per KMP slide), plus hyperscaler-partner referral: AWS Select Tier Services Partner,
Google Cloud Partner (with Generative AI and Infrastructure specialisations), Adobe Solution
Partner (Bronze), and a new (FY26) Alibaba Cloud strategic alliance for AI-driven cloud
migration. Inbound reputation channel: Google's "first 20 companies worldwide recognised for
GenAI expertise", Clutch Global rankings.

**Price segment.** Boutique/mid-market digital engineering — AR "About Systango" (p.1)
explicitly self-describes as a "boutique-style approach", 280+ team members (deck snapshot).
This is not Tier-1 IT-major scale (TCS/Infosys/Accenture-type multi-billion-dollar programmes)
and not the unorganised freelance/gig layer (Upwork/Toptal-sourced individual contractors).

**Explicit exclusions.** Pure BPO/voice-process outsourcing; hardware/infrastructure resale;
ERP/legacy-system implementation; ambient ("classic") custom software maintenance/testing
services not tied to AI/cloud/app/blockchain; consumer-facing crypto trading/speculation
(Systango builds B2B platforms for DeFi/trading clients, it does not run its own token or
exchange).

**Credibility read: BROAD.** These are macro backdrop statistics, not a scoped claim of "the

it is carried forward from an earlier pipeline stage's evidence base and used in Section 3B/5C
below to select the conservative SOM growth-rate scenario, consistent with the operator
instruction to size runway against delivered performance, not aspirational framing.

## SECTION 2: TAM ESTIMATION — MULTIPLE METHODS

FX used throughout: USD/INR 95.4 (US Federal Reserve H.10 release, 24-28 Aug 2026 range
95.23–95.81; midpoint used). $1bn ≈ Rs 9,540 cr.

### Method 1 — Top-down, service-line segment sum (global)

Sum of the four externally-sized market segments matching Systango's actual FY26 revenue
mix, each figure taken at the **lowest** value found across multiple named research houses
(conservative bias):

| Segment | 2025 size (conservative, lowest sourced) | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Custom software/app development | USD 44.2 bn | Global Market Insights (GMI), CAGR 17.3% 2026-2035 |
| Generative AI (software-scoped) | USD 22.2 bn | Grand View Research, narrower software-only scope |
| Cloud professional services | USD 25.5 bn | DataInsightsReports, CAGR ~16% to 2033 |
| Blockchain-as-a-Service | USD 1.76 bn | Mordor Intelligence, CAGR 17.28% 2026-2031 |
| **Sum (global)** | **USD 93.66 bn** | — |

= Rs 8,93,516 cr ≈ **Rs 8,93,500 cr**. This is the narrowest, most segment-matched TAM
figure and is used as the **conservative TAM anchor**.

*Caveat:* the Generative AI figure is a market-size proxy, not a pure-services number — it
includes some platform/model-licensing spend outside Systango's services scope, which
likely overstates this slice slightly; no services-only GenAI figure was found (flagged as a
data gap, not corrected by estimation).

### Method 2 — Bottom-up, geography-weighted IT-outsourcing spend

Addressable unit = enterprise/startup IT-outsourcing spend in Systango's actual client
geographies, scaled by its FY26 geography mix (UK 65.65% + US 28.04% = 93.69% of revenue).

| Geography | 2025 IT-outsourcing market size | Source |
|---|---|---|
| UK | USD 40.53 bn | MarketDataForecast, "UK IT Outsourcing Market Size, Share and Analysis, 2034" |
| US | USD 185.33 bn | Mordor Intelligence-pattern report ("...2026-2031" forecast horizon), IT outsourcing, United States |
| **UK+US sum** | **USD 225.86 bn** | — |

Scaled to Systango's full client-geography mix (UK+US = 93.69% of revenue, so full-mix
market = UK+US sum ÷ 0.9369): **USD 241.06 bn** ≈ Rs 22,99,700 cr.

This is a materially **broader** category than Method 1 (it captures all forms of IT
outsourcing — testing, staffing, infra ops — not just the four service lines Systango actually
sells), so it is treated as an **upper sanity-check bound**, not the conservative anchor.

### Method 3 — Peer revenue aggregation (flagged LOW confidence)

Named comparable India-based boutique/digital-engineering exporters with public revenue
disclosure:

| Peer | Latest disclosed revenue | Source |
|---|---|---|
| InfoBeans Technologies (Indore-based, AI-augmented app dev) | Rs 539 cr (FY26) | Company FY26 results, AI-augmented dev = 43% of revenue |
| Cigniti Technologies (digital assurance/engineering; absorbed into Coforge FY25) | Rs 103.22 cr (FY25, standalone reported figure, down 44.2% YoY due to Coforge restructuring) | Company FY25 filing — **flagged unreliable as a standalone comparator post-merger** |
| Systango (self) | Rs 90.4 cr (FY26) | This run |
| **Named-peer sum** | **Rs 732.6 cr** | — |

Per house convention, an unorganised-sector layer (freelance studios, small boutique shops,
30-60% typical add-on in Indian services categories) would sit on top of this, but only two
clean named peers were found in this search pass — the true competitive population (global,
not just India) runs into the hundreds/thousands of small studios and freelance networks, none
individually sized in public data. **This method materially undercounts and is excluded from
the conservative/realistic TAM selection below; it is retained only for Section 4C market-
structure context.**

### Method 5 — Geography/export-share cross-check (modified; per-capita benchmarking
does not apply to a niche B2B services exporter)

India's total IT-services export revenue is USD 224.4 bn (FY24-25, NASSCOM-sourced per AR
MD&A). Against a global IT-outsourcing market of USD 409.29 bn (Straits Research, 2025),
India-origin exporters (including Systango) compete for roughly 55% of the addressable global
outsourcing pool by geography-independent share (224.4/409.29 = 54.8%) — directionally
consistent with, and a sanity check on, the order of magnitude in Methods 1 and 2. Confidence:
LOW (directional only, not a scoped TAM number).

### Triangulation table

| Method | Estimate (USD) | Estimate (Rs Cr) | Confidence | Staleness |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 — Top-down segment sum (global) | $93.66 bn | ~8,93,500 | M | none (2025-vintage data) |
| 2 — Bottom-up geography-weighted | $241.06 bn | ~22,99,700 | M | none |
| 3 — Peer aggregation | n/a (Rs 732.6 cr named-peer floor) | n/a | L (undercounts) | none |
| 5 — Geography/export-share benchmark | directional (~55% global-outsourcing share) | n/a | L | none |

**Conservative estimate: USD 93.7 bn ≈ Rs 8,93,500 cr** (Method 1, narrowest segment match).
**Realistic estimate:** weighted 60% Method 1 / 40% Method 2 (weighting toward the
segment-matched figure since it reflects Systango's actual four-line mix, while acknowledging
some of its App Development revenue is generic staff-augmentation that competes in the
broader outsourcing pool captured by Method 2) = USD 152.6 bn ≈ **Rs 14,56,000 cr**.

Methods 1 and 2 diverge by 2.6x — flagged and explained above (category-boundary
difference: narrow four-line segment sum vs. the whole IT-outsourcing category), not averaged
away silently.

**Management's claim vs conservative estimate.** Using the broadest figure management
actually cited (global IT spending, USD 5.5 trillion, AR MD&A p.50) as the only available
benchmark of framing (see 1B caveat — this is macro backdrop, not a scoped TAM claim):
USD 5.5 tn ≈ Rs 52,47,000 cr. Ratio vs conservative TAM = 52,47,000 / 8,93,500 = **58.7x**.
Per the standard read (>2x = likely inflated), this is far in inflated territory — expected, since
it is whole-of-global-IT-spending, not a scoped figure. Read: **inflated (as a framing device);
not a literal TAM claim.**

## SECTION 3: SAM & SOM

### 3A SAM

Starting from the conservative TAM (Rs 8,93,500 cr / $93.7 bn):

**Filter 1 — Geography.** Systango's actual revenue is 93.69% UK+US. Applying the UK+US
share of the global IT-outsourcing pool (225.86bn / 409.29bn = 55.2%, Method 2/5 inputs) as a
proxy weight for the segment-specific TAM (no segment-specific geography split was found;
this is a cross-applied ratio, flagged as an approximation):
$93.66bn × 55.2% = **$51.68 bn ≈ Rs 4,93,000 cr**.

**Filter 2 — Customer/capability scope (SME/mid-market vs Tier-1-enterprise share).**
**NOT FOUND.** No sourced public split exists of how much of the geography-filtered pool is
SME/startup/mid-market (Systango's realistic target segment, per its own "target customers"
positioning) versus large-enterprise programmes that go almost exclusively to Tier-1 IT
majors. Per house rule, this is left as a named data gap rather than an invented percentage.
SAM below should therefore be read as an **outer bound** — it still includes enterprise-scale
demand Systango cannot realistically compete for.

**SAM = Rs 4,93,000 cr (~$51.7 bn); SAM as % of TAM (conservative) = 55.2%.**

### 3B SOM at 3 and 5 years

Current revenue: Rs 90.4 cr (~$9.48 mn). Current SAM share = 9.48mn / 51,680mn =
**0.018%** — a share so small that the standard "1-2pp in 3 years / 3-5pp aggressive"
percentage-point share-gain rules do not produce a meaningful signal at this TAM/company-size
ratio (1pp of a $51.7bn SAM = ~$517mn, roughly 55x Systango's entire current revenue —
mechanically impossible for a 285-person delivery organisation). SOM here is therefore built
bottom-up off Systango's own revenue trajectory rather than off SAM percentage points, per
the operator instruction to size runway honestly against delivered performance.

**Historical realized 3-year revenue CAGR (FY23→FY26): 20%** — Rs 52.3cr → Rs 90.4cr
(deck income statement, cross-checked against the deck's own stated "3 Year Revenue CAGR
20%" snapshot metric). This is **actual delivered growth**, not guidance.

**Conservative/execution-adjusted case (used for the formal SOM handoff below).** The
task brief carries forward a documented ~64% FY26 revenue-target miss and a D credibility
rating on forward guidance from an earlier pipeline stage. Per the pipeline's conservative-bias
rule ("when choosing between estimates, take the lower"), this stage applies an explicit,
disclosed halving of the historical realized CAGR (20% → 10%) as the conservative growth
assumption for SOM — **this halving is an analyst methodology choice made transparent
here, not a sourced market data point.** It reflects three named, evidenced risk factors: (i) the
documented forward-guidance unreliability itself; (ii) the Blockchain/Web3 slice (16% of FY26
revenue) carrying materially higher demand cyclicality than the other two lines, with no
independent sizing of its durability; (iii) base-effect scaling difficulty — growing off a Rs 90cr
base to Rs 145-225cr requires a much larger absolute dollar increment than the FY23-26 climb
off a Rs 52cr base.

| Scenario | 3yr revenue (Rs Cr) | 5yr revenue (Rs Cr) | CAGR used |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Conservative (reported SOM, formal handoff)** | **120.3** | **145.6** | **10%** |
| Upside / historical-continuation (sensitivity only, not the formal handoff) | 156.2 | 224.9 | 20% |

SOM 3yr = Rs 120.3 cr; SOM 5yr = Rs 145.6 cr. **SOM-implied revenue CAGR: 10% (yr3), 10%
(yr5)** — this is the FORMAL handoff to Stage 11.

### 3C Capacity cross-check

B07 capex-embedded growth figure was carried in as ~0 (asset-light services business; growth
is headcount- and mix-driven, not capex-driven). Systango's own disclosed metrics confirm
this: technical headcount was roughly flat over FY23-FY26 (281 → 244 → 251 → 285, deck
"Employee Bifurcation" chart) while revenue nearly doubled (Rs 52.3cr → 90.4cr), driven by
revenue-per-employee mix-shift (Rs 16.8L → Rs 29.4L per B04 input) toward higher-value
AI/ML work.

At the current Rs 29.4L/employee realization, the conservative SOM 3yr figure (Rs 120.3 cr)
implies ≈ 409 technical staff needed vs. 285 in FY26 — a **~44% headcount increase over
three years**. There is no capex bottleneck (asset-light model, confirmed by B07). The
capacity check is therefore: **sufficient, in the sense that no physical/capital constraint binds
this SOM** — but the real binding constraint shifts to hiring pace, talent-market competition
for AI/ML engineers, and utilisation, none of which were evaluated in this stage. Neither the
SOM nor a capex plan is "the optimistic one" here because there is no capex plan to compare
against; the open question is execution (hiring + delivery quality), consistent with the D
credibility rating carried into this stage.

studios and individual freelance networks globally (unorganised, no reliable aggregate sizing
found — flagged NOT FOUND); at the other end, Tier-1 IT majors (Accenture, TCS, Infosys,

representative of, a wider boutique-consolidation trend in this space. Top-3/competitor-
concentration figures specific to Systango's four-line niche were **not found** in public data.

### 5A Funnel

```

  -> TAM (realistic, 60/40 blend with geography-wtd)... Rs 14,56,000 cr ($152.6bn)
SAM (UK+US geography filter, 55.2% of TAM)............ Rs   4,93,000 cr  ($51.7bn)
  current SAM share.................................... 0.018%

  [sensitivity only: SOM 3yr/5yr at historical 20% CAGR = Rs 156.2cr / Rs 224.9cr]
Current FY26 revenue.................................... Rs       90.4 cr
```

### 5B Runway assessment

- Revenue headroom = SAM ÷ current revenue = 4,93,000 / 90.4 = **~5,454x**.
- Blended TAM growth rate (weighted by FY26 segment mix: 46% cloud-services-proxy CAGR
  16.85%, 38% custom-software CAGR 17.3%, 16% BaaS CAGR 17.28%) ≈ **17%**.
- Company CAGR vs TAM growth: historically (20% realized FY23-26) Systango has grown
  modestly **faster** than its blended underlying market (gaining share off a tiny base). The
  conservative SOM case used for the formal handoff (10%) is **below** blended TAM growth
  (17%) — i.e., the conservative scenario deliberately assumes Systango loses relative pace
  versus its market, consistent with the credibility discount applied in 3B.
- Years to saturate SAM at current growth: not a meaningful number at this ratio (SAM is
  ~5,454x current revenue) — this headroom is a market-ceiling artifact of a very large global
  services category against a Rs 90cr company, not a usable growth signal on its own.

### 5C Runway classification: **MASSIVE** — with an explicit caveat

Mechanically, the SAM/revenue ratio and blended TAM growth rate both sit at the top of the
runway matrix, so the classification is MASSIVE. **This describes the market ceiling only.**
Per the operator's explicit framing for this run, SOM — not TAM/SAM — governs the actual
runway read for a Rs 90.4cr company with a D-rated forward-guidance track record. The
conservative SOM case implies only 10% revenue CAGR, below the market's own 17% blended
growth rate. Read MASSIVE as "the market will not be the constraint for a very long time";
do not read it as a growth forecast. The binding constraints are execution credibility, hiring
pace for AI/ML talent, and client concentration (Section 3C, 4B) — none of which the TAM
number disciplines.

### 5D SAM expansion levers actually being pursued

- **Tech Alchemy Ltd (UK) acquisition** (in process, Letter to Shareholders p.13) — expands UK
  client base/talent; potential addition not sized (deal terms/revenue contribution NOT FOUND
  in the documents reviewed).
- **Alibaba Cloud strategic alliance (FY26)** — opens AI-driven cloud-migration deal flow beyond
  the existing AWS/Google Cloud partner channels; incremental addressable revenue NOT
  FOUND (too new to have disclosed contribution).
- **Further acquisitions** stated as an active IPO objective (Letter to Shareholders p.13,
  "confident we will hit the objective this year") — directionally expands SAM via inorganic
  geography/capability additions, magnitude NOT FOUND.

None of these levers carry a disclosed dollar-revenue-addition figure, so revised headroom
cannot be computed without estimating — left as NOT FOUND rather than guessed.

### 5E Final output card

At **10%** revenue CAGR implied by SOM (conservative case), with margin trajectory of
**~35%** PAT margin (stable FY25→FY26 at 35.3%→35.3%, deck income statement; no
further margin expansion assumed given no sourced basis for one), the earnings growth
embedded here is **~10%** CAGR (margin held flat, so PAT CAGR tracks revenue CAGR), which
**modestly supports** (PEG ≈ 1.0x) the current valuation of **10.0x** P/E (SYSTANGO, NSE,
28-Aug-2026, price Rs 233.90, market cap Rs 319.27 cr). At the historical/upside 20% CAGR
sensitivity, the same margin assumption implies ~20% earnings CAGR against 10.0x P/E
(PEG ≈ 0.5x) — meaningfully undervalued if the company reverts to its FY23-26 delivered
growth rate rather than the credibility-discounted conservative case. The gap between these
two readings is the single most decision-relevant number this stage produces: it is entirely a
function of whether Stage 11 trusts Systango's forward delivery, not of market size.
