## Business Understanding Narrative

Rappid Valves makes ball, gate, globe, butterfly, check and strainer valves from 15 mm to 600 mm, in steel, brass and bronze, at one plant in Palghar, Maharashtra. A valve opens, shuts or throttles flow in a pipe, and no process plant or ship can run its piping without one. Industrial valves made 67% of FY26 revenue and go into ethanol, chemical, oil and gas, water and power plants. Marine valves made 33% and go into navy and commercial ships, where each valve design must first pass a classification society approval, such as ClassNK or DNV, before a shipyard will accept a quote.

The marine buyers are the shipyards, Mazagon Dock, GRSE, Cochin Shipyard, Hindustan Shipyard, Udupi and L&T Shipbuilding, plus system integrators such as BHEL, Shree Refrigerations and Muller-BBM on the navy Fleet Support Ship programme. Government shipyards buy by tender at a fixed price with no escalation clause, and once a valve is approved into a vessel programme the yard rarely changes supplier. On the industrial side one customer, Praj Industries, gave about a quarter of FY26 revenue under a rate contract that is now being repriced. Repeat business is 60% in the company deck and "almost 75%" on its call, and the run could not reconcile the two.

Present demand comes from the named naval shipyards (Mazagon Dock, GRSE, Cochin Shipyard, L&T Shipbuilding), which are building vessels now. The Ethanol Blended Petrol Programme target and oil company ethanol capex sit behind the ethanol, brewery and wastewater vertical, the largest single vertical at 31% of FY26 revenue. GeM portal PSU industrial valve tender awards feed the fixed price industrial book.

Ministry of Defence capital outlay and the ₹3 lakh Cr defence production target for 2029 set the pace of naval orders, and Rappid took ₹30 Cr of Fleet Support Ship orders in Q1 FY27 alone. The US data centre customer contract conversion would open export demand, but that customer has sat at the testing and NDA stage for more than 16 months. The Praj Industries rate contract repricing, worth about ₹10 to 12 Cr, is the nearest industrial signal.

The marine line has a moat: its type approvals and approved vendor status are documented, and they score high on rare manufacturing capability and qualification lock in. It is not a sole source position, because qualified rivals such as KSB can bid on the same reverse auction tenders. The industrial line has no moat, since the company takes the metal price and the PSU tender terms as given, and material cost is 71% of revenue. The two structural asymmetry tests, talent and cannibalisation barrier, both score zero. The same PSU shipyard link that builds the lock in also slows collection of cash.

Naval valve mix grows, cash does not

Rappid Valves listed on NSE Emerge in September 2024 after a ₹30 Cr fresh issue at ₹222 a share. FY26 revenue was ₹53 Cr and PAT ₹6.5 Cr, all from the Palghar plant. Two thirds of sales go to process industries and one third to shipyards. The company reports half yearly and has held one earnings call, in June 2026.

The change underway is a mix shift toward the navy. Marine revenue rose from under 1% of sales in FY23 to 33% in FY26, and the annual report's own vertical table supports the figure. Q1 FY27 brought ₹30 Cr of Fleet Support Ship orders, and the executable order book reached ₹40 Cr, up 60% on a year earlier. The annual report does not split the marine third into navy and commercial shipbuilding. The other half of the IPO plan went the other way. The planned Pune foundry purchase was dropped, and ₹7.65 Cr of IPO money, a quarter of the issue, moved from acquisition and machinery to working capital. The mix shift is on record. Its payoff in margin and cash is not.

Revenue grew from ₹12 Cr in FY22 to ₹53 Cr in FY26, 45% a year, and PAT from ₹0.3 Cr to ₹6.5 Cr. Then growth stopped. FY26 rose 2%, with the first half up 47% and the second half down 25%. EBITDA margin held at 19.4% and PAT margin rose to 12.2%. Return on capital employed fell to 14% in FY26 from 17% in FY25 on the company's own disclosure. The Gate 0 scorecard gives 76 of 160. Growth scores full marks, cash conversion scores zero, and the cash rule caps the grade at AVERAGE.

Operating cash flow was negative in each of FY23 to FY26, a total of minus ₹27 Cr against ₹17 Cr of profit. In FY26 receivables rose 29% and inventory 65%, with finished goods up 94%, on 2% revenue growth. Short term borrowing doubled to ₹18 Cr and cash fell from ₹9.5 Cr to ₹2.5 Cr. The company booked no provision against either build. Management gives two accounts. The written results note blames higher order volumes. The CMD on the call pointed to ₹14 to 15 Cr of March dispatches and about ₹10 Cr of material held back until private buyers accept a price revision. Only 8% of receivables are more than six months old, which fits a timing story. Four straight negative years, including the high growth years, fit the other story: a fixed price, long cycle PSU business that eats working capital. The half year to September 2026 is the first print that can separate the two.

Management credibility grades C. The company guided 50% volume growth for FY26 in February 2025 and delivered 2%. The foundry deal called "advanced talks" in February 2025 was abandoned 13 months later. On the June 2026 call the CMD first put FY26 capex at about ₹3.65 Cr, the unspent IPO machinery money, against ₹1.8 Cr actually spent. A ₹119 Cr GeM tender pipeline from October 2025 never appeared in a filing again. The FY27 guide of "closing to 50% or more" is missing from the annual report published three months later, and Q1 FY27 grew 28%. The one kept promise is the quarterly business update. The promoter verdict is CONCERN. CMD Gaurav Dalal's pay rose from ₹48 lakh to ₹62 lakh, with the ceiling now ₹84 lakh, and his spouse drew ₹48 lakh as a non executive director. Against that, the promoter holds zero pledge, bought shares in the market in 2025 and lent the company ₹38.5 lakh in FY26. The FY26 annual report also carries a run of drafting errors, including an EPS of ₹0.1 on the face of the P&L against ₹12.5 in the notes.

No peer contradicts a Rappid claim outright, and demand looks healthy across all three. KSB reports marine and nuclear order growth, Meson Valves India (listed as Quest Flow Controls) ran its plant full on PSU, defence and marine work, and Atam Valves guided 35 to 40% growth. Meson, the closest match in nonferrous marine valves, earned 24 to 25% EBITDA margins in FY24 against Rappid's 19% in FY26, and credits its margin to scarce nonferrous skill, not to naval work. Atam puts the industry credit period at 90 to 120 days and KSB saw PSU and solar receivables run past 120 days, both well short of Rappid's 170. KSB still called the metal price spike a future risk in March 2026, while Rappid's order book had already shrunk by September 2025, which suggests the commodity excuse covers only part of the second half miss. No peer sizes the Indian naval valve market.

The market is not the limit. The serviceable market is about ₹5,600 Cr and Rappid holds about 1% of it. Research house estimates of the Indian industrial valve market differ by more than two times, ₹15,000 Cr to ₹32,600 Cr, while their growth estimates agree at about 7%. The plant is the limit. Management puts capacity at ₹120 Cr of revenue, and two years of 50% growth would reach it almost exactly. The run's share path gives ₹110 Cr of revenue in three years and ₹166 Cr in five, 27% and 26% a year. The import substitution pitch is weak on the company's own figures: India exported $2.66 Bn of valves in 2024 and imported $2.15 Bn.
