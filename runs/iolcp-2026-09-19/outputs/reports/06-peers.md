## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

**2A Demand environment.** No peer describes an ibuprofen-specific demand boom (none discloses the product). More broadly, the picture is mixed rather than a clean industry-wide upturn: Aarti Drugs describes soft domestic antibiotic demand offset by robust exports (AARTIDRUGS-Concall_Nov_2025_Transcript.txt p.3); Granules describes paracetamol inventory "easing down" and demand "building back up" but explicitly not on the back of price (GRANULES-Concall_Jan_2026_Transcript.txt p.12); Laxmi Organic describes cyclical, event-driven demand for its essentials business, with genuine volume recovery layered under the West Asia price shock (LXCHEM-Concall_Jul_2026 -> Aug_2026 transcripts). None of the three frames its recovery in the structural "multi-year demand revival" language IOL uses for ibuprofen; where peers do use recovery language (Granules paracetamol, Aarti exports), it is qualified, incremental, and tied to specific mix or customer effects rather than a sector-wide cycle turn.

**2B Pricing and input costs.** All three peers independently corroborate the same West Asia war/US-Iran conflict shock hitting raw material and freight costs from around March 2026 onward (AARTIDRUGS-Concall_May_2026_Transcript.txt p.4; GRANULES-Concall_May_2026_Transcript.txt p.8; LXCHEM-Concall_May_2026_Transcript.txt p.4-5), which matches IOL's own stated cost narrative for the same window. Where the peer set diverges from IOL is on price realisation: Granules explicitly states it got no price increase in paracetamol despite the cost inflation (Q4 FY26), while IOL claims it did realise higher prices in the same quarter (see Q4 above, CONTRADICTED). LXCHEM's essentials margin is spread-quoted and moved with the shock nearly mechanically. Aarti Drugs describes "positive rate variance" from elevated API prices generally, without paracetamol/DCDA-specific detail.

**2C Capex cycle.** All three peers are simultaneously expanding, not holding or cutting: Aarti Drugs (Sayakha methylamine plant commissioned Sep 2025, Tarapur salicylic-acid greenfield ramping, a fresh quasi-greenfield USFDA anti-inflammatory line planned, metformin capacity expansion to 1,700 MT/month plus a new USFDA block); Granules (~Rs600 Cr FY27 capex across a new API facility, a US distribution centre, and continued Ascelis/peptide CDMO infrastructure spend); Laxmi Organic (Dahej Phase 2, the newly commissioned world-scale ethyl acetate line at Lote, and the Vayu JV with Hitachi). This is an **industry-wide capacity race**, not a lone-expander situation -- IOL's own multi-project capex programme (Rs495 Cr near-term plus Rs1,200-1,400 Cr greenfield) sits inside a sector that is broadly capacity-building at the same time, which somewhat dilutes any first-mover framing IOL implies for its own expansions.

**2D Competitive mentions.** None. Grepped "IOL," "IOLCP," "IOL Chemical" across all 12 transcripts: zero matches. No peer names or unmistakably describes IOL in any call.

**2E Risks peers discuss that IOL's calls do not.**
- USFDA warning letter and multi-quarter remediation drag at Granules' Gagillapur facility (GRANULES-Concall_Jan_2026_Transcript.txt p.4, remediation substantially complete only by GRANULES-Concall_Jul_2026_Transcript.txt p.4) -- IOL's own four calls do not surface any comparable discussion of its own plants' USFDA compliance posture at this level of detail.
- Freight/logistics costs "doubled" with multiple new surcharges from the West Asia conflict (LXCHEM-Concall_May_2026_Transcript.txt p.5) -- a specific margin-risk mechanism IOL's calls do not quantify.
- China "anti-involution" policy commentary, i.e., Beijing-driven supply discipline in Chinese chemical/API capacity (AARTIDRUGS-Concall_May_2026_Transcript.txt p.9) -- a China supply-side policy risk/opportunity IOL does not discuss.
- Chinese price undercutting specifically targeting Indian backward-integration/import-substitution projects (Aarti's salicylic acid and Sayakha projects, AARTIDRUGS-Concall_Nov_2025_Transcript.txt p.13; Granules' DCDA pilot, GRANULES-Concall_May_2026_Transcript.txt p.9) -- structurally relevant given IOL's own DCDA/China-dependency claim (Q6), but not discussed by IOL as a risk to its own supply chain.
- Fresh supply-chain disruption from a South China typhoon layered onto the West Asia 2.0 crisis (LXCHEM-Concall_Aug_2026_Transcript.txt p.8) -- a live Q2 FY27 risk not on IOL's radar in this corpus.
- GLP-1 drugs as a long-term demand risk/opportunity for legacy anti-diabetic APIs including metformin (raised by an analyst on AARTIDRUGS-Concall_Nov_2025_Transcript.txt p.13) -- relevant to IOL's own metformin exposure inside its non-ibuprofen mix bet, not raised in any IOL call in this corpus.

## PART 5: CROSS-PEER HYPOTHESIS

All three peers describe living through the identical March-2026 West Asia/US-Iran war shock to input costs and freight, but they responded to it very differently on price realisation. Granules explicitly withheld price increases in paracetamol despite rising raw material costs ("no price increases we wish we had"). Laxmi Organic's ethyl acetate pricing is spread-quoted against acetic acid/ethanol benchmarks and moved with the cost shock almost mechanically, capturing margin upside within the same quarter. Aarti Drugs describes broad "positive rate variance" from elevated API prices generally.

The testable hypothesis: the sector is bifurcating by **pricing mechanism and buyer concentration**, not by product category. Businesses selling on a floating spread/formula basis into fragmented buyer markets (Laxmi's essentials) or into less-commoditised, higher-mix API baskets (Aarti's broader portfolio) can pass a sudden input-cost shock through to price within the same quarter. Businesses selling standardised, high-volume generics into a small number of large, consolidated global generic/OTC buyers (Granules' paracetamol) cannot, even facing the identical cost shock, because buyer power blocks the pass-through regardless of the supplier's own cost position.

If this bifurcation is real, IOL's claimed ability to raise paracetamol prices in the same window that Granules could not is the load-bearing, testable question: it requires IOL to have a materially different customer or contract structure for paracetamol than Granules does (e.g., a different buyer mix, different contract terms, or a genuinely differentiated cost position), for which this peer corpus offers no direct evidence. This is a sharper, more falsifiable version of the Q4 contradiction above and worth a direct customer-mix question in live verification.
