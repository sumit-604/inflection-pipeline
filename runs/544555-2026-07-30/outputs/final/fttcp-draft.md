## 7. THE CATALYST STORY IN PLAIN WORDS

**What could lift revenue.** Roughly Rs 68 Cr of Railway orders ex GST arrived across
four zones between February and July 2026: Rs 3.44 Cr from South Central, Rs 4.20 Cr from
Southern on a rate contract to March 2027, Rs 19.97 Cr from South Eastern over twelve
months, and Rs 40.24 Cr ex GST from Eastern over eighteen months. That is documented
order intake, not talk, and it lands in FY27 and FY28. It confirms if half year revenue
keeps running above the Rs 80 Cr that H2 FY26 printed. It dies if these orders prove to
be replacement rather than new, or if execution slips past the stated windows. A second,
weaker line is the conveyor belting unit, where Rs 12.26 Cr of the Rs 16.69 Cr planned
capex is spent and management expects revenue from FY27, though no customer or order for
it has been disclosed and the company says it has no prior experience in the product.

**What could lift margin, and why I am not paying for it yet.** Utilisation of 40% to
50% means operating leverage is available, the mix is moving toward bearings that grew
186% over two years, and modernisation capex is largely spent. All of that is plausible.
None of it showed up in FY26, which is the problem. The margin fell while the mix was
supposedly improving, and the second half printed 10.04%. Management named a margin band
twice in one call, gave two different bands, and then missed both. Until a half year
print lands at 20% or better with a stated reason, this is a story rather than a catalyst.

**What could fix cash, and here the honest answer is nothing yet.** I searched every
category the framework lists and found no working capital programme, no debtor day
target, no factoring facility, no inventory reduction target and no customer advances.
The Rs 5.95 Cr of debt repayment out of IPO proceeds is real and was delivered, but it is
a one time use of raised money and borrowings still rose. On the only call available, the
order book quantum was asked twice by two different analysts and refused twice. Silence
on the binding constraint is the single loudest signal in this draft.

**What could lift ROCE.** Deploying the last Rs 5.00 Cr of idle IPO cash and the
Rs 4.43 Cr of undeployed capex, then landing the order book on 40% to 50% utilised
capacity. This is the same order book that carries the revenue verdict, which is why it
is flagged as a shared catalyst below. Against it, FY26's Rs 29.46 Cr of capex is fresh
capital that earns nothing for a year or two.

## 10. THE VERDICT

The composite is minus 1 out of 8, which sits in the AVOID band. Revenue is genuinely
starting, on roughly Rs 68 Cr of documented Railway orders equal to 55% of a full year of
FY26 revenue, but margin is going backwards, cash conversion is going backwards with no
catalyst anywhere in the evidence, and capital efficiency is flat at best. The Kernex cap
engages, because cash conversion is DECLINING with catalyst NONE, though it does not
change anything here since a cap is a ceiling and the score already sits below it. The
TRIM rule does not apply.

The whole call turns on one number in the next half year print: whether cash conversion
improves without payables doing the work. Working capital days at 161.5 with inventory at
236 days and four straight years of negative free cash flow is a business funding its own
growth from the balance sheet, and roughly Rs 68 Cr of new orders to execute over seven
to eighteen months will demand more working capital, not less, before it delivers any
cash.
