## SECTION 1: THE BUSINESS MODEL IN PLAIN ENGLISH

### 1A. One-line description
Rathi Steel & Power is a secondary (scrap-based) steel converter in Ghaziabad (NCR) that melts scrap metal into billets and rolls them, hot, straight into carbon-steel TMT construction rebar and stainless-steel wire rod/billets/flats, selling rebar through dealers and stainless products directly to industrial buyers (Inv. Pres. slide 6; AR p.16-17).

### 1B. Money-flow chain by revenue stream
The company discloses only ONE reportable operating segment ("Steel and steel related products" — AR p.46, Note 3 segmental reporting, AR p.90-91) so a true product-wise revenue split (carbon TMT vs stainless) is NOT FOUND. Two revenue-from-operations line items are disclosed (AR p.83, Note 20):

**Stream 1 — Sale of steel products (99.6% of revenue from operations)**
[Scrap metal + purchased/own billets] → [melt in EAF/IF steel-melting shop, cast into billets, hot-charge directly into rolling mill to make TMT rebar or SS wire rod/billets/flats — no reheat cycle] → [TMT bars delivered to dealers/direct buyers; SS products delivered B2B to named industrial buyers] → [dealers/distributors pay for TMT; industrial buyers (KEI Wires & Cables, Bansal, Modi Hitech, Superon, Vividh, BS) pay for SS products] → [payment on normal trade credit terms; FOB exports were NIL in FY25, so this is a 100% domestic-payer business] (AR p.31 forex note; Inv. Pres. slide 16-17).

**Stream 2 — Other operating revenue (0.4% of revenue from operations)**
Nature not itemised in the AR; the accounting policy note references job-work/conversion charges as part of revenue (AR Note 2(e), accounting policies) and the Board's Report confirms only 193.4 of 47,440 tonnes rolled in FY25 was job work (0.4% of volume) versus 11,181 of 59,489 tonnes (18.8%) in FY24 (AR p.17) — so this stream shrank sharply in FY25 versus FY24. Exact rupee value and composition (job-work fees vs scrap/by-product sales) is NOT FOUND — check investor presentation or concall.

Separately, "Other income" of Rs 2.28 cr (228.17 lakh, AR p.83 Note 21) is non-operating (interest income, gains on asset sale, provision write-backs) and is excluded from revenue-mix analysis.

### 1E. The chai-stall-uncle version
Imagine you're not selling chai, you're recycling scrap metal — old car parts, rusty railings, broken machinery — the way a kabadiwala buys junk, except you melt it in a giant furnace until it turns to liquid, like ice melting into water, and pour it into moulds to make solid steel logs called billets. While those billets are still red-hot, you send them straight down a conveyor belt into a rolling machine that squeezes them into either construction rebar (the ridged rods that hold concrete buildings together) or shiny stainless wire — skipping the step where most rivals let the billet cool and then reheat it, which is like taking dough straight from the mixer into the hot oven instead of letting it rest, saving you fuel and money. You sell the rebar through your local hardware-dealer network and sell the stainless wire directly to companies that turn it into wire ropes, springs and cables. Because you're melting scrap, not digging iron ore, you're really a giant recycling plant, and by far your biggest cost is the price of that scrap and the electricity to melt it — not people, not advertising. Right now your furnace and rolling mill are running only about half full (Inv. Pres. slide 13), like a chai stall using half its stove capacity, so the whole game for the next few years is selling more cups from the same stove — every extra tonne should cost much less to make than the last one, because the furnace bill barely changes whether it's half-full or full.

### 2D. Industry lifecycle stage and RSPL's position
India's steel industry overall is described as mature-but-growing: per-capita consumption crossed 100 kg against a global average of 230 kg, with a National Steel Policy target of 160 kg/300 MT capacity by 2030-31 (AR p.19, p.41; Inv. Pres. slide 23). The stainless-steel sub-segment is explicitly framed as faster-growing (7-8% CAGR versus carbon steel, Inv. Pres. slide 25) and higher-margin. RSPL itself sits in an early-ramp/recovery phase within this growing-but-cyclical industry: it exited a failed Odisha unit (closed 2012), worked through bank NPAs and debt distress, achieved zero-debt status by March 2024, raised Rs 114.71 cr via preferential allotment (Feb 2024), and only restarted its idle TMT bar mill in Q1 FY26 (AR p.17, p.21; Inv. Pres. slide 9, 18). This is a turnaround/re-leveraging-for-growth story, not a steady-state incumbent.

### 4C. Quarterly monitoring checklist (10-15 items)

1. SMS (steel melting shop) capacity utilization % — good: rising toward 80% target; trouble: flat or falling
2. Rolling mill capacity utilization % — good: rising toward 80% target; trouble: plateauing below ~50%
3. Gap between SMS billet output and rolling-mill throughput — good: gap stable/narrowing; trouble: gap widening (implies reliance on purchased billets)
4. EBITDA margin % — good: back above ~4.5%; trouble: below 3.5% for 2+ consecutive quarters
5. Raw material cost as % of revenue — good: stable in the 75-80% band; trouble: rising above ~83%
6. Operating cash flow vs EBITDA — good: positive and converging; trouble: negative for another full year
7. Current ratio — good: rising toward/above 1.0x; trouble: falling further below 0.80x
8. Short-term borrowings — good: growth in line with or slower than revenue growth; trouble: growing materially faster than revenue
9. Interest coverage ratio — good: above 4x; trouble: below 2.5x
10. Trade receivables days — good: stable or falling (FY26 trend already improving per management, 20.31→12.66 days, Inv. Pres. slide 31); trouble: rising sharply alongside revenue growth (signals forced credit to move volume)
11. Job-work/conversion tonnage as % of total rolled tonnes — good: stable low single digits; trouble: rising sharply (signals lower-margin volume filling capacity)
12. Senior management/KMP stability — good: no further unplanned departures; trouble: further CFO/MD/CS churn
13. Contingent liability status — good: no new demands, existing ones stay stayed/dismissed; trouble: any crystallizing order
14. Green power % of total consumption — good: rising toward management's stated intent to increase further (Inv. Pres. slide 3); trouble: stalling (loses potential cost/certification benefit)
15. Any disclosed order-book or dealer-network expansion data — good: quantified growth; trouble: continued non-disclosure at a stage when the market needs visibility into demand pull

### 4D. Highest-value questions for management

1. What is the product-wise revenue split between carbon TMT bars and stainless-steel products, and how has that mix shifted over FY24-26? **Reassures:** a clear, growing share of higher-margin stainless. **Worries:** management cannot or will not disclose it, or the mix is shifting toward lower-margin TMT/job-work.

Operating cash flow was negative Rs 11.06 cr in FY25 despite positive PAT, funded by a swing from zero to Rs 34.17 cr of short-term borrowings (AR p.82, p.85) — what is the plan to normalize working capital and restore positive operating cash flow? **Reassures:** specific initiatives (inventory discipline, receivable terms) with a stated timeline. **Worries:** a vague answer or continued reliance on short-term debt to fund working-capital growth.

Pres. slide 3), and what specific fuel/yield savings are targeted? **Reassures:** a concrete capex figure, timeline and quantified savings. **Worries:** an open-ended "under evaluation" answer with no capex commitment.
6. With SMS at ~53% and rolling at ~51% utilization, what is the realistic timeline to the stated 80% target (Inv. Pres. slide 20), and what incremental capex is required? **Reassures:** a detailed phased ramp plan at low incremental capex ("expand at minimal cost," Inv. Pres. slide 20). **Worries:** utilization plateauing well below target with no clear debottlenecking plan.
7. Aggregate disputed VAT/excise/service-tax/GST/civil contingent liabilities are meaningful against a Rs 137.02 cr net worth (AR p.90-91), including a stayed GST demand of Rs 58.22 lakh plus penalty (AR p.33) — what is the realistic exposure and is any near-term settlement expected? **Reassures:** most disputes are old, stayed, with strong legal footing. **Worries:** a near-term settlement demand that dents already-thin liquidity.
