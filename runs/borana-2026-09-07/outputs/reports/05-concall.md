### 1C. Trigger evolution

- **BIS/China raw-material margin driver**: appears exactly once, live on the Q3FY26 call (27-Jan-2026), as the explicit answer to an analyst's margin question. It is **not** repeated, referenced, or explained in the Q4FY26 deck, the Q1FY27 deck, or the FY2026 Annual Report's MD&A/financial-performance section — despite the AR being approved on 14-Aug-2026 (per its signature date), seven months after the call, covering the exact period the driver explains. This is a driver stated once and then dropped from every subsequent document, including the one the company is legally most accountable for. See Section 2D.
- **160-loom Unit 4B tranche**: strengthening through Q3FY26 (64 of 160 commissioned, balance "in transit," per Q3FY26 deck p.33) and on the call (balance "will start in February" 2026), then **stalls**: the Q4FY26 deck (31-Mar-2026) still shows only 64 looms operational under "Unit 4B," with no completion figure and no restated timeline. By the Q1FY27 deck (30-Jun-2026) the 160-loom figure has vanished entirely, replaced by a fresh "+192 water-jet looms, +3 texturising machines" tranche targeted for December 2026. No document reconciles what happened to the original 160-loom commitment — whether it was completed, absorbed into the new 192, or abandoned. This is a **dropped/redefined trigger**, flagged below.
- **Hybrid power (19.79 MW)**: present in every deck from Q3FY26 onward, but the date **slips at every single re-statement** — see the full chain in `timeline_slippages`.
- **PLI Scheme participation**: a brand-new trigger, first appearing 01-Jul-2026, after the last deck in the corpus (Q1FY27, 30-Jun-2026). No investor-facing document has yet quantified it. Flagged as "why now" is unanswerable from the corpus — the approval date is simply when the Ministry of Textiles acted.
- **Doubling-capacity plan (Rs 350-400cr, 2,000 looms, March-2028)**: stated once, on the one call, with specific rupee and loom figures. It is never repeated with the same specificity in any subsequent deck. The Q1FY27 deck speaks only of "the next Unit 4 capacity block" without restating the Rs 350-400cr figure or the 2,000-loom target. Whether this is compression of messaging or quiet walking-back of the number cannot be determined from one data point, but the specificity loss itself is notable.

### 2B. Excuse pattern analysis

Only one instance of an acknowledged miss exists in the corpus with a company-given reason: the hybrid power delay, attributed entirely to external factors (wind conditions, monsoon, third-party grid-utility shutdown scheduling) — **external-blame** in classification, though the underlying claims (wind-turbine erection windows, monsoon road access, DISCOM shutdown coordination) are the kind of factors that genuinely sit outside a promoter's direct control in Indian renewable-project execution; this is not obviously an evasive excuse, but it is also never paired with an admission that the original timeline (three sequential internal targets before the delay was even disclosed) may have been optimistic. The Unit 4B 160-loom shortfall carries **no excuse at all** — it is not called a miss anywhere; it is quietly not mentioned again. That is **silence**, not admission, and the more concerning of the two patterns: a business does not need to explain a slip it never acknowledges happened.

On the one call available, management does not proactively raise any hard topic (margin sustainability limits, competitive intensity, the FY24-FY26 margin swing) — every substantive answer is in response to an analyst question, not offered unprompted in the opening remarks, which are promotional in tone ("strong operational and financial performance," "well positioned to deliver consistent and profitable growth").

- **Related-party jobwork to Borana Industries LLP** and the no-price-basis-disclosed related-party transactions flagged at B02: the AR discloses transaction amounts (loans given/repaid, interest) but, consistent with B02, gives no pricing basis anywhere reviewed at this stage either. **Unaddressed topic**.

## SECTION 3: COMPETITIVE INTELLIGENCE

### 3A. Competitor commentary and credibility check
Management makes almost no direct competitor commentary. The one relevant remark: "market fermentation [fragmentation], particularly among the small and less integrated peers, does not materially impact our operating dynamics" (call opening remarks, p.3) — an unfalsifiable, self-serving claim with no peer data cited. Cannot be checked against the transcript alone; referred to `peer_questions`.

### 3B. Industry/market intelligence dropped in the call
- Polyester filament yarn ≈ 80% of total man-made fabric production in India (call p.3, unsourced).
- "Over the past few months, demand condition of polyester yarn has improved and pricing are stabilized" (call p.3, management view, unquantified).
- Anti-dumping probe on imported polyester textured yarn and partially-oriented yarn, described as newly initiated (call p.3).
- BIS-certificate import restriction on Chinese POY/PTY removed in December 2025, materially lowering Chinese raw-material cost to Indian buyers (call p.6, the key claim to verify against peers).
- EU suspension of a generalisation (GSP-type) scheme affecting Indian textile export benefits, cited by an analyst, with management stating Borana has no direct export exposure — its buyers export, and demand simply redirects between geographies (call p.10).
- Value-added product mix ≈ 20-25% of production capacity, described as demand-responsive rather than a fixed strategic target (call p.11).

### 3C. Toughest analyst questions and whether satisfactorily answered
- **Raw-material/margin question** (Raman KV, Sequent Investments): pressed twice after an initially confusing answer; eventually got the BIS/ADD clarification. Satisfactory as far as it went, but the explanation was never repeated in writing — see 2D. Real risk: yes, since it makes FY26 margin partly a function of a reversible trade-policy change, not a structural improvement.
- **Value-added product mix** (Madhur Rathi, Counter Cyclical Investments): repeatedly probed for a hard number; management gave "20-25%" only after three follow-up prompts, and qualified it as variable ("there will be some churning"). Partially satisfactory — a number was extracted, but with low confidence attached by management's own framing.
- **Capacity utilisation by plant** (Ankur Gulati, Genuity Capital): pressed on why Plant 1 utilisation (76.6%) looked lower than the FY25 comparator (96%+); management explained it via product-width mix (single-width vs double-width runs), a plausible technical explanation, satisfactorily specific.
- **Asset-turnover / capex-to-revenue** (Miten Shah, individual investor): got a round-number answer ("around 2x to 3x") with no supporting calculation — this is the softest answer among the numeric questions and the one this stage sends to peers to verify (see peer_questions).
- No question on the corpus was asked about the FY24-FY26 margin swing, the SEBI matter, related-party pricing, or hedging — none of these had occurred or become public by the call date (27-Jan-2026), except the margin swing, which predates the call and was simply never raised by any analyst on the line.

### 3D. Customer/order-book signals
Management states Borana does not sell directly to export markets — its domestic buyers handle export, and demand simply reallocates between US and UAE/Middle East/Africa geographies as tariff and preference regimes shift (call p.10). No named customer wins, losses, or concentration figures are disclosed anywhere in the corpus reviewed at this stage (consistent with B02's flag on customer-concentration disclosure gaps generally). Texturised-yarn sales stated at ~7% of total sales, explicitly framed as a capacity buffer rather than a strategic product line (call p.12-13).
