- **Geographic**: Zambia (Africa, government JV) and Europe (regulated-market CDMO exports) are the two live geographic diversification vectors, both 📄-documented via signed agreements. Southeast Asia/Africa cited as "expected to gain increased traction" (Inv. Pres. slide 20) — 🎙️, no specifics.

## SECTION 3: THE 20-CATEGORY SCAN

### FAMILY A — PRODUCT & TECHNOLOGY

**A1. Rare manufacturing capability**
Akums holds EU GMP accreditation across multiple plants (Plant 1 renewed, Plant 2 newly received Jan 2026 following an October 2025 audit — a roughly 15-month qualification cycle, consistent with the 12-36 month timelines this category looks for), plus ANVISA (Brazil) approval for Plant 3, both received within FY26 (📄, Inv. Pres. slide 4; Q2/Q3/Q4 FY26 calls). A dedicated Penem facility (Kotdwar, since 2021) manufactures a beta-lactam sub-class that requires segregated, cross-contamination-controlled infrastructure few Indian CDMOs maintain (📄, Inv. Pres. slide 5). First European GMP approval was received in 2022, giving a multi-year accreditation track record (Q4 FY26 call, Sahil Maheshwari).
Evidence type: 📄. Strength: **Moderate**. Time to materialise: already partially active; incremental value over 12-24 months as more plants add EU GMP (plan for ~5 EU-GMP plants by FY28, per Q4 FY26 call).

**A2. Patent and IP pipeline**
No patent filings, filing trend, licensing revenue, or lab partnership disclosures appear anywhere in the three transcripts or the presentation. The company does market ~24 named proprietary "technology" platforms (Fast Absol, Solphilic, Saftalong, etc., Inv. Pres. slide 6) but these are branded formulation trademarks, not disclosed as a patent portfolio, and no filing counts or licensing revenue are given.
**NO EVIDENCE FOUND** for patents specifically.

**A3. Process innovation**
No yield disclosures. CDMO gross margin improved modestly (37.1% FY25 → 37.6% Q2 FY26 → 37.345% Q3 FY26, per Q2/Q3 FY26 calls), but management attributes this primarily to product mix and volume, not unit-cost/process innovation per se. SAP S/4HANA transformation initiated (Q3 FY26 call) is real automation investment but too early to show cost/quality outcomes.
Evidence type: 🔍 (inference from margin trend, cannot separate mix effect from process effect). Strength: **Weak**.

**A4. Product platform / modular architecture**
Company discloses manufacturing "over 20,000 SKUs annually" across "1,500 customers" (Q4 FY26 call, Sahil Maheshwari) and a broad differentiated-dosage-form catalogue (tablet-in-tablet, bi-layered sustained release, lyophilised vials, gummies, etc., Inv. Pres. slide 6), with R&D spend held steady at 3.2% of revenue (Inv. Pres. slide 8) despite this breadth. However, no trend data shows SKU count or launch frequency *accelerating* — only a single snapshot is disclosed, so the "emerging" (as opposed to already-mature/static) character of this category cannot be confirmed.
Evidence type: 🔍. Strength: **Weak** (downgraded from an initial Moderate read — see completionist recount below).

### FAMILY B — SUPPLY CHAIN

**B1. Backward integration and RM security**
Explicitly negated by management: "we cannot start manufacturing KSMs and all the rest of it" (Q4 FY26 call, response to Sajal Kapoor on the cost-plus/API pass-through model). No captive RM sourcing, long-term supply agreements, or captive power disclosed.
**NO EVIDENCE FOUND** (explicitly ruled out, not merely undisclosed).

**B2. Qualification lock-in**
The European CDMO contract is the clearest qualification-lock-in evidence in the entire scan: sole-supplier status from April 2027, six-year term to December 2032, awarded "after a thorough review by the partner" (Q4 FY26 call), fixed pricing already negotiated against inflation scenarios through the contract life (Q4 FY26 call, Richa Q&A). Multi-year EU GMP/cGMP accreditation across plants (see A1) also constitutes a general qualification barrier — a competitor cannot simply requalify an alternative facility quickly.
Evidence type: 📄 (signed contract terms disclosed on multiple calls). Strength: **Strong**. Time to materialise: 24-36 months (first commercial revenue FY28).

**B3. Supply chain network effect**
Akums is a manufacturer, not a platform connecting third-party suppliers and buyers. No hub-and-spoke or fragmented-supply-aggregation language anywhere in the record.
**NO EVIDENCE FOUND**.

**NO EVIDENCE FOUND** (no concentration metric disclosed to assess trend).

### FAMILY E — GEOGRAPHIC & ACCESS

**E1. Geographic first-mover**
Zambia JV: 51% Akums / 49% Government of the Republic of Zambia, $45mn total project cost, signed November 2025 (📄, Q2 FY26 call). This is a first-of-its-kind Akums structure — a government-partnered local manufacturing plant in an African market projected to grow to over $300mn by 2030 (Q2 FY26 call), where the government procures over 80% of pharma products, largely imported today (Q4 FY26 call). Interim direct export supply ($25mn/year, FY27-FY28) is already contracted.
Evidence type: 📄. Strength: **Strong**. Time to materialise: 12-24 months for direct export revenue (by Q2 FY27); 3-5 years for the local plant itself (CY2028/FY29).

**E2. China+1 beneficiary**
No explicit "China+1," supply-chain-shift, or competitor-exit-from-China language appears in any of the three transcripts. The European and Zambia expansions are framed by management purely as capability/market-access plays, not as beneficiaries of a China-sourcing shift.
**NO EVIDENCE FOUND** (category requires explicit shift language, which is absent).

### FAMILY F — TALENT & ORGANISATIONAL

**F1. Talent density**
Only R&D spend as % of revenue is disclosed (3.2% of revenue, FY26, Inv. Pres. slide 8) — no R&D headcount, PhD count, retention data, or ESOP-to-technical-staff disclosure.
Evidence type: 📄 (for the one data point that exists). Strength: **Weak** (single proxy metric only, category otherwise undisclosed).

**F2. Execution moat**
Cross-referencing the injected B05 promise-delivery record: 5 delivered / 2 partial / 2 missed, credibility grade C. Verified directly against the transcripts read for this stage:
- CDMO EBITDA margin progression 10.4% (Q2 FY26) → 13.75% (Q3 FY26) → 14.4% (Q4 FY26) — confirmed against segment disclosures in all three calls (📄, delivered/exceeded).
- Trade generics turnaround — confirmed: EBITDA moved from -INR10cr (Q4 FY25) to -INR3cr (Q3 FY26) to +INR1.4cr (Q4 FY26) (📄, delivered).
- Capex discipline — FY26 actual capex of INR222cr landed within the range guided across the Q2 and Q3 FY26 calls (📄, delivered).
- MISS — domestic branded formulation (Akumentis) grew 2.9% FY26 vs a "mid-teens" growth guide referenced by analysts and not disputed by management (📄, confirmed miss, Inv. Pres. slide 19).
- MISS — API cost/loss trajectory non-monotonic: FY26 EBITDA -INR40cr (better than FY25's -INR44cr) but the quarterly path worsened again in Q4 (-INR12cr) after improving in Q3 (-INR7cr) (📄, confirmed non-monotonic).
Evidence type: 📄 (the record itself is documented across three calls). Strength: **Moderate** (capped below Strong by the grade-C credibility finding and two confirmed misses — not scored as clean delivery). Time to materialise: already active, ongoing signal.

### FAMILY G — FINANCIAL & STRUCTURAL

**G1. War chest**
Headline cash figures look strong and rising: net cash >INR1,600cr (Q2 FY26) → INR1,573cr (Q3 FY26) → INR1,682cr (FY26 year-end, Inv. Pres. slide 12). **However, this must be read against the company's own cash-flow quality disclosure.** FY26 operating cash flow was INR1,181cr versus INR465cr in FY25 (the figure cited in the injected Gate 0 note as "improving"). But the investor presentation's own definitions slide states: "Adj OCF: Operating cash flow – INR954cr representing advance received for EU contract in FY26" (Inv. Pres. slide 23), and separately discloses Adj OCF of INR227cr for FY26 versus INR465cr for FY25, with Adj OCF/EBITDA falling from 90.7% to 34.9% (Inv. Pres. slide 15). **Once the one-off EU contract advance is stripped out, organic operating cash flow actually fell by roughly half year-on-year — the opposite of the "growing while investing" pattern this category looks for.** Working capital also lengthened (see G2). Finance cost rose 172% YoY to INR94cr FY26, though management characterises part of this as a notional (non-cash) interest entry tied to the EU advance rather than real debt cost (Q3 FY26 call, Bhavin Chheda Q&A). No rating action, undrawn credit line disclosure, or cost-of-debt-vs-peers comparison is available.
Evidence type: 📄 (the balance/cash figures are documented, but the underlying trend they support is weaker than headline). Strength: **Weak** (downgraded from an initial Moderate read once the organic-OCF caveat is applied — flagged explicitly below).

**G2. Working capital improvement trajectory**
This category is **not supported** — the actual trend runs the opposite direction. Net working capital days rose from 91 to 105 (FY25→FY26): debtor days roughly flat (68→67), creditor days *fell* (80→71, meaning Akums is paying suppliers faster, not slower), and inventory days *rose* (102→110) (Inv. Pres. slide 15). Management's own explanation: inventory was deliberately built up, "secured by advance payments to creditors," to protect supply continuity during a period of geopolitical (Middle East-linked) input-cost volatility (Q4 FY26 call, Sajal Kapoor Q&A). This is a reasonable operational hedge, but it is a **negative finding for this category**, not a moat.
**NO EVIDENCE FOUND** (working capital trajectory is worsening, not improving).

### FAMILY H — ECOSYSTEM & EXTERNAL

**H1. Industry consolidation beneficiary**
Management raised a Schedule M enforcement thesis in the Nov 2025 call — "very hopeful that this will result in a shift in business to quality-conscious manufacturers... this is a spare capacity which will come in handy" — paired with an observed surge in cough-syrup alternate-vendor-development (AVD) inquiries following a syrup-related regulatory incident (Q2 FY26 call). **This is a live credibility issue, not a clean tailwind.** When directly asked about Schedule M implementation in the Feb 2026 call, management responded: "No comment. As this is government method, it is not appropriate to comment on it." In the May 2026 call, the answer softened only slightly: "we'll have to look for how this gets rolled out." This retreat from a specific, quantified claim to two consecutive non-answers is consistent with the two evasions flagged in the injected B05 promise-delivery record (cash deployment, Schedule M) and the grade-C credibility rating.
Evidence type: 🎙️ (an unconfirmed, now-retreated management claim). Strength: **Weak**.

**H2. Strategic partnerships**
Two signed, named partnerships: the Zambia government JV (GRZ, 51/49, $45mn) and the European CDMO contract with "one of the largest pharma companies globally" (six-year, sole-supply, fixed-price to 2032). **Note: this category substantially reuses the same two underlying facts already credited in B2 (qualification lock-in) and E1 (geographic first-mover).** They are scored here as a distinct lens (partnership structure and counterparty quality, rather than lock-in mechanics or geographic novelty) per the framework's category design, but the operator should read B2/E1/H2 together as reflecting **two real developments**, not three independent moats.
Evidence type: 📄. Strength: **Strong**. Time to materialise: 12-36 months.

**H3. ESG moat**
No renewables %, ESG rating, SBTi commitment, or zero-liquid-discharge (ZLD) disclosure anywhere in the three transcripts or presentation. Consistent with the injected Gate 0 note that Block E scored 0 this run due to a data gap (data not supplied), not a confirmed zero.
**NO EVIDENCE FOUND**.
