## THE CATALYST STORY IN PLAIN WORDS

Read this as three businesses, because in eight days it is three businesses.

**Residual India Glycols (chemicals plus gases plus the JV) — the transition-alpha candidate, and the weakest backward.** What could make it fire: the NSU/performance-chemicals line scaling toward the Rs 150 Cr aspiration for FY27 (management aspiration, not guidance, and NSU revenue is not disclosed), the Clariant JV paying about Rs 46 Cr a year and a 24% stake sale planned for FY28-29 that management says will make the chemicals business debt-free, and bio-glycol prices recovering off the FY26 trough. What confirms it: bio-glycol realisation per tonne moving up from the FY26 Rs 620 Cr on 32,170 MT run-rate in the next segment disclosure. What kills it: realisation falling again while the Asia MEG index is flat or rising, which would prove the company is losing customers, not riding a cycle. Backward this is the worst of the three: BSPC revenue fell about 26% over three years while its own market grew about 6.7% a year. It is losing share in a growing market.

**IGL Spirits (spirits plus bio-fuel) — the profit engine, on a regulated base.** This is where the money is: the spirits business was about 56% of group operating profit in FY25 [AGENCY, dossier/CARE]. What could make it fire: continued IMFL volume growth and the CSD and paramilitary canteen channel adding brands. What confirms it: IMFL share of the spirits mix rising and realisation per case stabilising. What kills it: realisation per case falling a second straight quarter while volume rises, which is what Q1 FY27 already showed. The honest read is that the FY25 margin jump was government country-liquor price-setting, not brand pricing power, and the bio-fuel half is a shrinking government quota. Volume growth here is real; the quality of that growth is not premiumisation.

**Ennature Bio Pharma (pharma) — a micro-cap with one big product.** What could make it fire: Thiocolchicoside strength (up 26% in Q1) and nicotine capacity. What confirms it: sustained growth without leaning on the guar-gum line that moved in under the scheme. What kills it: a quarter where Thiocolchicoside revenue falls more than 20% on price, or a nicotine end-market regulatory hit. The Q1 FY27 +65% is partly perimeter: the Bio Polymers (guar) business transferred in from chemicals on the 1 April 2026 appointed date, so part of the jump is a line moving across, not new demand.

A note on catalyst honesty. No catalyst here is a signed, dated, documented operational lock. The strongest are management aspirations (NSU Rs 150 Cr, JV stake sale FY28-29) and a cyclical price move (bio-glycols). Nothing rises to the 📄 documented tier of a commissioned line or a signed factoring facility. That caps catalyst strength at Moderate across the board and is why the composite lands at DEEP WATCH, not BUY-candidate.
