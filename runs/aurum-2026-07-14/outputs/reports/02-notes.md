## PASS 3 — PATTERN RE-READ

Method: re-read both note sets one final time looking specifically for (a)
notes that contradict each other, (b) numbers in the notes that do not match
the primary financial statements, (c) disclosure that is deliberately vague
relative to the granularity shown elsewhere in the same AR, (d) prior-year
restatements/reclassifications, (e) post-balance-sheet events, and (f) going
concern language anywhere in the document (grepped across both audit reports
and both note sets, not just the standalone going-concern note).

Most of the contradiction/pattern-type findings in this AR were already
surfaced by the granular note-by-note reads in Pass 1 and Pass 2 because the
extraction brief for those passes already directed attention to
cross-references and "significant/material/unusual/restated" language. This
pattern pass's incremental contribution is to (i) confirm no further
contradictions exist beyond what Pass 1/2 found, (ii) do a targeted
going-concern grep across the full document (not just the notes already
read), and (iii) group the existing contradiction-type findings into one
place for scoring purposes. The specific new synthesis below is genuinely
new; no wholly new numeric findings emerged.

**P3-1. Going concern language — confirmed clean, checked document-wide.**
A full-document grep for "going concern" and "material uncertainty" across
both audit reports and both note sets returns only: (i) standard "Key Audit
Matters" boilerplate describing the auditor's *responsibility* to conclude
on management's use of the going-concern basis (both audit reports, generic
SA 570 language, p.9331-9396 in the text extract / standalone report, and an
identical block in the consolidated report at p.13727-13790); (ii)
management's going-concern basis representation in the accounting policies
(standalone Note 2(e), consolidated Note 2.(1)(e), p.130: "the Group has
prepared the consolidated financial statements on the basis that it will
continue to operate as a going concern"). No Emphasis of Matter, no material
uncertainty paragraph, in either the standalone or consolidated audit
opinion. This stands despite consolidated losses in both FY24 and FY25
(₹(4,447) Lakhs FY25, ₹(7,636)/₹(7,780) Lakhs FY24, Note 5.d p.141) and
negative net worth at two of the Group's largest subsidiaries by goodwill
(NestAway ₹(3,608) Lakhs, Helloworld ₹(1,742) Lakhs, Note 25 p.147). 🟢 Clean
on the going-concern question specifically — auditors were evidently
satisfied that Group-level equity (₹284.47 Cr, Note 24.d p.148) still covers
net debt notwithstanding subsidiary-level distress.

**P3-2. Contradiction inventory — consolidated view.** Pulling together all
notes-vs-notes and notes-vs-audit-report contradictions found across Pass 1
and Pass 2 into one list (each already anchored individually in those
passes): (a) IEPF — auditor's Rule 11 report states a transfer delay
occurred; both standalone and consolidated Note 10.b.2 state "no amount due
and outstanding" — direct contradiction, same fact pattern, same AR
(Pass 2, B3). (b) CARO Annexure B investment balance (₹15,242 Lakhs) vs
audited Note 4.a.1 (₹25,420-26,120 Lakhs) — >₹100 Cr unreconciled gap on the
identical subject, same auditor (Pass 2, A1). (c) K2V2 stake: Note 29 states
81.27%; three other tables (standalone Note 4.a.1(i), the Ind AS 27
disclosure table, and the consolidated Note 1 group-structure table) state
81.94% — confirmed as a genuine internal inconsistency, not a Pass 1
transcription error (Pass 2, B14). (d) Lease-liability "written back" gain:
FY25 figures reconcile exactly between Note 3.b(iv)/14 and the Cash Flow
Statement (₹1,028 Lakhs both places); the FY24 comparative does not (₹1,068
Lakhs in the note vs ₹699 Lakhs in the cash flow statement) (Pass 2, B7).
(e) Note 5.a deferred tax sub-table: FY24 total prints as ₹(1,237) Lakhs
internally-consistent but contradicts the ₹2,349 Lakhs net DTA figure stated
twice elsewhere in the identical note and in the balance sheet — verified
against a rendered PDF page image (Pass 2, B8). (f) Liquidity-risk
boilerplate ("consistently generated sufficient cash flows from
operations...") appears verbatim at both standalone (Note 24.c, p.118) and
consolidated (Note 24.c, p.156) level, in each case sitting alongside a
ratio or cash-flow fact that undercuts it: standalone DSCR of 0.11x (Note
26, p.119) and consolidated lease cash outflow (₹7,050 Lakhs) exceeding
operating cash generation (₹2,768 Lakhs) by roughly 2.5x (Note 3.b(v) p.135,
Cash Flow Statement p.128) (Pass 1 #9, Pass 2 A4/B17). Six internally
inconsistent or contradictory disclosures across one annual report,
concentrated in statutory-compliance and reconciliation-sensitive areas
(IEPF, investment balances, ownership %, deferred tax), is itself a
disclosure-quality signal distinct from any single item — this is the
consolidated view that feeds the disclosure transparency score below.

**P3-3. Restatement inventory.** The only *substantive* restatement is the
mid-year segment redefinition (SAAS/RAAS → Rental/Distribution/Capital,
comparatives reclassified, Note 23 p.154, Pass 1 #8). Beyond that, both note

regrouped/reclassified wherever necessary," standalone Note 33/similar,
consolidated equivalent) with no quantified impact disclosed — standard
practice, not a red flag on its own, but it does mean pre-FY25 segment
trend lines are not directly comparable without the restated base.

**P3-4. Deliberately vague vs. granular disclosure — no new instance beyond
Pass 1/2.** The clearest examples of disclosure that is thin relative to
the AR's own granularity elsewhere remain: the ₹447 Lakh "assets written
off" line with zero supporting detail (Note 18, Pass 2 B12) against a note

₹1,507 Lakh subsidiary equity issuance to unnamed outside investors (Cash
Flow Statement financing activities, Pass 2 B6) against an AR that names
individual related parties, directors, and even a director's personal
₹20 Lakh loan (Note 21(C)VI.A, p.152) elsewhere. No further such instances
found on this final read.

## F. ONE-LINE NOTES VERDICT

The notes reveal concerning accounting practices. Key concern: a confirmed
subsidiary-level loan default, a ~44% audit-coverage gap at the Group level,
and multiple unreconciled or directly contradictory disclosures (IEPF,
>₹100 Cr CARO investment gap, deferred tax) surface a governance and
disclosure-discipline problem that a clean, unmodified headline audit
opinion does not convey. Key strength: the RPT and segment disclosures are
otherwise granular and largely complete, litigation provisioning appears
reasoned, and going-concern status is genuinely unqualified with Group
equity still covering net debt. Overall accounting quality: 4/10.
