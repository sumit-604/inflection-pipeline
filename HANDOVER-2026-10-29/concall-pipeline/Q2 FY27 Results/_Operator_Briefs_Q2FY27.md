## OPERATOR BRIEF: Tinna Rubber and Infrastructure (TINNARUBR), Q2 FY27

Written by the orchestrator from the committed review files (commit 7a422476). No agent was re-run. Sources: work/review_tinnarubr_q2fy27.md (the review), extracted/ (A1 rows R###), and the press release (PR). The earnings call is on 26-Oct-2026, so nothing below comes from the call.

### 1. NARRATIVE

1. Tinna collects scrap tyres and breaks them down. It sells the rubber and steel it recovers. The main buyers are road builders, who mix its crumb rubber into bitumen (road tar).
2. Sales grew about 31% on a year ago, to Rs 157 Cr for the group. Profit grew faster, by about 45%, to Rs 17 Cr.
3. The growth is real. It comes from the core business, not from interest or one-time income.
4. Part of the jump is a low starting point. Q2 last year was a weak quarter. Over the full half year, standalone sales grew 23%, below the company's own 25% target pace.
5. Against the previous quarter, the picture is weaker. Sales stayed flat, but the operating margin fell about 3 points. Higher staff costs and other expenses caused it. Raw material costs did not.
6. The press release shows only year-on-year growth. So it does not show this fall from Q1.
7. The earnings call is on 26 October, so management has spoken only through the press release. The release claims a record half year. It promises the new rubber powder line by December 2026. It sets a goal of Rs 1,000 Cr in sales by 2029.
8. The good news: debt went down, the credit rating went up, and the overseas units now make a small profit.
9. The cash looks strong, but a one-time drop in money tied up in stock and dues paid for the whole debt cut. Without it, debt would have gone up.
10. The main worry is the Panipat plant. The company lost its last court appeal on the land this quarter. About 40% of that land was never released. The filing does not say how much output Panipat makes or what a move would cost.
11. A second worry is checking. No auditor reviewed the overseas units' numbers. One page of the auditor's group report is missing from the filing.
12. Watch next quarter's standalone operating margin. At 20% or above, Q2 was a one-time dip. At 18% or below, the lower margin is the new normal.

## Operator brief: Paisalo Digital (PAISALO), Q2 FY27

Source: the committed run files in runs/paisalo-q2fy27/ (review, forensics, audit, extracts), commit 54090e14. No agent was re-run. Live inputs added here: the BSE price and one web search. Call figures come from a machine transcript of the company's call audio (ASR), not a filed transcript.

### 1. NARRATIVE

1. Paisalo lends small loans to small businesses and micro-entrepreneurs. Delhi and Maharashtra hold almost half the book. Most loans have security. The average loan is about Rs6 lakh.
2. The business earns a spread. A spread is the loan interest it earns less the interest it pays on its own borrowings.
3. This quarter the loan book grew 33% in a year, to about Rs7,266 Cr. Profit after tax rose 27%, to Rs65.6 Cr.
4. That profit rise does not come from the core business. In Q1 the company set aside Rs15.7 Cr for bad loans. In Q2 it released a small amount. That swing explains about two-thirds of the yearly profit gain.
5. Core profit before loan provisions grew only 4% in a year. It fell 11% from Q1.
6. Costs grew about 35% in a year. Income after interest grew about 17%. Cost-to-income reached 41%; this is the share of income after interest that running costs consume.
7. Fee income fell 40%. Co-lending with SBI did not start, for the second quarter. Co-lending means a bank funds most of each loan and Paisalo earns a fee.
8. On the call, management said funding costs will rise to about 10.5%. They said the margin will fall to about 6%. They expect cost savings from the new app and AI only from Q1 FY28.
9. The good news: reported bad loans stay low at 0.66%, and the capital buffer is still large at 30.7%. The company also raised new funding through public bonds and commercial paper.
10. The worry: bad loans in rupees rose about 9% in a year. The ratio fell only because the book grew. Almost half the book is under six months old, so stress may show later. Debt also rose from 2.4 to 2.8 times equity in six months.
11. Watch next quarter: Q3 cost-to-income, the provision line, and bad loans in rupees. Also watch yield, and whether the app goes live by New Year. Q3 results are due by 14-Feb-2027.
12. The gate proposes AVOID because the thesis set two tests for Q2, and both failed. Cost-to-income had to stay at or below 35%; it was 41%. Fees had to reach Rs12 Cr; they were Rs10.1 Cr. Decision Status stays WATCHLIST until you rule.
