## Business Understanding Narrative

Delton draws copper into cable and sells it to people who build things. The
customer list is the business: Siemens, Siemens Energy India, Honeywell
Automation and Adani Power (doc xJGqde p.8), and BHEL (doc JTXAVa p.8). These
are EPC contractors and equipment makers who need power cable, control cable and
instrumentation cable to finish a project. Delton also makes railway and telecom
cable. Revenue is tonnes of copper converted, plus a conversion margin the
customer agrees to pay.

The economics of that are poor by default. Copper is a traded metal and the
buyer knows its price. A cable maker earns the spread between metal cost and
sale price, and that spread is thin unless the product is specified rather than
shopped. FY26 EBITDA margin was 6.7% and PAT margin about 1.5% [both DERIVED
from doc 31Ffa6 p.25]. That is what a price taker earns.

What is changing is the customer, not the product. Management pushed the mix
toward large private EPC players. The EPC segment grew 87% in FY26 (doc 31Ffa6
p.25). Exports went from Rs 7 Mn to Rs 140 Mn (doc 31Ffa6 p.25). The target is
80% branded by FY27 (doc 31Ffa6 p.19), and Rs 2,500 cr of revenue by 2030 (doc
31Ffa6 p.18) against Rs 986 cr in FY26.

Why now: because one number already moved, and it is the hard one. Working
capital days fell to 74 in FY26 from 218 in FY22 (doc 31Ffa6 p.16). A cable
maker that cuts its cash cycle by two thirds has changed how it operates. That
is not a slide. That is four years of collecting faster and holding less.

What must be true is that margin follows volume. It has not yet. Revenue grew
39.07%, EBITDA grew 37.1%, and adjusted PAT grew 21.49% (doc 31Ffa6 p.25).
Profit grew at about half the rate of sales. Growth is arriving and quality is
not arriving with it.

What breaks it is the land. Book value per share went from Rs 105 in FY25 to
Rs 397 in FY26, from a land revaluation under Ind AS (doc 31Ffa6 p.26). Nothing
was earned. The balance sheet was restated. A microcap whose book value nearly
quadruples on a revaluation is either sitting on a hidden asset or dressing a
window, and the reachable record does not say which.

## 2. Business model and archetype

Delton converts copper into insulated cable for three end markets: EPC and
industrial projects, railways, and telecom.

Archetype: order book business, in the EPC and capital goods sense. The
qualifying facts are the customer list and the 87% EPC growth. Delton does not
run the projects. It supplies the people who do, so its revenue tracks their
order books with a lag.

Segment split by revenue: NOT FOUND. The share of railway, telecom and EPC
cable in FY26 revenue is not held. That is a real gap, because the three have
different margins and different customers.

The one economic engine is working capital turns. At a 6.7% EBITDA margin the
business cannot earn its return on margin. It has to earn it on speed. Cutting
the cycle from 218 days to 74 roughly triples how many times the same rupee is
deployed in a year. Management commits to holding under 75 to 80 days (doc
31Ffa6 p.16). That commitment is the model.

Capex is described as a "Phased Capex Plan" following an asset light approach
(doc 31Ffa6 p.18). Amounts NOT FOUND.

## 3. Competitive advantages

Forming at best, and the evidence is one sided.

Cost advantage: not evidenced. A 6.7% EBITDA margin says Delton is not the low
cost producer in a market that contains Polycab, KEI and Finolex.

Switching cost: partial and real. Cable for a Siemens or BHEL project is
approved to a specification and to a customer's vendor list. Getting onto that
list takes time and audits. Staying on it is worth something. That is the
strongest advantage here, and it is why the customer names matter more than the
revenue figure.

Scale: no. Rs 986 cr of revenue is small against the listed leaders.

Pricing power: no, on the evidence. If it existed, EBITDA would not have grown
slower than revenue in a year when revenue grew 39%.

Scarcity: none.

The claimed advantage is brand. Management targets 80% branded by FY27 (doc
31Ffa6 p.19) and is "Entering New Sectors like Nuclear, Defense, Water & Smart
Metering" (doc 31Ffa6 p.12). Nuclear and defence qualification is a genuine
barrier when it is won. Neither is won yet in the reachable record.

Verdict: moat forming, on switching cost alone, and not yet visible in margin.

## 6. The transition and the quality ladder

FROM R1, commodity price taker. TO R2 or R3, cost advantaged converter moving
toward specified supplier.

The mechanism is customer mix. Selling branded cable to Siemens, BHEL and
Honeywell against a specification earns more than selling generic cable into a
trade counter. The claimed proof is the 80% branded target and the 87% EPC
growth.

Management's claim, quoted once: the FY2030 revenue target is "INR 2500 cr"
(doc 31Ffa6 p.18).

Test that against the base rate. Rs 986 cr to Rs 2,500 cr in four years needs
about 26% compound revenue growth. FY26 delivered 39%. So the target is not
absurd on volume. One rung per two to three years is the normal climb, and R1 to
R3 is two rungs in four years. That is at the fast end and needs watching.

The rung climb is claimed on brand. The evidence for it is volume. Those are not
the same thing, and the margin is the place where they would differ. It has not
differed yet.

## 12. Verdict card

**PROCEED to `/step1`.** With eyes open, and for a specific reason.

Delton is worth the spend not because the thesis is proven but because the
unknowns are the resolvable kind. A deep run opens the balance sheet, and the
balance sheet answers all three questions at once: what the revalued land is,
where the profit between EBITDA and PAT is going, and whether 74 day working
capital is real or a year end position. Those are forensic questions, and
forensics is what `/step1` does. The shallow read cannot settle them and should
not pretend to.

The positive case is not nothing. Working capital fell from 218 days to 74. That
is four years of hard operating work and it is very difficult to fake across an
audited balance sheet. The customers are Siemens, BHEL, Honeywell and Adani
Power. Exports went up twentyfold. A microcap at Rs 473 cr with a credible
Rs 2,500 cr target fits the mandate on size.

The negative case is equally plain. Profit grew at half the rate of sales, book
value quadrupled without anyone earning anything, and a large holder sold out in
March 2026.

Load-bearing facts to verify first:
1. The land revaluation. Quantum, valuer, location, and its route through
   reserves. Then ask whether the land is surplus and saleable or under the
   factory.
2. The gap between EBITDA and PAT. Interest cost, debt quantum, depreciation and
   the tax rate for FY26 against FY25.
3. Whether 74 working capital days holds at the half year, not just at 31 March.
4. Customer concentration. What share of revenue the five named customers take.
5. Who Vikrampati Singhania was to this company, and why he sold.

What would change the view:
- EBITDA margin above 7.5% on continued volume growth would fire the margin gate
  and turn this from RESEARCH to a live transition.
- A rating rationale confirming comfortable working capital limits would
  corroborate the 74 day claim from outside the company.
- Evidence that the revalued land is surplus and monetisable would change the
  asset event from a flag into an asset.
- A first order in nuclear or defence would be a real rung signal, because those
  qualifications do not go to price takers.

No valuation, no target price and no position is set here.
