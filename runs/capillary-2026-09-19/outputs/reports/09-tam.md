## SECTION 1: MARKET DEFINITION

### 1A. Precise boundaries

**Product scope.** Enterprise SaaS SPEND on loyalty management, customer
data platform (CDP) and customer engagement software — the SUBSCRIPTION
REVENUE POOL a software vendor like Capillary can actually capture.
Explicitly EXCLUDED: agency/creative/media services spend, loyalty
points/rewards redemption liability (a brand's own balance-sheet cost, not
software revenue), and in-house/DIY-built loyalty systems. This exclusion
is not an analyst assumption — it is management's own framing: "loyalty
software is far less than 10% of actual revenues [in loyalty], with
agencies like Epsilon (~$2bn) and Merkle (>$0.5bn) holding the rest" (Q1
FY27 concall, 04-Aug-2026, Aneesh, per 05-concall.md p.20). Getting this
boundary right is the single most consequential decision in this stage:
management's own $18.2B headline TAM claim (Section 1B) is for the BROADER
loyalty ecosystem spend (software + agency + rewards), not the software
slice Capillary's P&L can actually capture. Confusing the two makes every
later ratio meaningless — Section 2 keeps both readings visible rather than
picking one silently.

**Geographic scope.** Global. Capillary operates in 49-53 countries
(step-1 brief, B01); FY26 revenue by customer geography (AR Consolidated
Note 22.1, disaggregated revenue): United States ₹4,083.54mn (55.6%),
United Kingdom ₹1,134.76mn (15.4%), Others ₹2,127.69mn (29.0%) of ₹7,345.99mn
total. India is NOT separately disclosed as a revenue geography — it sits
inside "Others" alongside LATAM, Middle East, and the rest of APAC/EMEA.
Per the injected instruction, India is shown separately in Section 2 using
independent third-party estimates, but it is not material to Capillary's
own disclosed geography mix.

**Customer scope.** Enterprise and upper-mid-market brands (per Capillary's
own M&A segmentation, "Mid-Market" = $10-50M revenue firms and above) across
seven verticals named in the Zinnov industry report commissioned for the
IPO (UDRHP-I, June 2025): Retail (incl. QSR, e-commerce, grocery, fashion,
pharmacy, luxury, convenience), Travel & Hospitality, Consumer Packaged
Goods, Energy, Banking & Financial Services, and Healthcare (UDRHP-I
p.229, "Assessment of market size of industry verticals from 2022-2026").
Capillary is active in Retail, CPG, BFSI, Hospitality, Travel/Airlines and
(since FY26, one customer) Healthcare; no evidence of an Energy-vertical
customer anywhere in the corpus.

**Channel scope.** Direct enterprise sales plus System Integrator/Consulting
Partner referrals (~40% of FY26 new-logo customers, AR p.11) plus
acquisition (Persuade, Brierley, Rewards+, Kognitiv, SessionM). No
self-serve/PLG (product-led-growth) motion is evidenced anywhere in the
corpus — this matters for Section 3A, because a meaningful share of
"loyalty software" spend globally is small-merchant self-serve apps
(e.g. Shopify-app-store-style loyalty tools) that Capillary's high-touch,
SOC2/ISO-certified enterprise GTM does not compete for.

**Price segment.** Enterprise ACV. FY26 average new-deal size (ex one large
healthcare account) was ₹1.82 Cr (₹182 lakh; "Average deal size (excluding
1 large healthcare customer) 182", 65pp Inv. Pres. p.40/slide 41, +22% YoY);
this is an enterprise/mid-market contract size, not an SMB self-serve price
point.

**Explicit inclusions:** Loyalty+, Engage+, Insights+, CDP and aiRA
subscription revenue (89.3% of FY26 revenue per B04); installation revenue
(9.8%); campaign services on an agent basis (0.9%) — all as filed in AR
p.190/[headline table].
**Explicit exclusions:** agency/media spend, rewards/points redemption
cost, in-house build cost, SMB self-serve loyalty apps (excluded at the SAM
filter stage, Section 3A, not at the TAM stage, since they are still
"software" spend in principle).

## SECTION 2: TAM ESTIMATION, MULTIPLE METHODS

### Method 1 — Top-down (broad "loyalty management market," independent, NOT Capillary-commissioned)

Web search performed 2026-09-19: "global loyalty management market size 2025
billion Mordor Grand View."

| Source | 2025 estimate | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| MarketsandMarkets | $12.89B | growing to $20.36B by 2030, 9.6% CAGR |
| Grand View Research | $13.59B | growing to $31.11B by 2033, 10.7% CAGR (2026-33) |
| Roots Analysis | $13.63B | — |
| Fortune Business Insights | $15.19B | growing to $51.65B by 2034, 14.6% CAGR |

Conservative (lowest) = **$12.89B** → ₹12.89 × ₹8,550 Cr/$B = **₹1,10,200 Cr**
Realistic (average of the four) = ($12.89+13.59+13.63+15.19)/4 = **$13.83B**
→ 13.83 × 8,550 = **₹1,18,200 Cr**

All four sources are 2025-2026 dated reports (not stale). They cluster in a
~20% band around $13-14B, which is materially BELOW the $17-18.2B Zinnov
figure Capillary cites (Section 1B) — the Zinnov figure sits roughly
1.2-1.4x the independent average, at the edge of, but inside, the
"reasonable" 1.5x band for a like-for-like broad-market comparison.
Confidence: **M** (four independent firms, reasonably tight clustering,
current data; methodology differences between firms not fully
reconcilable from search-result summaries alone).

No further subtraction applied at this stage — Method 1 is deliberately
kept as the BROAD reference point; the narrowing to Capillary's actual
addressable software revenue happens explicitly in Method 2, so the
market-definition choice stays visible rather than buried in a single
top-down number.

### Method 2 — Bottom-up (software-only slice, the number that matters for Capillary)

Step 1: take management's own claim that loyalty SOFTWARE is "far less
than 10%" of total loyalty-ecosystem spend (Q1 FY27 concall, Aneesh, per
05-concall p.20). Applying a 10% ceiling to Method 1's independent
conservative estimate ($12.89B): 12.89 × 0.10 = **$1.29B** software-only
ceiling.

Step 2, cross-check with an independent SOFTWARE-SPECIFIC (not
ecosystem-wide) estimate. Web search performed: "loyalty management software
market size forecast 2030." Straits Research (2022 report, via
GlobeNewswire) sizes "Customer Loyalty Management Software" — the narrower,
software-only category — at **$1.82B by 2030**, 8.9% CAGR. Back-solving to
this run's reference years: 2026 value = 1.82 / (1.089)^4 = **$1.29B**;
2025 value = 1.82 / (1.089)^5 = **$1.19B**. This is a genuinely independent,
narrower-scope figure that lands within 8% of Step 1's ceiling — a real
convergence between two unrelated derivations.
**STALE FLAG: Straits Research report dated September 2022, >4 years before
this run date — informs direction only, not the headline number**, per the
stage's own staleness rule; it is used here only to corroborate the order
of magnitude of Step 1's estimate, not as a standalone anchor.

Conservative = **$1.0B** (below both the $1.19-1.29B convergence band,
reflecting management's own emphatic "far less than 10%" framing, not a
literal 10%) → 1.0 × 8,550 = **₹8,550 Cr**
Realistic = **$1.3B** (at the top of the convergence band) → 1.3 × 8,550 =
**₹11,115 Cr**

Confidence: **L**. This figure is a derived cross-check, not a directly
published "software-only loyalty+CDP+engagement TAM" from any single
named research house — no such category exists cleanly in the public
research corpus searched. It rests on (a) one management claim and (b) one
stale independent proxy. It is nonetheless the closest available estimate
of the revenue pool Capillary's subscription business model can actually
address, and is adopted as **THE headline TAM carried into Section 3
(SAM/SOM)** for that reason — see the boxed reasoning in Section 1A.

**Why these are not averaged.** The methods answer three different
questions: (i) what does the WHOLE loyalty ecosystem spend, including
agencies and rewards liabilities ($12.9-18.2B); (ii) what portion of that
is actual SOFTWARE revenue a vendor like Capillary can invoice for
($1.0-1.3B); (iii) what is the specific fragmented VENDOR universe
Capillary is consolidating via M&A ($5.6B, itself mostly agency-model
firms, not the software-only pool). Averaging $1.0B, $5.6B and $13.8B would
produce a meaningless blended number. **Method 2 ($1.0-1.3B) is adopted as
the TAM carried into Section 3**, because it is the only one of the three
that answers "how large is the pool of subscription/software revenue
Capillary's business model can actually capture" — the question a TAM/SAM/
SOM funnel needs answered.

**Management claim vs conservative estimate, two readings:**
- Against the correct denominator (Method 2 conservative, $1.0B / ₹8,550 Cr):
  ratio = $18.2B ÷ $1.0B = **18.2x → INFLATED** (far outside the "likely
  inflated" >2x threshold). This is the reading that matters for valuing
  Capillary as a software business.
- Against the broad-market denominator (Method 1 conservative, $12.89B):
  ratio = $18.2B ÷ $12.89B = **1.41x → REASONABLE** (inside the 1.5x band).
  This is the reading that is fair to the underlying Zinnov data itself.
Both are shown because collapsing them into one number would either unfairly
accuse Zinnov's estimate of fabrication (it has not been shown to be) or
unfairly let management's framing pass as a Capillary-specific opportunity
size (it should not).

**mgmt_claim_cr = $18.2B × ₹8,550 Cr/$B ≈ ₹1,55,610 Cr. mgmt_claim_ratio =
18.2x (vs Method 2 conservative). mgmt_claim_read = INFLATED**, for the
reason stated above, not because the underlying industry report is
disbelieved.

## SECTION 3: SAM & SOM

### 3A. SAM — five filters applied to the software-only TAM

Base: TAM realistic = **$1.3B / ₹11,115 Cr** (Method 2).

| Filter | Ratio applied | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Geography | 95% | Capillary operates in 49-53 countries (B01); near-complete coverage of NA/EMEA/APAC post-SessionM (adds LATAM), small residual gaps |
| Customer/vertical fit | 85% | Active in Retail, CPG, BFSI, Hospitality, Travel/Airlines, Healthcare (6 of 7 Zinnov-named verticals); no evidenced Energy-vertical customer; QSR presence thin |
| Channel/capability (enterprise-grade GTM excludes SMB self-serve long tail) | 65% | High-touch, SOC2/ISO-certified enterprise sales motion (B04, B07 B2) cannot economically serve the SMB self-serve slice of "software" spend that a Shopify-app-style loyalty tool captures; this is the single largest haircut |
| **Combined** | **0.95 × 0.85 × 0.65 = 52.5%** | |

**SAM = ₹11,115 Cr × 52.5% = ₹5,835 Cr** (realistic-TAM basis).
On the conservative-TAM basis (₹8,550 Cr × 52.5%): **₹4,489 Cr**.
**SAM as % of TAM = 52.5%.**

These filter ratios are ANALYST JUDGMENT applied to filed facts (country
count, named verticals, GTM model), not a filed number themselves — flagged
explicitly rather than presented with false precision.

### 3B. SOM at 3 and 5 years

**Current SAM share** = FY26 revenue ÷ SAM = ₹734.6 Cr ÷ ₹5,835 Cr =
**12.6%**.

Share-gain rule applied: **AGGRESSIVE (3-5pp in 3 years)**, justified by:
(i) a documented execution moat (F2, Moderate per B07: 15 consecutive
quarters of revenue growth, 5 of 9 tracked promises delivered per B05);
(ii) active, repeatable M&A capability (H1, Strong per B07 — five
completed bolt-ons with disclosed, if company-sourced, margin-turnaround
math); (iii) organic revenue CAGR of ~42% FY23-26 (B04) far outrunning the
~9.9-12% TAM growth rate. Against this: the H1 moat itself fails the I2
cannibalisation-barrier test (B07) — any well-funded competitor could run
the identical roll-up — so "aggressive" is chosen, not the higher
">5pp, competitor-exit-only" band, since no competitor exit or exclusive
acquisition right is evidenced.

- Share at Year 3: 12.6% + 4pp = **16.6%**
- Share at Year 5: 12.6% + 6pp = **18.6%**

SAM itself grows. Applying the Method 1/5 blended TAM growth rate of ~12%
(midpoint of the 9.6-17.7% range found across independent sources,
Section 2):
- SAM Year 3 = ₹5,835 Cr × 1.12³ = ₹5,835 × 1.4049 = **₹8,197 Cr**
- SAM Year 5 = ₹5,835 Cr × 1.12⁵ = ₹5,835 × 1.7623 = **₹10,285 Cr**

**SOM Year 3 = ₹8,197 Cr × 16.6% = ₹1,361 Cr**
**SOM Year 5 = ₹10,285 Cr × 18.6% = ₹1,913 Cr**

**Implied revenue CAGR from FY26 revenue (₹734.6 Cr):**
- Year 3: (1,361 / 734.6)^(1/3) − 1 = (1.8531)^0.3333 − 1 = **22.8%**
- Year 5: (1,913 / 734.6)^(1/5) − 1 = (2.6041)^0.20 − 1 = **21.1%**

**Cross-check against LBF1 (FY27 revenue guide Rs 1,000-1,050 Cr, organic
17%).** LBF1's FY27 TOTAL guided growth is 36-43% over FY26's ₹734.6 Cr —
well above the SOM-implied 21-23% medium-term CAGR. This is not a
contradiction: FY27 carries a one-off step-up because SessionM (closed
01-May-2026) contributes ZERO months to the FY26 base and roughly 11-12
months to FY27 — its ~$32M ARR (≈₹273 Cr) alone is (734.6+273)/734.6 − 1 =
**+37.2%**, which alone explains almost the entire guided growth range
without assuming any acceleration elsewhere. Stripped of that one-off
M&A-anniversary effect, LBF1's own ORGANIC guide (17%) sits BELOW this
stage's SOM-implied medium-term CAGR (21-23%) — the bottom-up market-share
math says the organic-plus-steady-M&A trajectory has room to run faster
than the single-year organic guide, not that the FY27 headline growth rate
is sustainable on its own. The SOM math cannot underwrite continued M&A
execution (that risk sits in B05/B07's Kognitiv/SessionM integration
tracking, not in market size), only the market-share ceiling on the
combined engine.

### 3C. Capacity cross-check

capex_embedded_growth_pct = **0 / NOT APPLICABLE** (B07): Capillary is
asset-light SaaS; growth capacity is not capex-gated. Corroborating
evidence: IPO-earmarked funds for cloud infrastructure (₹143.0 Cr) and R&D
(₹71.6 Cr) sat at **ZERO utilisation** as of the FY26 close, four-plus
months post-listing (CRISIL Monitoring Agency Report, 06-May-2026, per
B07 Section 2A) — there is spare, unspent capital, not a capacity
shortfall. **capacity_check = SUFFICIENT.** The real constraint on
delivering the SOM path is not physical/cloud capacity but (a) M&A
deal-sourcing and integration pace (Kognitiv migration not complete until
"latest Sept 2027," per B05) and (b) enterprise sales execution — neither
of which this framework's capex-embedded-growth formula is built to
measure, consistent with B07's own explicit finding.
