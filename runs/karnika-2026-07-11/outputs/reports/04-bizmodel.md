# STAGE 4: BUSINESS MODEL DECODER — Karnika Industries Ltd (KARNIKA)
Run date: 2026-07-11 | Source: Annual Report FY2024-25 (99 pages) | Investor presentation: NOT PROVIDED

**Sector-row note:** The pipeline manifest lists this company under a pharma sector code. That is a
mislabel. Karnika Industries manufactures and trades kidswear (readymade garments for infants,
boys and girls, plus a new adult jeans line) and operates a fabric-trading business; a retail
subsidiary (Kidcity Solutions Pvt Ltd) is expected to be consolidated for the first time in FY26
(per run brief; not itself disclosed in this AR, which states "no subsidiaries" as of 31.03.2025,
AR p.34). This is flagged for correction at phase 3 and is treated as apparel/textile throughout
this report.

## SECTION 1: THE BUSINESS MODEL IN PLAIN ENGLISH

### 1A. One-line description
Karnika designs and sells kids' clothing (and now adult jeans) mostly through wholesale/distributor
channels, outsourcing most of the actual stitching to third-party job workers, and separately
resells traded fabric to the same customer base as a value-add side business (AR p.6-7, p.83).

### 1B. Money-flow chain by revenue stream

**Stream 1 — Manufactured (branded/job-work) garments — ~80.8% of FY25 revenue (AR p.83, Note 21)**
[Input: yarn/fabric procured directly by Karnika, "direct yarn procurement tailored to client
specifications," AR p.6] → [Karnika designs (AutoCAD/Optitex), cuts, has garments stitched by
third-party job workers, then quality-inspects, irons and packs in its own ISO 14001:2015 units,
AR p.6-7] → [delivers finished kidswear under brand KARNIKA and sub-brands (Care, Cool, Cube,
Life, Key, Club) to retailers, distributors and commission agents across 28 Indian states, plus
export shipments to the Middle East/Southeast Asia, AR p.2, p.7] → [who pays: domestic
wholesalers/retailers/commission agents; overseas buyers for exports] → [how: credit-term sales on
open account; debtors turnover 2.74x implies ~133-day average collection (AR p.41)].

**Stream 2 — Fabric / stock-in-trade — ~19.0% of FY25 revenue (AR p.83, Note 21)**
[Input: finished fabric purchased from third-party suppliers, "purchase of stock-in-trade," AR
p.84 Note 24] → [Karnika resells without further processing, offered as "alternative options" to
existing garment collections, AR p.7] → [delivered to the same retailer/distributor client base] →
[who pays: same wholesale customers] → [how: credit terms, same working-capital cycle as Stream 1].

**Stream 3 — Scrap sales — ~0.16% of FY25 revenue (AR p.83, Note 21).** Immaterial byproduct of the
cutting process; no further analysis warranted.

**Stream 4 — Kidcity retail chain — NOT FOUND in this AR.** Per the run brief, subsidiary Kidcity
Solutions Pvt Ltd will be consolidated for the first time in FY26. This AR (FY24-25, signed
06.09.2025) explicitly states the Company has no subsidiaries, joint ventures or associates during
the year under review (AR p.34, Board's Report item 31). The FY24-25 AR also discloses, as a
post-balance-sheet material event, an authorised capital increase from Rs 25 crore to Rs 70 crore
and a 4:1 bonus issue in August/September 2025 (AR p.35) — consistent with capital being raised
ahead of a retail expansion, but the AR does not name Kidcity or quantify retail economics. Treat
all retail-segment figures as NOT FOUND for this stage; they are a forward flag, not an anchored
number.

### 1E. The chai-stall-uncle version
Imagine Karnika as the uncle who runs a wholesale cloth shop for kids' clothes. He doesn't have his
own tailors sewing every shirt — he designs the pattern, buys the cloth, and pays other tailoring
shops nearby to stitch it for him, which is why he only needs 134 people on his own payroll for a
Rs 172-crore business. He also has a side hustle: buying ready fabric from other suppliers and
reselling it to the same shopkeepers who buy his clothes, like a middleman on the side. His biggest
headache isn't making the clothes — it's that his customers (shopkeepers) take about four and a
half months to pay him back, while his own cloth and finished-stock sits on shelves for about the
same time, so he has to borrow from three different banks just to keep cash flowing while he waits
to get paid.

### 2D. Industry lifecycle stage
India's kidswear/textile industry is in a growth phase, propelled by a demographic tailwind (0-14
age group = 26% of population, AR p.4), rising disposable income and e-commerce reach (AR p.4, p.39).
However, the FY24-25 MD&A (signed Sept 2025) flags an acute near-term shock: US tariffs of 50% on
Indian textile/apparel exports, with the US accounting for 28-33% of India's textile/apparel exports
and an industry-wide forecast of a 20-25% export volume decline over the following six months
(AR p.38). Karnika sits as a small, sub-scale player within this growth industry, now facing an
export-specific headwind exactly as its own export revenue share was rising (6.1% of FY25 revenue
vs likely lower in FY24 — see 1C).

## SECTION 3: FINANCIAL METRICS THAT MATTER FOR THIS BUSINESS MODEL

### 3A. Ignore-these / track-these

| Commonly tracked ratio | Why it is MISLEADING or IRRELEVANT here |
|---|---|
| Blended gross margin | Cost of materials consumed (manufacturing) and purchase of stock-in-trade (trading) are two economically different cost lines blended into one P&L (AR p.69, Notes 23-24); a single "gross margin" hides that trading has thinner value-add than branded manufacturing. |
| "Return on Investment" as disclosed in Note 37 (3.31x / 331%) | This ratio's denominator is the Rs 866.32 Lacs non-current securities/mutual-fund book (Note 12, AR p.80), not productive operating capital. Using it as a capital-efficiency metric would wildly overstate returns; use Return on Capital Employed (22%, Note 37) instead. |
| EBITDA margin as management states it (16.88%, CFO letter AR p.5) | This is EBITDA / Total Income, where Total Income includes Rs 373.12 Lacs of Other Income (AR p.83, Note 22), of which Rs 173.74 Lacs is non-operating short-term capital gains on shares/mutual funds. EBITDA / Revenue from Operations is the cleaner figure (17.25%, computed from AR p.69). |
| Current ratio judged against a textbook "2x is healthy" rule | At 1.68x (AR p.41), the ratio looks tight by generic standards but is normal for a business whose current assets/liabilities are structurally tied to a ~219-day cash-conversion cycle funded by hypothecated cash-credit lines (Note 31, AR p.87); the relevant test is CC/limit utilization headroom, not the static ratio. |
| Debt-Equity ratio (0.85x, AR p.41) without composition | Roughly Rs 5,887 Lacs of the ~Rs 6,020 Lacs total debt is short-term working-capital cash credit (Note 7, AR p.79), not term debt funding fixed assets — this is working-capital-cycle leverage, not balance-sheet solvency risk in the conventional sense. |
| Fixed-asset turnover / capex-to-sales as a capacity signal | Misleading in a job-work model: production output depends materially on third-party job-work vendor capacity, not just owned PP&E (Rs 734.26 Lacs, only 4.3% of revenue, AR p.68). Owned-asset turnover understates true production capacity. |

### 3D. Unit economics — the physics of the business

- **Unit:** one finished garment piece (kidswear unit).
- **Revenue per unit:** approximately Rs 310/piece (computed: Rs 13,948.32 Lacs manufactured-goods
  revenue [AR p.83] divided by ~45 lakh pieces produced [AR p.6, stated as "over 45 lakh pieces," a
  floor, not an exact figure]). This is a rough blended approximation across domestic and export mix
  and product types; an SKU-level ASP is NOT FOUND in the AR.
- **Cost per unit:** NOT FOUND at SKU level; AR discloses cost lines only in aggregate (cost of
  materials consumed, job-work charges) rather than per-piece.
- **Margin per unit:** NOT FOUND (derivable only as a company-wide blended approximation, not
  disclosed).
- **Volume drivers:** capacity utilization (~44% of installed monthly capacity, approximate),
  job-work vendor availability, wholesale/distributor order intake, export demand (tariff-sensitive).
- **Price drivers:** raw-material cost pass-through (cotton/yarn/polyester), product-mix shift
  toward branded vs private-label job work, design differentiation, competitive intensity from
  organized/unorganized players (AR p.40).
- **Cost drivers:** raw material (cost of materials consumed = 41.2% of revenue, Note 23, AR p.84,
  the single largest cost line); job-work charges (16.9% of revenue, Note 28, AR p.85); packaging
  and freight (combined ~4% of revenue, Note 28).
- **Incremental margin / operating leverage:** employee benefit expense is low and largely fixed at
  only 3.5% of revenue (Note 26, AR p.84), so revenue growth should translate into margin expansion
  on the fixed-overhead layer. This is consistent with what happened in FY25: EBITDA margin (on
  Revenue from Operations) rose to ~17.25% (computed) as revenue grew 37.23% YoY (AR p.40). However,
  job-work charges and raw-material costs are largely variable, paid per unit/order, so the bulk of
  the cost base scales with volume and does not itself generate operating leverage — the leverage is
  concentrated in the thin fixed-cost layer (rent, admin, design team), not the dominant
  materials-plus-job-work cost base.
