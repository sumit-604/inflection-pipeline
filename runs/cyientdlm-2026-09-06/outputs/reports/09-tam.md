## Cyient DLM Ltd (CYIENTDLM) — run date 2026-09-06

## SECTION 1: MARKET DEFINITION

### 1A. Precise boundaries

Cyient DLM is an Integrated Electronics Manufacturing Services (EMS) provider. The
raw "global EMS market" is not this company's market. Three structural facts force a
much narrower definition (see task brief and B04 business-model block):

- **Product scope, INCLUDED**: high-mix, low-volume electronics manufacturing —
  PCBA (52% of FY26 revenue), Box Build (30%), mechanical/precision machining (16%),
  cable harness assembly (2%) — gated by regulated quality certifications (AS9100,
  NADCAP, ITAR-registration, ISO13485, IATF16949). Both engagement models: Build-to-
  Print (B2P, ~94% of FY26 revenue, execution on customer-owned designs) and
  Build-to-Spec (B2S, ~6%, design-led, annuity contracts of 9-20 years per AR
  FY2025-26 p.7).
- **Product scope, EXCLUDED**: consumer electronics assembly, high-volume
  telecom/IT/networking commodity assembly, non-certified low-complexity contract
  manufacturing, semiconductor wafer fabrication (Cyient DLM builds subsystems FOR
  semiconductor capital equipment OEMs, it does not fabricate chips). AI-data-centre
  and robotics work ("Lane 1/Lane 2" in the SET framework, Investor Presentation
  p.5-6; Q1 FY27 concall p.4-5) is explicitly pre-revenue and unsized by management
  ("we don't need any additional capex... probably we will let you know in the next
  two to three quarters," Q1 FY27 concall p.17) — treated as unquantified optionality,
  named in Section 5D, NOT counted inside TAM/SAM/SOM.
- **Geographic scope**: production is India (Mysore, Hyderabad, Bengaluru) + USA
  (Cyient DLM Inc and the acquired Altek Electronics Inc). ITAR-controlled US defence
  work is executable only from the US entity; the India entity cannot touch it. The
  India entity CAN serve non-ITAR European/Middle East/APAC defence and aerospace
  customers (Thales, Rafael, Safran, Honeywell, BAE-adjacent primes) that the pure-US
  entity does not uniquely own. Export mix reached ~86.5% of consolidated revenue in
  FY26 (per injected PRODUCTS input, corroborated at AR p.61 and Note 20 p.144); the
  India-domestic geography fell 83-84% in FY26 — that domestic opportunity is sized
  separately below and is NOT currently being captured.
- **Customer scope**: OEMs and Tier-1s in Aerospace & Defence, Industrial
  (automation, power electronics, semiconductor capital equipment), Medical/MedTech,
  and Automotive (EV/power electronics) who need design-in, certified, high-mix
  low-volume partners. Excludes consumer-facing hyperscaler ODM/high-volume channel
  demand, and excludes captive in-house OEM manufacturing that never reaches the
  outsourced market.
- **Channel scope**: direct OEM/Tier-1 relationships via RFQ and multi-year
  qualification cycles, not distributor/retail.
- **Explicit inclusions/exclusions summary**: IN = regulated, high-mix, low-volume,
  certified EMS across the four core verticals, served from an India+US
  manufacturing footprint. OUT = consumer electronics EMS, high-volume commodity
  assembly, chip fabrication, and unquantified AI/robotics optionality.

Vertical mix used throughout (FY26, per AR p.6 infographic): Aerospace 43%, Defence
9%, Industrial 27%, Medical 19%, Automotive & Transportation 2%. **Flag**: MD&A
prose at AR p.51 separately states Industrial "approximately 30%" and MedTech
"approximately 15%" — an unresolved internal inconsistency in the AR itself. Per
Stage 4 precedent this report uses the infographic figures (43/9/27/19/2) and flags
the discrepancy rather than averaging it away.

### 1B. Management's own TAM claim

Management states: "the global EMS market is poised for robust expansion... from
approximately $650 billion in 2025 to nearly $1.1 trillion by 2033, a solid CAGR of
6%" (Q4 FY26 concall, 21-Apr-2026, p.5). Repeated in near-identical form at Q1 FY27
concall (21-Jul-2026, p.4: "$625-650B... $1.1T by 2033... ~6%+") and in the Q1 FY27
Investor Presentation (p.5: "~$625-650B... ~$1.1T EMS market potential by 2033...
~6%+ blended market CAGR"). The AR itself states a very close but not identical
number: "the global EMS market, valued at approximately USD 648 billion in 2025, is
projected to exceed USD 1.1 trillion **by 2034**" (AR FY2025-26 p.51) — note the
terminal year drifts between 2033 (concalls/deck) and 2034 (AR), a small but real
internal inconsistency.

**Credibility read: BROAD, not SPECIFIC.** The number itself is directionally
consistent with independent third-party estimates (see Section 2) — this is not a
fabricated statistic. But it is the wrong denominator for this company. It is the
*entire global EMS industry*, dominated by consumer electronics and IT/telecom
(management's own words: "consumer and IT remain dominant," Q4 FY26 concall p.4),
which Cyient DLM structurally cannot serve. Stage 6 found no peer (AVALON, KAYNES,
SYRMA — twelve transcripts) citing this exact figure; it is unverified against the
peer record and is held here for the Section 2 ratio test, not accepted as this
company's opportunity.

## SECTION 2: TAM ESTIMATION, MULTIPLE METHODS

All figures ₹ Crore unless marked otherwise. FX used throughout: **1 USD = INR
94.3334**, the year-end (31-Mar-2026) rate disclosed for subsidiary consolidation
(AR FY2025-26, Annexure B / Form AOC-1, p.47). Web cross-check: USD/INR traded in a
91.6-94+ range through March 2026 per exchange-rate history sources, consistent with
the AR's disclosed rate (WebSearch, "USD INR exchange rate March 2026", Sep 2026).

### Method 1a — Top-down, India total EMS market minus non-relevant segments

- India total EMS market size, 2025: AR cites **$65bn (2025) → $75.7bn (2026) →
  $197.8bn (2032), 17.5% CAGR, source P&S Intelligence** (AR p.51 infographic).
  Cross-check (WebSearch, "India EMS market size 2025 2030"): estimates diverge
  sharply — PS Market Research $65.0bn, Mobility Foresights $53.2bn, Mordor
  Intelligence $35.41bn, KPMG $40-45bn (all for ~2025). This is a >80% spread across
  independent sources — flagged, confidence **MEDIUM**, not averaged silently.
  - Conservative anchor: KPMG $40bn (2025) — a named advisory-firm estimate,
    independent of the vendor report the AR itself cites.
  - Realistic anchor: AR/P&S $65bn (2025) — the company's own cited source, at the
    high end of the independent range.
- Relevant-vertical filter: subtract consumer electronics and IT/telecom (management
  itself: "dominant" — Q4 FY26 concall p.4). WebSearch on global EMS end-market
  splits ("EMS market by end-use industry segment share") returns, across sources:
  Industrial ~14% (one report) to 27% (another), Automotive ~12%, "Others" (which
  bundles aerospace, medical, specialty) ~10%. Conservative bias: take the **lower**
  reported share for Industrial (14%, not 27%). Relevant-vertical share = 14% +
  12% + 10% = **36%** of total EMS.
  - Conservative: $40bn × 0.36 = **$14.4bn** = ₹1,35,840 Cr
  - Realistic: $65bn × 0.36 = **$23.4bn** = ₹2,20,740 Cr

Uses AR-anchored global vertical sizes, scaled by AR's own stated "India's share of
global EMS... approximately 4% today" (AR p.50):

|---|---|---|---|
| Aerospace & Defence | $81.8bn | AR p.51 (anchored) | $3.27bn |

| Medical device contract manufacturing | $83.77bn | WebSearch, MarketsandMarkets 2025 estimate (conservative/lowest of the range found: $83.77-100.5bn) | $3.35bn |
| Automotive (12% of $648bn global EMS) | $77.76bn | derived: AR global EMS $648bn × WebSearch 12% automotive share | $3.11bn |
| **Total** | | | **$13.36bn = ₹1,26,036 Cr** |

This uses India's *current* global-EMS-share (4%) as a floor; it likely understates
India's true A&D/defence share specifically, since defence indigenisation is a named
national priority (AR p.50) growing faster than the overall 4% base. Kept as a floor,
consistent with conservative bias.

### Method 5 — Global benchmark (India's share of global EMS)

AR: "India's growing role... rising from approximately 4% today toward 7% or more by
the end of the decade" (AR p.50). Applied to the AR's own global EMS figure ($648bn,
2025, p.51):
- India total EMS (current, 4% share) = $648bn × 0.04 = **$25.92bn**
- Apply the same 36% relevant-vertical filter as Method 1a:
  $25.92bn × 0.36 = **$9.33bn = ₹88,013 Cr**

This is the **lowest** of the four top-down variants. Per the conservative-bias rule
("where estimates conflict, take the lower"), **₹88,000 Cr is used as the TAM
CONSERVATIVE anchor** for the funnel below. Method 1a-realistic (₹2,20,700 Cr) is
used as the TAM REALISTIC anchor. Method 1b (₹1,26,036 Cr) sits between the two and
corroborates that the range is not arbitrary.

### Method 4 — Import substitution (contextual, not independently convertible to a ₹Cr TAM)

AR p.50: 85-90% of India's electronics **component** value is imported — this
describes upstream input dependency, not Cyient DLM's finished-assembly output
market, so it does not convert cleanly into an EMS-revenue TAM figure (marked NOT
FOUND for a direct ₹Cr contribution). It does bound the localisation runway:
government scheme sizing — PLI for electronic components, valued at ₹2,29,190 mn
(=₹22,919 Cr) as of March 2025, and the Electronics Components Manufacturing Scheme
(ECMS) expanded to ₹4,00,000 mn (=₹40,000 Cr) in Budget 2026 (both AR p.50) — is
material government capital aimed at the ecosystem Cyient DLM sits inside, a
directional tailwind rather than a standalone TAM number.

**Management's claim vs conservative estimate**: management's $650bn global figure
converts at the stated FX to **₹61,31,671 Cr**. Ratio to the conservative TAM =
61,31,671 / 88,000 = **≈ 69.7x**. Standard read: **>2x = inflated.** At ~70x this is
extreme — but the inflation is a **scope mismatch**, not necessarily bad-faith: the
$650bn figure is the entire global EMS industry (all end-markets, all geographies),
while Cyient DLM's real addressable universe is a certified, high-mix, low-volume,
India+US-manufactured slice of four verticals. The number itself is roughly
consistent with independent industry data (WebSearch: Fortune Business Insights
$650.77bn 2025→$1,155.51bn 2034 at 6.7% CAGR; market.us $620.85bn 2025→$1,099.05bn
2033). It is the *use* of an undifferentiated global number as if it were the
company's own opportunity that is the problem — precisely the "TAM = SAM" dishonesty
the framework warns against.

## SECTION 3: SAM & SOM

### 3A. SAM — five filters applied to the conservative TAM (₹88,000 Cr)

Each filter below is an **analyst judgment call**, anchored to disclosed facts about
Cyient DLM's actual capability, footprint, and capacity, but not itself a sourced
external statistic — flagged explicitly, per instructions, rather than presented as
a found number.

| Filter | Multiplier | Reasoning (anchored) |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Product/capability fit | 0.65 | Cyient DLM covers PCBA, Box Build, mechanical/precision machining, cable harness, and growing B2S design capability (B04 block; AR p.6-8) — a broad but not complete process stack. It has no in-house PCB fabrication, no semiconductor packaging/test, and no large-scale RF/EW subsystem integration at the scale some regulated-vertical demand requires. |
| 2. Geographic/footprint fit | 0.70 | Manufacturing only from India (Mysore/Hyderabad/Bengaluru) + USA (Cyient DLM Inc + Altek). Cannot serve demand requiring EU/other local-content manufacture, and the India entity cannot execute true ITAR-controlled US-persons-only work. Partly offset because 86.5% of revenue is already export/ROW and management explicitly frames the "India-US hybrid model" as directly answering most China+1 demand (AR p.49, "widely accepted as a key strength"). |
| 3. Customer/channel fit | 0.60 | Excludes captive in-house OEM manufacturing that never reaches the outsourced market, and excludes demand structurally locked into incumbent MNC EMS relationships (Jabil/Flex/Sanmina-scale global-account programmes) that a company of Cyient DLM's size is unlikely to displace in the near term. |
| 4. Certification/scale realism | 0.90 | Cyient DLM holds a genuinely broad certification stack (AS9100, NADCAP, ITAR-registered, ISO13485, IATF16949 — B04 block) comparable to top India peers (WebSearch: Kaynes cited as holding "one of the broadest certification stacks among Indian EMS companies," including the same set). Small residual discount for scale versus global EMS majors competing for the very largest anchor programmes. |
| **Combined SAM/TAM ratio** | **0.65×0.70×0.60×0.90 = 0.2457 ≈ 24.6%** | |

**SAM = ₹88,000 Cr × 0.2457 ≈ ₹21,600 Cr.**

Cross-check against Method 3 (current organised peer-base revenue, ₹14,702 Cr): SAM
of ₹21,600 Cr sits ~47% above that current-capture figure — a sensible relationship,
since SAM should include latent/switchable demand no organised player has yet won.

**Current SAM share** = ₹1,261.49 Cr / ₹21,600 Cr = **5.84%**.
**Revenue headroom** = ₹21,600 / ₹1,261.49 = **17.1x**.

### 3B. SOM at 3 and 5 years

SAM growth rate used: **9% p.a.**, an analyst-adjusted proxy — half of the AR-cited
headline India total-EMS CAGR of 17.5% (AR/P&S, p.51), discounted because that
headline figure is disproportionately driven by mobile-phone/consumer-electronics
PLI-scheme growth, which SAM explicitly excludes; broadly consistent also with a
blend toward the AR-cited global A&D EMS CAGR of ~5% (AR p.51). Flagged as an
analyst estimate, not a single sourced figure.

- SAM (yr0, FY26) = ₹21,600 Cr
- SAM (yr3, FY29) = 21,600 × 1.09³ = **₹27,973 Cr**
- SAM (yr5, FY31) = 21,600 × 1.09⁵ = **₹33,234 Cr**

Share-gain trajectory (standard rules: 1-2pp/3yr normal, 3-5pp aggressive with
capacity+execution, >5pp only on competitor exit/M&A). Cyient DLM's evidence is
mixed: capacity headroom (utilisation only 55-60%, AR p.52) supports "aggressive,"
but the recent track record argues against it — FY26 consolidated revenue **fell**
17% YoY on completion of one large A&D order and West Asia disruption (AR p.41; Q4
FY26 concall p.6), before Q1 FY27 recovered +34.3% YoY (Investor Presentation p.9).
This is genuine execution volatility, not a clean multi-year uptrend. **Applied:
NORMAL trajectory** — 1.5pp by year 3, extending to 2.5pp by year 5 (not the full
3-5pp aggressive band).

- 3yr share = 5.84% + 1.5pp = 7.34% → **SOM 3yr = 7.34% × 27,973 = ₹2,053 Cr**
- 5yr share = 5.84% + 2.5pp = 8.34% → **SOM 5yr = 8.34% × 33,234 = ₹2,772 Cr**

Implied revenue CAGR from FY26 base (₹1,261.49 Cr):
- 3yr: (2,053 / 1,261.49)^(1/3) − 1 = **17.6%**
- 5yr: (2,772 / 1,261.49)^(1/5) − 1 = **17.1%**

These sit below the low end of what an analyst floated unanswered on the Q1 FY27
concall ("25% to 50% sort of growth for FY27" — Vaibhav Mishra / Kiran, Apr 2026
concall p.15; management repeatedly declined to confirm a number), and below
management's implicit book-to-bill-driven optimism, but are broadly consistent with
Q1 FY27's realised 34.3% YoY print moderating toward the underlying market's
high-single-to-mid-teens structural growth rate as the one-off order-completion
distortion washes out.

### 3C. Capacity cross-check

The injected capex-embedded-growth figure (B07) is **NOT COMPUTABLE** — no rupee
figures are disclosed for the BTS lab expansion, the Mysore land purchase, or the
Bengaluru machining expansion, and management states these new lanes need no
additional capex beyond the regular 1-2% of revenue run rate (Q3 FY26 concall p.13;
reaffirmed Jul 2026 concall p.17: "we don't need any additional capex apart from a
regular annual capex"). In its place, the AR's own disclosed capacity utilisation
figure is used: **approximately 55-60%** (AR p.52).

Implied 100%-utilisation revenue ceiling on CURRENT installed capacity = current
FY26 revenue ÷ midpoint utilisation = ₹1,261.49 Cr / 0.575 ≈ **₹2,194 Cr**.

- **3yr SOM (₹2,053 Cr) sits BELOW this ceiling** (by ₹141 Cr) — no capacity gap
  through year 3; consistent with management's no-additional-capex framing holding
  for the "Expand" phase (FY27-29).
- **5yr SOM (₹2,772 Cr) EXCEEDS this ceiling by ₹578 Cr** (≈26% over). By year 5, the
  SOM trajectory implies revenue beyond what today's installed capacity can produce
  at 100% utilisation, even before any mix shift. **The SOM is the mildly optimistic
  side of this pairing at the 5-year horizon** — either new capacity (beyond the
  disclosed "regular annual capex of 1-2% of revenue") will be required, or the
  5-year SOM estimate should be trimmed. This is a genuine, evidence-based flag, not
  a contradiction of management's near-term (3-year) capex claims.

### 4C. Market structure

- **Competitor count**: at least 3 major listed India peers (Kaynes, Avalon, Syrma)
  plus large MNC EMS majors with India operations (Jabil, Flex, Sanmina — named
  generically as "larger global... players" in AR p.57, not itemised by the AR
  itself) plus numerous private/unlisted players.
- **Top-3 concentration**: NOT FOUND for the regulated-vertical niche specifically.
  WebSearch found, for the *total* India EMS market (all segments): "top five firms
  hold roughly half the revenue" (Mordor Intelligence) — broader scope than this
  SAM, flagged as indicative only.
- **Organised vs unorganised split**: NOT FOUND as a sourced figure for this specific
  niche. The standard 30-60% India-manufacturing unorganised assumption was applied
  at the conservative (30%) end in Method 3, reasoning that certification barriers
  make this niche more formalised than generic India EMS (AR p.57 frames
  certification explicitly as an entry barrier).
- **Consolidating or fragmenting**: directionally consolidating in the certified
  niche (long qualification cycles and certification cost are named barriers to
  entry — AR p.51, p.57); the broader India EMS market (consumer/mobile end,
  PLI-driven) remains fragmenting/capacity-adding.
- **Price vs differentiation**: differentiation-led in this niche — AR p.51: "the
  high switching cost once qualified and the long qualification cycle serve as a
  barrier to entry, not just a constraint." Price-led competition sits in the
  excluded consumer/commodity segment.
- **Entries and exits**: Cyient DLM itself added new logos in the period (Deutsche
  Aircraft, a Japanese eVTOL company on a 9-year programme, a European defence
  organisation, new semiconductor-capital-equipment customers) — concalls Apr/Jul
  2026, AR p.51. No disclosed competitor exits from this niche.
- **Import share trend**: reversed vs the general narrative — 85-90% import
  dependency is on the COMPONENT (input) side (AR p.50); on the finished-EMS-OUTPUT
  side, India's EMS exports are forecast to TRIPLE by 2030 (AR p.50) — this is a
  net-export growth story for the niche Cyient DLM occupies, not an
  import-competition threat.

### 5B. Runway assessment

- Revenue headroom = SAM ÷ current revenue = 21,600 / 1,261.49 = **17.1x**.
- TAM/SAM growth rate applied: **9% p.a.** (analyst-adjusted; AR headline India EMS
  CAGR of 17.5% is the unadjusted upper reference).
