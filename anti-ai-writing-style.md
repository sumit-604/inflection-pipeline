# ANTI AI WRITING STYLE

House style for X posts, WhatsApp messages, narrative summaries, and any
reader facing prose. (Provided by the operator, 2026-07-14.)

## THE CORE PRINCIPLE
The brain does not want to think. Thinking burns energy. The reader wants to
believe. The job of the writer is to make believing easy by removing every
obstacle the reader's mind might use as an excuse to stop. Dashes are
obstacles. Drama is an obstacle. Complicated sentences are obstacles.
Adjectives the reader has to verify are obstacles. Hard words are obstacles.

Simple, direct, numbers led, fragment tolerant, dash free, drama free. That is
the bar.

## THE READING LEVEL RULE (RULE ZERO)
Public posts must be readable by a 15 year old. That is the ceiling, not the
floor. The reader is not a finance professional. The reader is a normal person
scrolling on a phone. If the post does not work for that reader, it does not
earn the share, the follow, or the comment.

Plain English words beat technical words whenever the plain word does the same
job.
- "Italica is not a stranger" beats "Italica is a related party".
- "The family's real control is 65.5 percent, not 60 percent" beats "Effective
  promoter group economic interest is 65.5 percent".
- "Aeroflex builds the product. Italica resells it. The extra profit on the
  resale stays with the family, not with Aeroflex shareholders" beats "The
  listed entity carries the capex and the first leg margin."

When a technical term is the only honest word, use it once and explain what it
does in one short sentence next to it. RPT, ROCE, CFO, ECL, KAM, CARO, Note 36,
debtor days, capex, working capital all need a plain English gloss the first
time they appear in a public post.

Test by reading out loud at normal speed. If you slow down, simplify, or
reread any line, that line is too complex. Rewrite it until it flows in one
pass. This rule overrides the peer to peer voice rule for any public post.

## WHAT TO AVOID
1. **Dashes and hyphens.** Never use em dashes or en dashes as punctuation
   inside sentences. Use commas, full stops, or restructure into two sentences.
   The rule extends to hyphens inside compound modifiers: "450 bed hospital"
   not "450-bed hospital"; "Tier 2 city" not "Tier-2 city"; "single location
   concentration" not "single-location concentration". The only exception is
   hyphens that are part of a proper noun, ticker, or filename.
2. **Dramatic qualifiers.** Never use: elite, robust, strong, outstanding,
   stellar, world class, masterclass, fascinating, remarkable, exceptional,
   phenomenal, mesmerizing, compelling, intriguing, transformative. The numbers
   already said it.
3. **Landing lines.** Do not end a section with a triumphant summary sentence.
   The numbers in the section already did the work. Stop writing.
4. **Virtue signal headings.** Headings describe what is in the section,
   nothing more. No "the honest read", "the real story", "the truth about",
   "what management will not tell you".
5. **Arrow notation.** Prefer the plain summary. "EBITDA margin rose every
   quarter through FY26, from 29 percent in Q2 to 33 percent in Q4" beats
   "29.0% to 31.1% to 33.0%". Use arrows only for a true four point sequence,
   and follow with a plain summary.
6. **Bullet as default.** Do not bullet spam. If a paragraph reads fine as a
   paragraph, leave it. Bullets only for true parallel short lists.
7. **AI tell vocabulary.** Never use: delve, dive in, unpack, explore,
   navigate, journey, leverage as a verb, comprehensive, multifaceted,
   holistic, paradigm, at the end of the day, it is worth noting, it is
   important to note, in essence, fundamentally, ultimately, that said, indeed.
8. **Hedge phrases.** Drop "in many ways", "broadly speaking", "more or less",
   "to a large extent", "by and large".
9. **Long or fancy words when a short one will do.** buy not purchase; use not
   utilise; help or let not facilitate; more not additional; about not
   approximately; then or later not subsequently; show not demonstrate; show or
   say not indicate; enough not sufficient; start not commence; end or stop not
   terminate; do or put in place not implement; try not endeavour.

## WHAT TO DO
- **Numbers carry meaning. Adjectives do not.** "EBITDA margin rose from 24
  percent to 33 percent over three quarters" not "Margins improved
  significantly to a strong level."
- **Short sentences. Fragments are fine.** "One hospital, one city." is a
  complete thought.
- **Plain prose over compressed shorthand.** "Free cash flow swung from minus 6
  crore in FY25 to plus 62 crore in FY26 as capex collapsed 74 percent" not
  "FCF +62 Cr FY26 vs -6 Cr FY25, capex -74%".
- **Symmetric treatment of bulls and bears.** The risk section uses the same
  calm voice and numbers first discipline as the bull section.
- **One thought per sentence.** If a sentence has two ideas, break it.
- **Explain structure with everyday pictures.** "The factory is paid for by the
  listed company. The extra money earned on the resale goes to the family
  company" beats any sentence that uses the word "margin" twice.

## HASHTAGS
Allowed, sparingly, only when they carry signal. The company or ticker (one
tag), or a widely followed theme tag. Never more than two. Never in the middle
of sentences. No generic marketing tags (#investing, #stockmarket).

## FORMAT CONVENTIONS FOR X LONG FORM
- Headings in Unicode mathematical sans serif bold (renders bold natively):
  e.g. the business.
- Section numbering in Unicode bold digits.
- Structure: a 4 to 5 word headline, then 4 or 5 numbered sections, then a
  Risks block, then a Verdict or Conclusion block. Each section opens with a
  one line heading and runs 3 to 5 short prose lines. Numbers in every section.
- Length about 3,500 to 4,000 characters for X long form premium; about 1,800
  for a non premium variant; a thread splits across 4 tweets each under 280.
- Verdict or Conclusion is non directional. No buy or sell. No price targets in
  the published version. State the binary gate and what would confirm or deny
  the thesis.
- Sign off optional, short, personal, one line. Do not force one.

## FORMAT CONVENTIONS FOR SHORT POSTS
Single paragraph or 4 to 5 point posts making one point well. Follow every rule
above. Drop the Risks and Verdict blocks. Pick the one thing the reader should
walk away knowing and build around it.

## THE MASS MIND PRINCIPLE (THE WHY)
The mass mind reads to believe, not to evaluate. Every line that makes the
reader stop and process loses readers. Adjectives ask the reader to evaluate.
Dashes ask the reader to figure out a relationship. Compressed shorthand asks
the reader to expand abbreviations. Drama asks the reader to decide trust. Hard
words ask the reader to translate. Each ask costs energy, and the brain that
does not want to spend energy clicks away. Numbers carry the claim. Short
sentences carry the thought. Plain words carry the meaning.

## QUICK CHECKLIST BEFORE PUBLISHING
- Read out loud at normal speed. Did you slow down on any line? Rewrite it.
- Could a 15 year old outside finance follow every sentence? If no, simplify.
- Is every technical term glossed in plain English on first use?
- Any long words (purchase, utilise, facilitate, subsequently, demonstrate,
  approximately)? Swap for the short word.
- Any em dashes, en dashes, or hyphens in compound modifiers? Strip them.
- Any virtue signal headings? Name the section plainly.
- Any banned dramatic qualifiers? Cut and let numbers speak.
- Any landing lines summarising the section? Delete them.
- Arrows replaceable by a plain summary? Replace.
- Any sentence longer than 25 words? Break it.
- Same calm voice in the risks as in the bulls? If not, rewrite the risks.
- Any AI tells (delve, unpack, explore, leverage, comprehensive, multifaceted)?
  Remove.
- More than two hashtags, or generic marketing tags, or tags mid sentence? Fix.
If every box is checked, post.
