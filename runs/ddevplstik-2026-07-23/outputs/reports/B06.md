## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

**2A Demand environment.** Apcotex's own end markets show a sequential recovery through FY26: "paper and domestic market overall does remain challenging" and "muted" (APCOTEXIND, Q1 FY26 call, Q&A with Aditya Khetan) improving to "paper is coming back" and "construction continues to boom in India for us" by Q3 FY26 (APCOTEXIND, Q3 FY26 call, Q&A with Rudraksh Raheja), while carpet, textile and tire "have seen a slight degrowth in volumes" attributed to US tariff spillover on Apcotex's customers' own export markets (same call). This is a generic Indian industrial-demand improvement signal through FY26, not a cable & wire-specific one, and provides no support or contradiction for DDev's ~2x-GDP cable-sector claim — it simply cannot reach that claim.

**2B Pricing and input costs.** A clear, consistent industry-wide pattern across all four Apcotex calls: raw-material (crude/styrene/acrylonitrile/butadiene) prices declined through Q1-Q3 FY26 (Aug 2025-Jan 2026), compressing revenue growth relative to volume growth even as EBITDA-per-tonne discipline held ("margins and revenues are two different things... we try and hold on to the absolute margins," APCOTEXIND, Q2 FY26 call, Q&A with Manav Vijay), then spiked sharply from the West Asia/Strait of Hormuz shock beginning ~1-Mar-2026, with management explicitly flagging pass-through lag and customer pushback risk: "I don't know these kinds of high energy prices... what the demand destruction will be... some of our customers... are being squeezed really hard" (APCOTEXIND, Q4 FY26 call, Q&A with Rudraksh Raheja). This is directionally consistent with DDev's own "margin caution on pass-through lag" framing (operator context) — a genuine cost-plus, crude-linked business managing the same shock, even though the products differ.

**2C Capex cycle.** Apcotex is an active expander, not a cautious holdout: Rs 210 crore (later ~Rs 130-140 crore) synthetic latex/NBR expansion approved 6-Nov-2025, funded by "a mix of debt and internal accruals" (APCOTEXIND, Q2 FY26 call), targeting Q1 FY27-28 commissioning, plus a new Rs 20-25 crore R&D centre (APCOTEXIND, Q4 FY26 call). Notably, Apcotex's own analyst Q&A references "another listed player in this space has also announced CAPEX in the latex segment" (APCOTEXIND, Q2 FY26 call, Q&A with Rudraksh Raheja) — i.e. Apcotex itself is not a lone expander in its own specialty-latex segment. Taken together with DDev's own aggressive multi-line capex (PVC/HFFR/XLPE/BESS), the cross-sector pattern is: mid-cap Indian specialty-polymer manufacturers broadly are in a capacity-build phase in FY26-27, funded conservatively (internal accruals plus limited debt, explicit ROCE thresholds of 20-25% cited by Apcotex management), targeting similar FY27-28 commissioning windows. This is a supportive macro backdrop for DDev's capital discipline framing, though it says nothing about whether DDev's specific end-market demand will absorb the new capacity.

**2D Competitive mentions.** None. DDev, DDev Plastiks, or any recognizable description of it (cable compound, XLPE, HFFR compounder) does not appear anywhere across the four Apcotex transcripts. This absence is unsurprising given the zero product overlap (2D0 confirmed rather than informative).

**2E Risks peers discuss that DDev's own materials may not emphasize equally.**
- Trade-remedy execution risk: Apcotex's anti-dumping duty case against NBR imports was recommended by DGTR but never notified by the Finance Ministry across three consecutive quarters ("about 80% to 90% of the cases in the last three months have not been notified yet," APCOTEXIND, Q3 FY26 call, Q&A with Aditya Khetan), illustrating that Indian trade-policy support for domestic specialty-polymer capacity cannot be assumed even when technically recommended — a generic India-regulatory-lag risk.
- Freight/logistics fragility on export corridors: repeated commentary on elevated Suez Canal and (later) Strait of Hormuz-linked freight costs disrupting specific export geographies (Turkey, Egypt) even absent direct war exposure (APCOTEXIND, Q1 FY26 and Q4 FY26 calls) — relevant given DDev's own export growth ambitions (FY26 exports +30% revenue per operator context).
- Overcapacity-driven margin destruction as a multi-year hangover from post-COVID capacity additions, specifically in nitrile latex, where margins took three-plus years to recover ("it has already taken three years, so we have been very patient," APCOTEXIND, Q4 FY26 call, Q&A with Aditya Khetan) — a cautionary industry pattern relevant to any new capacity DDev brings on (HFFR, XLPE, BESS) entering markets without an established order book.
- Customer price-elasticity/squeeze risk downstream of RM inflation: Apcotex flags that its own automotive-linked customers were "running the plant at losses for one or two months" rather than accept full RM pass-through (APCOTEXIND, Q4 FY26 call, Q&A with Rudraksh Raheja) — a reminder that DDev's own cost-plus pass-through assumption depends on customer tolerance, which is not unconditional even in adjacent polymer-consuming industries.

## PART 4: TRIANGULATION SUMMARY

- Claims verified (full VERIFIED bar of two independent peers): **0 of 6** — structurally impossible with only one substantive peer in the set, regardless of evidence quality.
- Claims partially verified: **2 of 6** (Q2 — RM shock magnitude/timing corroborated but normalization timing contradicted; Q6 — peer-fit assessment itself, meaningful only for macro items).
- Claims contradicted: **0 of 6** as a full CONTRADICTED verdict, though Q2 contains a genuine sub-claim contradiction (the "normalized by April" element) that should be treated as a priority item for synthesis even though the overall Q2 verdict is PARTIALLY VERIFIED rather than CONTRADICTED, because the core spike claim (existence, rough magnitude, timing) is well supported.
- Claims unverifiable: **4 of 6** (Q1, Q3, Q4, Q5), all for genuine peer-fit reasons stated per-item above, not evidence of DDev being wrong.

**Single most consequential item:** the "normalized by April 2026" component of Q2. Apcotex's own 7-May-2026 call states the Strait of Hormuz was still closed as of the call date, raw-material coverage was being managed on a week-to-week basis only through end-June, and competitive distortion from Chinese material displacing Apcotex in Turkey/Egypt was actively occurring at the time of the call — none of which reads as "normalized by April." This does not prove DDev's own RM position failed to normalize (different feedstocks, different geography mix), but it is independent evidence that the broader regional disruption Apcotex attributes to the same event was not fully resolved on the timeline DDev states, and synthesis should flag this for direct cross-check against DDev's own Q4 FY26/Q1 FY27 primaries rather than accept the "normalized by April" framing on DDev's word alone.

**Single strongest independent confirmation:** the existence, approximate >50% order-of-magnitude, and near-identical start date (Apcotex: "March 1st"; DDev: 28-Feb-2026) of the raw-material price shock tied to the West Asia/Israel-Iran conflict and Strait of Hormuz closure. An independently reporting Indian crude-linked specialty-polymer manufacturer, with no incentive to corroborate DDev's narrative, describes the same macro event in the same window with comparable magnitude (crude up ~70% in a couple of months). This is genuine, high-value independent evidence for the existence and severity of the shock, separate from the normalization-timing question above.

**Overall assessment:** the peer set **complicates more than it confirms**. It offers real, useful, symmetric evidence on exactly one macro item (the RM shock) — confirming the shock's existence and rough scale while complicating the recovery timeline — and is honestly silent, not falsely silent, on every product-specific claim (cable & wire growth rate, DDev's own market shares, competitor product-line entry, and the Adani/UltraTech sourcing claim) because the assigned peer genuinely does not operate in DDev's markets. The absence of any DDev mention across four Apcotex transcripts is also informative in a narrow sense: it confirms Apcotex, at least, sees no reason to discuss DDev as a competitor or reference point, consistent with the products not overlapping, though this should not be read as evidence about DDev's competitive standing one way or the other.
