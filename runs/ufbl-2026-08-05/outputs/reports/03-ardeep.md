### 6E. Quiet Abandonment Check (mandatory)

Reading the Chairman's letter and MD&A opening (pages 9-19) side-by-side with the operational/notes sections (Phases 1-3, subsidiary AOC-1 data, Note 7/10A):

**Instance 1 — "Subsidiaries continued to perform well" vs Red Apple's actual swing to loss and the CGU assumption changes.**
- Opening claim: Board's Report §2 (p.20) states "The subsidiaries of the Company continued to perform well, contributing 21% to the consolidated revenue," and separately, "The Indian Subsidiaries, which operates premium brands i.e., Toscano and Salt, collectively recorded revenue of Rs.160 crores... - Operational section where this should have shown up but did not: Note 7/10A's CGU impairment-testing assumptions (discount rate cut, new 12x EBITDA terminal floor) are disclosed only in the technical notes, with no cross-reference back to the front-matter subsidiary narrative; AOC-1 (Annexure-4, p.34) shows Red Apple's own Profit/(Loss) before taxation figures are not narratively contextualised anywhere in the MD&A/Board's Report opening sections despite being disclosed in the annexure.
- Classification: **(b) silent drop** — the opening narrative addresses subsidiary revenue and ROM (a favourable framing) but never addresses subsidiary-level profit trajectory or the fact that the impairment-test assumptions for the same subsidiaries moved in a direction that would only be necessary/relevant if performance had weakened.
- Materiality: **Moderate-to-high for the thesis.** This does not change the overall transition read (Phase 3's finding that the loss is primarily depreciation/lease-finance-driven rather than operating-margin-driven still holds), but it does mean the front-matter "subsidiaries performing well" claim cannot be taken as confirmation that subsidiary-level risk (Phase 2 Ranks 2, 5, 6, 7) has abated — the front matter is silent on precisely the subsidiaries where Phase 2's most serious findings concentrate (MENA, Red Apple).

**Instance 2 — Chairman's letter Premium CDR ROM ("over 20%") vs MD&A's own Premium CDR ROM ("around 18%").**
- Classification: **(a) implicit retraction** — not an omission but a direct numeric inconsistency between two sections of the same document, both purporting to describe the same metric for the same segment/year, with the more prominent (Chairman's letter) version being more favourable than the more granular (MD&A) version.
- Materiality: **Low.** A 2-point margin difference on a single segment does not change the overall thesis; flagged as a data-quality/drafting-consistency issue consistent with the broader "note-drafting quality-control gap" pattern already identified in Phase 2 (B02 Ranks 3, 12), now shown to extend beyond the technical notes into the narrative front matter as well.

**Instance 3 — "Tracking Our Performance" curated metric set (6C) as an implicit hedged retreat on the loss/cash narrative.**
- Classification: **(c) hedged retreat** — the front matter does not deny the existence of losses or cash deterioration (both are disclosed fully and accurately in the financial statements later in the document), but the company's own chosen five-metric performance dashboard curates toward the metrics that read best, functioning as a soft framing device rather than a factual misstatement.
- Materiality: **Low-to-moderate** — standard practice among listed companies to lead with favourable metrics in front-matter infographics; flagged for completeness per the 6E mandate but not treated as equivalent in severity to Instances 1 and 2.

No further quiet abandonments were identified beyond these three in the sections reviewed in this pass.
