## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

### 2A. Demand environment

The two operating peers split cleanly along end-market exposure, and the split itself
corroborates part of Goodluck's own excuse structure. HITECH (commodity/infra/construction/solar-facing)
shows consistently strong volume growth across all four analysed quarters: +21.66% revenue
(Q2 FY26), +40% YoY revenue with "highest ever quarterly performance" (Q3 FY26), +102% Q4 YoY
revenue (Q4 FY26), +79% YoY revenue (Q1 FY27), all driven by capacity ramp plus infrastructure,
construction and renewable-energy demand. RATNAMANI (higher-value/export/oil-and-gas-facing)
shows genuine demand softness across the same period: standalone Q4 FY26 revenue fell to
Rs 893 Cr from Rs 1,575 Cr a year earlier (a ~43% YoY decline), with management explicitly citing
"continued muted demand conditions and adverse geopolitical developments in the Middle East,
impacting order booking, project execution and overall market sentiment" (RATNAMANI-Concall_May_2026_Transcript.txt,
p.3). This is the SAME West Asia crisis Goodluck names as a headwind in its Q4 FY26 and Q1 FY27
calls, corroborated unprompted, independently, by a peer with no reason to support Goodluck's
narrative. This does not rescue Goodluck's overall D-grade credibility (the external-blame PATTERN
across guidance misses is separately and independently documented), but on this one specific
factual claim, the corroboration is real.

### 2B. Pricing and input costs

Corroborated on two fronts. First, HRC price direction and the 12% safeguard duty (see Q2 above)
match almost exactly, including the trigger date. Second, elevated gas costs: HITECH's Q1 FY27
call states "gas prices have more than doubled, which is impacting our total EBITDA" and cites
"the challenges arising from Iran war" (HITECH-Concall_Aug_2026_Transcript.txt, p.3-5); RATNAMANI's
Q4 FY26 call independently confirms "Increase has been substantial" on gas costs (RATNAMANI-Concall_May_2026_Transcript.txt,
p.19). Goodluck's own transcripts reference the West Asia crisis and a 2-3 month cost pass-through
lag (per B05 3B) but do not isolate gas costs as sharply as these two peers do; the peer set gives
independent, unprompted confirmation that the broader input-cost environment Goodluck describes
was industry-wide in the same period, not company-specific spin.

### 2C. Capex cycle

Industry-wide capacity race, not a lone expander. All three peers analysed are expanding
meaningfully in the same window Goodluck itself is expanding: HITECH is adding roughly 1 million
tons of capacity by FY29 (doubling its base), commissioning new plants nearly every quarter, funded
"entirely through internal accruals" by its own account, while also raising a promoter preferential
issue (~Rs 90 Cr) mid-cycle. RATNAMANI runs multiple concurrent expansions (Odisha, a Saudi JV,
RFSS spooling, RTL bearing rings) while remaining formally debt-free. BALUFORGE is mid-build on a
46-acre Hattargi facility lifting forging capacity to 150,000 MTPA and machining to 80,000 MTPA,
and its net cash position (Rs -603mn net debt, i.e., net cash, FY25) flipped to net debt of Rs 377mn
in FY26 as that capex ramped. This is a genuine capacity race across the whole small/mid-cap
steel-tube/forging peer set, which somewhat normalizes Goodluck's decision to expand at all; it does
NOT excuse the specific execution failures (the capex reconciliation gap, the multi-quarter timeline
slippage) that are Goodluck-specific, not industry-wide.

### 2D. Competitive mentions

No peer transcript names Goodluck India, GDAL, or an unmistakable description of either, in any of
the nine documents read. The silence is unremarkable given differing scale and segment overlap is
partial at best; it does not itself carry evidentiary weight in either direction.

### 2E. Risks peers discuss that Goodluck does not

- RATNAMANI flags a structural competitive/margin risk from "piercing technology" entrants into
  premium extruded stainless-steel tube segments, repeated across three of its four analysed calls,
  including a named product failure ("Recent fire or heat exchanger failure at one of the refineries
  in Rajasthan was a major failure") as a live quality/liability risk class (RATNAMANI-Concall_May_2026_Transcript.txt,
  p.12). Goodluck's calls never raise an analogous quality/liability risk category for its own products.
- HITECH names the EU's Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM) as an active regulatory/cost risk
  for exporters (HITECH-Concall_Feb_2026_Transcript.txt, p.11). Goodluck claims ~53% export revenue
  share (per B05 3D) but never discusses CBAM exposure in any of its four analysed calls.
- Both HITECH and RATNAMANI flag China+1/Europe+1 capacity-shift dynamics and new-entrant
  competitive intensity as live pressures on pricing and margin; Goodluck's calls frame competitive
  dynamics almost entirely through a generic "demand outstrips supply" lens (per B05 3A) without
  this structural competitive-intensity framing.
- RATNAMANI flags geography-specific duty/anti-dumping risk (Saudi stainless-steel localisation)
  as it builds international capacity; Goodluck discusses no analogous geography-specific regulatory
  risk for its own GDAL/aerospace international ambitions.

  incentive to support Goodluck's narrative, including RATNAMANI naming the identical geopolitical

  inflection point.
- Overall: the peer set is genuinely mixed on Goodluck's narrative, and symmetrically so. It
  strengthens two of the more consequential and previously ambiguous excuse claims (the HRC
  direction and the West Asia crisis), which B05 had flagged as internally contradictory or
  unresolved. It simultaneously undercuts the credibility of Goodluck's ORIGINAL capacity timeline

  GDAL growth case (global shell demand vs supply) genuinely unverifiable, exactly as management

  some ambiguity in Goodluck's favour (the external-blame excuses on price and geopolitics were, in
  fact, industry-wide conditions) while leaving the growth thesis's central unverified assumption
  exactly where it was.

## PART 5: CROSS-PEER HYPOTHESIS

Hypothesis: all three peers analysed are independently pivoting into defence/aerospace/nuclear-
adjacent verticals specifically because their core commodity or industrial segments face
structural margin compression from new-entrant capacity, and this looks like an industry-wide
flight-to-defence pattern rather than an idiosyncratic opportunity unique to Goodluck's GDAL thesis.

The evidence for each leg, none of which states this pattern explicitly on its own:
- RATNAMANI's management says outright, when asked about margin pressure from "piercing technology"
  entrants in stainless steel: "we continue to enter new segments like defence, which is increasing
  for us, aerospace, which is increasing for us. So, we will move up the ladder" (RATNAMANI-Concall_May_2026_Transcript.txt,
  p.12) - defence/aerospace named explicitly as the response to margin erosion in the base business.
- HITECH is not in defence, but shows the same underlying mechanic in miniature: aggressive,
  peer-wide capacity additions (2C) are compressing blended EBITDA/ton even as volumes grow, pushing
  it toward higher-value/export niches (CBAM-aware Europe strategy, API pipes for oil & gas) as its
  own margin-defence move.
- BALUFORGE, the peer furthest along this path, shows where it leads: its blended EBITDA margin
  already COMPRESSED from 27.2% (Q3 FY26) to 22.7% (Q4 FY26) and its ROCE fell from 30.1% (FY25) to
  21.7% (FY26) in the very same period its defence/aerospace/railway capacity (the highest-margin,
  most capital-intensive segment) scaled from 5% to 13% of revenue.

If this pattern holds, the testable implication for Goodluck's GDAL thesis is specific: as more
converters from adjacent industries (forging, tube-making, precision machining) pile into the same
national defence-capacity race that GDAL is riding, GDAL's own early-stage 38-70% defence margin
should be expected to compress toward the 22-27% blended range BALUFORGE already shows once GDAL's
capacity utilisation and the competitive field both mature, rather than holding at its current
ramp-up level. This is a testable prediction (watch GDAL's per-shell EBITDA trend as its own
400,000-shell capacity comes online against how many OTHER converters enter the same shell-supply
market over FY27-FY29), not a claim any single peer makes about itself or about Goodluck.
