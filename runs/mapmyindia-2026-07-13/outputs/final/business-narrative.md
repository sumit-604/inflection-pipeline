Strong mapping moat, stalled core, governance overhang

C.E. Info Systems, known as MapMyIndia, sells digital map data, location software, and IoT fleet telematics to automotive OEMs, enterprises, and government in India. Revenue was Rs474 Cr in FY26, up 2.3%, with map data and software licensing at 66% of the mix, IoT SaaS at 22%, and IoT hardware at 11%. PAT was Rs134 Cr, down about 9% even as revenue rose. The shares trade at Rs1,185 for a market cap of Rs6,488 Cr. Gate 0 scores the backward record at 72 out of 100, a GOOD+ with a STRONG moat, and the combined backward plus forward read lands at EXCELLENT+.

The transition is a shift away from selling map licences toward recurring IoT SaaS, government geospatial platforms, and deeper automotive attach rates. Management has set a Rs1,000 Cr revenue target for FY28, alongside 6 million integrated vehicles at a 25% attach rate and 500 enterprise clients. The emerging moat layer scores 30 and reads STRENGTHENING, anchored in signed contracts like the Rs110 Cr IOCL telematics deal and a compounding proprietary map database rather than narrative.

Map-led revenue, the highest margin core, fell 8.7% in FY26 to Rs316 Cr, and the 2.3% total growth was carried entirely by lower margin IoT. Verifiers found the core C&E and API business stuck near Rs80 to Rs85 Cr for three to four years, down from Rs100 to Rs120 Cr earlier, masked by IoT and government growth. Management did not dispute this when an analyst raised it directly.

Trade receivables rose 32% to Rs176 Cr on flat revenue, and working capital days climbed from 85 to 101. Cash from operations fell from Rs113 Cr to Rs93 Cr as PAT fell, leaving CFO at 0.77 times PAT. Other income ran above 20% of pre tax profit, driven by a Rs685 Cr treasury book rather than the operating business. The receivables deterioration sits almost entirely at the Gtropy hardware and IoT subsidiary, where the overdraft is fully utilised, while the standalone map and data core actually improved its bad debt charge.

Credibility grades C. The FY28 Rs1,000 Cr target implies roughly 45% annual growth from here against the 2.3% just delivered, and the timing language softened from firm to hedged across the FY26 calls. Management blamed government fiscal grant delays, state elections, and re-tenders in a pattern of external blame, and declined to quantify the deferred order size across two consecutive quarters when asked directly.

Governance flags at CONCERN. The founder attempted to transfer the consumer B2C business to his son through a 10% stake and a Rs35 Cr convertible debenture, reversed within about 24 hours of investor pushback, after InGovern rebuked the board publicly and the stock fell about 25%. Related party transactions with two promoter family directed entities, ClarityX and Zenithra, grew 638% to Rs6.1 Cr against Rs24 Cr of approved but mostly unused ceilings. The 74 year old sole managing director was reappointed to 2030 with no succession plan, and he sold Rs143 Cr of stock in a block deal. Against this, institutional ownership rose 1.89 points to 13.34%, the board reversed the self dealing, the auditor rotated, a new independent director joined, pledge stands at zero, and the standalone balance sheet is debt free.

Peers corroborate the excuse but not the symptom. Newgen and Nucleus, the closest government and BFSI facing analogues, both report the same FY26 large deal deferral pattern, which supports the demand narrative. Neither shows a receivables build like this one: Newgen DSO held near 123 to 125 days and Nucleus receivables declined, so the 32% rise is company specific, not industry wide. RateGain, the one peer with a multi year target, raised guidance and hit its goal a year early, the opposite of the MapMyIndia target timing hedge.

Market size is not the binding constraint, execution is. The addressable market runs Rs30,000 to Rs35,000 Cr with a serviceable Rs18,590 Cr, a MASSIVE runway against current revenue. The bottom up SOM implies roughly 16% revenue CAGR over three to five years, well below the 45% the FY28 target requires, and the management claim ratio of 1.56 reads inflated. The company holds about 2.5% of its serviceable market today, leaving room to grow into it if the core reaccelerates and government orders convert.
