## SECTION 1: MARKET DEFINITION

1A Boundaries.
- Product: forged and machined components (hot, warm, cold, ring rolled, aluminium forging), plus automotive castings (Investor_Presentation_1 slide 19; AR26 p.44).
- End markets served (standalone FY26, IP slide 12): domestic auto 47.0%, auto exports 26.7%, railways 7.5%, mining/earth moving/farm 5.9%, misc industrial 8.0%, non-auto exports 4.9%. Single reporting segment, no audited split (B04 input gap).
- Geography: India production (20 plants, 16 in Jamshedpur) and export sales, North America 58% and Europe 41% of FY26 exports (IP slide 13).
- Customer: OEMs and Tier-1s. Tata Motors 33.4% of consolidated revenue, 9MFY26 (IndRa, via B04 flags).
- Excluded: aftermarket, non-metal components, aerospace (zero order book, B05), and the rail wheel JV, which is not producing (B05).
- Market defined: India forging industry by value, with RKFL's export book sitting inside the same product class.

1B Management's own market claim.
- AR26 pp.35-36 cites Grand View Research: India metal forging market USD 11,346.9 million in 2025, CAGR 8.8% 2026-2033 to USD 22,515.7 million.
- No other TAM claim in AR26, IP or the calls. The only demand-size claims are the Rs 8,000 Cr revenue target by FY29 (22-25% CAGR, Q1 FY27 call, B05) and "Rs 9,000 Cr at 100% utilisation on a Rs 3,700 Cr net block" (Jul call, B05).
- The rail line: India needs about 2,00,000 forged wheels a year, 60-70% imported (AR26 p.41). No rupee value stated.
- Credibility read: BROAD. It is a third-party all-India, all-end-use figure with no RKFL serviceable carve-out. Management implies TAM = growth runway.

## SECTION 3: SAM AND SOM

3A SAM. The five filters need sourced percentages for end-use mix, export access and machined-share of the industry. None were found (all NOT FOUND). I do not apply guessed percentages. SAM is therefore set as the sourced floor: the FY26 revenue of the three named organised forgers that serve the same OEM and Tier-1 customers, INR 19,712 Cr. SAM as % of central TAM = 19,712 / 63,220 = 31.2% (37.2% of the conservative TAM). This is a lower bound; true SAM is larger once MM Forgings, CIE India and others are added, and smaller once Bharat Forge's overseas revenue is removed. The direction of those two errors is not known.

3B SOM.
- Current share of SAM: 4,238 / 19,712 = 21.5%.
- SAM growth carried: 8.8% (Grand View CAGR, AR26 p.35). It is a USD-terms forecast; INR fell 9.88% against USD in FY26 (web search, Outlook Business / Business Standard coverage), so INR growth could differ. Alternative reading: ICRA expects domestic CV volumes +4-6% FY27 and a 5-8% CAGR over 2026-30 (AR26 p.40). Both shown. The observation that separates them is the FY27 SIAM CV production growth against the 5-8% band.
- Share gain: 3 pp over 3 years (aggressive end of the rule, supported by capacity added: IP slide 10 and 6 Mar 2026 filing), 5 pp over 5 years. No competitor exit or acquisition is evidenced, so no more.
- 3-year (FY29): SAM = 19,712 x 1.088^3 = 19,712 x 1.28791 = 25,387. Share 24.5% -> SOM = 6,220. CAGR = (6,220 / 4,238)^(1/3) - 1 = 13.6%.
- 5-year (FY31): SAM = 19,712 x 1.088^5 = 19,712 x 1.5245 = 30,052. Share 26.5% -> SOM = 7,964. CAGR = (7,964 / 4,238)^(1/5) - 1 = 13.4%.
- Sensitivity at 6.5% SAM growth (CV mid-band): SAM FY29 = 23,810, SOM = 5,833, CAGR 11.2%.
- Cross-check to the run rate: H1 FY27 revenue 2,622 x 2 = 5,244 (+23.7% on FY26; H1 YoY +36.4%, IP slide 8). Carried at 8.8% for two more years: 5,244 x 1.088^2 = 6,208, within 0.2% of the 3-year SOM. Management's Rs 8,000 Cr by FY29 (23.6% CAGR) is 29% above the SOM. Two readings of H1: a recovery from a weak base (Q2 FY26 revenue 908) or a new level. The observation that separates them is Q3 and Q4 FY27 revenue at or above Q2's 1,405 Cr (B05 trigger 1).

3C Capacity cross-check (source used: filings and IP slide 10; B07 absent).
- Installed capacity 3,95,800 tonnes (IP slide 10): ring rolling 24,000, forgings 70,350, press 2,39,050, casting 62,400.
- Added in FY26 per footnotes: press 3,000 t (20 Jan 2026) + 40,000 t (6 Mar 2026) + 18,000 t at the casting subsidiary (31 Dec 2025) + casting 28,800 t (31 Mar 2026) = 89,800 t. That is 89,800 / 3,06,000 opening = 29.3% capacity growth. AR26 p.46 states only 43,000 t press and 28,800 t casting (71,800 t, 22.2%); the gap is the 18,000 t subsidiary line. Aluminium forging 3,000 t a year started 20 Jan 2026 (Reg 30 filing; capex INR 57.50 Cr). Cost of additions in the filings: 249.00 Cr (casting line), 80.00 Cr (40,000 t press), 57.50 Cr (aluminium).
- FY27 capex guide moved from INR 350 Cr to about INR 550 Cr (B05). H2 spend of about INR 160 Cr is machining (B05). No tonnage is attached, so no extra tonnes are credited.
- Revenue at full capacity: 3,95,800 t x 2.18 lakh = 8,628 Cr. At 80% (the management utilisation target): 6,903 Cr. Management's own "about Rs 9,000 Cr" matches full utilisation at a 2.5x asset turn, against an FY26 actual of 4,238 / 3,776 net block = 1.12x.
- SOM FY29 6,220 = 72% of full capacity and 90% of the 80% line: sufficient. SOM FY31 7,964 = 92% of full capacity; gap to the 80% line = 7,964 - 6,903 = INR 1,061 Cr, so more capex is needed before FY31.
- Management's FY29 Rs 8,000 Cr target equals 92.7% of installed capacity at today's price per tonne and exceeds the 80% line by INR 1,097 Cr. The optimistic side is the management target, not the SOM.
- Caveats: ring rolling output feeds forging, so the 395,800 t total may double count; H1 FY27 production was 137,819 t (IP slide 10) against 120,292 t sold. Casting realisation (Rs 1.33 lakh) is below forging, so a mix shift to casting lowers revenue per tonne; machining adds revenue without tonnes, which raises it.

## SECTION 5: SUMMARY AND RUNWAY

5A Funnel (INR Cr):
TAM realistic 63,220 (conservative 53,006; mgmt-cited 1,07,403) -> SAM floor 19,712 (31.2% of central TAM) -> SOM FY29 6,220 -> SOM FY31 7,964 -> FY26 revenue 4,238 (21.5% of SAM, 6.7% of TAM).

5B Runway.
- Headroom: SAM / revenue = 19,712 / 4,238 = 4.65x. TAM / revenue = 14.9x.
- TAM growth: 8.8% (USD forecast; cross-check 5-8% CV volume CAGR).
- Company vs market: FY26 revenue +5.0% (4,238 vs 4,034, IP slide 14) was below the 12.6% growth in domestic CV sales, so RKFL lost ground in FY26 (export fall 19.94%, AR26 p.45). H1 FY27 +36.4% is above 8.8%: gaining share, off a depressed base.
- Years to saturate the SAM floor at 13.6%: ln(19,712/4,238) / ln(1.136/1.088) = 35.6 years. The floor is not the binding limit; installed capacity is.

5C Class: GOOD. The 4.65x floor headroom and an 8.8% growing market support a long runway, but the end markets are cyclical and one customer is a third of revenue, and physical capacity binds at about 92% utilisation by FY31. The instruction file gives no numeric matrix; this is a judgement and is labelled so.

5D SAM expansion levers. Rail wheels (JV): addition NOT FOUND in rupees (no per-wheel price; 20,000-25,000 wheels FY27 guide). Passenger vehicle and EV: no revenue disclosed. Aerospace: zero order book. Aluminium: 3,000 t. None is quantified, so revised headroom stays 4.65x.

5E Output card.
- TAM central 63,220 / conservative 53,006; SAM floor 19,712; SOM FY29 6,220, FY31 7,964.
- SOM-implied revenue CAGR: 13.6% (3 years), 13.4% (5 years). Sensitivity 11.2% at 6.5% SAM growth.
- Valuation line: At 13.6% revenue CAGR implied by SOM, with an EBITDA margin trajectory held at the H1 FY27 level of 18.1% (IP slide 14), the EBITDA growth embedded here is 20.5% CAGR (FY26 643 Cr to 6,220 x 18.1% = 1,126 Cr; (1,126 / 643)^(1/3) - 1). PAT growth cannot be derived at this stage because depreciation (333 Cr FY26) and interest (210 Cr) paths are not set here; FY26 PAT is 72 Cr. Against the screener snapshot (price 785.6, market cap 14,581 Cr, screener-Data_Sheet.csv, snapshot date not stated) the trailing P/E is 14,581 / 72 = 203x. This does not support the current valuation on trailing earnings; whether forward EPS supports it is for stage 11.
- Handoff to stage 11: use 13.6% as the cross-check on the revenue basis. Management's 23.6% sits above it and needs 92.7% capacity utilisation to deliver.
