## Business Understanding Narrative

**What does it do, and how does it make money?** Garware makes polyester film and turns most of it into branded consumer films. It sells in 90+ countries, and exports were 80% of Q2 FY27 revenue (Oct call, page 5). Q2 FY27 revenue split 51% sun control film (SCF), 24% paint protection film (PPF) and 25% industrial polyester film (IPD) (Oct call, page 5). Value-added products were 87% of FY26 revenue against 48% in FY17 (CARE26, page 2). Margin comes from the consumer side. Top sun control products earn 40% to 45% EBITDA, PPF earns 20% to 25%, and IPD earns 10% to 12% (Oct call, pages 16 and 19).

**What is changing?** The commodity-to-specialty climb is already done. The current change is a second step: from B2B film supplier to an owned channel and owned inputs. A Rs 118 crore TPU line backward-integrates PPF (Q2, page 27). A Rs 191 crore SCF line adds about 1,200 lakh sq ft (AR26, page 50). Garware Home Solutions and application studios build a direct-to-consumer route (Q2, page 17).

**Why now?** FY26 was flat on US tariffs. Revenue grew 0.5% to Rs 2,120 crore (Q2, page 29). H1 FY27 broke that flat year. Revenue rose 25% to Rs 1,329 crore and PAT rose 50% to Rs 262 crore (Q2, page 16). Management raised FY27 EBITDA margin guidance by 200 bps to 27% plus or minus 2% (Q2, page 16).

**What must be true?** Three things. The architectural film push must keep pricing. SCF and PPF utilisation, 75% and 73% in Q2 (Oct call, page 9), must fill before the new line lands in June 2027. The TPU line must deliver the claimed 150 to 200 bps margin gain (Q2, page 29).

**What breaks it?** One customer, and the price already paid. Management confirmed a customer concentration "is definitely there" when asked about a reported 30% figure, and named a large US EV maker as an approving customer (Oct call, pages 7 and 8). The market capitalisation has also roughly doubled since 31 March 2026 (step 11).

## 2. Business model and archetype

One reported segment, "Polyester Films" (Q2, page 15). Management runs two divisions. The consumer products division (CPD) holds SCF, PPF, safety and PDLC films. It was about 71% of FY26 sales against 49% in FY23 (CARE26, page 2). IPD holds BOPET, shrink, thermal lamination and insulation films. Most IPD costs are passed through to customers (Oct call, page 16).

The engine is vertical integration under a brand. Garware makes its own base film, coatings and adhesives (AR26, page 49). It sells through 5,000+ tinters abroad, 850+ OEM dealerships and 250+ application studios in India (CARE26, page 2). In sun control and PPF, crude correlation is only 5% to 15% (Oct call, page 17).

**Archetype: two. Brand/franchise consumer for CPD. Commodity converter for the IPD slice, about 25% of revenue.** Section 1B v3.7 Amendment 17 binds the IPD slice. A later valuation must split them.

## 3. Competitive advantages

**Present, with one weak spot.** Pricing power is the clearest moat. In architectural film, Garware priced at 30% to 40% of the global leader. It now prices at 80% to 85%, and some products sit 10% to 20% above American peers (Oct call, page 11). India share is about 60% in PPF and 60% to 65% in SCF (Oct call, page 10). Cost advantage comes from integration from polyester chips to finished film (CARE26, page 2). Spec-in exists at OEMs, with four large domestic auto OEMs onboarded in FY26 (AR26, page 50). The weak spot is IP depth: about 168 trademarks and only about 10 patents (AR26, page 49). The brand and channel do the work, not patents. Anti-dumping protection against Chinese PPF was approved by Commerce but not by Finance (Oct call, page 10).

## 4. The promoters

The Garware family. Dr S B Garware is Chairman and Managing Director. Monika Garware is Vice Chairperson and Joint MD. Sarita Garware Ramsay is Joint MD. Sonia Garware is a Director (AR26, page 194). The board has 11 directors, 4 of them promoters and 6 independent (AR26, page 54).

Promoter holding at 31 March 2026 sums to 60.72% across 12 holders, unchanged from a year earlier. The S B Garware Family Trust holds 38.17% (AR26, page 183; the sum is computed). Pledge: NOT FOUND. Looked for in AR26 (only subsidiary-share and property pledges appear) and CARE26. No separate shareholding pattern is held.

Related-party dependence is real and recurring. Garware Industriees Private Limited, a promoter entity, received Rs 56.22 crore of processing charges in FY26 against Rs 54.02 crore in FY25 (AR26, page 195). Key management compensation was Rs 57.78 crore (AR26, page 195), 17% of FY26 PAT of Rs 338.23 crore. Skin in the game is a large, long-held stake with no change. CARE credits "experienced promoters" and an active next generation (CARE26, page 2).

## 6. The transition and the quality ladder

The decade climb from R1 commodity polyester to R3 spec'd specialty film is done. VAP went from 48% to 87% (CARE26, page 2). The current claim is **FROM R3 value-added supplier TO R4 franchise / share-of-wallet leader** for CPD. IPD stays at R1.

The mechanism is threefold. First, an owned channel: 14 home studios now, 50 by December 2027 (Q2, page 17), with D2C adding 10% to 15% margin on top (Oct call, page 19). Second, owned inputs: the TPU line for PPF. Third, a new end market in architectural film, now about Rs 400 crore annualised, and management sees Rs 1,000 crore (Oct call, pages 6 and 7). ROCE already sits at 23% to 27% and touched 32% in H1. That is R3 to R4 territory.

Management's claim: "We are moving up the value chain through specialized applications, product development, stronger brands, and deeper customer relationships" (Oct call, page 4). It is a one-rung claim, within the base rate.

## 8. Proof check

The triggers claim mix-led margin growth on rising volume. Both quarters move that way. Q1 FY27 margin was 30.3% against 24.8% in Q1 FY26. Q2 FY27 was 27.2% against 23.4% (Q2, page 26). Q2 absorbed Rs 37 crore of raw-material and logistics headwind (Oct call, page 9).

**The proof point is present: two consecutive record quarters, each above its prior-year twin.** The cleaner test is still ahead. Q3 is the seasonal low, and Q3 FY26 earned only 18.9%. Q3 FY27 also carries the Rs 16 crore tariff refund, which flatters the comparison.

## 9. Flags from the documents

- **Customer concentration.** Management confirmed it, did not deny a reported 30% figure, and said several other customers are half or a third that size (Oct call, pages 7 and 8). The annual report does not name or size the customer. Top 10 dealers took 46% of dealer sales in FY26 against 52% in FY25 (AR26, page 84).
- **Related party.** Rs 56.22 crore of processing charges went to a promoter entity (AR26, page 195). The nature of the processing is not explained.
- **Pay.** KMP compensation of Rs 57.78 crore is 17% of PAT (AR26, page 195).
- **Auditor.** The Key Audit Matter is contingent liabilities from disputes with regulators (AR26, page 156). No emphasis of matter was seen. Amounts not read.
- **Regulatory.** MPCB penalty of Rs 25 lakh for running an old boiler during modification (Oct call, pages 17 and 18). Small, but on record.
- **One-off income.** Tariff refunds: Rs 11 crore booked to date, Rs 16 crore expected in Q3 (Oct call, page 10).
- **Single segment.** Only "Polyester Films" is reported (Q2, page 15), so the CPD and IPD margins come only from the call.
- **Dilution:** none. Share capital is unchanged at Rs 23.23 crore (Q2, page 12).

## 10. Independent check: the credit rating

CARE upgraded the long-term rating to **CARE AA; Stable** from AA- on 7 July 2026 and reaffirmed A1+ (CARE26, page 1). The rating cites three things: a net debt-free balance sheet, rising value-added share, and healthy margins despite US tariffs. FY26 total debt was Rs 28.29 crore, mostly leases and acceptances, with gearing at 0.01x (CARE26, page 2). Constraints are crude-linked inputs, forex on 74% exports, and geography-specific regulation in SCF (CARE26, page 1). The downgrade trigger is a margin below 15% sustained, far from today's level. The agency backs the transition. Rating history shows CARE A+ in 2023, AA- in 2024 and 2025, and AA now (CARE26, page 5). The agency does not address customer concentration.

## 11. Posture: the Transition Decision Matrix

- **Proof gate: FIRED.** CPD rose from 49% to 71% of sales (CARE26, page 2). Two record quarters followed a tariff year. Guidance was raised, not just met.
- **Ugliness: ARTIFACT-OF-CLIMB.** The ugly optic is FY26's flat revenue and Q3 FY26's 12.2% PAT margin. Both trace to US tariffs, and refunds are now flowing back. The related-party processing charge is a governance question, not a decay signal.
- **Recognition gap: CLOSED.** Market capitalisation was Rs 7,860.10 crore at 31 March 2026 (AR26, page 51). The Bull AI company record gives Rs 16,170.8 crore on 2026-11-04 (service record, not a filed document). That is 2.06 times in seven months. On TTM PAT of Rs 426.07 crore (H1 FY27 plus H2 FY26, computed from Q2, page 12), the trailing PE is 38.0x. The R4 neighbourhood is about 21x. The TO rung is priced and more.

**Posture: EARNINGS-ONLY.** The climb is real and already paid for. Returns from here ride EPS growth alone.

## 12. Verdict card

**WATCH.**

This is a high-quality business with a proven climb, a debt-free balance sheet and an agency upgrade. It is not a recognition-gap name. The market doubled the price during the same seven months that the proof printed. A `/step1` run earns its cost only if a second growth leg, such as TPU, architectural film or the new SCF line, can carry EPS growth well above 30% into FY28 and FY29 without help from re-rating.

**Load-bearing facts a `/step1` must verify first**
1. Who the concentrated customer is, its share of revenue, and the term of the agreement. Management confirmed a concentration and named a US EV maker as an approving customer (Oct call, pages 7 and 8).
2. Q3 FY27 margin excluding the Rs 16 crore tariff refund, against 18.9% in Q3 FY26.
3. What Garware Industriees processes for Rs 56 crore a year, and on what terms.
4. TPU commissioning date and the first PPF margin read after it.

**What would change the view**
- A price fall that reopens the gap against FY28 delivery moves this to PROCEED.
- A Q3 FY27 margin near 19%, after the refund is removed, says H1 was seasonal plus tariff catch-up.
- A disclosure that the largest customer is above 30% of revenue, or that it is renegotiating.

No Role 1 valuation was run and no target price is set. This verdict decides only whether the name earns a full `/step1`. It is not a position.
