## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

**2A. Demand environment.** The peer set shows a consistent bifurcation rather
than a single industry-wide story: domestic/government-linked demand is
resilient-to-strong (PROTEAN's Aadhaar Seva Kendra, CERSAI and Bima Sugam
mandates; QUICKHEAL's government segment "robust momentum" in two of four
quarters), while large-ticket India/Middle East enterprise deal closures are
repeatedly delayed across the window, attributed variously to "AI-led
uncertainty" causing customer deferral (NEWGEN, named explicitly in the
Q3FY26 call as a driver distinct from budget: "customers are also having a
re-evaluation... slightly unsure about taking large order bets," Jan-2026,
p.11) and to the Middle East war (NEWGEN Q4FY26 p.8, PROTEAN Q1FY27 p.11).
This partially corroborates eMudhra's own Middle East war-driven order
timing disruption (per B05) as a shared, not eMudhra-specific, phenomenon in
the same quarter, and adds the AI-driven-deferral dynamic as a distinct
industry-wide demand risk that eMudhra's own concalls (per B05) never raise.

**2B. Pricing and input costs.** Two peers independently and specifically
flag hardware/technology procurement cost inflation in 2026: QUICKHEAL
quantifies it precisely — "the IT hardware market witnessed significant
price inflation of up to 400% during 2026 compared to 2025" (QUICKHEAL
Q4FY26, May-2026 call, p.4), which it says pulled its channel partners'
investment focus toward hardware and away from software, hurting its
consumer-segment collections. PROTEAN separately attributes part of its
Q1FY27 margin miss to "higher procurement costs for technology, hardware,
white goods" from geopolitical tensions (Aug-2026 call, p.4). Neither
directly maps to eMudhra's software/SaaS cost structure, but this raises a
plausible, testable link to eMudhra's own unresolved item: the "stock-in-
trade/distributor cost drag (~Rs 3 Cr/quarter, flagged Q3 FY26 [and] never
revisited or confirmed resolved" (per B05) could be the same industry-wide
hardware/component inflation these two peers name, rather than an
eMudhra-specific execution issue — a hypothesis eMudhra's own concalls never
test because no analyst asked the question there.

**2C. Capex cycle.** Not a uniform industry-wide capacity race, and not a
lone-expander situation either — it is a two-cluster split. PROTEAN is the
clear capex/build-out expander in this peer set: rolling out 190 Aadhaar
Seva Kendras nationally, running CERSAI and Bima Sugam turnkey builds, and
taking a strategic equity stake in NSDL Payments Bank, all while a new CEO
(Ajay Rajan, from June-2026) explicitly signals capital-allocation review
and a pivot from "volume" to "solution" selling. eMudhra's own FY26 asset
build (fixed assets Rs 419 Cr to Rs 727 Cr per B00/company memory, plus its
UAE data-centre spend) puts it in the same expanding cluster as PROTEAN.
Against that, NEWGEN is actively shrinking headcount (roughly 6-7% YoY
reduction across FY26/Q1FY27) and substituting AI-led automation for
capacity growth rather than building. QUICKHEAL sustains R&D spend
(~Rs 30-35 Cr/quarter) through losses but has committed no major capex,
sitting on Rs 224-249 Cr cash while weighing (undisclosed) M&A. Read: the
identity/DPI-infrastructure names (PROTEAN, eMudhra) are building physical
and regulatory infrastructure; the horizontal enterprise-software name
(NEWGEN) is optimising cost instead of expanding capacity in the same
window.

**2D. Competitive mentions.** No peer transcript names eMudhra or gives an
unmistakable description of it. QUICKHEAL names its own competitive set
explicitly (Norton, McAfee, SentinelOne, Sophos, Trend Micro, CrowdStrike)
— none of which is eMudhra, consistent with QuickHeal operating in
EDR/XDR/consumer AV, a genuinely different segment from PKI/CA. This is a
finding by absence: none of the three peers treats eMudhra as a competitor
worth naming on an earnings call, which is unsurprising given the weak
structural overlap (see run's peer-selection rationale) but is worth
recording as zero positive or negative peer-mention evidence either way.

**2E. Risks peers discuss that eMudhra's own concalls do not (per B05).**
- **Leadership/C-suite transition risk.** All three peers underwent a CEO
  change within roughly the same nine-month window: NEWGEN's Virender Jeet
  stepped down effective 1-Aug-2026 with Tarun Nandwani promoted (NEWGEN
  Q1FY27 call, p.2); PROTEAN's Suresh Sethi departed with V. Easwaran
  interim CEO through Q4FY26 before Ajay Rajan joined 1-Jun-2026 (PROTEAN
  Q4FY26 and Q1FY27 calls); QUICKHEAL disclosed an active CEO search as of
  the Aug-2025 call with the promoter (Kailash Katkar) as interim MD, still
  unresolved by the May-2026 call. eMudhra's own three transcripts (per B05)
  raise no leadership-transition risk at all — worth flagging as a sector
  pattern eMudhra investors should watch for, even absent any current
  eMudhra-specific signal.
- **AI-driven deal-deferral risk.** NEWGEN names this explicitly and
  repeatedly across Q3FY26 and Q4FY26 as a distinct cause of large-deal
  delay, separate from budget or macro conditions — customers "re-evaluating"
  whether to commit given AI uncertainty. eMudhra's concalls (per B05) never
  raise this as a demand-side risk to its own enterprise pipeline.
- **Regulator-driven pricing reset.** PROTEAN's CRA/pension business had its
  charge structure reset by the regulator (PFRDA) toward an AUM-linked model,
  causing an explicitly flagged short-term margin dip (PROTEAN Q3FY26 call).
  Not directly analogous to eMudhra (no comparable regulated-pricing line
  disclosed for eMudhra), but shows regulator-driven repricing is a live risk
  type in this adjacent government-linked-services space.

## PART 5: CROSS-PEER HYPOTHESIS

Across all three peers, a rising "higher-quality" revenue-mix share has
coincided with margin *deterioration or flatness during the investment
phase*, not with mechanical margin expansion — and each peer hit this
air-pocket at a different point in its own mix-shift curve. QUICKHEAL's
enterprise share crossed roughly 50% of revenue in FY26 while EBITDA went
negative (Rs -29 Cr) for the full year. PROTEAN's new-business share reached
17% of revenue in Q1FY27 while EBITDA margin cratered from 18.7% to 10% on
upfront implementation costs. NEWGEN is the only one of the three that held
margin through its subscription/mature-market mix shift, and management
attributes that explicitly to AI-led headcount reduction and cost
optimisation running in parallel — not to the mix shift itself paying for
itself.

The testable hypothesis this suggests, not stated by any individual peer:
**in this Indian identity/enterprise-software niche, revenue-mix shift
toward the "should be higher margin" line is necessary but not sufficient
for margin/ROE expansion — the expansion requires a separate, distinct
catalyst (an explicit cost-optimisation programme, or the mix-shift business
crossing a scale/utilisation threshold), and companies without that second
catalyst will show flat-to-declining profitability precisely during the
quarters when the mix-shift narrative is loudest.** This is directly
testable against eMudhra: if its Enterprise Solutions mix keeps rising
through FY27 without ROE moving, the pattern predicts ROE stays flat until
eMudhra names (or the corpus reveals) a distinct cost-optimisation or scale
catalyst — consistent with management's own answer when asked directly
about ROE on the Q1FY27 call (per B05: management "frames this as close to
a ceiling without a structural improvement path"). If ROE instead moves
without any such named catalyst, the hypothesis would be falsified and mix
shift alone would need to be credited.
