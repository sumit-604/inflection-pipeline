### Note 15 — Trade Receivables (Note 15, p.~93–95)
🔴 Red Flag — the single most important finding in the standalone notes, AND a notes-vs-financial-statements
inconsistency.
- Net trade receivables: ₹90.11L (1 Apr 2023) → ₹1,359.21L (31 Mar 2024) → ₹2,367.66L (31 Mar 2025).
  A 26x increase from FY23 to FY25 opening-to-closing, and a 74.2% YoY increase in FY25 alone, against 28.4%
  revenue growth — receivables growing 2.6x faster than revenue.
- Analytical Ratios (Note 48, standalone) confirm the deterioration explicitly in management's own remarks:
  "The Trade Receivables Turnover Ratio decreased from 6.34 in FY 2023-24 to 3.17 in FY 2024-25, mainly due
  to a substantial increase of approximately 157% in average trade receivables." (Note 48, p.~109) —
  receivable collection has roughly HALVED in speed year-on-year.
- ECL provision newly recognised in FY25: ₹9.83L (nil FY24) — first ever ECL provision, consistent with
  ageing lengthening (Note 33: "Expected Credit Loss" P&L charge ₹9.83L, new line item FY25).
- Ageing (FY25, standalone, Note 15): <6 months ₹1,041.84L; 6mo–1yr ₹844.18L; 1–2yr ₹491.46L; >2yr nil.
  6-months-plus receivables = ₹1,335.64L of ₹2,377.49L gross = 56.2% of total receivables are OVER SIX MONTHS
  OLD for a business that (per Note 4.17) recognises revenue on bundled treatment packages, i.e. this is not
  a slow-pay B2B receivable book but patient/individual receivables (Note 45 confirms "customers are
  individuals from whom payment is received as the services are provided") — 56% of the book aged >6 months
  for individual-patient receivables in a cash-pay/insurance-pay healthcare model is a significant collection
  quality signal.
- 🔴 NOTES-VS-FINANCIAL-STATEMENTS MISMATCH: The FY24 comparative ageing table in standalone Note 15 shows
  "Less than 6 months ₹1,677.76L + 6 months–1 year ₹310.76L = Total ₹1,988.52L" — but the standalone Balance
  Sheet (and Note 15's own summary table one page earlier) states FY24 Trade Receivables (net) = ₹1,359.21L.
  The ageing schedule total (₹1,988.52L) does not reconcile to the reported balance sheet figure (₹1,359.21L)
  — a difference of ₹629.31L. By contrast, the CONSOLIDATED Note 15 FY24 ageing table ("Less than 6 months
  ₹1,048.46L + 6 months–1 year ₹310.76L = Total ₹1,359.22L") DOES reconcile to the consolidated balance sheet
  FY24 trade receivables of ₹1,359.22L — and since the subsidiary was immaterial in FY24, standalone and
  consolidated FY24 receivables should be near-identical (₹1,359.21L vs ₹1,359.22L, which they are, at the
  TOTAL level). This strongly suggests the STANDALONE Note 15 "less than 6 months" FY24 figure of ₹1,677.76L
  is a drafting/typographical error (it appears to be transposed or duplicated from elsewhere) and the correct
  figure should mirror the consolidated breakdown (₹1,048.46L). This is a genuine internal inconsistency
  within the audited notes that the statutory auditor (SKGN & Associates LLP) did not appear to catch or
  reconcile — worth a specific question to management/auditors given IPO due diligence context.
- 🟡 Watch: Contract-liability/customer-advance disclosure is essentially absent. Standalone "Other Financial
  Liabilities" (Note 23) shows "Advance from customer" of only ₹0.48L at FY25 (₹0.16L FY23, nil FY24) — an
  immaterial figure for a company running "bundled packages" that would normally be paid for materially
  upfront in the IVF industry. The near-total absence of customer advances/contract liabilities, combined
  with the ballooning receivables and slowing turnover, is consistent with revenue being recognised well
  ahead of cash collection rather than the reverse (prepayment) pattern typical of bundled-package fertility
  clinics. This is the primary driver behind flagging cash conversion below.
