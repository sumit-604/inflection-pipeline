## 1. NARRATIVE

1. Aye Finance lends small business loans to tiny firms that keep no formal accounts. The average loan is about Rs1.6 lakh.
2. Most loans are unsecured or only partly secured. About 23% are loans against property.
3. This quarter, profit after tax was Rs86.72 cr. That is 2.5 times last year and 16% above Q1 (R:R180).
4. The bad-loan ratio (GNPA, loans more than 90 days late) fell to 4.20% from 4.49% (R:R402).
5. The profit looks better on the surface than underneath. Two items explain more than the whole rise from Q1.
6. First, the company sold a pool of loans for a Rs10.12 cr gain. Q1 had no such sale (R:R084).
7. Second, the reserve held against bad loans fell by Rs13 cr while the loan book grew (P:L663, P:L667-668).
8. Write-offs rose. That partly explains why the bad-loan ratio fell. For the third time, the company did not state how many loans newly went bad.
9. A lender covenant on late loans plus write-offs broke for a second quarter. The lender waived it. The new auditor now names it in its certificate (A3-03).
10. Management says collections are improving. But early-stage collections fell again, and management could not name a cause (C:L496).
11. Management now expects credit cost (loan losses as a share of assets) near 3.5% this year. That needs the second half near 3.05%, against 3.82% in Q2.
12. Watch next quarter: credit cost at or below 3.3% with reserves held. Above 3.82%, or thinner reserves, is the bear signal.

Position stays HOLD 4% at Rs132. Decision Status stays HELD. No exit trigger fired. The add-gate fails.
