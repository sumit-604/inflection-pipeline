**Cross-cutting calls**

5. Cash conversion, structural vs growth-induced: **INDETERMINATE**. Capex is near zero (capex to depreciation about 0.06 to 0.07 times in FY25), so the cash drain is a working-capital build, not growth investment, which blocks a clean growth-induced reading. The build is dominated by related-party receivables that are excluded from the bank's drawing power, so it cannot be assumed to unwind the way arm's-length trade receivables would, which points toward structural. But FY26 consolidated operating cash flow turned positive for the first time (plus Rs 16.25 cr against minus Rs 18.53 cr in FY25), so structural is not proven either. One positive consolidated year, sitting next to a standalone figure that worsened in the same year with no explanation, does not settle it. INDETERMINATE never resolves to a clean pass; it caps the disposition and names the missing evidence. Confidence: fairly sure the label is INDETERMINATE, genuinely uncertain on which way it resolves. Disproof either way: FY27 standalone CFO turning clearly positive alongside consolidated resolves it toward growth-induced; a second negative standalone year with consolidated also reverting resolves it toward structural.

6. ROCE, TEMPORARILY DEPRESSED vs DECLINING: neither applies. ROCE is not depressed. It rose from 8.26% in FY24 to 24.41% in FY25 to 28.62% in FY26 (operating EBIT excluding other income, capital employed net of current liabilities, anchored to the AR and results filings). The live question is sustain versus erode, and I rule it sustains (see ruling 10). Confidence: sure that it is not depressed. Disproof: the FY24 to FY26 ROCE series is misstated.

7. Revenue forward verdict: **FIRING (+2)**. The shift from subsidiary job-work to direct exports is documented and already delivering: FY25 revenue up 57%, FY26 up 27%, all four FY26 quarters positive year on year, exports 53.70% of FY25 revenue, and 6,000 MTPA of new capacity live from 1 April 2025. Confidence: fairly sure. Disproof: two consecutive quarters of negative year-on-year revenue, or export share falling back toward the job-work base.

8. Margin forward verdict: **FIRING (+2)**. Consolidated EBITDA margin rose from 5.05% in FY23 to 5.66% in FY24 to 7.60% in FY25 to about 9.55% in FY26, above the 7% to 9% band the rating agency projected, with an operating-leverage runway as utilisation rises from 31%. Confidence: fairly sure; genuinely uncertain whether it expands further or just holds, and I scored the firing state on the delivered expansion. Disproof: EBITDA margin falling below 7% for two consecutive quarters under raw-material or export-price pressure.

9. Cash conversion forward verdict: **STAGNANT (0)**, catalyst strength WEAK. FY26 consolidated CFO turning positive keeps it out of DECLINING, but there is no documented working-capital catalyst (no factoring facility, no debtor-day target hit, no payment-term renegotiation), debtor days are not compressing (about 70 to 78 days), and the fresh Rs 33.2 cr interest-free related-party loan runs the wrong way, so it does not reach STARTING. Confidence: genuinely uncertain between STAGNANT and DECLINING; the numbers used STAGNANT because the consolidated line genuinely turned positive. Disproof toward DECLINING: FY27 standalone CFO negative again with consolidated also reverting.

10. ROCE forward verdict: **FIRING (+2)**, quality-flagged. ROCE at 28.62% is high, it expanded for two straight years, and management guides no major capex in FY27 to FY28, so there is no fresh capital bloat to depress it and real utilisation headroom (31% today) to lift it. The flag: this ROCE is computed on a capital base netted of current liabilities, many of which are related-party payables, so the denominator is flattered by the same related-party structure that makes cash INDETERMINATE. Confidence: genuinely uncertain. The two readings: FIRING on the reported, source-anchored 28.62% with no capex drag; or STAGNANT if you discount the related-party-flattered denominator and treat the 31% utilisation ramp as unproven, which would drop the composite to +4. The numbers used FIRING, because the cash-quality defect is already scored once in the cash transition and double-penalising the same defect through ROCE is barred. Disproof: an RP-adjusted capital base (adding related-party payables back) pulling ROCE below 20%, or utilisation stalling at 31%.

11. Composite and position: mechanical composite **+6 out of 8**, which is the BUY-candidate band. The Kernex cap does not engage (no transition is DECLINING with catalyst NONE). The TRIM rule does not engage (not all four fire backward). But INDETERMINATE cash conversion caps the disposition: this cannot be a clean BUY-candidate. The practical position is **DEEP WATCH leaning BUY-ON-DIPS**, entirely conditional on cash confirming, and the entry work is Phase 3's under the corrected 22x cap. Confidence: fairly sure on the mechanical +6, fairly sure the cap holds it below a clean buy. Disproof: standalone CFO turning durably positive would release the cap toward the BUY-candidate the raw score implies; a second negative standalone year would drop it toward AVOID.

## THE CATALYST STORY IN PLAIN WORDS

Revenue and margin have a clear engine and it is documented, not promised. The company took a distressed plant out of insolvency, added 6,000 MTPA of FIBC capacity that went live on 1 April 2025, and switched from making bags on job-work for its own group to selling them directly for export. Exports were already 53.70% of FY25 revenue. That switch lifts both the topline and the margin, because direct FIBC sales carry a better price than commodity job-work. What confirms it firing is the next few prints holding double-digit revenue growth with margin at or above 9%. What kills it is export orders drying up or the margin falling under 7% when raw-material prices spike, which peers say happens with a 30 to 45 day lag.

ROCE fires for a simple reason: profit is growing fast while the company is not spending on new plant, so the same capital earns more. Management has guided no major capex through FY28. The risk is that the reported ROCE is partly an artefact of related-party payables shrinking the capital base, so it should be checked on an adjusted base in Phase 3.

Cash is the hole in the story and there is no catalyst to fill it. Across every category the protocol looks at, the search comes back close to empty. There is no factoring or receivables-discounting facility announced. There is no debtor-day target that has actually been hit. There is no payment-term renegotiation on record. Debtor days are not falling. The one fresh related-party move this year, a Rs 33.2 cr interest-free loan from the largest customer to the subsidiary, makes the entanglement worse, not better. The only positive is that FY26 consolidated cash flow turned positive, and even that is undercut by the standalone line going backwards in the same year. Under the intent-and-action test, management has words on deleveraging but no documented action on working capital, so the conservative read stands. What would confirm cash starting: standalone operating cash flow turning positive alongside consolidated, and debtor days dropping below 60, for two straight periods. What keeps it stuck: another year of the receivable base climbing faster than sales, funded by more borrowing.
