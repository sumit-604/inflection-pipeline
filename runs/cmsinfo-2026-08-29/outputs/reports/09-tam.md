# Stage 9 — TAM / SAM / SOM Market Sizing: CMS Info Systems Ltd (CMSINFO)
Run date: 2026-08-29 | Model: claude-sonnet-5 | Status: complete

## Headline finding (read this first)

CMS runs three platforms with three different runways, not one pond. The
spear pass back-solved a single ~Rs5,000-5,500 Cr pond from CMS's own
disclosed share percentages, and concluded CMS already holds ~43% of it
(a ~2x story). That method structurally excludes the CONVERSION
opportunity — banks not yet outsourcing, banking sites not yet on remote
monitoring — which is exactly what management's own TAM claims are built
on. Using management's own segment TAM statements, cross-checked
bottom-up against unit counts and CMS's own revenue-per-unit, the true
picture is platform-specific:

- ATM Management Solutions: TAM materially bigger than spear's figure
  (Rs10,000 Cr vs spear's Rs2,800 Cr), but CMS already holds ~16-21% of
  the better-defined addressable base and further gains here are now
  mostly SHARE-GAIN off a shrinking/flat unit count (AGS Transact's CIRP
  collapse), not market expansion. Spear's "small pond, mostly captured"
  framing is closer to right HERE.
- Retail Solutions & Currency Logistics: TAM materially bigger than
  spear's figure (Rs5,000-9,000 Cr vs Rs1,580 Cr) because it is tied to
  India's 5-6 lakh organized retail points, of which CMS serves ~65,000
  (11-13%). Genuine, still-early runway.
- Technology & Payment Solutions (HAWKAI Vision AI + ALGO + cards): TAM
  materially bigger than spear's figure (Rs8,000 Cr vs Rs800-1,000 Cr).
  Non-BFSI Vision AI (Rs5,000 Cr of the Rs8,000 Cr) is explicitly
  described by management as "a market in its early stages." This is
  the platform with the most genuine unpenetrated TAM, and it is the one
  the FY30 mix-shift thesis (16% to 20-22% of revenue) depends on.

Net: this is not simply a "2x share-of-wallet, small pond" story across
the board. It is a mature, largely-captured physical-cash pond (ATM
Management) bolted to two platforms with real, still-open runway
(Retail formalisation, and especially Vision AI). The blended SOM-implied
revenue CAGR (11.7-12.1%) sits close to, but slightly below, management's
own FY30 guidance (13-14%) — reasonable, not inflated.

## SECTION 1: MARKET DEFINITION

### 1A Boundaries

- **Product scope**: three platforms per CMS's own FY26 segment
  reporting (AR FY26 MD&A p.94-97): (1) ATM Management Solutions — ATM
  cash logistics, Brown Label ATM (BLA) management, ATM ancillary
  services; (2) Retail Solutions & Currency Logistics — retail cash
  management, currency-in-transit (CIT), bullion logistics; (3)
  Technology & Payment Solutions — HAWKAI Vision AI, ALGO ATM-software,
  payment cards. Product/hardware trading revenue (~7% of total revenue
  per B04) is EXCLUDED — it is a pass-through resale line (ATMs,
  recyclers, kiosks resold to banks), not a recurring addressable market.
- **Geographic scope**: India only. CMS states 97% district coverage
  (AR FY26 p.14/94); no meaningful international revenue disclosed.
  Global comparisons below are for benchmarking only, not counted into
  TAM.
- **Customer scope**: banks (PSU and private), NBFCs, and — for
  Technology & Payment Solutions and Retail Solutions specifically —
  non-BFSI enterprises (organized retail, quick commerce, e-commerce,
  EV infrastructure, QSR, logistics, warehousing).
- **Channel scope**: outsourced/managed-service contracts only (fixed-fee
  or transaction-linked). Self-managed in-house bank operations are
  EXCLUDED from current revenue but INCLUDED in TAM as conversion
  potential (this is the core of management's own TAM logic).
- **Price segment**: full-service integrated contracts (cash + hardware +
  software + AI), not point-solution CCTV/hardware-only sales.
- **Explicit exclusions**: card manufacturing/personalization as a
  standalone commodity business is folded into Technology & Payment
  Solutions revenue (~Rs75 Cr/yr, AR FY26 p.14) but has no separately
  disclosed TAM — excluded from the sizing below and flagged NOT FOUND.

## SECTION 2: TAM ESTIMATION, MULTIPLE METHODS

### Platform 1 — ATM Management Solutions

**Method 1, top-down (management-stated)**: Rs10,000 Cr (AR FY26 p.14, see
1B).

**Method 2, bottom-up (unit economics)**:
- Total India ATMs: RBI/press data ~251,000-253,000 (FY24-FY25) [Business
  Standard/Policy Circle citing RBI, undated headline but reporting FY24
  data]; AR's own chart shows 258,000 (AR FY26 p.15, "Source: IMF
  Financial Access Survey," no date printed on the chart — flagged
  STALE-uncertain since IMF FAS typically lags 1-2 years).
- Currently outsourced ATM base: ~120,000 machines, DOWN from ~140,000
  after a competitor's exit (AGS Transact, CIRP 25-Aug-2025) shrank the
  outsourced pool during FY26 (AR FY26 MD&A p.94, section 3.1).
- Not-yet-outsourced (the conversion opportunity): "over 100,000 ATMs are
  yet to be outsourced" (AR FY26 MD&A p.95, section 5.1) — implies a
  TAM-eligible universe of roughly 220,000 ATMs (120,000 already
  outsourced + 100,000+ conversion-eligible), below the full 251,000-258,000
  count because some ATMs (very small cooperative/regional banks,
  security-sensitive vault sites) will structurally never be outsourced.
- Revenue per ATM, AR-stated reference bands (AR FY26 p.22-23, industry
  reference, not CMS-specific actuals): currency-outsourced bank-owned
  ~Rs2.2 lakh/ATM/yr; BLA ~Rs4.5 lakh/ATM/yr; WLA ~Rs5 lakh/ATM/yr.
- CMS's own blended actual: ATM Management Solutions revenue (~Rs1,341
  Cr, see Section 3 calc) / ~71,500 ATMs managed (midpoint of the
  70,000-73,000 range cited across the AR) = **~Rs1.88 lakh/ATM/yr**
  [analyst calc].
- Full-conversion bottom-up TAM at the LOW (cash-logistics-only) rate:
  220,000 x Rs2.2 lakh = **Rs4,840 Cr**.
- Full-conversion bottom-up TAM at the BLA rate (where the industry is
  actually migrating — recyclers, fixed-fee integrated contracts): 220,000
  x Rs4.5 lakh = **Rs9,900 Cr** — this triangulates almost exactly against
  management's own Rs10,000 Cr claim.

**Method 3, peer aggregation**: CMS (Rs1,341 Cr ATM Mgmt segment, FY26,
derived), AGS Transact (in CIRP, FY26 revenue NOT FOUND — filings
unreliable post-insolvency), Hitachi Payment Services (unlisted, NOT
FOUND), Writer Business Services (unlisted, NOT FOUND), FSS (partially
absorbed by CMS, standalone revenue NOT FOUND). Peer aggregation is
directionally supportive of a multi-thousand-crore market but cannot be
independently computed — three of four named competitors have no
disclosed revenue in this corpus.

**Method 4, industry report cross-check**: Research and Markets, "India
ATM Managed Services & Cash Logistics Market," cites INR 305 billion
(~Rs30,500 Cr) [web search, publication date NOT FOUND in the available
snippet — treated as directional only, flagged STALE-uncertain]. This
figure appears to bundle BOTH ATM managed services AND cash logistics
(Platform 1 + Platform 2 combined per CMS's own definitions), so it is
NOT a like-for-like cross-check on Platform 1 alone; noted but not used
as an anchor.

### Platform 3 — Technology & Payment Solutions (HAWKAI Vision AI + ALGO + cards)

**Method 1, top-down (management-stated, HAWKAI/Vision AI slice
only)**: Rs8,000 Cr (Rs3,000 Cr BFSI + Rs5,000 Cr non-BFSI), see 1B.
ALGO (standalone) and payment cards TAM are NOT FOUND and are excluded —
this platform's true TAM is understated by an unquantified amount.

**Method 2, bottom-up (banking touchpoints)**: "Only approximately 40% of
India's estimated 375,000 banking touchpoints currently utilise remote
monitoring solutions" (AR FY26 MD&A p.96-97) — i.e., ~150,000 touchpoints
already have some form of remote monitoring (by any vendor), ~225,000 do
not. CMS's own revenue per monitored site: HAWKAI revenue Rs200 Cr (AR
FY26 p.4-5/52, reconciled figure per B04's flag on the internal Rs200mn
vs Rs200 Cr discrepancy) / 50,000+ monitored sites (AR FY26 p.5) = **~Rs40,000
per site/yr** [analyst calc]. Applying to the full 375,000-site banking
universe: 375,000 x Rs40,000 = **~Rs15,000 Cr for BFSI alone** — this is
materially ABOVE management's own stated Rs3,000 Cr BFSI-specific TAM.

**Divergence flagged, not averaged**: the bottom-up figure (~Rs15,000
Cr) exceeds management's stated BFSI Vision AI TAM (Rs3,000 Cr) by ~5x.
Plausible explanations (none confirmed): (a) CMS's Rs40,000/site rate
reflects its highest-value, most complex sites (ATMs, cash-handling
branches) and is not representative of the full 375,000-site universe,
much of which may only need basic passive-to-active upgrades at a far
lower price point; (b) a large share of the "already 40% penetrated"
sites may be on legacy CCTV contracts not realistically re-winnable
near-term, shrinking the TRUE addressable slice below the raw
un-penetrated site count. Conservative bias (rule 6) resolves this by
taking the LOWER, AR-anchored figure (Rs3,000 Cr BFSI) rather than the
bottom-up figure, but the divergence itself is unresolved and is named
as a Halt 1 question below.

**Bottom-up cross-check, sanity magnitude only**: total addressable
Vision AI sites across all named verticals (AR FY26 p.16-17 site table):
Bank ATMs ~200K + Bank Branches ~200K + NBFC/Insurance/MFI ~45K + Gold
Loan ~30K + Quick Commerce/Retail/QSR ~120K + Infrastructure (EV,
logistics, telecom) ~500K-1,000K = **~1.1-1.6 million sites total**. At
Rs8,000 Cr TAM ÷ ~1.35 million sites (midpoint) = ~Rs59,300/site — same
order of magnitude as CMS's own Rs40,000/site actual, a reasonable
cross-check at the WHOLE-platform level even where the BFSI-only slice
diverges.

**Conservative estimate = Realistic estimate: Rs8,000 Cr** (single
well-anchored management figure; the bottom-up serves as an upper-bound
sanity check showing understatement risk, not overstatement risk — so
conservative and realistic are held equal here rather than inventing an
unanchored midpoint. ALGO and cards TAM stay NOT FOUND per the no-estimate
rule, meaning this Rs8,000 Cr figure is itself a floor for the full
platform, not a ceiling).

SAM as % of realistic TAM = 21,100 / 27,000 = **78.1%**. This is a high

geography and nearly every relevant customer type. The barrier is
competitive intensity AMONG the few survivors, not CMS's own product,
geography, or capability reach.

### 3C Capacity cross-check (using injected capex figures — B07 not yet
available; FY26 capex ~Rs352 Cr management-stated / ~Rs409 Cr Gate 0
computed including intangibles; FY27 capex guided DOWN sharply to
Rs100-125 Cr, per Q1 FY27 call)

The 3yr SOM implies an ATM Management Solutions revenue uplift of Rs444
Cr (Rs1,341 Cr -> Rs1,785 Cr). At CMS's own blended rate (~Rs1.88
lakh/ATM/yr, Section 2 calc), that uplift needs ~23,600 additional ATMs
under management. FY26's elevated capex (Rs352-409 Cr) was explicitly
"build-out for major new contracts" (AR FY26 MD&A p.96) covering SBI
(4,000 ATMs), ICICI (10,000+ ATMs), HDFC (6,000 ATMs), and IPPB (1,000
units) — **~21,000 ATM units of capacity already funded and contracted**,
covering roughly 89% of the 3yr SOM's implied unit growth. The residual
gap (~2,600 units, ~Rs45-50 Cr of capex-equivalent by the same per-unit
rate) fits comfortably inside FY27's own guided Rs100-125 Cr capex
envelope on its own.

**Capacity check: SUFFICIENT for the 3yr SOM.** The 5yr SOM (a further
Rs340 Cr uplift on this platform alone, Rs1,785 Cr -> Rs2,125 Cr) depends
on a capex re-acceleration beyond FY27 that management has not yet
guided (FY28-29 capex plans NOT FOUND in this corpus). Between the two,
**the SOM is the achievable side; the capex plan is the one running
behind the growth it implies past year 3** — not because management is
being optimistic on SOM, but because FY27's capex guidance simply has
not yet been extended to FY28-29.
