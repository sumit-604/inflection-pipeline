## Business Understanding Narrative

**What does it do, and how does it make money?** Anuh makes bulk drugs (APIs) at one site in Tarapur, Maharashtra: 9 API blocks, 2 intermediate blocks, 2,400 MTPA (Deck, page 22). It is among the world's largest makers of first-generation erythromycin salts. It also sells anti-TB, anti-malarial, anti-diabetic and corticosteroid APIs (AR26, page 6). It buys intermediates, mainly from China, and sells APIs to formulators. About 46% of FY26 revenue was exports (Deck, page 15). Profit is the spread between API price and intermediate cost.

**What is changing?** Management says the mix is moving to higher-value products in regulated markets. "FY27 has opened on a confident note, with profit growth running well ahead of revenue - a deliberate outcome of our shift towards higher-value products in regulated markets" (Deck, page 7). The deck's product chart shows erythromycin falling from about Rs 63.6 crore to Rs 49.1 crore year on year in Q1. Higher macrolides rose from about Rs 19.7 crore to Rs 39.9 crore (Deck, page 13; label mapping inferred from chart text, the totals tie to reported revenue within 1%).

**Why now?** H1 FY27 profit after tax was Rs 22.64 crore against Rs 15.91 crore, up 42%, on revenue up only 1.8% (Q2R, page 1). The screener sees that jump.

**What must be true?** The margin gain must come from mix, not from cheaper Chinese intermediates. Revenue must start growing again. The company guides 15 to 20% for FY27 (AR26, page 8). H1 delivered 1.8%.

**What breaks it?** API price erosion. The chairman names "surplus capacities, price erosion" as the industry condition (AR26, page 6). Profit after tax fell for two years from Rs 60.06 crore in FY24 to Rs 41.05 crore in FY26 (AR25, page 35; AR26, page 34). The H1 jump is a recovery against a weak base. It is not yet a new high.

## 2. Business model and archetype

One reported segment, "Bulk drug and Chemicals" (Q2R, page 4). The engine is volume times spread on generic APIs. Erythromycin was 26% of FY22 sales (May 2022 transcript, page 2). In Q1 FY27 it was about 25% (Deck, page 13).

Q2 FY27 material cost, including traded goods and inventory change, was 74.4% of revenue against 77.8% a year earlier. Traded-goods purchases fell from Rs 18.39 crore to Rs 0.48 crore (Q2R, page 1). The 340 bp gross-margin gain is the whole H1 story.

**Archetype: commodity converter.** Section 1B v3.7 Amendment 17 binds. The chairman's "strategic CDMO focus" (AR26, page 6) has no revenue line anywhere in the corpus.

## 3. Competitive advantages

Narrow and forming in regulated markets. Absent elsewhere. The assets are scale in erythromycin salts and a filing stack: 8 CEPs, 5 WHO PQs, 3 more under assessment (AR26, page 6). A USFDA inspection closed with no Form 483 (AR26, page 35). Filings create switching costs for regulated buyers. They do not give pricing power. Backward integration is still "long term" and intermediates come "mainly from China" (AR26, page 53). Moat: forming, narrow.

## 4. The promoters

The Shah family's SK Group, an INR 8.5 bn group of about 2,200 people across formulations, trading, distribution and logistics (Deck, page 15). Ritesh Shah and Vivek Shah are Joint MDs. Bipin Shah is Vice Chairman (AR26, page 9).

Promoter and group: 71.80% on 10 August 2026 (Deck, page 20). The FY26 register adds to 71.79% (AR26, page 69). Mutual funds hold 0.00% and FPIs 0.05% (AR26, page 69). Free-float market cap is Rs 220 crore on the company's own figure (Deck, page 20).

Pledge: NOT FOUND. A grep of AR26 for pledge and encumbrance returns nothing.

Related party: sales to group formulator S Kant Healthcare were Rs 66.31 crore in FY26, 8.6% of revenue (AR26, page 46). Receivables from related parties rose to Rs 27.10 crore from Rs 16.61 crore (AR26, page 112). The JMD claimed 90-day terms on this account (May 2022 transcript, page 3). Commission of Rs 0.60 crore went to Gaurav Satish Shah, a non-executive director (AR26, page 46). Each Joint MD earns about 23 times median pay (AR26, page 47).

## 6. The transition and the quality ladder

FROM R1 commodity price-taker, at the R2 edge. ROCE ran 15.92% to 18.79% and fell with price (AR26, page 141). TO R3 value-added regulated-market supplier, claimed.

The claim is quoted in the narrative (Deck, page 7). The deck's own split does not yet support it. Exports fell to about Rs 70 crore from Rs 88 crore in Q1, and domestic rose to about Rs 124 crore from Rs 98 crore (Deck, page 11; series mapping inferred from chart text). Europe fell to 29.6% of exports from 35.5%. North America rose to 4.2% from 3.8% (Deck, page 12). Regulated-market share of exports fell. The product shift is visible. The regulated-market shift is not.

## 8. Proof check

The trigger claims profit growth ahead of revenue from mix. Two quarters do show it. H1 EBITDA margin was 9.74% against 7.13% (Q2R, page 4). PBT was Rs 31.98 crore against Rs 20.87 crore (Q2R, page 1).

The proof is partial. The base was the weakest half in three years. Sequentially, Q2 PAT of Rs 11.21 crore sits below Q4 FY26 at Rs 11.68 crore. Revenue fell 4.4% quarter on quarter. Tax was normal at 25.5% in Q2 (Q2R, page 1), so tax does not drive the jump. No proof point exists yet for the regulated-market mechanism.

## 9. Flags from the documents

- Customer concentration. The largest customer's receivable was Rs 94.46 crore at FY26, up from Rs 81.87 crore (AR26, page 122). That is about 40% of the Rs 238.63 crore receivables book (FinTxt). The customer is not named.
- Related-party sales of 8.6% of revenue, with related-party receivables up 63% (AR26, pages 46 and 112).
- Inconsistent company statements. The chairman says 85 countries and 1,200+ customers (AR26, page 6). The deck says 72 countries and 360 customers (Deck, page 15). The deck shows FY26 PAT at Rs 47.4 crore (Deck, page 9) against Rs 41.05 crore reported (AR26, page 34).
- Non-standard margin. The MD&A "operating margin" is 19.61% (AR26, page 53). The deck defines operating profit as PBT plus depreciation plus other expenses, less other income (Deck, page 8). Use the 8.56% EBITDA margin instead.
- Boilerplate. The FY26 chairman's 15 to 16% CAGR line and the VC's "now listed on BSE and NSE" line repeat the FY25 text (AR25, pages 7 and 8; AR26, pages 7 and 8).
- Working capital: FY26 operating cash flow negative, inventory doubled, Rs 19.48 crore of new borrowings.
- Unfulfilled advance-licence export obligation of Rs 2.77 crore, not provided (AR26, page 138). Total contingent liabilities are small at Rs 7.45 crore.
- No earnings call since May 2022.
- Auditor: standard key audit matters (capitalisation, revenue, contingent liabilities). No emphasis of matter found (AR26, pages 80 and 81).

## 11. Posture: the Transition Decision Matrix

- **Proof gate: NOT FIRED.** Margin recovered for two quarters. Revenue did not grow. The regulated-market share of exports fell. The mechanism management names is not visible in the numbers.
- **Ugliness: ARTIFACT-OF-CLIMB, provisional.** The FY26 inventory build and the depreciation step to Rs 11.38 crore (FinTxt) follow the 2,400 MTPA expansion and six launches. H1 FY27 partly reversed it. Price erosion behind the FY24 to FY26 profit fall could be structural. That is the risk to this call.
- **Recognition gap: CLOSED.** Market capitalisation is Rs 847.2 crore (Bull AI company record, 2026-11-04, a service record, not a filed document). Trailing PAT for four quarters is Rs 47.76 crore, so trailing PE is about 17.7x. That already sits between the R2 neighbourhood (15 to 17x) and R3 (about 19x). The market pays near the TO rung for a business that has not proven it.

**Posture: PRICED NARRATIVE (TRAP).** Clean optics, a narrated climb, a multiple near the TO rung.

## 12. Verdict card

**WATCH.** Hard watch, no `/step1` yet.

The screener hit is a base effect. Profit is recovering toward the FY24 level on flat revenue. The multiple already prices a climb that the export split does not show.

**Load-bearing facts to verify first**
1. Source of the 340 bp Q2 FY27 gross-margin gain: product mix, or cheaper China intermediates and less trading.
2. Q3 FY27 revenue growth against the 15 to 20% guide.
3. The identity of the customer behind Rs 94.46 crore of receivables.
4. The 15 September 2025 ICRA rationale.
5. The regulated-market share of revenue, with a clean export split.

**What would change the view**
- Q3 FY27 revenue growth in double digits with EBITDA margin at 10% or above. That fires the proof gate.
- A rising Europe and North America share of exports.
- A price fall that reopens the gap against a fired gate.
- Margin falling back to 7% makes the ugliness structural and moves the posture to VALUE-TRAP RISK.

No Role 1 valuation was run and no target price is set. This verdict decides only whether the name earns a full `/step1`. It is not a position.
