Detailed rationale

Crisil Ratings has upgraded its ratings on the bank facilities of Supreme Power Equipment Limited (SPEL) to 'Crisil BBB/Stable/Crisil A3+' from 'Crisil BBB-/Stable/Crisil A3’.

 

The ratings upgrade factor in the steady growth of the company over the last three fiscals ended 2025 at a compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of 14% and expected improvement in the scale of operations, with an order book of over Rs 300 crore as of February 2026. The company is also expected to sustain the operating margin of around 17% and improvement in working capital cycle on back of higher number of orders from private players. The financial risk profile has also remained comfortable for the company with low reliance on external debt, as reflected in gearing of 0.2 time as on March 31, 2025, and is expected to remain comfortable over the medium term as well. Execution of the order book reflecting improvement in the scale of operations, with sustenance of the operating margin and collection efficiency, will remain monitorable.

 

The ratings reflect the extensive experience of the promoters in manufacturing transformers, strong order book position and healthy financial risk profile. These strengths are partially offset by susceptibility to risks inherent in tender-based operations, raw material price volatility and large working capital requirement.

Analytical approach

Crisil Ratings has consolidated the business and financial risk profiles of SPEL and Danya Electric Company because SPEL holds 90% stake in Danya Electric Company, and both the entities have significant operational and financial linkages.

Key rating drivers - Strengths

Extensive experience of the promoters

The promoters — Vee Rajmohan and K V Pradeep Kumar — have more than two decades of experience in manufacturing transformers; their strong understanding of market dynamics and healthy relationships with customers and suppliers should continue to support the business. 

 

Strong order book providing revenue visibility

The company has achieved revenue of Rs 148.72 crore in fiscal 2025 against Rs 113.52 crore in fiscal 2024 aided by increase in demand and healthy order book position. In the first nine months of fiscal 2026, the company achieved revenue of Rs 111.03 crore. The company has an order book of ~Rs 300 crore as of February 2026, to be executed over the next 9–12 months with a healthy mix of orders — ~70% private orders and ~30% from government players — which should provide significant revenue visibility over the medium term. The company also has escalation clauses in place for orders for more than six months which constitute nearly ~50% of the order book.

 

Healthy financial risk profile

The capital structure should remain healthy due to low reliance on external funds. Gearing stood comfortable at 0.20 time and total outside liabilities to adjusted networth ratio at 0.63 time as on March 31, 2025. Debt protection metrics may continue to be strong owing to leverage and steady profitability. Interest coverage ratio was 11.11 times and net cash accrual to total debt ratio was 1.03 times for fiscal 2025.

Key rating drivers - Weaknesses

Susceptibility to risks inherent in tender-based operations and raw material price movements

As the company derives its entire revenue from tender-based orders, its ability to successfully bid for projects is critical. Furthermore, intense competition necessitates aggressive bidding, mostly compromising on the operating margin. The raw materials constitute the major portion (~70%) of the expense and any volatility in the input prices such as copper and transformer oil, can also have adverse impact on the operating margin. However, the raw material price volatility is partially mitigated by price escalation clauses present for projects with tenure more than six months.

 

The ability of the company to sustain profitability through operating efficiency will become critical and will be monitorable over the medium term.

 

Large working capital requirement

Gross current assets (GCAs) have been at 210–289 days for the past three fiscals (at 214 days as on March 31, 2025), driven by stretched receivables of 111–211 days and inventory of 82–96 days. The company needs to extend long credit period and maintain huge work in process and inventory to meet business needs.

 

However, on back of higher private order intakes over the last two fiscals, receivables improved to 111 days as on March 31, 2025, from 211 days as on March 31, 2024. With ~70% of the order book being from private players, the company is expected to have improvement in receivables cycle resulting in better GCAs over the medium term.

Liquidity Adequate

Liquidity should remain supported by the adequate surplus available in cash accrual and bank lines. Bank limit utilisation was just 35% on average for the 12 months through January 2026. Annual cash accrual is expected to be Rs 20–30 crore against yearly debt obligations of Rs 7–9 crore over the medium term. The current ratio was also healthy at 1.71 times as on March 31, 2025. Furthermore, low gearing and moderate networth will aid financial flexibility.

Outlook Stable

SPEL will continue to benefit from the extensive experience of its promoters, their established relationships with clients and strong order book providing revenue visibility for the medium term.

Rating Sensitivity Factors

Upward Factors

  • Sustained growth in revenue with operating margin of 15–17% on a consistent basis, leading to higher-than-expected cash accrual
  • Improvement in the working capital cycle on a consistent basis
  • Strengthening of the financial risk profile

 

Downward Factors

  • Steep decline in revenue and operating margin dropping to 12–13%, resulting in lower-than-expected cash accrual
  • Any decline in collection efficiency, leading to stretch in the working capital cycle weakening the liquidity position
  • Large, debt-funded capital expenditure

About the Company
Tamil Nadu-based SPEL, formerly known as Supreme Power Equipment, was constituted as a partnership firm in 1994. It got converted into a private limited company (Supreme Power Equipment Pvt Ltd) in 2005 and a public limited company under the current name in 2023.

The company is engaged in designing, manufacturing and assembling of various types of transformers, electrical transmission equipment and control units, with erecting, commissioning and testing of high-tension lines, electrical distribution panels, electrical, electronic and mechanical apparatus. The company got listed on the National Stock Exchange Emerge platform on December 29, 2023. Vee Rajmohan and K V Pradeep Kumar are the promoters.
