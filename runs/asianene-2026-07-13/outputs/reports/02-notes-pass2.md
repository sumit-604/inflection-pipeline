NEW FINDINGS (not in Pass 1)

1. **Cross-note numeric inconsistency in related-party revenue: Asian Indwell JV "sale of services"
   figure differs between the standalone and consolidated RPT notes for the identical AESL→JV
   transaction.** Standalone Note 44.B(3)(iii), p.124 states "Sale of services (including revenue
   received in advance)" to Asian Indwell Joint Venture = ₹10,812.34 Lakhs. Consolidated Note 46.B(2)
   (iii), p.199 states the same line item (identical wording) = ₹11,127.62 Lakhs — a ₹315.28 Lakh
   (2.9%) unreconciled gap. Cross-checked against every other RPT counterparty in the same table
   (Oilmax ₹1,872.01L, Zuberi ₹2,618.42L, AESL FFIL ₹3,948.77L, Asian Oilmax ₹52.39L) — all four
   match exactly between standalone and consolidated notes; Asian Indwell is the sole outlier. The
   standalone figure (₹10,812.34L), when summed with the other four related-party sale-of-services
   entries, ties out exactly to the "billing to related parties" total of ₹19,303.93 Lakhs disclosed in
   Note 50(c) (already flagged in Pass 1) — confirming the standalone figure is internally consistent
   and the consolidated figure is the one that does not reconcile to anything else in the report.
   Related-party trade receivables from Asian Indwell JV (₹6,026.47 Lakhs) and "amount paid on behalf
   of related party" (₹505.25 Lakhs) match exactly between the two notes — only the revenue line
   diverges. 🔴 Flag for Pass 3 pattern check and a direct question for management: which figure is
   correct, and why does the single largest, newest, and most receivables-concentrated related party
   have an unreconciled revenue figure between the standalone and consolidated notes?

2. **Undisclosed counterparty for a ₹65.00 Lakh loan given and fully recovered within the year.**
   Auditor's Annexure A, para 3(a), p.68 standalone: "The Company has provided loans to one (1)
   subsidiary company and one (1) other party during the year" — aggregate amount provided to
   "Others" = ₹65.00 Lakhs, balance outstanding at year-end = Nil (fully repaid), and "the receipt of
   principal and interest are regular." The identity of this "other party," the interest rate charged, and
   the business purpose are NOT disclosed anywhere in the standalone or consolidated notes — this
   loan does not appear in Note 9 (Loans) itself, which only discloses the subsidiary loans. 🟡 A clean
   in-and-out loan is not inherently concerning, but the counterparty is unnamed in a related-party-light
   annual report where the company's core model already runs through related JV vehicles — worth a
   direct question to management.

3. **Gross vehicle disposals of ₹153.53 Lakhs during FY25 (net book value ~₹77.89 Lakhs) with no
   corresponding gain/loss on disposal disclosed anywhere in the P&L or notes.** Note 3, p.93
   standalone (and consolidated Note 3, p.165) shows Vehicles: gross disposals ₹153.53 Lakhs,
   accumulated depreciation on disposal ₹75.64 Lakhs (net WDV disposed ≈ ₹77.89 Lakhs). Neither
   Note 30/36 (Other Income) nor Note 36 (Other Expenses) standalone contains a "gain/loss on sale of
   property, plant and equipment" line item — the disposal proceeds, and any gain or loss, are not
   traceable in the financial statements. Separately, Note 21(e) standalone (Net debt reconciliation,
   p.106) shows a ₹66.02 Lakh non-cash "Other adjustments" item reducing non-current borrowings
   during the year, described only as "pertains to repayment of borrowings other than by way of
   consideration in cash" — the notes do not state whether this relates to the vehicle disposals (e.g.,
   loan write-off/settlement against traded-in vehicles) or something else. 🟡 Disclosure-completeness
   gap: a fleet disposal event and a non-cash borrowings extinguishment both occurred in the same
   year without being linked or explained.

4. **New related-party payable to the Parent Company: AESL began owing Oilmax Energy Pvt Ltd
   ₹344.14 Lakhs in trade payables in FY25 (Nil in FY24).** Note 44.C, p.126 standalone (and
   consolidated Note 46.C, p.200) — "Trade Payables" to Oilmax Energy Private Limited: ₹344.14 Lakhs
   FY25 vs Nil FY24. This is a new, second direction of related-party working-capital flow layered on
   top of the already-flagged Oilmax trade receivable (₹1,026.12 Lakhs) and "Other financial liabilities"
   to Oilmax, which also grew (₹70.83L → ₹124.95L, +76%). 🟡 The company now both sells services to,
   and owes trade payables to, its own promoter entity — deepens the related-party entanglement in
   working capital already flagged in Pass 1 as a governance watch item, but from a new angle
   (AESL as debtor to its parent, not just creditor).

5. **Consolidated-only unhedged foreign-currency trade payable exposure of ₹946.07 Lakhs (net
   exposure -₹939.69 Lakhs) sitting almost entirely at the loss-making foreign subsidiaries, versus a
   negligible ₹16.77 Lakh standalone FX exposure.** Consolidated Note 41 "Foreign exchange risk,"
   p.189: trade payables denominated in AED, Singapore Dollar and Nigerian Naira total ₹946.07 Lakhs
   as at March 31, 2025 (₹857.14 Lakhs FY24), against financial assets of only ₹6.38 Lakhs — a
   materially unhedged net short foreign-currency position. The standalone entity's FX exposure by
   contrast is only a ₹16.77 Lakh USD lease liability (Note 41, p.117 standalone). The sensitivity table
   confirms the asymmetry: a 2.65% USD movement impacts standalone profit by only ₹0.44 Lakhs but
   the consolidated impact is ₹24.88 Lakhs (all attributable to non-USD currencies not separately
   sensitized). "The Group has not entered into any hedging contract for the above exposure." 🟡 This
   currency risk sits precisely at the entities already flagged in Pass 1 as cash-strapped shells (ADMCC,
   AOSL Petroleum) with nil/negligible turnover — an unhedged FX payable book at insolvent or
   deeply-loss-making subsidiaries is a compounding, not isolated, risk.

6. **Non-controlling interest loss-absorption asymmetry at Optimum Oil & Gas Pvt Ltd (74% AESL /
   26% NCI).** Note 52, consolidated, p.204-205: "Share of profit/(loss) attributable to NCI... Restricted
   to Nil for Optimum as NCI does not have contractual commitment to provide for any losses over and
   above it's share." Optimum has had negative equity for at least two years (-₹40.45L FY24, -₹41.02L
   FY25) with Nil turnover both years, yet the 26% non-controlling shareholder bears none of the
   ongoing losses — AESL absorbs effectively 100% of Optimum's losses despite owning only 74%. 🟢
   Immaterial in absolute size (~₹41 Lakhs equity deficit) but a genuine structural asymmetry worth
   noting alongside the broader subsidiary-quality flag already raised in Pass 1.

7. **A ₹154.22 Lakh doubtful advance to a supplier, 100% provided in FY24, was fully written off /
   derecognized in FY25 — consolidated-only, meaning it originated at a subsidiary and is not
   identified anywhere.** Consolidated Note 18 (Other current assets), p.173: "Advance to suppliers...
   Unsecured, considered doubtful — [FY25: Nil] [FY24: ₹154.22 Lakhs]... Less: Impairment allowance —
   [FY25: Nil] [FY24: (₹154.22)]." This item does not appear at all in the standalone Note 19, confirming
   it sits at one of the subsidiaries (most likely ADMCC, consistent with that entity's already-flagged
   pattern of impairments/write-offs — Note 36 exceptional items, prior year). Neither the supplier's
   identity nor the reason for derecognition (versus continuing to carry it 100%-provided) is disclosed.
   🟡 Another subsidiary-level asset-quality casualty layered on top of the receivable impairment,
   trade-receivable credit-impaired gap (₹1,884.80L, already flagged), and the ADMCC/AOSL Petroleum
   deficits already raised in Pass 1.

8. **Credit rating action: India Ratings & Research affirmed 'IND BBB' for AESL's bank facilities and
   revised the Outlook from Stable to Positive during the year.** Corporate Governance Report, p.58:
   "During the year under review, India Ratings & Research ('Ind-Ra') affirmed the rating of 'IND BBB'
   for Asian Energy Services Limited's ('the Company') Bank Facilities and revised the Outlook from
   stable to 'Positive'." 🟢 Not previously captured in Pass 1 (Pass 1 covered leverage ratios but not the
   external rating agency view). A sub-investment-grade-adjacent rating (BBB is investment grade
   floor in India but low) with a Positive outlook is a useful external corroboration/context point for
   cost-of-capital and covenant-headroom assessment, and is broadly consistent with the low leverage
   (0.06x D/E) already noted.

9. **Key Management Personnel now hold roughly one-third of all outstanding dilutive stock
   options, a sharp concentration increase.** Note 43.2(iii) standalone, p.123 (and consolidated Note
   44.2(iii), p.195): "Aggregate number of share options available with Key Management Personnel:
   151,000 (March 31, 2024: 29,314)." Total options outstanding across both schemes at March 31, 2025
   = 70,231 (AESL ESOP 2021) + 380,744 (AESL ESOP 2024) ≈ 450,975, so KMP holds ~33.5% of all
   outstanding options, up from roughly 8-9% a year earlier (29,314/roughly 2,06,000-plus then
   outstanding). 🟡 This sharpens the ESOP dilution/compensation-quality flag already raised in Pass 1
   (68.2% discount-to-market 2024 grant): the newly-generous scheme is disproportionately concentrated
   in the hands of the people who approved/benefit from it, rather than broad-based across the ~375
   employees.

10. **Board/ownership governance details: the Managing Director personally holds zero equity
    shares in the Company, and a father-son pair sits on the Board alongside the promoter's wife.**
    Corporate Governance Report, p.47: "As on March 31, 2025, Mr. Brij Mohan Bansal holds 7,000
    (0.015%) equity shares of the Company. No other Director holds any shares in the Company." This
    means MD Kapil Garg — despite being Executive/Managing Director — has no direct personal equity
    stake; his family's economic interest runs entirely through promoter holding company Oilmax
    (60.83%) and his wife Ritu Garg (0.14%, per Note 20(e) standalone, p.104). Separately, CG Report
    p.40, "Disclosure of relationships between directors inter-se": "Dr. Kapil Garg, Managing Director of
    the Company is Father of Mr. Aman Garg, Non-Executive Director of the Company." 🟡 Neutral
    governance fact pattern (family-controlled board is common for promoter-driven small-caps) but
    worth recording precisely: no direct personal skin-in-the-game for the MD himself, only via the
    holding company and a family member.

11. **The consolidated audit opinion relies materially on component (other) auditors for the exact
    items already flagged as concentration risks in Pass 1.** Consolidated Independent Auditor's
    Report, "Other Matters" paragraph 15, p.138: six (6) subsidiaries not audited by the principal auditor
    (total assets ₹2,156.64 Lakhs, total net loss after tax ₹627.27 Lakhs, before consolidation
    adjustments) and four (4) joint ventures not audited by the principal auditor, whose collective
    ₹618.66 Lakh equity-pickup profit (≈11% of consolidated PBT of ₹5,617.69 Lakhs) is included based
    solely on other auditors' reports furnished to the principal auditor. 🟡 This is standard practice for
    group audits, but it means the JV-concentration risk already flagged in Pass 1 (72.9% of JV profit
    from the 13-month-old Asian Indwell JV) and the subsidiary-quality risk (ADMCC, AOSL Petroleum)
    were verified by different audit firms than Walker Chandiok & Co LLP, adding a layer of reliance the
    reader should be aware of when weighing those findings.

12. **A previously-unnamed related party — Anirit Agro Hub LLP / Anirit Agritech Private Limited —
    transacted with the Company, with the specific controlling KMP/relative left unidentified.** Note
    44.A(f) standalone, p.123: category "Entities on which KMP & its relative have significant influence"
    lists Anirit Agro Hub LLP and Anirit Agritech Private Limited generically, without naming which KMP
    or relative controls them. Note 44.B(4)(i), p.124: Anirit Agritech Private Limited — Purchase of
    property, plant and equipment ₹5.00 Lakhs (FY24; Nil FY25); Note 44.C, p.126: Other financial
    liabilities ₹0.90 Lakhs (FY24, now Nil). 🟢 Immaterial in size and now fully unwound (Nil balance
    FY25), but the notes never disclose which director/KMP or relative controls these entities — a minor
    disclosure-quality gap consistent with the broader observation (Pass 1) that RPT categorization is
    sometimes generic rather than name-specific.
