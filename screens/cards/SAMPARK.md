## Business Understanding Narrative

**What does it do, and how does it make money?** Sampark is a B2B carrying and forwarding agent for OEMs and industrial customers (RHP, p.21). It moves part-truck and full-truck loads by road, air cargo across 47 airports, and rail (Deck, pp.7, 10). It also runs 8 leased warehouses of 1,24,500 sq ft (AR26, p.11). FY26 revenue was Rs 223.27 crore and profit Rs 10.53 crore (FY26 res, p.6). Air was 43% of revenue, surface 47%, rail 6%, warehousing 3%, umbrella 1% (Deck, p.7). Automobile customers gave 67% (Deck, p.7). The money is a spread. Revenue less operating expenses paid to vendors left 23.9% in FY26 (derived: FY26 res, p.6). The fleet is mostly hired; the company owns 56 vehicles (RHP, p.29).

**What is changing?** Two claims. First, mix: air rose from 29% of turnover in FY24 to 39% in FY25 (RHP, p.264) and 43% in FY26 (Deck, p.7). Second, an "umbrella supplier" model introduced in 2026 (AR26, p.14). Sampark sources, checks and ships parts for a client under one contract. It booked Rs 1.67 crore of umbrella revenue in FY26, all of it export earnings (AR26, pp.69, 75). The Deck names one "global US major" as the client (Deck, p.11).

**Why now?** H2 FY26 revenue rose 35.5% to Rs 127.29 crore and profit 56% to Rs 6.57 crore (FY26 res, p.6). EBITDA margin reached 9.0% for FY26 against 4.4% in FY23 (AR26, p.39; RHP, p.142). The IPO added about Rs 27.2 crore of equity (AR26, p.18).

**What must be true?** Revenue growth must hold in a first half, not only in a second half. Receivables must stop growing faster than sales. The IPO cash must replace 14% to 17.5% unsecured loans, not fund longer credit to customers. The umbrella line must earn more than the core spread.

**What breaks it?** H1 FY27 repeats H1 FY26, when revenue fell 10.3%. Receivable days pass 180 and a large customer delays payment. The finance seat churns again before the first half-year audit under the new CFO.

## 2. Business model and archetype

One segment, logistics services (FY26 res, p.9). FY26 revenue by note: freight Rs 195.13 crore net plus Rs 18.88 crore unbilled freight, warehousing Rs 7.58 crore, umbrella Rs 1.67 crore (AR26, p.69). Operating expenses were Rs 169.94 crore, of which freight charges Rs 136.44 crore (AR26, p.70). The spread was 23.9% in FY26 and 23.3% in FY25 (derived: FY26 res, p.6).

Concentration is moderate. The top customer gave 9.56% of 9M FY26 revenue and the top ten 34.34% (RHP, p.150). The top supplier took 25.15% of costs and the top ten 60.99% (RHP, p.150). Haryana, Tamil Nadu and Maharashtra gave 46.86% of 9M FY26 revenue (RHP, p.30).

**Archetype: Outsourcing partner** (client concentration, wallet share, capacity fill, contract stickiness, price per unit). Management's own levers are wallet share and network throughput (Deck, p.9). The core freight line carries **commodity-converter** traits: a spread over hired capacity, with "price often is the deciding factor in most tender awards" (RHP, p.151).

## 3. Competitive advantages

**Network reach: present, not scarce.** 50+ hubs, 14,000+ pin codes, 47 airports (Deck, p.8). Rivals can rent the same trucks and air capacity. **Switching costs: claimed.** The company claims "high switching costs" (AR26, p.16). The RHP calls the industry "highly commoditized" with low entry barriers (RHP, p.151). **Pricing power: absent.** The spread moved 0.6 points in a year (step 2). **Technology: thin.** The RHP says the company has no integrated software system (RHP, p.32). It lists 4 IT staff among 344 (RHP, p.152). An ERP of Rs 1.06 crore was capitalised in FY26 (AR26, p.67).

**The umbrella margin is not richer yet.** Umbrella revenue Rs 167.04 lakh less umbrella expenses Rs 127.97 lakh leaves 23.4% (AR26, pp.69-70). The core spread is 23.9%. [INFERENCE] The claim of a "structurally richer margin profile" (AR26, p.39) is not visible at gross level.

**Verdict: moat absent; a forming mix advantage in time-critical air cargo.**

## 6. The transition and the quality ladder

**FROM: R1, commodity price-taker, upper end.** Spread work on hired capacity, price-led tenders (RHP, p.151), margin 4.4% in FY23. ROCE near 21% runs on debt-funded receivables, not pricing power.

**TO, as claimed: R3, value-added spec'd supplier.** An "umbrella" orchestrator with "richer realisations, longer contract tenures, and higher switching costs" (AR26, p.39).

Management's claim, once: "the improvement in profitability in FY26 was a structural improvement in mix and network utilisation" (AR26, p.19).

Ladder check. R1 to R3 is two rungs. The base rate is one rung per 2 to 3 years, so the claim is itself a red flag. The umbrella line is 0.75% of revenue at the core spread (step 3). The evidenced step is smaller: R1 toward R2 on air mix and network density, margin 4.4% to 9.0% in three years.

## 8. Proof check

**Half-yearly reporter: the two-quarter test becomes a two-half-year test.** The last two halves move in opposite directions. H1 FY26 revenue fell 10.3% and PAT 4.7% against H1 FY25. H2 FY26 revenue rose 35.5% and PAT 56% against H2 FY25 (derived: FY26 res, p.6). One half proves growth. One half shows decline.

**The seasonality story conflicts.** The Deck says "the second half is seasonally SIL's stronger period" (Deck, p.6). The RHP, 24 days earlier, says "Our business is not seasonal in nature" (RHP, p.269). FY25 had the stronger first half: Rs 107.03 crore against Rs 93.94 crore (derived: FY26 res, p.6).

**Cash does not confirm the earnings.** H2 profit rose while FY26 receivables grew Rs 17.17 crore (FY26 res, p.8). The observation that separates the readings is H1 FY27 against H1 FY26: revenue and profit up, with debtor days below 173.

**Proof point: absent.**

- **Ugliness: STRUCTURAL-FEATURE.** Debtor days rose four years running: 101, 151, 161, 173 (RHP, p.98; step 5). The rise predates the 2026 umbrella pivot. The model funds customer credit with 14% to 17.5% loans. That reads as how the business works, not a cost of climbing.
- **Recognition gap: OPEN.** Trailing PE 15.3 (MD) sits below the R3 neighbourhood of about 19x. On the step 13 trend estimate, forward PE is about 12.1x.

**Posture: VALUE-TRAP RISK.** The matrix reads DEEP WATCH or AVOID unless the classification is disproven. The disproof is H1 FY27 debtor days below 160 with positive operating cash flow after the IPO money arrived.

## 12. Verdict card

**WATCH.**

A Rs 161 crore logistics forwarder trades at 15.3x trailing and about 12x a trend FY27. Profit rose 26% in FY26. Against that: the growth sits in one half, cash conversion is about 12% of profit over four years, and the finance seat changed within a month of listing. The H1 FY27 filing is two weeks away and is the test.

**Load-bearing facts to verify first**
1. H1 FY27 revenue and PAT against Rs 95.98 crore and Rs 3.96 crore (FY26 res, p.6).
2. Debtor days and operating cash flow at 30-Sep-2026, against 173 days and Rs 1.81 crore (AR26, p.68; FY26 res, p.8).
3. Use of the Rs 27.2 crore IPO money: working capital, debt repayment, or idle (RHP, p.96; Reg 32 statement, not held).
4. Any trade since FY25 with Fretex, OSPT, First Flight Air Freight or Sampark Couriers, and where the OSPT advance went (RHP, pp.25, 197).

**What would change the view**
- Up, to `/step1`: H1 FY27 revenue up more than 15% and PAT above Rs 5 crore, with positive half-year operating cash flow and finance cost below Rs 1.79 crore.
- Down to PASS: H1 FY27 revenue below H1 FY26; debtor days above 180; another KMP exit; a qualified or delayed half-year review.
