## SECTION 1: MARKET DEFINITION

### 1A. Precise boundaries

**Product scope (three sub-markets, matching B04's three operating verticals):**
1. **Senior Living / senior housing real estate** — for-sale senior
   residences (finance-lease income), development-management (DM) fee on
   partner-developed towers, and post-possession maintenance/club/F&B
   annuity. Excludes ordinary (non-age-restricted) residential real estate.
2. **Assisted Care** — organized, fee-paying residential care homes
   (assisted living, transition/rehab care, memory care) plus care-at-home
   clinical services (nursing, physiotherapy, personal/critical care).
   Excludes informal/family-provided care and unpaid caregiving (the vast
   majority of India's actual elder-care activity, but not a monetizable
   TAM by definition).
3. **Senior-focused consumer products (AGEasy)** — branded, senior-specific
   retail products across joint pain, lung health, fall prevention/detection
   and gut health (91 products/159 SKUs), sold D2C + marketplace + B2B.
   Excludes prescription pharmaceuticals, hospital/OPD spend, and diagnostics
   — these are adjacent categories seniors also spend on, but they are not
   AGEasy's product scope (this distinction turns out to matter a great deal
   in Section 2's mgmt-claim check).

**Geographic scope:** India only (this run does not evaluate the export
angle noted in unrelated peer commentary). Company's actual current
footprint is metro-concentrated: Senior Living — Noida, Dehradun, Gurugram
(Chandigarh announced); Assisted Care — NCR, Bengaluru, Chennai (8
facilities); AGEasy — pan-India online (D2C, marketplaces), some
offline/B2B.

**Customer scope:** organized/paid senior care is, by construction, an
urban, financially-independent-or-family-subsidized customer base — not the
full 162.2m elderly population, but the "Sec A/B" urban paying segment
management itself uses for AGEasy (~1.5 Cr / 15 million people, Inv. Pres.
p.28 — see 1B below).

**Channel scope:** direct sale + DM/JV partnership (Senior Living); direct
lease + hospital-referral/TPA (Assisted Care); D2C website + e-commerce
marketplace + offline/B2B (AGEasy).

**Price segment:** mid-premium to premium (₹16,000-22,000/sqft senior
residences; ₹6,100/day ARPOB care homes; ₹1,000+ AOV consumer products) —
explicitly NOT the low-cost/affordable senior-housing segment nor the
free/subsidized/NGO elder-care segment, both of which exist in India and are
excluded from this TAM.

**Explicit exclusions:** unorganized/informal elder care (family caregiving,
unregistered old-age homes) — sized only as a formalization opportunity, not
counted in TAM; general (all-age) home healthcare/medical-equipment demand;
general (all-age) OTC pharmaceutical market; hospitals and diagnostics
(CareEdge's broader "specialised senior care" scope includes these —
excluded here as not part of MAXIND's product scope, see Method 5).

## SECTION 2: TAM ESTIMATION, MULTIPLE METHODS (SUM-OF-PARTS)

### Sub-market 1 — Senior Living (housing + DM fee + annuity)

**Method 1, top-down (multiple industry reports, triangulated):**

|---|---|---|---|

Confidence: **M** (three independent commercial research houses converge
within a ~2x band on both current and 2030 values; no single government/ICRA
figure found for this specific sub-segment).

**Method 2, bottom-up (units × penetration × price):** Organized senior
housing supply = 22,157 units nationally as of Jun-2025 (up from 7,147 in
2014), concentrated in southern metros (JLL-ASLI) — note MAXIND has **zero**
senior-living projects in south India, a real SAM constraint (Section 3).
Demand estimated at 1.7m urban, financially-independent-senior households
(2025) rising to 2.3m by 2030 (JLL-ASLI/Anarock), i.e. current organized
penetration of demand is ~1.3% (22,157/1.7m) — this matches the AR's own
1.3% penetration citation almost exactly, a good internal-consistency check.
At the company's own achieved price point (~₹16,000-22,000/sqft, ~1,500-2,900
sqft units, Inv. Pres. pp.11,17) full-penetration bottom-up TAM ≈ 1.7m
demand-units × ~₹25-40 lakh average unit-linked revenue (sale spread over
lease-recognition + DM fee + annuity, blended) ≈ ₹42,500-68,000 Cr — broadly
consistent with the top-down realistic band. Confidence: **M**.

**Method 3, peer aggregation (organized players, + unorganized estimate):**
Known organized operators: MAXIND/Antara (₹92-97 Cr FY26 Senior Living
segment revenue, implied from 45.7% of ₹213.4 Cr per B04 mix — **anchored,
B04**), Columbia Pacific Communities/Serene Communities (India's largest
senior-living operator by units — ~1,700 units/2,400 seniors under
management per company website/press, FY revenue **NOT FOUND** in public
search), plus a long tail (Primus Life, Ashiana, Aakruti, others, revenues
**NOT FOUND**). Peer revenue aggregation cannot be completed to a reliable
total — organized players' disclosed revenues are too sparse/private for a
bottom-up sum; this method is downgraded to **confidence L, directional
only**, and the top-down/bottom-up methods (1, 2) carry the triangulation.

**Method 5, global benchmark:** India penetration of organized senior
housing vs demand: ~1.3% (AR) / ~1.5% (Inv. Pres. p.155) vs USA 6-7%, New
Zealand 14-15% (Inv. Pres. p.156-157). If India reached even half of the US
rate over a decade, the implied market would be several multiples of the
2030 estimates above — directionally supportive of the growth case but not
used as a numeric input given its long, uncertain horizon (confidence **L**
for the number, **H** for the direction).

**Triangulation — Senior Living current TAM:**
- Conservative: $1.8bn × ₹96.5 = **₹17,370 Cr**
- Realistic: ₹25,000 Cr (JLL-ASLI's own directly-stated INR figure for the
  current organized market — no FX round-trip needed, most direct anchor)

**Senior Living TAM growth to 2030 (for 5B/tam_growth_pct only, not part of
headline current TAM):** conservative $8bn (₹77,200 Cr) to aggressive
$12-14.14bn (₹1,15,800-1,36,451 Cr); consensus CAGR ~**33-35%**.

### Sub-market 2 — Assisted Care (care homes + care-at-home)

No single clean "assisted care" market-size report was found; built from
two components, each flagged for methodology.

**Component A — residential assisted/nursing/memory care (top-down,
residual):** Mordor's "senior living market" is explicitly segmented by
property type into independent living (64.5% share, 2025) vs assisted
living + memory care + nursing care (35.5% complement). Applying that split
to Mordor's $3.55bn (2025)/$4.47bn (2026) senior-living total: 35.5% ×
$3.55bn = $1.26bn (₹12,157 Cr, conservative); 35.5% × $4.47bn = $1.587bn
(₹15,315 Cr, realistic). Confidence **M** (derived split, not a
directly-reported assisted-care-only figure).

**Component B — care-at-home (top-down, flagged assumption):** India home
healthcare market estimates diverge sharply by scope/methodology: broad
estimates of $11.9-17.2bn (2025, Mordor/IMARC — includes medical
equipment/devices and all-age nursing, far broader than MAXIND's clinical
care-at-home scope) vs a narrower organized/branded estimate of **"above
₹12,000 Cr"** (search snippet, source/date imprecise — flagged **low
confidence, imprecise vintage**). Given the scope mismatch, the broad
billion-dollar figures are **not used** for the headline (would imply
₹1,00,000+ Cr just from care-at-home, implausible against 485-bed-scale
organized reality) and are kept only as directional upper-bound context.
Applying a senior-specific share to the narrower ₹12,000 Cr figure requires
an assumption not independently sourced: **50% senior-share is an internal
modeling assumption, flagged as such** (not a company financial figure —
this is TAM-methodology estimation as the stage instructions require,
distinct from fabricating an anchored number) — giving ₹6,000 Cr
conservative / ₹7,200 Cr (60% share) realistic.

**Assisted Care sub-market TAM:**
- Conservative: ₹12,157 Cr + ₹6,000 Cr = **₹18,157 Cr** (round ₹18,150 Cr)

### 3B. SOM at 3 and 5 years — arithmetic shown

Current SAM share = FY26 revenue ÷ SAM = ₹213.4 Cr ÷ ₹17,500 Cr = **1.22%**.

Share-gain assumption: the organized share of India's senior-care market is
low single digits at most (implied by 1.3-1.5% housing penetration and <0.5%
specialised-facility penetration, CareEdge) — i.e. unorganized/informal share
is comfortably >40%, so the stage's "faster possible where unorganized share
>40% is formalizing" carve-out applies. Company is simultaneously
capacity-building (485 beds up from 333 in one quarter, E361 Gurugram
launched, fresh capital via rights issue + warrants ₹80.35 Cr, 23-Jul-2026)
— this supports the **aggressive** (3-5pp) band rather than the normal
1-2pp band, but EBITDA losses persisting to FY27/28 (per operator-context
guidance) argue against going beyond the low end of aggressive. Headline
picks the low-to-mid aggressive band:
- 3-year cumulative share gain: **+3.0pp** → SOM share = 1.22% + 3.0pp = 4.22%
- 5-year cumulative share gain: **+5.0pp** (capped at the top of the
  "aggressive" band per the stated rule — not pushed past 5pp because there
  is no evidence of competitor exit/acquisition, only formalization tailwind)
  → SOM share = 1.22% + 5.0pp = 6.22%

**SOM_3yr = 4.22% × ₹17,500 Cr = ₹738 Cr**
**SOM_5yr = 6.22% × ₹17,500 Cr = ₹1,089 Cr**

Implied revenue CAGR (FORMAL handoff to Stage 11):
- Year 3: (738 / 213.4)^(1/3) − 1 = (3.459)^0.333 − 1 = **51.2%**
- Year 5: (1,089 / 213.4)^(1/5) − 1 = (5.104)^0.20 − 1 = **38.5%**

(Year-3 CAGR is higher than year-5 CAGR because the share-gain trajectory is
front-loaded — only +1.8pp of incremental gain in years 4-5 vs +3.0pp in
years 1-3 — and the year-5 figure compounds off the larger year-3 base; this
is a normal S-curve pattern, not an arithmetic error, and is flagged here so
Stage 11 doesn't read it as one.)

**Sensitivity (shown, not headline):** at the "normal" 2pp/3yr,
+3pp cumulative/5yr band: SOM_3yr = 3.22% × 17,500 = ₹564 Cr (CAGR 38.3%);
SOM_5yr = 4.22% × 17,500 = ₹738 Cr (CAGR 28.2%). The headline aggressive case
is roughly 30-45% above the normal case at both horizons — a real, material
choice, disclosed rather than hidden.

## SECTION 4: GROWTH DRIVERS, RISKS & STRUCTURE

### 4A. TAM growth drivers

| Driver | Impact | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Demographics | **High** | 60+ population 162.2m (2025) → 191.5m (2030) → 346-374m (2050), doubling by mid-century (multiple sources, converging) |
| Formalisation (unorganized→organized) | **High** | Organized housing penetration only 1.3-1.5% vs 6-15% in mature markets; <0.5% specialised-facility penetration (CareEdge); >95% of actual elder care remains informal |
| Nuclear-family / social shift | **Medium-High** | 70% nuclear families / rising child mobility (Inv. Pres. p.131); "kids pay, parents utilise" decision dynamic explicitly named by management as both a driver and a friction (complex decision cycles) |
| Per-capita/premiumisation | **Medium** | 78% of elderly have no pension yet NPS AUM up 226% (Inv. Pres. p.133-134) — rising financial capacity among the *children* who fund care, not the seniors themselves |
| Regulatory/policy tailwind | **Medium** | Union Budget 2026-27: 1,00,000 allied professionals + 1.5 lakh caregivers to be trained over 5 years (CareEdge, Apr-2026) — workforce-side enabler, not demand-side |
| Chronic-disease burden | **Medium** | 75% of elderly suffer chronic illness, 24% cannot perform daily activities independently (Inv. Pres. p.136-137) — directly expands Assisted Care and AGEasy addressable pools |
| New applications / technology enablement | **Low-Medium** | AGEasy's AI-driven recommendation engine, telemedicine, EHR build-out (Inv. Pres. pp.28-31) — early-stage, not yet a proven demand lever |

### 4B. TAM risks

| Risk | Monitoring signal |
|---|---|

### 4C. Market structure

## SECTION 5: SUMMARY & RUNWAY

### 5A. Funnel (₹ Cr, conservative basis)

```
TAM (India, sum-of-parts, current)  ............ ₹45,460 Cr  (realistic: ₹62,500 Cr)
   -> SAM (product/geo/channel/customer/capability filters) . ₹17,500 Cr  (38.5% of TAM)
      -> Current revenue (FY26) .................. ₹213.4 Cr  (1.22% of SAM)
      -> SOM, 3-year (aggressive share-gain, +3.0pp) .. ₹738 Cr   (4.22% of SAM)
      -> SOM, 5-year (aggressive share-gain, +5.0pp) .. ₹1,089 Cr (6.22% of SAM)
```

### 5B. Runway assessment

- Revenue headroom = SAM ÷ current revenue = 17,500 ÷ 213.4 = **~82x**
- TAM growth rate (blended, weighted by conservative sub-market TAM shares:
  Senior Living 38% × ~33% CAGR, Assisted Care 40% × ~27.35% CAGR (assisted
  living segment CAGR, Mordor), AGEasy 22% × ~7% CAGR) ≈ **~25%** (2025-2030
  horizon, blended)
- Company actual growth (FY26 revenue +30% YoY, anchored) is **modestly
  ahead of** the blended TAM growth rate (~25%) — the company is gaining
  share on the market, not merely riding it, though the margin of
  outperformance (5pp) is not large and could compress if the faster-growing
  Assisted Care/Senior Living legs decelerate from their current low base.
- Years to saturate SAM at current growth (30% YoY): n = ln(17,500/213.4) /
  ln(1.30) = ln(82.05)/ln(1.30) = 4.407/0.2624 ≈ **16.8 years** — a very long
  runway before SAM constrains growth.

### 5C. Runway classification: **MASSIVE**

Basis: ~82x revenue headroom, ~25% blended TAM growth, company already
outgrowing the market, and ~17 years to SAM saturation at current pace — all
four signals point to the top band of the standard matrix. The classification
should be read alongside the caveats already flagged above: the Assisted
Care and AGEasy TAM components carry L-M confidence (Section 2), and the
SOM_3yr figure sits ahead of what disclosed capacity/pipeline can bottom-up
support (Section 3C) — MASSIVE describes the addressable opportunity, not a
guarantee that the company's own capital and execution can capture it on the
faster of the two SOM timelines shown.

### 5D. SAM expansion levers actually being pursued

- **Geographic**: Senior Living has zero south/west India presence despite
  JLL noting organized supply is concentrated in the south — Chandigarh
  (announced) is the only new geography in the pipeline; a genuine south
  entry (Bengaluru/Chennai/Hyderabad, where Assisted Care already operates)
  would be additive. Directional estimate only (**not independently
  sourced**): if Senior Living's geographic SAM filter loosened from ~30% to
  ~45% of its TAM, that alone adds ~₹2,600 Cr to SAM.
- **AGEasy category expansion**: Gut Health launched FY26 (4th condition);
  IIT Delhi/Wellbeing Nutrition/boAt partnerships (operator-context,
  non-anchored) suggest further category adds are actively being pursued,
  not merely aspirational.
- **Care-at-Home national scale-up**: currently layered onto the 8
  Care-Home cities; HSSC accreditation for Care-at-Home NCR (operator-context)
  signals a formalization/accreditation push that could support geographic
  widening.
- **Bancassurance/insurance bundling**: Star Union Dai-ichi Life alliance
  (operator-context, non-anchored) — a potential channel lever to convert
  the "no pension/18% health-insured" affordability gap (Inv. Pres. p.133-135)
  into a distribution advantage, though too early to size.
- Combining the above qualitatively (geography + category + channel), a
  plausible revised SAM is in the **₹24,000-27,000 Cr** range within 3-5
  years if all three levers land — revised revenue headroom ~113-127x. This
  is a directional, non-anchored planning estimate, clearly flagged as such.

### 5E. Final output card

At **38.5%-51.2%** revenue CAGR implied by SOM (5-year / 3-year
respectively), against a margin trajectory that management guides toward
15-20% EBITDA at AGEasy maturity (₹400-500 Cr scale), 16-20% EBITDA per
mature Care Home, and 20-25% EBITDA on Senior Living maintenance income at
steady state (operator-context, non-anchored guidance, consistent in
direction with the FY26 EBITDA-loss narrowing already anchored: -₹83.1 Cr
FY26 vs -₹99.5 Cr FY25) — **the earnings growth embedded here cannot be
stated as a single CAGR number, because the base is currently negative
(EBITDA loss)**; forcing one would violate the "never estimate a missing
number" discipline. What can be said: **P/E is not meaningful** — MAXIND is
loss-making and its own valuation framework (B04) is explicitly SOTP, not a
consolidated P/E multiple. On the SOTP basis, the SOM-implied revenue
growth **supports** the segment-level breakeven/profitability targets
(senior living & AGEasy breakeven guided FY27, consolidated profitability
FY28) only on the year-5 (38.5% CAGR) reading, which is closer to what
Section 3C's bottom-up capacity check can support; the year-3 (51.2% CAGR)
reading is **not** well-supported by disclosed capacity and should be
treated as the upper, execution-dependent case rather than the base case
Stage 11 anchors on.
