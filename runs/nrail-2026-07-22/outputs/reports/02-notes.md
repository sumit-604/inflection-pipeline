## PATTERN PASS (Pass 3 lens: contradictions, mismatches, deliberate vagueness, restatements, post-balance-sheet events, going concern)

1. **Receivables and payables spikes are timing-concentrated, not obviously linked to a common counterparty.** Trade receivables +114.4% (Note 10, pp.127–128) and trade payables +266.9% (Note 25, pp.139–141) both show closing-balance growth far outpacing the turnover-ratio-implied average-balance growth (Note 57, p.162: receivables turnover +5.08%, payables turnover −8.69%), confirming both spikes are concentrated very close to FY25 year-end. No note discloses a shared customer/vendor or transaction that explains both simultaneously. This remains genuinely **NOT FOUND IN DOCUMENT** as a causal link; flagged as a pattern worth a direct management question (see Section D). Anchors: Note 10 (pp.127–128), Note 25 (pp.139–141), Note 57 (p.162).

2. **Internal contradiction confirmed**: Note 57's own narrative (p.162) attributes the Current Ratio decline to "increase in trade Receivables in current assets" — an asset-side increase that would mechanically improve, not worsen, the current ratio. The actual driver disclosed in the same sentence (increase in current maturities of long-term borrowings, a liability) is correct; the receivables clause is drafting imprecision, not a numeric restatement. 🟡. Anchor: Note 57 (p.162).

3. **Deliberately minimal disclosure vs. surrounding detail**: the whistleblower reference in CARO (para xi(c), p.94) is a single sentence with no elaboration anywhere else in a report that otherwise discloses granular detail (down to ₹0.04L unquoted investments and single-digit-lakh actuarial sensitivities). This asymmetry — high precision elsewhere, total silence on a governance-sensitive item — is itself a pattern-pass observation. 🟡. Anchor: CARO Annexure A para xi(c) (p.94).

4. **Prior-year restatement / regrouping**: Note 62 confirms prior-year figures were "regrouped/reclassified wherever necessary" but discloses no quantified restatement amount. No restated line item was identified anywhere in the cross-check between Pass 1/Pass 2 figures and the FY24 comparatives shown throughout. **No material restatement found.**

5. **Events after balance sheet date**: no quantified financial event disclosed (Director's Report, p.45, standard negative assurance). MD&A references to a new FBB product launch and the India-UK FTA (pp.8, 14) are qualitative/unaudited and outside the Notes. **No material post-balance-sheet event found in the Notes.**

6. **Going concern**: no going-concern doubt language anywhere in the Notes, Auditor's Report, CARO (para xix, p.95), or Directors' Responsibility Statement (p.47). **NONE.**

Net result of the pattern pass: no new standalone red flag beyond what Passes 1–2 already surfaced, but items 1–3 above sharpen three existing Watch items into pattern-level observations and are folded into the consolidated findings below (primarily items 4 and 15 in Section A).

### D. FIVE QUESTIONS FOR MANAGEMENT

1. What is the interest rate, tenor, and repayment schedule on the ₹353.50L new unsecured director loans drawn in FY25, and why did "Interest paid to Directors" remain flat at ₹10.39L despite this new balance (Note 18, p.133; Note 40, p.147)?
2. What valuation methodology and independent valuer basis supports the ₹28.00Cr Level 3 carrying value of the NSE India Ltd stake, and what is the strategic rationale for this non-core capital allocation at a time gearing rose from 41% to 44% (Note 4, p.121; Note 44a, p.153)?
3. What is the nature, number, and resolution status of the whistleblower complaints referenced in the CARO report (para xi(c), p.94) but not detailed in the Director's Report's Vigil Mechanism section or elsewhere?
4. What is driving the near-simultaneous, year-end-concentrated spikes in trade receivables (+114.4%) and trade payables (+266.9%, largely acceptances/vendor financing) — is there a common customer, vendor, or transaction timing driver, and what portion of the payables increase is expected to reverse or recur in FY26 (Note 10, pp.127–128; Note 25, pp.139–141)?
5. What is management's quantified forecast basis for future taxable profits supporting the new ₹22.30Cr deferred tax asset recognised on carried-forward tax losses (Note 22, p.138), given FY25 PBT collapsed 88.4% YoY, and how does this reconcile with the FY26 revenue guidance referenced qualitatively in the MD&A (p.8)?

### E. NOTES-BASED RED FLAGS

- **Earnings management / earnings-quality risk (not classic manipulation, but material misrepresentation risk if read at headline level)**: Non-operating and non-recurring gains (₹1,942.01L, Note 31 + Cash Flow Statement) exceed the entire reported PBT (₹1,595.62L); "Profit on Sale of Raw Material" (Note 30, p.142) is classified within Revenue from Operations rather than Other Income, modestly flattering top-line growth optics.
- **Aggressive accounting / judgment risk**: New ₹22.30Cr deferred tax asset recognised on tax losses in the same year PBT collapsed 88.4% (Note 22, p.138), resting on unquantified future-profitability judgment; ECL allowance on trade receivables held flat despite the receivables base doubling (Note 10, pp.127–128).
- **Undisclosed risk indicators**: NSE India Ltd (₹28Cr, Level 3) valuation methodology not disclosed (Note 4, p.121; Note 44a, p.153); director-loan interest rate not disclosed and unreconciled against flat interest-paid figure (Note 18, p.133; Note 40, p.147); whistleblower complaints referenced but not detailed (CARO para xi(c), p.94).

### F. ONE-LINE NOTES VERDICT

The notes reveal moderate to concerning accounting practices. Key concern: combined non-operating and non-recurring pre-tax gains of ₹19.42 Cr (investment fair-value gains plus profit on asset sales) exceed FY25's entire reported PBT of ₹15.96 Cr, implying the core paper business likely ran a pre-tax loss before these items, compounded by a receivables-and-payables-financed working capital cycle and a collapse in interest coverage from 17.60x to 2.34x. Key strength: disclosure is granular and internally consistent enough to allow this reconstruction from the notes themselves, with no going-concern doubt, no quantified restatements, and a clean audit trail. Overall accounting quality: 5/10.
