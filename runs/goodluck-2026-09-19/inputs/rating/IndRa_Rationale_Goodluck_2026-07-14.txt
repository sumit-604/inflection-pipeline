Analytical Approach
Ind-Ra has taken a fully consolidated view of GIL and its subsidiary, Goodluck Defence and Aerospace Limited (GDAL; GIL holds 79.43%
stake), jointly known as the Goodluck group, to arrive at the ratings, as the companies have common directors and banker, with cash
fungibility with each other, both the entities operate in a similar line of business, and GIL has strong financial and strategic linkages with
GDAL; additionally, the treasury operations are integrated. GDAL commenced operations in October 2025. Furthermore, GIL will be
infusing funds for GDAL’s planned capacity expansion over FY27-FY28.
Detailed Rationale of the Rating Action
The rating reflects the strong financial and strategic linkages between GIL and GDAL. The rating factors in the group’s healthy operating
performance, reflected in its strong consolidated revenue base and absolute EBITDA generation in FY26, which are likely to improve
further post the ramp-up of GDAL’s operations from FY27. The group benefits from a diversified business profile across multiple end-user
industries and product segments. Furthermore, the share of value-added and higher-margin products is likely to increase over the medium
term, supported by ongoing capacity expansions. This favourable shift in the product mix is likely to strengthen the consolidated
profitability and enhance the overall business profile of the group.
However, the rating is constrained by the likely peaking of net leverage (net debt/operating EBITDA) during FY27-FY28, primarily due to
the planned debt-led capex in GDAL and GIL for the expansion of capacities. The capex will be funded through a mix of external debt,
contribution from GIL, and internal accruals from GDAL. Ind-Ra expects a gradual improvement in the net leverage from FY29, led by an
increase in profitability, supported by volume improvement post timely ramp-up of the new capacities, and scheduled repayments of long-
term debt. The rating remains constrained by the intense competition and inherent cyclicality in the industry.

Detailed Description of Key Rating Drivers
Diversified Business Profile; Low Customer Concentration Risk: The group operates across four major segments - engineering
structure & precision fabrication; forgings; precision pipes and auto tubes; and cold formed profile, CR coils pipes & tubes within
GIL, which has a combined capacity of 500,000 tonnes per annum (TPA) across its core business segments. It has also
strategically entered into a high-margin defence segment through its subsidiary, GDAL. In addition, GIL has been progressively
focusing on increasing the revenue share of value-added products, which is likely to support margin expansion and improve the
overall business. The company has a healthy export exposure, with exports contributing 27% to the total revenue in FY26 (FY25:

25% and FY24: 27%). Additionally, GIL has been able to record a steady increase in its sales volumes on a yoy basis (FY26:
468,161 tonnes; FY25: 434,323 tonnes and FY24: 369,507 tonnes) The group benefits from low customer concentration, with none
of the customers contributing more than 10% to the total revenue. Furthermore, it has been able to maintain steady relationships
with marquee clients such as Larsen and Toubro Limited (debt rated at ‘IND AAA’/Stable/‘IND A1+’); Reliance Industry Limited (debt
rated at ‘IND AAA’/Stable/‘IND A1+’); ALF Engineering Private Limited; and Salem Steel North America LLC. These steady
relationships have resulted in stable and repeat business, providing revenue visibility and reducing dependency on any single
customer.
Operating And Financial Performance to Remain Healthy, Likely To Be Supported By Value Added Products: GIL has
demonstrated a consistent improvement in its financial performance. The consolidated revenue grew to INR41,003 million in FY26
(FY25: INR39,359 million; FY24: INR35,248 million), and the absolute EBITDA increased to INR3,983 million (INR3,108 million;
INR2,822 million). The operating EBITDA margin expanded to 9.7% in FY26 (FY25: 7.9%; FY24: 8%), driven by a favourable shift in
product mix toward value-added segments and improved operational efficiencies. GIL’s EBIDTA per tonne improved to
INR8,506/tonne in FY26 (FY25: INR7156/ tonne; FY24: INR7638/tonne), led by better absorption of fixed costs and an
improvement in the product mix. In the medium term, the company’s revenue growth and EBITDA margins are likely to be
supported by the increasing contribution of high-margin defence products, particularly as GDAL scales up its operations. In addition,
the ramp-up of existing capacities along with the commissioning of new capacities is likely to drive higher volumes and improve
operating leverage. Overall, the combined impact of capacity expansion, improved utilisation levels, and a gradual transition toward
higher-margin businesses is likely to strengthen both topline growth and margin profile over the medium term.
Commencement of Capacity and Likely Ramp-up of operations in GDAL: Under its defence subsidiary, GDAL, the company
commissioned an initial capacity of 150,000 shells per annum, which became operational in October 2025. During FY26, GDAL
earned a revenue of INR414 million. In FY27 till date, GDAL has secured two orders with an aggregate value of approximately
INR3,072 million (one order of about INR522 million and another order of about INR2,550 million). The orders are scheduled to be
executed within a timeline of three-to-10 months. These orders provide revenue visibility for FY27 and are likely to facilitate the
ramp-up of GDAL's existing capacity utilisation.
Considering the ramp of operations and receipt of orders, the company has planned a significant scale-up of capacity to 400,000
shells, with the expanded capacity likely to become operational between September-October 2027.
Net Leverage Likely to Peak during FY27–FY28, Followed By Gradual Deleveraging: Given the ongoing debt-funded capex,
the company’s consolidated net leverage is likely to increase in the near term, with a projected peak of around 3.5x during FY27–
FY28, depending on the timing and quantum of debt drawdowns for machinery and project execution. However, the net leverage is
likely to gradually reduce thereafter, supported by the operationalisation of the expanded capacities, the shift towards high-margin
products, and scheduled repayment of term loans. GDAL’s capacity expansion project involves a total project cost of approximately
INR4,000 million, out of which around INR2,750 million is proposed to be funded through term loans, for which GIL will be providing
corporate guarantee, and the debt tie-up has been completed. The balance requirement will be met through funding from GIL and
internal accruals generated within GDAL.
Additionally, GIL has undertaken capex of around INR700 million to increase its core capacity to 600,000TPA from 500,000TPA. Of
the total capex, approximately INR250 million is proposed to be funded through term loans, while the remaining investment will be
financed through internal accruals. The additional capacity is likely to become operational from 4QFY27 onwards, which is likely to
support volume growth and improve operating leverage in the medium term.
Given the likely increase in revenue and the healthy order book, the working capital requirements are likely to increase over the
medium term. GIL’s consolidated debt profile reflects a moderately leveraged capital structure, with borrowings largely aligned to
working capital requirements and term loans availed and to be availed for capacity expansion. In FY26, GIL witnessed an increase
in its working capital requirement to support its operations, which led to an increase in its interest costs; however, the impact of
these factors was largely offset by the rise in EBITDA. The consolidated net leverage (net debt/absolute EBIDTA) was 2.81x in
FY26 (FY25: 2.83x; FY24: 1.80x), and the interest coverage (gross interest cost/absolute EBIDTA) remained comfortable at 3.76x
(3.87x; 3.64x).
Intense Competition; Inherent Industry Risks: The company’s end-user segments primarily include automotive, infrastructure,
and construction sectors, which are closely tied to the economic cycle. Capital expenditure in these industries is influenced by
factors such as government spending, private investment, interest rates, and overall business sentiment. Consequently, any
slowdown in these sectors can directly impact demand for the company’s products. However, the company’s presence across
multiple segments provides a degree of diversification benefit, helping to partially mitigate sector-specific risks, although its overall
performance remains linked to the broader economic environment.

GIL faces intense competition from both domestic and international manufacturers. To mitigate the same, the company entered into
the high-margin defence sector via its subsidiary, GDAL. GIL’s ability to scale up the overall revenue and margin would be a key
monitorable.
Furthermore, the group is exposed to the inherently cyclical nature of the commodity market. Volatility in steel prices can affect the
operating margins, particularly in case of sharp price movements. However, a significant proportion of the company’s sales are
executed through contract-based orders and include price variation clauses, which provide partial protection against fluctuations in
raw material prices and help maintain margin stability.
In the defence segment, despite the higher-margin nature of the contracts, working capital requirements may remain elevated
because of elongated inventory and receivable periods. Furthermore, the defence business remains dependent on government
procurement cycles and policy decisions, which can influence the timing of order inflows and contract execution.
Liquidity
Adequate: The consolidated free cash balance stood at INR10 million at FYE26 (FYE25: INR18 million; FYE24: INR1,049 million). GIL’s
average monthly utilisation of the fund-based limits and non-fund based stood at 85% and at 44%, respectively, during the 12 months
ended April 2026. The utilisation is likely to have remained at similar levels in the subsequent period. The debtor days, including retention
money, stood at 43 days in FY26 (FY25: 47 days; FY24: 36 days). The inventory days increased to 84 days in FY26 (FY25: 63 days;
FY24: 69 days) on account of delays in dispatches. The creditor days reduced to 16 days in FY26 (FY25: 17; FY24: 19). The net working
capital cycle stretched to 111 days in FY26 (FY25: 93 days; FY24: 86 days) due to an increase in inventory days. The working capital
cycle has been elongated as the orders are project-based. Ind-Ra expects the free cash flows to remain negative (FY26: negative
INR2,606 million; FY25: negative INR4,106 million) in the near term due to the capex to be incurred in both GIL and GDAL for the
additional capacities. The debt tie-up of the term loan for GDAL’s capex has been completed. GIL has total repayment obligations of
INR539 million and INR485 million in FY27 and FY28, respectively. The agency has factored in continued support from GIL to GDAL for
the planned capacity expansion, which will remain a key rating monitorable. Any higher-than-Ind-Ra-expected cash outflow to other group
entities or unrelated business will remain a monitorable.
Rating Sensitivities
Positive: The following factors may lead to a positive rating action:
a steady ramp-up in operations on a consolidated basis, supported by value-added products, leading to growth in the scale of profitability;
and
an improvement of the consolidated credit metrics, with the net leverage remaining below 2.00x on a sustained basis
Negative: The following factors may lead to a negative rating action:
any higher-than-Ind-Ra expected debt-funded capex and/or deterioration in liquidity; and/ or
lower-than-Ind-Ra-expected profitability and significant deterioration in the working capital cycle; and/ or
the consolidated net leverage exceeding 3.00x, on a sustained basis.
Any Other Information
Standalone performance: GIL’s revenue was INR40,677 million in FY26 (FY25: INR39,359 million) and its absolute EBIDTA was
INR3,370 million (FY25: INR3,109 million). The net leverage is 2.69x in FY26 (FY25: 2.69x) and interest coverage remained comfortable
at 3.64x (3.87x).
About the Company
GIL is a publicly listed Indian engineering and steel products manufacturer, incorporated in 1986 and headquartered in Ghaziabad, Uttar
Pradesh. The company was founded by Mahesh Chandra Garg. GIL manufactures steel structures, industrial forgings and precision-
engineered products. GIL currently operates with an installed production capacity of 500,000TPA. GDAL, which commenced operations in
October 2025, has an operational capacity of 150,000 shells per annum.
GIL manufactures and supplies a diverse array of products to industries such as automotive, aerospace, oil & gas, infrastructure, power,
and telecom.
