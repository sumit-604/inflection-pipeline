Impact of geopolitical tensions on our business:

East, and this is having a small adverse effect on profitability. During the quarter, commodity 
prices, particularly steel, copper, aluminium, and nickel, skewed up to 10% fluctuation, though

Shipping and logistics for exports and imports have seen delays, reduced availability of ships

been no significant supply disruption. Most of the increased costs are expected to be absorbed 
through normal contingency provisions.

Consolidated financial highlights: 
Consolidated orders in hand as on September 30, 2026 is INR 10,273 crores. This is an all-
time high record for us. Total income for the quarter ended September 2026 is INR 1,860 
crores, about 10% higher than INR 1,696 crores for the quarter ended September 2025. 
Total income for the six months ended September 2026 is INR 3,853 crores, about 25% higher 
than INR 3,071 crores for the six months ended September 2025. Revenue was higher during 
the quarter for Isgec standalone and Isgec Hitachi Zosen, but much lower for Saraswati Sugar 
Mills. Isgec Hitachi Zosen is doing well with higher revenues and good orders in hand of INR 
1,161 crores. Saraswati Sugar Mills had lower cane availability last year, resulting in lower 
production. Accordingly, we are getting lower sales quotas and therefore revenues are lower. 
Saraswati Sugar Mills is taking several steps to improve cane availability in this season.  
Consolidated profit before tax for the quarter ended September 2026 is INR 102 crores, 100% 
higher than the INR 51 crores restated for the quarter ended September 2025. The consolidated 
profit before tax for the six months ended September 2026 of INR 155 crores is 62% higher 
than INR 96 crores restated for the six months ended September 2025. The reported 
consolidated profit figures for the quarter and six months ended September 2025 have been 
restated since the accounting of the Philippines business was shifted from the earlier 
classification as assets held for sale and discontinued operations to continuing operations. 
Let me now explain why consolidated profit before tax is lower than standalone profit before 
tax. The key reason for the reduction in the consolidated profit before tax compared to 
standalone PBT is the loss from the ethanol plant in the Philippines. As you know, the ethanol 
plant started commercial production for the current sugarcane season on December 17, 2025 
using sugarcane as feedstock and concluded sugarcane crushing on April 20, 2026. 
Thereafter, the plant has been operating on molasses as feedstock. The plant has been running 
at about 65-70% capacity, and ethanol sales are going smoothly. During the six months, the 
segment incurred a loss of INR 164 crores including interest. Most of this loss relates to 
interest, depreciation, and forex loss. 
The segment also reported an EBITDA loss during the six months which would have been 
largely recovered if the balance stock of about 3 million litres of ethanol had been sold during 
the quarter. The plant is presently under regular seasonal repair and maintenance and will 
restart when the new sugarcane season starts in December 2026. 
On a consolidated basis, net borrowings reduced by INR 320 crores during the six months to 
INR 156 crores as on September 30, 2026, from INR 476 crores on March 31, 2026.  
I will now talk about the ongoing expansion of capacity at our manufacturing plants. As 
informed, we are investing to add manufacturing capacity across most of our manufacturing 
product lines. This includes presses and machine building at the new plants coming up at 
Bhartoli in Haryana, the new facility to manufacture skids and modules coming up in our SEZ 
at Dahej in Gujarat, and the investment in capacity expansion and machining facilities at both 
our casting factories, the tube and piping shops at Rattangarh, and the standard mechanical 
presses factory at Bawal.

FY 2027 Outlook, Standalone: 
Looking ahead on a standalone basis, we expect FY 2027 revenue to grow by about 15% to 
17%. Margins in the manufacturing business should stay in the range of 12% to 13%, and in 
the projects business should improve to around 6% by the end of the year. Looking ahead, the 
enquiry pipeline and order booking outlook remains strong in both the domestic and export 
markets. The weaker Indian rupee is supporting our competitiveness in export markets. 
We recognize that the broader environment may continue to present both uncertainties and 
opportunities. We are taking steps to mitigate the risks and convert the opportunities into 
tangible benefits.  
To sum up, first, standalone profit grew strongly on better margins in both segments. Second, 
our order book is at an all-time high, which gives us good visibility. Third, our balance sheet is 
stronger with net borrowings nearly halved in six months.
