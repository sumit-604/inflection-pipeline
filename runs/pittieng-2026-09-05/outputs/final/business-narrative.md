# BUSINESS UNDERSTANDING NARRATIVE

Pitti Engineering stamps thin sheets of electrical steel and stacks them into the cores that turn inside motors, generators and locomotives. Traction motor and railway components are the largest line at 33% of FY26 revenue, sold as stator cores, gear cases, shafts and finished sub assemblies. Power generation adds 15%, supplying stator and rotor cores and machined housings for large generators. Industrial and commercial motors add 13%, mostly loose laminations and die cast rotors, and a mixed bucket of appliance, automotive and scrap sales carries 19%. The core is the working part of the machine, so a motor maker cannot swap suppliers without putting the part through sampling, audit and validation again. The customers are the Indian Railways production units CLW, ICF and BLW, global railway OEMs, generator set makers and industrial motor OEMs, all buying on purchase orders and ordinary trade credit rather than long contracts. The FY26 annual report names Caterpillar, Voith, Progress Rail, Siemens Mobility and Medha Servo as the demand visibility behind the new Rs 290 Cr plant, and discloses no purchase order figure behind any of them. Wabtec appears in that report only as a supplier award from 2019 and 2022, and Alstom never appears in it, which corrects the memory line putting 40% of revenue behind those two names. The calls put the railway share at 28% to 33% and falling. Two customers made 25% of consolidated revenue in FY26, down from 40% the year before, and the notes name neither. Present demand runs on Indian Railways production unit output at CLW, ICF and BLW, on the Union Budget railway capex allocation of Rs 2.78 lakh Cr, and on Caterpillar and Cummins genset shipments that feed both the power generation and the data centre lines. Forward demand rests on three checkable series: India data centre capacity additions in the CBRE and JLL trackers, CEA and MNRE capacity data behind the 500 GW renewable target, and DGCIS import data with the BIS approved mill list that gates the company's own steel supply. Two export customers sit in sampling with revenue guided for Q3 FY27, and two peers confirm the same cut in the US tariff from 50% to 18%. The competitive advantage sits in one place only, on qualified assemblies and machined components, where the OEM qualification barrier is real and the company calls it high by design. Loose laminations and raw castings carry no advantage, and the company itself calls that tier engineering commodities. The emerging moat scan scored 19 and read MODEST, with six categories at moderate or better, all of them resting on the same mix shift and export story rather than on separate sources of advantage. Category 21, talent asymmetry, scored zero, and Category 22, the cannibalization barrier, scored zero, because a large competitor could copy the model without giving up anything of its own. The same scan found the investor presentation selling R&D and tooling expertise while the statutory technology absorption disclosure reports R&D spend as Nil.

Scale lamination maker climbing on borrowed money

Pitti Engineering makes the steel cores inside motors, generators and locomotives. Six plants stamp, cast, machine and assemble. FY26 revenue from operations was Rs 1,913 Cr and total income Rs 1,953 Cr, up 12%, on about 81,500 tonnes of output. Railways take 33% of revenue, power generation 15% and industrial motors 13%. Exports were 27% of FY26 revenue.

The transition is a climb from loose laminations to qualified assemblies and machined components. A loose lamination is a commodity. A machined, qualified assembly is not. Value added volumes grew 21.8% to 37.1% in FY26 while loose lamination volume grew slower. Casting capacity moved from 18,600 to 24,000 tonnes and sheet metal capacity reached 108,000 tonnes. A further Rs 290 Cr plant takes castings to 36,000 tonnes and machining to 10.8 lakh hours. The annual report and all three calls date that plant to Q1 FY30, one investor presentation slide says Q1 FY29, and nothing reconciles the two. Company memory carried Q1 FY29; the corpus does not support it.

Gate 0 scored 58 out of 100 on the core, 69 out of 160 in total, and classified the business AVERAGE with 3 of 12 moats confirmed. Adjusted EBITDA rose 20% to Rs 326 Cr and margin reached 17%, above reported EBITDA of Rs 315.5 Cr. PAT fell to Rs 118 Cr from Rs 122 Cr because the effective tax rate rose from 24.3% to 29.7% on a deferred tax reversal, not because operations weakened. Net debt rose 26% to Rs 555 Cr and working capital days rose from 55 to 68. Cumulative free cash flow across FY22 to FY26 is minus Rs 42 Cr against Rs 441 Cr of cumulative profit. Receivables are the clean part: days fell from 54.5 to 39.4 and overdue beyond six months stayed under 0.5% of the gross book. ROCE fell three years running, from 18.4% in FY24 to 13.8% in FY26.

Management delivered five promises, part delivered four and missed three, which grades credibility C. FY26 revenue landed at Rs 1,953 Cr against a guide of Rs 1,900 Cr to Rs 2,000 Cr. The Rs 150 Cr capex commissioned on schedule and the casting debottleneck landed on schedule. Against that, inventory was promised down from Rs 500 Cr to Rs 300 Cr by April 2026 and stopped near Rs 390 Cr to Rs 400 Cr, the second missed date for the same promise. The EBITDA margin band of 17% plus or minus 50 basis points was undershot twice, at 16.6% and 16.8%. Tax guidance moved from about 33% for a couple of years to about 25% within one quarter, with no reconciliation when an analyst cited the old number. PAT guidance was withheld on two direct requests. The verifier pass added two more: the FY27 lamination guide was cut from 80,000 to 83,000 tonnes down to 78,000 tonnes without acknowledgement and then announced at 82,000 tonnes as a raise, and exports have been flat at Rs 135 Cr to Rs 139 Cr for five quarters while quarterly revenue rose from Rs 457 Cr to Rs 529 Cr.

Peers confirm one claim and contradict three. Sansera and RK Forge both report the same US tariff cut to 18% and the same customer acquisition pull. RK Forge contradicts the railway moderation story: its railway revenue share nearly doubled from 4.6% to 7.5% and it cites Rs 2,000 Cr of Indian Railways demand for the coming year. RK Forge also cut net debt to Rs 1,900 Cr from a Rs 2,500 Cr peak on a stated schedule, in the same year Pitti's net debt rose. Vilas Transcore contradicts the steel supply story: Chinese mills reopened under BIS, CRGO prices fell 15% to 20%, and licence renewals for two approved mills remain uncertain. Vilas sells a different steel grade, so read that last contradiction as directional. No peer could confirm the data centre genset surge.

The market runway reads GOOD. Conservative market size is Rs 25,000 Cr and realistic Rs 34,250 Cr, of which the addressable slice is about Rs 12,000 Cr. Pitti holds about 16% of that slice, which leaves 6.3 times revenue headroom. A three year share of Rs 2,754 Cr implies 12.9% revenue growth a year and the five year figure of Rs 3,453 Cr implies 12.5%. Management's own FY28 target of more than Rs 2,500 Cr sits close to that path at 1.11 times the implied rate. The five year figure sits Rs 150 Cr to Rs 450 Cr above management's own capacity ceiling of Rs 3,000 Cr to Rs 3,300 Cr, and that ceiling already assumes a Rs 400 Cr Bangalore plant the board has not approved. About half the conservative market size rests on an unsourced assumption about the precision grade share of Indian castings.

Promoter conduct reads CAUTION. The managing director sold 6.72 lakh shares for Rs 94.6 Cr at Rs 1,406.80 on 12 December 2024, seven days after the all time high of Rs 1,511.45; the shares trade at Rs 1,085 now, 23% below that sale. Family lease rentals to the chairman and his spouse ran about Rs 3.5 Cr in FY26 and grew again, with no independent benchmark disclosed. Three independent directors and one other director left together around September 2024 and no reason is on record. Against that, pledge is zero, mutual fund holding rose to 14.2% and then to about 15%, three credentialed independent directors joined in August 2024, and two promoter entities are being folded into the listed company.

The record has holes the corpus cannot fill. No results filings, no rating rationale, no exchange announcements and no shareholding filing reached this run. Goodwill of Rs 136 Cr, 13.8% of consolidated net worth, carries no CGU level impairment test anywhere in the notes. No document gives the rupee split between loose laminations, assemblies, castings and machined components, which is the one number the transition thesis needs most. The verifier pass caught 9 of 30 red flags an independent read produced, so the evidence work goes back for rework before deliberation.
