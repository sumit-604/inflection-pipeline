### 2B Excuse pattern analysis

Two attributable "miss or shortfall" explanations exist in the corpus:

1. DRHP-to-RHP working capital/holding-days correction: classified
   **honest-admission**. Company discloses this itself as a mandated
   Risk Factor rather than under external pressure, and names the cause
   plainly ("inadvertent calculation error"). RHP p.31.
2. FY26 receivables spike to 178 days / 48.90% of revenue, and the
   associated negative operating cash flow, are explained as driven by
   "unforeseen geopolitical instability and war-like conditions" that
   delayed foreign-customer collections, and by extension of trade
   payables to offset the impact: classified **external-blame /
   deflection**. This explanation does not engage with the fact that the
   single largest receivables counterparty is Quick Pay Private Limited,
   an investee company in which Millworks itself holds equity (Rs 5.75
   cr strategic investment) and which contributed 47.02% of FY26
   revenue. The RHP separately states these receivables "depend on
   [Quick Pay's] receipt of funds" and are "subject to completion of
   ongoing customer testing processes" — a related-party and
   counterparty-concentration explanation, not primarily a geopolitical
   one. Blaming external instability while the real driver looks more
   like related-party concentration is a pattern worth flagging.
   RHP p.29-30, p.90.

Pattern check: with two data points, one honest and one external-blame
on the specific issue (cash conversion) most material to this name,
call the pattern **mixed, tilted toward external-blame on the item that
matters most**. There is no case in this corpus of management
proactively raising a hard topic ahead of a mandated disclosure
requirement (all of the above are compelled RHP risk factor disclosures
under SEBI ICDR rules, not voluntary transparency).

- The RHP frames Quick Pay as a "strategic investment" and discusses it
  under Business Strategies (RHP p.130), but does not, in that section,

  sections of the document.

  does not address the margin level itself.

- No quantification of the dependency on Big Bang Boom Solutions

  execution beyond a qualitative risk factor (RHP p.25, Risk Factor #4).

### 3D Customer and order book signals

- Customer 1 (= Quick Pay Private Limited per Risk Factor #3, RHP p.25)
  contributed 47.02% of FY26 revenue (Rs 6,992.76 lakh), up from 33.51%
  in FY25 and down from 54.09% in FY24 — high but non-monotonic
  concentration. RHP p.28.
- Top 5 customers = 81.07% of FY26 revenue (up from 73.24% FY25); top 10
  = 92.06% (up from 88.01% FY25). Concentration is rising, not falling,
  even as the customer base nominally grew to 74 accounts. RHP p.28,
  p.24.
- Order book (44 customers, Rs 6,714.06 lakh total as of 05-Jun-2026) is
  itself concentrated: Customer 24 (Defence, Rs 2,577.37 lakh, entirely
  pending) and Customer 12 (Defence, Rs 1,560.68 lakh, Rs 1,557.30 lakh
  pending) together are ~62% of the total order book value. RHP p.129.
- New PO intake of Rs 53.74 cr (21-Jul to 19-Aug 2026) is attributed to
  "multiple domestic and international customers" in Aerospace,
  Defence, Railways, Semiconductor Machinery, Springs — names withheld
  under NDA per Annexure-A disclosure. Reg30 20.08.2026.
- No customer losses are disclosed; RHP explicitly states "we have not
  faced any loss of customers due to industry-wide competition in the
  last three Fiscals." RHP p.29.
