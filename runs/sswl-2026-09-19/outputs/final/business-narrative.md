# SSWL (Steel Strips Wheels Ltd): business narrative, phase 1

Phase 1 lite. Evidence only. Valuation, entry zone and decision form in phase 3.

## BUSINESS UNDERSTANDING NARRATIVE

Steel Strips Wheels makes wheel rims for vehicle makers, and in small volume it makes aluminium steering knuckles. Steel wheels bring about 63% of revenue. They go on trucks, tractors, cars, off road equipment and two and three wheelers. The rating agency puts the company's share of each domestic steel rim segment between 34% and 52%. Alloy wheels bring about 36% of revenue. These are cast aluminium rims, mostly for higher trim cars, and they sell for about Rs 4,730 a wheel against about Rs 2,070 for a steel wheel. Knuckles are about 1% of revenue and have two customers. Every part is built to the vehicle maker's specification and approved for one platform, so a buyer who wants a new supplier must first put a new part through approval.

The buyers are Maruti Suzuki, Hyundai, Mahindra, Honda, Kia, MG, Tata Motors, Ashok Leyland, Honda's two wheeler arm, Suzuki Scooters, and tractor and equipment makers such as Sonalika, Escorts, JCB and New Holland. The investor deck claims large shares of each buyer's purchases: 96% at Suzuki Scooters, 64% at Ashok Leyland and 58% at Tata Motors. No audit or contract stands behind those percentages. Plants sit next to Tata Motors in Jamshedpur and next to Hyundai in Mehsana. Exports are 7% to 9% of revenue.

Demand today follows vehicle output. The signals are the SIAM monthly production and sales release, TMA and FADA tractor data for the tractor business (13% of FY26 revenue), and the monthly sales of Tata Motors and Hyundai Motor India. All three listed peers confirm that the GST cut of late September 2025 lifted demand for cars, trucks, tractors and two wheelers at the same time.

Demand should grow for two reasons. First, cars are moving from steel to alloy wheels. Management expects the alloy wheel market to grow 12% a year and the steel wheel market 4% a year for five years, and one outside report puts alloy growth at 10%. Second, exports should recover if the US Section 232 and reciprocal tariff notices treat India as well as Vietnam and Thailand, and if EU CBAM costs on Europe bound wheels stay small. Both can be checked in the Federal Register and in the EU CBAM updates. The LME aluminium price sets what alloy wheels and knuckles cost, and the company passes that cost to buyers with a lag. Management is building new alloy wheel and knuckle capacity at Bhuj in Gujarat to meet the demand.

Steel wheels have a moat of moderate depth: segment shares of a third to a half, the widest product range of any listed maker, and a long steel supply tie with Tata Steel. Alloy wheels have no proven moat. The claim that buyers cannot leave rests only on management's own figures. Management said on two calls that domestic alloy margins are under competitive pressure, and the only listed steel wheel peer calls its own alloy business its lowest return segment. Knuckles are too new and too small to carry a moat. The forward moat scan scores the business MODEST at 17. The run did not establish separate scores for scan categories 21 and 22.

## Alloy shift real, margin and cash unproven

SSWL is a wheel maker founded in 1985 in Chandigarh, with plants in Punjab, Chennai, Jamshedpur, Mehsana, Saraikela and Bhuj. FY26 revenue was Rs 5,183 Cr, up 17%, and profit was about Rs 202 Cr. The promoter family holds about 61% with no shares pledged. Tata Steel and Nippon Steel hold small strategic stakes.

The change underway is a move from steel wheels toward alloy wheels and aluminium knuckles. In steel wheels the company is a large converter that passes steel costs through. Alloy rose from 28% of revenue in FY24 to about 36% in FY26. EBITDA per wheel rose from Rs 262 to Rs 314 in Q1 FY27. The problem is that EBITDA margin fell from 12.7% in FY22 to 9.9% in FY26 while the alloy share rose. Management has refused three times to split profit per wheel between steel and alloy. Alloy share has also stayed at 35% to 37% across four calls. One verifier reads the Q1 jump as higher prices won from buyers, not as mix. The data does not yet separate those two readings. The Q2 FY27 alloy share, set against EBITDA per wheel, will.

On the backward scorecard the company rates GOOD, 80 of 160, with no deal breakers. Cash quality and promoter alignment score best. Growth scores worst: revenue compounded below 10% a year and profit fell over five years. The rating is fragile. On a strict reading of the partial year rules it drops to AVERAGE, and on a looser reading of the scale test it rises. The operator has to rule on which reading applies.

Cash is the weak point. Standalone operating cash flow fell 36% in FY26, from Rs 518 Cr to Rs 331 Cr, while EBITDA rose about 5%. That is about 63% of EBITDA, against 104% a year earlier. Inventory absorbed Rs 209 Cr and receivables Rs 123 Cr. Two financing lines already flatter the reported figure. Suppliers are paid through a bank programme that covers Rs 677 Cr, two thirds of trade payables, and the annual report shows it for the first time. The company also factors customer bills against limits of Rs 400 to 500 Cr, and the annual report does not disclose that. Cash at year end was Rs 6.5 Cr. The rating agency expects free cash flow to turn negative in FY26 and FY27 while Bhuj is built.

Management guides with numbers and often beats them. FY26 revenue and EBITDA beat the guide given mid year, and EBITDA per wheel beat its target three quarters running. The misses cluster where management controls the outcome. FY26 exports came in at Rs 454 Cr against a Rs 600 to 650 Cr guide. Bhuj trial production moved from October 2026 to Q4 FY27. Knuckle sales fell short of guidance and still have two customers. The capex guide rose across three calls. The CFO and the MD gave different knuckle capacity figures on the same call. The concall grade is B, and the verifier who reread every call grades it closer to C.

The accounts carry a cluster of open questions around AMW Autocomponent, the Bhuj company bought out of insolvency in FY24. FY24 other income jumped to about Rs 487 Cr, against Rs 3 to 13 Cr in the years around it. AMW has lost money every year since, reported zero turnover in its own filing, owes the parent Rs 133 Cr, and carries a Rs 59 Cr tax asset on its losses. Rs 675 Cr and Rs 365 Cr moved between reserves in two years with no explanation. AMW's land title is not in its own name. The auditor, a regional firm, found no key audit matters. The audit committee chairman attended one of five board meetings. A customs notice of June 2026, about Rs 2 Cr and already repaid, is missing from the annual report signed in September. Accounting quality scores 4 of 10. Against that, the promoter took the pledge from 22% in 2021 to zero, bought more shares in September 2026, and took a pay cut in a weak cash year.

Peers confirm the demand story and the cost story. Wheels India, Uno Minda and Alicon describe the same GST led recovery and the same West Asia cost spike in the same months. Alicon's margin after metal cost stayed flat, which supports full pass through. Peers complicate the margin story. Wheels India says its alloy business earns its lowest return, and all three peers are adding aluminium capacity at the same time. No peer confirms SSWL's claimed tariff edge over Southeast Asia, its 80% share in electric scooter wheels, or a two player alloy market.

The market SSWL serves is worth about Rs 17,000 to 20,000 Cr and grows about 8.5% a year. SSWL already holds about 29% of what it can serve, so share gain has limits. A realistic three to five year path gives revenue growth of about 12.5% a year. That is half the 25% management guides for FY27, which looks like a single catch up year on the GST recovery and new capacity. The five year path also needs a second capex cycle that nobody has committed to.
