## Where the market stands

Picture the market as about 2,250 stocks. Each stock has three trend lines: a short line over 20 days, a middle line over 50, and a long line over 200. A stock above a line is in an uptrend on that clock. On Wednesday 663 stocks sat above the long line, about three in ten. About one in five sat above the middle and short lines. On Friday only 178 stocks held the short line. So the short count has gone up two and a half times in three days. The long count has risen by less than 90 in the same three days, and by five tonight.

The year gives these counts their scale. The spring bottom came on 23 March, when the long count fell to 287. The climb out began on 1 April, when more than 1,100 stocks rose hard in one session. The long count then climbed for four months, to 1,357 on 7 August. Autumn has been a staircase down from that peak, with shocks in mid September and early October. A repair lifted the count to about 935 on 16 October. Then last week broke the floor, and the count fell to 577 on Friday. Tonight's 663 is more than double the March low and about half the August peak.

The day itself was narrow next to Tuesday. 103 stocks rose four and a half percent or more. 53 fell that hard. That is about two risers for every faller, a positive day. Tuesday had more than six for every faller. One stock in five traded four percent above its prior close at some point in the day, against nearly four in ten on Tuesday. More than three stocks in ten stayed inside a three percent range all day, the quietest reading of the five sessions. The market did not sell. It rested.

The volume shrank with the width. Turnover came to about 422 crore. Monday and Tuesday had a little over 500 crore each. Friday's selling day had about 611 crore. So the third up day carried the least money of the four, and less than the same day a year ago, at about 439 crore. The share of stocks trading on heavy volume fell to the bottom of its recent range. Three up days on falling turnover say sellers stepped back. They do not say large buyers stepped in. The prior brief asked for a third day with heavier volume than the selling. The third day came, and the volume went the other way.

The far end of the damage keeps healing. About one stock in twenty is down ten percent or more over five days. On Monday more than one in four was. 13 stocks are up twenty percent or more over five days, against six down that much, the first time in two weeks the risers win that count. New yearly lows held at 93 for a second day. New yearly highs rose to 23 from 18. The pool near the bottom is still large: close to four stocks in ten sit within fifteen percent of a yearly low.

Fear eased a little and stays raised. The India VIX measures what traders pay to protect against big swings in the Nifty. It slipped about one percent on Wednesday and closed near 16.3, still up almost forty percent over three months. Gold gave no shelter this month. Gold funds are down about six percent over the month, while the Nifty is down about four. ### For the trader

**1. The bounce has three days and no volume.** Monday, Tuesday and Wednesday all closed with more hard risers than hard fallers. Turnover fell each day from Friday's 611 crore to about 422 crore. Three positive days on shrinking money is a pause, not a confirmed turn.

**2. Wednesday was about two risers for every faller.** 103 stocks rose four and a half percent or more against 53 that fell that hard. Tuesday had 270 against 41. ## What would change this read

The read is a three day bounce, wide on Tuesday and quiet on the other two days, on top of a falling staircase. It turns worse if the selling returns before volume confirms the buying. Another day of 300 or more hard fallers within the next week would repeat the March pattern, when runs of wide days failed. A long count back under about 580, Friday's low, would erase the bounce. New yearly lows back above about 300 would say the same. A quiet fade, with the short count slipping back under about 300, would leave the staircase in place without a fight. A VIX close back above about 18 would say fear has returned.

The cautious read fails if the buying turns heavy and keeps going. The first test is a wide day on turnover above Friday's 611 crore. The second test is the long count back above about 840, the old autumn floor. The third test is new highs past about 50 while new lows stay under 100. Wednesday passed the new low half of that test, at 93, for a second day. It failed the volume test, at about 422 crore. New highs reached 23. The weekly momentum crosses doubled to 19, led by large names, and that is the one fresh sign in the turn's favour. A VIX close under about 15 would support it. A fourth day near Tuesday's width, on heavier volume than Friday, would start to change the read. Three quiet days in a row on falling money would not.
