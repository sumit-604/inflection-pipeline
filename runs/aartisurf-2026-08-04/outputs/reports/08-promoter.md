### 1D. History before the current company; founder vs inheritor

Aarti Surfactants is an inheritance/demerger structure, not a founder-led company: it
was carved out of Aarti Industries Ltd's home-and-personal-care division via a 2018–19
Composite Scheme of Arrangement (AR FY21 p.13, Note 9.5 p.71). Nikhil Parimal Desai and
Chandrakant V. Gogri moved from group/parent roles into the demerged entity's
management. Parimal H. Desai's October 2025 move from Aarti Industries (parent, not
offering re-appointment as Executive Director there 📰) onto the Aarti Surfactants
board, alongside Nisha B. Shah's appointment the same day, continues this pattern of
family members rotating among Aarti-group entities rather than the board bringing in
outside talent (see 6D).

**Notable event — Aug/Oct 2020 SEBI SAST open offer.** On August 10, 2020, Jaya
Chandrakant Gogri and Nikhil Holdings Private Limited entered a share purchase
agreement to acquire an additional ~9.1% stake from a group of shareholders for
~₹20 crore (0.70 million shares at ₹284/share) 📰 (MarketScreener, accessed
2026-08-04). Because this crossed the creeping-acquisition threshold, it triggered a
mandatory open offer under the SEBI (Substantial Acquisition of Shares and Takeovers)
Regulations, 2011: a Detailed Public Statement was issued and an open offer to public
shareholders for up to 19,71,965 shares (26% of paid-up capital) at ₹284/share
(aggregating ~₹56.00 crore) ran with a tendering window of October 5–16, 2020 📰
(EquityBulls, accessed 2026-08-04). This is the origin of the AR FY21 shareholding
jump: Jaya Chandrakant Gogri went from 7.98% (pre-transaction, per the same source) to
21.17% (Note 9.4, AR FY21 p.71), and Nikhil Holdings Private Limited appeared for the
first time at 5.62%. **This was a fully disclosed, SEBI-regulated process with a public
Detailed Public Statement and Letter of Offer, not a stealth or opaque stake build.**
The ₹284/share acquisition price sat within the then-prevailing monthly trading range
(BSE Aug 2020: high ₹591.20, low ₹222.50 — AR FY21 p.34), i.e., it was not a lowball
related-party price grab. The subsequent ~3–4x price rise to a peak of ₹1,211 by
December 2020 (AR FY21 p.34) happened *after* the open offer concluded (mid-October
2020), so the promoter-group's acquisition was not timed to a price that had already
run up; it preceded the rally rather than following it. HDFC Trustee Company Ltd's
full exit (9.41% → 0%, Note 9.4, AR FY21 p.71) is temporally consistent with tendering
its holding into this same open offer window, though no source retrieved explicitly
confirms HDFC Trustee tendered into the offer rather than selling in the secondary
market — this causal link is a plausible inference, not a confirmed fact, and is
marked ❓ accordingly.

## SECTION 2: LEGAL & REGULATORY RECORD

### 2A. SEBI actions
No SEBI adjudication order, SAT appeal, insider-trading action, market ban, or
consent order naming Aarti Surfactants Ltd or its current promoters (Gogri/Desai
family, Nikhil Holdings Pvt Ltd) was found in web search (accessed 2026-08-04). A 2010
SEBI adjudication order concerning synchronized/circular trading in "Aarti Drugs Ltd"
and other scrips (2003–04 period) penalized a broker/client (Tejash D. Ghelani), not
Aarti Industries/Aarti Drugs or their promoters, and pre-dates Aarti Surfactants'
2018 incorporation by over a decade 📰 (web search, accessed 2026-08-04). Relevance to
the current promoter group is minimal; noted for completeness, not weighted as an
adverse finding against this company's promoters.

**LODR/listing non-compliance — genuine, sustained.** The FY21 AR itself discloses,
via the Secretarial Audit Report (Annexure B, p.20–21) and Directors' Report (p.17–18):
(i) the 75,84,477 equity shares were listed 313 days after the NCLT scheme order,
beyond the 60-day window prescribed by SEBI circulars, attributed to exchange query
delays; (ii) the 10,82,387 0% Redeemable Preference Shares (RPS), issued August 20,
2019, remained **unlisted as of the FY21 AR** (May 2021) because SEBI circular
CIR/IMD/DF/50/2017 requires a minimum AA- credit rating for RPS listing and the
company's actual CARE rating for the RPS was BBB- (Stable) — four notches below the
threshold — with the company's own explanation being that "since our Company has
recently been incorporated and does not have its own independent past track record,
getting this rating at this juncture is not possible" (AR FY21 p.17–18, Director's
Report). The RPS rating improved over subsequent years (BBB- → BBB → BBB+, per CARE
press releases dated 2021/2023/2024/2025, web search accessed 2026-08-04) but **never
approached AA-** at any point tracked; the compliance gap was never remedied on its
own terms. Per the same web search, on July 13, 2026 the company dispatched a
redemption notice to RPS holders and on July 17, 2026 trading/administration of the
RPS was suspended ahead of the scheduled August 19, 2026 maturity, with redemption to
be funded from FY26 profits and other means 📰 (web search, accessed 2026-08-04). **Net
assessment: this was a ~7-year (2019–2026) failure to meet a SEBI listing condition
for a promoter-heavy instrument (83.07% held by Promoter & Promoter Group per AR FY21
Note xii, p.35), resolved only by running out the clock to redemption rather than by
achieving compliance.** Minority (non-institution) holders of the RPS held 16.66%
(~₹3 crore of an ~₹18.5 crore instrument per CARE's most recent outstanding-amount
figure, web search accessed 2026-08-04) and were correspondingly denied a public
market/exit for those preference shares for essentially the instrument's full life.
This is recorded as an adverse finding (📰/✅ mixed — the non-listing fact and SEBI
circular citation are VERIFIED from the AR text itself; the current 2026 redemption
timeline is MEDIA REPORTED/web-derived).

### 3A. Related-party transactions
FY21 AR (Note 31, p.81–82) discloses related parties as: subsidiary Aarti HPC Ltd, and

no materially significant related party transactions... which may have potential
conflict with the interests of the Company at large" and confirm Form AOC-2
(disclosure of non-arm's-length RPTs) was not applicable. **This is a clean finding
for the FY21 period specifically** — no royalty, no above-market rent, no guarantees
for private promoter entities disclosed.

**The unexplained ₹820 lakh Inter-Corporate Deposit** (Note 11, p.72; repayment
schedule Note 11.3, p.73) is the one item that does not fit this clean picture. It
appears newly in FY21 (₹0 in FY20) as a component of Non-Current Borrowings, repayable
mostly within 1–2 years (₹824.48 lakh of the total scheduled in that bucket, which
nets against the ₹820 lakh + car loan). **Critically, this ICD does not appear in
Note 31 (Related Party Disclosures) at all** — meaning either (a) it is genuinely from
an unrelated third party and simply a normal corporate-treasury ICD borrowing, which
would be unremarkable, or (b) it is from a related party that was not captured in the
Ind AS 24 related-party note, which would be a disclosure gap. The AR does not name the
counterparty anywhere in the pages reviewed. This is recorded as an ❓ UNVERIFIED/flagged
item precisely because the counterparty is undisclosed — the ambiguity itself, not a
confirmed related-party loan, is the finding.
