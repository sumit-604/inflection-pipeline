## The picture in one paragraph

The market bounced on Monday after two heavy down days last week. 144 stocks rose four and a half percent or more, against 73 that fell that much. That is the first day since 16 October when risers beat fallers, and 33 of 35 indices closed up. The bounce came on light volume. Turnover fell well below Friday's, and the share of stocks on heavy volume dropped by about two thirds. The damage underneath barely moved. Only 597 stocks hold their 200 day line, about one in four, up 20 from Friday. 304 stocks still made new yearly lows, against nine new highs. The rally had no thrust behind it. A thrust day needs about 250 or more big risers on heavy turnover, and the last one came on 12 June. Fear rose with the prices: the VIX closed above 17. This is a relief day inside a confirmed slide in breadth, and the slide holds until buying arrives wide, heavy and repeated.

## Where the market stands

Think of the market as about 2,250 stocks. Each one has three trend lines: a short one over 20 days, a middle one over 50 days and a long one over 200 days. A stock above a line is in an uptrend on that clock. On Monday 597 stocks sat above the long line, about one in four. Only 286 sat above the middle line, about one in eight. 254 sat above the short line, about one in nine. On Friday the short count was 178, the lowest since the March bottom. So Monday lifted the short clock by about 75 stocks. The long clock barely moved.

The year's story gives these counts their meaning. The market made its low in late March. On 23 March 950 stocks fell hard. A two day bounce followed and failed. On 27 and 30 March the selling came back, with more than 600 and then more than 700 hard fallers. Only after that did the real turn come. On 1 April more than 1,100 stocks rose four and a half percent in one session. On 8 April almost 900 did. Traders call these thrust days. A thrust day is a session when buying is so wide that almost every part of the market rises at once. The long count then climbed through spring and summer to 1,367 on 7 August.

Autumn has been a staircase down. A shock on 15 September, a repair, a second shock on 8 October, a second repair to about 935 on 16 October. Then last week broke the floor. Hard fallers numbered 384 on Wednesday, 128 on Thursday and 359 on Friday. The long count fell to 577 on Friday, the lowest since 9 April. Monday is the first step back. It is small against what the slide took. The market lost about 340 long trend holders in five sessions and won back 20 on Monday.

Monday's size needs a fair yardstick. 144 big risers is a good day. It is also an ordinary good day for this autumn. On 6 October 199 stocks rose that hard. On 14 October 147 did. Both days came inside the same staircase down, and neither stopped it. A bounce right after a shock is common. What marks a low is a thrust, and none has come since 12 June.

Inside Monday's session, buyers held more ground than on any day last week. About one stock in four traded four percent above its prior close at some point. Nearly four in ten of those kept the gain into the close. On Wednesday only one in seven did. About one stock in five traded four percent below its prior close, and a third of those finished near the low. About six stocks in ten closed up. So the day was positive on almost every count of direction.

Volume is the weak part. Turnover reached about 519 crore, against about 611 crore on Friday. The share of stocks on heavy volume, against those on light volume, fell from near the top of its history to about the middle. Friday's selling came on heavy volume. Monday's buying came on light volume. That is the shape of a pause in selling, not the shape of fresh demand.

The far end of the damage has not repaired. More than one stock in four is still down ten percent or more over five days. That share sits at the top of its whole history, as it did on Friday. 27 stocks are down twenty percent or more in five days, against eight up that much. The extreme stretch eased a little. About one stock in twenty now trades more than ten percent below its 10 day average, down from about one in fourteen.

New highs are still almost absent. Nine stocks made a yearly high on Monday. 304 made a yearly low. That is fewer lows than Friday's 453, and still far more than any day in early October. About four stocks in ten sit within fifteen percent of a yearly low. Fewer than one in eleven sit that close to a yearly high.

The large companies tell a different story from the whole market. Among the 500 largest stocks, a little more than half trade above their 200 day average. In the whole market it is about one in four. The weakness lives in the smaller names. Yet the large company share is not strong either. On 23 March, at the spring bottom, it stood a little higher than it does tonight.

Fear rose on an up day. The India VIX measures what traders pay to protect against big swings in the Nifty. It rose about two percent on Monday and closed near 17.2. It is up more than forty percent over three months. Prices rose and the cost of protection rose with them. Buyers of protection did not stand down. Gold gave no shelter either. Gold funds are down almost eight percent over the month, while the Nifty is down about three.

## Thirty points

### For the trader

**1. The bounce came, and it is not proven.** 144 stocks rose hard against 73 that fell hard. That is the first day since 16 October when risers won. A proven bounce needs a second and third day like it, and then a thrust.

**2. The bounce is the size of the autumn's failed bounces.** On 6 October 199 stocks rose hard. On 14 October 147 did. Both came inside the same slide. Monday's 144 belongs to that family.

**3. Volume did not back the buying.** Turnover fell from about 611 crore on Friday to about 519 crore. The share of stocks on heavy volume fell by about two thirds. Heavy selling and light buying is the pattern of a pause, not a turn.

**4. No thrust day yet.** A thrust needs about 250 or more big risers on heavy turnover. The last one came on 12 June. The spring bottom ended with a day of more than 1,100.

**5. March warns against the first bounce.** After the shock of 23 March, two days of bounce followed. Then two days of heavier selling came on 27 and 30 March. The real turn came only on 1 April. The first bounce after a shock is often a pause.

**6. Strong openings held better.** Nearly four in ten stocks that traded four percent above their prior close kept the gain. On Wednesday one in seven did. That is the best reading of the past week. Buying strength during the day stays penalised until that share passes about half.

**7. The short clock lifted from the floor.** 254 stocks hold the 20 day line, up from 178. That is still about one in nine. Swing trades on the long side still work against the clock in most of the market.

**8. The stretch eased only a little.** About one stock in twenty trades more than ten percent below its 10 day average, down from one in fourteen. More than one in four is still down ten percent in five days. The snap back that stretch invited has begun, and most of the stretch remains.

**9. Shorting late carries more risk now.** 27 stocks are down twenty percent or more in five days, the most in this data. 128 stocks turned up from deeply sold daily readings. A crowded short side meets a widening bounce, and that setup is penalised for fresh shorts.

**10. The VIX rose on an up day.** It closed near 17.2, above the line the last brief set. The Nifty rose, so the large company shelter held. Fear that rises with prices says protection buyers still expect swings.

**11. The index and breadth agreed today.** 33 of 35 indices rose, and about six stocks in ten closed up. On Friday the Nifty fell a little while small names fell hard. Monday was a fair day, not an index trap.

## What would change this read

The read is a relief day inside a confirmed slide in breadth, on light volume, with fear still rising. It turns worse if the selling returns before the bounce widens. Another day of 300 or more hard fallers within the next week would repeat the late March pattern, when a bounce failed into heavier selling. A long count under about 450 would put the market back to early April. New yearly lows above about 700 would say the same; only three days in this record passed that mark. A VIX close above about 18 with the Nifty falling as hard as the small names would say the large company shelter has failed. A slide in the IT index while breadth stays this weak would take away the index's last prop.

The cautious read fails if buying turns wide, heavy and repeated. The first test is a thrust day, about 250 or more big risers on turnover above Friday's 611 crore. The second test is the long count back above about 840, the old autumn floor, within a few sessions. The third test is new lows back under about 100 while new highs climb past about 50. A VIX close back under about 15 would support all three. Monday passed none of these tests. It did pass two smaller ones: risers beat fallers for the first time in six sessions, and nearly four in ten intraday gains held. A second day like Monday on heavier volume would start to change the read. A quiet fade back into heavy selling would confirm it.
