## Business Understanding Narrative

**What does it do, and how does it make money?** Indo-MIM makes precision metal
components by metal injection moulding, a process that forms metal powder in a
mould like plastic and then sinters it. The output is small, complex,
high-tolerance parts that machining cannot make economically. It sells to
customers in 55 countries across automotive, medical and diagnostics, consumer,
firearms, industrial and aerospace end markets (RHP, FY2027, Q2, page 202).
Revenue from operations was Rs 4,192.99 crore in FY26.

**What is changing?** The geography of demand. Revenue from outside India was
89.92% of the total in FY25 and 77.20% in FY26 (RHP, FY2027, Q2, page 198). In
absolute terms exports still grew, from Rs 2,993.93 crore to Rs 3,236.97 crore,
but domestic revenue grew far faster. The "Others" domestic bucket went from Rs
199.12 crore to Rs 665.01 crore, and revenue from domestic customers inside the
top ten went from Rs 100.56 crore to Rs 591.68 crore, with the count of such
customers rising from one to three (RHP, FY2027, Q2, pages 23 and 360).

**Why now?** India arrived as a market and the IPO landed on it. FY26 revenue
grew 25.9%, restated profit before exceptional items and tax grew 19.0% to Rs
811.78 crore, and restated profit before tax grew 26.3% to Rs 733.74 crore
(PROSPECTUS, FY2027, Q2, page 376). Earnings per share went to Rs 11.06 basic
from Rs 8.79 (PROSPECTUS, FY2027, Q2, page 137).

**What must be true for the thesis to work?** Two things. The domestic
acceleration must continue, and the acquired UK business must stop destroying
value.

**What breaks it?** The price. This card's central finding is not about the
business, which is good, but about what the market is paying for it.

## 2. Business model and archetype

One process, many end markets. The company's own framing is that "our
manufacturing facilities are fungible due to the similarity of the manufacturing
processes employed in the production of products for different sectors", which
lets it shift capacity between sectors as demand moves (RHP, FY2027, Q2, page
202). That fungibility is the core economic asset: it converts sector cyclicality
into capacity utilisation.

Certifications are the entry barrier and they are deep: NADCAP, IATF 16949:2016,
AS 9100:2016, ISO 9001:2015, ISO 14001:2015, ISO 13485:2016, ISO 27001:2022 and
ISO 45001:2018. NADCAP and AS 9100 are aerospace; ISO 13485 is medical devices.
Each takes years and an audited customer qualification to obtain.

Key ratios, restated (DRHP, FY2027, Q2, page 175):

| | FY23 | FY24 | FY25 |
|---|---|---|---|
| EBITDA margin | 29.06% | 25.90% | 28.01% |
| PAT margin | 17.18% | 9.88% | 12.73% |
| Return on equity | 25.37% | 14.01% | 19.94% |
| Return on capital employed | 25.19% | 19.59% | 23.51% |
| Net debt to EBITDA | 0.61 | 1.15 | 1.15 |
| Debt to net worth | 0.40 | 0.53 | 0.57 |

An EBITDA margin near 28% with ROCE above 23% is genuinely high-quality
manufacturing. Fixed asset turnover of 1.24 times is low, which is what a
capital-intensive sintering business looks like.

**Archetype: outsourcing partner (CDMO/EMS pattern), precision variant.** Client
concentration, wallet share, capacity fill, contract stickiness and price per
unit bind.

## 3. Competitive advantages

Present, and the strongest in this run. Three legs.

First, process scarcity. Metal injection moulding at this scale is not widely
available in India, and the company describes cost-effective mass production as
the outcome of its process experience (PROSPECTUS, FY2027, Q2, page 137).

Second, the certification stack. NADCAP plus AS 9100 plus ISO 13485 together
means the same plant can serve aerospace, medical and industrial customers.
Switching a qualified MIM supplier requires re-qualification of the part, not
just the vendor, which is a genuine switching cost.

Third, customer diversification improving from a low base. Top ten customers
were 42.00% of revenue in FY24, 38.94% in FY25 and 38.41% in FY26 (RHP, FY2027,
Q2, page 23). Concentration is falling while revenue grows, which is the
harder and better way to do it.

The honest limit: 55 countries and 38% in ten names is still concentrated, and
no long-term contract is disclosed in what was read. Call it a **real moat,
moderate width**, resting on qualification rather than on brand.

## 6. The transition and the quality ladder

FROM R3 VALUE-ADDED / SPEC'D SUPPLIER. TO R4 FRANCHISE / SHARE-OF-WALLET LEADER.

Indo-MIM already sits at R3 on the evidence: spec-in with switching costs, ROCE
of 23.5%, and durable stickiness. The R4 claim would rest on share-of-wallet
capture, meaning customers consolidating more of their MIM spend into one
qualified supplier, with ROCE moving to the 25-30% band. FY23 ROCE of 25.19%
shows the business has touched that band before.

**Management makes no transition claim in this corpus.** There is no quoted
forward statement, no growth band and no margin target anywhere in the offer
documents read, and Bull AI holds zero guidance records for this company. The
rung migration above is inferred from the mix and return data, not asserted.

Half a rung, at most, and untested.

## 11. Posture: the Transition Decision Matrix

- **Proof gate: NOT FIRED** post-listing, though the FY26 record is strong.
- **Ugliness: ARTIFACT-OF-CLIMB.** The ugliest number is the FY24 trough, PAT
  margin of 9.88% and ROE of 14.01%. FY25 and FY26 recovered from it on the same
  asset base, which is cyclical absorption rather than decay. The UK impairment
  is a cleaning action, which the framework treats as artifact, not structure.
- **Recognition gap: CLOSED, hard.** Market capitalisation of Rs 57,537.80 crore
  against FY26 profit of Rs 533.54 crore is **107.8 times trailing earnings**.
  The ladder's R4 neighbourhood is near 21x, R5 near 24x plus a strategic
  premium. At 108x the market is paying five times the multiple the top rung of
  the ladder would justify.

**Posture: EARNINGS-ONLY, and failing that test.** Proof strong, ugliness an
artifact, gap closed. The framework says returns must then ride EPS growth
alone, and are actionable only if growth clears the Hurdle without help from
re-rating. Profit growing 25.9% against a 108x entry multiple does not clear a
25% compound target on any reasonable exit multiple.

## 12. Verdict card

**PASS at this price. The business is a PROCEED; the price is not.**

This is the highest-quality business screened in this run. Real process moat,
deep certifications, 28% EBITDA margin, 23.5% ROCE, falling customer
concentration, a domestic market opening, and management that wrote off a bad
acquisition rather than carrying it. On the ladder it is a genuine R3 with a
credible path to R4.

At 108 times trailing earnings none of that is investable under a 25% compound
target. The transition is real and entirely priced, which is the EARNINGS-ONLY
cell, and the earnings growth rate does not carry the multiple.

**A price-basis caution the operator must resolve first.** The Bull AI market
capitalisation of Rs 57,537.80 crore implies about Rs 1,192 per share against
roughly 48.24 crore shares, derived from Rs 533.54 crore of profit at Rs 11.06
basic EPS. The IPO price was Rs 485 on 30 July 2026. That implies the stock has
risen about 2.46 times in under two months. **That is possible for a hot listing
but it should be verified on live web before this verdict is acted on**, because
the entire PASS rests on it. At the IPO price of Rs 485 the trailing multiple
would be 43.9 times, which is expensive for the ladder but arguable.

Load-bearing facts a deep run must verify first:
1. **The current share price and market capitalisation**, on live web. This is
   the first check and it may change the verdict.
2. **The Q1 FY27 revenue and profit figures** (document 70ca2fe3, pages 6 to 9).
3. **The FY26 balance sheet and cash flow**, and current net debt after the Rs
   329 crore dividend.
4. **Promoter holding post-listing** and any lock-in expiry dates, given the
   offer for sale was 6.6 times the fresh issue.
5. **What Conway Marsh & Garrett was bought for**, and whether more overseas
   acquisitions are planned.

What would change the view to PROCEED. A market capitalisation near or below Rs
25,000 crore, which would put the multiple near 47 times and inside a debatable
range for an R3 business growing 26%.

No price target. No position.

Derivation. Bull AI holds **zero management guidance records** for this company,

table was not retrieved, so tier B is unavailable on this reading.

The tier C basis is the delivered rate with a mechanism. Profit compounded from

37.1%. That rate is not carried forward, because FY26 growth had already settled

year and shading down for the fixed-asset intensity. The mechanism is the
domestic mix shift, which added Rs 466 crore of revenue in FY26 alone.

FY27 also carries a mechanical tailwind not in the growth rate: the FY26 base
absorbed Rs 78.04 crore of exceptional impairment that should not repeat. Add it
back and the underlying FY26 profit was nearer Rs 594 crore, which would put
forward growth lower and the multiple on a cleaner base at roughly 96.9 times.

Market capitalisation is the Bull AI company record at 22 September 2026, a
service record, not a filed document, and it is the number this card flags for
live verification.
