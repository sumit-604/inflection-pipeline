## PASS 2 NEW FINDINGS

1. **Debt Service Coverage Ratio is below 1.0x in both years.** DSCR (Note
   49(f) standalone, p.110-111 / no separate consolidated ratio table) =
   0.39 FY26 vs 0.40 FY25, a further -2.65% YoY. A DSCR under 1.0x means
   earnings available for debt service do not cover scheduled principal and
   interest for either year; the company is structurally dependent on
   borrowing rollover/refinancing rather than free cash flow to service
   term debt. This sits alongside the FY26 operating cash flow swing to
   negative (Pass 1 finding #5) and is a second, independent balance-sheet
   confirmation of the same cash-conversion weakness. 🔴 Red Flag — not
   flagged in Pass 1 despite being in the same ratio table already cited
   for Debt-Equity.

2. **Majority of total borrowings are "payable on demand."** Note 46(c)
   standalone (p.109-110) / Note 43(c) consolidated (p.151, liquidity
   maturity table): of total borrowings ₹13,131.64 lakh (131.32 Cr) at
   FY26, ₹8,180.83 lakh (81.81 Cr) — **62.3%** — sits in the "Payable on
   Demand" bucket (cash credit + export packing credit + short-term working
   capital), up from ₹2,342.03 lakh (21.5% of FY25's smaller base). This is
   a large jump in on-demand liquidity exposure, not just a rate-mix shift
   (Pass 1 covered fixed-vs-floating; this is a different axis, contractual
   callability). 🟡 Watch — combined with the sub-1x DSCR, the company's
   liquidity depends materially on lender forbearance/renewal, more
   sensitive than the "no covenant breach" framing alone conveys, and more
   relevant given the pending merger into VTL (Note 50).

3. **Gratuity past-service-cost table figure does not match the narrative
   disclosure of the same event.** Note 38(i)/(iv) standalone (p.99-100) /
   Note 37(i)/(iv) consolidated (p.137-138) tabulate "Past Service Cost"
   for the Labour Codes at **₹191.31 lakh**. Note 38(xi) standalone (p.101)
   / Note 37(xi) consolidated (p.140) states in narrative text that
   "Employee Benefits Expense for the financial year... includes the
   incremental impact of Gratuity liability amounting to **₹223.54 lakh**"
   for the same Labour Codes event. The two disclosures of the same
   one-off item, four pages apart, differ by ₹32.23 lakh with no
   reconciliation offered. 🟡 Watch — a reader relying on the narrative
   figure (223.54) versus the table figure (191.31) to normalise FY26
   earnings would reach a materially different "clean" EPS.

4. **Standalone and consolidated gratuity DBO for the FY25 comparative
   year do not match, for what should be the identical Indian defined
   benefit obligation.** Standalone Note 38(iii)/(iv) (p.100): FY25 closing
   Defined Benefit Obligation = ₹422.74 lakh, net asset ₹66.66 lakh.
   Consolidated Note 37(iii)/(iv) (p.138-139): FY25 closing DBO =
   **₹427.71 lakh**, net asset ₹61.69 lakh — a ₹4.97 lakh discrepancy. The
   gap recurs as an exactly offsetting ₹4.97 lakh difference in "Benefits
   Paid" for FY26 (standalone ₹144.86 lakh vs consolidated ₹149.83 lakh),
   so both statements still arrive at the same FY26 closing DBO of ₹473.20
   lakh — i.e. the comparative-year base was silently adjusted between the
   two note sets with no disclosed reason (the subsidiary DMCC, UAE, has no
   Indian gratuity scheme, so consolidation should not change this number
   at all). 🟡 Watch — an unexplained restatement of a prior-year actuarial
   figure between standalone and consolidated notes of the same annual
   report.

5. **Continuum MP Windfarm FVTPL investment was never actually fair
   valued while held — it was carried at cost under an accounting-policy
   carve-out.** Note 1.5(i)(1)(b) standalone (p.87) discloses that equity
   investments are normally FVTOCI **"save & except passive long-term
   investments in a power producer company for sourcing of renewable
   energy which are measured at cost by treating it as fair value through
   Profit and Loss (FVTPL) as per the governing terms and conditions of
   the Power Purchase Agreement."** The consolidated Fair Value note 42(C)
   (p.150) confirms explicitly: "Investment in Continuum MP Windfarm
   Development Pvt. Ltd. ... is considered at fair value through profit or
   loss and valued as per terms and conditions of the agreement" — i.e.
   the ₹173.94 lakh carrying value shown at FY25 (Nil at FY26, fully
   exited) was cost, not a market-tested fair value, despite the FVTPL
   label. 🟡 Watch — a labelling/measurement inconsistency (an asset
   classified FVTPL that was never actually marked to fair value); now
   moot since the position is fully exited, but relevant if a similar PPA
   investment is entered again, and worth noting as a policy quirk an
   investor comparing this company's investment note to a peer's would
   otherwise misread.

6. **Scrap sales are ~4.5% of total reported "Revenue from Operations,"
   embedded in the headline growth number without separate visibility on
   the P&L face.** Note 26 (standalone p.96, consolidated p.136): "Sale of
   Scrap Materials" = ₹3,497.62 lakh (34.98 Cr) FY26 vs ₹3,228.94 lakh
   (32.29 Cr) FY25, +8.3% YoY — this is classified as "Other Operating
   Income" within Revenue from Operations, not broken out on the P&L face.
   Core "Sale of Products" grew 17.1% (₹627.79 Cr → ₹734.84 Cr) while total
   reported revenue (including scrap) grew 16.5% — the two growth rates are
   close enough that this does not distort the headline number materially,
   but scrap-sale revenue (a byproduct of copper/aluminium wire drawing,
   not a sold finished good) is qualitatively different from core product
   revenue and is not separately visible unless Note 26 is read. 🟢 Clean
   in isolation (fully disclosed, standard for a wire/cable manufacturer),
   flagged here only because Pass 1's revenue reconciliation did not
   surface the scrap-revenue quantum for the reader.

7. **Government subsidy income (Industrial Investment Promotion Incentive)
   receivable more than doubled.** Note 13 (standalone p.90, consolidated
   p.135): "Industrial Investment Promotion Incentives Receivable" =
   ₹763.28 lakh (7.63 Cr) FY26 vs ₹347.00 lakh (3.47 Cr) FY25, **+120.0%**.
   This receivable is a state capital-investment subsidy that is deducted
   from the PPE gross block, not routed through the P&L (Note 2 note (ii),
   p.91: adjustments of ₹416.28 lakh FY26 / ₹347.00 lakh FY25 "received as
   subsidy"). 🟡 Watch — a growing, uncollected subsidy receivable outside
   the trade-receivables ageing schedule that an investor would not see
   unless cross-referencing Note 13 to Note 2; no ageing or collection-risk
   commentary is given for this specific receivable.

8. **Derivative mark-to-market liability jumped 12.7x.** Note 23
   (standalone p.94, consolidated p.140) / Note 45(II)(b) fair value table
   (standalone p.106) / Note 42(II)(b) (consolidated p.150): "Provision for
   MTM of Derivative Instruments — Foreign Exchange Forward Contract"
   = ₹29.32 lakh FY26 vs ₹2.31 lakh FY25, **+1,169%**. Small in absolute
   terms but a large directional move in the mark-to-market position on
   the company's own hedges, consistent with (and additive to) the
   doubling of unhedged EUR sensitivity already flagged in Pass 1. 🟡 Watch,
   immaterial in quantum, directionally consistent with rising FX exposure.

9. **A new, previously-absent unhedged EUR trade receivable appeared this
   year.** Note 46(a)(i) standalone (p.107-108) / Note 43(a)(i)
   consolidated (p.151-152): Unhedged Financial Assets — Receivables in
   EUR = EUR 511,992.71 / ₹546.09 lakh at FY26 vs **Nil** at FY25 (only USD
   and GBP unhedged receivables existed at FY25). This is the direct driver
   of the EUR sensitivity doubling already noted in Pass 1 (₹34.12 lakh vs
   ₹16.02 lakh PBT impact); Pass 1 flagged the sensitivity delta but not
   this specific new-exposure origin. 🟡 Watch, mechanically explains the
   sensitivity jump, no new rating implication beyond what Pass 1 already
   assigned.

10. **Statutory dues (Other Current Liabilities) fell by half.** Note 24
    (standalone p.94, consolidated p.140): "Statutory Dues" = ₹291.56 lakh
    FY26 vs ₹582.78 lakh FY25, **-50.0%**. No narrative explanation is
    given. Plausible ordinary-course explanations exist (GST/TDS payment
    timing near year-end), but the magnitude (₹2.91 Cr swing) is large
    enough, and unexplained enough, to note as a working-capital item worth
    a management question. NOT FOUND IN DOCUMENT (reason for the decline).
    🟡 Watch.

11. **Shares in Birla Cable are held by five MCA-struck-off companies**
    (Note 49(b)(ii) standalone, p.111 / Note 46(B)(b)(ii) consolidated,
    p.150-151): Ardra Holdings Private Limited (400 shares), Badri Sarraf
    Finance and Mutual Benefit Company Limited (900 shares), Fair Growth
    Investments Limited (120 shares), Home Trade Limited (100 shares), and
    Manjiri Investments Private Limited (100 shares FY25, Nil FY26 — exited
    or the holding entity itself changed status). Combined ~1,520 shares
    out of 3,00,00,000 outstanding (0.0005%) — immaterial in size, and this
    is a standard MCA-mandated disclosure (not evidence of any wrongdoing
    by the Company), but it is a completeness item Pass 1 did not surface.
    🟢 Clean/immaterial, noted only for completeness.

12. **Note 51 standalone / Note 48 consolidated ("previous year figures
    regrouped/rearranged") gives no specifics on what was reclassified.**
    Standard boilerplate language, present in the prior year's report too
    presumably, but the notes never disclose which line items were
    regrouped or by how much (e.g. whether it explains the split from a
    single "Assessed Government Dues" bucket into current line items).
    NOT FOUND IN DOCUMENT (nature/amount of regrouping). 🟡 Watch — a
    procedural disclosure gap, not evidence of a specific number's
    unreliability, flagged for Pass 3's restatement-pattern check.

## PASS 2 NEW FINDINGS SUMMARY

Twelve new items surfaced on the second full read, none of which override
Pass 1's ranking of the merger (Note 50/47), customer concentration (Note
39(c)/38(c)), and the stale export receivable (Note 9(2)) as the three most

which sharpens the liquidity-risk picture beyond the fixed/floating-rate
framing Pass 1 used. The two gratuity-note internal inconsistencies
(findings 3 and 4) are minor in rupee terms but are the kind of small,
unreconciled figures that belong in Pass 3's cross-note contradiction scan.
