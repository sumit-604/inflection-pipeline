**CFO quality checks**: The FY26 negative swing is explained entirely by
working-capital absorption, not one-off items — Trade Receivables absorbed
₹3,262.56 lakh of cash and Inventories absorbed ₹2,777.37 lakh, versus FY25

respectively. This is the mechanical explanation for both years' extreme
swings: FY25's outsized CFO was itself substantially a working-capital
unwind, not sustainable steady-state cash generation, and FY26 reversed it
with a fresh, larger build. Neither year looks like the "true" run-rate.
Interest is classified within operating activities per the indirect-method
add-back (Interest Expense ₹905.34 lakh added back, Interest Paid ₹934.44

No sign of payable-stretching as an FY26 cash lever — trade payables actually

enough to offset the receivable/inventory build); the flagged MSME payable
growth (Phase 2, rank 13) shifted payable *composition* toward smaller
suppliers without materially extending overall payable *days*.
Capex/depreciation of 0.17x in FY26 is sharply below maintenance level
(₹191.05 lakh gross additions per Directors' Report vs ₹1,578.55 lakh
depreciation) — consistent with the Directors' Report's own description of
"judicious capital allocation," but also consistent with a company that is

The operational section that should show this optimism converting to results
— the Telecommunication Cables / OFC segment performance table two pages
later in the same MD&A (p.41) — instead reports: **"The Optical Fibre Cable
(OFC) business recorded a marginal decline, closing at ₹10,564.95 Lakhs in FY
2025-26, compared to ₹11,516.07 Lakhs in the previous year"** (a -8.3%
decline), attributed to "subdued demand for optical fibre cables across both
domestic and export markets" during the very year the opening narrative
describes global demand as beginning to recover. The entire FY26 earnings
improvement is instead sourced from the Copper/Structured LAN Cable segment
(+28.7%), a business the opening industry narrative barely discusses.

**Classification**: (c) **hedged retreat**. The opening narrative does not
claim OFC results were strong (it explicitly and candidly states the OFC
segment declined, which is itself a credibility positive per Phase 4C) — but
it surrounds that admission with two pages of bullish global-industry framing
and a closing assertion that the Company "remains well-positioned… to
capitalize on emerging opportunities," without connecting the two: a reader
finishing the opening section would reasonably expect OFC to be a source of
FY26 strength, and only two pages later learns the opposite is true for the
segment the opening narrative is actually about. This is not an implicit
retraction (the operational section does not contradict the opening without
acknowledgement) or a silent drop (OFC performance is fully addressed) — it
is the hedged-retreat pattern of pairing a real admission with framing that
minimises its prominence relative to the surrounding bullish narrative.

**Materiality**: Moderate, not thesis-changing on its own — the LAN segment
genuinely carried FY26 results and this is disclosed accurately. But it is
directly relevant to any forward-looking read of "OFC recovery" as an
investment driver: this AR's own operational section shows the Company's OFC
business moving in the opposite direction from the narrative built around it
in the same document, in the same year.

No second instance of comparable weight was identified. The Board
self-evaluation "satisfaction" statement (6A) versus the 20%-attendance
director is a related but distinct pattern (an omission of context rather
than a retreat from a stated claim) and is carried under 6A rather than 6E.

### Phase 6 Verdict
🟡 **Watch.** The front matter is more candid than average on segment-level

same cluster of cash-flow and concentration facts that Phase 4 found missing
from the risk section, and frames the merger in unmixed synergy language
