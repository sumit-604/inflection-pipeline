# INA (Insolation Energy Ltd) — business narrative

Run: ina-2026-09-06. Phase 1 (evidence stages 0 to 9 plus verifiers). No valuation has been run.

## BUSINESS UNDERSTANDING NARRATIVE

Insolation Energy makes solar photovoltaic modules, the flat panels that turn sunlight into direct current electricity. Own manufactured modules were 81.9 percent of FY2026 revenue, bought and resold goods booked as trading sales were 17.0 percent, sale of electricity from the company's own plants was 0.2 percent, and other operational income was 0.5 percent. Only two streams clear ten percent of sales, so this is a module maker with a trading desk attached. A buyer building a solar plant cannot skip the panel, but can buy the same panel from about ten other listed Indian makers. What the buyer cannot substitute is a panel on the government ALMM approved list, because scheme linked projects accept nothing else.

Customers fall into three classes: government scheme developers, EPC contractors who build plants for them, and a dealer channel. One customer is named in the accounts, Solarworld Energy Solutions Ltd, at 12.4 percent of FY2026 revenue and nil the year before. The dealer channel is shrinking: dealer count fell from 93 to 82 and dealer channel sales fell from 12.44 percent to 11.61 percent of total sales in one year. Qualification runs through the ALMM list, not through the maker, so a customer can switch supplier without requalifying anything. Peers say orders turn firm only against advances of 5 to 15 percent of value, while INA says it moved customers to 45 day letter of credit terms.

Demand today comes from government procurement, and three named signals size it: SECI and NTPC solar tender awards and cancellations, PM-KUSUM state nodal agency tender and PPA awards, and MNRE ALMM List I and List II updates and effective date notifications. Each of the three is published outside the company, so the demand driver is verifiable without management.

Demand should grow because the Indian module market is Rs 110,880 crore to Rs 128,120 crore and is growing about 16 percent a year, against INA's 2.7 percent share of the serviceable slice. The growth is not uniform. Peers state that DCR demand under government schemes is tight while non DCR module demand is oversupplied and unprofitable, so the forward signals that matter are the module and cell ASP benchmark trackers, the silver and polysilicon commodity price indices, and the US anti dumping and countervailing duty rulings on Indian solar imports, all published monthly or on event.

Competitive advantage sits in one place and is not yet built. The module line has no moat: the emerging moat scan scored 9 of 92 against a 12 point floor and classified NONE, R and D spend was 0.00 percent of total R and D plus capex, and exports were NIL percent of turnover. The trading line has no moat and is stated plainly as resale. The IPP line holds a licence like position through PM-KUSUM allotments but produced Rs 4.22 crore of revenue, so the run did not establish its economics. Only two forward categories scored active: backward integration into cells, which has real capital behind it and a line under construction, and the ALMM regulatory tailwind, which management itself says is shared equally with Waaree, Premier Energies and Websol. On the operator's two structural asymmetry checkpoints, talent asymmetry and cannibalization barrier, INA scored zero on both.

Revenue tenfold in four years, cash negative

Insolation Energy makes and sells solar panels from Jaipur and Sawarda in Rajasthan. Modules made in house are 82 percent of sales, resold goods 17 percent, and power sold from its own plants under the PM-KUSUM scheme 0.2 percent. FY2026 revenue was Rs 2,146 crore, up 61 percent, with EBITDA of Rs 305 crore and profit of Rs 201 crore. The company moved from the BSE SME platform to the Main Board on 9 March 2026.

The transition claimed is a climb up the solar chain, from buying cells and assembling panels to making the cells itself. Capacity went from 80 MW at founding to 5.5 GW, with the 4.5 GW Sawarda module plant commissioned inside FY2026. A 4.5 GW cell plant at Narmadapuram is under construction and funded, sitting inside Rs 901 crore of capital commitments. Cells bought from outside are the reason raw material is about 78 percent of revenue, so making them is the whole margin case.

The transition has not yet reached the accounts. The company reports one segment, so no EPC, IPP or O and M margin exists in any filing. Guided commissioning for the cell plant has moved three times and appears in the corpus as Q3 FY2027, Q4 FY2027 and December 2026, twice inside the same call. The aluminium frame plant capacity was cut from 54,000 tonnes to 18,000 tonnes with no acknowledgement anywhere.

Gate 0 scores the backward record 54 of 100 on the core blocks and 69 of 160 overall, classification AVERAGE. The score splits into two eras. FY2022 to FY2025 shows real improvement: margins widened, ROCE held, and FY2025 turned 90 paise of every rupee of profit into operating cash. FY2026 breaks it. Profit rose 59 percent while operating cash flow went to minus Rs 73 crore, working capital days went from 31 to 63, and borrowings went from Rs 108 crore to Rs 888 crore.

The cash number is the run's central open question. Receivables aged beyond six months went from 5.7 percent of the book two years ago to 12.1 percent to 39.5 percent, Rs 111 crore of a Rs 282 crore book, with no bad debt provision in any bucket in any year. All of it sits in the subsidiary; the listed parent's own book stayed near 6 percent. Management said in February 2026 that FY2026 cash accruals would be about Rs 220 crore. The audited figure was minus Rs 73 crore, and no call has reconciled the two.

Peers say the financing mechanism INA blames is real. Websol confirmed that letter of credit backed receivables are an industry norm. Websol also turned Rs 303 crore of FY2026 profit into Rs 255 crore of operating cash, 84 percent conversion, using that same mechanism. INA converted none. Peers also contradict two other INA claims: the 142 GW national cell capacity figure INA cites matches a module number, not a cell number, and INA's alternate January 2026 date for the ALMM Part 2 rule is wrong against all three peers, who name 1 June 2026, which has since been pushed to December 2026.

Management delivery grades D. Of eleven tracked promises, one was delivered, five were partial and five were missed, and the verifier found three more misses the tracker had not recorded. Every miss was blamed on something outside the company. The delivered one is real and large: module capacity guided at 4 GW by FY2026 end came in at 5.5 GW. Against that, FY2026 revenue guidance of Rs 3,300 crore landed at Rs 2,146 crore, FY2026 profit guidance of Rs 300 crore landed at Rs 201 crore, and the FY2028 revenue vision was cut from Rs 8,500 crore to above Rs 5,000 crore without being called a cut.

Governance carries a CONCERN verdict with evidence on both sides. Against: Rs 32.5 crore was put into two LLPs that are 90 percent owned by the two promoter families with no stated reason, the factory land is mortgaged in the two promoters' personal names while the accounts and the auditor both say title sits with the company, a promoter's spouse sits on the committee that sets promoter pay and is mislabelled independent in the same document, and corporate guarantees rose 16.6 times to Rs 1,654 crore, which is 205 percent of net worth. For: both promoters bought stock in the open market during FY2026, three independent directors with checkable outside records joined and were approved by more than 99.85 percent of votes, and the Main Board move raised the disclosure bar. The legal and regulatory record came back clean across 26 searches.

The market is large and the runway is not the constraint. The addressable Indian module market is Rs 110,880 crore to Rs 128,120 crore, INA holds 2.7 percent of the serviceable slice, and headroom is 36.7 times current revenue, classified MASSIVE. Management's own no overcapacity argument, though, reads 4.73 times inflated against an independently triangulated cell market, and the five year capture estimate slightly exceeds what the currently committed 5.5 GW can physically produce.

Two figures sit outside this corpus and both matter. Q1 FY2027 profit was Rs 37 crore against Rs 70 crore the quarter before, roughly a halving, and neither the filing nor its call is in the run's document set. The ALMM Part 2 date that the cell margin case rests on moved from June 2026 to December 2026 in peer calls dated after INA's newest call.
