## The picture in one paragraph

The market bounced after the washout, and the bounce was thin. On Thursday 122
stocks rose four and a half percent or more, and only 24 fell that hard. That is
a good day. It is also the same size as the good days of early September, and
those did not stop the slide. A thrust day in this record needs 300 risers, and
none has come. The volume behind Thursday's rise was the lightest since 20 July.
About one stock in four now sits above its twenty-day line, up from one in five.
Fewer stocks still sit above their two-hundred-day line than below it. The worst
crop of new yearly lows came on Wednesday, and it eased on Thursday. Fear fell
back and is cheap again. Healthcare still leads, and small pharma, cables and
pipes fill the lists. Banks were the only share indices to fall. The flush is
done and the repair has started. The proof that buyers are back in size has not
come.

## What would change this read

The bounce fails if a second washout arrives before any thrust, as happened in
March. That means 300 or more stocks falling four and a half percent in one
session. It fails if the two-hundred-day count closes under about 900, which
would take out the 11 June low of 902. It fails if fresh yearly lows climb back
above about one stock in twenty, the Wednesday level. It also fails if healthcare
drops under half its stocks above the forty-week line. That would take away the
last broad leader.

The cautious read fails if a thrust day arrives, 300 or more stocks jumping four
and a half percent in one session. It fails if the twenty-day count climbs above
about 800 by Tuesday, which would mark the washout as a shakeout. It fails if the
two-hundred-day count returns above about 1,150. It also fails if the large-cap
list above the day's average price climbs above fifty while more than a quarter of
bank stocks return above their four-week line. Banks sit at about one in five
tonight.
