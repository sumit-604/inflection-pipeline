# Market read, 16 November 2026

This brief covers Monday 16 November. The 21:00 download brought all 34 of 34 table files from the two public Chartink dashboards, and every file carries Monday as its latest date. The previous brief covered Friday 13 November, so this one compares Monday with Friday. The full figures sit in the data appendix, `2026-11-16-appendix.html`. Tonight's sheet restates Friday's three trend counts a little lower than Friday's brief gave them, by two to seven stocks. This brief uses tonight's figures.

The record counts a market of about 2,340 stocks. Claude Code has no web access. This brief checks no news and gives no reason for any move. It describes what the market did. It is not advice to buy or sell anything. The operator decides.

## The picture in one paragraph

Monday split the market in two. The Nifty rose about half a percent and closed near 25,290, lifted by consumer staples and banks. Under it, more than two stocks fell for each one that rose. 215 stocks fell four and a half percent or more, against 69 that rose that hard. That is the most hard fallers since 30 October. The count above the 50 day line dropped by 179 to 1,081. That is back under half the market, and it is the same count as one week ago. The short count lost 317 stocks in one session. The long count fell by 77 to 1,053. Small and micro companies took the damage, along with pharma, metals, autos and realty. Turnover stayed ordinary at about 450 crore, so the selling was wide but not heavy. New yearly highs held at 66, and new yearly lows rose to 38. The fear gauge edged up to 15.7. The index says the week began well. The average stock says the repair lost three days of work.

## Where the market stands

Each stock in the record has three trend lines: 20 days, 50 days and 200 days. A stock above a line is in an uptrend on that clock. On Monday 1,273 stocks stood above the short line, a little over half the market. On Friday it was 1,590. 1,081 stood above the middle line, against 1,260 on Friday. 1,053 stood above the long line, against 1,130. The short trend still belongs to the buyers, by a thin margin. The middle trend went back to the sellers. The long trend stayed with the sellers, and the gap widened to more than 230 stocks.

The year gives these counts their scale. On 23 March the long count hit a low of 287. On 1 April came a great thrust day, when 1,129 stocks rose hard. The long count climbed through the summer and peaked at 1,373 on 26 August. It slid through September, and 23 September was the last day with more stocks above the long line than below it. The floor broke in October. On 21 October 411 stocks fell hard in one day. Two days later only 605 stocks held the long line and only 188 held the short line. Monday's long count still sits about 450 stocks above that low.

The bounce is three and a half weeks old, and Monday was its hardest day since 30 October. That day brought 295 hard fallers. The down day of 6 November brought 150, and Friday brought 59. Monday brought 215. The week before had built up slowly. Wednesday was the wide day, Thursday held it, and Friday gave back a little. Monday took back the rest. The middle count is exactly where it stood on Monday 9 November, at 1,081. The long count is two stocks above that day's level.

The damage on Monday was wide, and it stuck. About one stock in four traded four percent below its prior close at some point, almost twice Friday's share. More than half of those closed down there. On Friday about a third did, and the rest recovered. Monday's share is the highest of the last twenty sessions, higher than any day of the October break. About one stock in eight traded four percent above its prior close, and fewer than a third of those kept the gain. The share of stocks in a quiet three percent range fell from four in ten to about three in ten. Turnover came to about 450 crore, the same as Friday. A year ago the same session saw about 370. About five stocks traded on heavy volume for every ten on light volume. The sellers were many, but they did not come in size.

The top of the market held while the bottom grew. 66 stocks made a new yearly high, against 68 on Friday. New yearly lows rose from 14 to 38, which is still few. About one stock in four now sits within fifteen percent of a yearly low. About one in six sits within fifteen percent of a yearly high. On Thursday the two groups were level.

Fear rose a little on a day when the Nifty rose. The India VIX measures what traders pay for protection against big Nifty swings. It gained about one percent and closed at 15.7. It sits about nine percent above its thirty week line. A fear gauge that rises with the index agrees with the breadth, not with the index. Gold funds are down about nine percent over the month. The Nifty is up about thirteen percent over the same month. Monday opened a new week, so the weekly change on the sheet equals the daily change for every index.

## Thirty points

### For the trader

**1. The index rose and the market fell.** The Nifty gained about half a percent. More than two stocks fell for each one that rose. The gain came from consumer staples and banks. A trader who watched only the index saw a good day that most stocks did not have.

**2. Hard fallers came back in number.** 215 stocks fell four and a half percent or more, against 59 on Friday. That is the most since 30 October, when 295 fell that hard. It is still under the 300 that earlier briefs set as the line. One such day is a warning. A second one soon after would be a pattern.

**3. The volume did not confirm the selling.** Turnover came to about 450 crore, the same as Friday. About five stocks traded on heavy volume for every ten on light volume. Wide selling on ordinary volume means many small sellers and few large ones.

**4. The swing vote went back to the sellers.** 1,081 stocks hold the 50 day line, down 179 in one session. Half the market is about 1,170. Friday's brief said one more down day would bring the count to the edge. Monday took it about 90 stocks past the edge.

**5. The short count sits just under its marker.** 1,273 stocks stand above the 20 day line, down 317. The marker was about 1,280, the level of 6 November. The count is a few stocks under it. A little over half the market still holds the short line, so the bounce has not lost the short clock yet.

**6. Falls stuck at the close.** About one stock in four traded four percent down at some point. More than half of those closed there. On Friday about a third did. Monday's share is the highest in twenty sessions, above every day of the October break. Buyers did not use the weakness this time.

**7. Gains faded more than before.** Fewer than a third of the stocks that traded four percent up kept that gain. All last week the share ran a little under four in ten. Sellers still use strength to leave, and on Monday they used more of it. Half or more holding would mark a real change.

**8. The small end reversed its edge.** On Thursday and Friday small companies fell less than large ones. On Monday the microcap index fell about one and a half percent and the small company 250 index about one. The Nifty rose. Money moved up the size ladder in one session.

**9. FMCG shows the index trap.** The FMCG index rose almost two percent, the best of all. About one FMCG stock in three holds the forty week line. A few large names carried the index. The members have to follow before the index move can be trusted, the same test realty failed last week.

**10. Banks passed a harder test than Friday's.** Bank indices rose on a day when two thirds of stocks fell. Nine bank stocks in ten hold the four week line. A leader that rises on a wide down day is still the leader. A sharp fall in banks on heavy volume remains the event that would change the tape.

**11. Pharma took the worst index fall.** The pharma index fell about one and a half percent. The share of healthcare stocks on the four week line fell from almost eight in ten to about six in ten. That is the fourth slip in a row, and the first one with force. The slow width did not move.

**12. Autos lost short ground fast.** The index fell a third day. About six auto stocks in ten hold the four week line, down from more than eight in ten. Parts makers still supply the names at yearly highs. The gap between parts makers and the index is open for a third session.

**13. The short side had its first good day in weeks.** About one stock in fifty is down ten percent over five days, three times Friday's share. New yearly lows rose to 38. Yet only three stocks are down twenty percent over five days, against 15 up that much. One day does not make shorting a favoured setup.

**14. Fresh momentum thinned.** Daily crossings fell to 59 from 91. Weekly crossings fell to 24 from 97, partly because a new weekly bar began. The largest names on the daily list are hospitals and consumer companies. The fast money has fewer places to go than it had on Thursday.

**15. The thrust test moved further away.** The earlier briefs asked for 200 or more hard risers on turnover above about 610 crore. Wednesday came closest with 185. Monday brought 69 on about 450. The bounce has run three and a half weeks without a thrust day, and it has now had a hard down day first.

**28. Foreign filings back many of the leaders.** Filatex, Kilitch Drugs, SG Mart, MSTC, Nitin Spinners and Manaksia Steels carry a foreign rise and sit on five or more screens. The filings describe the past quarter. They say nothing about this week, but they show the move has holders behind it.

**29. Retail crowding met its first real test.** Dhanlaxmi Bank, Shalimar Paints and Style Baazar show a retail rise and no institutional one on the sheet. Retail float tends to sell first on broad down days. All three still closed at a yearly high on Monday. A second hard day would test that float again.

**30. Fear is cheap, and the gap with gold widened.** The VIX closed at 15.7, up about one percent. Gold funds are down about nine percent over the month while the Nifty is up about thirteen. Protection costs little after a day when one stock in eleven fell four and a half percent.

**31. Pipeline names on the lists, as memory to weigh and never as evidence.** Kabra Extrusion ranks in the top ten over three months. Kronox sits on both the foreign and the domestic buying lists. Aurum shows a foreign rise of about twenty points. The ticker PERMAGN ranks in the top ten over one month; the pipeline file is named PERMAGNET, and this brief does not confirm the match.

## What would change this read

The read is an early uptrend that took its first real hit, with banks and a narrow group of leaders still standing. It turns worse if a second hard down day follows soon. A day of 300 or more hard fallers would match the October pattern. A long count under about 1,050 would confirm that last week's progress is gone, and Monday closed three stocks above it. A short count under half the market, about 1,170, would hand the last clock back to the sellers. A long count under about 605, the 23 October low, would erase the whole bounce. New yearly lows above about 100 would say sellers have returned in force. A VIX close above about 18 would say fear has taken charge again. A sharp fall in banks on heavy volume would remove the group now carrying the index.

The cautious read fails if Monday proves to be a single flush. The first sign is the 50 day count back above half the market, about 1,170, within a few sessions. The second is the long count turning up toward half from 1,053. The third is a wide up day, with 200 or more hard risers, on turnover above about 610 crore. The fourth is a VIX close under 15, against 15.7 on Monday. The fifth is gains held into the close. About half of the stocks that trade four percent up should keep it, and Monday held fewer than a third. The first of these, with any one of the others in the same week, would put the repair back on its track.
