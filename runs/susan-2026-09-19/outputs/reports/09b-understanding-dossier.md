### PART A: THE FROM STATE

**A1. Archetype.** Two lines, because the business runs two different archetypes at once (B04):
- Manufacturing line (LT/HT/MVCC cable, aluminium conductor, Al/Cu winding wire): **Build-to-spec
  component maker** crossed with **Order-book business (EPC/discom tender)**, content-per-unit and
  design/qualification lock-in on one side, tender-driven order inflow and working-capital cycle on the
  other.
- Trading line (aluminium rod/wire/ingot resale, 38.38% of FY26 revenue): **Commodity converter**, and a
  weak one; the RHP itself calls this line's entry barriers "relatively low" (B04, RHP p.24). B04 flags
  it as a Stage 11 SOTP candidate, separate from the manufacturing engine.

**A2. The simple analogy.** Susan buys aluminium and copper, draws it into wire, winds it into coils for
transformer makers, or insulates it into cable that state power boards and contractors buy by tender to
carry electricity from substations to homes and shops. Alongside that, it also just buys raw aluminium rod
and resells it unprocessed, a trading sideline that briefly became over a third of revenue. Three plants,
all in Ghaziabad, Uttar Pradesh. It listed on the BSE SME platform 18-Jun-2026, three months before this run.

### PART B: THE TRANSITION

**B1. From → To.** Manufacturing line: from **R1 COMMODITY PRICE-TAKER** (winding wire and LT cable,
weak pricing power, margin exposed to metal-price pass-through) toward **R2/R3 boundary** (HT/MVCC cable,
where BIS/DISCOM qualification gives partial spec-in stickiness and a claimed ~20-25% product margin,
B05). Trading line: does not transition; it stays **R0/R1**, a resale sideline the company itself frames
as low-barrier (RHP p.24, B04).

**B2. The engine.** Two things must physically change: (1) manufacturing capacity must rise 7,500 to
12,000 km/yr by Feb-2027 (B05, B09), and (2) product mix inside that capacity must shift from winding wire
and LT cable toward HT/MVCC cable, which management states carries materially higher per-unit EBITDA
(B04, B05).

**B3. The proof gate.** Two hard, quarter-by-quarter observables, because the engine has two moving parts:
(a) CWIP appears and grows against the Rs 10.30 Cr Unit-III capex object (zero CWIP and nil capital
commitments at 31-Mar-2026, B02 Note 33.04, Note 32); (b) HT+MVCC share of quarterly revenue climbs from
the Q1 FY27 print of 7.11% (B05, 14-Aug-2026 press release) toward whichever FY27-end target management
confirms once the three figures on one call (15%, 50%, 30-35%, B05) are reconciled. Neither leg has fired
as of this run: capex utilisation was 11.7% of the object at 30-Jun-2026 (B02, B05) and HT+MVCC sits at
7.11%.

**B4. The recognition gap (OPEN QUESTION, resolved at Stage 11).** Whether the market already prices the
TO state (an HT/MVCC-weighted, better-margin cable maker) into the current quote is an open question this
dossier does not answer. Stage 11 resolves it via the destination-PE gap. No number or conclusion is stated
here.

**B5. The ugliness test.** Today's ugly optics are: operating cash flow negative in FY24, FY25 and FY26
against rising PAT (B01, B02); borrowings up from Rs 24.79 Cr to Rs 66.72 Cr over the same window (B02);
an emerging-moat score of 10.7, just under the 12-point floor (B07); and an accounting-quality score of
4/10 driven by restatement and thin disclosure (B02, B03). Two readings compete and neither is resolved:
**ARTIFACT-OF-CLIMB**, a doubling of revenue funded by working capital ahead of a capacity and mix build
that has not yet finished; or **STRUCTURAL-FEATURE**, cash flow was already negative in FY24 at much
lower growth and lower working-capital days, and days nearly doubled in FY25 without a matching growth
jump and never reverted (gate-recommendation.md FLAG-CASH). The gate recommendation calls this
INDETERMINATE and names the H1 FY27 cash flow statement as the resolving print. This dossier does not
choose a reading; B5 is left as the named contest, for the operator to classify at sign-off.

**B6. The transition falsifier.** HT+MVCC share stalls near 7-10% through FY27 while the ~50% claim is
quietly dropped without a reconciled explanation (B05 trigger, priority 2); or CWIP is still zero at Q3

specifically, independent of whether the underlying manufacturing business survives.

## SECTION 3: BUSINESS UNDERSTANDING NARRATIVE

*(Per prompts/13-synthesis-pipeline.md BUSINESS UNDERSTANDING NARRATIVE spec, drafted at Halt 1 from
B01-B09; Stage 13's copy is the final version. Reproduced from outputs/final/business-narrative.md,
opening five-question section, unmodified.)*

Susan Electricals makes power cables, overhead conductors and winding wire in three plants in Ghaziabad,
and it also resells raw aluminium rod that it does not process. Low tension (LT) cables and aerial bunched
cables carry power on the last stretch of a distribution network, from the local transformer to homes and
shops, and LT cable was about 31% of FY26 revenue. Winding wire is the aluminium or copper wire that
transformer makers wind into coils; it was 24% of revenue in Q1 FY27, down from 48% a year earlier. High
tension cable up to 33 kV and medium voltage covered conductor (MVCC), an insulated overhead line that
replaces bare wire for safety and reliability, form the new line and were 7% of Q1 FY27 revenue. The
aluminium rod trading line brought in ₹72 Cr in FY26, and all bought in products resold together came to
₹103 Cr, or 38% of revenue.

A discom cannot upgrade its network without BIS certified cable from a registered vendor, so the licence
and the registration matter more than the product. The buyers are state discoms in Uttar Pradesh, Haryana,
Telangana, Jharkhand, Bihar, Madhya Pradesh and Rajasthan, EPC contractors such as Monte Carlo and RVNL,
transformer makers, and buyers of raw metal. Discoms buy by tender at the lowest price, and the government
share of revenue fell from 91% in FY24 to 36% in FY26 as private buyers took over. These buyers do not stay
long: repeat customers fell from 89% to 45% of revenue over the same two years, and the top ten customers
fell from 98% to 64%.

Present demand comes from grid upgrade spending under RDSS, the central scheme for discom reform, with
₹2.81 lakh Cr sanctioned across 46 discoms. Demand should grow as MVCC replaces bare conductors under RDSS
and as data centres, electric vehicles and rail electrification add load, in a market the company's own
commissioned industry report puts at 8% a year to 2035. Whether that growth reaches Susan depends on
capacity rising from 7,500 to 12,000 km a year by February 2027. The scheme is also the main risk, because
₹2.81 lakh Cr of the ₹3.04 lakh Cr outlay was already sanctioned by January 2026 and the window closes in
FY27 or FY28 unless extended.

The cable lines hold the only evidenced advantage: eight BIS product licences, ISO certificates, and
vendor registrations in seven states that a new entrant needs time to earn. A funded rival can earn the
same registrations and buy the same imported CCV line from the same Chinese vendor, so the cannibalization
barrier scores zero, and talent asymmetry also scores zero. Winding wire shows no moat, and the one listed
winding wire peer earns a company margin of 4% to 5%. Trading has no moat by the company's own prospectus,
which calls its entry barriers relatively low. The emerging moat scan scores 10.7 against a 12 point floor
for modest development, which the run classifies as no meaningful emerging moat.

## SECTION 5: PLAIN-LANGUAGE SUMMARY

1. Susan Electricals makes power cables, aluminium conductors and copper/aluminium winding wire in three
   plants in Ghaziabad, Uttar Pradesh.
2. It also buys raw aluminium rod and resells it without processing it. This trading line made up 38% of
   FY26 revenue.
3. It listed on the BSE SME platform on 18 June 2026, three months before this report.
4. Buyers are state power boards (discoms), EPC contractors, and transformer makers who need certified
   winding wire.
5. A power board cannot buy cable without a BIS certificate and vendor registration, so the certificate
   matters more than the product itself.
6. The government share of revenue fell from 91% to 36% over two years as private buyers grew.
7. Demand is not the constraint. The company's total addressable market is roughly 179 times its current
   manufacturing revenue.
8. Whether that demand reaches Susan depends on building more cable capacity and shifting product mix
   toward higher-voltage cable, which needs capital spending that has barely started.
9. The mental model here is a climb: from a low-margin metal trader and wire winder toward a cable maker
   with some pricing power, proven only if capacity gets built and higher-voltage cable actually grows as
   a share of revenue.
10. The evidence for that climb is fragile. Five things must go right together, and three of the five rest
    mostly on what management says on calls, not on filed, audited numbers.
11. Cash flow from operations has been negative for three years running while profit rose, and borrowings
    nearly tripled. Whether that is normal for fast growth or a warning sign is not yet answered.
12. The company's own margin claim for its winding-wire segment matches that segment's revenue share to
    two decimal places, an unlikely coincidence a verifier flagged and no analyst on the single earnings
    call asked about.
13. The corpus could not establish who buys the traded aluminium rod, or who the single customer was that
    made up over a third of one year's revenue. Both stay unnamed in every filing read.
14. The corpus also could not establish a full-year segment profit and loss statement for any product line;
    all margin figures by product come from one earnings call, not from audited accounts.
15. The two biggest open questions: which of three different growth targets management actually meant on
    its one call, and whether the negative cash flow turns around once the first post-listing half-year
    results are filed.
