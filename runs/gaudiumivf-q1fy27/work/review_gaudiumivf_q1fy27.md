### 2C. Six mandatory diagnostic answers

1. **Did revenue grow YoY?** Yes. Standalone +11.44% (12.27 → 13.68 Cr), consolidated +9.13%
   (17.76 → 19.38 Cr). No Notion base case exists to compare against (pre-position). Growth is
   real but modest for a company that has just raised Rs.90 Cr fresh and is meant to be rolling
   out new centres — the growth is not yet capex-driven because 98% of the new-centre capex is
   unspent (A3-F6a).
2. **Did operating EBITDA margin expand, contract, or stay flat YoY?** Contracted hard.
   Standalone Q1FY27 15.90% vs Q1FY26 33.86% = −1,796 bps. Consolidated 12.51% vs 29.02% =
   −1,651 bps. Not sequential noise; this is a clean YoY comparison on the same-quarter base.
   Driver: sales-and-marketing expense (Note 7) jumped standalone 159.72L → 382.96L = **+140%**
   (consolidated 162.72L → 404.33L = **+148%**), against revenue up only ~11%. Marketing spend
   ran far ahead of the revenue it is meant to buy.
3. **Did core operating PBT (ex-Other Income) grow YoY?** No — it fell sharply. Standalone
   −61.80% (3.08 → 1.18 Cr); consolidated −67.24% (4.05 → 1.33 Cr). **Reported PBT/PAT
   overstates operational health.** The headline standalone PBT down only 25% and PAT down only
   29% conceals a core-operating decline of ~62%. Name the gap explicitly: reported PBT was
   held up almost entirely by Rs.1.12 Cr of new Other Income that did not exist a year ago.
4. **What drove the gap between core-operating-PBT decline and reported-PAT decline?** For
   standalone: Other Income +1.12 Cr (of which Rs.1.02 Cr is FD interest on unspent IPO cash,
   note 1e) added back almost the whole core shortfall; finance costs −0.17 Cr helped (debt
   repaid); tax −0.09 Cr helped. Each delta is quantified in the Step 4 bridge. The Other-Income
   line is the single reason the reported number does not look as weak as the operations.
5. **Are D&A and finance costs scaling faster than revenue?** D&A yes (SA +16.4%, Consol +18.8%
   vs revenue +9-11%) — mild absorption drag, consistent with pre-revenue centre build. Finance
   costs the opposite: down 24-35% because the IPO repaid all borrowings (Rs.1,807.37L, line
   921-923). Net: the company is de-levering while depreciation creeps up — a normal early-rollout
   signature, but with capex still 98% undeployed the absorption story has barely started.
6. **Is Other Income concentration changing?** Sharply. Other Income / PBT went 0.0% (Q1FY26) →
   48.86% standalone / 45.86% consolidated (Q1FY27). Strip it and the quarter is weak. This
   concentration is transient by construction: it is FD interest on Rs.56.12 Cr of unspent IPO
   proceeds and it **decays as the capex deploys** (A3-F1b, A3-F6a). This is the single most
   important interpretive point in the review.

## PLAIN-LANGUAGE BRIEF (MANDATORY FINAL SECTION)

### 1. Summary narrative

Gaudium IVF ran a soft operating quarter under a calm headline. Standalone revenue grew 11
percent to Rs.13.68 crore. Consolidated revenue grew 9 percent to Rs.19.38 crore. Reported
profit fell less than the operations did. Standalone profit after tax was Rs.1.66 crore, down
29 percent. Consolidated profit after tax was Rs.1.78 crore, down 42 percent. Those drops look
mild, but they are propped up by new money the business did not earn last year. Other income was
Rs.1.12 crore this quarter versus nil a year ago, and almost all of it is bank interest on the
IPO cash the company has not yet spent. Strip that interest out and the real operating profit
fell about 62 percent standalone and about 67 percent consolidated. The reason is marketing.
Sales and marketing spend more than doubled, up 140 percent, while revenue rose only 11 percent.
That crushed the operating margin from about 34 percent to about 16 percent standalone. So the
key question for a buyer is simple. Is that marketing spend buying future patients, or is it
just money going out the door. The filing does not answer it. The balance sheet is clean. The
company repaid all its debt with IPO money and now sits on about Rs.56 crore of unspent cash.
The auditor gave a clean opinion with no warnings. But 98 percent of the money earmarked to
build new fertility centres is still unspent five months after listing, so the growth story has
not actually started. There is no prior thesis on file and no position. This is an
evidence-gathering name. The right move is to wait for two things, both close: the FY26 Annual
Report, due before the September 28 AGM, and the Q2 results in November, which bring the first
cash flow statement and the first honest read on whether the marketing is working. Cash
conversion cannot be judged yet because a Q1 filing carries no cash flow statement.

### 2. Sector intelligence

Sector is IVF, fertility, and women's-health hospital services. **[Provenance: this quarter's
filing confirms single segment "Health Care Business", lines 294 / 616; sector label corrected
from the prior run's erroneous "Pharma/CDMO" tag.]** This is a hospital-services business, not
a drug maker and not a CDMO. Demand for fertility care in India is structural and rising, driven
by later marriages, later first pregnancy, and wider willingness to seek treatment. **[Provenance:
general knowledge, not in this filing.]** The model is mostly cash-pay and out-of-pocket. IVF is
largely outside insurance in India, so pricing is set by the clinic and volumes track disposable
income and brand trust rather than payer reimbursement. **[Provenance: general knowledge; this
filing discloses no payer mix.]** Regulation matters: the ART (Regulation) Act now governs
fertility clinics and raises compliance and record-keeping cost, which tends to favour organised
branded chains over single-doctor clinics. **[Provenance: general knowledge, not in this filing.]**
The listed comparison set from the prior run is HCG, Rainbow Children's, and Kaya. **[Provenance:
prior-run / peer context, not this filing.]** HCG is oncology hospitals, Rainbow is pediatric and
maternity, Kaya is skin clinics; none is a pure IVF peer, so the peer read is directional only.
What the filing does not disclose and the sector view needs: cycle volumes, average revenue per
IVF cycle, success rates, occupancy, and city-level mix. All ND this quarter.

### 3. Business-model intelligence

The company earns money two ways, and this quarter shows one of them wobbling. The core is the
fertility and women's-health clinic business, run in the listed parent. **[Provenance: this
filing, standalone P&L.]** On top sits a subsidiary, Gaudium International, which is a drugs and
consumables trading operation: consolidated drug purchases are Rs.7.07 crore versus only Rs.0.92
crore standalone, so the extra Rs.6.15 crore of drug buying sits in the subsidiary. **[Provenance:
this filing, standalone vs consolidated, lines 228 / 522.]** The clinical service cost is
identical in standalone and consolidated in every period, which tells you the subsidiary does no
clinical work; it trades product. **[Provenance: this filing, A3 F3, lines 227 / 520.]** Here is
the model drift this quarter. The subsidiary added only Rs.0.11 crore to consolidated profit,
versus Rs.0.73 crore a year ago, even though its revenue held around Rs.5.7 crore. Its net margin
fell from about 13 percent to about 2 percent. **[Provenance: this filing, standalone-vs-
consolidated PAT gap, A3-F2.]** Whether that is a one-off inventory timing swing or a permanent
squeeze on the trading arm is unresolved and is a management question. On unit economics the
honest read is limited: the filing gives no cycle counts, no revenue per patient, and no
occupancy, so per-unit profitability is ND. The one hard number is capital. The company is now
debt free, holds about Rs.56 crore of unspent IPO cash, and earns bank interest on it that is
currently doing most of the heavy lifting in reported profit. **[Provenance: this filing, IPO
utilisation table and Note 1e.]** That is a placeholder, not a business. The model only proves
itself when the Rs.49 crore of unspent centre-building money turns into open centres and paying
patients.

### 4. Competition intelligence

Where Gaudium can win: it is an organised, branded, single-specialty fertility chain in a market
still full of single-doctor clinics, and it is now debt free with cash to expand while smaller
players cannot. **[Provenance: this filing for the balance sheet; general knowledge for market
structure.]** Where it is structurally weaker versus named peers: it is far smaller and
single-specialty. HCG has a national oncology network, Rainbow has a multi-city pediatric and
maternity footprint, and even Kaya has a broad clinic chain. **[Provenance: prior-run / peer
context and general knowledge, not this filing.]** None of those is a direct IVF competitor, so
the sharper competition is other fertility chains such as the larger branded IVF networks, which
this filing does not name. **[Provenance: general knowledge; peer detail not in this filing.]**
The competitive risk to watch is customer acquisition cost. Marketing spend doubled this quarter
and margin fell hard. In a cash-pay, brand-led market, if a company has to keep spending 140
percent more on marketing to grow revenue 11 percent, a bigger and better-known chain can outspend
it or win the same patients cheaper. **[Provenance: this filing, Note 7 S&M line.]** So the
competitive test and the margin test are the same test, and it resolves next quarter: does the
marketing convert to patients, or is it the price of standing still against stronger brands.
