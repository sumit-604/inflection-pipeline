PAT rose 21.24% to Rs 25.02 Cr (FILED). About 71% of that rise came from interest on unspent IPO money and a one-time tax credit (INFERENCE). Per share, Q2 profit is flat to slightly down on the full post-IPO share count (Rs 2.04 vs Rs 2.06, INFERENCE). Cash from operations in the half fell 43% to Rs 22.06 Cr (FILED). Management promises margin recovery from Q3, but says it will be partial. This is a first read. No thesis exists. The decision is to watch. The Q3 gross margin will show if Q2 was a timing lag or a loss of pricing power.

### 2. SECTOR INTELLIGENCE
- Input cost: polymer prices moved from about Rs 92/kg to Rs 160/kg (MGMT). Management calls it the highest in 30 years. Converters with conversion-cost contracts recover the rupees per kg but lose percentage margin while prices rise (MGMT, INFERENCE).
- Freight: Red Sea disruption raised export freight (FILED, press L831-833). The size is not disclosed.
- Demand: management cites pet food growth and a shift from paper bags to woven PP bags because paper is scarce and costly (MGMT). No external data was passed to test this.
- Cycle: management says Q3 and Q4 are seasonally stronger (MGMT). FY26 H2 revenue was 16.9% above H1 (FILED).
- Not disclosed: industry capacity, polymer price data, freight index, export market growth. Peer comparison (HCP Plastene Bulkpack and others): not passed this run; no peer claim is made here.

### 3. BUSINESS-MODEL INTELLIGENCE
- How it makes money: Knack converts polypropylene into printed, laminated woven bags and pinch-bottom bags, sold to food, pet food, fertiliser, cement and other buyers in 75 countries (FILED deck, press). About 45-50% of customers buy on a conversion-cost formula linked to polymer (MGMT). The biggest customer reprices each quarter on the prior quarter's average (MGMT).
- Unit economics (INFERENCE on filed revenue and MGMT tonnes): realisation Rs 262/kg in Q2 vs Rs 240 in Q1 and Rs 195 a year ago. Operating EBITDA per kg Rs 35.60 in Q2 vs Rs 51.98 in Q1 and Rs 32.25 a year ago.
- Model drift: three items. First, growth is now price-led on a full plant (90-91% used, MGMT). Second, profit leans on treasury income until the Rs 320 Cr Borisana plant is built (0.22% spent, FILED). Third, a loss-making JV in Mexico now needs group funding beyond its equity (FILED, INFERENCE).
- Mix lever: pinch-bottom bags are 21% of revenue vs 17-18% last year (MGMT). Tonnes for this line are not disclosed.
- Not disclosed: export share, realisation per kg, customer shares for H1 FY27, receivable days by market, unit-wise capacity.

### 4. COMPETITION INTELLIGENCE
- Where Knack claims to win: about 10.1% share of Indian flexible bulk PLWPP bags in FY25, and the first laser-cut, easy-open pinch-bottom bag in India and Asia (deck citing Technopak; MGMT/SECONDARY, not verified). Customer stickiness: packaging is only 1-2% of the buyer's product cost (MGMT). FY26 customer continuity 93.75% (FILED deck).
- Where it is weaker: capacity is full until Oct-2027, so it cannot take share on volume. One customer contract equals about 18% of FY26 revenue and ends Dec-2028 (FILED deck, INFERENCE). Pricing power at peak polymer is partial: management expects to get 3-4% when it asks for 5% (MGMT).
- Named peers: HCP Plastene Bulkpack has a Notion page that was not passed. No peer numbers are used. Competitive risk to watch: whether rivals with spare capacity hold prices while Knack pushes pass-through. That is not observable in this quarter's filings.
