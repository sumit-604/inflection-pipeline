
# VRL Logistics Ltd (VRLLOG): business narrative, phase 1

Run vrllog-2026-11-12. Phase 1 lite: evidence only. Valuation has not run, so this file has no entry zone, no decision and no destination multiple. Units: Rs Cr unless stated. The annual report and the results filings state Rs lakh. Figures from them are converted at 100 lakh to 1 Cr and rounded for reading. The sources for each paragraph are listed at the end.

## BUSINESS UNDERSTANDING NARRATIVE

VRL Logistics moves small consignments by road across India on its own trucks, and less than truckload (LTL) parcel freight was 90% of FY26 revenue. A shipper hands goods to one of 1,337 branches, and VRL pools them with other loads at one of 50 hubs, runs them on an owned truck and delivers them at the far end. Full truckload (FTL) hire was 9% of revenue and fills spare trucks and return trips, while courier and scrap sales were under 1% each. A shipper with a few cartons cannot pay for a whole truck, so a scheduled network that pools small loads is its only way to move goods between states at a known price.

The customers are more than 10 lakh businesses registered for GST, and the largest is 1% of goods transport revenue while the top ten are 3%. Most pay at booking or delivery, and credit accounts were only 16% of H1 FY27 bookings. No qualification cycle and no contract floor hold these customers, and management's own figures show 14% to 18% of customers leaving each year through the price reset while 15% to 20% joined.

Present demand is the goods these businesses ship. Textiles are 17% to 18% of VRL volume and agri inputs 10% to 11%, so end customer sector output for textiles, FMCG, agri inputs, pharma and autos is the first demand signal. GST collections and e-way bill generation track goods movement and the shift of parcels to carriers that comply, and the diesel retail price sets about a quarter of cost and, by management's rule of Rs 2 a litre for 1% of freight, much of the price.

Formalisation should move volume from small operators, since over 80% of fleet owners run fewer than five trucks, but the run found no figure for that migration, and the 70% unorganised share is management's claim with no source. Peers show the market growing at about 10% a year: TCI Express quarterly tonnage rose 7.5% and Delhivery PTL tonnage rose 14% to 18%, and both are quarterly prints anyone can track. MoRTH vehicle scrappage and 15 year vehicle rule notifications may push small operators out, but the same rule forces VRL to replace 814 of its own vehicles, 13.7% of the fleet.

Only the LTL line shows a moat, and it is moderate: the Emerging Moat scan scored 12.8, just above the floor of 12, on network density, owned land and hubs, the delivery record and the balance sheet. The run did not measure the economic benefit of any of the four, because VRL discloses neither productivity per branch nor rent saved per owned hub. Talent asymmetry (Category 21) and the cannibalisation barrier (Category 22) both scored zero, because the owned fleet and hubs are a lead built over 50 years, not a structure a rival would have to break its own model to copy. The FTL line has no moat. Bulk diesel buying was once a cost edge, but it fell from about 40% of fuel in FY26 to 7% in H1 FY27 because bulk diesel cost more than retail.

Parcel trucker proved price, now prove volume

VRL is a road parcel carrier, 50 years old, run by the Sankeshwar family. The family held 60.2% at September 2026 and will hold 63.4% after the buyback, because it did not tender. FY26 revenue was Rs 3,221 Cr and PAT Rs 237 Cr. The company owned 5,941 vehicles at September 2026, and fleet, buildings and leased premises make up 89% of its assets. Working capital is only 3% of revenue, about 12 days, because most customers pay at booking or delivery.

The change is a price reset. Over FY25 and FY26 VRL dropped contracts that earned low margins and raised its rates. FY26 tonnage fell 7% to about 3,955 thousand tonnes on the company's quarterly chart, while revenue rose 2%. Realisation per tonne therefore rose about 10%. Operating EBITDA margin, which is revenue less freight, staff and other costs and before other income, rose from 18.2% to 20.2%. Smaller credit notes and discounts gave 37% of the revenue growth, and that part does not repeat. H1 FY27 is the first half year with both legs: tonnage rose 10% to 2,099 thousand tonnes, and Q2 realisation rose 9% to Rs 8,840 a tonne. Tonnage is still 3% below H1 FY25, so the volume lost to the reset has not fully come back.

The headline Q2 FY27 margin of 23.3% includes other income and divides by total income. On the operating basis, Q2 was 23.0% and H1 was 22.1%, against management's band of 21% to 22%. Gate 0 rates the business GOOD at 74 of 160, with a core of 63 of 100 and a moat score of 11 of 60. Shareholder alignment is the strongest block at 18 of 20. The balance sheet is the weakest at 6 of 20, because the current ratio is 0.49 and debt including leases equals equity. The GOOD label is fragile. The framework verifier showed it falls to AVERAGE if the missing capex years FY17 to FY24 score zero, and it sits on the band floor once the promoter window is scored strictly. The capex lines in the older annual reports settle the first question.

Cash looks better than it is. Operating cash flow was Rs 654 Cr in FY26, 97% of EBITDA, and receivables are 10 days with 0.6% over six months. But operating cash comes before about Rs 196 Cr a year of lease payments. Free cash after leases was Rs 159 Cr in FY26 and minus Rs 79 Cr in FY25. In FY26, free cash after lease payments and interest was Rs 127 Cr, and dividends paid were Rs 175 Cr, part of it the FY25 final dividend. Disclosed ROCE of 24.6% leaves out Rs 698 Cr of lease liabilities. With leases in, ROCE is 17.4%. Net debt excluding leases fell to Rs 337 Cr at September 2026 from Rs 440 Cr in March. That figure nets Rs 281 Cr of investments held for the Rs 280 Cr buyback, which settled on 9 November. After settlement, net debt excluding leases is about Rs 617 Cr on the run's arithmetic, with Rs 683 Cr of leases on top. FY27 capex now points to Rs 308 Cr to Rs 318 Cr, against the Rs 220 Cr to Rs 240 Cr band given in August.

Management earns a B for credibility. Of 18 promises over four quarters, it delivered 11, partly delivered 4 and missed 3. It has met or beaten its tonnage, realisation and margin guidance since May 2026, and it executed the buyback as announced. But the beat came against a guide it had set low. The FY27 tonnage guide was 10% to 11% in February, was cut to 6% to 7% in May with no reason, and then rose to 8% and to 9% to 10%. The capex guide changed three times. In the November call management gave the margin three ways: 21% to 22%, 23% plus, and 21% plus. The calls never answered ton kilometres, the earlier promoter stake sales or the October director resignation. The red flag verifier also found an own fleet claim walked back: lorry hire rose, a capacity shortage was admitted, and the May statement on scrappage was reversed in August.

The promoter read is TRUSTWORTHY, but by a thin margin and from a partial search. The run found no pledge, no regulatory action and no loans to group companies. Related party flows are 0.46% of revenue. Against that, the promoters sold about 9.4 points of equity for about Rs 512 Cr in December 2022 and September 2023 and gave no reason. A promoter company bought the bus division in 2022 for Rs 230 Cr, 6.7 times its net worth. The run did not find the valuation report or the minority vote. The FY26 related party note stopped naming counterparties line by line. The chairman, aged 76, is reappointed from January 2027 with fixed pay up 48%. The books have no database audit trail, the March 2026 stock statement to banks was not filed, and the auditor is in its first year. A Rs 52 Cr income tax demand sits outside the contingent liability table as "very remote". SEBI, MCA, NCLT and court registries were not searched.

Peers confirm two things. Every peer passes diesel on with a lag of days to a month, and no LTL or PTL peer earns near VRL's margin. TCI Express earned 12.7% in Q2 FY27, and Delhivery PTL earned about 11% in Q3 FY26. The gap overstates VRL's edge, because VRL owns its trucks and books their cost below EBITDA, while peers that hire trucks book that cost above it. Peers contradict the claim that the 9% realisation rise is mostly fuel. TCI Express netted 1.5% of price, TCI put the fuel effect at 1% to 3%, and Delhivery put less than 30% of its PTL yield rise down to cost inflation. On VRL's own Rs 2 a litre rule, fuel explains between half and two thirds of the 9%, depending on which diesel rise is used. The rest is price that a fuel fall need not take back but competition might. TCI is the only peer that names VRL, and it says VRL takes express volume on lower price. TCI also warns that the Western DFC could hurt fleet owners on some routes.

The runway is MODERATE. The only market size built on filings puts the serviceable market at about Rs 6,945 Cr, the revenue of three named organised players, and VRL already holds 46% of it. That gives headroom of 2.2 times. The market grows at about 10% a year, and a normal share gain implies VRL revenue growth of about 11% a year. A vendor estimate puts all freight below a full truck at Rs 3.1 lakh Cr, 13.5 times the figure built on filings, but its definition is not visible. In H1 FY27 VRL grew tonnage at market pace, so it is riding the market, not taking share. The revenue of the unlisted organised carriers (Safexpress, Gati, V-Trans) is the number that would move the runway from MODERATE toward STRONG.

## The four facts the intake named

MARGIN PAIR. Closed on the figures. The 23.3% (Q2 FY27) and 22.6% (H1 FY27) tie to the presentation and include other income over total income. The operating basis is 22.97% and 22.14%. The company's stated reason is "Increase in Volumes & Freight Rates leads to increase in margins". Open: which margin is the plan, and which Q2 cost lines (staff, admin, toll) do not repeat.

VOLUME AFTER THE PRICE RESET. Closed on the figures. FY26 tonnage was 3,955 thousand tonnes, minus 7.4%, from the chart labels; the annual report says minus 7%. H1 FY27 was 2,099 thousand tonnes, plus 10%. The guidance trail is 10% to 11% (February), 6% to 7% (May), 8% (August), and 9% to 10% (November); the intake trail missed the February start. Open: the tonnage lost to dropped contracts is NOT FOUND.

PROMOTER AND RELATED PARTIES. Closed on the figures. The buyback was Rs 280 Cr at Rs 320 for 87.5 lakh shares, 3.94 times subscribed, and the promoters did not tender, so their stake goes to 63.41%. The earlier sale was two sales: Vijay Sankeshwar sold 5.4% for about Rs 273 Cr on 14 December 2022, and Anand Sankeshwar sold 4% for about Rs 238 Cr in September 2023 (press reports). The related party note shows flows of 0.46% of revenue, and promoter company Vijayanand Travels bought the bus division, the air business and some fixed assets. Open: the bus sale valuation and vote, and whether VRL leases property from promoter entities.

CASH AND CAPEX. Closed on the figures. H1 FY27 operating cash flow was Rs 362.5 Cr, net debt excluding leases was Rs 337 Cr against Rs 440 Cr, leases were Rs 682.5 Cr, and H1 capex was Rs 158 Cr. Open: whether internal cash covers the buyback plus capex. Management said net debt would end the year flat. Its own funding arithmetic (Rs 500 Cr less Rs 240 Cr is Rs 260 Cr, against Rs 280 Cr) does not close, and the Q3 FY27 balance sheet is the test.
