## Business Understanding Narrative

**What does it do, and how does it make money?** Tirupati Forge makes carbon
steel forged flanges, forged components and automotive components from one
five-acre site at Hadamtala, Rajkot. It reports a single operating segment
(Outcome of Board Meeting, FY2027, Q1, page 3). Installed forging capacity is
15,000 tonnes a year, with ring rolling at 15,000 TPA and a 630-tonne Lasco
hydraulic extrusion press at 10,000 TPA. It sells into oil and gas, defence,
aerospace, power, mining and automotive, and 55% of revenue comes from overseas,
largely the USA, Canada, Malaysia, Europe and Africa (Outcome of Board Meeting,
FY2026, Q4, pages 25 and 26).

**What is changing?** Volume, hard. FY26 revenue from operations was Rs 162.48
crore against Rs 114.98 crore in FY25, up 41% (Outcome of Board Meeting, FY2027,
Q1, page 3; Reg. 34(1) Annual Report, FY2025, Q4, page 59). The company is
raising capital to expand again.

**Why now?** This is the question the numbers answer badly. Revenue grew 41% and
profit fell. FY26 net profit was Rs 6.30 crore against Rs 7.86 crore in FY25
(Outcome of Board Meeting, FY2027, Q1, page 3; Reg. 34(1) Annual Report, FY2025,
Q4, page 58). Earnings per share fell to Rs 0.51 from Rs 0.74.

**What must be true for the thesis to work?** The incremental volume must start
earning. Something in the FY26 mix, price or cost absorbed the entire benefit of
a 41% revenue increase and then some.

**What breaks it?** It is already breaking. Q1 FY27 continued the pattern:
revenue Rs 38.10 crore against Rs 32.30 crore, up 18%, and net profit Rs 1.22
crore against Rs 1.41 crore, down 13% (Outcome of Board Meeting, FY2027, Q1,
page 3). Two consecutive periods of rising revenue and falling profit is not
noise.

## 2. Business model and archetype

One engine: convert steel bar into forged shapes and sell by the tonne. Revenue
is tonnes multiplied by realisation per tonne. Margin is the spread between
realisation and steel cost, less power and labour, against a fixed asset base.

The FY26 cost structure shows where the spread went (Outcome of Board Meeting,
FY2027, Q1, page 3), Rs lakh for the year ended 31 March 2026: revenue 16,247.85;
cost of materials consumed 6,965.54; purchases of stock in trade 1,394.18; other
expenses 5,487.37. Other expenses are 33.8% of revenue and larger than half the
material cost. Depreciation was Rs 772.28 lakh and finance costs Rs 304.66 lakh,
both up sharply on a bigger asset base.

**Archetype: commodity converter.** Section 1B v3.7 Amendment 17 binds any later
valuation: spot-year ROCE and rupee-denominated working-capital trends must not
be fed into Section 1B or FTTCP for this name. Spread, utilisation, cycle
position and cost-curve rank are the variables.

## 3. Competitive advantages

Claimed, thin, and issuer-sourced. The company says it is "Amongst only 3 firms
in India to have installed 630 Ton of Lasco Press Line" and "Amongst leading
Indian suppliers of flanges", holding IATF 16949:2016, ISO 9001:2015, PED AD
2000 and CRN certifications (Outcome of Board Meeting, FY2026, Q4, page 25).

Take the equipment claim at face value and it is still not a moat. A press is
purchasable; three firms today is not three firms in five years. The
certifications are table stakes for export flanges, not differentiators. CRN
matters for Canada and PED for Europe, so they open markets rather than protect
margin.

The FY26 result settles it. A business with pricing power does not grow revenue
41% and lose absolute profit. **Moat absent.** This is a price-taker converting
steel.

## 8. Proof check

**The proof check fails, and it fails twice.**

The triggers claim growth through capacity. Revenue did grow: 41% in FY26, 18%
in Q1 FY27. So the volume trigger delivered.

But the last two periods move the wrong way on the line that matters. FY26 net
profit fell 19.9% on 41% more revenue. Q1 FY27 net profit fell 13.6% on 18% more
revenue. The proof point for a capacity thesis is operating leverage, meaning
profit rising faster than revenue. The observed result is the exact inverse in
both periods.

The named absence: there is no disclosure anywhere in this corpus of realisation
per tonne, capacity utilisation, or the steel cost the spread is measured
against. The one number that would explain the margin collapse is not published.

## 11. Posture: the Transition Decision Matrix

- **Proof gate: NOT FIRED.** Revenue delivered; profit did not. The gate is
  profit.
- **Ugliness: STRUCTURAL.** Falling net margin across three years on a rising
  asset base, with two-fifths of pre-tax profit coming from currency, is not the
  shape of a build absorbing start-up cost. There is no commissioning event in
  this corpus to which the margin fall can be attributed, and the fall began in
  FY25 to FY26, before the newest expansion.
- **Recognition gap: CLOSED, emphatically.** Market capitalisation of Rs 924.06
  crore against FY26 net profit of Rs 6.30 crore is **146.7 times trailing
  earnings**. On Q1 FY27 annualised it is 189.6 times. The R1 neighbourhood on
  the quality ladder is near 12x.

**Posture: AVOID.** The matrix has an explicit dominating overlay: gap closed
plus structural ugliness maps to AVOID. Here the gap is not merely closed, it is
inverted by an order of magnitude.

## 12. Verdict card

**PASS. Do not run `/step1`.**

A 147 times trailing multiple on a commodity forging business whose absolute
profit is lower than it was two years ago, diluting shareholders to fund more of
the same capacity, is the clearest AVOID in this run. No further machinery is
warranted.

This verdict rests on filed numbers, not on judgement about the business. The
forging operation is real, exports 55% of output, holds the right certifications
and has no reported debt default. The objection is entirely to the price against
the earnings and the direction of the margin.

What would change the view, and it would need all three:
1. **Two consecutive quarters of profit growing faster than revenue**, showing
   the capacity finally earning.
2. **Disclosure of realisation per tonne and utilisation**, so the margin
   collapse has an explanation that is not simply price-taking.
3. **A very large fall in the price.** At Rs 6.30 crore of annual profit, the
   current capitalisation prices roughly twenty years of flawless execution.

Load-bearing facts a deep run would verify first, should the view change: the
FY26 annual report and balance sheet, the exchange price-movement query, the
"Action(s) initiated or orders passed" filing, the promoter holding, and the
terms and dilution schedule of the July 2026 promoter warrants.

No price target. No position.
