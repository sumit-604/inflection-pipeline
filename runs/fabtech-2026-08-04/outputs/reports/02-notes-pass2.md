## NEW FINDINGS BY NOTE

### 1. Property, Plant & Equipment — Note 5 standalone (AR p.115) and Note 49/47 Leases (AR standalone p.136, consolidated/RHP p.354-355)

- **A ₹19.71 Cr freehold-land purchase FROM A RELATED PARTY, entirely absent from the Related Party Transactions note.** Note 5's PPE footnote states: "Freehold land has been acquired from Fabtech Technologies International Private Limited (Formerly Fabtech Technologies International Limited) during the year and title deed is transferred vide agreement dated November 6, 2024." Amount: **₹1,970.84 Lakh** (Note 5, gross block, "Owned — Freehold land," AR standalone p.115). Fabtech Technologies International Pvt Ltd is a **"company/firm in which directors/KMP have significant influence"** per Note 47(I)(e) — i.e., a promoter-controlled related party, and the same entity that sold FT Institutions Pvt Ltd and FABL International Technologies LLP to the company in FY24 (see finding 5 below). **This ₹19.71 Cr capital-asset acquisition from a related party does not appear anywhere in Note 47's related-party transaction table** (which lists Purchases, Installation, Sales, Rent, Remuneration, Reimbursements, Interest Income, Investment, Trademark Charges — no "Property"/"Fixed Asset acquisition" line). Pass 1's related-party purchase percentages (~11.7% of revenue, ~22% of purchases) covered only Purchase of Stock-in-trade and would entirely miss this item. 🔴 **Red Flag** — a related-party capital transaction of this scale should cross-reference into the RPT note; an investor reading only Note 47 would never see it. Question for management: was this land independently valued; on what terms; why does it not appear in Note 47?

- **A near-11x, one-year lease-liability build-up (₹28.97 Cr ROU asset addition) with no lessor identified.** Note 49 (standalone, AR p.136) / Note 47 (consolidated, RHP p.354): Lease liability balance rose from **₹262.13 Lakh (1-Apr-2024) to ₹2,902.59 Lakh (31-Mar-2025)** — additions of **₹2,790.97 Lakh** in FY25 alone (matching ROU-asset additions of **₹2,897.30 Lakh** the same year, Note 5/49). The balance then stayed roughly flat into FY26 (₹2,676.44 Lakh, additions of only ₹40.28 Lakh). This is one of the single largest balance-sheet movements in the two years of data reviewed and **Pass 1 did not surface it at all** despite Pass 1's own extraction checklist naming "ROU and lease liability amounts" as a target. The Leases note gives no breakdown by lessor or property. 🔴 **Red Flag** — scale (₹26-29 Cr) and total absence of counterparty disclosure warrant a management question.

- **Circumstantial evidence the FY25 lease counterparty is the related party Fabtech Turnkey Projects LLP.** Cross-referencing Note 47(II) (AR standalone RPT table, p.133) against RHP Note 45(I): "Rent paid" to **Fabtech Turnkey Projects LLP** was a steady ₹78.00 Lakh (FY23) → ₹82.50 Lakh (FY24) → **₹96.00-97.30 Lakh (FY25)** across three years, then **collapsed to ₹8.50 Lakh in FY26** (AR Note 47, p.133) — a ~91% one-year drop. In the same window, the **security deposit** held with Fabtech Turnkey Projects LLP fell from ₹97.80 Lakh (FY23/FY24) / ₹91.44 Lakh (FY25, RHP Note 45(II)) to **₹0.25 Lakh (FY26, AR Note 47(III))**. Fabtech Turnkey Projects LLP is independently disclosed elsewhere (Note 25, borrowings security details) as the **titleholder of the company's own Andheri (Janki Centre) office premises pledged as bank-facility collateral**, so it is a confirmed real-estate-holding related party with an existing rental relationship with the company. The timing (rent collapses and deposit is returned/adjusted in the same year the company's lease liability balance stabilizes after its FY25 jump) is consistent with — but not explicitly confirmed by any note as — a conversion of the pre-existing Turnkey LLP tenancy into the large capitalised Ind AS 116 lease. **NOT FOUND IN DOCUMENT: explicit lessor identification for the ₹26-29 Cr lease.** 🔴 **Red Flag** — flag as an unresolved, high-materiality management question; do not treat as confirmed without management response, but the circumstantial fit is strong enough to warrant top-tier scrutiny.

- **Finance costs rose 122.8% YoY (₹195.18 Lakh → ₹434.94 Lakh, standalone Note 35, AR p.124) despite falling bank borrowings, driven almost entirely by lease-liability interest.** Interest expense on lease liabilities alone rose from ₹29.22 Lakh (FY25) to **₹249.24 Lakh (FY26)**, a +753% increase (Note 35/49). This is a material, previously unflagged offsetting fact to Pass 1's "deleveraging post-IPO" narrative (Pass 1 Section 7): an investor reading only the Borrowings note would conclude finance costs are falling; they are not — total finance costs more than doubled, entirely because of the new lease. 🟡 **Watch** — nuance/correction to Pass 1's borrowings section, material for any run-rate profitability model.

### 2. Trade Receivables — ECL provision matrix, Note 13 standalone (AR pp.117-118)

- **The ECL rate CURVE SHAPE changed materially YoY — not merely "flat," but a specific, unexplained change in estimate concentrated in the middle ageing buckets.** FY25 loss-allowance rates escalated with age in the normal pattern: <6mo 0.25% → 6mo-1yr 1.10% → 1-2yr 2.04% → 2-3yr 3.46% → >3yr 10.48%. FY26 rates instead show: <6mo 0.36% → 6mo-1yr 2.82% → **1-2yr 10.10% → 2-3yr 10.10%** → >3yr 10.16% (Note 13(b), AR standalone p.118). The **1-2yr bucket rate nearly quintupled** (2.04%→10.10%) and the **2-3yr bucket rate nearly tripled** (3.46%→10.10%) in a single year, while the >3yr rate — the bucket the auditor's KAM is specifically about — barely moved (10.48%→10.16%, essentially flat to slightly down). No note explains this change in estimate methodology. This sharpens Pass 1's Red Flag #2 (flat ECL) into something more specific and more discussable with management: the company effectively harmonised its 1-2yr/2-3yr/>3yr rates to a common ~10% band this year, which increases FY26 provisioning conservatism in the middle buckets but leaves the oldest, KAM-flagged bucket's rate essentially unchanged. 🔴 **Red Flag** — NOT FOUND IN DOCUMENT: rationale for the change in ECL curve shape; a direct question for management/auditor.

- **Balances not yet confirmed by counterparties — Note 50 standalone (AR p.137, a two-line note easy to miss).** "The balances in respect of trade receivables and payables and loans and advances, as appearing in the books of accounts are subject to confirmations from the respective parties and are pending reconciliations/adjustments arising there from, if any. The same is not expected to have any material impact on the financial statements." This is a standard boilerplate note but, layered directly on top of the auditor's KAM on receivables recoverability (Pass 1 Section 4) and the ECL curve-shape change above, it means the very trade-receivable and loan balances at the centre of the KAM had **not been independently confirmed by counterparties** as of the reporting date. 🟡 **Watch** — compounds, rather than resolves, the uncertainty already flagged as the auditor's sole KAM.

- **Advances against supplies to individual promoter-family members**, not just corporate RP entities: Mr. Aatif Khan ₹1.18 Lakh (FY26)/₹1.11 Lakh (FY25); Mr. Aamer Aasif Khan ₹2.47 Lakh (FY26)/₹2.47 Lakh (FY25) (Note 47(III), p.134). Small in absolute terms but a related-party-to-individual cash-advance channel Pass 1 did not capture.
- **Interest income earned from related parties, by counterparty** (Note 47(II), p.134): Fabtech Technologies International Pvt Ltd ₹45.42 Lakh, FABL International Technologies LLP ₹66.54 Lakh, Fabtech Technologies LLC UAE ₹214.23 Lakh, Mark Maker Engineering Pvt Ltd ₹10.00 Lakh — all FY26. ### 5. CARO clause (vii)(a) statutory-dues qualification — the actual rupee amount, found for the Holding Company

Pass 1 flagged the clause (vii)(a) "unfavourable or qualified" CARO finding across three entities (Holding Company, FT Institutions Pvt Ltd, Mark Maker Engineering Pvt Ltd) but could not locate the underlying quantum. The **standalone Auditor's Report Annexure A (AR p.92)** for the Holding Company itself gives the figure: **TDS under Income Tax Act Section 194C, ₹22,371 (≈ ₹0.22 Lakh), relating to FY 2025-26, "Rs. 4,979 and Rs. 17,392 paid"** — i.e., the entire qualifying amount was effectively cleared shortly after year-end. This is immaterial in scale and **substantially deflates the governance-signal severity of Pass 1's Red Flag #10 as it applies to the Holding Company specifically** — a trivial, already-remediated TDS-timing lapse, not a systemic statutory-dues failure. **NOT FOUND IN DOCUMENT: the equivalent quantum for FT Institutions Pvt Ltd or Mark Maker Engineering Pvt Ltd** (their standalone CARO annexures are not reproduced in this AR). 🟡 Watch (downgraded from Pass 1's implicit Red-Flag framing, for the Holding Company only — the other two entities' amounts remain open).

- **Cross-check that reconciles cleanly (a positive finding):** CARO Annexure A (AR p.90) discloses total guarantees outstanding at year-end of **₹4,429.57 Lakh**, provided "to Customers of the company." This equals the contingent-liability note's performance guarantees (₹2,829.57 Lakh, Pass 1 Section 3) **plus** the related-party corporate guarantees to Fabtech Technologies Cleanrooms Ltd and Fabsafe Technologies Pvt Ltd (₹1,000.00 + ₹600.00 = ₹1,600.00 Lakh) = ₹4,429.57 Lakh exactly. 🟢 Internal consistency across the CARO annexure and the contingent-liability note holds up; not itself a finding of concern, but worth recording as a check that passed.

### 6. Multi-year context from the RHP that refines (does not reverse) two Pass 1 findings

- **Trade payables turnover, three-year context:** RHP Note 46 (Analytical Ratios, consolidated) shows FY23 1.95x → FY24 2.04x → **FY25 2.59x** ("Company has paid off payable within due time, which lead to improve ratio" — company's own explanation) → **FY26 (AR) 2.17x**. Pass 1's "payable days lengthening" framing compared only FY25→FY26; the fuller run shows FY25 was itself a payment-discipline improvement year, and FY26 is a partial reversal of that improvement rather than a multi-year secular deterioration. 🟡 Watch — nuance, not a reversal, of Pass 1 Section 8.
- **Debt-equity ratio volatility, three-year context:** RHP Note 46 shows consolidated Debt-Equity ratio swinging from 0.39x (FY23) to 0.07x (FY24) to 0.32x (FY25), before the IPO reset it to a net-cash position in FY26. Leverage was not on a steady deleveraging path pre-IPO; it whipsawed. Relevant context for anyone modelling the company's "normal" capital structure. Also newly visible: **standalone ROE nearly cratered from 27.25% (FY25) to 8.86% (FY26)**, and ROCE from 20.05% to 12.07% (Note 48/44 standalone, AR pp.129/135) — entirely explained by the IPO equity-base increase per the company's own disclosure, but a reminder that pre-IPO return ratios are not a reliable read-through to the post-IPO capital base. 🟡 Watch.
