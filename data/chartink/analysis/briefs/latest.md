## The picture in one paragraph

Monday was a relief day inside a falling market. After a week with three heavy down days, 144 stocks rose four and a half percent or more, against 73 that fell that hard. Risers had not beaten fallers since 16 October. 33 of 35 indices closed up. The buying was light. Turnover fell to about 519 crore from about 611 crore on Friday, and the heavy volume share dropped back to ordinary. The deep damage barely moved. Only 597 stocks hold their 200 day line, about one in four, against 1,357 in early August. 304 stocks made a new yearly low and nine made a new high. No thrust day came, the kind of day when buying is so wide that almost everything rises, and none has come since 12 June. Fear rose with prices, and the VIX closed above 17. The slide in breadth holds until buying turns wide, heavy and repeated.

## Where the market stands

Picture the market as about 2,240 stocks. Each stock has three trend lines. The short one averages the last 20 days. The middle one averages 50 days. The long one averages 200 days. A stock above a line is in an uptrend on that clock. On Monday 597 stocks sat above the long line. That is about one stock in four. 286 sat above the middle line, about one in eight. 254 sat above the short line, about one in nine. Friday's short count was 178. So Monday lifted the short clock by about 75 stocks. The long clock moved by 20.

The year gives these counts their scale. The spring bottom came in late March. On 23 March the long count fell to 287, the lowest reading of 2026. That day 950 stocks fell four and a half percent or more. A short bounce followed and failed. On 27 March more than 600 stocks fell hard. On 30 March more than 700 did, and more than 900 stocks made a new yearly low. Only then did the real turn come. On 1 April more than 1,100 stocks rose hard in one session. On 8 April almost 900 did. Those were thrust days. After them the long count climbed for four months, to 1,357 on 7 August.

Autumn has been a staircase down from that peak. A shock came on 15 September, when more than 350 stocks fell hard. A repair followed. A second shock came in early October, and a second repair lifted the long count to about 935 on 16 October. Then last week broke the floor. 384 stocks fell hard on Wednesday, 128 on Thursday and 359 on Friday. The long count fell to 577 on Friday. Monday's 597 is the first step back.

Today's count is about double the March bottom. That is the honest comfort in the data. The market is weak, and it is not at the spring extreme. It sits about where it stood on 8 and 9 April, when the climb out of the bottom had just begun. The difference is direction. In April the count was rising off a thrust. Tonight it is falling off a peak.

Inside the session, buyers held more ground than on any day last week. About one stock in four traded four percent above its prior close at some point. Nearly four in ten of those kept the gain into the close. On Wednesday only one in seven did. About one stock in five traded four percent below its prior close, and a third of those finished near the low. About six stocks in ten closed up.

Volume is the weak part. Turnover reached about 519 crore. That is below Friday's 611 crore. It is still above the year ago level of about 434 crore, so the market is not quiet by long standards. The share of stocks on heavy volume, against those on light volume, fell from the top of its recent range to about the middle. Friday's selling came on heavy volume. Monday's buying came on light volume. That is the shape of a pause in selling more than the arrival of fresh demand.

The far end of the damage has not repaired. More than one stock in four is down ten percent or more over five days. That share sits at the top of its whole history, as it did on Friday. 27 stocks are down twenty percent or more over five days, against eight up that much. The extreme stretch eased a little. About one stock in twenty trades more than ten percent below its 10 day average, down from about one in fourteen on Friday.

New highs are almost absent. Nine stocks made a yearly high on Monday. 304 made a yearly low. Friday had 453 lows, so the flood eased. Four stocks in ten sit within fifteen percent of a yearly low. Fewer than one in eleven sit that close to a yearly high.

The large companies hold up better than the whole market. Among the 500 largest stocks, a little more than half trade above their 200 day average. In the whole market it is one in four. The weakness lives in the smaller names. Yet the large share is not strong either. On 23 March, at the spring bottom, it stood a little higher than it does tonight. The Nifty itself is down about seven percent over three months and sits about four percent under its thirty week line. 30 of 35 indices sit under that line.

Fear rose on an up day. The India VIX measures what traders pay to protect against big swings in the Nifty. It rose almost two percent on Monday and closed near 17.2. ## Thirty points

### For the trader

**1. The bounce came, and it is not proven.** 144 stocks rose hard against 73 that fell hard. Risers had not won since 16 October. A proven bounce needs a second and a third day like it, and then a thrust.

**2. The bounce is the size of the autumn's failed bounces.** On 6 October 199 stocks rose hard. On 14 October 147 did. Both came inside the same slide. Monday's 144 belongs to that family until a bigger day arrives.

**3. Volume did not back the buying.** Turnover fell from about 611 crore on Friday to about 519 crore. The heavy volume share dropped from the top of its recent range to the middle. Heavy selling followed by light buying is the pattern of a pause.

**4. No thrust day yet.** A thrust needs about 250 or more big risers on heavy turnover. The last one came on 12 June. The spring bottom ended with a day of more than 1,100. Breakout trades stay penalised until a thrust prints.

**5. March warns against the first bounce.** After the shock of 23 March, two days of bounce followed. Then heavier selling came on 27 and 30 March, with more than 600 and then more than 700 hard fallers. The real turn came only on 1 April. The first bounce after a shock is often a pause.

**6. Strong openings held better.** Nearly four in ten stocks that traded four percent above their prior close kept the gain. On Wednesday one in seven did. That is the best reading of the past week. Buying strength inside the day stays penalised until that share passes about half.

**7. The short clock lifted from the floor.** 254 stocks hold the 20 day line, up from 178. That is still about one in nine. A long swing trade still runs against the short trend in most of the market.

**8. The stretch eased only a little.** About one stock in twenty trades more than ten percent below its 10 day average, down from one in fourteen. More than one in four is still down ten percent in five days. The snap back has begun, and most of the stretch remains.

**9. Fresh shorts late in a fall carry more risk.** 27 stocks are down twenty percent or more in five days. 128 stocks turned up from deeply sold daily readings. A crowded short side meets a widening bounce. Fresh shorts in stretched names are penalised by that setup.

**10. The VIX rose on an up day.** It closed near 17.2, up almost two percent. Prices rose and protection got dearer. Protection buyers still expect big swings, and they did not stand down on the bounce.

**11. The index and breadth agreed.** 33 of 35 indices rose, and about six stocks in ten closed up. This was a fair day, not an index trap. A trap would show the Nifty up with most stocks down, and Monday did not.

**15. Banks did not join.** The Bank Nifty rose less than a fifth of a percent. Large lenders steadied on the size list, but the index barely moved. A bounce without the banks has a weak floor under the Nifty.

**17. Froth stays low.** 20 stocks rose ten percent or more, and only eight are up twenty percent over five days. The rights entitlement rows at the top of the gainer list do not count. The risk is a lack of buyers, not a blow off.

## What would change this read

The read is a relief day inside a confirmed slide in breadth, on light volume, with fear rising. It turns worse if the selling returns before the bounce widens. Another day of 300 or more hard fallers within the next week would repeat the late March pattern, when a bounce failed into heavier selling. A long count under about 450 would put the market back to the first days of April. New yearly lows above about 700 would say the same, and only three days in this record have passed that mark. A VIX close above about 18 while the Nifty falls as hard as the small names would say the large company shelter has failed. A slide in the IT index while breadth stays this weak would take away the index's last prop.

The cautious read fails if buying turns wide, heavy and repeated. The first test is a thrust day: about 250 or more big risers on turnover above Friday's 611 crore. The second test is the long count back above about 840, the old autumn floor, within a few sessions. The third test is new lows back under about 100 while new highs climb past about 50. A VIX close back under about 15 would support all three. Monday passed none of these tests. It passed two smaller ones. Risers beat fallers for the first time in six sessions, and nearly four in ten intraday gains held. A second day like Monday on heavier volume would start to change the read. A quiet fade back into heavy selling would confirm it.
