## PART 3: CITATION FINDINGS

### Finding 1 — MAJOR: internal citation inconsistency (Emmbi capex quote)
B06 Part 1, Q5 verdict narrative states: *"a 3,000-tonne addition funded on a 'low capex cycle' (Rs 7-10cr, Aug 2021 call, p.12)"*. The Aug 12 2021 (Q1 FY22) call, page 12, actually contains a different, correct citation ("Around 5 Crores to 6 Crores... not expecting any major capex during next 18-24 months"). The "Rs 7-10cr... low capex cycle" quote with the 3,000-tonne addition is verbatim-correct but is located in the **Nov 8 2021 (Q2 FY22) call, page 10**, not the Aug 2021 call. Notably, B06's own Part 3 Peer Coverage Map correctly attributes this same quote to "Nov 8 2021... p.10" — so the report is internally inconsistent between Part 1 and Part 3 on this citation. Decision-relevant content is unaffected (correct evidence exists, correct classification stands), but a reader following the Part 1 citation to source-check it would fail to find the quote there.

### Finding 2 — MAJOR: mis-cited call, and conflation of two distinct trade agreements
B06 Part 1 Q1 net read states: *"per the same peer's Aug 2025 call p.16-17, Bangladesh at 'about 12% of the EU imports'"*, and B06 Part 1 Q4 states Kanpur *"frames the pending India-EU FTA and 0% EU duty as a structural offset to Bangladesh's current ~12% EU import share (Aug 2025 call, p.16-17)"*. Neither the "12% of EU imports" figure nor any EU-FTA/0%-duty content appears in the Aug 19 2025 call at pages 16-17 (that section covers US tariff exposure and CRISIL rating risk factors only). The actual source is the **Feb 20 2026 (Q3 FY26) call, page 16**: *"Bangladesh had a 0% duty in EU, which India will have from next year... today Bangladesh has about 12% of the EU imports."* This also conflates two distinct facts: the Aug 2025 call's FTA discussion (p.12) is about the **UK**-India FTA specifically ("India was facing stiff competition in UK from Bangladesh and Vietnam... about UK first, so I think the FTA is a brilliant move"), while the 0%-duty/12%-EU-imports figure concerns the separate **EU**-India FTA, sourced only from the Feb 2026 call. Presenting both under one Aug-2025 citation risks a reader conflating the UK and EU trade-agreement threads.

### Finding 3 — MAJOR: mis-cited call (forex hedging policy, appears 3x)
B06 states in Part 2 (2E), in the YAML `risks_peers_raise` list, and implicitly in the May 2026 peer_coverage_map row: *"Formal, weekly-reviewed forex hedging policy (Kanpur Q4/FY26 call, May 4 2026, p.13)"*. This quote — *"we follow a specific hedging policy for our foreign exchange exposures... reviewed on a weekly basis every Saturday"* — is not present at May 4 2026 call p.13 (that page covers Asia-market competitive intensity). It is verbatim-correct but located in the **Feb 20 2026 (Q3 FY26) call, page 13** (Manoj Agarwal answering Parth Patel). The same wrong attribution is repeated in three places in the report (narrative, coverage-map contribution list, and the YAML risks list), suggesting a systematic quarter-mislabelling rather than an isolated typo.

### Finding 4 — MAJOR: unflagged ambiguity in a load-bearing quote
B06 Part 1 Q4 states: *"standard product could be around 12% EBITDA, the other [food-grade/complex] could be around 7% EBITDA"* (Aug 2025 call, p.8), and this margin split is carried into Part 4 as *"the single strongest independent confirmation"* of HPBL's specialised-vs-commodity margin-durability claim. The actual transcript text is genuinely ambiguous on directionality: *"The difference of the margin that is there between standard product and food grade product is substantially high. One could be around 12% of EBITDA, the other could be around 7% EBITDA."* — the passage never explicitly states which of "standard" or "food grade" maps to 12% versus 7%. B06's chosen mapping (standard=12% higher, food-grade=7% lower) runs counter to the conventional expectation that specialised/food-grade product commands the margin premium, and if inverted, would undercut rather than support HPBL's forward-margin-band claim. B06 should have flagged this as an interpretive judgment call rather than presenting it as settled fact, particularly given its use as the report's single strongest corroborating data point. (Note: the cleaner, unambiguous FIBC-vs-fabric breakdown from the Feb 2026 call, p.7 — FIBC 12.5-13.5% vs fabric/multifilament ~7% — is correctly cited and does not carry this ambiguity; B06's reliance on that cleaner data point in Part 4 partially offsets this issue but does not resolve the Aug-2025 citation's own ambiguity.)

### No findings of type "substantive without findable citation" or "verdict upgraded from silence"
Despite the four mis-citations above, in every case the underlying quoted content is genuine, verbatim-accurate, and drawn from an actually-read peer transcript — just attributed to the wrong quarter's call. No fabricated evidence was found. No CITED-ONLY peer was found on spot-read to contain claim-relevant material that B06 missed (Emmbi Jun 2021 and Feb 2022 calls were confirmed as reasonably classified).
