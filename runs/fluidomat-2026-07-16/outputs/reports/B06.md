# B06 — Peer Concall Verification: Fluidomat Ltd (FLUIDOMAT)
Run date: 2026-07-16
Peers: ELECON (Elecon Engineering Company Ltd, four transcripts, Q1-Q4 FY26) and TRF Ltd (one transcript, Q4 FY21, stale)

Segment note: ELECON's Gear and Material Handling Equipment (MHE) divisions are power-transmission /
rotating-equipment adjacent to Fluidomat's fluid-coupling niche — same end-customer sectors (power, steel,
cement, sugar, mining, ports) but different specific product (gearboxes and bulk-material-handling systems
vs. fluid couplings). TRF is a bulk-material-handling / EPC player, also MHE-adjacent, but its only available
transcript is from June 2021 (Q4 FY21, COVID-era, ~5 years stale). Weighting below reflects this: ELECON
evidence is treated as substantively relevant with partial-mapping caveats stated; TRF evidence is used only
for structural/industry points (competitive landscape, legacy-project risk) and explicitly flagged as stale
wherever cited for any current-demand claim.

---

## PART 1: CLAIM-BY-CLAIM VERIFICATION

### Claim 1 — Government-driven demand tailwind (25 new thermal plants, 15 coal mining projects, 500 GW
power target, 300 MTPA steel target) and pace of order conversion in power-transmission/rotating equipment

**Verdict: PARTIALLY VERIFIED (directional tailwind), with an explicit caution on conversion pace**

Peer evidence:
- ELECON repeatedly cites power and steel capex as the principal demand driver across all four FY26
  quarters: "there is a good opportunity for us as MHE to grow... because there are many projects that
  have already been announced by the various end users like NTPC and other state government bodies"
  (ELECON, Q3 FY26 call, Kaushik Patel, MHE head). "We also received very good orders from the L&T, MHI,
  and QLR... we are getting the orders from the power industry" (ELECON, Q3 FY26, Dipak Dalwadi, Gear
  head). Q4 FY26: "steel is also now expanding their executions. And power, of course, they are demanding
  the projects to be closed very fast" (ELECON, Q4 FY26, Dipak Dalwadi).
- However, no peer transcript ever cites the specific national targets Fluidomat's management used — 25
  new thermal plants, 15 coal mining projects, 500 GW power target, or 300 MTPA steel target. These
  specific figures are **UNVERIFIABLE**: informative silence, not corroboration or contradiction.
- On pace of conversion, the peer evidence is the more consequential finding. ELECON's order-to-revenue
  conversion has been repeatedly slower than management's own guidance across the whole of FY26: Q3 FY26
  order intake was flagged by an analyst as "the slowest in the last eight quarters" (ELECON, Q3 FY26,
  Sanjay Ladha, analyst, unchallenged by management); FY26 guidance was cut mid-year ("FY26 revenue may be
  lower by up to approximately 5 percent and adjusted EBITDA margins may be lower by up to approximately 2
  percent compared to our earlier guidance," ELECON, Q3 FY26, Narasimhan R., CFO); and even that revised
  guidance was missed, with Q4 FY26 Gear division revenue down 21% YoY on "delayed order inflows, extended
  dispatch timelines and customer-led deferment of delivery amid ongoing macroeconomic and geopolitical
  uncertainties" (ELECON, Q4 FY26, Chintan Shah, CFO).

Peers silent: TRF's 2021 transcript predates the current power/steel capex cycle entirely and offers no
comparable evidence; excluded from this claim, other than as an illustration that MHE-adjacent order books
have historically been lumpy and slow to convert (TRF's own order book was 60% "legacy" — orders running six
to eight years — Alok Krishna, MD, TRF, June 2021).

Net read: The directional tailwind (power and steel capex driving orders) is corroborated by the one live
peer available. But the specific government targets Fluidomat cites are never corroborated by any peer, and
the "pace of conversion" half of the question is answered with caution-worthy evidence: ELECON's own
order-to-revenue conversion has been persistently slower than guided throughout FY26, undercutting any
assumption that the tailwind translates briskly into revenue. If Fluidomat's own execution shows a similar
order-to-revenue lag, that would be an industry-wide pattern, not company-specific weakness — but it is not
evidence of a fast-converting boom either.

### Claim 2 — Unquantified input-cost-pressure or product/customer-mix-shift margin headwinds in FY26, and
whether peers can quantify raw-material cost drivers

**Verdict: PARTIALLY VERIFIED**

Peer evidence:
- Product/customer-mix shift as a margin headwind is corroborated strongly and repeatedly, with actual
  quantification of the mix (unlike Fluidomat's own apparent gap): ELECON discloses engineered-product (EP)
  vs. catalogue-product (CP) mix every quarter — Q1 FY26: EP 43% / CP 57% (Kamlesh Shah, Group CFO); Q2 FY26:
  EP 46% / CP 54% (Narasimhan R., CFO); Q3 FY26: EP 48% / CP 52% (Narasimhan R.); Q4 FY26: EP 45% / CP 55%
  (Management, Q4 FY26 call). Management repeatedly attributes EBIT margin declines to this mix shift plus
  employee-cost inflation, e.g., "The EBIT margin declined to 18.2 percentage in Q2 FY '26 compared to 21.5
  percentage in the same quarter last year, primarily due to the increase in employee costs and change in
  the product mix" (ELECON, Q2 FY26, Narasimhan R.).
- Raw-material cost pressure specifically is where ELECON's own commentary is internally inconsistent
  across quarters, which is itself informative. In Q3 FY26 (January 2026): "there is no such increment in
  the commodity markets... our raw metal prices are almost stagnant... we don't see any increase" (ELECON,
  Q3 FY26, Dipak Dalwadi) — a direct statement that raw-material cost pressure was NOT a factor at that
  point. By Q4 FY26 (April 2026), management reverses: "we are anticipating there is going to be an increase
  in some raw material costs or logistic costs. We are already filling that into our new orders" (ELECON,
  Q4 FY26, Aayush Shah, Director) — but this remains unquantified, exactly as Fluidomat's own disclosure gap
  is described in the handoff.
- No peer quantifies a specific raw-material cost driver (e.g., a percentage or per-unit steel/alloy cost
  increase) in any of the four calls.

Peers silent: TRF (2021) does not discuss raw-material costs in a way relevant to FY26 conditions; excluded.

Net read: Peers corroborate that margin headwinds in FY26 are real, industry-wide, and driven predominantly
by product/customer mix (which peers CAN quantify, unlike Fluidomat) plus employee cost — but on the
specific raw-material cost driver, peer evidence is genuinely unquantified and even self-contradictory
quarter to quarter (stagnant in Q3, rising in Q4). This matches, rather than resolves, Fluidomat's own
disclosure gap: the "unquantified" nature of the input-cost claim looks to be a sector-wide reporting norm,
not evidence being withheld selectively by Fluidomat.

### Claim 3 — Capacity utilisation near Fluidomat's stated ~96%, and comparable capex cycles with clearly
stated, non-conflicting completion timelines

**Verdict: CONTRADICTED (on utilisation level), PARTIALLY VERIFIED (on capex-cycle comparability)**

Peer evidence:
- ELECON discloses a specific capacity utilisation figure only once, in the Q4 FY26 call: "capacity
  utilization is concerned, we are at this moment with the addition of capex is around 56% to 60%
  utilization" (ELECON, Q4 FY26, Dipak Dalwadi, Gear head). This is far below Fluidomat's stated ~96% and
  directly contradicts any assumption that ~96% utilisation is a normal or achieved level for a comparable
  power-transmission equipment maker in the same period. The segments are adjacent, not identical (custom
  gearboxes vs. fluid couplings), so the gap may partly reflect different product mix, customer-driven
  dispatch deferrals inflating WIP/finished-goods rather than genuine idle capacity, or a straightforward
  definitional difference in how "utilisation" is measured — but the magnitude gap (56-60% vs. ~96%) is wide
  enough to flag as a real point of divergence rather than noise.
- On the capex cycle: ELECON is in an active, clearly and consistently stated multi-year capex programme —
  "our capex plan... of these INR400 crores between FY26 to FY28" (ELECON, Q4 FY26, Chintan Shah, CFO),
  restated consistently across all four quarters (Q1: "capital expenditure budget for FY '26 to FY '28 is
  INR 400 crores," Kamlesh Shah; Q2: "capex budget for FY '26 to FY '28 is estimated at INR400 crores,"
  Narasimhan R.; Q3: same figure, Narasimhan R.). Spend-to-date was reported consistently too (~INR95 crore
  spent by Q4 FY26 of the INR400 crore total, per Chintan Shah). This is a genuinely non-conflicting,
  clearly stated multi-year timeline — comparable in structure (not magnitude) to a capex-led capacity
  build.
- Notably, ELECON is expanding capacity while utilisation sits at 56-60%, and while acknowledging
  "accelerated depreciation without corresponding revenue contribution from the new facility" as an active
  margin drag in Q1 FY26 (Kamlesh Shah) — i.e., building capacity ahead of demand materialising, which is
  the opposite of a capacity-constrained ~96%-utilisation story.

Peers silent: TRF's 2021 transcript discusses no capex programme of comparable scale (TRF was preserving
cash, not expanding); excluded from magnitude comparison but useful for the general observation that
capital-intensive equipment makers in this space have historically over-built relative to demand (see Part 2C).

Net read: The utilisation comparison is a genuine point of tension: if Fluidomat's ~96% is accurate and
anchored per Section 1B v3.3, it is materially higher than the one peer that discloses a number, which
warrants a flag rather than automatic credit as an industry-standard "tight capacity" story. The capex-cycle
comparison, by contrast, is structurally similar (both are multi-year, internally-funded expansion
programmes with stated completion timelines) and does not show conflicting timelines.

### Claim 4 — Distinct aftermarket/spares revenue line behaving as an annuity independent of new-order
cyclicality

**Verdict: PARTIALLY VERIFIED**

Peer evidence:
- ELECON does disclose a distinct, and sizeable, aftermarket/service revenue line, confirming that such a
  line exists and is material in this industry: "for this quarter Q1 from our revenue, we have generated
  32% from service, which includes both breakdown services or the spare parts plus refurbishment also"
  (ELECON, Q1 FY26, Kamlesh Shah, Group CFO, Gear division). For MHE: "the service component in this
  quarter is 41% in MHE sector" (ELECON, Q1 FY26, Kamlesh Shah). Q4 FY26: MHE margins were "supported by a
  balanced contribution from equipment sales and aftermarket services" (ELECON, Q4 FY26, Chintan Shah).
- However, ELECON's own commentary undercuts the "annuity, independent of new-order cyclicality" framing.
  In the Q3 FY26 call, when asked why MHE margins were unusually strong, management responded: "the other
  case is that if you see the product and the service mix, so that also includes in the margins. So this
  quarter, that is another one case, the service portion from the revenue that also impacted the margins"
  (ELECON, Q3 FY26, Management) — i.e., the service/aftermarket proportion itself swings quarter to quarter
  and is treated as another product-mix variable that moves WITH the broader business cycle, not as a
  stable, counter-cyclical annuity stream.
- ELECON is also actively building an export refurbishment/service capability (U.S. service team already
  placed, Europe planned — Kamlesh Shah, Q1 FY26), suggesting the aftermarket line is still in a growth/build
  phase internationally rather than a mature, steady annuity.

Peers silent: TRF's 2021 transcript does not disclose a distinct spares/aftermarket revenue line; TRF's
business in that period was legacy EPC project completion and retention-money recovery, structurally
different. This silence is worth noting: not every peer in this adjacent space frames its business around a
spares annuity.

Net read: Peer evidence supports the *existence and materiality* (30-41% of quarterly revenue in the one
peer with a comparable segment) of an aftermarket/spares line in power-transmission-adjacent equipment
manufacturing — this is a genuine, quantifiable phenomenon, not something unique to Fluidomat. But it does
NOT support the specific "annuity, independent of new-order cyclicality" characterisation: the peer's own
data shows this line's contribution moving with, not against, quarterly cyclicality.

### Claim 5 — Export demand trends for power-transmission/rotating equipment vs. Fluidomat's declining
standalone export sales alongside its 15+-country global-footprint claim

**Verdict: PARTIALLY VERIFIED (one peer corroborates; direction aligns)**

Peer evidence:
- ELECON's global-footprint claim is larger in scale but structurally the same kind of claim: "global
  footprint spans nearly 95 countries" (ELECON, multiple quarters, e.g., Q2 FY26, Prayasvin Patel, CMD).
- Despite this footprint claim, ELECON's actual export/overseas revenue has been flat-to-declining across
  FY26: Q1 FY26 overseas revenue fell 7% YoY (INR124 crore vs. INR133 crore), attributed to Middle East
  conflict-driven delivery holds (Kamlesh Shah, Q1 FY26); even adjusting for the ~INR14 crore directly tied
  to those holds, the underlying overseas trend was still soft (confirmed by Kamlesh Shah under direct
  analyst questioning). An analyst explicitly challenged management on this gap in Q3 FY26: "if we
  benchmark export against FY '23 pace, the business has delivered only a marginal annual growth of around
  4% to 5% despite a consistent effort and focus" (ELECON, Q3 FY26, CA Garvit Goyal, analyst) — a claim
  management did not dispute, only reframed around a longer-term FY30 ambition. By Q4 FY26, international
  revenue share had fallen further to 18% of consolidated revenue (from 21-24% in earlier FY26 quarters),
  with management stating exports had merely been "maintained" at prior-year levels, not grown, and that
  "the Middle East obviously has suffered significantly" (ELECON, Q4 FY26, Aayush Shah, Director). New US
  tariffs (50% under "Section 2(32)") were cited as a further, distinct export headwind prompting a new
  Mexico assembly subsidiary (ELECON, Q4 FY26, Management).
- This directly aligns with Fluidomat's own pattern of a broad multi-country footprint claim (95 countries
  for ELECON vs. 15+ for Fluidomat) coexisting with declining or stagnant actual export realisation.

Peers silent: TRF's 2021 transcript contains no export commentary relevant to current conditions; excluded.

Net read: One live peer (ELECON) corroborates the direction of Fluidomat's claim — a broad global-footprint
claim can and does coexist with declining/underperforming actual export revenue in this industry, driven in
ELECON's case by Middle East conflict and US tariffs specifically. Because only one peer provides usable
evidence (TRF is silent/stale), this cannot be upgraded to VERIFIED under the two-independent-peer standard,
but the alignment is clear and the underlying causal drivers (geopolitical disruption, tariffs) are
plausible, generalisable risks that could equally affect Fluidomat, even if the precise causes differ.

## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

**2A Demand environment.** ELECON's four quarters show a consistent management narrative of "underlying
demand remains healthy/constructive" repeated almost verbatim every quarter, even as actual results
disappointed and guidance was cut mid-year (Q3 FY26) and then missed again (Q4 FY26, Gear revenue -21% YoY).
This is a pattern worth flagging generically: management commentary asserting resilient underlying demand
should be weighed against the peer's own repeated inability to convert that demand into revenue on the
timeline it originally guided. Sector consensus across ELECON's calls: power and steel are the strongest
verticals; cement is "stagnant," more brownfield than greenfield (Dipak Dalwadi, Q4 FY26); sugar was weak all
year but hoped to recover for the FY27 crushing season on a speculative ethanol/crude-substitution logic tied
to the same geopolitical conflict depressing exports (Dipak Dalwadi, Q4 FY26) — an internally tension-filled
narrative, since the same war narrative is blamed for both export weakness and hoped-for sugar-sector demand.

**2B Pricing and input costs.** No peer describes strong pricing power; margin commentary in all four ELECON
quarters centres on product/customer mix and employee cost, not raw-material pricing power. Raw-material
commentary itself flipped from "stagnant, no increase expected" (Q3 FY26) to "anticipating... increase in
raw material costs or logistic costs" (Q4 FY26) within two quarters — a real-time illustration of how
quickly an input-cost narrative can shift in this sector and a caution against treating any single-quarter
"stagnant" claim as durable.

**2C Capex cycle.** ELECON is expanding capacity (INR400 crore Gear + INR35-40 crore MHE, FY26-28) while
running at only 56-60% utilisation (Q4 FY26) and while accelerated depreciation on newly capitalised assets
is actively dragging margins ahead of matching revenue (Q1 FY26). This reads as an industry-wide pattern of
capacity being built ahead of demand materialising, not a lone-expander story — but it also means that if
several peers across this equipment niche are capitalising new capacity simultaneously, the sector could see
a capacity race that outpaces near-term order growth, a genuine oversupply risk worth naming explicitly in
synthesis. If Fluidomat's own capex program is timed similarly, this is "industry-wide capacity race," not
"a lone expander."

**2D Competitive mentions.** One genuinely high-value, unprompted data point: in the ELECON Q3 FY26 call, MHE
head Kaushik Patel named TRF directly and unfavourably: "TRF has already announced to shut down their
material handling operations," alongside "Mcnally Engineering... has become bankrupt" (ELECON, Q3 FY26,
Kaushik Patel). This corroborates, from an active competitor's mouth, the distress visible in TRF's own 2021
transcript (accumulated losses, negative reserves of INR282 crore on INR11 crore equity, chronic reliance on
parent Tata Steel, no forward guidance given — Alok Krishna/N.S. Raghu, TRF, June 2021) and confirms that
exit has continued or completed since. No peer transcript mentions Fluidomat by name or unmistakable
description — expected, given Fluidomat's narrower fluid-coupling niche versus ELECON's gear/MHE and TRF's
bulk-handling/EPC focus.

**2E Risks peers discuss that may not appear in Fluidomat's own analysis.**
- Geopolitical/tariff pass-through risk to exports: Middle East conflict, Strait of Hormuz disruption risk to
  the UAE entity specifically, and 50% US tariffs under "Section 2(32)" (ELECON, Q4 FY26, Aayush Shah) — a
  concrete, named, current risk category.
- Persistent gap between order-book strength and actual revenue conversion, driving repeated guidance cuts
  within the same fiscal year (ELECON, Q3 and Q4 FY26) — an execution-credibility risk distinct from demand
  risk.
- Legacy M&A goodwill impairment risk: ELECON recognised a INR102 crore goodwill impairment in Q4 FY26
  tied to a 2010-11 European acquisition (Elecon, Q4 FY26, Chintan Shah) — a balance-sheet risk category tied
  to inorganic history that would not appear in an organic-growth small cap like Fluidomat but is a useful
  category to check for in any peer or acquisition-active company.
- Capital-allocation drag from large conservative investment balances: ELECON holds INR700-800 crore in
  conservative instruments yielding only ~8%, cited by management itself as diluting ROCE (Chintan Shah, Q4
  FY26) — a capital-efficiency risk relevant to any capex-heavy company sitting on excess cash.
- First-of-a-kind defense-order margin drag: ELECON's initial Navy gearbox order carried 1-2 percentage
  points lower margin due to "developmental initiatives" (Chintan Shah/Dipak Dalwadi, Q4 FY26 and Q3 FY26) —
  a specific execution-learning-curve risk category for any company diversifying into new defense-adjacent
  work.
- TRF's own 2021 disclosures illustrate, as a structural cautionary tale (not current evidence), the downside
  of undiversified, EPC/legacy-project-dependent equipment manufacturing: negative net worth, chronic losses,
  MSME late-payment interest provisions inflating finance costs, and complete dependence on parent-company
  goodwill for any capital-structure repair (Alok Krishna/N.S. Raghu, TRF, June 2021).

## PART 4: TRIANGULATION SUMMARY

- Claims fully VERIFIED (two or more independent peers corroborating direction and magnitude): **0 of 5**.
  This is a structural finding in itself: with only one live peer (ELECON) providing current, substantive
  evidence and TRF too stale to corroborate anything current, no claim in this batch can clear the
  two-independent-peer bar for full VERIFIED status, regardless of how strong ELECON's single-peer evidence
  is. Downstream synthesis should treat every finding below as single-source (ELECON) corroboration, not
  cross-peer consensus.
- Claims PARTIALLY VERIFIED: **4 of 5** (government tailwind direction/pace caution; margin-headwind
  direction/RM-unquantified; aftermarket line existence without annuity-behaviour; export-vs-footprint
  pattern).
- Claims CONTRADICTED: **1 of 5** — capacity utilisation. Fluidomat's stated ~96% utilisation sits well above
  the one peer datapoint available (ELECON Gear division, 56-60%, Q4 FY26), a genuine numeric divergence that
  should be flagged rather than smoothed over, even allowing for imperfect segment mapping.
- Claims UNVERIFIABLE (no peer evidence either way): the specific government policy targets cited by
  Fluidomat (25 new thermal plants, 15 coal mining projects, 500 GW power target, 300 MTPA steel target) are
  never mentioned by any peer in any of the five transcripts — informative silence on the precise figures,
  even though the general power/steel capex direction is corroborated.

**Single most consequential contradiction:** The capacity-utilisation gap (Fluidomat ~96% vs. ELECON's
disclosed 56-60% for its Gear division in the same period) is the most concrete, numeric point of tension in
this batch. If Fluidomat's own utilisation figure is accurate, it implies either a materially tighter
capacity position than the nearest comparable peer, a different capacity-measurement convention, or a
genuinely different (and much smaller-scale) manufacturing footprint where utilisation math behaves
differently — any of which is worth a direct question to Fluidomat management rather than an assumption.

**Single strongest independent confirmation:** ELECON's repeated, quantified disclosure of product/customer
mix (engineered vs. catalogue) as the dominant margin driver — corroborated in every one of its four FY26
quarters with specific percentages — strongly supports the general shape of Fluidomat's margin narrative
(mix-driven, not simply raw-material-driven) even though it cannot resolve the specific magnitude of
Fluidomat's own raw-material cost claim.

**Overall assessment:** The peer set complicates more than it simply supports or undercuts Fluidomat's
narrative. It supports the broad direction of the demand story (power and steel capex is real and
peer-corroborated) and the general shape of the margin story (mix-driven headwinds, unquantified
raw-material effects are a sector norm, not evidence withheld). It complicates the pace and confidence with
which that demand should be assumed to convert into revenue (ELECON's own guidance has been cut and then
missed within the same fiscal year) and complicates the capacity-utilisation claim outright with a lower
peer benchmark. It neither confirms nor undercuts the aftermarket-annuity framing cleanly — the underlying
revenue line exists at scale but does not behave as an independent annuity in the one peer that discloses
it. Given that only one of the two peers supplied usable, current evidence, every finding here should be
weighted as single-source corroboration, not peer consensus, when carried into synthesis.
