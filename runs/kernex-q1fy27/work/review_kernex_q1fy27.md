### Six mandatory diagnostics (consolidated basis)

1. **Did revenue grow YoY?** Yes — massively: +800.4% (9.00x) to Rs503.58 Cr from Rs55.93 Cr. Against the Notion base-case FY27 of Rs1,200 Cr, this single quarter is 42% of the full-year base. Against the order book Rs3,641 Cr (incl GST, L272), ~13.8% of the gross book was billed in one quarter — lumpy execution off the CLW order (45% supplied, L273), not a stable run-rate. The >Rs500 Cr level trips the Notion early-warning line (item below Step 6).

2. **Op EBITDA margin YoY?** Expanded: Q1 FY27 32.33% vs Q1 FY26 22.49% = **+984 bps**. But this is AFTER absorbing the new Rs30.05 Cr KAVACH/signalling warranty provision (Note 8, L274) sitting in Other Expenses — a ~597 bps drag. Ex-warranty operating EBITDA would be Rs192.87 Cr / 38.30% margin. The reported margin sits at the TOP of the Notion Green band (28-32%) even carrying that charge — consistent with the FTTCP "margin STAGNANT/FIRING at peak" read.

3. **Did core operating PBT (ex-OI) grow YoY?** Yes: +1506% to Rs147.65 Cr from Rs9.19 Cr. Other Income is only 0.33% of PBT (L186) — this is genuinely operational, NOT treasury-driven. This is the one honest bull signal in the quarter: unlike many small-caps, the PAT growth here is not manufactured by Other Income.

4. **Gap between core operating PBT growth (+1506%) and reported PAT growth (+1382%)?** Reported PAT grew SLOWER than core operating PBT because tax turned into a real drag: total tax rose from Rs2.14 Cr to Rs38.29 Cr (the prior-year quarter had zero current tax and a DTC benefit). Within that, a Rs8.59 Cr deferred-tax CREDIT (L210, A3-12) shields Q1 FY27 PAT — reported ETR 25.85% vs an ex-credit current-tax ETR of 31.65%. Strip the DTC and clean PAT is Rs101.26 Cr. Other Income delta (+0.13 Cr) is negligible; finance-cost delta (−10.68 Cr) and D&A delta (−1.10 Cr) are the recurring drags (see Step 4 bridge).

5. **Are D&A and finance costs scaling faster than revenue?** D&A +131% vs revenue +800% — D&A is NOT scaling with revenue (asset-light model; capex mostly expensed via project execution). Finance costs +418.8% (5.19x) — growing far faster than the asset base but slower than revenue; the 5.2x jump signals a **material borrowing step-up to fund the working-capital build** behind the revenue ramp (A3-03). Annualised finance cost ~Rs52.9 Cr vs FY26 Rs28.15 Cr. Absolute borrowings are ND (no balance sheet in a Q1 filing) — routed to a management question and the AR pending list.

6. **Is Other Income concentration changing?** No — Other Income is de-minimis and shrinking as a share of PBT (3.77% Q1FY26 -> 0.33% Q1FY27). There is no treasury-timing distortion to strip. This is a clean read.

Kernex sells railway safety systems (Kavach train-collision avoidance and signalling) plus software, almost entirely to Indian Railways and its production units such as CLW [this filing, Note 3/Note 7, L254/L272]. The demand backdrop is a government-driven Kavach rollout across the national network — a genuine multi-year structural tailwind, and the reason revenue can rise 9x in a year as orders enter execution [sector context: Notion thesis; this quarter's revenue: filing L185]. The flip side is that this is a lumpy, tender-based, single-payer sector: revenue arrives in bursts as percentage-of-completion milestones are hit, one large customer (CLW) dominates the current book, and payment cycles from government customers are long — the 268-day debtor position and the deeply negative multi-year cash conversion in the thesis are the sector's structural signature, not a company quirk [Notion: 268 debtor days, 10-yr CFO/PAT −6.17x; this quarter: debtor days not disclosed]. Regulation and audit scrutiny are elevated for this name specifically (prior auditor resignation, T2T penalty) [Notion]. Net: strong ordering tailwind, weak cash-collection structure — a sector where a great order pipeline and a poor cash cycle coexist.

### 3. Business-model intelligence
The company builds and installs safety hardware and software under fixed-scope railway contracts, recognising revenue as projects progress [this filing, Note 3, L254]. This quarter tells us three things about the model. First, it is asset-light: depreciation is tiny (Rs1.94 Cr) and barely moves even as revenue multiplies, so the cost base is materials (Rs268 Cr) and project execution (Rs40 Cr), not plant [this filing, L190/L193/L196]. Second, growth is debt-funded working capital, not fixed capex: finance cost rose 5.2x while depreciation did not, meaning the money is going into receivables and inventory ahead of collections [this filing, L195/L196]. Third, the subsidiaries and JVs are essentially empty vehicles — employee and depreciation figures are byte-identical to the standalone company, so the consolidation adds no real operations except the one unaudited US sub [this filing, L194/L472, L196/L474; A3-04]. The model's unit economics look excellent on the income statement (32% operating margin) but the model's Achilles heel is that profit is not yet shown to become cash, and this filing gives no cash figure to check [not disclosed]. A new warranty liability (Rs30.05 Cr) also signals that field-performance costs will recur as the installed base grows [this filing, Note 8, L274].

### 4. Competition intelligence
The named competitive benchmark is HBL Power, positioned in the thesis as the stronger Kavach peer [Notion]. Kernex's structural disadvantages carry over unchanged this quarter: the thesis records a 17-month Kavach 4.0 lag behind HBL, and 268 debtor days versus HBL's 71 on the same customer — i.e. Kernex funds far more working capital to win the same railway business, and this quarter's 5.2x finance-cost jump is consistent with exactly that disadvantage [Notion; this quarter's finance cost: filing L195]. Where Kernex wins is route coverage and order presence in the current Kavach cycle (it holds a large share of recent NCR route-km per Notion notes), which is what powers the revenue ramp [Notion, outside this filing]. The competitive risk to watch is that the order book has now slipped below the red line while a better-capitalised peer keeps pace: if Kernex must keep borrowing to fund collections that a peer does not, its return on capital erodes even as revenue grows [order book: this filing L272; peer comparison: Notion]. The load-bearing competitive question — is Kernex closing or widening the cash-conversion gap to HBL — is exactly what this cash-flow-free Q1 filing cannot answer, and is routed to the FY26 AR and concall.
