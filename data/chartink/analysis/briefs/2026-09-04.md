## The picture in one paragraph

The big index has gone nowhere for three months. Small companies have gone
up a lot in the same time. Under the surface, the market has split into two
crowds. One crowd of stocks sits near its highest price of the year. That
crowd is as large as it has ever been in this data. The other crowd sits
near its lowest price of the year, and that crowd grew this month. The
middle, the ordinary stock that just drifts along, is emptying out. This
is a market for picking stocks, not for buying the index.

## Where the market stands

Think of the market in three layers. The largest companies sit right on
their long-term trend line, not above it, not below it. The mid-sized
companies sit a little above theirs. The small and very small companies sit
well above theirs, about nine percent up. This past week, the large
companies fell more than one percent while the small ones stayed flat. Over
three months, the Nifty is flat and the small-cap index is up about seven
percent. The money has been going to the small end.

The market had a crash in March. About one stock in eight was above its
long-term average at the worst point. April brought two huge up days, the
kind that start a new leg. The rise ran into early May, then a sharp shock
hit on 12 May. Since July the market has sat in a range. The share of stocks
above their long-term average has stayed close to half for ten weeks. That
is a base. A base is not a trend. What matters next is which way the market
leaves it.

Half of all stocks are still below their long-term average. That sounds
weak. In this history it is actually a high reading, because the average
over the last eighteen months was much worse. The larger, better-known
companies are in better shape. About seven in ten of the Nifty 500 are
above their long-term average. The full market of more than two thousand
stocks is not there yet. The good end of the market has healed. The tail
has not.

This week ended with a two-day bounce. Wednesday was a washout, with only a
third of stocks above their short-term average. Thursday and Friday
recovered most of that. But the bounce came on ordinary volume. In this
history, the days that truly start a new leg see three hundred or more
stocks jump four percent or more in one session. Friday saw 129. That is a
good day, not a launch day.

One more thing sits under all of this. Fear is very low. The India VIX, the
market's fear gauge, is near its lowest level of the quarter. Gold, on the
other hand, rose about eight percent in a month while the Nifty barely
moved. A calm stock market and a rushing gold market do not usually agree.
One of them is telling the wrong story.

## What the sectors say

Healthcare is the strongest sector in the market by a clear margin. Seven in
ten healthcare stocks are above their medium-term trend. And yet healthcare
fell more than two percent this week. That is a leader taking a rest, not a
leader breaking. The line to watch is whether that medium-term trend holds.

Banks and defence are strong over the long run but tired right now. Over a
year, seven in ten stocks in each sector are in an uptrend. Over the last
month, only about half are. The defence index still looks fine because a few
big names carry it. Fewer stocks are doing the work.

Auto broke this week. The auto index fell almost four percent, the worst of
any sector. Only three in ten auto stocks hold their short-term trend, while
almost six in ten still hold their medium-term trend. That is the sharpest
short-term break in the whole sector table. But the parts makers, the
companies that sell to the car makers, are leading many of the stock scans.
The sector is split between the assemblers and the suppliers.

IT and realty have the same odd shape. The IT index had the best quarter of
any sector, up nearly seventeen percent. But only a third of IT stocks are
in a short-term uptrend. The rally lived in a handful of big names. Realty
is the same: the index is up nine percent over the quarter, but only a third
of realty stocks are trending up. When an index rises on a few names, it can
fall just as fast when those names tire.

Metals led on Friday and had a good quarter. The sector is not stretched;
about half its stocks are in an uptrend. A sector that leads on the day
while its breadth is only middling has room to run.

Some sectors are turning up from the bottom. Plastic products is the clearest
case: a year ago only a quarter of its stocks were trending up; now nearly
half are over the last month. Building materials and textiles show the same
early turn. These are the places where a company changing its business for
the better tends to show up first, before the crowd notices.

Power and utilities, media, and telecom services are the weakest sectors
across every time frame. FMCG had the worst quarter of any index, down
almost six percent.

## What is leading

About a hundred stocks closed at a one-year high on Friday. Two out of three
are small companies. Most came with heavy trading volume, which means real
buying, not a thin drift. Sixteen of them made the high after rising twenty
percent or more in a single week. That last group is the parabolic tail.

Several clear groups stand out. Jewellery stocks are running, and they are
running because gold is running. Dye and pigment chemical makers moved
together as one group, all in the same week. Textile companies are the
largest group on the long-term breakout scan. Fibre and telecom cable makers
are strong while ordinary power cable makers lag. Oil service companies are
rising even though the energy index is falling. Auto parts makers lead while
car makers fall. Among the large companies, the momentum sits in power
equipment and defence electronics.

There is also froth. Twenty-two stocks rose ten percent or more on Friday.
Seven stocks trading under five rupees closed at one-year highs. Penny stocks
at highs do not mark the top by themselves. They do show that money has
reached the tail end of the market.

## Who is buying

The ownership files show three patterns.

Foreign investors have been adding to one theme for three quarters: power
transmission, telecom fibre, and defence electronics. The prices of those
companies have confirmed the buying. This is where foreign money went.

Domestic institutions have been adding to large banks and large financials
whose prices have gone nowhere. Kotak, City Union Bank, ICICI, Delhivery.
The bank index is flat over the quarter. Institutions buying into a flat
price for three quarters is the pattern that often comes before a large-cap
leg. It has not started yet.

Retail investors have been crowding into the winners. Several stocks that
rose five or ten times in a year now have retail holding above forty
percent. The framework does not treat low institutional holding as a risk,
and that rule stands. This is a different point. When a stock has already
made its big move and retail has piled in after the move, that retail
holding is the supply that sells first when the mood turns.

## Thirty-six points

### For the trader

**1. The bounce is unproven.** Two good up days came on ordinary volume. A
real new leg announces itself with a thrust day, where hundreds of stocks
jump at once. That has not happened since June. Treat this as a bounce until
it does.

**2. The middle of the market is the swing vote.** Less than half of stocks
are above their short-term average. When that number crosses half, the
middle has turned and the bounce becomes a trend. If it falls back under
Wednesday's washout level, the range has broken down.

**3. Shorting has not worked.** On eighteen of the last twenty days, more
stocks jumped than fell hard. Very few stocks are falling four percent in a
day. That changes only on a day when the hard fallers outnumber the hard
risers by a wide margin. The last such day was in early July.

**4. Do not chase the parabolic sixteen.** Sixteen stocks made a one-year
high after a twenty-percent week. In this data, those are the stocks that
show up next week on the list of stocks that fell twenty percent in five
days. That list is already fuller than normal.

**5. Jewellery is a gold trade in disguise.** The jewellers are up because
gold is up eight percent in a month. Watch gold, not the jewellers. If gold
turns, the jewellers will follow it down.

**6. Metals has room.** It led on Friday, had a good quarter, and only half
its stocks are trending up. A sector that leads before it is crowded is the
kind that keeps leading.

**7. Auto is a spread, not a sector.** Car makers fell hard this week. Parts
makers made new highs on huge volume. The gap between the two is the setup.
The gap closes either when car makers recover or when parts makers catch
down.

**8. Large financials are the one large-cap group turning.** Insurance
names, PFC, Angel One, Groww, and Kotak all crossed from oversold on the same
day. Domestic funds have been buying these for three quarters. If a large-
cap leg comes, it starts here.

**9. IT is a five-stock rally.** The best index of the quarter has the worst
breadth of any large sector. The IT index sits right on its long-term line
after a bad week. A close below that line, with breadth this thin, is a
failed leader.

**10. Realty is two markets under one name.** The index rises on the big
developers. The scans fill up with tiny developers and land companies. The
sector's breadth is flat. Do not read the index as a sign for the small
names.

**11. The froth is real but it is not a timer.** Penny stocks at highs and
twenty-plus stocks up ten percent in a day tell you the tail is being
bought. That can last for weeks. It says be careful with size, not that the
top is in.

**12. Money is coming in, but it is going to a narrow list.** Turnover is
nearly half again what it was a year ago. Yet the volume spread across
stocks is only average. Concentrated buying is where the sharp reversals
come from.

**13. Both extremes are growing.** The count of overbought stocks and the
count of oversold stocks are both rising at the same time. A market pulling
apart like this rewards picking the right stock over guessing the index.

**14. Textiles is the widest early turn with real volume.** Nine textile
names made one-year highs and nine sit on the long-term breakout scan, the
most of any sector. Something is driving it. That something is not in these
files. Find it before sizing.

**15. Dyes and pigments moved as a group.** Five or six makers all made
one-year highs in the same week. A whole sub-industry moving at once is a
sector event, not a stock story. Retail holding in the leader of the group
is already high.

**16. Oil services are running against their own index.** The energy index
fell over the quarter, yet the drilling and offshore service companies are at
highs with foreign and promoter buying. Rotation inside a weak sector.

**17. Cables split: fibre up, power cable down.** Telecom fibre names are on
five scans with foreign buying. The plain power cable makers fell this
week. Same word, two different trades.

**18. In the smallest stocks, size your position for both directions.** The
count of stocks that rose twenty percent in five days and the count that
fell twenty percent in five days are both above normal at the same time. The
tape is violent both ways down there.

### For the investor

**19. The market is building a base, not a trend.** Five months after a
crash, ten weeks in a range. The share of stocks above their long-term
average has held near half since July. Which side it leaves that range
decides the next six months.

**20. The quality end has healed; the tail has not.** Seven in ten Nifty 500
stocks are in a long-term uptrend. Only half of the full market is. For a
holder with a three-to-five-year view, the mid and large names have the
more stable footing. The excess sits in the micro names.

**21. This is a barbell market.** A record share of stocks sits near yearly
highs. A growing share sits near yearly lows. The middle is emptying into
both ends. The index will give you nothing. Stock selection is the whole
return.

**22. The base is eroding under a strong top.** Over the last month the
number of stocks above their long-term average fell, while the number near
yearly highs held at a record. This is either healthy rotation or quiet
distribution. The resolving sign is whether the long-term count recovers
while the highs list holds.

**23. Long-run strength sits in defence, banks, healthcare, and
industrials.** Long-run weakness sits in power, media, telecom services,
building materials, realty, and IT. The weak list is where a company
climbing the quality ladder from a low rung would come from. The strong
list is where the market already pays up.

**24. Watch the sectors turning up from the bottom.** Plastic products is
the largest turn in the file, from a quarter of stocks in uptrend to nearly
half. Building materials and textiles follow. Transition names appear in
these sectors before the market names them.

**25. Yesterday's leaders are tiring.** Auto, defence, and banks all show a
big gap between long-run strength and recent weakness. The defence index
still looks fine because a few names carry it. Fewer stocks doing the work
is how leadership ends.

**26. Healthcare is the leader and it is resting.** Strongest breadth in the
market, worst week among the leaders. The medium-term trend line is where
the sector proves whether this is a pause. Ten healthcare names made yearly
highs; fifteen sit on the long-term breakout scan.

**27. Foreign money has one theme.** Power transmission, telecom fibre,
defence electronics. Three quarters of steady buying, confirmed by price in
every case. This is not a scattered bet. It is a conviction.

**28. Domestic money is quietly building in large financials.** Three
quarters of buying into flat prices. This is what a large-cap base looks
like from the inside. It has not paid off yet. It usually does, or the buyer
was wrong.

**29. Promoter buying at a high needs a reason.** A few promoters added
stock while their shares sat at yearly highs. That is rare. Before reading
it as confidence, check how they bought: open market, a preferential issue,
or a reclassification. Each means something different.

**30. Retail crowding is the supply risk in the winners.** Several stocks up
five or ten times now have retail holding above forty percent, and it rose
after the move. That holding is the float that turns over first when the
mood changes.

**31. Nine stocks have led on every time frame from one week to one year.**
Only three of those nine show institutions buying alongside. The other six
have run on retail and promoter holding alone. Persistence with
institutional confirmation is rarer and worth more.

**32. Large-cap momentum lives in power equipment.** Hitachi Energy, Apar,
PTC Industries, Voltamp, Solar Industries, MTAR. These carry the strongest
momentum among big companies. Consumer durables is the one index with
falling momentum.

**33. Gold beating stocks by eight points in a month is a warning.** It
happened while the fear gauge sat at a quarter low. A calm stock market and
a rushing gold market disagree. One is wrong.

**34. Fear is cheap right now.** The VIX is near its lowest level of the
quarter. Low fear does not tell you when a shock comes. The May shock came
five sessions after breadth peaked. Low fear tells you protection is cheap
when it may be worth having.

**35. Two pipeline names have moved before the work finished.** Cyient DLM
sits at a yearly high, more than doubled in three months. The price has
already made its move. The recognition-gap test is live for that name.
Ceigall and Balu Forge are up modestly with small institutional and retail
changes. INA, VOEPL, and Orchid Pharma show small moves. KECL appears on no
scan. This is memory to weigh, not evidence.

**36. More money, same index.** Turnover is up nearly half on a year ago,
and the index is flat for the quarter. The money went to the quarter of
stocks near their highs, and some of it went to penny stocks. Liquidity is
here. Its quality is mixed.

## What would change this read

The constructive view fails if the share of stocks above their short-term
average falls back under Wednesday's washout level, or if a day comes where
hard fallers swamp hard risers, or if the count of stocks above the long-
term average keeps sliding while the highs list stays full.

The cautious view fails if a real thrust day arrives, with hundreds of
stocks jumping at once, or if more than half the market reclaims its short-
term average on rising volume, or if the largest companies climb back above
their long-term line.
