## SECTOR CAP ROW QUESTION (answered first, per task)

**Procured equipment/materials share of contract value vs design/engineering/validation
services content, and which Section 1B sector-cap row applies.**

Consolidated revenue disaggregation, FY26 (AR Note 31, consolidated, p.179 txt):
| Line | Rs lakh | % of revenue |
|---|---|---|
| Sale of products (pharmaceutical machinery/equipment) | 37,227.82 | 90.6% |
| Sale of services (installation and commissioning) | 3,672.15 | 8.9% |
| Export incentives | 177.21 | 0.4% |
| **Total** | **41,077.18** | **100%** |

(AR p.179 txt, Note 31 "Revenue from Operations", consolidated)

The company's own revenue-recognition policy confirms the products line is not a
pass-through label: turnkey contracts are "a single performance obligation, where
control of goods and services is transferred progressively over the contract period
... Contract revenue and related costs ... are recognized based on actual shipments
as of the reporting date" (AR p.158 txt, Note D "Revenue recognition", consolidated
accounting policies). The Q1 FY27 investor presentation restates this in plain
language: "Revenue is recognised on the basis of shipments" (Inv. Pres. Q1FY27 p.7,
"01 THE QUARTER").

The RHP is explicit that the delivered value is procurement-heavy by design: "Our
Company has adopted an asset light approach wherein we procure key equipment and
materials for our projects from Related Entities ... to reduce our dependence on
third party suppliers" (RHP p.203-204, "Our Business" overview). The RHP's own
procurement-cost table shows Related-Entity-sourced equipment at 25.68% of total
procurement cost in FY25 (down from 36.82% in FY23), i.e. the remaining ~74% of
procurement cost is third-party-sourced equipment and materials, not design labour
(RHP p.208 txt, "Asset-light and integrated business model"). Design & Engineering is
named as one of five service pillars (disease profiling, design & engineering,
equipment supply, project management, audit/validation) but it is delivered as an
embedded input to a fixed-price turnkey contract, not billed or disclosed as a
separate design fee anywhere in the RHP or AR. The only place a standalone services
line is disclosed at all is the AR's "installation and commissioning" line, 8.9% of
FY26 revenue.

**Conclusion: the business model supports the "EPC / Civil construction" row (20x),
not "Consulting / Engineering services" (25x).** Over 90% of billed revenue is
recognised as equipment/product sale on a shipment basis; design and engineering
content is real but un-billed as a distinct fee line and does not change the revenue
recognition basis. The company's own investor presentation self-classifies the
business as "Turnkey EPC, design to delivery" (Inv. Pres. Q1FY27 p.14, "The business
at a glance") and separately states "Q4 is typically the strongest quarter in EPC
businesses ... Seasonal concentration in H2 is characteristic of the sector" (Inv.
Pres. Q1FY27 p.7). This is the company describing its own sector as EPC, not
consulting/engineering services. B00's provisional pick of "EPC / Civil construction"
is confirmed, not merely retained by default.

**Archetype fit (CLAUDE.md library): Order-book business (EPC/defence/capital
goods).** Order inflow, book-to-bill, execution pace, working capital and margin-on-
backlog are exactly the levers this section documents below (order book Rs 90,441.87
lakh / Rs 904.42 Cr as of 31-Jul-2025, RHP p.204 txt; book-to-bill ratio 1.48x FY25,
RHP p.208 txt KPI table). It does not fit "Outsourcing partner" (no single dominant
client wallet-share relationship survives year to year, see Section 2A) and does not
fit "Build-to-spec component maker" (Fabtech does not itself manufacture the
equipment it installs).

## SECTION 1: THE BUSINESS MODEL IN PLAIN ENGLISH

### 1A. One-line description
Fabtech designs, procures the equipment for, and builds pharmaceutical and biotech
manufacturing facilities (clean rooms, purified water, process systems) as fixed-
price turnkey projects, then bills the customer in stages as it ships and installs
the work (AR p.158 txt; RHP p.203-208 txt).

### 1B. Money flow chain, per revenue stream

**Stream 1: Turnkey project (equipment + installation), ~90-99% of revenue by
project count** (RHP: turnkey projects were 74.54% of FY25 revenue, 86.50% of FY24,
90.01% of FY23, RHP p.208 txt; on an all-in product+service basis for FY26 the
combined product+installation lines are ~99.5% of consolidated revenue, AR p.179
txt).
[Customer signs a fixed-price contract for a clean facility] → [Fabtech designs the
facility, sources the required equipment mainly from Related Entities/third-party
manufacturers (RHP p.207-208 txt) and manages site installation] → [it delivers a
GMP-compliant clean room / process line, validated and commissioned] → [the
pharma/biotech/healthcare customer pays] → [in milestone instalments tied to
shipment and project stage, with revenue booked "based on actual shipments as of the
reporting date" (AR p.158 txt)].

**Stream 2: Standalone equipment supply and logistics** (offered "on a standalone
basis" outside a full turnkey scope, RHP p.203 txt; not separately quantified in the
AR revenue note, folded into "Sale of products").
[Customer needs specific bio-air/water/process equipment without a full turnkey
scope] → [Fabtech procures and supplies the equipment plus logistics management] →
[delivered equipment, installed or ex-works] → [customer pays] → [on delivery/
shipment terms, same Ind AS 115 "sale of products" recognition, AR p.158 txt].

**Stream 3: Installation and commissioning services / consultancy**, 8.9% of FY26
consolidated revenue, Rs 3,672.15 lakh vs Rs 770.36 lakh FY25 (AR p.179 txt, Note 31).
[Customer needs installation, commissioning or management-consultancy input, often
alongside an equipment order from elsewhere] → [Fabtech's engineering team performs
the service] → [service completed and accepted] → [customer pays] → [recognised "when
the services are performed" (AR p.158 txt, Note D.ii)].

**Stream 4: Export incentives**, 0.4% of FY26 revenue, Rs 177.21 lakh (AR p.179 txt).
[Government export-promotion scheme] → [Fabtech claims eligible credits] → [credit
posted to the export passbook] → [company] → [recognised "on the basis of credits
afforded in the passbook or amount received" (AR p.158 txt, Note D.iii)]. Not a
customer-paid stream; a policy pass-through.

### 1E. The chai-stall-uncle version
Think of Fabtech as a contractor who builds a clean, sealed kitchen for a big
restaurant chain, not just any kitchen: one that passes strict health inspections
every time. It does not make the stoves and filters itself; it buys them from group
companies and other suppliers, then designs, fits and tests the whole kitchen to
spec (RHP p.203-208 txt). The restaurant chain pays in instalments as each shipment
of equipment arrives and each stage is done, not all at once (AR p.158 txt). Because
it is fitting kitchens in nine countries with contracts of 12 to 36 months (Inv.
Pres. Q1FY27 p.14), one quarter can look quiet on the P&L even while the order book
behind it is healthy (Inv. Pres. Q1FY27 p.7-8). And because the chain does not pay
the last instalment until well after the work is done, cash comes in slower than
profit is booked (AR p.44-45 txt).

### 4C. Quarterly monitoring checklist (10-15 items)

1. Order book (Rs Cr), quarter-on-quarter — good: growing or flat above Rs 900 Cr; trouble: declining
2. Book-to-bill ratio — good: >1.3x; trouble: <1.0x
3. CFO/PAT ratio (trailing 4 quarters) — good: >0.5x; trouble: staying near zero or negative
4. Trade receivables >365-day share of gross receivables — good: falling from 43.6%; trouble: rising further
5. Debtor days — good: falling from the 182-day level (B00); trouble: rising
6. Other income as % of PBT — good: <15%; trouble: >30%
7. FX hedge coverage vs monetary FX exposure — good: hedge ratio rising from the FY26 near-nil level (B02); trouble: unchanged thin hedge on a growing FX book
8. Contribution/gross margin — good: stable 45-50%+; trouble: compression below 40%
9. Top-5 customer concentration and identity — good: diversifying without losing repeat business; trouble: concentration rising again or another customer falling off entirely
10. Geographic revenue mix shift — good: no single non-India market exceeding ~30% for long; trouble: sudden dependence on one geopolitically exposed market
11. Employee cost as % of revenue — good: flat or falling with growth (operating leverage); trouble: rising faster than revenue
12. Related-Entity share of procurement cost — good: declining trend continuing (36.82%→25.68%, FY23→FY25); trouble: reversing upward with no disclosed reason
13. IPO proceeds deployment (inorganic-growth bucket) — good: rupees actually deployed against the Rs 3,000 lakh bucket; trouble: continued zero utilisation (B02 rank 9)
14. Related-party-labelled borrowing/RPT counterparty disclosure — good: the Rs 2,350.83 lakh loan is finally named; trouble: remains untraceable another quarter (B02/B03)

### 4D. Highest-value management questions

1. What share of contract value is procured equipment (own and third-party) versus in-house design and engineering hours, and how has that mix shifted over the last three years? — *Reassures*: procurement share stable/declining as design value-add rises. *Worries*: procurement share rising, meaning Fabtech is becoming more of an equipment reseller and less of an engineering house.
2. Of the Rs 900+ Cr open order book, how much is signed fixed-price contract versus letter-of-intent or preferred-vendor status? — *Reassures*: mostly signed. *Worries*: a large LOI/soft component that can be re-tendered.
3. Why did trade receivables aged beyond 365 days rise to Rs 5,887.55 lakh, and what concrete collection actions are underway? — *Reassures*: named customers, agreed payment plans, provisioning adequate. *Worries*: vague "under discussion" language with no named counterparty or timeline.
