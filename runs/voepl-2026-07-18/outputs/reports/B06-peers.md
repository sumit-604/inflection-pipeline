## PART 1: CLAIM-BY-CLAIM VERIFICATION

### Claim 1 — OEM brand in-house capacity (esp. Voltas) vs Tier-2/3 outsourcing growth
**Claim**: Are OEM appliance brands expanding in-house AC capacity enough to structurally cut
third-party ODM/EMS order flow, or is Tier-2/3 outsourcing growing fast enough to offset it?
[AMBER, ELIN, EPACK, PGEL]

**Verdict: VERIFIED (direction), with a documented near-term countervailing dynamic**

**Peer evidence**:
- AMBER (Feb-2026 call, Jasbir Singh, CEO): on Mitsubishi's ₹2,100cr backward-integration
  compressor/RAC capex — "there is no risk... since 2021 when PLI got announced all 6 major
  brands announced their own factories... markets did assume Amber will not do well... but we
  could see Amber moved into a different category... kept on growing more than the industry."
  Describes three prior in-house/outsource policy swings in 25 years, always offset by moving
  up the value chain (components → full box → non-RAC components).
- AMBER (Feb-2026): "whenever a brand puts up a factory, they do some kind of backward
  integration plus assembly... but other components are required to be supplied just in time by
  suppliers like us" (Toyota/tire analogy).
- EPACK (Aug-2025, Ajay Singhania, MD/CEO): "outsourcing... has been gradually increasing from
  an earlier 20% kind of overall market being outsourced to 25%, 30%... currently anywhere
  between 30%, 35%."
- EPACK (Nov-2025): "of the top 10 brands sold in India, 9 of them have their own manufacturing
  facility... and despite this, their dependence on OEM or outsourcing today currently stands at
  more than 35%... no brand wants to do 100% manufacturing in-house."
- PGEL (Nov-2025, Pramod Gupta, CFO): "it doesn't make a lot of economic sense to do their own
  plant... capital efficiencies are low for most of them... given the fact that if you want to
  put up backward integrated plants, they are capital intensive. The value accretion... is
  actually not visible even in most of the brands which have put up their own capacities."
- PGEL (Feb-2026): concedes a **countervailing near-term fact** — "in the last 3, 4 years
  because of the PLI... there has been a good capacity addition, which has happened in the
  brands and a lot of in-sourcing has also shifted... since 2021, some of that gap... went
  actually to the in-sourcing capacity of the brand" — but argues most brand in-house plants
  remain uneconomical (2-4% costlier than outsourcing) and that outsourcing should resume growing
  once PLI expires (FY27 for RAC-adjacent schemes).

**Peers silent**: ELIN does not address RAC-specific brand insourcing (its exposure is
lighting/fan/motor/appliance, not RAC); its closest analogue is the Signify Lighting JV shift,
which is a demand-loss event, not an insourcing-vs-outsourcing debate.

**Net read**: Three of four suggested peers (AMBER, EPACK, PGEL) independently and
un-coordinatedly argue that outsourcing is structurally resilient and, on their own data, has
been *increasing* (20%→35% per EPACK) even through a period of heavy brand PLI-driven capex.
PGEL is the most useful because it names the counter-argument (PLI-driven in-sourcing did take
share 2021-2025) before concluding it's economically unsustainable. This corroborates VOEPL's
framing that outsourcing offsets in-house expansion, with the caveat that PGEL's own numbers
show 2021-2025 was actually a period of *net in-sourcing gain*, not straight-line outsourcing
growth — the "offset" thesis is more a forward view than a backward-looking fact from the peer
set.

### Claim 2 — RAC channel-inventory trends and BEE table-change disruption timeline
**Claim**: What RAC channel-inventory/secondary-sales trends are peers reporting through Q4
FY26/Q1 FY27, and did the BEE table-change disruption resolve on VOEPL's stated timeline (dried
up Oct/Nov-2025, rebuilding Dec-2025)? [AMBER, EPACK, PGEL]

**Verdict: PARTIALLY VERIFIED — December-2025 production surge is well corroborated; the
"dried up Oct/Nov" framing is not clearly corroborated and is arguably contradicted**

**Peer evidence** (chronological):
- EPACK (Aug-2025, pre-BEE): "brands are aligning production to begin with somewhere around end
  of September or October" for the next season — i.e., normal-course ramp, not BEE-specific.
- EPACK (Nov-2025, Q2 FY26 call): "the beginning of quarter 3 seems to be kind of muted"
  (October); channel inventory at Q1-end ~5 million (3.5M channel + 1.5-2M brand), reduced ~50%
  to ~2.5-3M by Q2-end (Sept-2025) after a brief GST-cut demand pulse; "production start from
  mid of December, that's when the actual production starts for the calendar year '26" — the
  new-BEE ramp date matches VOEPL's "rebuilding Dec-2025."
- EPACK (Jan-2026, Q3 FY26 call): but by this point, overall trade+brand inventory of the
  **old-rated** stock is estimated at **4-4.5 million units** — i.e., *higher*, not "dried up,"
  because manufacturers raced to build/ship old-rated stock before the 31-Dec cutoff (retailers
  can sell it through June). "There has been liquidation of primary inventory by most brands...
  channel inventory is definitely a little high."
- AMBER (Feb-2026, Q3 FY26 call): "the quarter witnessed a channel filling ahead of BEE rating
  upgrade" — Q3 (Oct-Dec) industry volume actually turned **slightly positive** (+3-5%) versus
  Q1 (-5 to -10%) and Q2 (-35%), the opposite of "dried up."
- PGEL (May-2026, Q4/FY26 call, Vishal Gupta, MD-Finance): "anticipation of the BEE rating
  transition led to **preponement** of production and sales in the month of December 2025,
  resulting in **less offtake in the month of Jan and Feb**" — confirms a December surge but
  frames Jan/Feb (not Oct/Nov) as the actual demand air-pocket.
- PGEL (May-2026): channel inventory **peaked at ~5 million units** during FY26, normalizing
  only by April/May 2026.

**Peers silent**: none of the three flag an Oct/Nov "dry-up" as VOEPL frames it; if anything
Oct/Nov is described as merely muted-to-flattish while Dec sees a pre-cutoff production/channel
peak (not a "rebuild" from a trough, but a scramble to ship old-rated units).

**Net read**: The Dec-2025 production/channel-filling surge ahead of the BEE cutoff is
well-anchored across all three peers and matches VOEPL's "rebuilding Dec-2025" leg. But the
"dried up Oct/Nov" leg does not match peer evidence — Oct/Nov is described as muted-to-slightly-
positive, and the real post-BEE air-pocket that peers flag lands in **Jan-Feb 2026**, not before
December. VOEPL's stated timeline appears one leg right (Dec ramp) and one leg mismatched
(the "dry" period is Jan/Feb per PGEL, not Oct/Nov).

### Claim 3 — Copper/RM cost pass-through magnitude (~10-15% industry-wide) and margin resilience
**Claim**: What copper/RM cost pass-through magnitude (VOEPL cites ~10-15% industry-wide) and
margin resilience are peers reporting for FY26? [AMBER, ELIN, EPACK, PGEL]

**Verdict: VERIFIED on magnitude; CONTRADICTED on "margin resilience"**

**Peer evidence — magnitude**:
- PGEL (May-2026, Vishal Gupta): "Since December '25 till April '26, the industry implemented
  **cumulative price hike of around 10% to 15%**" — an almost exact match to VOEPL's own figure,
  independently generated.
- EPACK (Jan-2026): BEE re-rating + commodity impact together "currently seems around **8% to
  10%**"; by May-2026: "the overall price increase in the product... due to both the revised
  commodities as well as the revised rating levels is **15% to 20%**, varying between model to
  model" through end-March, with further increases in April-May.
- AMBER (May-2026): CCL up 60% YoY, gold up ~60% YoY (PCB inputs); finished-goods price increase
  of "**14%**" in Consumer Durables versus prior year (Sachin Gupta, WTD).
- ELIN (Q4 FY26 call, Akash Sethia): "pricing has had to be increased between **10% and 18%**
  just to sustain earlier margins," driven by a Middle East war-linked spike in polymer/crude,
  copper, aluminum and steel plus ~20%-ish rupee weakness contribution.

**Peer evidence — margin resilience (the weaker half of the claim)**:
- AMBER: guides 50-100bps consolidated margin pressure for FY27, calls it "temporary."
- EPACK: "if the commodity impacts are passed as it is... the margins will — might look
  slightly subdued" (Achal Lohade Q&A, Jan-2026); Q4 FY26 EBITDA margin fell to 4.37% from
  11.21% YoY; full-year EBITDA margin 6.01% vs 7.26% prior year — a real compression, not a
  resilient pass-through.
- PGEL: Q4 FY26 gross-margin impact ~250bps that could **not** be fully passed through
  ("with weak demand and excess inventory in the channel, the ability to fully pass through
  these increases remain constrained, suppressing gross margins across the entire value chain");
  FY26 consolidated EBITDA fell to ₹441.76cr from ₹519.16cr YoY.
- ELIN: Q4 FY26 EBITDA collapsed to ₹6cr from ₹20.2cr YoY (390bps gross-margin hit), explicitly
  attributed to the commodity spike outrunning the quarterly-lag pass-through mechanism.

**Net read**: The 10-15% RM/price pass-through magnitude VOEPL cites is closely and
independently corroborated (PGEL's own number matches almost verbatim). But "margin resilience"
is not supported — every peer that reports FY26/Q4-FY26 numbers shows real, sometimes severe,
margin compression because the pass-through carries a structural one-to-two-quarter lag that
does not fully protect against a fast-moving commodity/currency shock. This is a meaningful
nuance for synthesis: the magnitude claim holds, the resilience claim does not.

### Claim 4 — QCO reciprocating-compressor 40% import allowance and two new local compressor competitors
**Claim**: Do peer filings confirm the QCO reciprocating-compressor 40% import allowance (to
Mar-2027) and the two new local compressor competitors VOEPL describes (end-Q3/Q4 FY27,
mid-FY28)? [AMBER, ELIN, EPACK, PGEL]

**Verdict: CONTRADICTED on the import-allowance figure; PARTIALLY VERIFIED on the new-entrant
timeline**

**Peer evidence — import allowance**:
- AMBER (May-2026, Jasbir Singh): "government has allowed to **import 30%** of last year
  imported volumes to cater to the shortage. Coming on the 2-tonne front, they have extended it
  for 1 year."
- EPACK (May-2026, Ajay Singhania): "there was a circular in the first week of May, 8th May...
  wherein the **import has been allowed up to 30%**, approximately 30-odd percent of import is
  allowed. This is the quantity which was imported in FY24."

Both AMBER and EPACK, independently, cite the **same 30% figure** (not 40%) and even reference
what appears to be the same early-May-2026 government circular. This is a clean, double-sourced
contradiction of VOEPL's 40% claim.

**Peer evidence — compressor manufacturing landscape / new entrants**:
- AMBER (May-2026): describes **five already-operating/expanding** manufacturers serving
  room-AC compressors — LG, Daikin, Mitsubishi (new PLI-driven plants), GMCC (capacity increase),
  and Highly (considering expansion) — framed as an established landscape, not "two new"
  entrants on a forward calendar.
- EPACK (May-2026): "domestically, we have **four** air-con compressor manufacturers, Highly,
  and then GMCC... Daikin, and LG" — import dependency fell from "100% to today, 45%... in last
  3 years." Also established-player framing.
- PGEL (May-2026): PGEL itself is standing up a **new, self-funded rotary-compressor facility**
  (the earlier China-JV compressor plan was abandoned — "we are going on our own... we don't
  need any approval for this. There is no JV in this"). Timeline: machinery ordered, installation
  begins **August-2026**, commercial operations targeted **Q4 FY27** — capacity 2 million units
  phase 1, expandable to 4 million phase 2, ~60% localized value-add. This is a concrete, dated,
  new local entrant whose commercial-production timeline (Q4 FY27) closely matches the first of
  VOEPL's two dates ("end-Q3/Q4 FY27").

**Net read**: The 40% import-allowance figure is contradicted by two independent, closely-worded
peer citations of a 30% allowance tied to an early-May-2026 circular — a priority item for
synthesis given both anchors are dated and specific. The "two new local compressor competitors"
claim gets partial support: PGEL is *itself* a new entrant matching VOEPL's Q4-FY27 date almost
exactly, but no peer names a second entrant on a mid-FY28 timeline, so that half remains
UNVERIFIABLE. Overall verdict recorded as CONTRADICTED given the clean, load-bearing 40%-vs-30%
mismatch.

### Claim 6 — Peer EMS/appliance makers in similarly capex-heavy phase, CapEx overshoots vs guidance, funding
**Claim**: Are peer EMS/appliance makers in a similarly capex-heavy phase showing CapEx
overshoots vs guidance, and how are they funding it? [ELIN, EPACK]
*(Expanded to all four peers below since AMBER and PGEL also disclose detailed capex guidance
trails.)*

**Verdict: PARTIALLY VERIFIED — the capex-heavy phase is universal; the "overshoot vs guidance"
pattern is not — it is peer-specific, not sector-wide**

**Peer evidence**:
- **AMBER — clear overshoot**: FY26 capex guided ₹700-850cr (Nov-2025 call) → Q4 FY26 call
  (May-2026) discloses actual FY26 capex of **~₹1,070cr** ("capitalized capex is only ₹550cr,
  balance is under CWIP... overall capex is around ₹1,070cr" — Sudhir Goyal, CFO), a ~25-53%
  overshoot versus the original range, described as "front-ending." Funded via ₹1,000cr QIP
  equity raise plus ₹1,750cr ILJIN CCPS fundraise; FY27 capex guided sharply higher again
  (₹1,800-2,000cr).
- **ELIN — underspend, not overshoot**: FY26 capex guided ₹100-120cr (Aug-2025) → actual capex
  came in well below guidance across the year (H1 ₹14.5cr, 9M ₹24.5cr, full FY26 "₹32.5cr
  across all our existing facilities excluding Bhiwadi" per the Q4 FY26 call) because the
  Bhiwadi plant's commissioning slipped from Mar/Apr-2026 to Jul/Aug-2026. Funded entirely from
  internal operating cash flow; balance sheet stayed net-cash throughout.
- **EPACK — roughly on plan**: capex guided ₹450-500cr over 12-18 months (Aug-2025) → 9M actual
  ₹218-220cr (Jan-2026 call), revised total guide of ₹470cr over 18-24 months with ₹300cr
  capitalized by end-March-2026 (May-2026 call) — tracking close to plan, not overshooting.
  Funded via a mix of term debt (gross debt ~₹700cr by Q4 FY26) and prior IPO proceeds.
- **PGEL — guidance cut, then met the lower number**: FY26 capex *originally* guided
  ₹800-900cr (Aug-2025) but immediately revised down to ₹700-750cr in the same call due to
  caution after the early-monsoon demand shock and a Chinese-partner compressor-JV delay;
  actual FY26 capex landed at ~₹785cr (May-2026 call) — essentially matching the *revised*
  (lower) guide, not the original one. Funded from cash reserves (~₹900cr+ cash/equivalents)
  with no incremental debt required.

**Net read**: All four peers confirm they are in a structurally capex-heavy phase (new plants
in Bhiwadi/Sri City/Supa/Greater Noida/YEIDA, backward integration into compressors/PCB/
components). But only AMBER shows a genuine overshoot vs its own guidance; ELIN clearly
underspent (construction delay), EPACK tracked its revised plan, and PGEL cut guidance
proactively and then hit the reduced number. Funding mix also varies by balance-sheet strength:
AMBER and EPACK leaned on debt/equity raises, ELIN and PGEL self-funded from cash/operations.
This is a mixed, peer-specific picture rather than a uniform sector-wide overshoot phenomenon.

## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

### 2A. Demand environment
All three RAC-direct peers (AMBER, EPACK, PGEL) independently describe FY26 as a genuinely bad
year for room ACs — early/prolonged monsoon collapsing the Q1 season, a GST-cut announcement
(15-Aug-2025) that froze purchases for six weeks awaiting implementation, and a BEE table change
that further disrupted Q3-Q4 ordering patterns. PGEL adds a fourth, VOEPL-external shock: a
March-2026 commercial-LPG shortage tied to a Gulf conflict that forced ~2-week plant shutdowns
and triggered reverse labor migration, costing PGEL an estimated ₹420cr of Q4 revenue. All three
peers are broadly optimistic for FY27 (12-20% volume growth guided) off the weak FY26 base.

### 2B. Pricing and input costs
Consensus across all four peers: copper, aluminum, CCL and gold prices rose sharply through
FY26 (13-60%+ YoY depending on input and period), compounded by ~20% YoY rupee depreciation
(PGEL's figure) tied to the same Gulf-conflict period. All peers describe a standard
one-to-two-quarter lagged pass-through mechanism to brand customers, and all report that this
lag structurally compresses margins in the quarter the shock hits, even where full pass-through
is eventually achieved. This nuances VOEPL's "margin resilience" framing (see Claim 3).

### 2C. Capex cycle
Not a uniform capacity race. AMBER is the clear outlier — aggressively front-loading capex into
PCB/electronics diversification (Ascent, Shogini, Korea Circuits JV, Power-One, Unitronics) and
even entering a new, unrelated mobile-phone assembly business with Oppo (Jun-2026 call) —
overshooting its own capex guidance materially. PGEL and ELIN, by contrast, pulled back or
delayed capex in direct response to the FY26 demand shock, staying capital-disciplined and
self-funded. EPACK tracked its plan. This is a **lone-expander (AMBER) diversifying away from
RAC**, not an industry-wide RAC capacity race — worth flagging distinctly from VOEPL's own
capex narrative, which should be benchmarked against the cautious majority (ELIN/EPACK/PGEL)
rather than against AMBER's outlier electronics-driven build-out.

### 2D. Competitive mentions
No peer transcript, across all 16 calls, mentions Virtuoso Optoelectronics (VOEPL) by name or by
unmistakable description. This silence is itself informative: VOEPL does not appear to register
as a competitive reference point for these four larger, listed EMS/ODM peers on their own
investor calls, consistent with VOEPL operating at meaningfully smaller scale within the same
value chain.

### 2E. Risks peers raise that the main company's claim set (per the injected questions) does not
surface
- Rupee depreciation (~20% YoY per PGEL) as a distinct, currency-specific margin risk layered on
  top of commodity inflation — flagged repeatedly by AMBER, ELIN and PGEL, each with unhedged-
  liability mark-to-market losses (PGEL: ₹38.77cr FY26 forex loss).
- Geopolitical/Gulf-conflict-linked commodity and logistics disruption (PGEL: LPG shortage, truck
  shortage, ~₹420cr Q4 FY26 revenue loss; ELIN: crude→polymer price spike, war-linked).
- Minimum-wage revisions in UP and Haryana (Amber: 22-35% increases) and the new Labour Code
  (AMBER, PGEL, ELIN) adding cost pressure independent of RM/currency.
- PLI-scheme terminal-year dynamics: PGEL flags that FY26/FY27 is the "last year of PLI target
  achievement for all brands," creating "desperation" and abnormal discounting/production
  behavior across the industry as competitors chase targets — a distortion risk to demand/pricing
  signals that is not RM- or BEE-related.
- Compressor supply-chain execution risk cuts both ways: EPACK abandoned nothing but flagged a
  customs show-cause notice on ASEAN copper-tube imports affecting 2,000+ importers industry-wide
  (unresolved as of Nov-2025); PGEL's own compressor JV with a Chinese partner **collapsed**
  after ~10 months of Chinese-government approval delays, forcing a pivot to a slower, self-
  funded build — a cautionary data point on compressor localization execution risk that VOEPL's
  claim set (framed around QCO/import policy) does not capture.
- EPACK also disclosed (May-2026) an auditor-qualified, disputed ₹19.61cr trade receivable and a
  reversal of ₹32.42cr of previously-recognized PLI income pending government re-verification —
  both counterparty/scheme-compliance risks generic to the ODM model that are worth naming in
  the missing-risks register even though they are EPACK-specific, not VOEPL-specific.
- ELIN flags that Chinese suppliers continue to under-price unpredictably even after BIS/QCO
  tightening and rupee depreciation ("Chinese pricing is somewhat of a mystery... a lot of Indian
  industry at large" — Akash Sethia, Q4 FY26 call), suggesting import-substitution/localization
  tailwinds may not translate into pricing power as cleanly as a QCO-driven thesis implies.
