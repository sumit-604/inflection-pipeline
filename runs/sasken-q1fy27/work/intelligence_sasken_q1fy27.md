# SASKEN Q1 FY27 — Intelligence Brief (summary, sector, competitors, forward view)
Operator-facing layer on top of the A1-A5 pipeline review. 01-Aug-2026.

## 1. SUMMARY OF THE COMPLETE ANALYSIS

Revenue 339 crore, up 24% on the year. Reported profit 23.5 crore, up 135%. Operating margin 9.5%. Auditor signed clean. On the surface, a strong quarter. Under the surface, three problems and one missing proof.

- The profit is the parent company's. Standalone profit 28.9 crore was higher than the whole group's 23.5 crore, because the subsidiaries (Borqs hardware and Sasken Silicon) lost 5.3 crore between them this quarter, after making 9.3 crore last quarter. The services business is carrying the group.
- The cash proof is absent. First quarter Indian filings need not include a cash flow statement, and this one does not. You had named the Q1 cash number as the single most important signal. It cannot be read. What is visible is worrying: inventory up 30 crore and receivables up 58 crore in the product segment whose sales actually fell.
- The margin missed. 9.5% against your 10% pass mark. The product segment margin halved, from 12.8% to 5.9%.
- Part of the product revenue is not real demand. Management concedes memory-cost pass-through inflated the reported product topline.

Nothing broke a red line. Margin is not below 8%. Concentration is not above 60%. No goodwill written off. Governance unchanged at three old flags. The year on year trend is genuinely strong and roughly 115% of the profit growth is recurring/core, not treasury.

Pipeline verdict: PROCEED WITH CAVEATS. The master decision gate is NOT CLEARED (margin fail, cash indeterminate, working-capital strategy undisclosed, concentration ambiguous). The real answer moves one quarter out, to the September half-year filing that must carry a cash flow statement. Decision Status stays AVOID at 2,121 (fair value near 784, buy zone below 430).

## 2. SIMPLE NARRATIVE

Sasken is an engineering-services company that designs software and chips for other people's products: phone chipsets, cars, 5G networks, hyperscaler devices. For years it was a small, cash-rich, slow-growth services house. In April 2025 it bought Borqs, a hardware/ODM business, to move up the stack from writing software to shipping whole devices. That acquisition is the whole story now. It roughly doubled the growth rate, but it also halved the margins and, this quarter, it lost money and swallowed cash into inventory and receivables.

So the quarter is two companies in one envelope. The old services business is firing: growing, high-margin, disciplined. The new hardware business is stumbling: shrinking sales, collapsing margin, ballooning working capital. The consolidated headline looks good only because the services engine is strong enough to hide the hardware drag. The one number that would tell you whether the hardware bet is turning cash-positive or bleeding, the cash flow statement, is simply not in the document. Until September, you are being asked to trust the profit without the cash proof. That is exactly the situation your framework tells you not to wave through.

## 3. SECTOR INTELLIGENCE (ER&D services, India)

The backdrop is one of the best secular stories in Indian IT. India's engineering R&D outsourcing was roughly 44-45 billion dollars in 2023 and is projected at 130-170 billion by FY30, about 22% of the global ER&D sourcing market. Digital-engineering spend inside that is compounding around 20% a year. Automotive is the largest vertical (about 28% of the Indian ER&D market), and semiconductor/electronics is the fastest, around 13% CAGR. The demand drivers are exactly Sasken's shopfront: chip design (India semiconductor self-reliance push), software-defined vehicles and EV programs, 5G and Open RAN telecom, and hyperscaler custom silicon.

Two sector realities matter for reading this quarter. First, the vertical mix is a tailwind for Sasken: semiconductor design, automotive connectivity, 5G infrastructure and hyperscaler engineering are all named in its deal list and all are structurally growing. Second, the whole pack saw margin pressure this quarter, not just Sasken. Tata Elxsi's margin fell to 19% from 22%, KPIT's fell to 16% from 18%. So some of Sasken's softness is industry weather, not only company-specific. The difference is scale and level: peers are compressing from 16-19%; Sasken is doing 9.5%.

The comparison is unforgiving on two axes and flattering on one.

- Scale: Sasken at roughly 40 million dollars a quarter and 2,658 people is 3x to 9x smaller than every listed peer. In ER&D, scale buys the large multi-year platform deals and absorbs the fixed cost of tools and talent. Sasken is sub-scale.
- Margin: 9.5% is about half the pack. The gap is structural, not cyclical. Peers are pure high-value services; Sasken's blended number is diluted by roughly 31% Borqs hardware/ODM mix, which is a lower-margin business by nature, plus memory-cost pass-through that adds revenue at near-zero margin.
- Growth: Sasken's +24% is the fastest in the group, but it is boosted by consolidating a full quarter of Borqs against a base that had little or none, so a large part is inorganic rather than same-store demand. Strip Borqs and the organic services growth is closer to the pack's low-to-mid teens.

Net: Sasken is the small, low-margin, fast-but-inorganic name in a strong sector led by larger, higher-margin franchises. Its verticals are right; its position within them is marginal.

## 5. FORWARD VIEW (house view)

The bet is coherent and the verticals are correct, but the evidence needed to underwrite it is one quarter away, and the price already assumes the bet has worked.

**What would make this investable.** The services engine is genuinely compounding at a 30% segment margin, and the deal list (a multi-year hyperscaler engineering expansion, a Massive MIMO Open RAN chip-ready design, automotive 4G/5G telematics, 5G timing sync in North America) is real, high-quality, and in the fastest-growing corners of the sector. If Borqs turns profit-positive, the product working-capital build reverses into cash, and Silicon scales past 50 crore, then the full-stack chip-to-cognition story starts to look like a smaller Tata Elxsi in the making, and the margin can climb off 9.5% toward the pack.

**What holds it back.** Sasken is sub-scale and structurally lower-margin, the Borqs acquisition is currently dilutive, loss-making, and carries roughly 173 crore of goodwill that faces a year-end impairment test, and the cash transition, which has been the binding constraint for two years running (FY26 group operating cash flow was negative 22.8 crore), is still unproven. The memory-cost pass-through flatters revenue without adding profit. And at 2,121, the stock trades around 53x trailing earnings, roughly 5x to 6x higher than fair value near 784, so there is no margin of safety even on the bull path.

**The three things that decide it, in order.**
1. September half-year cash flow (the re-set gate). Positive operating cash with the product working-capital build reversing turns the story. Negative cash, or working capital still expanding, confirms the cash trap and pressures the whole thesis.
2. Product / Borqs margin bridge. 5.9% must climb back toward the low teens. A second quarter near or below current levels moves the 8%-for-two-quarters thesis-broken trigger into view.
3. Borqs goodwill at FY27 year-end. A loss-making unit plus 173 crore of goodwill is an impairment waiting to be tested. A writedown is a defined thesis-break.

**One live development to verify.** External sources report Sasken scheduled an analyst earnings call for early August. If true and repeated, that partly relaxes the structural no-concall assumption that has capped the management grade at B, because it restores a Q&A channel. Worth confirming before next quarter, because it is a genuine change to a load-bearing input.

**Forward stance:** AVOID held, not for lack of a story but for lack of proof and margin of safety. This is a watch-and-verify name whose September cash print is the single event that reprices the thesis in either direction. Bull case needs price near 430 and a positive H1 cash turn; bear case is a negative H1 cash print or a Borqs writedown at a valuation that still prices success.
