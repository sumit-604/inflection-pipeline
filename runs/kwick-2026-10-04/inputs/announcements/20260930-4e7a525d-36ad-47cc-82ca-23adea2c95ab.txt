Pioneering India's Forensic Science & Evidence Management Ecosystem
Founded in 2005, Kwick Forensic Solutions Limited is a Chennai, 
Tamil Nadu-based provider of integrated forensic science, digital 
investigation, and evidence management solutions. The 
Company transitioned from a software development firm to a 
specialized forensic solutions provider, with its journey rooted in the 
development of India's Interactive Crime Scene Simulator, 
supported by the Ministry of Science & Technology, the 
Directorate of Forensic Science, and TIFAC.
Kwick operates R&D and product development facilities in Chennai, 
focusing on indigenous forensic technologies under the 'Make in 
India' initiative, while maintaining partnerships with leading global 
OEMs and leveraging its DSIR-recognized R&D capabilities.
The Company has established a R&D Center & Private Forensic 
Lab Facility as a Proof of Concept to demonstrate its proposed 
laboratory infrastructure and forensic capabilities to government 
authorities, forensic officials, and prospective customers. The 
facility is intended solely for demonstration purposes and is not 
engaged in commercial sample processing.
Its solutions are trusted by Police Departments, Forensic Science 
Laboratories, Fingerprint Bureaus, Crime Investigation Agencies, 
Training Academies, and Educational Institutions, supported by 
globally recognized quality certifications and strong technical 
expertise.

Transformative Reforms Supporting the Forensic Industry
The chain of custody framework has been 
strengthened, with videography of search and 
seizure operations now mandatory.
Digital and electronic evidence has been clearly 
defined and recognized as legally admissible in court 
proceedings.
e-FIR and Zero FIR provisions have enhanced 
accessibility to justice, particularly for women and 
vulnerable sections of society.
A dedicated Modus Operandi Bureau is being 
established to strengthen crime mapping and 
criminal intelligence capabilities.
Forensic investigation has been made mandatory for 
offences punishable with imprisonment of seven 
years or more.
The provision of Trial in Absentia has been introduced 
to address cases involving absconding accused 
persons.
The number of mobile forensic laboratories has 
expanded from virtually none in 2021 to over 1,000 
today.
