## NEW FINDINGS (not covered in Pass 1)

### 1. AGM Notice, Special Business Items 5 & 6 (pp.147–148) — FY26 related-party ceilings sought
🔴 **Red Flag — most significant Pass 2 addition, directly extends Pass 1 finding #1.**
The Notice of the 14th AGM seeks shareholder approval for two Material Related Party Transaction
ceilings for FY2025-26:
- Item 5: transactions with **Aimtron Corporation, USA up to an aggregate value not exceeding
  INR 100.00 crore.**
- Item 6: transactions with **Aimtron Systems LLC, USA up to an aggregate value not exceeding
  INR 20.00 crore.**

Combined authorised ceiling = **₹120.00 crore for FY26**, against FY25 standalone revenue of only
₹158.31 crore (Note 19, p.105) — i.e. management is seeking headroom equal to roughly **76% of
trailing revenue** to transact with just these two related counterparties. The resolution text
itself states the statutory RPT materiality threshold as the lower of 10% of consolidated
turnover (₹15.92 crore, on FY25 consolidated turnover of ₹159.18 crore) or ₹50 crore — i.e. the
requested ceilings are **6.3x and 1.3x that materiality threshold respectively**, which is exactly
why a special shareholder resolution is required. Pass 1 flagged that RPT revenue concentration
declined as a % of revenue in FY25 (44.41%→27.68%, Note 35) even as the absolute rupee amount
stayed high; this Notice item is the first evidence of the trajectory management is planning for
FY26, and it points toward continued or larger absolute RPT scale, not a structural reduction.
(AGM Notice pp.147–148, cross-ref Note 35/34, Note 19)

### 3. Notice Item 3 vs Note 35 — Chairman's "Non-Executive" classification vs remuneration paid
🟡 Watch — NEW, governance/nomenclature inconsistency.
The AGM Notice (Item 3) describes Mr. Mukesh M Vasani, Chairman, as a **"Non-Executive Director"**
retiring by rotation. Yet Note 35(ii) discloses "Remuneration to Directors" paid to him of
₹24.00 lakh FY25 (up 3.0x from ₹8.00 lakh FY24) — a materially different compensation category
from the token "Directors sitting fees" paid to the company's stated independent/non-executive
directors (Prerna Bokil ₹0.60 lakh, Nischal Sanghvi ₹0.45 lakh FY25, Note 35). The financial
statement signing blocks (pp.89, 91, 97, 109 etc.) list him only as "Chairman & Director" without
specifying executive/non-executive status. Neither the Notes nor the signing block reconciles a
₹24 lakh annual "remuneration" line with a "Non-Executive" designation — if it is commission
under Section 197 for a non-executive director, that requires specific disclosure not found in
these Notes. (Note 35, p.109; AGM Notice p.147)

- **"Loan repaid"**: Aimtron Technologies Private Limited ₹0 FY25 vs ₹87.00 lakh FY24 (a

Including Purchase and Rent, total identifiable gross RPT flow (sales + services provided +

### 5. Note 24 (Finance Costs) vs Note 26 (Other Expenses) — apparent Bank Charges reclassification
🟡 Watch — NEW, unexplained cross-note reclassification (matches Pass 2's mandate to look for
notes whose numbers don't reconcile / that were reclassified without explanation).
"Bank Charges" appears under two different note headings with an unexplained shift in weighting
between years:
- FY24: Note 24 (Finance Costs) Bank Charges ₹4.69 lakh; Note 26 (Other Expenses, Administrative)
  Bank Charges ₹0.02 lakh. ~99.6% of bank charges sat in Finance Costs.
- FY25: Note 24 Bank Charges ₹0 (nil); Note 26 Bank Charges ₹6.81 lakh. 100% of bank charges now
  sit in Other Expenses/Administrative.
Combined bank charges actually **rose** 44.6% YoY (₹4.71 lakh → ₹6.81 lakh), a fact invisible from
either line read in isolation because the classification flipped completely between the two
notes, and neither note nor Note 40 (Regrouping) discloses this reclassification specifically.
(Note 24, p.106; Note 26, p.106)

### 6. Note 18 (Other Current Assets) — export-incentive receivable line disappears, unexplained
🟡 Watch — NEW. "IGST Receivable - Export Rebate Claim" (₹182.15 lakh FY24) is nil in FY25,
replaced by a new line "Export Rebate Claim" at ₹64.63 lakh — a net decline of roughly
₹117.5 lakh in export-incentive-related receivables, with no note explaining whether this is a
scheme change (e.g. transition between rebate mechanisms), a collection, or a write-off, despite
export sales continuing (+3.0% per Note 31, already flagged in Pass 1 finding #8 as a shrinking
share of total revenue). (Note 18, p.104; Consolidated Note 17, p.138)

### 7. Note 6.1 vs 6.2 (Trade Payables Ageing) — MSME overdue pattern is a scale effect, not delay
🟢/🟡 Watch — NEW, partially mitigating nuance to Pass 1 finding #9 (not a contradiction of it).
In FY24, 100% of MSME dues (₹63.36 lakh) sat in the "Undue" (within credit terms) bucket — zero
MSME amount was overdue. In FY25, MSME dues grew 35.8x to ₹2,267.27 lakh (Note 6), but 99.93%
(₹2,265.69 lakh) is still "Undue"; only ₹1.59 lakh is overdue, and that only in the <1 year
bucket. Read together with the ageing tables, the MSME payables surge looks like a pure scale
effect tracking purchase-volume growth rather than a payment-delay pattern — worth noting as
context alongside Pass 1's flag on the missing MSMED Section 22 interest disclosure, which
remains a genuine gap regardless. (Note 6.1, p.100; Note 6.2, p.100)

### 8. Consolidated vs Standalone Trade Payables/ratios — a second rounding mismatch beyond DSO
🟡 Watch — NEW, reinforces Pass 1's pattern-pass candidate on consolidation-mechanics gaps.
Pass 1 flagged a 0.01 rounding gap between the standalone Note 38 receivables-turnover ratio
(3.08) and the MD&A table (3.07). A second, analogous mismatch exists inside the notes
themselves: standalone Note 6 "Due to others" = ₹2,415.08 lakh vs consolidated Note 6
"Due to others" = ₹2,415.09 lakh (₹1,000 gap), and standalone Note 38 Trade payables turnover
ratio = 4.35 vs consolidated Note 37 Trade payables turnover ratio = 4.38. Per Form AOC-1, the
subsidiary's Total Liabilities excluding capital/reserves = 0, meaning it has no trade payables of
its own — so these consolidated figures should tie to standalone exactly, not merely approximate
it. Immaterial in rupee terms but a genuine, repeated small inconsistency in how the consolidated
notes were assembled. (Note 6, p.99 vs Consolidated Note 6, p.133; Note 38, p.111 vs Consolidated
Note 37, p.145; Form AOC-1, p.113)
