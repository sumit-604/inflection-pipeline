## Business Understanding Narrative

Borana Weaves makes greige fabric. Greige is unfinished woven cloth. The company
weaves synthetic yarn on water jet looms in Surat, then sells the cloth to
processors and traders who dye and finish it. Revenue is metres of fabric times
price. Margin comes from the spread between yarn cost and fabric price, from loom
efficiency, and from making its own texturised yarn (backward integration) so it
controls part of the input (IP FY26 Q4 p.9). This is a converter, not a brand.

What is changing is scale. Revenue rose from 199 cr in FY24 to 389 cr in FY26, a
34% jump in the last year (IP FY26 Q4 p.27). Capacity reached 392 mn metres
across four units at 81% utilisation (HlJATJ p.6). Management wants to double
capacity in 24 months (HlJATJ p.33). A solar plant commissions in June 2026 to
cut power cost (HlJATJ p.32). The claimed step up is toward coated technical
fabric (RPU-coated) for jackets and technical wear (HlJATJ p.33).

Why now: the company is freshly listed (2025 IPO), flush with capacity, and
growing fast off a small base. The FY26 margin looks high for weaving: 24% EBITDA
and 17% PAT (IP FY26 Q4 p.27).

What must be true: the 24% EBITDA margin must be durable, not a spread windfall.
Demand must absorb the doubled capacity. The related-party input chain must be
priced fairly.

What breaks it: a reversal in synthetic yarn spreads, a demand air-pocket after
the capacity build, or related-party leakage. The margin is the whole question.

## 2. Business model and archetype
Synthetic greige fabric weaver. Water jet looms give "precision, uniform texture
... higher efficiency ... than traditional methods" (IP FY26 Q4 p.9). Backward
integration into texturised yarn gives "greater control on margins" (IP FY26 Q4
p.9). FY25 gross margin 38.8% and COGS 177.74 on revenue 290.31 (IP FY26 Q3
p.29). Archetype: commodity converter (Section 1B v3.7 Amendment 17 binds). The
one economic engine is the yarn-to-fabric spread run through high-utilisation
looms.

## 3. Competitive advantages
Thin. Weaving is a low-barrier trade with many Surat players. The claimed edge is
water jet loom technology, backward integration, and promoter execution (IP FY26
Q4 p.9). None of these is a durable moat by itself. Cost position and utilisation
can give a temporary edge. Pricing power is absent: this is a price-taker on both
input (yarn) and output (greige). Moat verdict: absent to forming.

## 4. The promoters
Borana family. Chairman & MD Mangilal Ambalal Borana, 24+ years in textiles;
Executive Directors Ankur Borana and Rajkumar Borana, 10+ years each (IP FY26 Q4
p.9). The concern is related-party dependence, not experience. The FY25 annual
report lists POY (yarn) purchases from R&B Denims (common director) and RB
Industries (director is a partner), grey job work from Borana Industries LLP
(common management), and wastage sales to Sachin Paper Mills (director's brother)
(AR FY25 p.54). Material related-party transactions were expected to cross the
10%-of-turnover threshold in FY25-26, needing a special resolution (AR FY25
p.39). The input chain runs partly through related entities. This must be
price-tested. Holding and pledge levels are not in the held corpus.

## 5. Financial trajectory
Multi-year (IP FY26 Q4 p.27): revenue 199 (FY24), 290 (FY25), 389 (FY26); EBITDA
41 (21%), 63 (22%), 92 (24%); PAT 24 (12%), 40 (14%), 65 (17%). Quarterly FY26
shows margin rising through the year: EBITDA margin 21% (Q1), 23% (Q2), 24% (Q3)
(IP FY26 Q3 p.24). Leverage fell: debt/equity 1.45, 0.78, 0.30 across FY24 to
FY26, helped by IPO equity (IP FY26 Q4 p.30). Current ratio 4.33 (FY26). The
inflection is earnings-led and margin-led together. But note ROE fell 49% to 46%
to 23% and ROCE fell 27% to 32% to 19% (IP FY26 Q4 p.30). The fall is mostly the
IPO cash sitting in the denominator, not operational decay. Watch whether ROCE
rebounds as the new capacity fills.

## 6. The transition and the quality ladder
FROM R1-R2, a commodity price-taker/cost-advantaged converter. TO an R2-R3
neighbourhood if coated technical fabric (RPU) and backward integration give
partial spec-in and stickier margin (HlJATJ p.33). Management's claim, quoted
once: "Medium-term vision: To Double Production Capacity in next 24 months"
(HlJATJ p.33). That is a scale claim, not yet a rung-climb claim. The rung climb
depends on technical fabric becoming a real share of mix, which the corpus does
not yet quantify.

## 7. Growth-trigger register
- Capacity double in 24 months, ~5 cr metres added. Source HlJATJ p.33. Status: stated.
- Solar plant, commissioning June 2026, 70-80% power from renewables. Source HlJATJ p.32. Status: underway.
- RPU-coated technical fabric introduction. Source HlJATJ p.33. Status: stated.
- Anti-dumping duty on Chinese yarn freed from December, aiding gross margin. Source Concall FY26 Q3 p.6. Status: delivered (Q3), Q4 tailwind expected.

## 8. Proof check
The last quarters do move the way the growth story claims: revenue and EBITDA
margin rose through FY26 (IP FY26 Q3 p.24). But the proof point cuts both ways.
Management attributes the Q3 gross-margin gain to the government freeing the
anti-dumping duty on Chinese raw material, and expects Q4 to be better for the
same reason (Concall FY26 Q3 p.6). That is an input-cost and policy tailwind, not
a pricing-power gain. It confirms the margin is spread-driven and policy-sensitive.
Proof of scale: yes. Proof of durable margin: not yet.

## 9. Flags from the documents
- Related-party input chain: POY bought from R&B Denims and RB Industries, job
  work through Borana Industries LLP (AR FY25 p.54). Surfaced, not investigated.
- Margin source is a yarn spread plus an anti-dumping-duty change, not pricing
  power (Concall FY26 Q3 p.6). A converter margin that rides policy can revert.
- ROCE fell to 19% in FY26 (IP FY26 Q4 p.30). Benign if IPO-cash driven, a
  concern if the new capacity underfills.
- Recent IPO. Short public record. Standards of disclosure still young.
