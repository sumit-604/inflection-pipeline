## SECTION 3: THE BULL CASE (Why Buy)

**3A. Quality Evidence.** Operating economics are better than the headline. Reported ROCE 13.7% is depressed by Rs 1,654 Cr idle IPO cash (about 48% of the capital base); idle-cash-adjusted operating ROCE is 26.3% audited, 28 to 30% on the operator's ex-cash basis used for Pillar 1 [fttcp-deliberation, B10]. Balance sheet is clean: net cash Rs 1,564 Cr, Total Debt/OPBDITA 0.2x, no long-term repayment obligations, ICRA [ICRA]AA (Stable)/[ICRA]A1+ with liquidity called "Strong" (10 Apr 2026) [B11, fttcp-deliberation]. Cash conversion is GROWTH-INDUCED, not structural: adjusted CFO/PAT about 0.99x once the Rs 1,032.31 Cr customer advance artefact is stripped, so the Kernex-style structural cap tail is closed [fttcp-deliberation]. CFO grew 154% YoY on the audited statement [B01].

**3B. Growth Triggers (ranked by conviction) [B05, B07].**
1. European CDMO Plant 2 ramp. EUR 35m/yr, exclusive oral-liquid supply contract to Dec 2032 (EUR 200m total). Conviction H. Confirms on first commercial dispatch FY28. Kills on European country-registration delays or loss of sole-supplier status. EU GMP approval already received Jan 2026 (about 1 month slip). EUR 100m advance already received and audited.
2. CDMO core volume growth. 16%+ Q3 to 25%+ variance Q4 FY26. Conviction M. Confirms on sustained double digit into Q1/Q2 FY27 WITH a stated driver. Kills on reversion to flat/low single digit. Management admits the driver "is still to be thought through."
3. Zambia JV. USD 25m/yr India-to-Zambia supply guided by end Q2 FY27; USD 45m JV plant (51% Akums), commissioning slipped to FY29. Conviction M to H.
4. API turnaround and margin normalisation (conviction L to M; FY26 loss minus 40 Cr vs FY25 minus 44 Cr, only about 9% better).
5. Domestic branded formulation reaching IPM-parity (conviction L to M; FY26 delivered 2.9% against a mid-teens guide, a miss).

**3C. Moat Assessment.** Combined TURNAROUND: an AVERAGE core (69/100, 1/12 confirmed moats) meeting a STRENGTHENING forward moat (27.3, crossed the 25 threshold this run). Genuine documented developments: EU GMP and multiple regulatory accreditations (Strong qualification lock-in), Zambia geographic first-mover (Strong), strategic partnerships (Strong), 14 granted patents / 129+ filed / 8 first-in-world triple-layer products, 370+ scientists incl 200+ doctorates [B07, B04]. Direction is strengthening, not expanding: the IP is real but unmonetised (no licensing income, no new-product revenue share disclosed), and R&D is only 0.86% of revenue on the P&L Note 45 basis (3.2% on the broader total-income measure) [B07].

**3D. Management Quality.** Promoter verdict CONCERN [B08]. Countervailing positives are genuine: 0% promoter pledge throughout, a credentialed independent board (Kewal Handa ex-MD Pfizer India chairs Audit; Anil Amin ex-Viatris; Satwinder Singh), a blue-chip IPO anchor book (ADIA, BlackRock, SBI MF, HDFC Life), rising DII ownership, dividends initiated within 2 years of listing, and Quadria Capital's full exit at a reported 3x (monetisation, not distress) [B08]. Delivery grade C (Mixed) [B05]: delivered CDMO margin/volume and trade-generics turnaround as promised and made a rare honest "year of miss" API admission, but missed domestic branded guidance and evaded cash-deployment and Schedule M questions across three straight calls.

**3E. Market Opportunity.** TAM runway GOOD [B09]. Conservative domestic-CDMO TAM Rs 13,880 Cr, SAM Rs 11,630 Cr, revenue headroom 3.34x, TAM growth 13.2%. SOM-implied blended company CAGR 13.9% (yr3) to 14.3% (yr5); CDMO segment alone 15.7 to 16.1%. Enough runway to support a low-teens base case, not a 25%+ compounder.

**3F. Peer Positioning.** Akums is the largest domestic CDMO by disclosed share. The peer read "complicates" the bull case [B06]: API price and Middle East input-cost trends corroborate Akums cleanly, and the sector IS in a genuine capex cycle (Cohance about Rs 215 Cr FY26 to Rs 300 Cr FY27, close to Akums). The surge is at best a share shift Akums cannot itself explain.

## NARRATIVE (plain English)

Akums is the largest contract manufacturer of pills in India, sitting on a big pile of IPO cash it will not say how it plans to spend. Strip that idle cash out and the operating business earns a respectable high-twenties return, the balance sheet is clean, and there is a real, if back-loaded, growth story: an exclusive European supply contract running to 2032 and a Zambian government JV, both landing in FY28 to FY29. The moat is genuinely strengthening on paper, patents, accreditations, first-in-world products, though almost none of it earns money yet.

The problem is price, not story. At Rs 702 the stock trades at 42x earnings that are themselves flattered once you normalise a one-off tax hit, against a multiple this quality of business earns of about 23x. Even giving the bull case its best normalised earnings, the numbers do not clear a 25% return hurdle; the expected three-year return is negative. On top of that sit real governance overhangs: a tax search with an auditor caveat, a fraud qualification at three group entities, and a large unsecured loan to a deeply loss-making subsidiary. Management headlines "adjusted profit up 27%" while GAAP profit fell 25%, and dodged the cash-deployment question three calls running.

So this is an AVOID, and not a marginal one, four separate rules force it. It is worth tracking, because the transition is real and back-loaded and the business could grow into a defensible price. The discipline is the entry conjunction: even if the stock falls into Rs 254 to Rs 286, buy only if it got there cleanly. If it drops into that zone because working capital blew out, the tax demand crystallised, the CDMO surge reversed, or the subsidiary loan impaired, the zone is withdrawn, not an invitation.
