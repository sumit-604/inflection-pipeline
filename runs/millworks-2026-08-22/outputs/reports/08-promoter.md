### 2D. Other regulators — MCA compounding (the most material finding in
this stage)
Two Companies Act non-compliances are self-disclosed and under compounding
(RHP, risk factors, p.36-40, region read):
1. **Section 10A (subscription-money routing) lapse at incorporation**: at
   the company's formation, subscription money from two subscribers
   (Rashmi Sridhar Acharya and Sowmya Madhu) was routed through incorrect
   bank accounts — a partnership-firm account and Mr. Madhu's personal
   account respectively — instead of the company's own account. A
   compounding application was filed and has since been disposed via the
   adjudication route. 📰/✅ VERIFIED (self-disclosed filing fact).
2. **Section 185 violation — loans to directors**: the company advanced
   loans to two director-promoters, Rashmi Sridhar Acharya (₹10 lakh) and
   Sowmya Madhu (₹1.14 lakh in FY24, ₹2 lakh in FY25), in breach of the
   Companies Act's prohibition on companies lending to their own
   directors. A compounding application has been filed; the loans are no
   longer outstanding. ✅ VERIFIED (self-disclosed).
3. **FCGPR (FEMA foreign-investment reporting) delay**: allotments to a
   foreign subscriber (Dhawal Arvind Thakker and others) were not
   reported to RBI within the prescribed FCGPR timeline; as of the RHP
   date the regularisation remains in process with RBI. ✅ VERIFIED
   (self-disclosed, unresolved as of filing).

None of these are SEBI, criminal, or market-abuse matters; each is a
procedural Companies Act/FEMA compliance lapse, self-disclosed, under
active regularisation. Taken individually each is minor; taken together
with the compliance-calendar pattern below, they describe a company whose
back-office statutory discipline was weak through its pre-IPO growth
phase and is only now being professionalised.

Separately, and materially worse in volume: the RHP discloses **multi-year,
recurring delays in EPF and ESIC statutory filings**, month after month
from mid-2023 through March 2026, with delays as long as 74 days (Jan-Apr
2024 EPF cycle) and, notably, *worsening* in the run-up to listing — 73
days late in December 2025, 52 days late in February 2026, 42 days late
in January 2026 (RHP, p.34-36, ESIC delay table). The company's stated
corrective measure each time is identical boilerplate ("strengthened its
accounts compliance… dedicating a team from HR"), repeated across ~40
disclosed delay instances without evident effect on the trend. No show-
cause notices from regulators have been received (RHP, p.36). ⚠️ This is
a documented, years-long pattern, not a one-off lapse, and it continued
through the listing preparation window.

MCA/RoC disqualifications, NCLT oppression, CCI, NGT, consumer/labour
forum actions against the promoters: none found in the RHP or in web

### 6B. Classification
**CAUTION.** No SEBI, criminal, tax-evasion, or market-conduct red flags;
0% pledge; no promoter share sales; fresh-issue-only IPO; credible,
domain-relevant independent directors. Against that clean core sit three
real, company-disclosed items that are not deal-breakers individually but

statutory filings running into the listing window; (ii) two Companies Act
compounding matters, one of which (Section 185, loans to directors) is a
