- **Inventory rundown**: a modest ₹66.07 lakh net positive contribution from inventories — not a material driver either way.

**Conclusion**: the CFO collapse is a genuine, structural working-capital/quality-of-earnings problem centered on the trade receivables buildup already flagged by B02 (Finding #2, FLAG-CASH), not an accounting artifact of interest classification or one-time items. **DuPont decomposition** (Net Profit Margin × Asset Turnover × Equity Multiplier, closing-balance basis):
- FY25: 16.26% × (15,830.73/23,020.45 = 0.688) × (23,020.45/15,508.64 = 1.484) = **16.60%** ROE
- FY24: 14.63% × (9,297.59/9,250.32 = 1.005) × (9,250.32/5,169.93 = 1.789) = **26.31%** ROE

**Is ROE operational or leverage-driven?** The ROE decline from FY24 to FY25 is driven almost entirely by the collapse in the equity multiplier (1.789x → 1.484x), itself a direct consequence of the ₹7.76cr IPO equity infusion diluting the leverage base — this is a **capital-structure effect, not an operating deterioration**. However, the improvement embedded in Net Profit Margin (14.63%→16.26%) is itself **not operational** either — it traces to non-operating tailwinds (interest income up sharply post-IPO cash pile, finance costs down 84.5% post debt repayment; per B02 Finding #6) even as EBITDA margin compressed ~390bps. **Net conclusion: ROE quality is mixed on both axes — the balance sheet is genuinely stronger (less leveraged, net cash) but the earnings feeding into ROE are not cleanly operational, and the headline ROE decline understates rather than reveals the true degree of operating-margin compression.**
