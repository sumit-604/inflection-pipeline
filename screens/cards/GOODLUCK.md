## Business Understanding Narrative

Goodluck buys steel and turns it into shapes. CR sheets and GI pipes at the commodity end,
precision and auto tubes in the middle, forged flanges, transmission towers, solar
structures and railway bridges at the engineered end (Concall p.3, p.4, p.5). Revenue is
volume times realisation. Profit is the spread between steel bought and product sold.
Q1FY27 standalone volume was 1,22,718 tonnes at 98% utilisation (Concall p.4).

What is changing is where profit comes from. Goodluck Defence and Aerospace makes 155 mm
artillery shells. **The parent holds 79.43%** and the subsidiary **began operations in
October 2025** (Ind-Ra, 14 Jul 2026, p.2). In Q1FY27 it made Rs 80 crore of revenue at a
38% EBITDA margin (Concall p.9). That is 6% of consolidated revenue and 22% of
consolidated EBITDA. Consolidated EBITDA rose 46% while standalone rose 15%.

Why now: orders. Rs 255 crore for about 50,000 ERFB shells, Rs 52 crore for 20,000 M107
shells, plus a DGQA certificate (Concall p.4, p.7). Shell capacity is 150,000 a year,
expanding toward 400,000 (Concall p.15). Set that against FY26, when defence executed
**Rs 46 crore in the whole year** (guidance file s.4). The step change is eleven months old.

What must be true: the 30% to 35% shell margin must hold as volume scales, the delayed
expansion must reach financial closure, and the group must carry the debt funding it.
Ind-Ra expects **net leverage to peak across FY27 and FY28**, improving only from FY29.

What breaks it: the record. Of three testable multi-year promises, two missed. Rs 5,000
crore of revenue by FY26, guided in November 2023: delivered Rs 4,100 crore. Free of
long-term debt by FY25, targeted in 2022: still repaying in FY27. The 10% EBITDA margin
target was met at 10.2% (guidance file s.5).

## 2. Business model and archetype
Two businesses under one roof.
The base is a steel converter. Pipes and CR sheet earn 3% to 5% EBITDA, solar structures 7%
to 8%, infrastructure 10% to 11%, precision tubes and forgings 12% to 13% (Concall p.11).
Margin is a conversion spread over a steel price management calls a live risk (Concall
p.11). Archetype: commodity converter with a value-added tail.
The second is an ordnance maker at 38% to 42% EBITDA (Concall p.9, p.15). That is a licence
and scarcity business, gated by DGQA qualification and government demand.
The converter funds the ordnance maker. Ind-Ra takes "a fully consolidated view" because
the two share directors and bankers, have "cash fungibility with each other" and integrated
treasury, and because "GIL will be infusing funds for GDAL's planned capacity expansion
over FY27-FY28".

## 3. Competitive advantages
In the converter the advantage is scale and breadth, not price. Goodluck claims over 30%
share of solar support structures and makes 50,000 tonnes a year of transmission towers
(Concall p.4). Its 245 mm hydraulic tube substitutes an imported seamless tube and sells
into the United States against a 50% duty (Concall p.5). That earns 12% to 13%.
The breadth is now independently confirmed. Ind-Ra lists "diversified business profile; low
customer concentration risk" as a rating strength. The counterparty record shows 53
customers: ONGC, Indian Oil, BHEL, NTPC, Power Grid and Reliance; Larsen & Toubro, Indian
Railways, the high-speed rail project, Alstom and ABB; DRDO, ISRO, HAL and BEML; Audi, BMW
and Mercedes (ownership file s.6). No name dominates.
In defence the advantage is regulatory: DGQA qualification and a licensed line already
running at 150,000 shells.
Against both, pass-through is unpredictable. Asked whether customers claw back the benefit
when steel and oil fall, the CEO said "Totally unpredictable" (Concall p.14). Moat: forming
in defence, thin in the converter, with real customer diversification.

## 4. The promoters
The Garg family. M. C. Garg Chairman, Ram Aggarwal CEO, Sanjay Bansal CFO.
**Promoter holding is 54.00% after a 29 June 2026 transaction**, 1,79,48,855 of 3,32,38,509
pre-bonus shares (SAST Reg. 29(2), FY2027 Q2, p.3). Pledge NOT FOUND.
**The family sold Rs 115.69 crore on 1 July 2026 at about Rs 1,420 pre-bonus**, across seven
entities: Manish Garg Rs 41.20 crore, Dhruv Aggarwal Rs 22.73 crore, Munni Lal HUF Rs 15.62
crore and four smaller (ownership file s.2). SageOne Flagship Growth bought the largest
block. The same family bought 4,62,500 shares at Rs 600 in May 2025. Open market, disclosed
properly. Weigh it.
Three capital-allocation decisions. First, the defence subsidiary was funded by selling
stake to outsiders rather than by a rights issue at the parent. Four analysts asked why;
each time the answer was that the financial adviser preferred it (Concall p.7, p.9, p.16,
p.18). The sweep sizes the leak: the parent keeps **79.43%**. Second, promoter entity
Goodluck Green Energy is being merged in with no swap ratio disclosed (Concall p.16).
Third, the guidance record above. Discount future guidance accordingly.

## 5. Financial trajectory
FY26 consolidated: total income Rs 4,120.5 crore, up 4.1%. EBITDA Rs 418.49 crore, up 26%.
PAT Rs 182.58 crore, up 10.2%. Volume 4,68,161 MT, up 5.8%. Utilisation about 94%. Defence
executed Rs 46 crore (guidance file s.4). Derived: EBITDA margin 10.2%, PAT margin 4.4%.
Q1FY27 standalone: revenue Rs 1,205.94 crore up 23%, EBITDA Rs 110.53 crore up 15%, EPS
Rs 14.94 against Rs 12.62.
Q1FY27 consolidated: total income Rs 1,287.44 crore up 31%, EBITDA Rs 139.66 crore up 46%,
PAT Rs 67.22 crore up 67%, EPS Rs 19.13 (Concall p.6).
Derived: consolidated EBITDA margin 10.8%, standalone 9.2%. Volume grew 8.8% against 23%
standalone revenue growth, so realisation rose too.
Rs 25 crore of debt was repaid in Q1FY27 against Rs 62 crore for the year (Concall p.9).
Capex is about Rs 400 crore in defence and Rs 100 to 150 crore standalone (Concall p.14).
Against that, **Ind-Ra warns net debt to EBITDA is likely to peak across FY27 and FY28,
"primarily due to the planned debt-led capex in GDAL and GIL", improving only from FY29.**
CRISIL upgraded Rs 854.75 crore of facilities two notches on 30 June 2026.
FY27 guidance: 15% to 20% consolidated revenue growth, steel volume 13% to 15%, defence
revenue Rs 300 to 350 crore, cut from Rs 350 to 400 crore because the expansion slipped
(Concall p.5, p.8, p.18).
Inflection classification: earnings-led and mix-led. Volume grew 8.8%; profit grew 67%.
Not a volume cycle. A margin mix change driven by one subsidiary eleven months old.

## 6. The transition and the quality ladder
Management's claim, once: "we have been consciously moving towards becoming the
diversified, engineering-led company with a greater focus on value-added,
technology-driven, application-specific products" (Concall p.3).
FROM R2, cost-advantaged converter. Most tonnage earns 3% to 13% on a steel spread.
TO a split destination. The converter climbs slowly toward R3 as precision tubes, hydraulic
tubes and forgings take mix share: hydraulic tube utilisation rose 50% to 60-65% in one
quarter and the segment does over Rs 1,000 crore (Concall p.5, p.16). The defence
subsidiary sits on a different ladder near R4 or R5 on its 38% margin.
So the group is not climbing one rung. It is growing a higher-rung business inside a
lower-rung one, and owns 79.43% of it. Rung-jump base rates do not apply cleanly to a
subsidiary structure. Ind-Ra supports the direction, expecting "the share of value-added
and higher-margin products" to rise over the medium term.

## 8. Proof check
The quarter proves the earnings claim. The record undermines the guidance claim.
Fired: consolidated EBITDA up 46% and PAT up 67% on revenue up 31%, the gap explained by a
38% defence margin. Utilisation 98% standalone. Exports up 53%. Two agencies acted
favourably within a fortnight, one a two-notch upgrade. The 10% margin target was met.
Not fired: the defence expansion has slipped 6 to 9 months on financial closure, FY27
defence guidance was cut, and the FY28 Rs 1,000 crore target was pushed out with no
replacement (Concall p.8, p.13, p.14). Standalone EBITDA grew 15% against 23% revenue
growth, so the base is losing margin while the subsidiary gains it.
Named proof point: whether the defence expansion reaches financial closure and starts in
Q4FY27. A second slip makes three misses in four, a pattern rather than an incident.

## 9. Flags from the documents

  of repayment. The most useful thing the sweep found.
- **Two guidance misses in three.** "Concentrating on deleveraging the balance sheet"
  appears in the FY26 call and in an H1FY25 presentation two years earlier.
- **The promoter family sold Rs 115.69 crore on 1 July 2026**, taking holding to 54.00%.
- **Value migration, now sized.** The subsidiary raised Rs 285 crore at an implied pre-money

- **No segment disclosure for defence.** "This is only 1 segment, iron and steel, even

- **Management could not answer basic operating questions.** Shell volume: "That I will have

Not seen: pledge, absolute debt, cash flow, related-party schedule, auditor opinion,

## 10. Independent check: the credit rating
**FOUND. The first pass recorded NOT FOUND and was wrong.**
**CRISIL, 30 June 2026: UPGRADE.** Rs 854.75 crore of facilities. Long term **AA+/Stable,
up from A+/Positive**. Short term **A1+, up from A1** (rating file s.1). That is two
notches, with the outlook resetting from Positive to Stable at the higher level. It was
issued three weeks before the family sold.
**India Ratings, 14 July 2026: NEW ASSIGNMENT IND AA-/Stable and IND A1+** on Rs 1,150
crore of facilities (rating file s.2). Ind-Ra consolidates parent and subsidiary fully.
Strengths: diversified profile, low customer concentration, GDAL ramping from FY27, rising
value-added share. Constraints: net leverage peaking FY27-FY28, competition, cyclicality.
The two agencies sit two notches apart, AA+ against AA-. Only page 2 of the Ind-Ra
rationale is indexed, so the tables behind the leverage view are not held.

## 12. Verdict card
**PROCEED to `/step1`, with leverage through the FY27-FY28 capex peak ranked first and the
structure question demoted to second.** That reordering is what the sweep forced.
Goodluck is a real transition. A converter earning 3% to 13% has grown a defence business
earning 38%, from Rs 46 crore in all of FY26 to Rs 80 crore in one quarter, with orders in
hand, a DGQA certificate and 98% base utilisation. CRISIL upgraded the parent two notches.
That is the shape the strategy hunts, now with independent confirmation.
The first card ranked the carve-out above everything because the parent's stake was
unknown. It is 79.43%. What replaces it at the top is the balance sheet: about Rs 550 crore
of capex against Rs 62 crore of scheduled repayment, and a rating agency stating in writing
that net leverage peaks for two years before it improves.
Load-bearing facts a deep run must verify first:
1. Absolute total debt, net debt to EBITDA and operating cash flow for FY26 and FY27.
   Retrieve Ind-Ra pages 3 onward. Apply Amendment 17.
2. Why CRISIL rates AA+ and Ind-Ra AA- on the same group, three weeks apart.
3. The Green Energy merger: swap ratio, valuation report, promoter interest.
4. The defence subsidiary's own accounts. The parent reports one segment.
5. Financial closure of the defence expansion, and whether Q4FY27 holds.
6. Promoter pledge and the full pattern behind 54.00%.
What would change the view: a demerger of the subsidiary to Goodluck India shareholders, or
a published valuation on the Green Energy merger, removes the structural residue and makes
this a strong PROCEED. A second expansion delay, or FY27-FY28 leverage running beyond what
Ind-Ra models, makes it a WATCH. No price here.
