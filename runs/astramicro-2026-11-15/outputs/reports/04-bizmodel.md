## SECTION 1: THE BUSINESS MODEL IN PLAIN ENGLISH

### 1A One line
Astra designs and builds the radio-frequency "eyes and ears" (radar modules, electronic-warfare parts, communication radios, satellite electronics) for Indian defence, space and weather customers, sells almost all of it to government bodies on multi-year orders, and is trying to move from selling parts to selling whole systems (complete systems 27.8% of revenue in FY26 against 12.01% in FY23, AR p.26).

### 1E The chai-stall-uncle version
Astra is like a workshop that makes the special engine parts for a few big government buyers. Each buyer places a large order and the workshop delivers over years. Most of the money arrives late, after the buyer accepts the goods. Last year the workshop made more profit per rupee because it built richer parts, not because it charged more. The big risk is that a few buyers are most of the business and they pay slowly. A fat order book is like a full diary of bookings. It tells you work is coming, but not when the cheque clears.

### 4B Valuation method applicability (formal handoff to Role 1)

| Method | Applicable? | Why |
|---|---|---|

| Dividend yield, asset value | Not applicable | No dividend base; asset-light for value |
| Replacement cost | Not applicable | Qualification, not plant, is the asset |

PRIMARY: P/E on forward, order-book-anchored earnings. SECONDARY: EV/EBITDA cross-check. TERTIARY: SOTP, only after demerger carve-out figures are filed.
Cycle stage that matters: position in the order-execution cycle (when the HAL order's revenue starts and at what margin) and the working-capital cycle (receivables at year-end vs mid-year), not a commodity cycle. The multiple choice is not made here; the Section 1B destination PE governs it.
