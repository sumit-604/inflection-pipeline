# Market read, 1 October 2026

This brief uses the 34 Chartink table files for the 1 October trade date. All
34 were present, so every section has its data. The full figures are in the
data appendix, `2026-10-01-appendix.html`. Claude Code has no web access. This
brief checks no news and gives no reason for any move. It describes what the
market is doing. It is not advice to buy or sell anything. The operator

This brief compares Thursday with the 30 September brief. That brief named
four ways the cautious view would get worse. Two of them came true on
Thursday. The fifty-day count fell through its washout low. New yearly lows
climbed past 165 while the fear gauge rose above its thirty-week average. The
other two did not come true. The twenty-day count held its washout low by four
names, and no second washout came. None of the four ways the cautious view
would fail came true. Wednesday's flat floor gave way.

## The picture in one paragraph

Thursday broke the floor that Wednesday held. The count of stocks above the
two-hundred-day line fell from 970 to 899, the lowest since 18 May. The
fifty-day count fell to 618 and broke its washout low of 705. It is the lowest
fifty-day count since early April. New yearly lows jumped to 214, the most
since 30 March. New highs fell to 45. About one stock in four rose and three in
four fell. Turnover ran about a quarter above its year-ago level, so real money
sold. Fear rose. The VIX climbed about seven percent in a day and now sits
above its thirty-week average. Only the IT index rose. This was a hard day, not
a washout. 108 stocks fell four and a half percent, against 342 on the
September washout. The twenty-day count held its washout low by four names.
The leaders shrank but did not break. Healthcare, defence and castings still
hold most of their stocks above the long line. The small end still sits above
its trend. The large end sits further under its own.

## Where the market stands

Size still orders the long trend, and every size fell on Thursday. The
microcap index fell more than one percent. It still sits about five percent
above its thirty-week trend line, the furthest of any stock index. It is the
only stock index up over three months, by about three percent. The smallcap 50
sits about two percent above its line. The smallcap 250 sits just above its
line. The midcap 150 sits almost four percent under its line. The Nifty closed
at about 22,420, down almost one percent. It sits more than six percent under
its thirty-week line and is down about eight percent over three months. The
small end still holds its long trend, but the cushion is thinner each day. The
large end is further from its trend than at any point this week.

The story of the last month runs like this. On 7 September, 1,255 stocks held
their two-hundred-day line. On 15 September came the washout, when 342 stocks
fell four and a half percent in one session. The long count dropped to 1,035.
Buyers lifted it to 1,185 by 23 September. Then it slid for four sessions to
about 970, where it sat flat on Tuesday and Wednesday. On Thursday it fell to
899. That is below the 11 June low of 909. The last lower reading came on 18
May, at 882. About four stocks in ten hold the long line now. About six in ten
sit below it.

The fifty-day line broke. 618 stocks sit above it, down from 730. On washout
day that count was 705, and the last brief called it the hinge. The hinge gave
way by almost 90 names. The last time the fifty-day count sat this low was 7
April, the day before the April thrust that started the spring rally. The
twenty-day line held, barely. 481 stocks sit above it, down from 628. Its
washout low was 477. So the short line has fallen back to the edge of its low
in one day. The repair of the last two weeks is almost gone.

The yearly highs and lows turned hard against the market. 214 stocks printed a
new yearly low on Thursday, against 83 on Wednesday. That is the most since 30
March, when the count reached 913. Only 45 printed a new high. Lows now beat
highs for six sessions running, and the gap is close to five to one. About
three stocks in ten sit within fifteen percent of a yearly low. Only two in ten
sit within fifteen percent of a yearly high. Within thirty percent, the high
side still leads, but only just. The crowd near the bottom grew fast again.

The day was broad and heavy. About 24 stocks in 100 rose and 75 fell. 46
stocks rose four and a half percent. 108 fell that hard. That is a bad day, but
it is about a third of a washout. Turnover ran about a quarter above its
year-ago level. Light-volume stocks still outnumbered heavy-volume stocks,
about three to two, but the gap narrowed from Wednesday. So the volume came in
on the selling side. Only about one stock in six traded inside a three percent
range, the fewest in a month. Stocks moved wide in both directions inside the
day.

The day's action showed sellers reaching and buyers catching part of it. More
than a third of all stocks traded four percent below their prior close at some
point. That is close to the widest intraday drop of the month. Only about a
quarter of those closed near the low. So many stocks found buyers off the
bottom. About one stock in ten traded four percent above its prior close, the
fewest of the month. Three in ten of those held the gain. The down tail grew
again. About one stock in 23 is now down ten percent in five days. That share
has risen for five sessions running.

Fear woke up. The India VIX, the market's fear gauge, rose about seven percent
to about 14.5. It is up about nineteen percent this week. It now sits about six
percent above its thirty-week average. On Wednesday it sat just under that
average. Gold ETFs fell about three percent over the month. The Nifty fell
about six percent over the same month. Both still fall, and gold still falls
less.

## What would change this read

The read tonight is cautious, and it got worse on Thursday. The cautious read
fails if the fifty-day count climbs back above its washout level of about 705
and holds. It fails if the two-hundred-day count climbs back above about 970,
where it sat before Thursday. It fails if new yearly highs outnumber new lows
for several sessions, which is far from tonight's 45 against 214. It also
fails if a thrust day arrives, 300 or more stocks jumping four and a half
percent in one session, on volume above its year-ago level. In May and June
the long count recovered from this zone within two weeks. A thrust would put
Thursday in that pattern.

The cautious read turns worse if the twenty-day count closes under about 477,
its washout low. That is only four names away. It turns worse if the
two-hundred-day count falls under about 880, the May low. It turns worse if a
second washout arrives, 300 or more stocks falling four and a half percent in
one session. It also turns worse if the VIX keeps climbing above about 14.5
while new lows stay above 200. The twenty-day line is the first place the next
step would show.
