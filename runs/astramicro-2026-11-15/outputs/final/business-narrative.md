# ASTRAMICRO (Astra Microwave Products Ltd): business narrative, run 2026-11-15

Mode: Phase 1 lite. Stages 10 and 11 and the FTTCP deliberation have not run. This file carries no valuation, no entry zone, no decision and no multiple. The Mental Model Declaration is unsigned.

## BUSINESS UNDERSTANDING NARRATIVE

Astra Microwave designs and builds the radio frequency hardware that lets Indian radars, electronic warfare kits, military radios and satellites send and sense signals. Defence work was 72.5% of FY26 standalone revenue of Rs 1,156 Cr, and it covers transmit and receive modules, antenna units, radar subsystems, a few complete radars, and missile electronics. The largest new product is the active antenna unit for the Uttam radar on the Tejas Mk1A, ordered by HAL as 122 units plus 121 interface frames. Exports including deemed exports were 14.0% of revenue, and the run infers that most of this line is sales to Astra Rafael Comsys, the 50% joint venture with Rafael that builds software defined radios and electronic warfare systems. Space electronics for ISRO (9.5%) and weather radars for IMD (4.0%) make up the rest, and both lines leave the listed company in the demerger. A customer cannot drop these parts easily, because each one is qualified on a long DRDO or DPSU programme and a new supplier must pass the same tests again. The buyers are almost all the Indian state: DRDO took 28.2% of FY26 revenue, BEL and other DPSUs took 22.7%, and HAL is now the largest single buyer through one Uttam order of Rs 1,879 Cr. Three unnamed customers took 53% of FY26 revenue, so the buyer sets the terms and pays only after delivery, customer tests and third party inspection. Present demand comes from indigenisation: the FY27 MoD capital acquisition budget is about Rs 1.85 lakh crore with 75% earmarked for Indian firms, and the Defence Acquisition Council acceptance of necessity approvals fill the pool of programmes Astra can bid for. The nearest demand signal is the HAL Uttam AAAU Phase 1 deliveries, 12 units by September 2027, which follow the LCA Mk1A 97 aircraft schedule. Forward growth rests on awards that have not landed yet: the BEL QRSAM contract signing with Rs 700 to 800 Cr of Astra subsystem orders, the DRDO development to production milestones for Virupaksha, Angad and the AMCA antenna unit, and India defence export value. Each driver has an outside signal in HAL and BEL exchange filings, PIB releases on DAC approvals and exports, and the Astra Rafael Comsys order book and revenue through its rating rationale and MCA filings. The run sizes the market Astra serves at about Rs 4,460 Cr in FY27, growing 10.5% a year, but that figure is management's own programme pool, so it is an order of magnitude only. Astra already holds about a quarter of that pool, so growth depends on the pool expanding and awards arriving on time more than on share gain. The defence line has the clearest advantage: qualification lock in on multiyear programmes such as Uttam is documented and scores Moderate in the emerging moat scan. The Rafael partnership gives the radio and electronic warfare line a second documented Moderate advantage, but Astra's Rs 196 Cr receivable from that venture has no stated credit terms. Policy tailwind also scores Moderate, on claim evidence only, and every Indian peer shares it, while talent asymmetry and the barrier to cannibalisation (categories I1 and I2) both score zero. Pricing power comes from product mix, not price, the run did not establish a separate moat for the space and weather lines, and the scan totals 16.5 (a verifier recount gives 19.2), MODEST on both counts.

Uttam order lands, cash still lags

Astra is a Hyderabad defence electronics maker with five units and a Bengaluru R&D centre. Its FY26 annual report is its 35th. FY26 standalone revenue was Rs 1,156 Cr, up 10.7%, and standalone PAT was Rs 178 Cr, up 24%. Consolidated PAT was Rs 193 Cr. The standalone order book stood at Rs 3,937 Cr on 30 September 2026, up from Rs 2,156 Cr three months earlier. The promoters hold only 6.54%, so professional managers run the company.

The transition is from parts supplier to system maker and volume radar producer. Complete systems were 27.8% of FY26 revenue against 12.0% in FY23. The HAL Uttam order of Rs 1,879 Cr is 48% of the book, and it runs within five years, with 12 units due by September 2027. Without it the book is Rs 2,058 Cr, below the Rs 2,141 Cr of March 2026. No filing or call discloses the Uttam margin or advance terms, and management says PSU production orders carry no advance. The demerger also matters. Space and weather supplied 56% of FY26 growth. The part that stays listed grew about 5.1%, and defence sales fell 0.5%.

The scorecard is average, and cash is the reason. Gate 0 scores 68 of 160 with a core of 53 of 100, classified AVERAGE, and a cash conversion deal breaker caps it there. Returns are strong: consolidated ROCE was 22.9% in FY26, and net debt fell to Rs 123 Cr from Rs 397 Cr. Cash conversion is weak: ten year cumulative CFO to PAT is 0.47, and CFO was negative in FY23, FY24 and FY25. FY26 CFO of Rs 387 Cr leaned on a working capital release, with payable days stretched to 72 from 38 and a Rs 160 Cr deposit sitting in year end cash. H1 FY27 reversed it: standalone CFO was minus Rs 146 Cr, inventory rose to Rs 793 Cr from Rs 609 Cr, and short term borrowings rose to Rs 411 Cr from Rs 224 Cr. Receivables more than two years old rose to Rs 82 Cr from Rs 46 Cr, while their provision cover fell to 14.9% from 19.6%. CRISIL upgraded the long term rating to A+/Stable on 28 August 2026 for an improving working capital cycle, yet it still calls the requirement large and expects it to stay at 420 to 440 days. The run cannot yet say whether the H1 outflow is a growth build or the old pattern. The FY27 year end cash flow and inventory will tell.

Margins improved, and growth is back loaded. Gross margin rose to 49.2% from 43.9% in FY26, and EBITDA before other income rose to 28.1% from 25.5%. Q2 FY27 was strong: standalone revenue was Rs 261 Cr, up 22.5%, at a 33.2% EBITDA margin. H1 FY27 revenue grew only 6.6% to Rs 437 Cr. The FY27 guide of Rs 1,350 Cr needs about Rs 913 Cr in H2 against Rs 745 Cr in H2 FY26. Q4 carried 42% of FY26 revenue. Net material cost stayed flat while revenue grew 10.7%, and the annual report gives no reason. So the margin gain could be mix, or it could be timing.

Management hits hard numbers and misses soft ones. On the concalls, the run grades credibility B: 5 promises delivered, 3 partial and 3 missed. The red flag verifier counts 4 delivered and reads it B minus. FY26 revenue of Rs 1,156 Cr met the Rs 1,150 Cr guide, ARC met Rs 360 Cr, and both the HAL order and the demerger scheme arrived on time. The FY26 growth guide of 15 to 20% ended at 10.7%, QRSAM slipped on three calls, and the promise to keep cash flow positive broke in H1 FY27. In six months the turnover goal moved from doubling in three to four years, to nearly tripling, to six or seven times in five years. The ARC FY27 sales target fell from over Rs 600 Cr to Rs 360 Cr between two calls. Management also volunteered that part of the inventory is legacy stock built over about three decades and that it carries obsolescence risk.

Governance reads CAUTION, not CONCERN. The run found no SEBI order against the company, no pledge, no fraud finding and no auditor exit. The related party limit for ARC passed with 35.7% of institutional votes against. A non independent director who holds ARC shares and warrants sits on the Audit Committee that approves those transactions. SEBI sent an inquiry on the FY24 results in March 2026, and the run found no outcome. Insiders took about 71% of a warrant issue at Rs 864, with Rs 130.5 Cr due by 29 December 2026. The company had three CFOs in FY26, and the departing MD gets Rs 1.36 Cr in cash compensation. March 2026 lender returns were not filed. Against that, two independent directors with strong records joined in May 2025, the MD succession was internal and passed 99.9999% in favour, mutual funds rose to 15.6% and FPIs to 10.5%, and the demerger is a one for one mirror with no cash. Accounting quality scores 6 of 10.

The peers confirm the margin and complicate the cash. Only Data Patterns and Apollo have transcripts. Paras has none, so the peer set is two. Both peers confirm that customer approvals and inspections delay revenue, and both report margins at or above Astra's level: Apollo's standalone EBITDA margin is 34 to 35%, and Data Patterns was at 40% in FY26. Apollo still produced positive H1 FY27 cash flow of Rs 22 Cr, so peers do not explain an outflow of Astra's size. Both peers are adding capacity well ahead of orders, while Astra plans Rs 40 to 50 Cr a year. That shows the peers' own plans and does not test Astra's need. Data Patterns says, with a hedge, that Astra is the DcPP partner for the MCA variant and not the Su-30 variant. Apollo reports a 50:50 split of first lot QRSAM missiles between BDL and private industry, which explains part of the QRSAM delay.

The market runway is good, but the sizing rests partly on management. The total market runs from Rs 17,325 Cr on the conservative view to Rs 66,052 Cr on the realistic view. The serviceable pool of Rs 4,460 Cr a year comes from management's own programme list, and Astra already holds 24.8% of it. On the defence and export base of Rs 1,000 Cr that stays after the demerger, the obtainable share gives Rs 1,567 Cr by FY29 and Rs 1,980 Cr by FY31, about 16% and 15% a year. The FY28 guide of Rs 1,600 Cr equals the FY29 figure, so guidance runs about a year ahead of the share lens. The six to seven times turnover claim is larger than the whole FY31 serviceable pool. Installed and committed capacity of about Rs 1,386 Cr is Rs 181 Cr short of the FY29 figure, unless Uttam content is outsourced or passed through. Several market searches failed, so this stage is partial.
