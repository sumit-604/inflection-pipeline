## Evidence taxonomy used throughout
📄 DOCUMENTED — capex committed, product launched, approval received, disclosed metric.
🎙️ MANAGEMENT CLAIM — stated in a concall or presentation, not backed by a filed number.
🔍 ANALYST INFERENCE — inferred from cross-referencing two or more sources.

Corpus credibility context carried forward: B05 grades concall credibility **D** (guidance
walked down four consecutive quarters, an unreconciled PBT/PAT-turned-positive claim, an
undisclosed growth-basis switch). Every 🎙️ item below is weighted 0.7x per the standard
formula regardless of grade, but the grade is the reason this report leans harder on 📄 and
treats 🎙️ narrative ("moat," "1/3 market share," "capex cycle behind us") as unproven until a
filed number backs it.

### 2C. Growth embedded in capex — the arithmetic
Historical fixed-asset turnover (consolidated): FY26 revenue Rs181.29cr ÷ FY26 net PP&E
Rs81.56cr (Rs8,155.87 lakh, Note 3, Annual_Report_FY2026.pdf PDF p.160) = **2.22x**.

Applying this ratio to the only capex genuinely "under execution" — the FY27 guided range of
Rs6-10cr (🎙️, Q4 FY26 call), midpoint Rs8cr — implies incremental revenue of:
Rs8cr × 2.22x = **~Rs17.8cr**, or **~9.8% above the FY26 revenue base** (Rs181.29cr).

The hospitality Rs70-80cr programme is deliberately **excluded** from this arithmetic: it is
not under execution (still "planning stage," deferred twice, zero ground broken per B05's
timeline_slippages), so including it would overstate embedded growth on an uncommitted
number, violating the never-estimate rule. **capex_embedded_growth_pct = ~10%**, and it is a
routine-maintenance-capex figure, not a capacity-transformation one — winery capacity itself
is not being added to in the pipeline the company has actually committed to.

## SECTION 3: THE 22-CATEGORY SCAN

### FAMILY A — Product & Technology
**A1 — Rare manufacturing capability.** NO EVIDENCE FOUND. Wine manufacturing is not a
scarce global capability; no qualification-timeline, long-lead-equipment, or multi-year
regulatory-approval story exists in the corpus.

**A2 — Patent and IP pipeline.** NO EVIDENCE FOUND, and the corpus contains explicit
anti-evidence: the FY26 AR's own Technology Absorption annexure states R&D expenditure
(capital, recurring, total, and as % of turnover) is **NIL**, and "efforts made towards
technology absorption" is marked NA (📄, Annual_Report_FY2026.pdf PDF p.74). The
much-repeated "proprietary grape clones" story (below, under B1) is genuine multi-year
agronomical work, but it is unpatented, unfiled, and un-costed as R&D — it is tacit
know-how, not an IP pipeline in the sense this category scores.

**A3 — Process innovation.** NO EVIDENCE FOUND. No yield disclosure, no unit-cost-vs-quality
data, no automation metric, no waste-to-value initiative is disclosed. Solar adoption
(covered under H3) is an input-substitution move, not a process-innovation one.

**A4 — Product platform / modular architecture.** MODERATE. Evidence: 📄 five distinct SKU
launches inside 18 months (Shiraz Cabernet, Fratelli Brut, Sette 15th Anniversary, three
further Shotgun flavours plus Jamun announced) reusing the same winery, vineyard, and
31,000-touchpoint distribution infrastructure, against a disclosed R&D spend of NIL — i.e.
launch cadence is accelerating without a matching cost base, consistent with genuine shared-
platform reuse rather than bespoke product-by-product investment. Impact is capped at Medium:
none of these SKUs is individually large, and no case-level revenue/margin is disclosed to
show the platform is actually monetising faster than the cost of running it. **Likelihood
High × Impact Medium (raw 3), evidence 📄 → 3.0.** Time to materialise: already happening.

### FAMILY B — Supply Chain
**B1 — Backward integration and RM security.** MODERATE. 400 acres under own active farming
plus ~1,000 acres under long-term contract farming (📄, consistent across
Annual_Report_FY2026.pdf PDF p.13, Investor_Presentation_Q1FY27_Aug2026.pdf p.24, and the
Q1 FY26 / Q4 FY25 concalls), securing the grape supply for 12 imported varietals grafted
onto Indian rootstock over >15 years. This is genuine, multi-source-corroborated backward
integration into the single most important raw material. Caveat: the exact duration of the
"long term" contract-farming agreements is never stated as a number (no "5-year" or "7-year"
figure appears anywhere), so the qualification-hierarchy top band ("5+ year supply
agreements") is not fully met on its own terms. Impact is also capped: the wine segment this
backward integration feeds has posted two consecutive years of segment losses (-Rs10.49cr
FY25, -Rs10.61cr FY26, Note 37), so grape-supply security has not yet translated into segment
profitability. **Likelihood High × Impact Medium (raw 3), evidence 📄 → 3.0.**

**B2 — Qualification lock-in.** MODERATE. The Canteen Stores Department (CSD) channel is a
genuine institutional-approval process: Merlot and Noi Sparkling received CSD approval in
FY26 but sales only commence H1FY27 (a real approval-to-revenue lag), and Shotgun-Strong,
approved, does not start selling until H2FY27 (📄, Investor_Presentation_Q4FY26_May2026.pdf
p.16, Q4 FY26 call). CSD already contributes 8% of FY26 top line (📄, same deck). Management
separately claims ~45% share of the CSD wine channel (🎙️, Q4 FY26 call, unverified against
any peer disclosure). No competitor CSD-incumbency data is available to establish
exclusivity. **Likelihood Medium-High × Impact Medium (raw 3), evidence 📄 (revenue
contribution is the load-bearing fact; the 45% share claim is not) → 3.0.**

**B3 — Supply chain network effect.** NO EVIDENCE FOUND. Fratelli is a linear
producer-to-distributor business, not a platform aggregating fragmented supply or connecting
third-party buyers and sellers.

### 4B. Government policy tailwinds
The one material policy development on file, the **India-EU Free Trade Agreement**, is a
**headwind, not a tailwind**, for the domestic-wine pricing-power moat every Indian producer
currently enjoys: India's prevailing ~150% wine import duty is set to fall to ~75% after one
year and toward ~20% (premium)/~30% (mid-price) over a 7-10 year period, subject to a EUR2.50
CIF floor price (📄, Investor_Presentation_Q4FY26_May2026.pdf p.19). Management's own framing
is that EU wines will still land around Rs2,000 MRP in Maharashtra, "limiting direct overlap
with most domestic wines" (🎙️, same slide) — an assertion, not a filed number. No PLI
scheme, import-substitution measure, or company-specific procurement preference exists for
wine anywhere in the corpus. The tariff protection (and its erosion) is sector-wide, not
company-differentiating.

### 4C. Regulatory moat assessment
**Active vs emerging**: CSD access is the only company-specific, presently-active regulatory
angle, and it is a channel-access advantage, not a subsidy or scarcity right — nothing on
file shows Fratelli holds an exclusive slot competitors cannot also obtain. **Time to kick
in**: CSD sales already starting H1-H2FY27 (near-term). **Sustainability**: contingent on
continued listing, which is renewable/contestable, not a structural moat. Net R1 score: **0**
as a Fratelli-specific moat category; the EU-FTA erosion is carried forward as a risk in
Section 6, not as a positive R1 contribution.
