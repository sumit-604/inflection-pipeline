## Part 1: Claim by claim

### Q1. Gelatine pricing in FY26 H2 and FY27 H1; capsule makers buying lower (NGL ASP -5.20% FY26)
- Verdict: PARTIALLY VERIFIED
- Peer evidence:
  - NATCAPSUQ Q4 FY25, MD Sunil Mundra, p.4: "we observed a softening of gelatin price in international markets... We anticipate that domestic prices will follow soon."
  - NATCAPSUQ Q2 FY26, Mundra, p.11: gelatine prices "dropped by around 4%" in FY25; "current six months also... prices go down by quarter on to quarter by at least about 4% to 5%". The phrase is ambiguous (per quarter or over the half). Even at 4-5% over the half it matches NGL's -5.20%; per quarter would be steeper.
  - Same answer: buys imported gelatine from Brazil, Argentina and Thailand "at a reasonable price", 18-20% of annual need, duty free.
- Peers silent: Q4 FY26 call has no gelatine price statement. Nothing covers H2 FY26 or FY27 H1.
- Net read: direction and rough size match for FY25 to H1 FY26, from one buyer. The H2 FY26 and FY27 H1 window is unverified. No peer says prices stabilised or rebounded.

### Q2. Capsule demand and import versus domestic procurement (NGL gelatine volume +16.17%)
- Verdict: PARTIALLY VERIFIED
- Peer evidence:
  - Q4 FY25, p.11: capsule demand "grows at a consistent rate of 6% to 7%".
  - Q2 FY26, p.12: India about 6%, international about 4%. p.21: pharma gelatine capsules 4% to 6%.
  - Q4 FY26, p.6: 36-day order backlog. p.18: gelatine capsule lines ran above 93% for years. NATCAPSUQ gelatine capsule sales were about Rs 163 Cr in FY26 against a Rs 171 Cr guide (p.10).
  - Imports: 18-20% of NATCAPSUQ's gelatine is imported (Q2 FY26, p.11). No trend is given, so "shifting to imports" is unproven.
- Peers silent: no peer discusses domestic gelatine makers' volume or share.
- Net read: demand is steady at mid-single digits. NGL's +16.17% is about 2.7x the stated market growth, so share gain or capacity release explains it, not market lift. One buyer imports a fifth of its gelatine. That is a competing supply for NGL.

### Q3. Crushed-bone and imported raw-material prices since H2 FY26; users importing gelatine
- Verdict: UNVERIFIABLE for the bone and raw-material price clause. PARTIALLY VERIFIED for "users also importing gelatine" (NATCAPSUQ Q2 FY26, p.11, 18-20% imported).
- Peers silent: NATCAPSUQ never discusses bone, ossein or crushed-bone cost.
- Net read: the structural-versus-cyclical test (B05 question 3) cannot be run on peer evidence. Fact 1 stays open on the cost side.

### Q4. HPMC or plant-based substitution as a volume risk, and share
- Verdict: PARTIALLY VERIFIED
- Peer evidence:
  - Q2 FY26, p.10: HPMC is 5% of volume and 10% of revenue. This conflicts with Q4 FY26 figures (about Rs 8 Cr HPMC against about Rs 171 Cr capsule, p.7, p.10), which give about 5%.
  - Q2 FY26, p.20-21: HPMC sells at 2.8 to 3 times the price of gelatine capsules; it goes to nutra, Ayurveda and herbal, "gelatin capsules are going in for pharma". Gelatine grows 4-6% in pharma.
  - Q4 FY26, p.21: HPMC weakness came from the US tariff and nutra slowdown, not from demand shifting.
  - NATCAPSUQ does not cite substitution as a risk to its gelatine volume. It treats HPMC as a margin lever (HPMC EBITDA about 18% against about 12-13% gelatine, Q4 FY26, p.11).
- Peers silent: no other peer.
- Net read: substitution is a real, small, nutra-led segment and pharma stays gelatine. A peer that makes both does not rank it as a primary barrier. The AR's "primary barrier" framing is not supported in size. A market-share figure is NOT FOUND.

### Q5. Gelatine or DCP capacity being added in India
- Verdict: UNVERIFIABLE
- Peer evidence: none on gelatine or DCP capacity. Only adjacent items: gelatine makers consolidating, "one of the larger companies has acquired two other companies" (Q2 FY26, p.11, unnamed); "new entrants in the lower end" are capsule makers (p.11). DCP appears in no call.
- Net read: whether NGL's +600 MT gelatine and +1,500 MT DCP land in a tight or loose market cannot be read from peers. Consolidation in gelatine is a supply-side fact worth chasing live.

## Part 5: Cross-peer hypothesis
Hypothesis (inferred from NATCAPSUQ, NGL via B05, and Nitta screening, stated by none): producer margin gain is a spread, not a pass-through. The buyer saw gelatine prices fall 4-5% while NGL's materials ratio fell 511 bp and Nitta's OPM rose from 18-20% to 24-28%, so bone-side cost fell faster than gelatine price. Buyers got a partial share. NATCAPSUQ's capsule margin did not widen, but confounders exist. Caveat: Nitta's business mix is NOT FOUND in the tables, and its Sep-26 figure post-dates the NGL data. Test: NGL materials ratio holds near 50.6% (Q1 FY27) with gelatine ASP flat or down in Q2-Q3 FY27, and Nitta OPM stays above 24%. Kill: NGL materials ratio rebounds above 53% while gelatine ASP is unchanged.
