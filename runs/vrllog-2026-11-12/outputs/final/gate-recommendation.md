## 4. Caveats carried

Source fidelity (numerical verifier, MAJOR, gate held):
1. The freight rate sensitivity in the business model report (a 1% rate change is about Rs 3.2 Cr of quarterly EBITDA) does not follow from its inputs. 961.9 Cr times 1% is Rs 9.62 Cr (Q2 FY27 results p.4, 96,192 lakh). These files do not use the 3.2 figure.
2. The "freight cost to revenue 6.05% from 6.27%" line in the Gate 0 report is the Hamali (loading and unloading) row, on presentation PDF p.9. Freight, handling and servicing cost was 567.8 of 961.9 = 59.0% in Q2 FY27, against 480.4 of 797.0 = 60.3% in Q2 FY26. The stated reason, "Increase in Volumes & Freight Rates leads to increase in margins", is on PDF p.9, not p.7.
3. The Gate 0 balance sheet and H1 cash flow anchors are one slide off. The balance sheet is on presentation PDF p.13 (current investments 280.8 Cr). The cash flow is on PDF p.14: H1 FY27 CFO 362.5 Cr, CFI minus 440.5 Cr, CFF plus 74.5 Cr, tax paid 54.0 Cr against 6.8 Cr. The values match the source. Net debt of 337 Cr and 440 Cr, and capex of 158 Cr, on PDF p.16, are correct.

Analysis caveats (red flag and framework verifiers, MAJOR):
4. Erosion of the own fleet claim was only partly caught. In May management said "scrappage will not be there", and in August said vehicles were "scrapped... in this quarter". The February claim that further demand runs on own vehicles sits against rising lorry hire and an admitted capacity shortage. The capacity and fleet triggers rest on this claim.
5. The Gate 0 classification depends on two readings the operator should rule: the capex proxy and the promoter window (section 3 above).

Stage flags the deliberation must weigh:
6. Margin quality. The FY26 step up came from realisation and smaller credit notes (2,246 of the 6,081 lakh of revenue growth, Rs 22.46 Cr of Rs 60.81 Cr). It did not come from fuel or operating leverage, yet the MD&A and Note 36(A) credit cost control (FY26 AR Note 20(c) p.210, Note 36(A) p.226).
7. Margin definition. The 23.3% includes other income over total income. The operating basis is 22.97% for Q2 and 22.14% for H1. Management gave 21% to 22%, 23% plus and 21% plus in one call.
8. Leases. Printed ROCE (24.56%), D/E (0.40) and DSCR exclude leases. ROCE including leases is 17.35%. The FY25 comparatives were re-based by a change of definition (FY26 AR Note 36, p.225-226).
9. Controls. There is no database audit trail. The March 2026 quarterly stock return to banks was not filed. The Directors' Report says there are no qualifications, while the auditor's books paragraph reads "except for" (FY26 AR p.40, p.162-163, Note 40).
10. Capex reconciliation. The August band was Rs 220 Cr to Rs 240 Cr. H1 capex of Rs 158 Cr plus the H2 guide of Rs 150 Cr to Rs 160 Cr gives Rs 308 Cr to Rs 318 Cr (derived), and the November answer does not reconcile the two.
11. Margin basis against peers. VRL owns its fleet, while TCI Express and TCI hire. The EBITDA gap of 23.3% against 12.7% overstates operating superiority by an amount nobody has measured.
12. The Emerging Moat score of 12.8 sits 0.8 above the floor of 12. One raw point off E1 or G1 flips it to NO MEANINGFUL EMERGING MOAT.
13. Wage cushion. Peers report no contractual pass through for minimum wages. VRL's November call reopened the timing of the wage increment.

## 6. Contradicted claims (priority monitoring)

Contradicted at the peer stage:
1. "The 9% realisation rise is mostly fuel pass-through." Delhivery Q1 FY27, Sahil Barua: "a 37 paisa improvement in yield, of which really only about 6 paisa is coming from the fuel inflation." Delhivery Q2 FY27: "less than 30% of the increase in yield... in PTL has come as a consequence of underlying cost inflation linked price revision." Both transcripts are one word per line, so these quotes carry no page anchor, and Delhivery's figure is PTL, an adjacent segment. TCI Express Q2 FY27 p.8 gives a net price rise of 1.5%. TCI Q1 FY27 p.10 gives a fuel effect of 1% to 3% on rates. The red flag verifier tempers the size but not the direction. VRL's cost per litre rose Rs 11 to 12, so on VRL's own Rs 2 = 1% rule fuel explains about 5.5% to 6% of the 9%. The non-fuel part is then about a third, not half. TCI's 1% to 3% and the mid-May timing of peer hikes are the stronger evidence.

Contradicted by the company's own record (carried for the same monitoring):

3. "FY27 capex Rs 220-240 Cr, year end debt flat" (Q1 FY27 call). H1 was Rs 158 Cr and the H2 guide is Rs 150 Cr to Rs 160 Cr (Q2 FY27 call).
4. "Rates carry a premium to unorganised operators" (Q4 FY26 call) against "we are not charging any premium or something much different from the other competitor" (Q2 FY27 call).

## 7. Monitorables and triggers

1. Tonnage. Each quarter, read tonnage on the investor presentation volume chart. Q3 FY27 should grow 9% or more on Q3 FY26's 1,007 thousand tonnes. A quarter above 1,104 thousand tonnes should come within three quarters. Growth under 6% for two quarters running breaks the volume half of the thesis.
2. Realisation per tonne. On the same chart, Q2 FY27 was Rs 8,840. Check it against the PPAC diesel price each month. A fall of more than 3% quarter on quarter without a matching diesel cut shows the price gain was not durable. That would bear out the peers' fuel attribution.
3. Operating margin. Compute it from the results face: revenue from operations, less freight, handling and servicing, employee cost and other expenses, all over revenue. Do not use the presentation's 23.3%, which includes other income. H1 FY27 was 22.14%. The thesis needs 21% or more, and two quarters below 20% break it.
4. Employee cost as a share of revenue. Q2 FY27 was 16.5%. If it stays at or under 16.5% with tonnage growing 9% to 10%, operating leverage is real and margin can hold at 22% to 23%. Above 17.5%, the margin falls back to the guide or below. Watch the wage increment decision due by March 2027.
5. Buyback settlement and net debt. In the Q3 FY27 balance sheet, check share capital for the buyback cut. Check net debt excluding leases against about Rs 617 Cr (derived) and Rs 440 Cr (March). Net debt including leases over LTM EBITDA was 1.69 times at Mar-26. Above 2.5 times means the buyback and capex were not covered by internal cash, as management claimed.
6. Capex and fleet. In the Q4 FY27 results and the FY27 annual report, set total capex against the Rs 220 Cr to Rs 240 Cr band and the Rs 308 Cr to Rs 318 Cr implied path. Vehicles should be at least half of it. Fleet count (5,941 at Sep-26) and capacity (81,985 tonnes) should rise with tonnage, and lorry hire as a share of income should fall. If not, the own fleet engine is thinning.
7. Related parties and promoter. In the FY27 annual report, check the related party note. Flows above 0.46% of revenue, unnamed counterparties, or a 2022 bus sale valuation below fair value move the promoter read to CAUTION. Also check each quarterly shareholding pattern for any promoter sale.
8. Controls and tax. In the FY27 annual report, check the CARO report and Note 40 for a database audit trail and filed quarterly stock returns. Check Note 29(k) for the section 154 rectification order on the CPC demand of 5,186 lakh (Rs 51.86 Cr), now kept outside the contingent table.

## 8. Falsification line

The Q3 FY27 print (quarter to 31 Dec 2026) is the test. A margin below 20%, measured as operating EBITDA over revenue from operations on the results face, with realisation per tonne held at or above Rs 8,840, would do the most damage. Price would have held and the cost base would still have failed, which breaks both readings of the margin step up.
