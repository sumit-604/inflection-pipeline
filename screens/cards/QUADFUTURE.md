## Business Understanding Narrative

Quadrant runs one business that earns money and one that does not. The first makes
electron-beam irradiated cables. A 2.5 MeV accelerator cross-links the polymer insulation
without chemicals, which makes the cable lighter, tougher and fire-safe (IP p.15). Those
cables go into trains, naval ships and submarines, and now into solar and EV
applications. Indian Railways' RDSO and defence's DGQA have approved the products, and
72% of cable revenue comes from the public sector (IP p.14). The second business is
KAVACH, India's indigenous automatic train protection system. Quadrant designed the
hardware and software in-house and is one of only five vendors to have developed a
KAVACH system to Indian Railways' requirement (Concall p.4; IP p.9).

What is changing is supposed to be KAVACH turning on. In December 2024 the company won a
Rs 829 crore order to fit 1,200 locomotives, plus Rs 155 crore of trackside work through
RailTel (Concall p.4). The KAVACH order book stands at about Rs 8,054 Mn against a cable
order book of Rs 407 Mn (IP p.6, p.14). Nothing has shipped. KAVACH revenue was nil in
Q1FY26 and the deck describes the system as still "undergoing live field validation" in
August 2026 (Concall p.5; IP p.4).

Why now: the company says certification is at its final stage. RDSO has approved
passenger trials, "the final regulatory milestone before full-scale commercial
deployment" (IP p.4, p.6).

What must be true: certification must arrive, and the Rs 805 crore of orders must convert
before the contracts lapse or are re-tendered.

What breaks it: the schedule already broke once. The December 2024 purchase order carried
a completion condition of 11 December 2025 (Concall p.6). In July 2025 management was
"very hopeful of completing the order and executing it within the scheduled timeline". It
is now September 2026, certification has still not been granted, and the company has
reported five consecutive quarters of negative EBITDA (IP p.7).

## 2. Business model and archetype
Two archetypes, and only one is operating.
Specialty Cables is a build-to-spec component maker. Revenue is tonnes of cable against
approvals (RDSO, DGQA, BIS, AERB) and tenders. Backward integration runs from copper
processing through polymer compounding to irradiation and testing, which management says
gives "margin resilience against commodity price swings" (IP p.14). Orders are
short-term, won through competitive bidding, and lumpy: "Our orders are short-term orders
and awarded through tendering process and competitive bidding process" (Concall p.5).
Capacity is 1,900 MT a year.
Train Control and Signalling is a licence and scarcity business with no revenue. Five
approved vendors, SIL-4 safety certification, an interim Independent Safety Assessment,
an MoU with RailTel naming Quadrant as OEM (IP p.9). Capacity is built for about 2,200
locomotives and 4,500 station units a year (IP p.11).
The one economic engine today is the cable business paying part of the KAVACH burn. In
Q1FY27 Specialty Cables earned about Rs 41 Mn of EBITDA while the group lost Rs 29 Mn, so
signalling burned roughly Rs 70 Mn in the quarter (IP p.7).

## 3. Competitive advantages
Genuine, regulatory, and untested commercially.
KAVACH is the real barrier. Only five vendors have developed a system to Indian Railways'
specification, safety certification runs to SIL-4, and the approval chain is RDSO plus an
independent safety assessor (Concall p.4; IP p.9). Quadrant built the hardware and
software in-house and reached "equivalent level compared to our peers, who have been
developing this system for more than 10 years" (Concall p.4). If it certifies, a
five-vendor market against a national rollout is a strong position.
The cable business has narrower advantages: an AERB-licensed 2.5 MeV accelerator, RDSO
and DGQA approvals, naval qualification since 2018, and backward integration. Those are
real but they have not produced growth. Cable revenue was Rs 1,510 Mn in FY23 and
Rs 1,530 Mn in FY26 (IP p.14). Four flat years is the answer to whether the moat converts
to pricing power. Moat: high in signalling, present but non-earning in cables.

## 5. Financial trajectory
Revenue (Rs Mn): Q1FY26 287, Q2FY26 344, Q3FY26 333, Q4FY26 566, Q1FY27 406.
EBITDA (Rs Mn): Q1FY26 -75, Q2FY26 -104, Q3FY26 -101, Q4FY26 -59, Q1FY27 -29 (IP p.7).
Five consecutive quarters of negative EBITDA. The loss is narrowing, from Rs 104 Mn to
Rs 29 Mn across four quarters, which is the one clearly favourable trend in the numbers.
Q1FY27 revenue rose 41.3% year on year, all of it cables, and Specialty Cables EBITDA was
about Rs 41 Mn, up 59% (IP p.7). So the cable division earns roughly a 10% EBITDA margin
and the signalling division consumed about Rs 70 Mn in the quarter.
Cable revenue by year (Rs Mn): FY22 1,042, FY23 1,510, FY24 1,506, FY25 1,462, FY26 1,530
(IP p.14). Flat since FY23.
The segment loss in signalling grew from Rs 4.24 crore in Q1FY25 to Rs 10.09 crore in
Q1FY26, "primarily due to increased cost for building up team for Kavach deliveries,
maintenance costs for software, technical expenses, including testing and inspection
charges" (Concall p.5).
As at July 2025 the company held about Rs 52 crore of KAVACH material, Rs 16 crore in
purchases and Rs 36 crore in work in progress, plus advances already released to
off-the-shelf suppliers (Concall p.8, p.9).
**A profit after tax figure now exists, and reading it carefully is the point.** Q4FY26 PAT
was **positive Rs 11 million** (Q4FY26 deck p.30). The same page records a **Rs 125 million
tax expense credit** in the quarter, with the stated reason "Tax credit offset operational
losses". DERIVED: a PAT of plus Rs 11 million after a Rs 125 million credit implies a
**pre-tax loss of about Rs 114 million**. The company's first positive profit line is
entirely an accounting entry. Any screen showing Quadrant's PAT turning positive in Q4FY26
is showing a deferred tax credit, not trading profit.
Two anchored figures the first pass lacked: **the TCAS order book is Rs 8,054 million**, or
Rs 805 crore, about 4.6 times the whole company's annual revenue (Q4FY26 deck p.4); and
Specialty Cables installed capacity is **about 1,900 MT a year**, backward integrated at
Basma (p.28). IPO proceeds repaid Rs 236 million of working capital term loan in FY26
(p.34), and Rs 244 million is earmarked for Electronic Interlocking development, to begin
only after KAVACH approval (p.24, p.28).
Full-year FY26 PAT, the balance sheet, total debt and cash flow are still NOT FOUND.
Inflection classification: **none has occurred.** There is no inflection to classify. The
cable business is flat, the signalling business has no revenue, and the group loses money
at the EBITDA line. What the corpus shows is a narrowing loss, not an inflection.

## 6. The transition and the quality ladder
Management's claim, quoted once: "With full execution readiness already established, the
company is well positioned for swift deployment of its Kavach order book post approval"
(IP p.6).
FROM R2 to R3, on the cable business as it stands: a spec'd supplier with approvals and
backward integration, whose flat four-year revenue and roughly 10% divisional EBITDA
margin place it at the lower end of that band. Group ROCE cannot be computed and would be
negative on current EBITDA.
TO R4 or R5, on the KAVACH claim: a licensed participant in a five-vendor market serving
a national safety mandate, with SIL-4 certification as the barrier. That is a
scarcity-licence business.
This is a claimed multi-rung leap, and the ladder's own rule applies: "A claimed
multi-rung leap requires extraordinary proof and is itself a red flag." The proof
required is binary and external. It is not a margin trend or a mix shift that management
can influence quarter by quarter. It is a certificate issued by RDSO and an independent
safety assessor, and it has now been pending for longer than management told the market
it would take.

## 8. Proof check
The last quarters prove the cable recovery and refute the KAVACH timeline.
What fired: Q1FY27 cable revenue up 41.3% year on year with divisional EBITDA up 59%, and
the group EBITDA loss narrowing from Rs 104 Mn to Rs 29 Mn over four quarters (IP p.7).
The cable division CEO appointment appears to be working. RDSO has approved passenger
trials, which is a real step and the last one before certification.
What has not fired, and this is the whole thesis: KAVACH. The order was awarded in
December 2024 with a completion condition of December 2025. In July 2025 management said
a locomotive would be issued "in one day or two days' time", that field trials were "not
a long or a defined process", that the peer gap "will keep on reducing", and that three
months would be enough to deliver all 1,200 units (Concall p.6, p.8, p.10, p.12).
Fourteen months later the system is still in live field validation and no KAVACH revenue
has been recognised in any quarter shown.
Named proof point: the ISA and RDSO certificate itself, and the first invoiced KAVACH
delivery. Nothing before that counts. A second named proof point: an explanation of why
the KAVACH order book is about Rs 179 crore smaller than it was in July 2025 with nothing
shipped.

## 11. Posture: Transition Decision Matrix
- Proof gate: NOT FIRED, and it has failed to fire past its own deadline. The gate is
  external, binary and already 14 months late.
- Ugliness: the negative EBITDA is presented as ARTIFACT-OF-CLIMB, the cost of building a
  KAVACH team and inventory ahead of certification (Concall p.5). On the documents that
  reading is defensible for the signalling division. It is not defensible for the group,
  because the cable division that is meant to fund the climb has not grown in four years.
  Classify the group ugliness as CONTESTED, tilting structural, until either
  certification lands or cable revenue breaks out of its Rs 1,500 Mn band.
- Recognition gap: INDETERMINATE from the corpus, and the honest prior is CLOSED. KAVACH
  is among the most heavily narrated themes in Indian small caps, and this company's
  entire equity value rests on an order book that has produced no revenue.
Proof NOT FIRED plus a contested-to-structural ugliness reading plus a probably-closed gap
sits between RESEARCH / WATCH and **PRICED NARRATIVE (TRAP)**, the cell the framework
names as the most seductive. The narrative is excellent: a five-vendor national safety
mandate, in-house technology, capacity built and waiting. The proof gate has not fired.
And if the market has already paid for the rollout, the downside is double, because the
certification could slip again and the multiple would compress on an EBITDA-negative
business.

## 12. Verdict card
**WATCH. Do not run `/step1` yet.** Quadrant owns something rare: an indigenous KAVACH
system, one of five approved, with SIL-4 compliance, an interim ISA approval, an OEM MoU
with RailTel, capacity for 2,200 locomotives a year and 15 installation teams already
built. If certification lands, the Rs 805 crore order book converts against a cost base
that is already paid for.
It is not yet worth a heavy run for three reasons. The company has never earned an
operating profit, with five straight negative EBITDA quarters. The contract it is waiting
on carried a completion condition of December 2025 and that date passed nine months ago
with nothing delivered. And management's own timeline guidance in July 2025 was
confident, specific and wrong, which is the evidence that matters most when the entire
thesis is a schedule.
Load-bearing facts to verify first, before this becomes a `/step1`:
1. The ISA and RDSO certification status today, from a primary filing, not a deck.
2. The status of the December 2024 purchase order: extension, amendment, liquidated
   damages, or re-tender. The completion date has passed.
3. Why the KAVACH order book fell from Rs 984 crore to about Rs 805 crore with no revenue
   recognised.
4. The balance sheet: cash, debt, the Rs 52 crore inventory position now, and IPO
   proceeds utilisation. A loss-making company holding inventory for an uncertified
   product has a funding clock.
5. KAVACH contract margin. It is not disclosed anywhere in the corpus, and it decides
   whether Rs 805 crore of revenue is worth having.
6. Promoter holding and pledge.
7. Why cable revenue has not grown since FY23 despite the approvals.
What would change the view: the certificate, plus one quarter of invoiced KAVACH revenue
at a disclosed margin, would move this straight to PROCEED and make it the highest-torque
name of the nine. Another certification slip, or evidence that the December 2024 order is
being re-tendered, moves it to PASS. No price here, and note the specific warning: this is
the profile the framework flags as a priced narrative, so establish the price before
spending anything further on it.
