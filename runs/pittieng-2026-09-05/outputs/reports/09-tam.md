## SECTION 1: MARKET DEFINITION

### 1A Precise boundaries

**Product scope (included):** loose electrical steel laminations (CRNGO, motor grade); stator/rotor cores and welded stators; die-cast rotors; integrated stator/rotor sub-assemblies (with shafts, frames); iron and steel castings (grey iron, SG/ductile iron, alloy steel, 50 kg-3 tonnes) for motor/generator housings, frames, end shields; precision-machined components (shafts, gear cases, stator frames, wheel hubs, pedestal shafts); press tools (in-house use). (AR FY26, PDF p.9-11)

**Product scope (excluded):** finished motors/generators/gensets/locomotives themselves (Pitti sells components INTO these, not the end-equipment); transformer-grade CRGO electrical steel and transformer cores (a different steel grade, different customer set, not named anywhere in Pitti's product portfolio); automotive engine-block/ICE castings (Pitti's castings serve rotating-electrical-equipment housings, not auto engine components); the c.EUR 2mn green-hydrogen electrolyzer plate business (immaterial, flat, company itself skeptical of growth — Concall Aug 2026 Transcript, p.12).

**Geographic scope:** India (six manufacturing locations, Telangana/Maharashtra/Karnataka) plus direct exports to 11+ countries across 6 continents, concentrated in North America (US, Canada, Mexico) and Europe (Germany, Spain, Switzerland) (AR FY26, PDF p.14-15). Exports were 27% of FY26 revenue, Rs 531.31 Cr (AR FY26, PDF p.14), confirming the step-1 brief lead.

**Customer scope:** OEMs and Indian Railways production units (CLW/ICF/BLW) requiring an OEM-qualified, audited supplier; not retail, not aftermarket distribution. Named customers: Caterpillar, Voith, Progress Rail, Siemens Mobility, Medha Servo, Wabtec, GE, Cummins, Marathon, Nidec, CG Power, TMEIC, ELIN (AR FY26, PDF p.14, p.19; Inv. Pres. Q1FY27, slide 23).

**Channel scope:** direct B2B supply against long-cycle OEM qualification (AR FY26, PDF p.15, "Qualify > Deliver > Expand > Integrate"), not distributor/retail.

**Price segment:** precision-engineered, OEM-audited, mission-critical-application grade; not the low-spec/commodity end of lamination stamping or generic iron casting (loose laminations sit at the low end of Pitti's OWN portfolio and are itself described by the company as "the portfolio's foundation product," i.e. even Pitti's own basic product tier requires OEM qualification to sell into a rotating-machine supply chain — AR FY26, PDF p.9, p.11).

**Market definition (one line):** the India + export B2B market for electrical steel laminations, motor/generator stator-rotor cores and assemblies, iron and steel castings, and precision-machined components, sold to OEM-qualified customers building rotating electrical equipment for railways, power generation, industrial/special-purpose motors, mining/oil and gas, renewables and data centres.

## SECTION 2: TAM ESTIMATION, MULTIPLE METHODS

Given the mixed-archetype scope (Section 1A), Pitti's addressable market is best sized as a component-level market in three product lines — laminations/assemblies, castings, and machined components — since the end-equipment markets management cites (motors, generators, locomotives) wildly overstate Pitti's own addressable slice (see Section 1B). No single third-party report sizes "the Indian market for laminations + castings + machined components for rotating electrical equipment" as one number; the corpus and web search were used to size each product line separately and sum them.

### Method 1 — Top-down, product-line component markets

**1a. Laminations/assemblies — India CRNO (motor-grade) steel lamination market**
- Conservative: India CRNO steel lamination market projected to "surpass ~US$ 1 Bn by the end of 2027," CAGR ~14% from a 2019 base (Transparency Market Research, cited via web search, 2026). Base year 2019 is now >4 years old — per the staleness rule this CAGR informs direction only, not the headline number; the endpoint absolute figure ($1Bn/2027) is kept as the CONSERVATIVE anchor since it is the lower of two available estimates.
  - $1 Bn × Rs 95/USD = **Rs 9,500 Cr** (2027 figure, used as the conservative, current-cycle anchor)
- Alternative/higher: a second vendor (2026 vintage) sizes "India CRNO Steel Lamination Market... at $1.8 Billion in 2026... to $3.81 Billion by 2035, 8.70% CAGR." This diverges from the first estimate by ~1.8x for a similar reference year — **flagged, not averaged**. $1.8Bn × Rs95 = Rs 17,100 Cr (2026); $3.81Bn × Rs95 = Rs 36,195 Cr (2035).
  - The 8.70% CAGR is the more current-vintage (2026-2035) growth rate and is used below for forward projection (Section 3B), in preference to the older 14% figure, per the staleness rule.

**1b. Castings — India metal casting market, Pitti-relevant slice**
- Full India metal casting market: USD 13.923 Bn (2025) to USD 22.141 Bn (2034), CAGR 5.29% (AR FY26, PDF p.22, source cited: IMARC Group, India Metal Casting Market Report, 2025). Full market = Rs 1,32,269 Cr (2025) at Rs95/USD — but this spans ALL India casting demand, dominated by automotive (~32-35% of demand, per web search, Research & Markets/Mordor Intelligence-type industry trackers) and pipes/general engineering, most of which Pitti does not compete for.
- Web search on the India foundry end-use split (multiple aggregator sources, 2025-26 vintage): automotive ~32-35%, heavy equipment/industrial ~10-15%, oil & gas ~5-10%, railways+defence "less than 10%." No single sourced split gives a clean "railways + power generation + mining/oil&gas + renewables" aggregate at Pitti's OEM-qualified/precision grade specifically (as opposed to generic commodity-grade castings in those same end-uses, most of which is served by small unorganised foundries — see Section 4C).
- **ASSUMPTION (not a sourced figure, flagged)**: this report applies a 10% "precision/OEM-qualified castings for rotating electrical equipment across railways, power generation, mining/oil&gas and renewables" slice to the full India metal casting market, as a conservative interpolation between the sourced "railways+defence <10%" datapoint and the broader heavy-equipment/oil&gas buckets that partially overlap Pitti's scope. This is the single largest source of uncertainty in this TAM build.
  - 10% × USD 13.923 Bn = USD 1.3923 Bn = **Rs 13,227 Cr**

**1c. Machined components — India machine components market**
- IMARC Group's "India Machine Components Market" size: USD 237.7 Mn (2025) to USD 338.9 Mn (2034), CAGR 3.86% (web search; also referenced qualitatively at AR FY26, PDF p.22, "Source: IMARC Group, India Machine Components market, 2026," though the AR text does not print the number itself).
  - USD 237.7 Mn × Rs95 = **Rs 2,258 Cr** (conservative; this appears narrowly scoped and is likely an under-count of Pitti's actual machined-components addressable market — see divergence note below)
- Alternative/broader: IMARC's "India Precision Engineering Market," USD 500 Mn (2024) to USD 930 Mn (2033), CAGR 7.2% — Rs 4,750 Cr (2024). This broader definition (precision engineering components generally, vs a narrow "machine components" product code) likely fits Pitti's shafts/gear-cases/stator-frames business better, but per the conservative-bias rule the LOWER figure (Rs 2,258 Cr) is carried into the conservative TAM sum; the higher figure is flagged for Section 2 triangulation.

**Method 1 TAM (conservative, component-level, India-domestic) = Rs 9,500 + Rs 13,227 + Rs 2,258 = Rs 24,985 Cr ≈ Rs 25,000 Cr**

### Method 2 — Bottom-up, tonnage

Unit: one tonne of finished lamination/assembly/casting/machined output (per B04 unit-economics field). Pitti's FY26 blended realisation: **Rs 23.5 lakh/tonne** (Rs 1,912.8 Cr revenue ÷ ~81,529 tonnes combined volume — B04-bizmodel.yaml, calculated not company-disclosed). Loose-lamination-only realisation is materially lower than the blended figure (value-added tiers earn more per tonne — AR FY26, PDF p.11); assume a loose-lamination realisation band of roughly Rs 15-18 lakh/tonne (ASSUMPTION, not sourced, used only for a directional cross-check).

- India CRNO lamination market, conservative anchor Rs 9,500 Cr (2027) ÷ Rs 16 lakh/tonne (midpoint assumption) ≈ **594,000 tonnes** total India lamination-market volume by 2027.
- Pitti's FY26 domestic-weighted lamination+assembly volume ≈ 73% (domestic share) × 69,517 t (AR FY26, PDF p.4) ≈ 50,750 t.
- Implied Pitti share of the bottom-up tonnage estimate: 50,750 / 594,000 ≈ **8.5%**.

This 8.5% tonnage-based share estimate is in the same order of magnitude as the ~12-15% revenue-based domestic lamination share implied by Method 1 (see Section 3A/5A), giving reasonable — not perfect — cross-method corroboration. The gap (8.5% vs ~12-15%) is consistent with Pitti's realisation being ABOVE the market-average assumption used here (Pitti is mix-shifting toward higher-value tiers, AR FY26 PDF p.4), so a tonnage-share estimate using a flat "loose lamination" realisation will understate Pitti's true revenue share. Confidence: **L** (built on two chained assumptions).

### Method 3 — Peer revenue aggregation

Named organized competitors in electrical steel lamination stamping for motors (web search, not corpus — B04/B07 flagged that no competitor names appear in the AR or investor presentation):
- Pitti Engineering: Rs 1,912.8 Cr (FY26, consolidated, AR FY26 PDF p.96)
- Vilas Transcore Ltd: Rs 461 Cr (screener.in, latest annual figure at time of search, undated precisely — **flagged, freshness unconfirmed**; also a stage-6 peer)
- Jay Bee Laminations Ltd: "crossed annual revenues of Rs 200 Cr as of 2023" (web search) — **STALE, >2 years old, direction only**

Visible organized-player revenue: Rs 1,912.8 + 461 + 200 ≈ **Rs 2,574 Cr**

Unorganised-sector addition: India's foundry/casting industry is ~85-95% small-scale by unit count (web search, multiple industry trackers), but this figure describes GENERIC casting units, most of which cannot pass OEM qualification for the precision, rail/mining/power-gen grade castings Pitti supplies (a qualification-lock-in moat explicitly identified at B07, category B2). Applying the STANDARD 30-60% unorganised-sector uplift used for generic Indian industries would overstate the overlap; this report applies the LOW end (30%) given the qualification barrier, flagged as a judgment call:
- Rs 2,574 Cr × 1.30 ≈ **Rs 3,346 Cr**

This Rs 3,346 Cr figure is **current visible SUPPLY**, not TAM (TAM is defined as 100% of DEMAND, not current organized supply). It sits well below the Method 1 demand-side estimate (Rs 25,000 Cr), which is the expected pattern in a market with (a) meaningful commodity-grade/unorganised supply Pitti does not compete with directly, (b) demand served by OEM captive in-house production, and (c) import-sourced supply (see AR's own 1,50,000-tonne domestic CRNGO-steel supply deficit, PDF p.4 — though that figure is about RAW STEEL input scarcity upstream of Pitti, not a finished-goods import statistic, and is not treated as TAM evidence here). Confidence: **M** for the organized-peer figure, **L** for the unorganised uplift.

### Method 4 — Import substitution

Not computable with the available evidence. The AR discloses a domestic CRNGO (motor-grade) electrical steel supply deficit of ~1,50,000 tonnes (AR FY26, PDF p.4) and states Pitti "secured strategic sourcing arrangements with BIS-approved steel producers in Korea and Japan" — but this is a RAW MATERIAL (input) shortage, upstream of Pitti's own finished-goods market, not an import-substitution opportunity in Pitti's OUTPUT (laminations/castings/machined components). No corpus or web-search evidence was found on FINISHED-GOODS import volumes of stator/rotor cores, motor castings or machined components into India or into Pitti's export markets. **NOT FOUND — Method 4 not applied.**

(Separately, web search on "CRGO" import/production statistics — India produces ~40-50,000 t/yr domestically against ~400,000 t/yr import requirement — describes a DIFFERENT steel grade (CRGO, grain-oriented, used for TRANSFORMERS) from Pitti's own input (CRNGO, non-grain-oriented, used for motors/generators). This CRGO data is NOT used as Pitti-specific evidence anywhere in this report; it is noted here only to flag the distinction and avoid a wrong anchor downstream.)

### Method 5 — Global benchmark

- Global electric motor & generator market: USD 137.39 Bn (CY2026) to USD 160.07 Bn (CY2030) (AR FY26, PDF p.21, source: Research and Markets, Jan 2026).
- India electric motor market: USD 4,134.03 Mn (FY2025) to USD 9,549.05 Mn (FY2030), stated CAGR "9.4%" (AR FY26, PDF p.21, source: IMARC Group, 2025). **Note: the printed start/end figures imply an ~18.3% CAGR over 5 years (2.31^(1/5)-1), not the 9.4% printed alongside them — an unreconciled internal arithmetic inconsistency in the AR's own citation, flagged here rather than silently corrected.**
- India's implied share of the global electric motor+generator market: USD 4.134 Bn ÷ USD 137.39 Bn ≈ **3.0%** (2025/26), versus India's ~3.5-4% share of global nominal GDP and ~17.8% share of global population. This places India's electric-motor manufacturing base modestly UNDER its GDP weight and far under its population weight — consistent with a genuine catch-up/localisation growth driver (Section 4A), though the size of the catch-up gap should not be over-read given the AR's own internal CAGR inconsistency just noted.

### 3B SOM at 3 and 5 years

Current SAM share: Rs 1,912.8 Cr (FY26 revenue) ÷ Rs 12,000 Cr (SAM) = **15.9% ≈ 16%**.

Share-gain case selected: **AGGRESSIVE (3-5pp over the period)**, justified by:
- Committed, funded capacity expansion (sheet metal +20%, castings nearly doubling to 36,000 t, machining +50% to 10.8 lakh hours — AR FY26 PDF p.14) — satisfies the "capacity and execution" precondition for the aggressive band.
- Explicit, sourced OEM-consolidation tailwind: "International OEMs are increasingly consolidating sourcing with integrated manufacturing partners capable of delivering scale, quality, and execution certainty" (AR FY26, PDF p.5) — a formalisation dynamic against a fragmented, heavily-unorganised (85-95% small-scale, web search) underlying supplier base, meeting the "faster possible where unorganised share >40% is formalising" condition.
- NOT the >5pp band: no competitor exit or acquisition evidence exists in the corpus.

Applied trajectory: **+3pp by year 3 (to 19%), +5pp cumulative by year 5 (to 21%)** — the low end of the aggressive band, chosen for conservative bias given the un-resolved capex-timing conflict (Q1FY29 vs Q1FY30, carried from B04/B07) that could delay the capacity this share gain depends on.

SAM projected forward at the blended TAM growth rate (6.45%, see Section 5B for derivation):
- SAM(yr3) = 12,000 × (1.0645)^3 ≈ 12,000 × 1.2062 ≈ **Rs 14,474 Cr** (using precise 6.45%; Rs 14,495 Cr if rounded to 6.5% — both shown for transparency, 6.5% used going forward for simplicity)
- SAM(yr5) = 12,000 × (1.065)^5 ≈ **Rs 16,441 Cr**

SOM(3yr) = SAM(yr3) × 19% = 14,495 × 0.19 ≈ **Rs 2,754 Cr**
SOM(5yr) = SAM(yr5) × 21% = 16,441 × 0.21 ≈ **Rs 3,453 Cr**

**Implied revenue CAGR from FY26 base (Rs 1,912.8 Cr):**
- Yr3: (2,754 / 1,912.8)^(1/3) - 1 = (1.4399)^(1/3) - 1 = **12.9%**
- Yr5: (3,453 / 1,912.8)^(1/5) - 1 = (1.8053)^(1/5) - 1 = **12.5%**

**Cross-check against management's own FY28 revenue guidance** (Company Memory load-bearing fact #1: "FY28 turnover above Rs 2,500 Cr at 90,000 t"; Concall Aug 2026, p.15-16):
- Implied management FY26→FY28 (2-year) CAGR: (2,500 / 1,912.8)^(1/2) - 1 = (1.30697)^0.5 - 1 = **14.33%**
- Ratio vs this stage's SOM-implied yr3 CAGR (12.9%): 14.33 / 12.93 = **1.11x**
- Read: **reasonable** (well within the 1.5x band). Management's own near-term guidance runs marginally FASTER than an independently bottom-up-derived market-sizing path, not slower — a mild positive signal for guidance credibility, though it also means the FY28 number leaves limited room for error if the underlying market (Section 2) does not expand as assumed.

### 3C Capacity cross-check

Management's own disclosed capacity ceilings (Concall Aug 2026 Transcript, p.14-15):
- "From the current capacity, if I exclude the Rs 290 crores ongoing Capex... it should be about Rs 2,500 odd crores" — i.e., current + near-complete Rs 150 Cr brownfield programme peaks around **Rs 2,500 Cr**.
- "So, Rs 400 crores plus the Rs 290 crores, which we have announced, would be capable of taking us to a top-line of closer to about Rs 3,000 crores to Rs 3,300 crores" — i.e., current + the ALREADY-ANNOUNCED Rs 290 Cr castings/machining programme + a FURTHER, board-UNAPPROVED ~Rs 400 Cr Bangalore/equipment programme together cap out at **Rs 3,000-3,300 Cr**.

Comparing to this stage's SOM:
- **SOM(yr3) = Rs 2,754 Cr** falls inside the Rs 2,500-3,300 Cr bracket, but only on the assumption the Rs 290 Cr castings/machining capex is online — commissioning is dated Q1 2029-30 by the AR and all three concalls but Q1FY29 by the Q1FY27 investor-presentation slide (unresolved conflict, carried from B04/B07). A ~Sept-2026 run date puts year 3 at roughly Sept 2029 — right at the edge of either commissioning date. **Timing is tight; SOM(yr3) is deliverable only if the Rs 290 Cr programme holds to its EARLIER stated date or is not required in full.**
- **SOM(yr5) = Rs 3,453 Cr exceeds even the higher, currently-unapproved capacity ceiling (Rs 3,000-3,300 Cr)** by Rs 153-453 Cr.

**Capacity check: gap of ~Rs 150-450 Cr at year 5 versus management's own stated capacity ceiling (which itself assumes a further ~Rs 400 Cr Bangalore capex that is not yet board-approved). The SOM is the optimistic side at the 5-year horizon; delivering it requires capital commitments beyond what management has stated it will spend.**

### 5A The funnel

### 5B Runway assessment

- **Revenue headroom = SAM ÷ current revenue** = 12,000 / 1,912.8 = **6.3x**
- **TAM growth rate (blended)**: weighting the three Method-1 component TAMs by their share of the Rs 25,000 Cr conservative sum (laminations 38.0% at 8.70% CAGR; castings 52.9% at 5.29% CAGR; machined components 9.0% at 3.86% CAGR):
  0.380 × 8.70 + 0.529 × 5.29 + 0.090 × 3.86 = 3.31 + 2.80 + 0.35 = **6.45% ≈ 6.5%**
- **Company CAGR vs TAM**: Pitti's FY24-FY26 consolidated revenue CAGR was ~24% (Rs 1,244.2 Cr to Rs 1,912.8 Cr — Inv. Pres. Q1FY27, slide 25), far above the 6.5% blended TAM growth rate — Pitti has been GAINING SHARE, not riding the market, consistent with the SAM-share increase this stage's SOM projects forward (16% -> 19% -> 21%).
- **Years to saturate SAM at current growth**: at Pitti's own recent ~24% revenue CAGR against a SAM of Rs 12,000 Cr (growing itself at 6.5%/yr), Pitti would reach 100% of SAM in roughly 8-9 years (solving 1,912.8 × 1.24^n = 12,000 × 1.065^n for n) — well beyond the 3-5 year SOM horizon this stage targets, meaning capacity (Section 3C), not market size, is the nearer-term constraint.

### 5C Runway classification

No numeric MASSIVE/STRONG/GOOD/MODERATE/LIMITED matrix was found defined in the frameworks corpus (checked Master_Project_Prompt_v3_6.md, which names the five-way label but not its thresholds). This report applies a stated, transparent heuristic: MASSIVE (headroom >15x, growth >15%), STRONG (8-15x, >10%), GOOD (4-8x, 5-10%), MODERATE (2-4x, 3-5%), LIMITED (<2x or <3%).

At **6.3x headroom** and **6.5% TAM growth**, Pitti lands in **GOOD**.

### 5D SAM expansion levers actually being pursued

- **Castings capacity near-doubling** (18,600 t to 24,600 t by H1 FY27, to 36,000 t by Q1 FY29/FY30 — date conflict noted) directly targets the largest and least-penetrated slice of this stage's TAM (the castings component, Rs 13,227 Cr of the Rs 25,000 Cr conservative sum) — potential addition to SAM if the "product fit" haircut (Section 3A, -40% on the castings slice for non-precision scope) narrows as Pitti moves further up the value chain: each 5-point narrowing of that haircut adds roughly Rs 660 Cr to SAM.
- **Machining capacity +50%** (7.2 to 10.8 lakh hours) targets the machined-components slice directly.
- **Export/global sourcing diversification** (China+1): if Pitti's export share rises from 27% toward, say, 35% of revenue over the SOM horizon, the "realistic" TAM scaling in Section 2 (currently Rs 34,250 Cr at 27% export mix) would rise further, though this stage does not attempt to model that forward given the assumption-heavy nature of the export scaling already flagged.
- **Bangalore facility (~Rs 400 Cr, board-unapproved)**: per Section 3C, this is the capital commitment that would be needed to close the year-5 capacity gap; it is explicitly NOT yet approved (B07 optionality register, "kill signal: no approval within FY28").
