## SECTION 1: THE BUSINESS MODEL IN PLAIN ENGLISH

### 1A. One-line description

Prizor Viztech buys, assembles, and — starting in FY26 — increasingly manufactures branded CCTV cameras, NVR/DVR systems and allied display products (LED TVs, monitors, touch panels), and sells them pan-India through an 11,000+ dealer network to retail, government, education and infrastructure buyers (AR p.28: "the company is engaged in the business of Trading Assembling Servicing and Like wise of Security Systems and Electronic Home and Commercial items"; AR p.75; Inv. ### 1E. The chai-stall-uncle version

Think of Prizor like a neighbourhood electronics shop that grew into a small factory. For years it bought finished CCTV cameras and TVs from other manufacturers, put its own "Prizor" sticker on them, and sold them through a huge web of local dealers — like a chaiwala who buys milk, tea leaves and sugar from others but sells his own branded chai. Then the government said: only certain certified, India-made cameras can be sold for serious security work, and many foreign suppliers got locked out. So Prizor rushed to build its own small factory in Gandhinagar — buying the tea garden and the stove, instead of just buying ready-made chai — so it can make more of the camera itself, keep more of the profit, and be one of the few shops allowed to sell to the biggest customers. It's still early: the factory only really got built out in the most recent year, so nobody yet knows if home-made chai tastes as good (or is as cheap) as the stuff they used to just resell.

### 2D. Industry lifecycle stage

The Indian video-surveillance/CCTV market is in a growth phase, not maturity: the presentation's own market-size chart shows India surveillance-systems revenue rising from roughly Rs.78 billion (2024) to a projected Rs.200 billion (2029E), and global surveillance-market volumes are projected to rise from 1.11 billion to 1.60 billion units through FY30 at a 10.4% market-value CAGR (Inv. Pres. slide 20). Within that growth industry, Prizor is a small, fast-growing, share-gaining entrant riding a specific regulatory catalyst (exclusion of non-certified foreign brands), not an established scale leader — its FY26 revenue base (Rs.147.9 crore) is a rounding error against the addressable market size disclosed in its own presentation.

**Cycle stage that matters for valuation:** early-scale-up / mid-business-model-transition. The company is not yet in a steady state — FY25 and FY26 have fundamentally different cost structures (trading-dominant vs manufacture-dominant).
