## SECTION 1: THE BUSINESS MODEL IN PLAIN ENGLISH

### 1A. One-line description

SSWL designs and manufactures steel wheel rims, alloy wheel rims and aluminium steering
knuckles to OEM specification, for passenger vehicles, commercial vehicles, tractors,
two/three-wheelers and off-the-road equipment, sold mostly to Indian vehicle makers with a
smaller export book (AR p.143, Note 1 "Corporate Information": "a leading manufacturer of
Automotive Wheel Rims and other auto components"; AR p.174, Note 44: "manufacturing of Wheel
Rims and other automotive components").

### 1B. Money flow chain, one chain per revenue stream

**Steel Wheels** (largest stream): [Hot-rolled steel strip, mostly from Tata Steel, a strategic
investor and stakeholder (AR p.76: "one of the key supplier[s] of the main commodity that we
consume (Steel) also happens to be our stakeholder")] → [SSWL stamps, welds and paints wheel
rims to an OEM-approved design, at five plants: Dappar, Chennai, Jamshedpur, Mehsana, and the
Saraikela backward-integrated hot-rolling mill (Jul-2026 deck p.10)] → [delivers finished
wheels on a scheduled basis direct to OEM assembly lines] → [OEM pays] → [on standard trade
credit terms; debtor days 32.8 FY26 standalone (AR p.125, Key Financial Ratios)].

**Alloy Wheels**: [Aluminium ingot, partly in-house recycled (40% of aluminium ingot input
FY26 vs 25% FY25, AR p.~94 BRSR "recycled input material")] → [SSWL casts, machines and
finishes alloy wheels at the Mehsana plant, expanding into Bhuj (AMW Autocomponent) via
NCLT-acquired capacity] → [delivers to OEMs, mostly for higher trims of PV models: Creta,
Alcazar, Venue, Harrier, Nexon, Duster and others named as "Key Customers" (Jul-2026 deck
p.12,17)] → [OEM pays] → [trade credit].

**Aluminium Steering Knuckles** (newest, smallest stream): [Aluminium ingot] → [cast and
machined at Mehsana/Bhuj, commercialised FY25 (Jul-2026 deck p.6)] → [delivered to "2 leading
OEMs" only, as of Q1 FY27 (Jul-2026 deck p.13)] → [OEM pays] → [trade credit]. This stream
is a young, narrow-customer-base product line, structurally different in predictability from
the other two.

### 1E. The chai-stall-uncle version

Think of SSWL as the workshop that makes the wheel your car rolls on, but it never sells to
you directly — it sells to Maruti, Hyundai, Tata Motors and the rest, who bolt the wheel onto
the car before it reaches the showroom. For years SSWL mostly made steel wheels: cheap, tough,
and it simply passes on the price of steel to the carmaker, like a tailor who charges for the
cloth plus a stitching fee. Now it is also making alloy wheels (lighter, shinier, used on
costlier car trims) and a brand-new part called an aluminium steering knuckle, sold so far to
just two carmakers. The idea: alloy and knuckle parts fetch a better stitching fee per wheel
than plain steel. The catch, visible in the company's own five-year numbers: even as alloy's
share of sales has climbed from about a quarter to over a third, the company's overall profit
margin has actually slipped, not risen — so the "better stitching fee" story has not yet shown
up in the total bill.

## SECTION 2: INDUSTRY DYNAMICS & COMPETITIVE POSITION

### 2A. Five forces, plainly

| Force | Reading | Helps / Hurts / Neutral |
|---|---|---|
| Competition | AR names an "unorganized market" that is shifting to "brand-based product offerings" (AR p.127) — implying a fragmented base of small/regional wheel makers below the organised, OEM-qualified layer that SSWL competes in. Among listed peers, no other Indian company spans steel AND alloy wheels AND knuckles; Wheels India (steel/CV/tractor only) and Uno Minda (alloy wheels as one line within a large diversified auto-parts portfolio) are the closest comparators (peer set per this run's Step 1 brief) | Neutral-to-helps (SSWL's combined product breadth is a genuine differentiator versus single-product peers) |
| Entry barriers | Multi-year OEM vendor qualification per platform, plant proximity to OEM clusters, capital-intensive tooling, ISO/TS16949 certification | Helps |

### 2D. Industry lifecycle stage

Per the company's own framing (Jul-2026 deck p.16): the steel wheel market is MATURE
(company-cited industry growth ~4% p.a. over the next 5 years), the alloy wheel market is in
GROWTH (~12% p.a.), and aluminium knuckles are EMERGING (commercialised FY25, two OEM
customers as of Q1 FY27). SSWL's own position: a scaled incumbent in a mature steel base,
a scaling challenger in a growing alloy segment, and an early-stage entrant in knuckles.

**The core finding for LBF2**: the revenue-mix shift toward alloy wheels is real and cleanly
anchored (alloy revenue share ~28% FY23 → ~36% FY26 per Ind-Ra p.4 and the decks). The
"EBITDA per wheel Rs 314 vs Rs 262" figure in company memory is a genuine, derivable
quarter-on-quarter (Q1 FY27 vs Q1 FY26) blended metric, not a fabricated number — but it is a
COMPANY-WIDE average, not a per-product margin, because no document in this corpus discloses
product-level EBITDA. And it sits alongside a FULL-YEAR EBITDA margin that has fallen, not
risen, over FY22-FY26 even as the alloy share climbed. Both facts are true at once; the mix
shift has not yet demonstrably lifted the company's overall margin on a full-year basis.

## SECTION 4: RISKS, VALUATION APPROACH & MONITORING

### 4A. Business-model-specific risks

| Category | Risk | First financial line item to watch |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue model | OEM/platform concentration: several customers each account for a majority share of business within their own sourcing (Honda 96%, MG Motors 100%, Suzuki Scooters 96% — Jul-2026 deck p.12); loss or de-listing of a single platform is a concentrated hit | Quarterly volume by product line in the deck's "Volumes (in Lakh units)" panel |
| Revenue model | Export exposure to trade policy: exports fell from Rs 561 Cr (FY25) to Rs 454 Cr (FY26) on US tariff disruption (May-2026 deck p.15,23) | Export revenue (Rs Cr) line, quarterly P&L |

### 4C. Quarterly monitoring checklist

1. Steel wheel volume (lakh units), QoQ and YoY — good: growing in line with auto industry volumes; trouble: declining while alloy grows (share dilution without total growth)
2. Alloy wheel volume (lakh units) — good: growing faster than steel; trouble: flat or below company's own ~12% p.a. segment-growth guidance
3. Knuckle volume and OEM customer count — good: customer count rising beyond 2; trouble: still 2 OEMs, capacity built ahead of demand
4. Revenue mix % by product — good: alloy+knuckle share rising; trouble: reverting toward steel
5. EBITDA margin % — good: at or above the 10-11% rangebound level Ind-Ra describes; trouble: below 9.5% for two straight quarters
6. Blended EBITDA per wheel (derived, see 3D) — good: rising YoY same-quarter; trouble: flat or falling despite rising alloy mix
7. Export revenue (Rs Cr) and % of total — good: recovering toward FY25 levels; trouble: further decline
8. Capacity utilization %, steel and alloy — good: above 80%; trouble: below 70% (idle capex)
9. Bhuj capex progress / trial-production status — good: on the guided timeline; trouble: delay, cost overrun (verify against concall transcripts; not found in this stage's document set)
10. Net debt / EBITDA (adjusted leverage) — good: trending toward Ind-Ra's 2.5x positive-trigger; trouble: above 3.2x (the 9MFY26 level)
11. Cash and bank balance — good: a rising buffer; trouble: at or near zero, as at both FY25 and FY26 year ends on the standalone balance sheet
12. Cash conversion cycle (days) — good: toward the ~90-day FY25 level; trouble: above 100 days
13. Steel/aluminium input price trend vs the ~3-month pass-through lag — good: stable prices; trouble: a sharp spike, which will show up in gross margin before it is recovered
14. KMP and board stability — good: no further departures; trouble: additional CFO/MD/director churn
15. New OEM platform wins named in the deck's "Key Platforms" table — good: net additions; trouble: net losses

### 4D. Highest-value questions for management

1. What EBITDA margin, or contribution margin, does the company actually earn on alloy wheels and knuckles versus steel wheels? Reassures: a disclosed, durable per-product margin premium. Worries: management cannot, or will not, quantify it, or the premium disappears once shared overheads are allocated.
2. Why has full-year EBITDA margin fallen from 12.7% (FY22) to 9.9% (FY26) even as the higher-margin alloy share of revenue has risen from roughly a quarter to over a third? Reassures: a clear, time-bound explanation (raw-material lag, export decline, capex-phase costs) with a credible path back up. Worries: a vague or shifting explanation.
3. What is the status and expected trial-production date for the Bhuj alloy/knuckle expansion, and is the FY27 revenue guidance of ~Rs 6,500 Cr contingent on it? Reassures: on-schedule, funded, with named offtake. Worries: delay, cost overrun, or guidance not de-risked from Bhuj timing.
4. Beyond the current two OEM customers, what is the knuckle order pipeline, against 1.1 Mn units of FY27E capacity being built for a business that sold only ~2.6 lakh units in FY26? Reassures: named additional OEM commitments. Worries: capacity built well ahead of demand.
5. With net adjusted leverage above 2.5x (Ind-Ra) and standalone cash of Rs 1.5-6.5 Cr across recent year/quarter ends, how is the ~Rs 500 Cr FY27 capex plan being funded, and what is the plan for the Rs 141.4 Cr intercompany loan to AMW? Reassures: committed facilities and a clear AMW repayment plan. Worries: reliance on short-term working-capital lines to fund capex.
6. What caused the CFO change (Nov-2025) and the Deputy Managing Director's resignation (Jun-2026), and what is the succession plan? Reassures: routine, orderly handover. Worries: repeated senior turnover during a capex-heavy expansion phase.
7. Why did export revenue fall from Rs 561 Cr (FY25) to Rs 454 Cr (FY26), and is the ~Rs 600 Cr FY27 export guidance dependent on a US tariff outcome outside the company's control? Reassures: diversified export geography reduces single-market dependence. Worries: guidance hinges on an external policy outcome.
