Growth compounds in revenue not in cash

Zaggle runs a digital corporate spend platform in India, selling expense management, prepaid cards, rewards and procure to pay software to companies and their employees. FY25 gross revenue was Rs 1,304 cr, but roughly 55% of that is Propel gift card resale that carries a 94% cost, so the number that describes the real business is net revenue near Rs 625 cr. Adjusted EBITDA margin on that net revenue basis was 19.9% in FY25 and 21.7% in FY26. Three streams make up the top line: program fee from interchange sharing with bank partners at 42%, Propel gift cards at 55%, and true SaaS or platform fee at only 3%. The base is 3,455 corporate customers and 3.28 mn activated users, with market capitalisation of Rs 2,731 cr at Rs 203.

The transition on offer is a mix shift toward higher quality revenue and deeper cross sell into existing accounts. Cross sell moved from 16% at IPO to 21% by the second quarter of FY26, then management stopped disclosing it. Standalone net revenue grew about 35% in FY26. Management is bolting on acquisitions (Rio.Money closed, DICE signed as a restructured asset purchase at Rs 68 cr, down from Rs 123 cr) and building a retail co brand credit card business, Zagg.money with AU Bank, where the credit risk is stated to sit with the partner bank.

Gate 0 lands on AVOID, and the reasons are mechanical, not a verdict on the franchise. Median ROCE across the four available years is 9.78%, just under the 10% line, and cumulative operating cash flow to PAT is negative 0.44. The score is then downgraded one tier for a four year history that starts only at the September 2023 listing. The trough is FY24 to FY25, right after the IPO and QIP: capital employed rose about sevenfold from FY23 to FY26 while operating profit rose about 3.5 times, a dilution of the capital base rather than a break in unit economics. The forward facing numbers are strong: revenue and PAT compounded at 51% and 82% over three years, every year of revenue growth was positive, and the balance sheet is net cash.

The cash side is where the thesis has to be earned. Operating cash flow was negative in three of the four years (FY23, FY24 and FY26), positive only in FY25. FY26 operating cash flow was negative Rs 51 cr and free cash flow was negative Rs 159 cr, the worst of the four years, against reported PAT of Rs 138 cr. Standalone cash conversion in FY25 was 22.7 paise of operating cash per rupee of profit. The company capitalised about Rs 107 cr of development cost in FY26, took Rs 40 cr to 50 cr of short term borrowing while holding roughly Rs 440 cr of cash, and let the incentive and cashback ratio drift to about 68% of program fees against a stated path toward 50%. Propel gross margin fell to 4% in the fourth quarter of FY26 versus a 7% to 9% recovery promised back in FY25.

Management delivery grades C. Of ten tracked promises across the last three quarters, two were delivered, three partial and five missed. The misses cluster in international expansion (MENA and the US both slipped and were blamed on regional war), the OMC fleet contract that stayed hopeful for three quarters with no name, the EffiaSoft acquisition that went from near closing to abandoned, and Propel margin. The question of when operating cash flow turns sustainably positive was deflected in each of the three quarters. The CFO and CTO both resigned in early 2026, and an external Group CFO was appointed within about six weeks. On promoters the read is trustworthy: the promoter group has been buying shares in the open market, pledge is about 0.55%, there is no related party leakage, and audit opinions are unqualified. Carry as watch items the delayed statutory ROC filings, an NSE warning over PAN on letterhead, and non audit fees exceeding audit fees in FY25.

Peers neither confirm the growth story nor contradict its facts. No peer names Zaggle across seven transcripts. Two peers, Capillary and RateGain, independently corroborate the AI productivity claim that development cycles compress from 75 to 80 days toward under 30; Tanla is silent on the topic, and an earlier reading that credited a third peer was a misattributed RateGain quote, now corrected. No peer describes a demand boom matching Zaggle's 40% to 45% guidance, and the fastest peer grows near 22%. Peers routinely disclose churn, client concentration and named client losses; Zaggle disclosed no churn figure across three quarters. The gap with peers is candor and unverifiable market sizing, not a contradicted fact.

The market runway is real but smaller than the headline. Conservative fee pool TAM is about Rs 8,000 cr, the realistic case about Rs 16,000 cr, with serviceable market near Rs 11,600 cr. Current serviceable share is 7.3%, leaving about 13.8 times revenue headroom, and the pool grows about 16% a year, classed GOOD. Management's Rs 1,75,000 cr headline claim is a definitional mismatch (payments in India, not Zaggle's fee niche) and reads 21.9 times inflated. The share gain math implies a 23% to 24% revenue CAGR, which sits below the 35% net revenue growth just posted, so sustaining recent pace needs the aggressive share gain band rather than the base case.

Three input gaps shape confidence. The prospectus is absent, so the backward baseline runs only on post listing years. Exchange announcements are absent, so the material event record was reconstructed from the web. The shareholding pattern beyond March 2025 is absent, so the institutional absence qualifier could not be affirmed from a filing and was withheld.
