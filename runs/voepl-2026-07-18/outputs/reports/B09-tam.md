## SECTION 1: MARKET DEFINITION

### 1A. Precise Boundaries

**Product scope (included):** Third-party B2B contract manufacturing (ODM/OEM) of (i) room air conditioners — indoor unit (IDU) + outdoor unit (ODU) sets, (ii) small reciprocating hermetic compressors (<2TR, refrigeration-grade, used in domestic/commercial refrigerators and freezers), (iii) small commercial refrigeration equipment — deep freezers, hard-top and glass-top coolers, (iv) EMS/PCBA/LED lighting components and remote/controller boards for consumer-durable OEMs, and (v) ancillary backward-integrated components (heat exchangers, plastics/EPS, sheet metal, copper tubing, cross-flow fans) sold internally or to third parties.

**Excluded:** captive/in-house manufacturing retained by branded OEMs (VOEPL cannot capture this even though it is part of gross industry output); rotary AC compressors (VOEPL "not finalized anything" on this — Concall_Jun_2026_Transcript, line 82, Shrey Patel exchange); large cold-chain/warehouse commercial refrigeration systems; premium/luxury appliance segment; retail/branded consumer sales (VOEPL has no owned consumer brand); export markets (immaterial — UL certification exists for the US but exports are not a disclosed revenue line, Investor_Presentation_1.txt milestones page); mobile-phone/telecom/auto-electronics EMS (the bulk of India's overall EMS market, which VOEPL does not compete in).

**Geographic scope:** India domestic. VOEPL is a Nashik/Chennai/Sanand-based manufacturer selling almost entirely to Indian OEM brands; no material export revenue disclosed.

**Customer scope:** Branded consumer-durable OEMs sourcing under OEM or ODM contracts (VOEPL states 8–10 customers across categories, 4 AC customers currently — Investor_Presentation_1.txt p.15; Concall_Jun_2026_Transcript "we are currently catering to 4" line 34).

**Channel scope:** B2B contract-manufacturing / long-term supply agreements only — not retail.

**Price segment:** Mass-market and mid-tier consumer durables; VOEPL does not disclose participation in the ultra-premium/inverter-flagship AC segment that OEMs like Daikin/LG keep in-house.

A wrong definition here would overstate TAM by many multiples (e.g., quoting the full India EMS market of ~$40bn, which is 95%+ irrelevant to VOEPL's product mix) — hence the segment-by-segment scoping below.

Two distinct management claims exist, and they should not be conflated:

1. **Capacity-based revenue ceiling:** "the current set of expansion... is good enough to take us beyond INR2,500 crores" — Sajid Shaikh (CFO), Concall_Jun_2026_Transcript, p.11/25 (Kaushal Sharma exchange). Tempered later in the same call: "a jump from INR800 crores to INR2,500 crores in two years is a big ask... it probably will come, but it will take a little more time" (Bala Murali Krishna exchange, p.13/25). 2. **Industry-overview slide claims** (Investor_Presentation_1.txt, pp.33-34): AC penetration India 8% vs global 30%; LED penetration India 4% vs global 30%; India deep-freezer market ~1.3 million units (2022); freezer market USD 474.82mn (2022) → USD 3,234.23mn forecast by 2031; water dispenser market CAGR 8.9% (2023-2033); AC industry "9.5-10.0 million units per annum, 70% residential." These slides carry no date stamp and sit alongside internal editorial notes ("Need to Update the data," "Need to confirm the change," "Need to ask about the change") on adjacent pages, strongly indicating this is unrefreshed IPO-era (~2022) boilerplate. Credibility read: **broad and stale** — used for direction only in Section 4, never as a headline TAM number (staleness rule).

## SECTION 2: TAM ESTIMATION, MULTIPLE METHODS

### Segment A — RAC ODM/OEM Outsourcing (VOEPL's core, ~60% of FY26 revenue)

**Method 1 — Top-down.**
- India RAC industry volume, FY26: ICRA forecasts **11.0–11.5 million units** (down 10-15% from a record 12.5-13.0 million units in FY25), dated September 24, 2025 (icra.in press release, via web search). This conflicts with the B06 peer cross-check figure of **13-15 million units FY26** cited from AMBER/EPACK/PGEL commentary; PG Electroplast's own FY26 disclosure states "the RAC industry... experienced a decline of approximately 15% during FY26" (web search, ICICI Direct/scanx.trade), which is directionally consistent with ICRA's decline call but the two volume bands (11-11.5mn vs 13-15mn) do not fully reconcile — flagged, conservative (ICRA, lower) figure used for the headline TAM below.
- Blended manufacturing-value realization: VOEPL's own disclosed average realization "at a CBU... about 20-odd thousand" rupees per complete built unit (Concall_Jun_2026_Transcript, Rupal Ratnawat exchange, p.16/25).
- Total India RAC manufacturing value = 11.0-11.5mn units × ~₹20,000/unit ≈ **₹22,000-23,000 Cr** (conservative, ICRA-based) to 13-15mn units × ₹20,000 ≈ **₹26,000-30,000 Cr** (peer-cited, higher band).
- Subtract captive/in-house manufacturing: "Around 65-70% of India's RAC manufacturing capacity is held by the OEMs [captive/in-house]... the remaining 30-35% is held by contract manufacturers" (web search, marketsandata.com / industry commentary) — this is the addressable ODM/EMS-outsourcing slice VOEPL can compete for.
- **RAC-ODM outsourced-segment TAM = ₹22,000cr × 30% ≈ ₹6,600 Cr (conservative)** to **₹28,000cr × 30-35% (mid) ≈ ₹8,500 Cr (realistic)**.

**Method 3 — Peer revenue aggregation (cross-check).**
- EPACK Durable FY26 consolidated revenue: **₹1,894.46 Cr** (screener.in / whalesbook, via web search), stated market share "24% in the ODM space for RAC" (web search, equitymaster/multiple sources) → implied total organised RAC-ODM market = 1,894 ÷ 0.24 ≈ **₹7,890 Cr**.
- Amber Enterprises FY26 total revenue **₹12,186 Cr** (up 22% YoY, whalesbook via web search) with ~46% RAC-ODM share cited — but Amber's revenue spans electronics, mobility/EV and railway subsidiaries well beyond RAC-ODM, so this figure cannot be used directly as a RAC-ODM-only cross-check (noted, not used quantitatively).
- PG Electroplast FY26 total revenue **₹5,288 Cr** (up 8.6% YoY despite a 15% RAC-industry decline; RAC segment revenue grew ~9% for the full year — icicidirect/univest via web search) — also diversified beyond RAC, not decomposed further here.
- The EPACK-implied ₹7,890 Cr sits inside the Method-1 range (₹6,600-8,500cr), closer to the realistic end — **reasonable triangulation, Confidence: Medium-High.**

**Method 5 — Global benchmark (directional only, STALE).**
India AC household penetration ~8% vs global ~30% (Investor_Presentation_1.txt p.34, undated/likely ~2022) — if the gap closes even partially over a multi-decade horizon this implies multi-fold unit-volume upside, but the underlying datapoint is stale (see Section 1B) and is used only as a qualitative growth driver in Section 4A, never as a headline TAM figure, per the staleness rule.

**Segment A conclusion: TAM conservative ₹6,600 Cr / realistic ₹8,500 Cr. Confidence: Medium (industry-volume sources diverge 11-15mn units; ODM-share split is a single web-sourced estimate).**

### Segment B — Reciprocating Compressor Localisation / Import Substitution

**Method 4 — Import substitution (primary method for this segment).**
- Total India reciprocating (refrigeration-grade) compressor demand: **~22 million units currently** (Sukrit Bharati, MD, Concall_Feb_2026_Transcript, p.11/23: "the refrigeration market quickly, it is about 22 million currently in India"). Of this, **13-14 million units currently imported** (same source), the balance domestically produced mainly by large Korean-owned captive manufacturers.
- Jun-2026 concall reframes the same gap as "**the total gap in the market is about 14 million or 15 million as of today**" with "total capacities, all capacities put together... expected to be between 15 and 18 million" once VOEPL and two announced new entrants ramp (Concall_Jun_2026_Transcript, p.19/25, Hitaindra Pradhan exchange) — broadly consistent with the Feb-2026 figures.
- VOEPL's own unit economics: 2.8 million unit capacity ↔ "**a top line of about INR400 crores**" at full utilisation (Concall_Feb_2026_Transcript, p.11/23) → implied average realisation ≈ ₹1,428/unit, confirmed later as "**Average realization for compressor is about INR1,400 to INR1,500**" (Concall_Jun_2026_Transcript, p.24/25, Bala Murali Krishna follow-up).
- **Total reciprocating-compressor market value = 22mn units × ~₹1,450/unit ≈ ₹3,190 Cr** (single-source-anchored — this whole segment estimate rests on one management verbal disclosure of "22 million units," not an independent third-party report; flagged Confidence: Low-Medium).
- **Import-substitution-addressable pool = 13-14mn units × ₹1,450 ≈ ₹1,885-2,030 Cr** — this is the value of currently-imported compressors that QCO mechanically forces toward local sourcing.

**QCO clarification (resolves the B06 flagged discrepancy).** Web search of the actual DPIIT policy (angelone.in, sjexim.services, May 2026) confirms: **the import relaxation is 40% for small reciprocating compressors used in refrigerators** (VOEPL's segment) **and 30% for small rotary compressors used in air conditioners** (AMBER/EPACK/PGEL's segment — they don't make reciprocating fridge compressors). This is **not a contradiction between VOEPL and peer disclosures as B06 flagged** — both figures are correct, for two different compressor types. Flagged as resolved.

**Segment B conclusion: TAM conservative ₹3,100 Cr / realistic ₹3,200 Cr (total market), with a distinct ₹1,885-2,030 Cr import-substitution-addressable sub-pool. Confidence: Low-Medium (single-source volume anchor).**

## SECTION 3: SAM & SOM

### 3A. SAM

Apply five filters to conservative TAM (₹10,400cr) and realistic TAM (₹12,600cr):

1. **Product fit** (VOEPL doesn't do rotary AC compressors, large cold-chain systems, or the ultra-premium AC tier): **-10%**
2. **Geography**: already India-scoped in Section 2, **0% further cut**
3. **Channel** (B2B contract-manufacturing only; some of the compressor/commercial-refrigeration TAM estimates may include non-outsourced channels): **-5%**
4. **Customer** (folded into capability below — VOEPL serves 8-10 OEM accounts today, not the full universe of Indian appliance brands)
5. **Capability** (single-region footprint — Nashik/Chennai/Sanand; western-India location advantage cited but limited presence to reach captive/entrenched northern and southern OEM relationships; entrenched incumbents Amber/EPACK hold combined ~70% of RAC-ODM share): **-15%**

Cumulative retention = 0.90 × 0.95 × 0.85 ≈ **72.7%**

- **SAM (conservative) = ₹10,400cr × 0.727 ≈ ₹7,560 Cr**
- SAM (realistic) = ₹12,600cr × 0.727 ≈ ₹9,160 Cr
- **SAM as % of TAM ≈ 72.7%**

### 3B. SOM at 3 and 5 Years

**Current SAM share:** FY26 revenue ₹823.6cr ÷ SAM (conservative) ₹7,560cr = **10.9%**.

Cross-check by segment: VOEPL AC revenue (60% × 823.6 = ₹494cr) vs a similarly-filtered RAC-ODM SAM (₹6,600cr × 0.727 ≈ ₹4,798cr) → VOEPL's implied share of the RAC-ODM SAM ≈ 10.3%, sensibly below EPACK's stated 24% and Amber's 46% market shares in the same segment — VOEPL is the smaller, newer scaled player. Consistent.

**Share-gain trajectory (per the share-gain rules):**
- RAC-ODM (mature, competitive, 3-4 established players): **normal** gain territory, 1-2pp in isolation.
- Reciprocating compressor (currently >60% import-supplied/"unorganised," QCO mandates 60% local sourcing, VOEPL is the sole reciprocating-compressor OEM/ODM player today with committed capacity already funded — 2.8mn→6mn units, ₹150cr tied up): squarely matches the **"faster possible where unorganised share >40% is formalising"** carve-out — but two new entrants are expected online by Q3/Q4 FY27 (Concall_Jun_2026_Transcript, p.19/25), which will compress this advantage within 12-18 months.
- Commercial refrigeration (new glass-top range, strong stated demand): modestly above-normal.
- Blended across the SAM (RAC-ODM ~63% weight, compressor ~30%, commercial refrigeration ~7%), the compressor segment's faster-formalising dynamic pulls the company-wide blended gain into the **aggressive** band (3-5pp) even though RAC-ODM alone would only justify normal gains. No competitor-exit/acquisition event is assumed, so the >5pp band is not invoked for the 3-year mark; the 5-year figure is extended modestly beyond 5pp on the same formalising-market logic, not on a competitor-exit assumption.

Assumed: **+4.0pp share gain by yr3, +6.5pp by yr5** (company-wide blended).

**TAM/SAM forward growth (see Section 4A/tam_growth_pct = ~14%, weighted):** RAC-ODM segment ~16-17% (AR MD&A, "the Indian AC market alone is projected to grow at ~16-17% CAGR till 2029" — 6ff4905d..., p.66) weighted 63%; compressor segment ~9% (no direct anchor found for total-market volume growth; used as a placeholder proxy from general refrigerator-market growth commentary, flagged NOT FOUND for a precise anchor) weighted 30%; commercial refrigeration ~6.3% (Grand View Research, via web search) weighted 7%. Weighted blend ≈ 0.63×16.5 + 0.30×9 + 0.07×6.3 ≈ **13.5%, rounded to 14%.**

**Implied revenue CAGR:**
- 3yr: (1,669 ÷ 823.6)^(1/3) − 1 = **26.6%**

Both sit below management's own stated 35-40% CAGR guidance (Concall_Jun_2026_Transcript, "35%, 40% kind of a CAGR going forward in the next three to five years") — a legitimate, evidence-based gap: management's number assumes faster share capture and/or a higher-realisation product mix than this conservative-bias SOM build allows for.

### 3C. Capacity Cross-Check

Using the injected B07 capex-embedded-growth figure: **+122% over current revenue** → capacity ceiling ≈ ₹823.6cr × 2.22 ≈ **₹1,828 Cr**, based on the named, currently-quantified FY27 capex pipeline (compressor ~₹150cr to 6.0mn units; AC ₹40-50cr to 1.8mn units; deep freezers ₹25-50cr to 400k units; EMS ₹25cr to 12L cph).

- **vs SOM_3yr (₹1,669cr): capacity is SUFFICIENT** — ₹159cr of headroom. The 3-year SOM does not require capacity beyond what is already committed/costed.
- **vs SOM_5yr (₹2,533cr): capacity gap of ~₹705 Cr.** The currently-quantified capex plan tops out at ₹1,828cr, well short of the ₹2,533cr yr5 SOM.

**Which side is optimistic?** Notably, management's own independent capacity-ceiling claim of "**beyond ₹2,500cr**" (Section 1B) lands almost exactly on this report's independently-derived SOM_5yr of ₹2,533cr — a near 1:1 match between two separately-built figures. That convergence suggests **neither the SOM nor management's claim is the optimistic side** — rather, **the currently-disclosed/costed capex plan (stage-7's ₹240-275cr FY27 pipeline) is the lagging/incomplete side.** Both the SOM and management's own framing implicitly assume a further, not-yet-fully-costed expansion phase — consistent with concall colour on aspirational next-phase compressor capacity (7.5-10mn units, beyond the funded 6mn) and EMS/AC capacity beyond what's currently budgeted. **capacity_check = "gap of ~₹705 Cr for yr5 SOM; the capex plan (not the SOM or management's claim) is the presently-incomplete side."**

### 4C. Market Structure

- **RAC-ODM:** competitor count ~3-4 organised scaled players (Amber, EPACK, PGEL, VOEPL) plus captive OEM in-house manufacturing (65-70% of total RAC capacity, per web search) — top-3 organised concentration is high within the outsourced slice; segment is "organised sector dominates" per VOEPL's own deck (p.34); price-competitive, gradually consolidating among scaled ODM players.
- **Reciprocating compressor:** currently near-monopoly (VOEPL sole OEM/ODM player) inside a market that is majority import-supplied (>60%) — about to fragment as 2 new entrants arrive; differentiation-driven today, shifting toward price competition as capacity comes online; import share on a mandated declining trend (QCO).
- **Commercial refrigeration/deep freezers:** fragmented, multiple regional and national players; VOEPL a recent entrant (started FY25) riding new-category demand (glass-top range).

### 5B. Runway Assessment

- Revenue headroom = SAM ÷ current revenue = 7,560 ÷ 823.6 ≈ **9.18x**
- TAM growth rate ≈ **14%** (blended, weighted by segment)
- Company CAGR vs TAM: SOM-implied CAGR (25-27%) is well above the blended TAM growth rate (14%) — VOEPL is **gaining share**, not merely riding the market (share rises from 10.9% to 17.4% of SAM over 5 years in this build).
- Years to saturate SAM at current SOM-implied growth: at a ~26% company CAGR against a ~14% SAM CAGR, VOEPL would reach 100% notional SAM share in roughly 18-20 years at these differential rates (illustrative only; share-gain physically caps out long before then given competitive structure — not a realistic scenario, shown only to confirm headroom is not a near-term constraint).

### 5C. Runway Classification

No explicit numeric matrix was provided in this run's inputs; a standard heuristic is applied and stated here for transparency: MASSIVE (>20x headroom, >15% TAM growth), STRONG (10-20x), GOOD (5-10x), MODERATE (2-5x), LIMITED (<2x). At **9.18x headroom** and **~14% TAM growth**, VOEPL sits just under the STRONG threshold, squarely in the upper part of the GOOD band, reinforced by demonstrated share-gain (not just market-riding) and a fast-formalising compressor sub-segment.

**runway_class = GOOD**
