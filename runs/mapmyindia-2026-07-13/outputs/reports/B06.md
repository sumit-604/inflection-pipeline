## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

**2A Demand environment.** The peer set splits along end-market lines. NEWGEN and NUCLEUS — both enterprise/BFSI/government-facing software vendors, the closer analogues to MMI's core government/enterprise business — independently describe the same phenomenon across FY26: healthy pipeline and funnel, but persistently slow large-deal decision-making and closure, concentrated in India and the Middle East, worsening or flat through Q2-Q3 FY26 before modest signs of improvement into Q3-Q4 (Nucleus: "conversions have started to happen," Feb 2026 call, PDF p.9; Newgen: still citing "elongated decision cycles" as late as Jan 2026). RATEGAIN, in the structurally different travel/hospitality end market, shows the opposite — record revenue, raised guidance, and 30%+ YoY booking growth through the same period (Feb 2026 call, PDF p.5-6). Read together: MMI's demand-side deferral story is corroborated specifically by its closest analogues (enterprise/government software), while the outlier (RateGain) operates in an unrelated end market experiencing a genuine boom, not a general software-sector malaise. This is a meaningfully mitigating read for MMI relative to a pure company-specific-failure hypothesis.

**2B Pricing and input costs.** No peer discusses raw-material or component cost inflation matching MMI's DRAM/NAND commentary (see Q8). The dominant cost theme across NEWGEN and NUCLEUS instead is labor-cost inflation from the new Indian Labour Code: Nucleus took a one-time Rs18-35cr hit flagged across its Feb 2026 and May 2026 calls and expects the cost-of-delivery ratio to step up going forward (Ashok Kumar Bhura, Feb 2026 call, PDF p.6-7); Newgen rolled out wage revisions to ~3,500 of 4,500 employees effective October 2025 (Q2 FY26 call, PDF p.10). MMI's B05 concall analysis does not mention the Labour Code at all — this is a real, dated, quantifiable cost pressure hitting comparable Indian software peers in the same fiscal year that MMI's own transcripts are silent on, and it belongs in the missing-risks list.

**2C Capex cycle.** NEWGEN and NUCLEUS are both cautious, organic-growth investors (sales-structure rebuilds, incremental headcount, no major M&A) during a soft demand window — consistent with a "hold, don't expand" posture across the closer peer set. RATEGAIN is the clear outlier: a large, debt-funded acquisition (Sojern, ~$150m+ scale) executed mid-FY26, financed via a $125m loan, with visible near-term PAT drag from amortization and deal costs (Feb 2026 call, PDF p.5-6). This is a lone-expander situation specific to RateGain's travel-tech consolidation thesis, not a signal of an industry-wide capacity race among MMI's software peers; it does not bear directly on MMI's own capital-allocation choices (Rs120cr organic FY26 spend, small M&A) but illustrates that when Indian software companies do have cash to deploy, at least one peer chose aggressive inorganic scaling over MMI's cautious organic-plus-small-M&A approach.

**2D Competitive mentions.** No peer transcript mentions MapmyIndia, C.E. Info Systems, or any unmistakable description of MMI's business (mapping, geospatial, IoT telematics) by name. This is unsurprising given the confirmed adjacency gap (none of the three peers competes in geospatial/mapping or vehicle telematics) and should not be read as either a positive or negative signal.

**2E Risks peers discuss that MMI does not.** (1) New Indian Labour Code cost impact — quantified and flagged by both Newgen and Nucleus, absent from MMI's B05 concall record entirely. (2) Global platform-player competitive entry into India — Newgen names Salesforce, ServiceNow, Mendix and OutSystems entering its space (Q2 FY26 call, PDF p.16), a level of competitive specificity MMI's management never volunteers (per B05 3A, MMI avoids naming any competitor across four consecutive calls). (3) Board-level/regulatory due-diligence escalation as a specific mechanism lengthening BFSI deal cycles — Nucleus names this explicitly; MMI's government-deferral narrative cites fiscal-grant timing and elections but never names a comparable governance-escalation mechanism. (4) M&A integration and leverage risk — RateGain's debt-funded Sojern acquisition introduces real amortization/finance-cost drag on PAT that MMI, with its far smaller Rs25cr Zepto-scale commitments, does not face, but it illustrates a risk category (acquisition integration risk) that is live at scale in this peer set and worth monitoring if MMI's own M&A ambitions (Rs120cr FY26 organic-plus-small-M&A allocation) grow.

**The single most consequential contradiction:** MMI's ~32% trade-receivables rise on flat FY26 revenue (Rs133cr to Rs176cr) has no peer analogue whatsoever — Newgen's DSO moved only 123 to 125 days over three quarters and Nucleus's receivables actually declined across the same window. This directly undercuts any "industry-wide government-payment-cycle" mitigating read and strengthens the case that MMI's working-capital deterioration is a company-specific collections or customer-mix issue, reinforcing the cash-conversion caveat already flagged in B05.

**The single strongest independent confirmation:** Newgen's Q3 FY26 call (Jan 20, 2026 — the same calendar quarter as MMI's own Q3 FY26 call) independently describes an almost identical mechanism to MMI's AI-readiness billing-delay claim: customers "having a re-evaluation in terms of how is AI going to impact that area of the business" and remaining "slightly unsure about taking large order bets" pending AI-strategy clarity. The precision and time-alignment of this match make it the single most credible piece of external corroboration in the entire peer set, materially raising confidence that MMI's AI-scope-change explanation is a genuine sector-wide transition friction rather than a company-specific excuse — even though the verdict is capped at "partially verified" because only one of the three named peers corroborates.

**Overall assessment:** The peer set gives MMI's FY26 narrative a mixed but net-informative read, weighted differently across claims. On the two questions that matter most to the credibility grade — whether the government/BFSI order-deferral story and the AI-scope-change excuse are genuine sector patterns — peer evidence leans toward corroboration, particularly from Newgen, MMI's closest analogue among the three (enterprise software selling into government and large-enterprise accounts, similarly cautious about large-deal timing). On the two questions closest to balance-sheet execution — receivables/DSO discipline and multi-year target-timing confidence — peer evidence actively contradicts MMI, with both Newgen and Nucleus showing far milder working-capital deterioration and RateGain showing rising (not falling) guidance confidence on its own multi-year target. Symmetrically stated: the peer set supports MMI's external-market narrative (client-side deferral, AI-driven decision friction are real, sector-visible phenomena in FY26) while undercutting MMI's execution narrative (the receivables build and the target-timing hedge look more like company-specific weaknesses than industry-wide conditions). Both reads should carry forward to synthesis with equal weight; neither should be allowed to silently dominate the other.
