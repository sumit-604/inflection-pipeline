## Business Understanding Narrative

**What does it do, and how does it make money?** Qualitek sells testing and
inspection. A customer sends a sample. Qualitek runs the test in an accredited
laboratory and sells the certificate. It reports one segment, "Testing and
Inspection Services", and one geography, within India (Outcome of Board Meeting,
FY2026, Q4, page 13). The company runs ten owned laboratories and six
public-private-partnership laboratories, sixteen in all (Press Release, FY2027,
Q1, page 4 series). Segments served are pharmaceuticals, food and feed,
transport, defence and mechanical.

**What is changing?** Scale, by purchase. Qualitek bought Interstellar Testing
Centre and Quality and Testing Infosolution in September 2024 (Outcome of Board
Meeting, FY2026, Q4, page 13). Consolidated revenue then went from Rs 29.18
crore in FY24 to Rs 70.23 crore in FY25 to Rs 124.52 crore in FY26, a four-fold
build in two years (Press Release / Media Release, FY2027, Q1, page 2). In FY26
it added a food laboratory in Mumbai, a USFDA-approved pharmaceutical laboratory
in Bengaluru, a second Bhubaneswar laboratory, and capacity at Panchkula and
Chennai.

**Why now?** The margin moved with the mix, not against it. Consolidated EBITDA
margin went to 24% in FY26 from 23% in FY25, and PAT margin to 12% from 11%,
while revenue grew 77% (Investor Presentation, FY2027, Q2, page 10). Acquired
scale usually dilutes margin first. Here it did not.

**What must be true for the thesis to work?** Management must convert capacity
into profit at the rate it claims. It guides FY27 to 35-40% revenue growth,
EBITDA margin of 25-26%, and PAT margin of 15-17% (Investor Presentation,
FY2027, Q2, page 25 series). PAT margin today is 12%. The claim is a 300 to 500
basis point step up in one year.

**What breaks it?** The balance sheet. Standalone long-term borrowings went from
Rs 19.16 crore to Rs 48.76 crore in FY26, short-term from Rs 9.06 crore to Rs
16.24 crore (Outcome of Board Meeting, FY2026, Q4, page 14). Operating cash was
Rs 7.15 crore. Investing outflow was Rs 38.72 crore. Financing brought in Rs
31.89 crore. The expansion is debt-funded, and the plan is another Rs 63 crore
of capex in FY27 against FY26 profit of Rs 14.6 crore.

## 2. Business model and archetype

One economic engine: accredited capacity sold by the test. Revenue is the number
of samples multiplied by price per test. Cost is the laboratory, the instrument,
and the analyst, mostly fixed. So utilisation drives margin, and a new
laboratory dilutes until it fills.

Standalone FY26 revenue was Rs 67.60 crore against consolidated Rs 124.52 crore
(Investor Presentation, FY2027, Q2, pages 10 and 18). The subsidiaries are now
46% of the group. Direct expenses on testing material were Rs 5.78 crore against
standalone revenue of Rs 29.18 crore in FY24 (Reg. 34(1) Annual Report, FY2024,
Q4, page 103), so materials are under 20% of cost. This is a fixed-cost
utilisation business, not a converter.

**Archetype: outsourcing partner.** The variables that bind are capacity fill,
contract stickiness, accreditation scope, and price per test.

## 3. Competitive advantages

Forming, and the moat is regulatory rather than commercial. A laboratory cannot
sell a certificate a regulator will accept without accreditation, and
accreditation takes time. Qualitek obtained NABL accreditation for its second
Bhubaneswar laboratory with effect from 2 October 2025 (Outcome of Board
Meeting, FY2026, Q4, page 13) and holds a USFDA-approved pharmaceutical
laboratory in Bengaluru. USFDA approval is the highest barrier on this list and
the hardest to replicate.

Against that, the customer can switch to any other accredited laboratory at the
next sample. There is no contracted volume in this corpus. Pricing power is
unproven: the corpus nowhere states price per test. Call it a forming moat
resting on accreditation scope, not on customer capture.

## 6. The transition and the quality ladder

FROM R2 COST-ADVANTAGED CONVERTER. TO R3 VALUE-ADDED / SPEC'D SUPPLIER.

The mechanism is accreditation scope. A general materials-testing laboratory
competes on turnaround and price, which is R2. A USFDA-approved pharmaceutical
laboratory and an NABL food laboratory sell a certificate the customer cannot
get elsewhere quickly, which is spec-in with switching cost, R3.

Management's claim, quoted once: "Deliver 35-40% revenue growth, driven by
geographic expansion and new segment additions" and "Achieve PAT margins of
15-17%, underpinned by improved profitability across core business segments"
(Investor Presentation, FY2027, Q2, page 25).

One rung per two to three years is the base rate. A single rung is claimed here,
which is credible. The long-term line, "Be One of the Leading Global lab by
2031-32", is not a rung claim and is not treated as one.

## 11. Posture: the Transition Decision Matrix

- **Proof gate: FIRED.** H2 FY26 delivered 56% revenue growth with margin

- **Ugliness: ARTIFACT-OF-CLIMB.** The ugly optics are rising debt, thin cash
  and stretching receivables. All three are what a laboratory chain building

- **Recognition gap: OPEN on the disclosed numbers.** Market capitalisation of
  Rs 651.93 crore against FY26 group profit of Rs 14.6 crore is 44.7 times

  not the guided one.

**Posture: RE-RATING LIVE.** The climb is proven for one period, the ugliness is
build-phase, and the multiple has not paid for the guided margin step.

The honest qualification: the gap reads OPEN only because the guidance is
believed. On delivered FY26 numbers alone, 44.7x is close to fair, and the cell
would be EARNINGS-ONLY.

## 12. Verdict card

**PROCEED to `/step1`, with caveats.**

Load-bearing facts a deep run must verify first:
1. The **consolidated** balance sheet and cash flow for FY26. The parent's is
   held; the group's is not. Group leverage and group cash conversion are the
   two numbers that decide this name.
2. **Whether the PAT margin actually stepped to 15-17%** in H1 FY27. That half
   ended 30 September 2026 and reports in November.
3. The **credit-rating rationale**, if one exists. An agency holding
   management's numbers is the cheapest check on the debt-funded plan.
4. **Receivable ageing.** 67% growth against 47% revenue growth is the single
   number that would turn this from a build into a working-capital problem.

What would change the view. A H1 FY27 PAT margin below 12% with debt still
rising moves this to DEEP WATCH: the guided margin would then be narrative. A
consolidated net-debt-to-EBITDA above 2.5x would do the same. Conversely, PAT
margin at or above 15% with receivable days flat makes this the best name in the
run.

No price. No position.
