## NEW FINDINGS, IN NOTE ORDER

### A. Consolidated Note 47 — profit attribution by entity (NOT extracted in Pass 1 at the entity level)
Pass 1 flagged the 12.2% consolidated PAT decline as its top finding but did not break the decline down by entity. Note 47's "Statement of net assets and profit or loss attributable to owners and non-controlling interest" (p.326-327) does this directly:
- **Shyam Sel and Power Limited (SSPL), the wholly-owned flagship operating subsidiary**: profit contribution to consolidated P&L fell from ₹722.34 cr (70.20% of consol P&L, FY24) to ₹417.15 cr (45.88%, FY25) — a **−42.2% decline in absolute profit contribution**, even as SSPL's net assets grew +27.4% (₹4,488.07 cr → ₹5,716.82 cr) and even as SSPL was the primary recipient of FY25 parent capital (₹800 cr OCD + carry-forward of ₹597 cr FY24 QIP proceeds, per Pass 1 section 2/6). 🔴 Red Flag — this is the single most important new number in the notes set: it identifies and quantifies WHERE the consolidated PAT decline actually occurred (the core subsidiary, not just the small loss-making units flagged in Pass 1), and shows profitability deteriorating even as capital is being pumped in.
- **Shyam Energy Limited** (power-generation subsidiary): near-nil P&L profit contribution (₹(0.03) cr, FY25) but a disproportionate **₹76.30 cr of consolidated Other Comprehensive Income (58.3% of Group OCI)** — an unusual, OCI-heavy result from an operating subsidiary with no P&L explanation visible in the notes. 🟡 Watch — worth a management question on the source (likely a fair value gain on an investment or hedge held within the entity, but not disclosed).
- Non-controlling interest (NCI) held 6.43% of consolidated net assets but only 0.13% of consolidated P&L in FY25 (vs 6.51%/−1.98% FY24) — consistent with the pattern that minority-owned entities (chiefly 60%-owned Ramsarup) are barely profitable relative to their asset base.

### B. Consolidated Auditor's Report / CARO Annexure — Group-wide cash-loss pattern (materially understated in Pass 1)
The consolidated Independent Auditor's Report's CARO qualification summary table (p.260) lists 13 Group entities and the clause under which each was qualified. Pass 1 named only two entities with CARO issues (Ramsarup, Shree Venkateshwara Electrocast). The full table shows:
- **11 of the 13 entities carry a clause 3(xvii) qualification** ("cash losses in the current or immediately preceding financial year"): Shyam Energy Ltd, Shree Venkateshwara Electroplast/Electrocast Pvt Ltd (also 3(xix), going-concern-adjacent), Meadow Housing Pvt Ltd, Nirjhar Commodities Pvt Ltd, Whispering Developers Pvt Ltd, Shree Sikhar Iron & Steel Pvt Ltd, SMEL Steel Structural Pvt Ltd, S.S. Natural Resources Pvt Ltd, Ramsarup Industries Ltd (also 3(i)(c)), Kolhan Complex Pvt Ltd (associate), and Kalinga Energy & Power Ltd (JV).
- Only the Holding Company (SMEL) and SSPL are exempt from clause 3(xvii); both instead carry 3(i)(c) [title deeds not in the company's name] and 3(vii)(a) [statutory dues] qualifications.
- **SSPL itself carries the same 3(i)(c) and 3(vii)(a) qualifications as the parent** — meaning the flagship, most-heavily-capitalised subsidiary has its own unresolved title-deed and statutory-dues issues, a fact Pass 1 did not surface (Pass 1's title-deed discussion was standalone-only).
🔴 Red Flag — this is a systemic, Group-wide cash-loss pattern (84.6% of listed entities), far broader than the single aluminium-foil unit Pass 1 identified, and it directly corroborates and explains the rising unrecognised DTA (₹955.21 cr gross, Pass 1 finding #2) and the SSPL profit decline above.
Anchor: Consolidated Independent Auditor's Report, "Report on Other Legal and Regulatory Requirements," clause 3, CARO qualification table, p.260.

### C. Note 7(a)/7 standalone and consolidated + Note 18(e)/(f) — circular cross-holding with promoter entities (missed entirely in Pass 1)
Note 7 (Investments, both standalone p.220 and consolidated p.289) discloses unquoted equity investments (FVOCI) in four/five private companies: Dorite Tracon Private Limited, Narantak Dealcomm Limited, Subhlabh Commercials Private Limited, Shubham/Subham Capital Private Limited (plus, at consol level only, a small ₹0.03 cr stake in Karva Automart Limited). Total carrying value ₹352.31 cr (consolidated, FY25, up from ₹218.65 cr FY24, +61.1%) / ₹253.05 cr (standalone, FY25, up from ₹218.65 cr — standalone/consol nearly identical, confirming the holding sits almost entirely at parent level with minor group additions).
Cross-checking against Note 18(e)/(f) (Shareholding pattern, p.296-297): **Dorite Tracon Private Limited (5.09%), Narantak Dealcomm Limited (15.48%), and Subham Capital Private Limited (14.61%) are themselves major PROMOTER shareholders of SMEL**, together holding 35.18% of the Company's own paid-up equity (and, with Subham Buildwell 21.86% and Kalpataru Housefin 7.67%, the full promoter group holds ~64.7%).
**This is a circular/cross-holding structure: SMEL owns a ₹352.31 cr equity stake in the very promoter holding companies that in turn own ~35% of SMEL.** Fair value gains on this portfolio flow through OCI, not P&L (+₹124.66 cr consol OCI gain FY25 per Note 19(g)/29), building book net worth via a related-party fair-value mechanism rather than operating earnings. Note 42's related-party list (p.315) does formally name these entities as "enterprises over which KMP are able to exercise... significant influence" and the FY24 RPT table shows a "purchase of investments" line of ₹241.39 cr that reconciles to the FY24 build in this portfolio (so the transaction itself is technically disclosed) — but its structural significance (the Company investing in its own controlling shareholders) was not commented on anywhere in the notes, and Pass 1 did not identify it.
🔴 Red Flag — new; a governance/related-party-fairness item with no precedent commentary in the AR.
Anchor: Note 7(a) standalone p.220, Note 7(a) consolidated p.289; Note 18(e)/(f) p.296-297; Note 42 p.315, p.317 (RPT "purchase of investments" line).

### D. Consolidated Independent Auditor's Report — audit-scope and internal-controls language sharper than Pass 1 characterized
- **Server backup compliance**: Pass 1 characterized this as an unresolved uncertainty. The actual "Report on Other Legal and Regulatory Requirements" (p.258) draws a clear distinction: for the Holding Company and ONE subsidiary, the auditors state they are "unable to comment" on whether backups were kept on India-located servers (an evidence gap); but for OTHER (unnamed, plural) subsidiaries, the auditors make an **affirmative finding that backup of books of account "has NOT been kept in servers physically located in India on a daily basis"** — a confirmed compliance failure under the Companies (Accounts) Rules, not merely an open question. 🔴 Red Flag — upgrade of a Pass 1 item; materially worse than previously stated.
- **Internal audit reports unavailable**: standalone CARO Annexure B, clause (xiv)(b) states the statutory auditor "were unable to obtain some of the internal audit reports of the Company for the quarter ended 31 March 2025," and considered internal audit only to the extent reports were made available. This is a distinct, previously unflagged audit-scope limitation. 🔴 Red Flag — new.
- **Whistle-blower complaints**: standalone CARO Annexure B states the auditors "have taken into consideration the whistle blower complaints received by the Company during the year while determining nature, timing and extent of audit procedures" — confirming whistle-blower complaints were received in FY25. Nature, number, and resolution are not disclosed anywhere else in the AR. 🟡 Watch — new, warrants a direct management question.
- **Additional unaudited/non-principal-audited perimeter**: beyond the previously-flagged unaudited DMCC subsidiary, the consolidated auditor's report separately discloses (p.256-257) that the Group's share of net profit (₹1.72 cr, FY25) of TWO associates and ONE joint venture was audited by OTHER (non-principal) auditors, and that a SEPARATE joint venture's financial information is entirely UNAUDITED and management-furnished (share of profit ₹Nil, judged "not material" by the principal auditor). 🟡 Watch — new, widens the previously-identified single-entity issue.

### E. Legacy statutory dues and cross-note numerical discrepancies (new)
- **Water Cess**: standalone CARO Annexure B discloses ₹12.03 cr owed to the Department of Water Resources, Government of Odisha, relating to the period September 2006 to April 2022, undisputed and "immediately due," still UNPAID as of the FY25 balance sheet date. This ties to a balance-sheet "Liability for water charges" of ₹12.31 cr (FY25) / ₹12.03 cr (FY24) at Note 25 (consolidated, p.305) / equivalent standalone note. 🟡 Watch — new; an undisputed legacy statutory liability outstanding for close to two decades.
- **Subsidy claim receivable — figure mismatch**: the consolidated Key Audit Matter narrative (Independent Auditor's Report, p.255) states the West Bengal government subsidy claim receivable is **₹41.83 cr (gross)**, while Note 16 to the consolidated financial statements (p.294) discloses the SAME claim at **₹57.16 cr (gross)** — an unreconciled ₹15.33 cr gap between the KAM text and the note both dated the same balance sheet date. 🟡 Watch — new cross-reference discrepancy; Pass 1 extracted only the Note 16 figure and did not check it against the KAM narrative.
- **Electricity duty dispute — figure mismatch**: standalone CARO Annexure B discloses the disputed electricity-duty demand as "amount demanded ₹65.74 cr, amount paid ₹6.57 cr" (net exposure ₹59.17 cr), while Note 41(c) contingent liabilities (both standalone and consolidated) states the same dispute, net of the ₹6.57 cr protest payment, at ₹54.17 cr — a ₹5.00 cr unreconciled gap between the two disclosures of the same item. 🟡 Watch — new cross-reference discrepancy.
- **Undisclosed guarantee beneficiaries**: Note 7(e) (Investments pledged as lien for corporate guarantees, p.290) shows two guarantee lines released during FY25 — "Andhra Minmet Private Limited" (₹5 cr) and "Glowing Realty Private Limited" (₹5 cr) — neither of which appears anywhere in the Note 42 related-party list or the Note 46 subsidiary list. The Company extended (and has now released) guarantees to entities outside its formally disclosed related-party/subsidiary universe. 🟡 Watch — new, disclosure-completeness question.

### F. Standalone-vs-consolidated divergence, sharpened (Pass 1 flagged the consolidated numbers only; standalone-level comparison is new)
- **Unhedged FX exposure**: standalone unhedged net foreign currency exposure DECLINED 51.8% (₹430.03 cr FY24 → ₹207.16 cr FY25, Note 43(A)(b) standalone, p.247), even as the consolidated Group figure Pass 1 flagged ROSE 33% (₹1,076.12 cr → ₹1,433.25 cr, Note 43(A)(b) consol, p.320-321). This confirms the FX risk build-up is concentrated almost entirely at subsidiary level (chiefly SSPL and the import-heavy capex programme), not the parent. 🟡 Watch — new, sharpens a Pass 1 item.
- **Gearing**: standalone net debt/equity gearing rose only modestly, from 9.31% to 10.46% (Note 44 standalone, p.249; net debt ₹571.47 cr → ₹699.33 cr), versus the consolidated Group's 14.80% → 21.08% rise Pass 1 flagged (Note 44 consol, p.323). Confirms the leverage build-up is a subsidiary-level, not parent-level, phenomenon. 🟡 Watch — new, sharpens a Pass 1 item.
- **Treasury/investment income as a driver of PBT growth**: standalone Other Income grew 49.7% (₹96.40 cr → ₹144.32 cr, standalone P&L note), driven by fair-value gains on FVTPL investments (per cash flow statement reconciliation, +63%, ₹31.25 cr → ₹50.97 cr) and interest income on amortised-cost financial assets (+47.6%); together these ~93% of the Other Income increase, and this increase alone represents roughly 23% of the ₹204.42 cr standalone PBT increase (₹456.04 cr → ₹660.46 cr). Standalone Note 45(e) explicitly attributes the ratio's Return-on-Investment jump (8.36% vs 6.25%, +34% variance) to "increased treasury related activities during the year." A parallel pattern exists at consolidated level: Other Income +45.1% (₹158.98 cr → ₹230.63 cr), with fair-value gains on FVTPL investments +36.6% (₹67.95 cr → ₹92.80 cr per cash flow statement) and interest income on amortised-cost assets +69.7% (₹73.86 cr → ₹125.36 cr). 🟡 Watch — new; a quality-of-earnings nuance (PBT growth is meaningfully non-operating/treasury-driven, not purely steel-cycle driven) that Pass 1 did not isolate.

### G. Capex execution — CWIP ageing (new)
Consolidated Note 4 (Capital work-in-progress, p.287) discloses a CWIP ageing schedule: of ₹2,708.41 cr total CWIP at FY25-end, **₹1,134.31 cr (41.9%) has been under construction for MORE THAN 3 YEARS**, even though the note separately states no projects are "overdue" or "temporarily suspended" relative to original plan. This is a large proportion of long-aged capex still incomplete, worth monitoring against management's implied capex-completion/deleveraging timeline (relevant given the gearing trajectory flagged in Pass 1 and above). 🟡 Watch — new.

### H. Note 53 — restatement/regrouping note is boilerplate with no substantive detail (new)
Consolidated Note 53 (p.330) reads in full: "Figures of previous years have been regrouped/rearranged/rectified wherever necessary to make them comparable with the current periods figures." This is the entire disclosure — no indication of WHAT was regrouped, in which line items, or the quantum involved, which precludes independent assessment of prior-year comparability. Standard boilerplate, but genuinely uninformative given the magnitude of YoY swings elsewhere in the notes (e.g., the FY24 tax-recognition tailwind, the SSPL profit decline). 🟡 Watch — new; triggered by the Pass 2 "restated"/"rectified" keyword screen.
