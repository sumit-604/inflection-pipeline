### 2C. Tax and revenue — **the one live regulatory action found**

VERIFIED (announcements/SSWL-showcause_2026-06-17.pdf, Reg 30 filing):
a **Show Cause Notice dated 16-Jun-2026** was received from the Office
of the Principal Commissioner of Customs, Mundra Port, Kutch, alleging
that SSWL violated Section 65 of the Customs Act, 1962, by availing
Duty Drawback of Rs 1.378 Cr and RoDTEP scrips of Rs 0.324 Cr
unlawfully on exports made 09-Nov-2023 to 08-Jul-2025 (an additional
Rs 0.131 Cr in RoDTEP scrips remains unutilised). SSWL had already
repaid the drawback and RoDTEP amounts with applicable interest
**before** the SCN was issued, and states it does not expect a
financial liability, though penalty is alleged under Customs Act
Sections 113, 114(iii), 114AA and 117, unquantified. This is a
compliance/procedural matter, small in quantum (~Rs 1.84 Cr against
Rs 5,183 Cr FY26 consolidated revenue), self-corrected before the
notice arrived.

**⚠️ Governance finding, not a legal finding**: the SCN (16-Jun-2026)
predates the AR's own signing date (03-Sep-2026) by 2.5 months, yet
does not appear anywhere in the AR — no contingent liability note, no
subsequent-events disclosure, no litigation mention; the Board's Report
and CARO both affirmatively state no pending/material litigation
(cross-referenced from B02/B03 FLAG-MISSING-DISCLOSURE; confirmed by
full-text search of the AR finding no match on "show cause" or
"Mundra"). The underlying matter is minor and self-remediated; the
omission from a signed statutory document is the more relevant finding
for promoter-quality purposes (accuracy of representations to
shareholders).

No DRI, SFIO, benami, or transfer-pricing action was found.

### 2D. Other regulators

### 2E. Civil litigation

VERIFIED via web search and WebFetch of the judgment text: **Steel
Strips Wheels Ltd vs Shri Avil Menezes (Resolution Professional of AMW
Autocomponent Ltd) & Ors., NCLAT New Delhi.** SSWL's Rs 138.15 Cr
resolution plan for AMW Autocomponent (Bhuj) received 98.55% Committee
of Creditors approval on 26-Aug-2021; after CIRP expiry, the
Adjudicating Authority irregularly allowed a late, non-compliant
competing plan to be considered. SSWL appealed and NCLAT **set aside**
that order, holding that a CoC-approved plan cannot be reopened after
CIRP expiry. **No adverse finding against SSWL's conduct** — the
ruling protected SSWL's legitimately won plan from a procedural
irregularity by the tribunal. This is the correct legal record behind
LBF4 (AMW acquisition); it corroborates the AR's own account of the
Rs 138 Cr consideration and confirms no litigation taint on the
acquisition itself.

No family-partition suit, JV dispute, or defamation action was found.

## SECTION 3: BUSINESS CONDUCT & ETHICAL TRACK RECORD

### 3A. RPT history

VERIFIED (Note 41, standalone AR pp.170-172; Board's Report AR p.29):
FY26 standalone RPT total Rs 54.96 Cr (FY25: Rs 58.86 Cr), split across
KMP remuneration, dividends, and enterprise transactions. Specific
items of note:

- **Rent paid to a promoter-group enterprise rose 3.5x YoY**: Rs 42.15
  lakh (FY25) to Rs 147.44 lakh (FY26) — the largest single-year jump
  in the RPT table (Note 41). The AR's own column grouping does not
  cleanly resolve which named entity received it (table extraction
  ambiguity in the source PDF between the "Enterprises — significant
  influence" and "Others" columns); flagged for management-question
  follow-up.
- **Loan to subsidiary (AMW Autocomponent Ltd)**: Rs 133.15 Cr
  outstanding, unchanged for two years, interest-bearing (interest
  received Rs 8.71 Cr FY26, accrued interest receivable Rs 8.27 Cr) to
  a subsidiary that has posted losses every year since acquisition —
  already the central item in B02/B03's AMW cluster (Rs 59.31 Cr DTA,
  adverse CARO land-title remark, zero KAM). This is the single
  largest RPT exposure and the one requiring closest position-sizing
  discipline (per B03's analyst note).
- **CSR contribution routed through a group enterprise**: Rs 315.00
  lakh of the Rs 634.95 lakh FY26 CSR spend went to "Enterprises over
  which KMP are able to exercise significant influence" (Note 41). This
  is disclosed and audit-committee-approved, but it means roughly half
  the CSR budget is deployed via a promoter-linked vehicle rather than
  directly or via an independent CSR implementing agency — a mild
  transparency point, not evidence of diversion (no forensic basis in
  this corpus to allege otherwise).
- Purchase of power from associate Clean Max Astria (Rs 5.84 Cr) and
  from "Others" — captive renewable-power arrangement, standard for
  the sector, arm's length per the Board's affirmation.
- No corporate guarantees for private promoter entities were found in
  Note 41.
- The Board's Report affirms all RPTs were at arm's length, in the
  ordinary course, Audit-Committee pre-approved (omnibus + specific),
  with **no material RPT requiring Form AOC-2** (p.29).

**Rating: ⚠️** — not evidence of self-dealing, but the AMW loan/DTA
concentration and the rent jump both warrant management follow-up
before treating the RPT book as fully clean.
