## Business Understanding Narrative

**What does it do, and how does it make money?** Lohia makes the machines
that make woven plastic sacks: tape extrusion lines, circular looms, winders,
coating and lamination lines and bag conversion machines. Its customers make
cement, fertiliser and food-grain bags and bulk containers (RHP page 207).
Circular looms were 33.28% of FY26 revenue and tape lines 20.30% (RHP page
212). Spare parts were 11.34%, an annuity on an installed base of over
101,452 looms (RHP pages 207 and 212). The money is machine margin plus the
aftermarket.

**What is changing?** Margin and ownership. EBITDA margin rose from 9.03% in
FY24 to 19.53% in FY26 (RHP page 209). The business was demerged from the
old Lohia Corp into this company, effective 1-May-2024 (RHP page 206), and
listed through a pure offer for sale on 30-Jul-2026 (Q1 FY27 results, page 6).

**Why now?** The order book. It stood at Rs 1,778.2 crore on 30-Jun-2026,
against Rs 909.7 crore a year earlier (Q1 FY27 deck, page 34). Management
says the book executes in 6 to 9 months (same page).

**What must be true?** The margin gain must be structural, not the top of a
capital-goods cycle. This is the load-bearing question. Step 6 sets it out.

**What breaks it?** The cycle itself. The demerged business earned Rs 1,847.01
crore of revenue in FY23 and Rs 1,165.81 crore in FY24 (DRHP page 56; RHP
page 209). A 37% revenue drop in one year is the history this card must
respect.

## 3. Competitive advantages

Present, with one caveat. **Issuer claim:** 15.4% of the global woven raffia
machinery market and 40.7% of India's by value (RHP page 207). The filed
evidence behind it: 71 Indian and 56 foreign patents (RHP page 208), sales to
about 100 countries (RHP page 208), and an installed base that buys spares.
Scale in a niche this small (US$ 1,008 million globally in 2024, RHP page
211) is a barrier. The Indian rivals named in the RHP had revenue of Rs 72.49
crore and Rs 53.16 crore in FY24 (RHP page 205).

The caveat: the business began in 1981 as a joint venture with Starlinger of
Austria (RHP page 208). Starlinger is now the global peer (RHP page 211).
Moat: present in India on share and aftermarket; contested abroad.

## 6. The transition and the quality ladder

FROM R3, a spec'd capital-equipment supplier with switching cost and spares.
TO R4, the share-of-wallet leader whose aftermarket and scale earn 25% to 30%
ROCE through a cycle.

Management's claim, quoted once: "Over the years, we have evolved from a
manufacturer of circular looms to a provider of end-to-end solutions ranging
from prototyping, development, machining, manufacturing, assembly, testing,
training and after-sales service" (RHP page 212).

**The honest reading is two readings.** ROCE went from 10.45% to 40.92% in
two years (RHP page 209). The quality ladder says a one-rung move takes two
to three years, so a move this fast is either a cycle or a real change in
cost and mix. The market data leans cycle. The India market was US$ 248
million in FY22, US$ 123 million in FY24 and US$ 150 million in FY25 (RHP
page 211, issuer claim). FY26 revenue is still below FY23. The observation
that separates the readings is **FY23 EBITDA margin**, a higher-revenue year.
It is NOT FOUND. If FY23 margin was well below 19.5%, the gain is structural.

## 8. Proof check

The triggers claim demand and margin. Both show. Q1 FY27 revenue rose 59.5%
year on year and EBITDA margin held at 19.9% against 20.2% in Q4 FY26 (Q1
FY27 deck, page 34). The book grew another Rs 419.7 crore in the quarter
after Rs 503.02 crore of revenue was shipped. That is book-to-bill above 1.8
in the quarter.

One caution. Q1 FY26 comparatives were "not ... subject to limited review or
audit" (Q1 FY27 results, page 6). The 59.5% growth rate rests on an
unreviewed base.

## 11. Posture: the Transition Decision Matrix

- **Proof gate: FIRED.** Margin held into Q1 FY27 and the book nearly doubled.
- **Ugliness: ARTIFACT-OF-CLIMB.** Leesona losses and demerger-era
  related-party flows are the ugly optics. Both are small against Rs 193.45
  crore of PAT.
- **Recognition gap: CLOSED.** Price basis, not a corpus document: Bull AI
  company record, market capitalisation Rs 6,289.34 crore. That is 32.5 times
  FY26 PAT and 20.9 to 23.7 times FY27E. The R4 neighbourhood is about
  21 times.

**Posture: EARNINGS-ONLY.** The climb is real on current numbers and already
priced near R4. Returns ride EPS growth. Whether that growth lasts beyond one
upcycle is the open question.

## 12. Verdict card

**PROCEED to `/step1`.**

The evidence is strong for a new listing: a filed order book that nearly
doubled, a quarter that held margin, net debt at 0.36 times EBITDA and cash
conversion above 1.6 times PAT. The question a deep run must answer is cycle
against structure. It can be answered.

**Load-bearing facts a `/step1` must verify first**
1. FY23 EBITDA margin of the demerged business.
2. Order inflow by quarter and cancellations since June 2025.
3. Leesona turnaround plan, or closure.
4. FY26 related-party transactions as a share of revenue.
5. Customer concentration and the export order mix.

**What would change the view**
- FY23 margin near 19% makes the gain structural and strengthens the case.
- An order book below Rs 1,358.52 crore at 30-Sep-2026 signals the cycle has
  turned.

No Role 1 valuation was run and no target price is set.
