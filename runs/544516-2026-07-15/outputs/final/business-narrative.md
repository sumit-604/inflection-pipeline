Rail components maker grows, cash conversion worsens

Airfloa Rail Technology, formerly Airflow Equipments India, is a Chennai maker of rail rolling stock interior components and turnkey coach interiors: AC ducting, lighting, seating, doors and blinds, sold to Indian Railways production units and metro projects. It also runs a smaller aeronautical and defence precision parts line. The shares listed on the BSE SME platform in September 2025, so the public record runs two years, FY25 and FY26. FY26 revenue was Rs 320 Cr, up 66%, PAT was Rs 39 Cr at a 12.2% margin, and EBITDA was Rs 64 Cr at 20.1%. One customer, Integral Coach Factory, was 53.6% of FY25 sales, the top five were 82%, and the top ten were 93%.

The transition is a push out of pure rail interiors toward defence and aerospace. Management wants a 50:50 defence to railways mix within two years, is stepping R&D from 4% of sales to 8 to 9%, and has committed Rs 25 Cr to a Big Bang Boom Solutions JV in electronic warfare and AI platforms. It has a Janatics door systems MOU targeting Rs 95 Cr over FY27 and FY28, and a new 14 acre facility on the plan. Defence and aerospace sit at roughly 6% of the order book against that 50:50 ambition, and the JV is not yet incorporated.

Gate 0 scored the company AVERAGE at 76 of 160, core 47 of 100, moat 15 of 60. Three deal breakers set that ceiling: the cash sub block scored 0 of 20, cumulative operating cash flow to PAT ran negative 0.93 times across the two years, and the listing history is under three years. The forward moat scan came back MODEST at 14.5, and execution scored a documented negative, so the combined read stays AVERAGE rather than upgrading.

Cash is the central problem and it moved the wrong way after listing. Standalone operating cash flow was negative Rs 4.4 Cr in FY25 and negative Rs 57.5 Cr in FY26. Trade receivables absorbed Rs 86 Cr in FY26 alone, more than the entire Rs 59 Cr the IPO earmarked for working capital, and that allocation was spent without fixing the conversion. Receivables older than six months rose from 15.95% to 23.38% across three years with zero provisioning for doubtful debt at any point. Debt service coverage sat below 1.0 times in every disclosed year, which predates the growth surge.

Management delivered on the top line and walked back most of the rest. FY26 revenue of Rs 320 Cr beat the ">Rs 300 Cr" promise. Against that, the Rs 1,000 Cr order book target for March 2026 landed at Rs 469 Cr, FY27 revenue guidance was cut from Rs 550 Cr to Rs 500 Cr before the year began, the defence mix is running at 6% against 50%, and the JV incorporation has slipped twice. Only 21% of the IPO machinery capex was spent by FY26 end, delayed by a China export control rule on the equipment. The record is one promise delivered, four partial, three missed, which grades a C.

Peers cut both ways. The company blamed a sector wide steel inflation squeeze for its margin falling from 25% to 20.1%, but a direct listed peer, FRONTSP, expanded margin 533 basis points to 26.80% in the same steel window through vendor negotiation and tender selection, so the sector wide framing does not hold. On the other side, TEXRAIL disclosed a six year gap between Rs 1,625 Cr of cumulative EBITDA and Rs 50 Cr of operating cash flow, which corroborates that stretched cash conversion is a real structural pattern in this sector, though not a universal one.

The runway is genuinely large. The addressable market is around Rs 8,600 Cr, the three year serviceable slice is Rs 568 Cr against current sales near Rs 320 Cr, and the class reads STRONG. Two caveats sit next to it: management's Rs 16.7 lakh crore headline framing is whole of sector spend, roughly 152 times the real addressable number, and the Rs 568 Cr serviceable figure already runs Rs 118 Cr past the company's own stated near term capacity ceiling.

Governance grades CAUTION rather than clean. Both promoter Managing Directors carry RoC adjudicated penalties from January 2026 for multi year Companies Act laxity, now under appeal, and Rs 38 Cr of company debt is personally guaranteed against family land, one Rs 8.73 Cr parcel of which has unregistered title. Set against that, promoter pledge is zero, no promoter has sold a share since listing, and the independent directors are credentialed, including an ex Integral Coach Factory General Manager who led Vande Bharat and a 35 year Siemens veteran. The evidence base itself is an IPO Prospectus used in place of an annual report, with standalone figures for FY23 to FY25 and consolidated for FY25 only, shareholding and announcement data from operator provided secondary sources, and no credit rating or third party research on file.
