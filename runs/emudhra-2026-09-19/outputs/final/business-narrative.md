# eMudhra Ltd (EMUDHRA): business narrative, phase 1 lite, run 2026-09-19

## BUSINESS UNDERSTANDING NARRATIVE

Trust Services, 20% of FY26 revenue, issues digital signature certificates, eSign and SSL certificates under a government certifying authority licence. A digital signature certificate is a legal online identity, and people need one to sign tax filings, tenders, bank forms and contracts. Enterprise Solutions, 59% of revenue, is owned software for document signing (emSigner), login control (SecurePass) and certificate issue and tracking (CertiNext and emCA). Once a bank or a ministry wires that software into core systems, removal is costly, and 67% of FY26 revenue came from existing customers. Services, 21% of revenue, is consulting from a US bench, and it did not grow in FY26. Individuals and small firms buy certificates mostly through partners, who sell about 70% of volume and keep 45 to 50% of the fee. Enterprise buyers are 1,374 banks, insurers, government bodies and companies, among them a defence agency, a public sector bank and the US InCommon university consortium. The top five customers fell from 32% of revenue in FY24 to 22% in FY26. Demand today comes from rules: the Controller of Certifying Authorities sets the licence model, and the CA/Browser Forum and the browser root stores decide whose certificates a browser trusts. Forward demand rests on dated deadlines: certificate life cut toward 47 days by 2029, post quantum migration in 2029 and 2030, India's DPDP Phase 2 on 13 November 2026 and the EU EUDI Wallet by 31 December 2026. NIS2 and DORA should help Cryptas in Europe, but no peer confirmed that effect, and the UAE regulator TDRA has not yet granted the in country licence. Gartner and IDC spend trackers, and news from DigiCert, Keyfactor, Venafi and Entrust, give an outside check on a category the run sized at about 21% annual growth. The moat sits in Trust Services, where the licence, WebTrust accreditation and browser root listing are hard to copy. Even there, 24 licensed Indian certifying authorities compete on price at the low end. Enterprise Solutions has switching costs but no licence, and it faces DigiCert, Entrust, Microsoft and Okta, which spend more on research than eMudhra's 7 to 8% of revenue. The emerging moat scan scored 22.1, a MODEST reading, led by regulatory tailwinds that every rival shares. The two Family I categories, talent asymmetry and cannibalisation barrier, scored zero for want of evidence. Services has no moat.

Licence business funds a global software bet

eMudhra is an Indian digital identity company. It holds one of 24 Indian certifying authority licences and sells its own security software to banks, governments and companies in more than 35 countries. FY26 revenue was Rs 702 Cr, up 35%, and PAT was Rs 110 Cr, up 26%. International customers gave 64% of revenue. The order book for the software line stood at Rs 238 Cr, up 25%.

The change underway is a move from Indian certificate issuance to owned security software sold abroad. Enterprise Solutions grew 55% in FY26, but only 23 points of that was organic. The other 32 points came from buying Cryptas in Europe and AI Cyberforge in the US, both consolidated from 1 July 2025. The margin story has not arrived yet. The software segment outside India earned 21.9% in FY26, down from 26.0%, while the India segment earned 57.5%. That is dilution, not the operating leverage the thesis needs. Q1 FY27 EBITDA margin of 26.2% is the first better print, but management says lower sales of low margin tokens lifted it and calls about 25% sustainable.

The backward scorecard reads GOOD+ at 74 of 100 core and 91 of 160 overall, with growth at 18 of 20 and the balance sheet at 20 of 20. A verifier reads the same data as GOOD at 69, because the working capital score rests on a two year window. The operator has to rule on that window. Debt is near zero, at 0.03 times equity, and the company holds net cash. Return on capital is weaker, at 13 to 15%, and management called an ROE of 14.5 to 15% close to what it can maintain.

Free cash flow turned negative in FY26, at minus Rs 52.5 Cr against plus Rs 18.4 Cr a year earlier. Operating cash of Rs 133 Cr did not cover Rs 185 Cr of spending on assets and software, which more than doubled. Earnings still turned into cash: operating cash ran 1.2 times profit, and working capital days fell from 81 to 69. The pressure sits elsewhere. Unbilled revenue rose 41% and payables rose 91%, both faster than sales. Management guided FY26 capex at Rs 75 to 80 Cr, and the annual report's own breakdown matches that at Rs 81 Cr. The cash flow statement says Rs 185 Cr, and nobody has reconciled the two. The company memory figure of 420 payable days does not reproduce; the notes give 31 days.

Management delivered on the big numbers and drifted on the rest, which earns a C for credibility. FY26 total income of Rs 713 Cr beat the Rs 700 Cr guide. Trust Services grew 32% against a 22 to 25% guide, though part of that was partner stocking that reversed in Q1 FY27. Q1 FY27 PAT rose 28%, on guide. Against that, organic growth ran near 15% against an 18% guide, the PAT guide narrowed to a flat 25% while called unchanged, and the UAE licence slipped. The Cryptas profit story moved from a small profit to breakeven to "not profitable at all" across three calls without reconciliation.

Governance carries a CAUTION verdict with no deal breaker and no pledge. 3i Infotech alleged fraud over an old disinvestment and claimed more than Rs 128 Cr, about 14% of net worth. The Navi Mumbai Economic Offences Wing closed the complaint as civil on 13 August 2026. eMudhra knew of the allegations from January 2024 and said nothing on the 3 February 2026 earnings call, held the day the complaint was filed. The chairman has a personal investment link to Capital MXT, which bought 5% of 3i Infotech, and he disclosed it only when asked. Two of his sons joined the board from 1 April 2026, taking the family to 4 of 9 seats. The CFO's pay rose 42% against 7% for the median employee. The chairman and a promoter company lent Rs 8.3 Cr to overseas subsidiaries, a first. The annual report leaves the 3i claim out of quantified contingent liabilities and out of its risk section.

No listed Indian peer does what eMudhra does, so the peer check confirmed nothing outright and contradicted nothing outright. Newgen, the closest Middle East and banking software comparison, grew 6% in FY26 and 11% in Q1 FY27, which makes eMudhra's 15 to 18% organic guide look aggressive. Newgen and Protean both confirmed Middle East war delays to orders, the strongest independent support in the run. Protean's eSign Pro competes directly for bank signing work. Quick Heal won a bank consent management deal about a year ahead of eMudhra's PrivaTrust, which is still in pilots.

The market is real but not large. The run sized the total market at Rs 8,250 Cr on a cautious basis and Rs 16,570 Cr on a realistic basis, growing about 21% a year. The part eMudhra can serve is Rs 1,890 Cr, and it already holds about 37% of that, leaving 2.7 times revenue headroom. That makes the runway MODERATE. The serviceable share path implies 25% revenue growth a year, but only if acquisitions continue at the FY26 pace; without them the path falls back toward the 15 to 18% organic guide. Management cites global cybersecurity spend of USD 240 billion, 244 times the run's cautious market size and not a measure of its niche.
