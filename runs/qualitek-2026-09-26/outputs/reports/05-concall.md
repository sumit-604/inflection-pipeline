### 2B. Excuse pattern analysis

No live analyst Q&A exists in this mode, so the "external-blame vs
honest-admission vs deflection" test cannot be run on spoken answers. On
the written record, the dominant pattern is **silence, not excuse**: for
the FY25 revenue miss (on either basis), the Hyderabad slippage, the
EBITDA-margin target's re-dating and eventual drop, and the quiet removal
of three long-term targets between the May-2026 MD&A and the Sep-2026 AR,
management gives no reason at all rather than blaming an external factor or
admitting fault -- each change is simply absent from the next filing. Set
against this, management DID volunteer some negative information without
being asked: the H1 FY25 deck names a 10-point EBITDA-margin hit from
pre-operative costs; the H1 FY26 MD&A names a Rs 3.3 Cr EBITDA loss from
nine new labs; and the FY26 MD&A explains the H2 standalone margin
softening (25.9% vs 28.9% H2 FY25) by named capex/gestation costs. Cycle 1's
line that "no document anywhere acknowledges a miss" was too strong for
these three margin items, which ARE explained; the pattern of silence holds
cleanly for target-level and completion-date misses (Hyderabad, the 30%
target, the three dropped FY31/FY32 targets, the FY25 revenue projection),
but not uniformly for margin softness within a period, which management
does explain when it appears inside a single document's own numbers. The
one candid exception at the strategic level is the FY26 AR chairman's
letter itself: "FY26 was a year of transformation. The next phase will be
about converting that transformation into sustainable, profitable and
enduring growth" (FY26 AR, chairman's letter, p.24) -- an unusually honest
admission that a "transformation" year (heavily M&A-funded, cash-negative)
has not yet proven sustainable or profitable.

`excuse_pattern: "silence-heavy"` -- the record shows omission on
target-level misses far more than either external-blame or
honest-admission, moderated by genuine, unprompted disclosure of
period-level margin softness.

## MANAGEMENT COMMUNICATION HANDOFF SUMMARY

Qualitek's written record over three years shows a company that delivers
on hard, checkable commitments (IPO fund use, promoter loan repayment,
named acquisitions it chooses to complete on time) but goes quiet, rather
than explaining, when a promise slips, a target is re-dated, or a
long-term number is dropped between two filings four months apart. The
rework of this stage corrects the SIZE of one negative finding (the FY25
revenue miss is closer to 5.6% than 22% once the pro forma basis is
accounted for) and the MECHANISM of another (the 30% margin target was
re-dated and then dropped, not diluted into the FY27 guide), while adding
two negative findings the first pass missed entirely: the FY26 AR's quiet
removal of three FY31/FY32 targets set four months earlier, and the
Sep-2026 capital raise that funds "unidentified acquisitions" without
naming the one acquisition (Hyderabad) that has been publicly promised
twice and is now overdue. The single largest disclosure gap at IPO is
narrower than first stated: the DRHP's formal strategy section still
promised organic-only growth, but it did flag the company's first
acquisition target (ITCPL) as a related party with a conflict-of-interest
risk, so that specific acquisition was foreshadowed even though its scale
and the four further acquisitions/vehicles that followed were not. Grade C
holds: genuine delivery evidence exists, but across every correction and
every new finding, it does not outweigh the pattern of silent target
re-dating, dropping, and non-reconciliation, now evidenced by more items
than cycle 1 found, even though the single largest number in that
original case (the 22% revenue miss) has been corrected downward.
