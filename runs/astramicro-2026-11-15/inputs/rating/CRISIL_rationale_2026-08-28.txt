Detailed Rationale

Crisil Ratings has upgraded its rating on the long-term bank facilities and corporate credit rating of Astra Microwave Products Limited (AMPL) to ‘Crisil A+/Stable’ from ‘Crisil A/Positive’ and reaffirmed its ‘Crisil A1’ rating on the short-term bank facilities of the company.

The upgrade reflects the material strengthening in the business risk profile of the company over the past years, supported by increasing scale of operations, significant improvement in revenue visibility, expansion in operating profitability, improving working capital profile and continued strength in the financial risk profile. 

The business profile has strengthened considerably, as reflected in revenue growth of 11% to Rs 1,163 crore in fiscal 2026 and expected to grow 15-18% on year in the near term. The growth is supported by significantly enhanced order book and increasing contribution from higher value-added defence programmes. The company's order book increased to ~Rs 4,341 crore as of August 2026 from Rs 2,141 crore as on March 31, 2026, following the receipt of a large order from Hindustan Aeronautics Ltd (HAL) under the Uttam Radar programme. The order book is predominantly domestic defence, with nearly 84% comprising defence orders (including exports orders), providing strong revenue visibility over the medium term. The operating margin continues to improve, driven by increasing technological capabilities and higher value addition resulting in favourable product mix. Operating margin improved to 29.6% in fiscal 2026 from 25.1% in fiscal 2025. The improvement has been supported by increasing contribution from domestic defence programmes, higher share of complex radar and electronic warfare systems and backward integration initiatives through subsidiaries and joint ventures. The operating margin is expected to remain healthy at 28-30% over the medium term, supported by favourable product mix and increasing contribution from high-margin indigenous defence programmes.

The overall working capital cycle of the company has also improved, which remained historically high. Crisil Ratings-adjusted debtors reduced to ~218 days in fiscal 2026 from 273 days in fiscal 2025, while gross current assets (GCAs) improved to about 429 days in fiscal 2026 from more than 500 days in the previous fiscal. Improvement in collections, inventory management and disciplined approach to working capital management resulted in increased operating cash flow in fiscal 2026. While the working capital cycle will continue to remain elevated due to the nature of long-gestation defence contracts, the sustained improvement in collections provides comfort.

The company is planning to demerge the space, metrology and hydrology businesses from fiscal 2028 into a wholly owned subsidiary, Astra Space Technologies Ltd (ASTL), which will be subsequently listed after the completion of the demerger. It will have limited impact on the current business risk and the financial risk profiles of the company. These businesses contributed ~14% of the total revenue of AMPL in fiscal 2026. The restructuring is expected to increase management’s focus on the core defence business while maintaining strong profitability and credit metrics. The impact on networth and capital structure is expected to remain limited, and the growing defence business is expected to comfortably offset the revenue impact of the demerged businesses through continuous growth and execution of the large defence order book.

The financial risk profile continues to remain healthy, supported by a comfortable capital structure. Total debt in the company dropped to Rs 286 crore as on March 31, 2026, compared to Rs 423 crore as on March 31, 2025 on account of improvement in the working capital cycle and stronger accrual. The decline in debt was the result of improvement in debtors and slight reduction in inventory holding period leading to overall reduction in the short-term debt of the company. Consequently, leverage ratios remained comfortable with gearing and total outside liabilities to tangible networth (TOLTNW) ratio at 0.22 time and 0.5 time, respectively, in fiscal 2026 and expected to be less than 0.5 time and 1 time, respectively, over the medium term. Debt protection metrics improved with interest coverage ratio at 6.57 times in fiscal 2026 compared to 5.15 times in fiscal 2025. It is expected to improve to above 7 times in the near term, driven by growing operating profitability.

Liquidity remains comfortable, supported by Rs 190 crore of unencumbered cash and equivalent as on March 31, 2026, and strong net cash accrual (NCA) of Rs 216 crore in fiscal 2026 which is expected to improve to above Rs 250 crore per annum in the near term, comfortably covering annual debt obligation of Rs 10 crore through fiscal 2029. Annual capital expenditure (capex) of Rs 75-80 crore for the near to medium term will be funded through a prudent mix of debt and equity. Further liquidity support is expected from the proposed warrant conversion, which will result in an equity infusion of Rs 131 crore.

These strengths are partially offset by large working capital requirement, execution risks associated with large defence contracts and inherent risks associated with long-gestation projects.

Analytical Approach

Crisil Ratings has combined the business and financial risk profile of AMPL, Astra Defense Technologies Pvt Ltd, Aelius Semiconductors Pte Ltd (ASPL) and Astra Space Technologies Ltd (ASTL). These entities, collectively referred to as the AMPL group, have common ownership and management and strong business synergies. Also, Crisil Ratings has moderately consolidated the joint venture, Astra Rafael Comsys Pvt Ltd, owing to operational and financial linkages

Key Rating Drivers - Strengths

 Improving market position in the defence segment, supported by strong in-house technical capabilities, reputed clientele and growth prospects for the sector 

AMPL has strengthened its business profile over the last few years by transitioning from a supplier of microwave and radio frequency (RF) components to a strategic defence player with participation across critical indigenous defence programmes. The company is now involved in several marquee programmes including Uttam AESA Radar, Virupaksha Radar, Angad Electronic Warfare Systems, Software Defined Radios (SDR), QRSAM, AMCA and AEW&C platforms. The company possesses strong in-house capabilities across radar technologies, electronic warfare systems, SDR communication systems, microwave and RF applications and semiconductor design capabilities through its subsidiary Aelius Semiconductors. Through its joint venture ARC, the company has also strengthened its presence in tactical communication and defence communication systems.

AMPL enjoys longstanding relationships with key customers such as Hindustan Aeronautics Ltd (HAL), Bharat Electronics Ltd (BEL), Defence Research and Development Organization (DRDO), Indian Space Research Organization (ISRO) and other defence establishments. The proposed demerger of the space, meteorology and hydrology businesses is expected to increase management focus on the core defence business. 

 Healthy order book and revenue visibility supporting growth prospects

The company benefits from a strong order pipeline and healthy execution visibility. The order book increased to ~Rs 4,341 crore as of August 2026, supported by Rs 2,205 crore Uttam Radar order from HAL for supply of critical subsystems under the indigenous radar programme. Defence-related orders account for 84% of the total order book (including export orders) as in August 2026, providing strong revenue visibility over the next several years.

The order book is expected to be further supported by Virupaksha Radar, Angad EW Systems, anti-drone systems, indigenous radar products and electronic warfare programme of DRDO for which confirmed orders are expected to be received in the next few months.  

Revenue grew 11% in fiscal 2026 to Rs 1,163 crore and is expected to grow 15-18% in fiscal 2027 driven by domestic orders from the defence segment. The defence segment (including exports) contributed ~86% of the total revenue in fiscal 2026 and will contribute 80-85% of the total revenue in fiscal 2027. Beyond fiscal 2027, post the demerger of the other businesses, revenue will be generated from the defence segment. 

The strong order position and healthy pipeline support sustained growth despite the proposed demerger of the non-defence businesses.

 Comfortable financial risk profile

The financial risk profile remains healthy, as reflected in comfortable capital structure and debt protection metrics. Total debt in the company reduced to Rs 286 crore as on March 31, 2026, compared to Rs 423 crore in the previous fiscal 2025, on account of improvement in the working capital cycle and stronger accrual. The decline in debt was the result of improvement in the receivables cycle and slight reduction in inventory holding period leading to overall reduction in the short-term debt of the company.  Consequently, leverage ratios remained comfortable with gearing and TOLTNW ratio at 0.22 time and 0.5 time, respectively, in fiscal 2026 and are expected to be less than 0.5 time and 1 time, respectively, over the medium term. Debt protection metrics improved with interest coverage ratio improving to 6.57 times in fiscal 2026 compared to 5.15 times in fiscal 2025. It is expected to improve to above 7 times in the near term, driven by growing operating profitability.

Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses

 Large working capital requirement despite improvement in fiscal 2026: The company has sizable working capital requirement due to higher gestation period of the product and complex nature of the products. Longer gestation period has led to sizeable inventory and receivables owing to many tests and approvals required from customers and 3rd party inspections before clearance of the bill. However, in fiscal 2026, the company has improved the overall working capital requirement due to moderation in inventory and receivables. Overall inventory and debtors moderated to 275 days and 218 days, respectively, (Crisil Ratings-adjusted) in fiscal 2026 from 286 days and 273 days, respectively, in fiscal 2025. This resulted in moderation in overall GCAs (net of cash) to 429 days as on March 31, 2026, from over 500 days in fiscal 2025. This resulted in reduction in working capital debt, through strong focus on realisation of receivables and innovative inventory management. The GCAs are expected to be maintained at 420-440 days over the medium term as the company continues to rationalise debtors and strong inventory management practices. 

 Exposure to defence procurement cycles, execution risks and tender-based business 

The business depends on successfully bidding for tenders from defence public sector undertakings and research establishments. Establishments such as DRDO invite tenders from qualified vendors for their research and development (R&D) requirement and commence bulk production on successful completion of product development. Long-term revenue visibility is driven by the success of R&D projects at DRDO and mass production.  Defence orders typically have long execution cycles, exposing the company to project implementation risks arising from design modifications, supply chain disruptions, cost overruns and delays in customer approvals. Timely execution of the order book remains critical for revenue recognition, margin sustenance and working capital management.

Liquidity Adequate

Liquidity remains comfortable with Rs 190 crore of unencumbered cash and equivalent as on March 31, 2026. NCA improved to Rs 216 crore in fiscal 2026 which was sufficient for the debt obligation of Rs 19 crore during the year. NCA is expected to improve to above Rs 250 crore annually in the near term and will be sufficient for yearly debt repayment of Rs 10 crore through fiscal 2029. Annual capex of Rs 75-80 crore in the near to medium term will be funded through a prudent mix of debt and equity. Also, equity raise of Rs 131 crore before December 2026, through private placement of warrants, will aid liquidity.

Outlook Stable

The AMPL group will benefit from continuous improvement in the business risk profile supported by healthy operating performance and increased order book. The financial risk profile is expected to be healthy, driven by comfortable capital structure and debt protection metrics.

Rating sensitivity factors

Upward factors:

 Higher-than-expected ramp-up in revenue and operating profitability leading to return on capital employed (RoCE) of 18-20% on sustained basis

 Sustained improvement in the working capital cycle with adjusted GCAs below 400 days

 Sustenance of healthy financial risk profile

Downward factors:

 Revenue degrowth due to delay in execution of existing contracts or decline in operating margin impacting the business risk profile

 Weakening of the financial risk profile on account of higher-than-anticipated debt or stretch in the working capital cycle with GCAs of over 500 days

About the Company

AMPL was incorporated as a private limited company in 1991 and reconstituted as a public limited company in 1993. It is promoted by P A Chitrakar, C Pramelamma and B Malla Reddy. The company designs, develops and manufactures customised subsystems and components for microwave communication systems used in defence, space and telecommunication sectors.

In fiscal 2014, AMPL floated Astra Defense Technologies Pvt Ltd (erstwhile Bhavyabhanu Electronics Pvt Ltd), which is 100% owned as a captive supplier of raw material for overseas orders. In fiscal 2015, AMPL floated the 100% owned ASPL in Singapore as a supplier of monolithic microwave integrated circuit (MMIC) products for semiconductors. In fiscal 2019, AMPL set up a joint venture, Astra Rafael Comsys Pvt Ltd, with Rafael Advanced Defence Systems for production of communication systems and subsystems for defence. AMPL has incorporated ASTL as a wholly owned subsidiary, which will cater to the requirement of space and satellite technologies. AMPL will be demerging its space, metrology and hydrology businesses to ASTL. After the demerger, ASTL will be listed on stock exchanges mirroring the shareholding pattern of the AMPL.
