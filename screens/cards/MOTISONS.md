## Business Understanding Narrative

Motisons sells gold, diamond, kundan and silver jewellery from Jaipur. Three addresses appear across
its own filings: Johri Bazar in the old city, Lalkothi on Tonk Road, and Gautam Marg in Vaishali
Nagar (General FY26 Q3 p.4, FY26 Q4 p.4). The FY27 filings drop the third and list two (General FY27
Q1 p.3). The company describes itself as "one of the leading jewellery companies in the organised
jewellery retail sector in India" (AR FY25 p.105). On the evidence held, it is a three-store
retailer in one city.

It makes money on the spread between what gold costs and what a finished ornament sells for, plus
making charges. It manufactures in house: "digitalized design and covers CAD, CAM designs, laser
cutting, casting and filling, polishing, plating" (Concall 2024 p.4). Revenue reached Rs 489.54
crore in FY26 and profit after tax Rs 63.71 crore (Results p.4).

What is changing, on the numbers, is margin. Profit after tax went Rs 32.23 crore in FY24, Rs 43.17
crore in FY25, Rs 63.71 crore in FY26 (AGM p.12; Results p.4). It nearly doubled in two years. Over
the same two years total income went from Rs 417.18 crore to Rs 495.84 crore, up 18.9%. Net margin
went from 7.7% to 13.0%. Profit grew four times faster than sales.

What is changing, on management's own account, is nothing recent. The last thing this company told
investors directly was in June 2024: "Our long-term goals are basically expansion, expansion pan
India" (Concall 2024 p.7). Twenty-seven months later the filings still show Jaipur.

Why now: the company raised Rs 150 crore in a qualified institutional placement that closed on 11
June 2026, at Rs 11.05 a share (Results p.5). It spent Rs 129.37 crore of the net Rs 139.37 crore
inside the same quarter, all of it on "Funding working capital requirements" (Results p.5). In a
jewellery retailer, working capital is gold on the shelf.

What must be true: the margin gain must be operating, not a gold price effect, and the new
inventory must turn into sales at that margin.

What breaks it: the corpus cannot tell you whether a single extra ornament was sold. There is no
volume figure, no store count trend, no same-store sales line and no hedging policy anywhere in the
documents. A jeweller that holds inventory through a rising gold market books higher profit on the
same number of pieces. That reading fits the numbers exactly as well as the operating one, and
nothing filed separates them.

## 2. Business model and archetype
Buy gold, make ornaments, sell them from a shop. Revenue is grams times a price that moves with the
metal, plus a making charge the retailer sets. Gross margin is thin and the balance sheet is fat:
purchases of stock in trade ran Rs 166.49 crore in Q1 FY27 against revenue of Rs 107.33 crore, with
inventory rising Rs 95.65 crore in the quarter (Results p.4). The economic engine is inventory turn,
not price.

Archetype: **Brand/franchise consumer** is the archetype the company claims, and the directors' own
words invoke it: "trust and goodwill of a brand is a major impact factor" (AGM p.12). The corpus
does not support it. With three stores in one city, no advertising efficiency data and no pricing
disclosure, the honest classification is a **regional retailer with no demonstrated pricing power**.
The dials that matter here are store count, revenue per store, inventory turn and making-charge
realisation. Not one of the four is disclosed.

## 3. Competitive advantages
Absent, on the held evidence. A Johri Bazar address and a local name carry real goodwill in Jaipur,
and that is the only candidate. There is no switching cost in jewellery, no cost advantage disclosed,
no scale against Kalyan, Titan or PN Gadgil, and no scarcity. The company's own management discussion
lists the pressures rather than the defences: high import duties, fluctuating gold prices, lack of
standardisation, and "competition from synthetic and imitation products, such as lab-grown diamonds
and artificial jewellery" (AR FY25 p.105). Growth channels named on the last call were "Instagram and
Facebook" (Concall 2024 p.10). Verdict: **no moat present, none forming on the evidence held.**

## 4. The promoters
The board is one family. Sandeep Chhabra is Chairman and Whole Time Director, Sanjay Chhabra is
Managing Director, and the directors' report records that the Joint Managing Director, Namita Chhabra,
Kajal Chhabra and Kaustubh Chhabra "are related to... each other as per definition of Relative under
Section 2(77) of The Companies Act, 2013" (AGM p.46). Kaustubh Chhabra is the Chief Financial Officer
and also a director's relative. The two signing directors share one residential address, B-9
Vivekanand Marg, C-Scheme, Jaipur (AGM p.46). No independent voice appears anywhere in the held
documents.

A promoter-group conflict is disclosed: "Kaustubh Chhabra (Promoter Group) and Mr. Prakkhar Chhabra
(Promoter Group) of Motisons Jewellers Limited are partners in Moti Developers" (General FY27 Q1
p.3). The filing's own "Potential Conflict of Interest" heading is left blank beneath it.

Promoter holding percentage: **NOT FOUND.** Looked for in the results filings, the directors' report
and the annual report pages held; no shareholding pattern is in the corpus. Pledge: **NOT FOUND**,
same places. For a family-controlled jewellery business, where gold stock is routinely financed
against promoter security, an unverified pledge position is a material hole.

Skin in the game is therefore unquantified. Capital discipline is visible and it points one way: no
dividend for FY25, with "the entire net profit amounting to Rs. 4,317.11 Lakhs" retained (AGM p.12),
and three capital-raising events in thirty months.

Equity at 31 March 2026: share capital Rs 100.18 crore plus other equity Rs 398.90 crore, so net
worth about Rs 499 crore (Results p.4). Return on equity for FY26 is therefore about 12.8%. After the
June QIP, equity is roughly Rs 650 crore, which pushes the same profit stream below 10%.

Leverage is low and falling. Finance cost of Rs 6.04 crore on Rs 489.54 crore of revenue is under
1.3%, and the quarterly charge fell from Rs 1.52 crore to Rs 1.08 crore year on year. The IPO paid
down debt: "successful subscription of IPO, we have reduced our debt significantly which has resulted
in increased profits" (Concall 2024 p.7).

The quarters are violently seasonal and the trend is not clean. Q4 FY26 earned Rs 8.30 crore on
Rs 143.23 crore of income, a 5.8% margin, the worst period in the table. Q4 FY26 also carried
Rs 5.76 crore of other income against Rs 0.01 crore in Q1 FY27, and an exceptional charge of
Rs 0.30 crore. By subtraction, the nine months to December 2025 earned Rs 55.41 crore of the year's
Rs 63.71 crore.

**Inflection classification: MARGIN-LED, source not separable.** Revenue grew 18.9% over two years
while profit grew 97.7%. That is not operating leverage at a retailer with flat store count; the
fixed-cost base is too small. Employee cost was Rs 11.61 crore and other expenses Rs 10.64 crore for
all of FY26, together 4.5% of revenue (Results p.4). There is almost no operating leverage available.
The remaining explanations are making-charge realisation, product mix, and gold inventory
revaluation. The corpus distinguishes none of them. Cash conversion is **INDETERMINATE**: no cash
flow statement is held, and the framework caps that at PROCEED WITH CAVEATS with the missing evidence
named.

## 6. The transition and the quality ladder
FROM **R1 COMMODITY PRICE-TAKER**. A regional jeweller sells a metal at a metal price and adds
labour. No pricing power, cash swings with gold, returns cyclical.
TO **R4 FRANCHISE / SHARE-OF-WALLET LEADER** is what the language claims: "one of the leading
jewellery companies in the organised jewellery retail sector in India" (AR FY25 p.105), with brand
"trust and goodwill" as the stated engine (AGM p.12).
Mechanism claimed, quoted once, from the last time management spoke: "Our long-term goals are
basically expansion, expansion pan India. Just we start from Rajasthan or nearby... Then gradually we
increase our showroom year by year that is our goal" (Concall 2024 p.7).
**Ladder ruling: the claim is a three-rung leap and nothing in the corpus evidences even the first
rung.** The rung-jump base rate is one rung per two to three years. A stated R1 to R4 climb, with no
store additions visible and the expansion capital spent on inventory rather than stores, is itself
the red flag the ladder warns about.

## 8. Proof check
The last two quarters do move, and they move the way a capital raise moves a balance sheet, not the
way a transition moves a business.

Q1 FY27 revenue rose 23.3% year on year, from Rs 87.05 crore to Rs 107.33 crore, and profit after tax
rose 37.6%, from Rs 8.03 crore to Rs 11.05 crore (Results p.4). Taken alone that is a good quarter.
Set against Q4 FY26, revenue fell 21.9% and the comparison is seasonal and meaningless without a
store count.

**The proof point, named by its absence.** The triggers claim store expansion. Store count is not
disclosed, so the claim cannot be tested at all. What can be tested is where the money went, and it
went to inventory: Rs 129.37 crore of the Rs 139.37 crore net QIP into working capital inside one
quarter, with inventory up Rs 95.65 crore in the same quarter (Results p.4, p.5). Money raised for a
retail chain and spent on stock is not a store rollout.

**The proof gate has NOT FIRED.** No trigger has a measurable outcome, and the one trigger that
delivered was a financing event.

## 9. Flags from the documents
- **Serial dilution, three events in thirty months.** IPO December 2023; warrant conversions to a
  single non-promoter investor, Nexpact Limited, in FY26 Q3 and Q4 (General FY26 Q3 p.4, Q4 p.4); and
  a Rs 150 crore QIP closing 11 June 2026 (Results p.5). Share capital went from Rs 98.45 crore in
  June 2025 to Rs 100.18 crore in March 2026 to Rs 113.75 crore in June 2026 (Results p.4). The QIP
  alone diluted holders 13.5%.
- **The QIP priced at Rs 11.05, a 4.57% discount to a Rs 11.58 floor** (Results p.5). Qualified
  institutional buyers took stock at that price in June 2026. The identity record puts the share near
  Rs 16.8 in September 2026, about 52% higher, with one quarterly result filed in between.
- **The entire raise went into inventory in one quarter** (Results p.5). Fast, and it leaves the
  proceeds unproven as sales.
- **Face value inconsistency in the results table.** The share capital line header reads "Facevalue
  Rs 10/- each" while note 6 on the facing page states the QIP shares have "face value Rs 1 each"
  (Results p.4, p.5). The capital movement confirms Re 1. An error on the primary results statement.
- **Governance concentration.** Every named director and the CFO are one family, related under
  Section 2(77), with two signing directors at the same home address (AGM p.46).
- **Promoter-group related party.** Two promoter-group members are partners in Moti Developers, a
  real-estate partnership, disclosed with the conflict-of-interest field left blank (General FY27 Q1
  p.3).
- **Auditor.** Keyur Shah & Co. is a sole proprietorship in Ahmedabad auditing a company of this size.
  The opinion is unmodified (Results FY26 Q4 p.12), and the signature block carries two different
  membership numbers, 153774 and 135784 (Outcome FY26 Q4 p.5).
- **No dividend for FY25**, entire profit retained (AGM p.12).
- **Disclosure silence.** No presentation ever; no call in twenty-seven months. For a listed company
  that has raised capital three times in that window, this is the loudest flag on the card.

## 11. Posture: the Transition Decision Matrix
- **Proof gate: NOT FIRED.** Step 8. No operating trigger has a measurable outcome.
- **Ugliness: there is none, and that is the problem.** Profit nearly doubled, margins expanded, debt
  fell, the audit opinion is unmodified, investor complaints are nil. The optics are clean. Under the
  matrix, clean optics with an unproven transition is not reassurance.
- **Recognition gap: CLOSED.** Rs 1,909 crore of market value against Rs 63.71 crore of FY26 profit is
  about 30 times trailing earnings; against Q1 FY27 annualised it is about 43 times. Against net worth
  of roughly Rs 650 crore after the QIP it is about 2.9 times book. The market is already paying a
  franchise multiple for a three-store regional retailer whose franchise is undemonstrated. The stock
  has also moved about 52% above the price institutions paid in June.

**Posture: PRICED NARRATIVE (TRAP).** Proof not fired, ugliness absent, gap closed. The framework
calls this "the most seductive cell: no ugliness warns you off, and the downside is double (the
transition fails AND the multiple compresses). AVOID or hard WATCH with no position until the proof
gate fires AND the price re-opens the gap."

## 12. Verdict card

**PASS.**

Not because the business is bad. It is profitable, lightly levered and clean on audit, and the
profit line has genuinely doubled. The pass is on a different ground: **this company cannot be
underwritten from its disclosures.** A 25% CAGR thesis on a three to five year hold needs to know
store count, volume, same-store sales, inventory turn, making-charge realisation and gold hedging.
Motisons discloses none of the six, has published no investor presentation in its listed life, and has
not held an earnings call in twenty-seven months while raising capital three times. A full `/step1`
would read the same statutory filings this card read and hit the same wall.

Weigh it fairly against the bull case. Profit after tax of Rs 32.23 crore to Rs 63.71 crore in two
years is real. The QIP was taken by institutions who did their own work. Gold retail in India is a
large and growing market. None of that resolves the one question that decides the thesis: did volume
grow, or did gold. On the held evidence that question is unanswerable, and at roughly 30 times
trailing earnings the price already assumes the flattering answer.

**Load-bearing facts to verify first, if the operator overrides this pass:**
1. Store count by year, FY23 to date, and revenue per store. Not in any held filing.
2. Volume sold in grams, and same-store sales growth. The single fact that separates an operating
   inflection from a gold price effect.
3. The gold hedging and inventory valuation policy, and how much of the FY25 and FY26 margin gain is
   revaluation.
4. Promoter holding and pledge. Both NOT FOUND in the corpus.
5. Who Nexpact Limited is, and the terms of the warrant issues that preceded the QIP.
6. The FY26 balance sheet and cash flow statement: inventory days, debtor days, and whether operating
   cash flow has ever matched profit.
7. Any credit rating, from any agency. None exists in the corpus.

**What would change the view.** To WATCH: the company resumes earnings calls and publishes a
presentation with store count and volume. To PROCEED: two consecutive quarters showing store
additions funded by the QIP, with revenue per store flat or rising, plus a disclosed hedging policy
that explains the margin. Either would take at least two quarters to appear.

**This is not a position.** No valuation, no target price, no entry zone. The shallow read decides
only that a full `/step1` is not worth running on this name today.
