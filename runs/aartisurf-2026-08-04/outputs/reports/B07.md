## SECTION 3: THE 20-CATEGORY SCAN

### FAMILY A — PRODUCT & TECHNOLOGY

**A1 — Rare manufacturing capability.** NO EVIDENCE FOUND. AARTISURF's 113,000 MT capacity (CARE Report p.2) sits alongside named global competitors (BASF, Croda, Evonik, Solvay, Stepan, Dow) and larger domestic peers (Galaxy Surfactants is explicitly called "the largest pure play surfactant manufacturer" in the same report, CARE Report p.3). No qualification-timeline, long-lead-equipment, or multi-year regulatory-approval advantage is disclosed. B01's own peer-relative moat tests (M2 cost, M5 scale, M9 margin) all scored 0 for AARTISURF as the smallest of four supplied peers — consistent with no rarity here.

**A2 — Patent and IP pipeline.** NO EVIDENCE FOUND. The word "patent" does not appear anywhere in the AR, results filings, presentation or CARE report.

**A3 — Process innovation.** WEAK. 📄 Documented, specific, quantified initiative: replacement of air-conditioning systems with high-efficiency Fan Coil Units, "reducing cooling energy use by nearly 70%" (AR PDF p.16, Environment section); also VFDs replacing conventional motor controls and upgraded pumps (AR PDF p.16). These are real, numbered efficiency gains, but there is no yield disclosure, no unit-cost trend, and no automation metric tied to product quality — the evidence is genuine but narrow.

**A4 — Product platform / modular architecture.** NO EVIDENCE FOUND. 50+ products is a static count (Inv. Pres. slide 3; AR PDF p.4); no SKU-growth time series, launch-frequency data, or shared-architecture language is disclosed.

### FAMILY B — SUPPLY CHAIN

**B1 — Backward integration and RM security.** NO EVIDENCE FOUND. CEO letter states intent only ("backward integration initiatives," AR PDF p.6) — no captive source, 5+ year supply agreement, or declining RM-%-of-revenue metric is disclosed. Listed only in the Optionality Register.

**B2 — Qualification lock-in.** MODERATE. 📄 Documented certifications: ISO 9001:2015, EFFCI, HALAL, COSMOS CERTIFIED, RSPO, KOSHER (AR PDF p.18, Certifications page). These are the standard entry tickets for supplying multinational FMCG personal-care buyers. Reinforcing evidence: CARE Report names AARTISURF as "a preferred supplier for Hindustan Unilever Limited, Procter & Gamble Home Products Pvt. Ltd., Colgate Palmolive (India) Limited, and Dabur India Limited," and explicitly attributes this to "high entry barriers associated with becoming a supplier to such clients" (CARE Report p.2, p.3) — third-party language directly describing a qualification-lock-in dynamic. Caveat: certifications of this type are largely table-stakes across the surfactants/specialty-chemicals peer set (Galaxy, Rossari, FCL typically hold comparable certs), so this is real but not differentiated versus peers — hence Moderate, not Strong.

**B3 — Supply chain network effect.** NO EVIDENCE FOUND.

### FAMILY F — TALENT & ORGANISATIONAL

**F1 — Talent density.** WEAK. 📄 Documented, quantified: ESOP 2024 plan implemented (AR PDF p.40); Employee Stock Option expense grew from ₹18.09 lakh (FY25) to ₹70.47 lakh (FY26) — a ~289% increase (AR standalone note 37, PDF p.131; FY26 Results Cash Flow Statement p.8). **F2 — Execution moat (NO-CONCALL substitute: capex-completion evidence).** WEAK. In the absence of a concall-based promise-delivery record, the closest available cross-check is capex pacing against the CARE-disclosed programme: ~₹23cr spent by H1FY26 (Sep-2025) against an ~₹85cr, 2-year (FY26-FY27) budget targeting December 2026 completion at Pithampur (CARE Report p.3); full-year FY26 capex reached ₹45.10cr standalone (FY26 Results p.8), roughly ~53% of the total two-year budget spent within year one — a pace that is not obviously behind schedule. But this is a single in-progress project with **no completed, verifiable prior promise-to-delivery cycle** to check it against (no prior-year AR announced this specific programme at a stated cost/date that can now be marked delivered or missed), and the FY24-25 capex step (₹19.93cr) versus FY23-24 (₹16.09cr, AR Cash Flow Statement, PDF p.98) shows a rising but unremarkable trajectory before the CARE-disclosed programme was announced. **Genuine execution-track-record evidence cannot be established this cycle** — flagged as an input gap, consistent with the injected NO-CONCALL degradation instruction.

### FAMILY G — FINANCIAL & STRUCTURAL

**G1 — War chest.** NO EVIDENCE FOUND — and the disclosed direction is the opposite of a war chest. The ~₹85cr capex programme is being funded with **~₹60cr of new debt** (CARE Report p.3), and total debt rose from ~₹110.39cr (Mar-25) to ~₹118.19cr (Sep-25) (CARE Report p.2). **G2 — WC improvement trajectory.** WEAK. 📄 Documented ratio improvement: Inventory Turnover 4.58x (FY25) → 5.72x (FY26) and Trade Receivable Turnover 8.86x (FY25) → 9.43x (FY26), both standalone (FY26 Results, Reg 52(4) disclosures, p.9).
