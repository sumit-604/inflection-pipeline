Integrated steel expander mid transition, core still deteriorating

Shyam Metalics is an integrated metals producer in eastern India, making sponge iron, wire rod, TMT bars, ferro alloys, stainless steel, aluminium foil and iron ore pellets across backward integrated plants with captive power. FY26 consolidated revenue was Rs 18,552 Cr. It sells commodity and semi commodity long steel, ships intermediate products on merchant terms, and earns a growing slice from value added stainless and aluminium. Raw material runs about 74% of revenue by design, and the company is a price taker in a cyclical industry. The shares trade at Rs 1,022 for a market value near Rs 28,541 Cr.

The company is spending a Rs 13,902 Cr capex programme to shift its mix from commodity long steel toward stainless steel, aluminium foil and specialty products, where EBITDA per tonne runs roughly 5 to 10 times the carbon steel base. Through FY26 only about Rs 3,285 Cr of that had been executed. Seventy six percent remains, with commissioning dates stretching to FY29. The balance sheet is net cash and carries CRISIL AA plus with a stable outlook, so the build has been self funded so far. Four named global technology partners sit behind the new lines.

The screening posture is weak. Return on capital employed fell from 23% to 13% over FY18 to FY26, EBITDA margin compressed from 20.85% to 13.67%, and free cash flow was negative Rs 614 Cr in FY26 against active heavy capex. The Gate 0 score landed at AVOID. Part of that is a data gap artifact: the block covering shareholding and contingent liabilities scored zero only because those documents were absent from the file, not because the items are bad. Underneath the artifact the deterioration is genuine.

Cash conversion is the sharpest concern. At the standalone parent, operating cash flow to profit collapsed to 0.57 times in a year profit grew 39%, and standalone free cash flow was negative Rs 223 Cr. The mechanics trace cleanly to a roughly Rs 924 Cr swing in trade payables, an inventory build, and cash pumped into the core subsidiary. At the consolidated level working capital days actually improved, but finished goods inventory grew 54% against 15% revenue growth and the inventory writedown expense jumped ninefold. This reads as growth and timing rather than a broken model, yet the core subsidiary's own profit contribution roughly halved even as capital went in, and that is the part to watch.

Management delivers the capex it promises on the quarter it promises it. The Ramsarup blast furnace and the CRM Phase II line both commissioned on schedule, and every call tracks capex spend line by line. Against that, the PLI stainless benefit and a flange beam line were quantified then dropped without explanation, a 90 MW power plant guided for a specific quarter was never confirmed commissioned, FY27 growth guidance jumped from a repeated 15 to 20% band to about 30% with no reconciliation, and management deflected outright on an Enforcement Directorate coal notice. The credibility grade is a B.

Accounting quality grades 5 out of 10. The parent holds equity in three entities that are themselves promoter shareholders owning 35% of the company, a circular cross holding the annual report never explains. The group reports a single business segment while its own notes show sharply different profitability by product line. About 23% of pre tax profit growth came from treasury gains. And 10 of 13 group entities carry an auditor cash loss qualification, with one small aluminium foil subsidiary flagged going concern adjacent.

Two live April 2026 actions sit over the promoter. The Enforcement Directorate provisionally attached Rs 160 Cr of assets at the material subsidiary under money laundering law tied to alleged illegal coal mining, and a pollution regulator ordered a plant cluster closed with conditional three month remediation relief. The Chairman and Managing Director roles were combined in one person in 2025. Offsetting that, a SAIL and NMDC calibre independent director joined in 2024, a Rs 1,385 Cr institutional placement explains the promoter stake drop as pure dilution and not a sale, pledge is zero, and succession passed to the second generation in an orderly way.

Peers independently confirm the demand backdrop and the safeguard duty story, though the duty lapsed in late 2025 before being reimposed, so sustained pricing is only partly supported. Every listed peer is expanding capex at the same time, so this is an industry wide capacity race and not a lone bet. The market runway is genuinely large: the addressable market supports a bottom up five year revenue path near Rs 33,800 Cr, which sits about Rs 8,500 Cr below management's own FY31 target of Rs 42,500 Cr. Peer coverage has a real hole. There was no stainless, aluminium or nickel comparator in the set, the exact segments the growth thesis leans on.
