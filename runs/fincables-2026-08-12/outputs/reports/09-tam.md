- EHV/HV underground power cables (>66kV). Finolex participates only via its 49%-held Finolex J-Power Systems (FJPS) joint venture with Sumitomo, which is equity-accounted, not consolidated into the ₹6,321cr revenue base (FJPS FY26 revenue ~₹458cr, profit ~₹21cr, order book ₹350cr — sourced via web aggregation of Q4 FY26 disclosures, publisher tndindia.com, direct fetch blocked by egress proxy, corroborated by two independent search-result snippets). - Broad FMEG catalogue items Finolex does not make (ACs, refrigerators, large kitchen appliances) — excluded even though generic "India FMEG market" reports include them.
- Non-cable telecom equipment (routers, ONTs, network gear) — not a cable, excluded even where BharatNet/data-center demand commentary bundles them.

**Geographic scope:** India domestic. Finolex export revenue % is NOT FOUND in available documents (input gap; treated as a minor, unquantified SAM-expansion lever, Section 5D, not part of core TAM).

**Customer scope:** retail/dealer consumers and institutional/project buyers for wire & cable; telecom operators, system integrators and government broadband programs (BharatNet) for comms cable; retail/institutional buyers for FMEG-lite.

**Channel scope:** company's established electrical dealer/distributor network; direct OEM/telecom accounts for comms cable; general trade plus limited modern trade for FMEG (a real constraint — see SAM filter 3B).

**Price segment:** BIS/ISI-compliant organized-segment product, not premium/luxury cable, not sub-standard unorganized product (though unorganized-to-organized formalization is itself a TAM growth driver, Section 4A).

## SECTION 2: TAM ESTIMATION, MULTIPLE METHODS

All figures in ₹ Crore unless stated. FX conversion where source is USD-denominated: ₹95/$ (spot, Federal Reserve H.10 release, 10 Aug 2026, per WebSearch; source reports originally published in 2024-2025 likely used ₹83-88/$, so conversions carry a modest, flagged imprecision — noted, not corrected, since no report-specific historical rate was independently confirmed).

### Method 1 — Top-down, electrical wires & cables (core bucket)
Two independent industry-report estimates, both India-specific, both 2025-base-year (not stale):
- **IMARC Group** (via WebSearch synthesis; direct fetch blocked): India electrical wires & cables market **USD 7.0bn in 2025** → USD 11.3bn by 2034, CAGR 5.31%. = ₹66,500cr at ₹95/$.
- **Samarwealth / Fortune-Business-Insights-class aggregation** (samarwealth.com, "India's Cables & Wires Industry"): India wires & cables market **USD 10.01bn in 2025** (up from $9.32bn in 2024) → USD 17.08bn by 2032, CAGR 7.94%. = ₹95,100cr at ₹95/$.

These two reputable-tier estimates diverge by ~1.4x on the same nominal market and year — flagged per instructions rather than averaged. Likely explanation: differing inclusion of industrial/power cable sub-categories and export volumes. **Conservative bias applied: ₹66,500cr used as the Method-1 conservative anchor; ₹95,100cr as the realistic anchor.**

**Cross-check via organized-segment trade data:** a trade-body-sourced estimate (via tradejini.com summary, fetch blocked, WebSearch snippet only) puts the **organized** electrical W&C segment at ~₹92,000cr in FY25, growing to ₹1,43,000cr by FY29 (11.8% CAGR). Separately, organized-vs-unorganized share data is itself inconsistent across sources found: one set shows organized share rising from 45% (2018) to 78% (2025) to >85% (2026E); another shows organized at ~72% today with unorganized still 40-50% **by volume** (value vs volume metrics not comparable, both cited, WebSearch aggregation, publishers not independently confirmed via direct fetch). Grossing the ₹92,000cr organized figure up for an unorganized layer of 15-28% (value-basis) implies a total market of roughly ₹1,08,000-1,15,000cr — higher than either Method-1 report. This third data point is **not** used as the headline (conservative bias: it would only push TAM up), but it corroborates that ₹95,100cr (realistic) is not an outlier and that ₹66,500cr (conservative) is genuinely conservative.

### Method 3 — Peer revenue aggregation (light-touch cross-check, not a primary anchor)
Known organized competitors and approximate FY26 scale, sourced via WebSearch (precision limited — flagged): RR Kabel crossed ₹9,722cr total FY26 revenue (+27.6% YoY, WebSearch/BusinessToday-class coverage); Polycab, KEI Industries both reported industry-leading growth (24% and 21% respectively over FY22-26, per a WebSearch-sourced comparison); Havells carries a smaller dedicated cable segment within a larger FMEG-heavy revenue base; Finolex's own Electrical Cable segment was ₹5,490cr YTD FY26 (presentation p.5). Summing Finolex + RR Kabel + estimated Polycab/KEI/Havells-cable + dozens of smaller organized players (Apar, V-Guard, Universal Cables, and others, not individually sized here — NOT FOUND to full precision) plausibly lands in the ₹80,000-1,00,000cr band for organized electrical W&C revenue alone, which is directionally consistent with the Method-1 range and does not contradict it. Precision limits acknowledged: Polycab and KEI FY26 exact revenue figures were not independently confirmed to the crore.

### Method 5 — Independent segment benchmark for EHV (tests the management claim directly)
An independent industry estimate (WebSearch aggregation across a Mordor-Intelligence/GMInsights-class report; specific publisher and exact publication year not independently confirmed — direct fetch of mordorintelligence.com was blocked by the egress proxy) puts **India's HV/EHV underground cable market at ~USD 498.9 million**, with India cited as ~11.7% of a broader (likely Asia-Pacific) category, and a forecast **CAGR of 4.7%**. This independent current-year figure ($498.9mn) sits almost exactly at the low end of management's own "$500-750mn" **current-year** claim — the two sources corroborate the starting point. They diverge enormously on the growth path: independent 4.7% CAGR vs. management's implied ~76% CAGR to reach $4-5bn in 3-4 years.
Projecting the independent $498.9mn base forward 3.5 years at 4.7% CAGR: $498.9mn × (1.047)^3.5 ≈ **$586mn ≈ ₹5,570cr** (at ₹95/$).
Management's claim, midpoint: **$4.5bn ≈ ₹42,750cr**.

**Communication cables (OFC) — top-down:** India OFC market estimates found range widely: **$0.55bn (2025) → $1.03bn by 2031, 11.03% CAGR** (conservative anchor, WebSearch synthesis of an IndexBox/TechSci-class report) vs. a much more bullish **$1.73bn (2024) → $7.5bn by 2032, 20.11% CAGR** (broader-scoped report, likely including accessories/equipment) vs. Allied Market Research's **"$1.66bn by 2026."** Conservative bias: **$0.55bn (2025) ≈ ₹5,225cr** used as the anchor; realistic case uses a blended ~₹10,000cr acknowledging the AI/data-center tailwind evidenced separately (India data center capacity 950MW→1,800MW by 2026; AI data centers require ~70% higher fibre density than conventional; global data-center fibre demand +76% YoY in 2025, 30% of global fibre demand by 2027 — WebSearch, LightReading/IEEE ComSoc/India Infoline-class sources, directional India-specific ₹ figure NOT FOUND).

**FMEG-relevant (fans + lighting + switches only; matched to Finolex's actual FMEG catalogue, not the broad India FMEG market):**
- Fans: ~₹10,000cr (Indian Fan Manufacturers Association, via WebSearch/BW Retail World; exact year not stated, treated as current, moderate confidence).
- Lighting: ~₹14,000cr (same source cluster, same caveat).
- Switches/switchgear-lite: $409.13mn (2024) → $784.96mn by 2032, CAGR 8.24% (Credence Research, WebSearch synthesis) = ₹3,890cr (2024 base, ₹95/$).
- Water heaters, conduits, accessories: NOT FOUND a sourced market-size figure for India; excluded rather than estimated (conservative, per "never estimate a missing number").
FMEG-relevant conservative total: ₹27,900cr. Note: broad "India FMEG market, ₹3,00,000cr by 2029" figure exists (WebSearch, BW Retail World-class source) but is explicitly NOT used — it includes ACs, refrigerators, and large appliances entirely outside Finolex's catalogue; using it would overstate TAM.

### 3B. SOM at 3 and 5 Years

**Current share of SAM:** ₹6,321cr revenue ÷ ₹85,100cr SAM = **7.4%**.

**Share-gain trajectory applied** (per standard rules): Finolex is a mature, established #2/3-tier organized player in a fragmenting-to-formalizing market with modest, not aggressive, capex (~4-5% of revenue in FY26/FY27 per B07 injected figures — capacity enhancement, not step-change expansion). This fits the "1-2pp in 3 years normal" band, not the aggressive 3-5pp band. Used: **+1.5pp by yr3 (7.4%→8.9%), +3.0pp by yr5 (7.4%→10.4%)** — the 5-year step leans toward the aggressive end given the OFC fibre-draw capacity doubling (to 8mn fkm, B07) and formalization tailwind (unorganized share still meaningfully large on a volume basis per Section 2), but stays under the 5pp ceiling reserved for competitor-exit/M&A scenarios (none evidenced here).

SAM is grown forward at its own conservative segment CAGRs (11.8% electrical, 11% comms, 11% FMEG — Section 2 sourcing) to avoid comparing a future SOM to a static-today SAM:
- SAM at yr3 = 88,256 (elec) + 7,148 (comms) + 22,901 (FMEG) = **₹1,18,305cr**
- SAM at yr5 = 110,304 (elec) + 8,804 (comms) + 28,207 (FMEG) = **₹1,47,315cr**

**SOM(3yr) = 8.9% × 1,18,305 = ₹10,529cr**
**SOM(5yr) = 10.4% × 1,47,315 = ₹15,321cr**

**Implied revenue CAGR, shown arithmetic:**
- yr3: (10,529 / 6,321)^(1/3) − 1 = (1.666)^0.333 − 1 ≈ **18.5%**
- yr5: (15,321 / 6,321)^(1/5) − 1 = (2.424)^0.2 − 1 ≈ **19.4%**

These sit close to FY26's actual reported growth (19% YoY, presentation p.4), which is a reasonable internal-consistency check — the market-share-gain math roughly reproduces the growth rate the company is already delivering, rather than requiring a break from trend.

### 3C. Capacity Cross-Check

B07 (Emerging Moat stage) capex-embedded growth figure: **~36%**. FY26 capex ~₹240cr; FY27 planned ~₹300cr (₹200cr capacity enhancement including doubling solar-cable capacity and EHV augmentation via the JV; ~₹100cr to complete the OFC preform + fibre-draw program to 8mn fkm).

Comparing: capacity-embedded growth (~36%) comfortably exceeds the SOM-implied revenue CAGR (~18.5-19.4% at yr3/yr5). **Capacity check: sufficient.** Installed-plus-committed capacity can physically produce more than the SOM math requires — the gap (36% capacity headroom vs. ~19% SOM-implied need) means capacity is not the constraint on hitting SOM; competitive share-gain execution and channel reach (especially in FMEG, per the 60% channel filter above) are the binding constraints instead. Put differently: **the SOM, not the capex plan, is the more conservative/limiting figure here** — the company could physically grow faster than SOM implies if it could win share faster, which is the harder variable to underwrite.

### 5B. Runway Assessment
- **Revenue headroom = SAM ÷ current revenue = 85,100 / 6,321 = 13.5x**
- **TAM growth rate (blended, conservative) ≈ 11.5%** (weighted: 66,500cr @11.8% electrical + 5,225cr @11% comms + 27,900cr @11% FMEG, ÷ 99,600cr total)
- **Company CAGR vs. TAM:** company grew revenue 19% YoY in FY26 (presentation p.4) vs. TAM growth ~11.5% — the company is **gaining share**, not merely riding the market; this is internally consistent with the SOM share-gain math in 3B.
- **Years to saturate SAM at current growth (illustrative, static-SAM approximation):** solving 6,321 × (1.19)^n = 85,100 → n ≈ **15 years**. This is illustrative only — SAM itself compounds at ~11.5%/year, so the real saturation point is much further out; it is shown only to size the order of magnitude of headroom, not as a forecast.

### 5C. Runway Classification: **STRONG**
Rationale: 13.5x revenue headroom, double-digit (~11.5%) TAM growth, and demonstrated share gains (19% company growth vs. 11.5% TAM growth) place this above MODERATE/GOOD. It is held at STRONG rather than MASSIVE because TAM growth itself (~11.5%) is solid but not explosive, and the headroom, while large, is not in the 20x+/hyper-growth range that would justify the top tier.

### 5D. SAM Expansion Levers Actually Being Pursued

1. **EHV cable scale-up via FJPS JV** — capacity utilization was >80% in FY26 (WebSearch, up from FY25), order book grew from ₹200cr to ₹350cr YoY, and FY27 capex includes EHV augmentation (B07 injected figure). Even fully discounting management's $4-5bn claim and using only the independent 4.7% CAGR path, the EHV market itself is real and growing; if a mid-case (e.g., grid-capex-driven, RDSS/Green-Corridor-supported growth materially above 4.7% but well below management's 76% claim — no independently sourced mid-case figure exists, so this is not quantified) plays out, this is a genuine call option not currently in the core SAM. **Not added to the quantified funnel** — structural JV/equity-accounting reason stated in Section 1A.
2. **BharatNet Phase 3** — ₹1.39 lakh cr program (STALE flag, approved Aug 2023). If a cable-cost fraction of ~10-15% of program capex applies (this fraction is an ASSUMPTION, not sourced — flagged explicitly, not used as a hard number), the theoretical incremental comms-cable opportunity is ~₹13,900-20,850cr cumulative over the multi-year rollout. At Finolex's ~11-12% comms/OFC share, a proportionate capture would be roughly ₹1,500-2,500cr cumulative — **illustrative only, not added to core SAM**, and independently flagged 2026 commentary notes slow execution and rising input costs as real headwinds to this materializing on schedule.
3. **Export markets** — unquantified; export revenue % NOT FOUND in available documents.
4. **Data-center/AI fibre demand** — already embedded qualitatively in the 11% OFC-segment growth assumption used in Section 2/3B; not layered on as a separate addition to avoid double-counting.

If BharatNet's illustrative ₹1,500-2,500cr and a nominal, heavily-discounted EHV contribution were included as an upside scenario (not the base case), SAM could stretch toward roughly ₹1,00,000-1,05,000cr, lifting revenue headroom toward ~16x. This is presented as upside color only — the base-case SAM/SOM above does not include it.
