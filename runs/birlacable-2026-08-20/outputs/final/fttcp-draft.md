## THE CATALYST STORY IN PLAIN WORDS

**Revenue.** The optical fibre cycle turned. Two peers say so on their own calls with their own numbers, and Birla Cable printed 51.1% revenue growth in the June quarter. What would confirm it: Q2 FY27 revenue above ₹250 Cr and any disclosure that splits copper from optical fibre. What would kill it: revenue below ₹200 Cr in Q2 FY27, or any sign the customer that buys 45% of output has pulled back. The weakness in this story is that the recovery belongs to the sector. Nothing in the record shows Birla Cable taking share from anyone.

**Margin.** Something real happened to the cost line. Raw material fell from 82.3% of revenue to 74.3% in a single quarter, and margin went 5.32%, then 9.46%, then 17.24% across three quarters. The company has never said why. The most likely explanation is that fibre bought at old prices was sold into a price peak. If that is right the gain unwinds. The peer leader said on 30 April 2026 that prices had reached almost their final level and would not rise further, and peers describe bare fibre going from ₹250 to above ₹300 per km with preform costs up 20% to 25% and germanium supply controlled from China. Birla Cable makes no fibre. It buys from a group company. That is the exposure. What would confirm the durable reading: Q2 FY27 margin above 12% with raw material below 78% of revenue. What would kill it: margin back under 8%.

**Cash conversion.** There is no catalyst. I looked in every category the protocol lists and found nothing, and the absence is the finding. No factoring facility. No debtor day target. No renegotiated payment terms. No inventory programme. No customer advances. The annual report instead claims effective working capital management in the year operating cash flow was minus ₹20.91 Cr. There are no earnings calls, so there is no forum in which management has ever been asked about it. What would create a catalyst: an exchange filing announcing a sanctioned and operational working capital facility with an amount and a date, or a disclosed debtor day target that the company then hits. What confirms the negative: a Q2 FY27 print with revenue growth holding and no cash flow statement attached.

**Capital efficiency.** The catalyst is arithmetic rather than strategy. Profit is rising on a capital base that is shrinking because the company is barely investing. That works while it lasts. It stops working when either the margin normalises or working capital swells with revenue, and both are likely inside twelve months. What would confirm a real recovery: FY27 return on capital above 13% with positive operating cash flow in the same year. What would kill it: Q2 FY27 operating profit below ₹15 Cr.

**The event that overrides all four.** On 21 March 2026 the board approved a scheme that merges Birla Cable into Vindhya Telelinks. Shareholders receive 10 Vindhya Telelinks shares for every 115 Birla Cable shares. The appointed date is 1 April 2026. The exchanges issued their letters in August 2026 and the NCLT approval is pending. CARE expects completion roughly ten to twelve months from the board date, which is January to March 2027, inside the twelve month capital efficiency window. If the court sanctions it, Birla Cable is dissolved and stops existing as a listed company. Every transition above then belongs to somebody else's balance sheet.

That has a hard consequence for anyone holding the share. At ₹285, the exchange ratio implies Vindhya Telelinks must be worth about ₹3,277 per share for the swap to be value neutral. This run folder holds no anchored source for the Vindhya Telelinks share price, so I will not state one. That single number, plus a workup on Vindhya Telelinks itself, is what any real decision on Birla Cable now depends on. It is outside this run's evidence and it needs to be brought in.
