## SECTION 1: MARKET DEFINITION

### 1A. Precise boundaries

**Product scope (IN):** precision-engineered, mission/life-critical rotating
and stationary metal components manufactured to Tier-1 OEM specification —
compressor/turbine airfoils and blades (fixed, moving, last-stage), 3D
rotating parts, hot-section nozzle vane segments, engine/APU components,
actuator & hydraulic-system parts, unison rings/arms/levers, and O&G
drilling-equipment subsystems (slips, drill bits, hatch covers, bonnets,
frames). Source: B04-bizmodel revenue-stream split (Energy & O&G components
79.1%, A&D components 17.9%, scrap/incentives 3.0%) and Investor Presentation
May 2026 printed pp.15-16 (product/end-use table).

**Product scope (OUT, explicit exclusions):** full turbine or aero-engine
assembly, full aircraft manufacture, avionics/electronics, raw-material
smelting/forging-grade-steel supply, MRO/aftermarket service revenue (no
disclosed service revenue line), full drilling-rig/pump/wellhead/BOP
manufacture, and non-qualified commodity machining. Azad's own "Way Forward"
slide (Investor Presentation, printed p.26) signals intent to move into
landing gear, nuclear/steam turbines and end-to-end ATGG integration for GTRE
— these are FUTURE scope, not current TAM, and are treated as SAM-expansion
levers in Section 5D, not counted in the base TAM.

**Geographic scope:** global OEM procurement, not India-domestic consumption.
92-93% of FY26 standalone revenue is export (Investor Presentation printed
p.14), sold into 12 countries (US, UK, Switzerland, Hungary, Poland, France,
Sweden, Germany, Italy, South Africa, Saudi Arabia/UAE, Singapore, Japan) plus
India. Manufacturing base is Hyderabad, India, with a Saudi Arabia JV MoU
signed (printed p.10, p.26) for future in-Kingdom manufacture.

**Customer scope:** named Tier-1 qualified OEM relationships only — GE
Vernova, Siemens Energy, Mitsubishi Heavy Industries, Baker Hughes/Nuovo
Pignone, Honeywell Aerospace, Pratt & Whitney Canada, Rolls-Royce plc,
Safran/Airbus-linked programs, Eaton Aerospace, HAL, GTRE/DRDO, BHEL, Arabelle
Solutions. Top-5 customers = 51.1% of FY25 revenue (B04, AR p.173). Not a
supplier to distributors, retail, or unqualified spot-buyers.

**Channel scope:** direct long-term supply agreements / LTCPAs with zero-PPM
quality requirements and 30-48 month qualification cycles (B04 moat table;
Investor Presentation printed p.17). No distributor/retail channel exists in
this business.

**Price segment:** premium, mission/life-critical certified grade (aerospace,
nuclear, defence qualification standards) — not commodity general
engineering/machining.

### 3B. SOM at 3 and 5 years

Current SAM share = current revenue ÷ current SAM = ₹590 Cr ÷ ₹15,230 Cr ≈
**3.9%.**

Share-gain trajectory: the standard rule allows 1-2pp in 3 years "normal" and
3-5pp "aggressive" only with capacity and execution evidence. Azad has both:
capex-embedded growth of 146% (four new dedicated OEM-specific facilities
inaugurated since listing, Phase 2 underway — B07 handoff), an order book at
~13x trailing revenue providing multi-year revenue visibility, and
demonstrated wallet-share execution (A&D segment +84.1% YoY in FY25, overall
standalone revenue +30.3% in FY26, 3-year FY23-FY26 CAGR of 33.3%). This
qualifies for the AGGRESSIVE band, taken conservatively at the low end:
**+3pp over 3 years, +6pp cumulative over 5 years** (not the >5pp band, which
per the rules is reserved for competitor exit or acquisition scenarios, not
evidenced here).

- SAM at CY29 (3yr out): ₹19,180 Cr (Section 3A)
- Share at yr3: 3.9% + 3pp = 6.9%
- **SOM 3yr = 19,180 × 6.9% ≈ ₹1,325 Cr**
- Implied 3yr revenue CAGR from ₹590 Cr (FY26) to ₹1,325 Cr: (1,325/590)^(1/3)
  − 1 ≈ **31.0%**

- SAM at CY31 (5yr out): TAM grown forward from CY29 using segment CAGRs
  (A&D 11%, Energy ~5%, O&G ~3% proxies) — A&D 2,190×1.11²=2,698bn; Energy
  310×1.05²=342bn; O&G 930×1.03²=987bn; Total TAM CY31 ≈ ₹4,02,600 Cr; SAM
  CY31 = 4,02,600 × 5.6% ≈ ₹22,550 Cr
- Share at yr5: 3.9% + 6pp = 9.9%
- **SOM 5yr = 22,550 × 9.9% ≈ ₹2,232 Cr**
- Implied 5yr revenue CAGR from ₹590 Cr to ₹2,232 Cr: (2,232/590)^(1/5) − 1 ≈
  **30.5%**

Cross-check: these SOM-implied CAGRs (31.0% / 30.5%) sit close to Azad's own
recent historical CAGR (33.3%, FY23-FY26 standalone) — a reasonable,
internally-consistent continuity rather than a discontinuous jump, which
supports the plausibility of the share-gain assumptions used.

### 3C. Capacity cross-check (B07 handoff: capex-embedded growth 146%)

Installed + committed capacity implies ~146% incremental revenue over the
current base: ₹590 Cr × (1 + 1.46) ≈ **₹1,451 Cr** of capacity-supported
revenue potential once currently-committed capex is fully ramped.

- **Vs SOM 3yr (₹1,325 Cr): capacity is SUFFICIENT**, with ~₹126 Cr of spare
  headroom — the capex plan is not the binding constraint for the 3-year SOM.
- **Vs SOM 5yr (₹2,232 Cr): capacity is INSUFFICIENT** on currently-committed
  capex alone. Gap = ₹2,232 Cr − ₹1,451 Cr = **₹781 Cr**. Closing this gap
  requires capex phases beyond what is captured in the 146% figure — Phase 2
  (67,267 sqm, per Investor Presentation printed p.22-23), the stated
  inorganic-acquisition strategy, and the planned Centre of Excellence (AR
  printed p.7) are the disclosed but not-yet-quantified path to this
  additional capacity. **The SOM 5yr figure is the more optimistic side of
  this cross-check** — it assumes capacity investment continues at a pace
  beyond what is currently committed and disclosed.

### 4C. Market structure

- **Competitor count / concentration:** Aircraft OEM level is a duopoly
  (Boeing + Airbus >90% share, IP printed p.18). Gas-turbine OEM level is
  highly concentrated — GE Vernova, Siemens Energy, Mitsubishi Power control
  "nearly two-thirds of global construction" (AR printed p.17); Azad's own
  customers control ~90% of the "global turbine components" market by its
  own count (75%+10%+5%, IP printed p.19). Component-supply level (where
  Azad sits) has more players but a small qualified set given 30-48 month
  qualification cycles.
- **Organised vs unorganised:** THIS SEGMENT IS ORGANISED-DOMINATED, a
  deviation from the standard India small/micro-cap assumption of 30-60%
  unorganised share (per instructions) — capital intensity and multi-year
  OEM qualification barriers structurally exclude informal-sector capacity.
  Visible India-listed organized peer set: MTAR Technologies, Dynamatic
  Technologies, PTC Industries, Unimech Aerospace, Azad itself, plus PSU
  players HAL/BHEL in adjacent scope.
- **Consolidating or fragmenting:** Consolidating at the OEM-relationship
  level — OEMs are reducing supplier counts and deepening wallet share with
  fewer, scaled, qualified Tier-1 partners (Azad's own "wallet-share gain"
  strategy is a direct beneficiary/driver of this).
- **Price vs differentiation competition:** Differentiation-led (zero-PPM
  defect requirements, qualification barriers, know-how) rather than
  commodity price competition, though a cost-advantage-vs-China/Europe/
  US/Japan claim is also cited (B04 moat, unverified).
  competition.
- **Entries and exits:** High barriers limit new entrants; recent India-listed
  entrants (e.g. Unimech Aerospace, IPO'd 2024-25) show fresh organized
  capital is entering, but the 30-48 month qualification lag delays revenue
  realization for any new entrant.
- **Import share trend:** Not classic India import substitution — Azad
  substitutes for incumbent Western/Japanese Tier-1 suppliers WITHIN global
  OEM chains ("China+1"/diversification dynamic), while itself exporting
  92%+ of output from India.
