## PASS 3 — PATTERN RE-READ

Approach: not sequential note-by-note, but targeted checks for contradictions, cross-note number
mismatches, deliberately thin disclosure next to detailed disclosure elsewhere, restatements, and
going-concern language, plus spot verification of the two most consequential Pass 2 findings against
the source text directly.

**1. Going-concern language — checked directly in source, both Directors' Responsibility Statements
and both Auditor's Reports (standalone p.47/106-107, consolidated p.160-161).** Only standard
boilerplate found: Directors confirm accounts prepared on a going-concern basis (p.47); auditors'
opinion contains the standard ISA/SA-570-style paragraph on management's responsibility to assess
going concern, with no material-uncertainty paragraph, no emphasis-of-matter, no qualification. 🟢
**NONE** — no going-concern doubt anywhere in the document.

**2. Restatements/reclassifications — checked directly.** Two generic instances found, both
immaterial: (i) standard closing boilerplate "Previous year figures have been regrouped/reclassified,
where necessary, to conform to this year's classification" (Note 47 standalone p.159 wording area /
equivalent consolidated note) — no quantified item attached, no restated prior-period P&L or balance
sheet figures presented anywhere; (ii) a single "**Reclassified" footnote against the FY24 comparative
Borrowings row (₹18.40 Cr / lakh 1,840) in the consolidated financial-liabilities maturity table (Note
30, consolidated p.202) — the amount is unchanged from the borrowings figure reported elsewhere for
FY24 (Note 14), so this reads as a maturity-bucket presentation reclass, not a value restatement. 🟢
Neither rises to a genuine prior-period restatement; contrasts with the deferred-tax prior-period
**correction** (Section 10, both passes), which is economically restatement-like (fixing an
under-provision from earlier years) but was consciously routed through current-year P&L rather than
restated — this routing choice is itself already captured as Red Flag #1 below, not a new pattern
finding.

**3. Promoter shareholding cross-check — verified independently at a second location in the
document.** The −5,00,000-share / −1.20pp reduction for Rakesh Kumar Verma and the −1.26pp total
promoter-group reduction (Pass 2 Red Flag) are reported identically in two separate tables (Note 12
standalone shareholding-pattern sub-table, and the near-duplicate consolidated-book shareholding table)
— internally consistent, not a contradiction, but confirms the finding is not a single-location
transcription artifact. Search for "pledge" or "encumbrance" anywhere in the document returns exactly
one hit in the entire annual report: the company's own ₹19.00 Cr fixed-deposit pledge securing
Gtropy's bank facility (Note 36/35, already captured in Pass 1 Sections 2/3/7). **No promoter
share-pledge disclosure exists anywhere in the document** — confirms Pass 2's "NOT FOUND IN DOCUMENT"
finding rather than an extraction gap. 🔴 Stands as Red Flag.

**4. Deliberately thin disclosure next to detailed disclosure — one additional instance beyond what
Passes 1-2 already caught.** The Level 3 fair-value book (₹26.64 Cr consolidated / ₹23.64 Cr
standalone, both passes) sits inside a Financial Instruments note that otherwise gives granular
Level 1/2/3 hierarchy tables, currency-exposure sensitivity tables, and interest-rate sensitivity
tables — i.e. the note format clearly supports detailed quantitative disclosure, making the *absence*
of any valuation-technique or unobservable-input sensitivity table for Level 3 (already flagged Pass
2) a more pointed gap than a blanket "disclosure is generally light" comment would suggest: the
company demonstrably knows how to disclose granularly elsewhere in the same note and chose not to for
its fastest-growing, most judgment-dependent asset class. 🟡 Sharpens (does not newly discover) the
Pass 2 Level-3 disclosure-gap finding.

**5. No new contradictions between notes and the primary statements were found on spot-check** of the
headline figures already relied upon in Passes 1-2 (consolidated revenue ₹463.25 Cr, PAT ₹147.59 Cr,
total equity ₹791.70 Cr, gratuity PBO/plan-asset figures, RPT totals) — all reconcile to the note
tables cited.

**Conclusion: PASS 3 yields no additional standalone red flags beyond sharpening/confirming existing
Pass 1-2 findings.** No fabricated significance added. Proceeding to consolidation.

## E. NOTES-BASED RED FLAGS

- **Prior-period deferred-tax correction of ₹5.17 Cr** expensed through FY25 P&L (not restated, not labelled exceptional) for unprovided tax on unrealised gains on unlisted investments from earlier years — distorts YoY comparability and signals a historical under-provisioning pattern on the FVTPL book (Note 26 consolidated p.197-198; Note 27 standalone p.143-144).
- **Two related parties (ClarityX Analytics, Zenithra Tech) with combined transactions +638% YoY**, one traceable to Non-Executive Director/Promoter-group member Rakhi Prasad, thin arm's-length evidence, large unused Board-approved headroom, and an unnamed common director for the second entity (Note 31A/B standalone p.147-149; Note 30a/b consolidated p.203-204; director-bio p.226).
- **Unexplained promoter/MD share-count reduction (−1.20pp Rakesh Kumar Verma; −1.26pp total promoter group) with no pledge disclosure anywhere in the document**, confirmed by a document-wide search (Note 12 standalone p.135-137).
- **Receivables/ECL deterioration at the consolidated level (ECL allowance +117%, bad-debt charge +120%) concentrated almost entirely in the Gtropy subsidiary**, masking a standalone-level improvement — candidate FLAG-CASH, weighted to the hardware/IoT subsidiary rather than the core map-data/SaaS business (Note 8/23/29 consolidated; Note 8/24/30 standalone).
