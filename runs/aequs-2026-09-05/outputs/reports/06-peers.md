## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

### 2A. Demand environment
All three peers describe a broadly favourable demand backdrop consistent with Aequs'
framing. AZAD frames FY26/FY27 as riding an "unprecedented synchronized high growth
cycle" across energy, aerospace, and defence (Q1 FY27, Rakesh Chopdar, p.3). UNIMECH
describes "structural multi-year expansions of all global aerospace industries" (Q3 FY26,
Ramakrishna Kamojhala, p.3) and later confirms the recovery materialized (Q4 FY26,
Anil Kumar Puttan, p.3). DYNAMATECH's 2024 narrative describes a rare period where
"commercial and fighter jet booming at the same time" (p.7) alongside a post-COVID
global supply-chain breakdown that favoured established suppliers. No peer contradicts
the demand-tailwind narrative. The one material qualifier: UNIMECH itself lived through
a severe, idiosyncratic demand SHOCK in Q3 FY26 — US tariffs cut its aero-tooling order
pickup and roughly halved sequential revenue (Rs61cr to Rs34cr; Q3 FY26, Ramakrishna
Kamojhala, p.7) — a risk type of a different character (US tariff exposure on exports) than
anything Aequs discloses in its three calls, despite Aequs also being ~93-94% USD-linked
per the stage-5 finding. This is a demonstrated real-world shock that hit a very similar
business model within the same period and was not visible in Aequs' own risk framing.

### 2B. Pricing and input costs
AZAD attributes margin stability to long-term fixed-price contracts with a narrow (~5%)
customer-shared raw-material cost-fluctuation band (Q2 FY26, Rakesh Chopdar, p.9-10)
and a near-total natural FX hedge (93.9% exports; Q2 FY26, Rakesh Chopdar, p.9).
UNIMECH's cost commentary centres on tariff-cost-sharing (~5% absorbed on US-bound
parts; Q4 FY26, Ramakrishna Kamojhala, p.5) rather than raw-material pricing, consistent
with its lower-materials-intensity tooling model. Neither peer corroborates the specific
West Asia lead-time/logistics-cost shock Aequs describes (see Q2 above); both frame
margin resilience as a function of contract structure and FX hedging rather than input-cost
trends. This neither supports nor contradicts Aequs' margin narrative directly, but it does
NOT corroborate the specific West Asia-driven cost shock as an industry-wide event.

### 2C. Capex cycle
This is an industry-wide capacity race, not a lone-expander situation. AZAD capitalized
INR392cr in FY26 alone and continues guiding further capex through FY27-28 (Q4 FY26,
Ronak Jajoo, p.6-7). UNIMECH deployed INR450cr on the Hobel acquisition, committed
$30mn (51% share) to a Saudi JV, and separately sought board approval for an enabling
INR750cr QIP resolution (Q1 FY27, Aakash Jaiswal, p.18). DYNAMATECH's 2024 narrative
similarly describes continuous multi-geography capacity investment (UK, Germany,
India) as a permanent feature of its model, not a one-off response to a single order win.
Read against Aequs' own capex acceleration (Rs660cr FY27, Hosur Rs1,900cr/10yr,
Karnataka Rs2,856cr/5yr, group 5-year USD350-400mn), this is best read as a sector-wide
capacity race rather than a company-specific overreach signal — though it also means
sector-wide ramp/stabilization-timing risk (not demand risk) is the common thread, since
AZAD has now described FY26 as a "stabilization year" across all four of its calls in this
set and UNIMECH's own qualification-cycle language runs in parallel (see Part 5).

### 2D. Competitive mentions
No peer transcript mentions Aequs by name or by unmistakable description (confirmed
via a full-text search across all nine files). Dynamic Industries is named once in Aequs'
own Q3 FY26 call (per stage 5) as an adjacent Airbus-door-program customer, but no
reciprocal mention of Aequs (or Dynamic Industries, for that matter) appears in any peer
transcript reviewed here. This yields no competitive intelligence in either direction.

### 2E. Risks peers discuss that Aequs does not
- **US tariff exposure on export-oriented aerospace supply.** UNIMECH's entire FY26
  narrative centres on a US-tariff-driven order-pickup slowdown (Q3 FY26, Anil Kumar
  Puttan, p.3) that halved sequential revenue. Aequs' three calls (per stage 5) discuss
  West Asia logistics disruption but no US tariff risk, despite Aequs being ~93-94%
  USD-linked. This is a plausible, undisclosed risk analogue worth flagging.
- **Manpower/hiring bottleneck as an explicit capacity constraint.** AZAD discusses
  a dedicated 1,000-worker hiring program, sourcing 150-200 people/month, and
  a 50-90-day training pipeline as a named execution risk (Q3 FY26, Vishnu Malpani,
  p.13-14; Q1 FY27, Vishnu Malpani, p.16). Aequs' calls (per stage 5) do not name
  labour/hiring capacity as an explicit constraint.
- **Client in-house insourcing of tooling work.** UNIMECH was asked directly whether
  a client was moving MRO tooling work in-house (Q4 FY26, Ananya Nichani, p.12) —
  a supplier-disintermediation risk type not raised in any Aequs call.
- **Formal certification/qualification-cycle risk named explicitly (NADCAP, AS9100).**
  Both AZAD and UNIMECH name specific certifications (NADCAP for coatings/heat
  treatment; AS9100 for Hobel's path into aerospace) as multi-quarter gating items
  with defined timelines (Q1 FY27, AZAD Vishnu Malpani, p.13; UNIMECH Rajanikanth
  Balaraman, Q4 FY26, p.11: "6 to 9 months for the certification"). Aequs' calls discuss
  audits/validations in general terms but do not name specific certification standards
  as a gating risk with a timeline the way both peers do.
- **Geopolitical delay risk to an overseas facility plan.** AZAD's own Saudi Arabia
  (Baker Hughes) facility timeline was pushed out due to regional conflict (Q4 FY26,
  Vishnu Malpani, p.13: "the time lines have been shifted"). Aequs has no
  Middle East facility exposure, so this is not directly transferable, but it is a related
  risk category (execution risk tied to regional conflict) that Aequs' own West Asia
  commentary does not extend to facility/capex-timeline risk, only to logistics cost.

## PART 4: TRIANGULATION SUMMARY

- **Claims verified:** 3 of 9 (Q4 demand/order-book corroboration; Q6 NWC-day norms; Q8 capex-financing pattern)
- **Claims partially verified:** 2 of 9 (Q3 margin band — direction confirmed, magnitude runs higher at peers; Q9 import dependency — direction confirmed, magnitude unquantified)

- **Claims unverifiable:** 4 of 9 (Q1 India-share %; Q2 specific lead-time/logistics magnitudes; Q5 concentration %; Q7 consumer/toys, by construction)
- **The single most consequential contradiction, if any:** none rises to a clean contradiction. The closest candidate is Q3 (margin band): two listed peers post materially HIGHER blended EBITDA margins (30s) than Aequs' 20-27% aerospace-segment band, which complicates rather than supports the "vertically integrated ecosystem gives us a margin edge" framing Aequs' management used when pressed by analysts (per stage 5, 3C). This is a priority item for synthesis even though it is graded PARTIALLY VERIFIED rather than CONTRADICTED, because it undercuts the qualitative thrust of Aequs' own answer to that exact analyst question.
- **The single strongest independent confirmation:** Q4 (Airbus/Boeing single-aisle demand) and Q6 (NWC-day norms) are both cleanly VERIFIED by two independent, current-period peers each. Of the two, Q6 is the more decision-useful: AZAD runs consistently HIGHER working-capital days (140-200) than Aequs across three consecutive quarters, which materially weakens any reading of Aequs' 120-151-day NWC profile (and its FY26 negative operating cash flow, per stage 5) as an outlier execution failure specific to Aequs; it is closer to sector-normal for this business model.
- **Overall:** the peer set neither uniformly supports nor uniformly undercuts Aequs' narrative — it is a genuinely mixed and mostly complicating read.
