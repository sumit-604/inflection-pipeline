## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

**2A Demand environment.** All three peers describe FY27 as a recovery/growth year
after a subdued FY25-26: QLINE guides ~30-35% FY27 revenue growth off a 9% FY26 print
(QLINE, transcript p.4-5); MOLBIO guides ~25% topline growth, explicitly non-linear
because it "operate[s] in public health" (MOLBIO, transcript p.9-10); TARSONS
delivered a 21% Q1 FY27 growth surprise after several tepid years and management now
frames "15%" as an achievable baseline with upside from new products (TARSONS, Q1
FY27 call, transcript p.9, p.12-13). This is a broadly consistent, sector-wide
picture of improving demand into FY27, which loosely supports Avience's optimistic
framing of the period. But none of the three peers guides anywhere close to
Avience's >=60% FY27 growth target; Avience's number is an outlier in scale even
against a genuinely improving sector backdrop, and that gap is exactly what rests on
the single unverified Uttarakhand order.

**2B Pricing and input costs.** No peer discusses diagnostic-reagent-specific raw
material trends. TARSONS (plastics/polymer inputs) reports a sharp, geopolitically
driven cost spike with only partial, lagged pass-through and continued domestic
price competition from post-COVID overcapacity; QLINE and MOLBIO report margin
EXPANSION over the period (QLINE EBITDA margin 22.5% -> 28.7%; MOLBIO EBITDA margin
holding near 24-25%), with no commentary on reagent/chemical input cost pressure.
This gives no direct read on Avience's own margin narrative, but shows that within
this peer set, the businesses closer to Avience's reagent/instrument category
(QLINE, MOLBIO) were margin-expanding in the same window Avience reports flattish-
to-improving margins, which is at least not contradictory.

**2C Capex cycle.** QLINE (4th manufacturing facility commissioned FY26, planning
further utilisation) and TARSONS (Rs550-650 Cr multi-year Panchla/Amta build, still
mid-ramp) are both active capacity expanders, similar to Avience's YEIDA build.
MOLBIO is the outlier: it explicitly states it needs "minimal capex" to double
revenue given 58%/42% existing utilisation on test kits/devices, deploying only
~Rs72 Cr of its Rs200 Cr IPO proceeds to automation rather than new capacity (MOLBIO,
transcript p.6, p.14). Read together: this is an industry-wide capacity-building
period, not a lone-expander situation — two of three peers are mid-capex-cycle at
the same time as Avience, which is the less alarming of the two scenarios named in
the brief, but it does NOT mean the capex will convert on schedule (see Q4 above).

**2D Competitive mentions.** No peer mentions Avience, Mindray, or an unmistakable
description of either across any of the six transcripts. Silence noted, not treated
as evidence either way.

**2E Risks peers discuss that Avience's own materials do not.**
- TARSONS names a structural government-procurement risk not raised anywhere in
  Avience's corpus: the Government e-Marketplace (GeM) "L1" lowest-bid process has
  "significantly brought down business volumes" for government-linked lab-consumable
  sales over 5-7 years, with government now only ~15-20% of the ~Rs1,300 Cr Indian
  plastic labware market versus a materially higher historical share, because
  research institutes have "abolished rate contracts" in favour of per-purchase GeM
  bidding (TARSONS, Q1 FY27 call, 11-Aug-2026, transcript p.17-18). This is directly
  relevant to B00 LBF1's government-order-plausibility test: it is a live example, in
  an adjacent lab-consumables business, of government-channel revenue proving less
  durable and less linear than a single "order in hand" headline implies.
- MOLBIO's TB testing runs on a periodically renegotiated "two-year rate contract"
  with the government (just renewed), a renewal-risk structure Avience's Uttarakhand
  order narrative never addresses (no stated contract duration or renewal terms).
- QLINE discloses that a material share of its business runs through a related-party
  distributor (its largest, UP-based distributor is a promoter entity) and that it
  is actively winding down historical corporate guarantees extended to related
  parties (QLINE, transcript p.13-14) — a related-party governance risk category
  structurally analogous to (though not identical to) Avience's own related-party
  exposure to its subsidiary DR Meditech (B00 LBF3).
- TARSONS names acute, externally driven raw-material and logistics volatility
  (Middle East conflict, container/freight availability, US tariff uncertainty) as a
  recurring, multi-quarter margin and export-timing risk. Avience's call does not
  discuss import/logistics risk on its Mindray-sourced traded reagents/instruments at
  all, despite ~72% of FY26 revenue being traded goods.

## PART 5: CROSS-PEER HYPOTHESIS

All three peers with a recurring-revenue "placed-hardware, sold-consumable" model
(QLINE and MOLBIO directly; TARSONS at one remove through its cell-culture/bioprocess
push) anchor the credibility of that model in specific, disclosed UNIT ECONOMICS:
QLINE gives per-analyzer lifetime value (~Rs 25,000-30,000/month reagent spend,
~Rs 30 lakh lifetime value over a 10-year life, split by analyzer type); MOLBIO gives
device counts, tests-per-disease, and a stated device replacement cycle (still zero
replacements after 6-7 years); TARSONS, while not a razor-blade business, discloses
concrete asset-turnover assumptions (0.7-0.8x) and per-facility breakeven revenue
figures for its own capex. Avience discloses none of the equivalent numbers for its
own placed-instrument base: no average reagent revenue per placed instrument, no
count of currently-active rental contracts, no per-instrument lifetime value (flagged
as NOT FOUND in B04's unit_economics block). The testable hypothesis: sector practice
at this business-model stage is to disclose per-unit economics as the evidentiary
backbone of a recurring-revenue thesis, and Avience's failure to disclose any
equivalent figure is not merely a data gap but a signal that its own placed-
instrument base and reagent attach-rate may be too small, too recent, or too poorly
tracked internally to support the same disclosure standard its comparable peers
already meet at a similar or earlier stage of listing. This should be tested directly
by asking management for active-installation count and per-instrument reagent
run-rate at the next opportunity (already flagged in B04's mgmt_questions); if the
company cannot produce it, the razor-and-blade claim stays structurally unverifiable
regardless of how many quarters pass.
