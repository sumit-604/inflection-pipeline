# India Glycols splits into three listed companies

## Business understanding narrative

India Glycols sells four different things through one listed shell. Bio-based specialty and performance chemicals (glycols, glycol ethers, specialty molecules, industrial gases) make up 29% of net revenue. Potable spirits (IMFL, country liquor, extra neutral alcohol) make up 32%. Fuel-grade ethanol sold under the government Ethanol Blending Programme makes up 35%. Plant APIs, nutraceuticals and nicotine under the Ennature bio-pharma line make up 5%. Each line serves a different customer for a different reason. Oil marketing companies buy the ethanol on a fixed quota at a set price each supply year. State excise systems and consumers buy the spirits, with Bacardi (a 15 year tie), Amrut distribution, and a new canteen and paramilitary channel behind the branded volume. Industrial buyers and sustainability-mandated European and American brand owners buy the bio-MEG and glycol ethers. The demand you can see today tracks a short list of outside signals: OMC ethanol tender results, Uttar Pradesh and Uttarakhand excise revenue, the Brent crude price that sets bio-MEG competitiveness, the China glycol-ether price index that sets export margin, and the Clariant joint venture. The demand that could grow tracks four things: any move from 20% ethanol blending toward E25 or E100, more institutional spirit approvals, bio-amine customer qualifications at BASF, Unilever and Mibelle, and premiumisation of the spirits mix. The moat is not spread evenly. Spirits carries the one real lock-in, the qualification and exclusivity ties to Bacardi, Amrut and the canteen channel, scored Strong. Chemicals rests on a "sole Indian producer" claim that is self-reported, unverified against peers, and losing share while its market grows. Bio-fuel has no moat; it is a quota the government sets, and the quota fell 22% this year. Ennature holds a narrow lead in one API, Thiocolchicoside, with all the risk that single-product concentration carries. The emerging moat score is 33 and STRENGTHENING, driven by the spirits exclusivities and a six year margin trend, not by the chemicals story.

## What the shares are today

Rs 1,191 per share and Rs 7,998 Cr of market value price the whole pre-demerger group. That group stops existing on 1 September 2026. The scheme, sanctioned by the NCLT Allahabad bench on 17 July 2026, splits it three ways with a record date of 2 September 2026. A holder keeps the residual India Glycols (chemicals plus gases). A holder receives one IGL Spirits share for each share held (spirits plus bio-fuel). A holder receives one Ennature Bio Pharma share for every three held (bio-pharma). Every backward metric in this file describes a consolidated entity on a single earnings base. Three separately listed, separately rated companies inherit pieces of it on different bases eight days after this run.

## What the numbers say

40 out of 100 on the Gate 0 core score, an AVERAGE grade, corrected down from 42 after a framework re-band. The grand total is 44 out of 160. Zero moats scored high enough to confirm, mostly for lack of peer data, not proven absence. Return on capital sits in the low teens, near 12% on the framework formula and 10% on the annual report's own consolidated figure. Revenue compounded 5.97% over nine years, unremarkable, and the headline profit growth is flattered by a depressed base year and a one-off other-income year. The one bright number is cash conversion: operating cash flow ran above twice reported profit across ten years.

## The cash reality

0.85 is the current ratio for FY26, below 1.0 and down from 0.90 the year before. On the framework formula it reads 0.74. Point-in-time liquidity is tightening even as the working-capital cycle improves; both readings are true at once. Free cash flow was negative in both years, though it moved from minus Rs 398 Cr to minus Rs 54 Cr as a Bio-MEG and ethylene-oxide capex programme ran off. Capex was 5.3 times depreciation. A Rs 467 Cr preferential equity issue in November 2025 went mostly to working capital (Rs 350 Cr) and term-loan repayment (Rs 117 Cr), not a cash build. The one accounting red flag: the foreign-exchange hedge book collapsed 92% in the year, from USD 52.86 million notional to USD 3.73 million, while unhedged exposure roughly doubled, with no explanation anywhere in the report.

## What management says against what it does

Grade B on credibility. Management delivered the core financial guidance on margin path and debt reduction, and it separates aspiration from projection. Against that, it stayed silent on the hedge-book collapse across all three calls, never named the 22% ethanol allocation cut while calling bio-fuel "well-positioned for sustained growth," and deflected a repeated segment-debt question. It prepaid Rs 804 Cr of debt in one quarter, but that netted to only about Rs 220 Cr of net-debt reduction because the rest was a cash-credit limit cut. The scorecard on nine tracked promises: 2 delivered, 4 partial, 3 missed.

## The promoter read

TRUSTWORTHY, on a partial-status stage. 0% of promoter holding is pledged on the current snapshot. The personal legal record is clean across SEBI, criminal, and enforcement checks. The transition evidence is real and multi-pronged: institutional allottees including QRG Investments and 360 One in the equity raise, an outside professional hired as spirits COO rather than a family member, a coordinated independent-director refresh, and the promoter holding company folding into the listed entity rather than adding a layer. One item stays open: a Rs 20.4 Cr legacy receivable from the 2013 NSEL default, still carried as good 13 years on, with the annual report's own notes disagreeing on whether the balance is even open at year-end.

## What the peers confirm and complicate

Three independent peers confirm the core claim that the ethanol industry has excess capacity against blending demand. That corroborates the risk in the bio-fuel line being demerged out. Two peers directionally confirm that OMC ethanol allocations are falling, but no peer confirms the specific 22% magnitude; treat the direction as verified, the number as unconfirmed. The apparent contradiction on Uttar Pradesh market size (India Glycols cites 23 to 25 lakh cases a month, Globus cites near a crore) is reconcilable: India Glycols is counting an IMFL-only slice while Globus counts the total regular-and-outside market. It is a segment-scope mismatch, not a factual conflict. The bio-glycols realisation decline of 30% cannot be peer-checked at all, because no peer in the set makes glycols.

## Where the market opportunity sits

MODERATE runway for the residual chemicals entity, the one a continuing holder keeps. Its addressable market grew about 6.7% a year, yet BSPC segment revenue fell about 26% over three years, from Rs 1,626 Cr to Rs 1,202 Cr. The company is losing share in a growing market. The largest driver of that fall is price, not demand: bio-glycols realisation dropped 30% while volume rose, and production fell to 37,522 MT from 47,146 MT. Current share of the addressable market is 6%, so headroom exists on paper at 16.6 times. Whether the residual entity converts headroom into revenue depends on the petro-MEG spread turning, which management itself ties to elevated crude.
