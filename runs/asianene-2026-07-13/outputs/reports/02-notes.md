PASS 3 NEW FINDINGS
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════

1. **The Corporate Overview narrative states the Kuiper Group acquisition "was closed," directly
   contradicting the audited notes, which treat it strictly as a post-balance-sheet Share Purchase
   Agreement not yet reflected in any FY25 number.** Page 13 ("Acquisition of Kuiper Group"): "We
   entered into an agreement to acquire a 100% equity stake in Kuiper Group, UAE, from Gulf Capital,
   a marquee private equity investor. **The deal was closed** at a total cash consideration of US$
   9.25 Million." This "closed" language is repeated in substance at p.6 ("100% Stake, Acquisition of
   Kuiper Group, UAE" listed as a completed "Proven Excellence" metric alongside "31 Years of
   Experience" and "50+ Projects Successfully Completed") and p.18 Business Overview ("The strategic
   acquisition of Kuiper enables Asian Energy not only to nearly double its size..."). Against this,
   the audited consolidated Note 48B (subsequent events, p.201, per Pass 1 §0) states only that
   "ADMCC entered a Share Purchase Agreement **after 31-Mar-2025** to acquire Kuiper Holdings Ltd and
   Kuiper Group Ltd" — SPA execution, not confirmed completion/closing, and explicitly dated after
   the balance sheet date. Nothing in the audited FY25 financials (standalone or consolidated)
   reflects any Kuiper asset, liability, revenue, expense, or cash consideration outflow. Also note a
   secondary inconsistency in the deal economics as narrated: p.10 gives cash consideration of
   "US$ 9.25 Million" for a business with "revenues of approximately US$ 68 Million" (FY24) and an
   expected "incremental revenue stream of approximately US$ 70 Million" — a purchase price under
   14% of one year's revenue for the target, which is either an extraordinarily attractive deal or a
   sign that the reader cannot verify deal terms independently from the glossy narrative alone; the
   audited notes provide no purchase price allocation, funding structure, or consideration
   confirmation because the transaction sits entirely outside the audit boundary. 🔴 Investors valuing
   the business off the narrative "nearly double its size" framing are working from unaudited,
   non-GAAP-reconciled representations that use the word "closed" where the financial statements use
   "subsequent event / agreement entered." This is a disclosure-consistency red flag independent of
   whether the underlying deal itself is sound.

2. **The company's own headline "EBITDA" and "EBITDA margin" figures used throughout the Corporate
   Overview and Financial Highlights are a non-GAAP measure that includes the equity-method share of
   profit from joint ventures — a below-the-EBITDA-line item under any conventional definition —
   inflating the reported operating profitability metric.** Page 14, Financial Highlights: "EBITDA
   rose to ₹72.3 Crores, with margins improving to 15.5%, up from 14.2%..." with a footnote:
   "*EBITDA includes share in profit/loss from joint ventures and associates." The Group's share of
   JV profit for FY25 is ₹618.66 Lakhs (Note 7 consolidated, p.168, already flagged in Pass 1 as
   72.9% concentrated in the 13-month-old Asian Indwell JV). Equity-method JV income is, by
   definition, the JV's own post-tax, post-interest, post-depreciation result flowing through a
   single line — including it inside "EBITDA" (Earnings **Before** Interest, Tax, Depreciation and
   Amortization) is a non-standard and profitability-flattering construction, even though it is
   disclosed via footnote rather than hidden. Consolidated Key Ratios on p.19 (Debtors' Turnover,
   Current Ratio, Operating Profit Margin, Net Profit Margin, Return on Net Worth) are presented
   without similar caveats and a reader skimming the glossy front-matter would reasonably assume
   "EBITDA margin 15.5%" reflects core seismic/O&M/CHP operating performance alone. 🟡 Disclosed, not
   concealed, but an aggressive presentation choice that a GARP-focused investor modeling multiples
   off reported EBITDA should adjust for.

No other pattern-level contradictions, prior-period restatements, or going-concern language were

item d), and both the standalone and consolidated Independent Auditor's Reports contain no material

5. What is driving contract assets (unbilled work-in-progress) to grow 234% against 52% revenue growth — is this a genuine timing lag in customer certification/billing cycles, and what is the ageing and collectability outlook for this unbilled balance, particularly the related-party-JV portion of it?

## E. NOTES-BASED RED FLAGS

- **Cross-document contradiction on Kuiper:** Corporate Overview narrates the acquisition as "closed" (p.13) and a completed "100% Stake" metric (p.6), while audited Note 48B treats it strictly as a subsequent-event SPA — investors relying on the glossy narrative risk materially misreading FY25 financial reality.
- **MD remuneration disclosed inconsistently** (₹277L vs ₹157L) between two sections of the same annual report, unresolved as of the report date.
- **Asian Indwell JV revenue mismatch** between standalone and consolidated RPT notes (₹315.28L / 2.9% gap) — the single largest, newest, most receivables-concentrated related party has internally inconsistent numbers.
- **Non-GAAP "EBITDA"** in the Financial Highlights includes JV equity-pickup profit, inflating the headline operating-profitability metric versus a conventional EBITDA definition (disclosed via footnote, not concealed, but easily missed).
- **Simultaneous deterioration in all four working-capital efficiency ratios** despite strong reported earnings growth — a classic earnings-quality warning sign; feeds FLAG-CASH.
- **No impairment on the ADMCC investment** despite a substantial accumulated deficit and nil turnover; governance lapse in pursuing recovery of the AOSL Petroleum related-party loan per the auditor's own language ("reasonable steps have not been taken").
- No evidence of outright earnings manipulation, off-book liabilities, or restated prior-period figures was found; the flags above are disclosure-consistency, concentration, and presentation-quality issues rather than indicators of fraud.

## F. ONE-LINE NOTES VERDICT

The notes reveal moderate accounting practices with unresolved disclosure contradictions. Key
concern: cross-document inconsistencies (Kuiper narrated as "closed" versus Note 48B's subsequent-event
treatment, MD remuneration mismatch, Asian Indwell JV revenue gap) layered on genuine cash-conversion
deterioration despite strong reported earnings. Key strength: low leverage, conservative oil-asset
accounting, unmodified audit opinions, and full first-year CSR compliance. Overall accounting quality:
6/10.
