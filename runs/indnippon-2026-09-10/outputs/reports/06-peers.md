## PART 1: CLAIM-BY-CLAIM VERIFICATION

### Q1. Industry growth comparator
**Claim:** INEL cites 2W industry growth of 11%, 14%, 19%, 20% for Q2FY26-Q1FY27 respectively,
naming no source.

**Verdict: PARTIALLY VERIFIED**

**Peer evidence:**
- Q2FY26 (Jul-Sep25): Varroc 2W production grew 10.6% YoY (VARROC Nov2025, page 5); Minda
  reported overall auto industry +10.1% YoY (MINDACORP Nov2025, page 4). INEL's 11% sits almost
  exactly on Varroc's reading.
- Q3FY26 (Oct-Dec25): Varroc 2W grew 15% YoY, 9M FY26 2W +8.8% YoY (VARROC Feb2026, page 4);
  Minda reported overall industry +16.8-17% YoY (MINDACORP Feb2026, pages 4-5). INEL's 14% sits
  close to, slightly below, Varroc's 15%.
- Q4FY26 (Jan-Mar26): Varroc 2W grew 20.7% YoY (VARROC Jun2026, page 3-4); Minda reported 2W
  +21% YoY, overall industry +20% (MINDACORP May2026, page 4). INEL's 19% is directionally
  correct but sits below both peer readings.
- Q1FY27 (Apr-Jun26): Varroc 2W grew 22.8% YoY, EV 2W volume +91% YoY (VARROC Aug2026, page 3);
  Minda reported 2W +23%, overall industry +22% (MINDACORP Aug2026, pages 3-4); Pricol cited
  "industry put together weighted average grew by 22%" the same quarter (PRICOLLTD Aug2026,
  page 17). INEL's 20% is now noticeably below all three peer readings (22-23%).

**Peers silent on:** No peer transcript names SIAM or any other industry body as the source for
its own cited growth figures. All three peers state industry production/growth numbers as
management assertions, unattributed, exactly as INEL does. The source-naming gap is therefore
an industry-wide disclosure norm, not an INEL-specific opacity.

**Net read:** The trend and rough magnitude of INEL's comparator track peer-reported 2W growth
closely for three of four quarters, then diverge meaningfully in Q1FY27, where INEL's 20%
denominator understates the ~22-23% peer consensus. If the true industry benchmark for Q1FY27
was closer to 22-23%, INEL's claimed outperformance (+35.5% vs +20%) overstates its edge by
roughly 2-3 points (true gap closer to 12-13pts than the claimed 15.5pts). The source-attribution
gap is not a red flag specific to INEL; it is standard peer practice.

### Q2. Rare-earth NdFeB magnet supply and pass-through mechanism/lag
**Claim:** INEL names magnet supply constraints and China trade dynamics as headwinds and
describes cost recovery as "effectively managed," with no mechanism or lag disclosed.

**Verdict: VERIFIED** (that a concrete, quantified mechanism and lag exist industry-wide;
this exposes INEL's disclosure as materially thinner than sector norm)

**Peer evidence:**
- Varroc: rare-earth magnet crisis cost ~4% of India revenue in Q2FY26 (Rs750mn), "recoverable
  from customers" for freight costs, "no bottom-line impact on margins... other than loss of
  sales" (VARROC Nov2025, pages 3-4, 14). Crisis "largely passed" by Q3FY26 (VARROC Feb2026,
  page 9, Ronak Jain Q&A). By Q1FY27, Varroc quantifies a distinct "genuine under-recovery"
  of 0.5% EBITDA plus a further 0.25% "numerator-denominator effect," with explicit guidance
  that "there could be... a lag of at least a quarter, but we are expecting to be compensated
  in full despite this lag" (VARROC Aug2026, pages 5, 8, Tarang Jain and Naman Maheshwari Q&A).
- Pricol: "we are indexed back to back 100% with all customers... there will be only a lag of
  three to six months. However, it is all recoverable from the customer" (PRICOLLTD Feb2026,
  page 3, explicit answer to Akhilesh Rawat). By Q4FY26, Pricol quantifies a six-month FOREX
  correction lag and states cost recovery will be "tripartite" (OEM, Tier-1, end customer each
  absorb some) rather than 100% automatic (PRICOLLTD May2026, page 8).
- Minda: rare-earth issue at Flash Electronics "relatively solved" by Q2FY26 through ferrite
  and magnet-less motor development, a named engineering fix rather than only a commercial
  workaround (MINDACORP Nov2025, page 9). Commodity pass-through described as "back-to-back
  arrangement" with vendors and customers, true-up "every quarter" (MINDACORP Feb2026, page 9);
  by Q4FY26, Minda quantifies 30-40% YoY commodity escalation and states pass-through carries
  "no profit element," so EBITDA margin still absorbs an optical hit despite full cost recovery
  (MINDACORP May2026, page 10).

**Peers silent on:** none of the three declined to discuss lag or mechanism when asked.

**Net read:** Every peer volunteers or answers directly on mechanism (indexed/back-to-back
contracts) and lag (one quarter to six months, quantified). INEL's "effectively managed"
framing, with zero mechanism and zero lag disclosed across five decks, is a materially thinner
disclosure than the sector standard set by all three peers, not evidence the underlying
recovery process differs.

### Q4. OEM receivable cycles — THE CRUX QUESTION
**Claim:** INEL's receivables near-term-overdue bucket rose 152.4% to a three-year-worst 22.3%
overdue share with zero ECL recognised; customers are TVS Motor, Hero MotoCorp and Bajaj Auto.

**Verdict: VERIFIED (industry-wide benign picture); this CONTRADICTS a reading that INEL's
build is an industry-wide OEM payment-cycle effect**

**Peer evidence:**
- Varroc, on direct analyst question about a Q1FY27 working-capital increase: "there is no
  change [in] receivable days. Yes, inventory went up to some extent, largely in preparation to
  the peak season... it's a temporary increase" (VARROC Aug2026, page 17, Mahendra Kumar to
  Neha Garg, verbatim). Varroc's customer base is Bajaj-concentrated (58% of FY27 order book,
  VARROC Aug2026, page 6) rather than the TVS/Hero/Bajaj tri-customer mix INEL carries, but
  Bajaj is a shared counterparty across both books.
- Pricol attributes a finance-cost increase entirely to working-capital usage tied to "large
  growth in our sales," not to receivable-day deterioration or overdue buckets (PRICOLLTD
  Feb2026, page 7, Ganesh and Bastia to Karan Gupta). No mention of any overdue-ageing concern
  in any of the four Pricol calls.
- Minda: no receivable-days or ageing discussion appears in any of the four Minda transcripts;
  working-capital commentary is confined to capex funding and net debt levels.

**Peers silent on:** none of the three volunteered an ageing or ECL disclosure unprompted; only
Varroc was asked and answered directly and specifically.

**Net read:** This is the single most consequential finding of this stage. The one peer that
was asked the direct question — on the identical topic, in the identical quarter (Q1FY27) — 
explicitly denied any change in receivable days and attributed its own WC increase to inventory
and invoice-timing, not customer payment delay. No peer flags an overdue-receivables build of
any kind. This weakens the benign, payment-cycle-timing reading of INEL's 152.4% overdue-bucket
spike. The evidence available points toward a company-specific deterioration at INEL, not a
sector-wide OEM payment-cycle shift.

### Q5. EV component economics for a magneto-led supplier
**Claim:** whether EV content expands or destroys content for a magneto-led (ICE ignition
system) supplier like INEL.

**Verdict: VERIFIED** (EV content expands total addressable spend per vehicle, but the
expansion accrues to EV-specific component lines, not to legacy ICE ignition-system parts)

**Peer evidence:**
- Varroc quantifies EV content at "5 to 7x" ICE content per vehicle (VARROC Nov2025, page 13).
  Varroc's EV revenue share rose from ~11% (Q2FY26) to ~16% (Q1FY27) with EV revenue growth of
  87-91% YoY, but this growth flows through e-powertrain, motor-controller and BMS product
  lines that Varroc built specifically for EV, not through its legacy ICE ignition/engine-
  electricals lines (VARROC Aug2026, pages 3, 14). Varroc explicitly separates "engine agnostic"
  products from powertrain-specific ones when discussing this transition (VARROC Aug2026,
  page 14).
- Minda/Flash Electronics: EV revenue share at Flash rose to ~30% with 90% YoY growth in
  Q1FY27 (MINDACORP Aug2026, page 4), driven by newly developed magnet-less/ferrite traction
  motors and power-electronics products, an entirely new product category built to capture the
  EV opportunity, not a repricing of existing ICE parts (MINDACORP Nov2025, page 9; MINDACORP
  Aug2026, page 19).
- Pricol: no direct EV-vs-ICE component economics discussion found; Pricol's DIS/ACFMS products
  (clusters, sensors, brakes) are largely powertrain-agnostic and unaffected either way.

**Net read:** EV content does not expand automatically for an incumbent ICE-parts maker; it
expands for whichever supplier has built or acquired the specific EV-native product (motor,
controller, high-voltage harness). Both Varroc and Minda answer this by standing up new EV
product lines (via Turntide, Flash, dedicated e-powertrain capacity) rather than by claiming
their legacy ICE product set captures EV upside. This is directly relevant to INEL, whose core
products (Fly Wheel Magneto, CDI) are ICE-specific: absent a comparable pivot into EV-native
components, rising 2W EV penetration is a content-destruction risk for INEL's legacy lines,
not a content-expansion tailwind, regardless of overall industry volume growth.

## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

**2A. Demand environment.** All three peers describe a consistent, strengthening 2W/3W/PV/CV
growth trajectory across Q2FY26 through Q1FY27, driven by GST 2.0 rate cuts (Sep 2025), festive
season, rural income recovery and accelerating EV adoption (registrations crossing 5 lakh units
in a single quarter for the first time, MINDACORP Aug2026, page 3). This corroborates INEL's
framing of a genuine industry tailwind, though the peer-reported Q1FY27 industry pace (22-23%)
runs somewhat ahead of INEL's own comparator (20%), as noted under Q1 above.

**2B. Pricing and input costs.** All three peers converge on the same margin narrative: raw
material and rare-earth cost increases are contractually indexed and recoverable, but recovery
lags by one quarter to six months, and EBITDA margin absorbs a temporary "numerator-denominator"
drag even when 100% of the cost is eventually passed through (Varroc Q1FY27: 0.75% EBITDA hit
split 0.5% genuine under-recovery + 0.25% arithmetic effect; Minda: "no profit element attached
to the escalation" so margin still compresses on a percentage basis). INEL's margin narrative
("effectively managed") does not engage with this mechanism at all, a materially thinner
treatment than the sector standard.

**2C. Capex cycle.** This is an industry-wide capacity race, not a lone expander. All three
peers are running the heaviest capex programmes in their respective histories simultaneously:
Varroc Rs450-550cr FY27 (VARROC Aug2026, page 16), Pricol Rs680-700cr planned for FY26 alone
plus a further Rs700cr FY27-28 cycle explicitly tied to a demerger to attract capital partners
(PRICOLLTD May2026 page 13; Aug2026 page 8), Minda Rs400-450cr FY27 within a Rs2,000cr five-year
plan (MINDACORP Aug2026, page 12). All three fund this from internal accruals with net
debt/EBITDA held below 0.6x (Varroc net debt/EBITDA ~0.5-0.6x; Pricol near-zero net debt of
Rs63cr as of FY26-end, PRICOLLTD May2026, page 5; Minda net debt/equity ~0.5x). INEL's disclosed
Rs42.15cr FY26 capex sits at a fraction of peer absolute spend, with no comparable content-
expansion narrative attached to it in any deck.

**2D. Competitive mentions.** No peer transcript names India Nippon Electricals or gives an
unmistakable description of INEL, positively or negatively, across all twelve calls. This
silence is itself a finding: INEL's Fly Wheel Magneto/CDI niche does not register as a
competitive reference point for these three larger, more diversified electronics-focused
peers, consistent with INEL's narrower product scope rather than evidence of irrelevance.

**2E. Risks peers discuss that INEL does not.**
- Nexperia semiconductor shortage, a distinct industry-wide chip crisis layered on top of the
  rare-earth magnet issue in the same period (PRICOLLTD Nov2025, page 4; Feb2026, page 3) —
  absent from INEL's disclosures per the B05 corpus review.
- New labour-code/minimum-wage cost inflation, quantified by all three peers (Varroc labour-
  code exceptional item ~Rs22.5mn, VARROC Feb2026 page 4; Minda new labour-law exceptional
  ~Rs4cr, MINDACORP Feb2026 page 6; Pricol minimum-wage increase ~Rs21cr annualised, PRICOLLTD
  Aug2026 page 6) — not referenced in INEL's corpus per B05.
- One-time tooling-sale margin dilution disclosed with precise EBITDA-point quantification
  (Varroc: 0.8% EBITDA impact from a single large tooling sale, VARROC Aug2026, page 16) — a
  level of revenue-quality granularity absent from INEL's decks.
- Named litigation/arbitration exposure with a quantified amount (Varroc's OPmobility
  arbitration, EUR66mn, VARROC Feb2026, page 17) — a transparency standard not matched in
  INEL's corpus per B05's gap notes.
- West Asia/Iran-war-driven commodity, freight and currency shock (Pricol extensively, Q4FY26-
  Q1FY27) is a partial match rather than a gap: INEL's own decks do cite "Middle East" as a
  named headwind per B05's excuse-pattern note, so this corroborates rather than exposes a
  fresh gap.

## PART 5: CROSS-PEER HYPOTHESIS

All three peers show the same three-part pattern simultaneously: (1) a temporary EBITDA margin
compression of roughly 0.5-1.5 percentage points from commodity/rare-earth pass-through lag,
recoverable within one to two quarters; (2) the heaviest capex programmes in each company's
history, funded overwhelmingly by internal accruals rather than debt (net debt/EBITDA held
below 0.6x across all three); and (3) an explicit strategic pivot toward capturing incremental
content-per-vehicle through EV-native or premium electronics product lines (Varroc's e-
powertrain and passenger-vehicle lighting wins, Minda's Turntide/Flash/Toyodenso joint ventures,
Pricol's disc brakes/BMS/e-cockpit portfolio and demerger to attract technology partners), each
funded and disclosed at the individual-project level every quarter.

No single peer states this as a competitive dynamic, but the combined pattern suggests the 2W/3W
ancillary supply chain is mid-way through a structural re-rating attempt: suppliers are
deliberately keeping balance sheets light specifically to preserve capacity for a content-value
