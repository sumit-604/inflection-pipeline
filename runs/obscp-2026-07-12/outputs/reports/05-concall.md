### 2B. Excuse pattern analysis

- The one clear, acknowledged miss (margin-expansion pace) is explained with an **honest, external-blame** narrative: tariffs, the Iran/West Asia conflict, and rising shipping costs (Q4 FY26 call, Saksham Leekha: "this has been, no doubt, a challenging year... to give you a solid number is difficult"). Management does not hide behind vague language here — it is candid about difficulty and proactively lowers the guide rather than restating the old 4-5pp target.
- Two other misses (AS9100D certification timeline, the broken quarterly-disclosure cadence) are handled by **silence** — neither is raised proactively by management, and neither original promise is referenced or reconciled against the new call's content. This is a different pattern from either honest-admission or classic external-blame deflection: it is simply not addressed. Because the input set has only two calls a year apart, no analyst on the Q4 FY26 call had the standing (or apparent memory) to call this out directly.
- No instance of management saying "we made a mistake" was found in either transcript.
- Hard topics (free cash flow still negative, margin-drag risk from the capex ramp) are raised only under direct analyst pressure (Samarth Gupta; Agastya Dave), not proactively by management in opening remarks.

**Pattern check:** management does credit itself for beats in opening remarks ("surpassed on both," "beat your guidance quite handsomely across the Board" — echoed approvingly by analyst Agastya Dave) while never revisiting the two clear misses. This is a mild but real accountability asymmetry: self-promotion on wins, silence on misses, honest external-blame reserved for the one miss too large to ignore (margin).

## SECTION 3: COMPETITIVE INTELLIGENCE FROM CONCALLS

### 3A. Competitor commentary and credibility check

- Management names **Azad Engineering** as an aerospace precision-machining reference point ("perhaps we'll have similar capabilities like an Azad Engineering has right now in terms of five-axis milling") — a specific, checkable comparison, useful for peer verification (though Azad Engineering is not one of the four peers named for this run).
- When asked directly what differentiates OBSC from other precision-engineering players (Vardhman Lunia, Q4 FY26 call), Saksham Leekha's answer was notably generic — "no magic pill," "discipline," "first principles," "I don't know how our peers are different from us" — a real communication weakness when pressed for a specific competitive moat.
- General competitive framing is China+1/reshoring based rather than company-vs-company: "India is the only viable alternative," and INR depreciation vs the Yuan (~20%) cited as a structural export-competitiveness tailwind (Q4 FY26 call).

### 3B. Industry/market intelligence dropped in the calls

- **Tariffs:** US tariff uncertainty required OBSC to give customers ~10% price subsidies to keep export projects viable through FY26; management says the situation has "resolved to a certain level" as of the Q4 FY26 call, with original pricing restored and a surge in US exports expected in the next 2-3 months.
- **Europe:** described as slowing, with Chinese EV competition seen as a threat to the European auto industry; OBSC's own strategic focus has shifted toward the US and Mexico.
- **CV cycle:** one analyst flagged industry projections of a CV volume slowdown into H2 (year not fully specified); management pushes back, saying it continuously wins new CV projects that offset base-volume softness.
- **Defense indigenization:** BEL "Zero Defective Award" received for Q4 FY25; new BDL supplier registration completed in FY26; quoting started with other DPSUs (YIL, MIL).
- **Humanoid robotics and medical implants** cited as emerging precision-engineering demand verticals the company has newly entered.

### 3C. Toughest analyst questions

| Question | Call | Response | Satisfactory? | Real risk? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| When will free cash flow turn positive? (Samarth Gupta) | Q4 FY26 | "Tough to say... cash flow... going back into the business" | No — vague, no timeline | **Yes** — real risk given continuous heavy capex funded by equity/debt |
| Won't the large capex ramp (Supa etc.) drag margins via depreciation/overheads? (Agastya Dave) | Q4 FY26 | Reassurance only ("frugal," "phased," "test the soil first"), no quantification | Partially | **Yes** — genuine risk, not numerically addressed |
| What differentiates OBSC's capability from other precision players? (Vardhman Lunia) | Q4 FY26 | Generic — "discipline," "first principles," no specific moat named | No | Moderate — more a messaging gap than a fundamental threat |
| Is the order book confirmed or merely indicative? (Deepak Poddar, Tej Patel) | H2 FY25 | Detailed, specific answer on nominated vs. confirmed orders and binding-document mechanics | Yes | Low — well explained |

### 3D. Customer and order-book signals

- **Tata AutoComp:** 10th largest customer as of ~H2 FY25 call → 2nd largest customer as of Q4 FY26 call ("this month") — a major concentration shift toward one relationship, tied to Tesla-linked India EV demand via Tata AutoComp/Tata Toyo Radiator (largest single order ~Rs.250cr, EV aluminium precision parts, dedicated Plant IV, ~Rs.50cr/year nominated revenue).
- **Tenneco:** existing top-3 customer, expanded relationship via the newly confirmed Sanand plant dedicated to DaVinci shock-absorber-rod technology (~Rs.40cr incremental revenue expected from that line).
- **ZF:** growing in absolute revenue terms, but management explicitly says recent strategic focus has shifted away from Europe/ZF toward the US/Mexico — a subtle relative de-prioritization of a top-3 relationship even as its absolute revenue keeps growing.
- **Order book composition:** total order book grew from Rs.290cr (FY24) → Rs.700-750cr (FY25) → Rs.1,200cr+ (FY26), a consistent and corroborated growth trend. However, the **non-automotive order book actually declined nominally, Rs.250cr (FY25 call) → Rs.230cr (FY26 call)**, even as the automotive book roughly doubled (Rs.460cr → Rs.980cr). This is worth monitoring — it could reflect faster conversion of prior non-auto bookings into revenue outpacing new non-auto order intake, or a genuinely slower non-auto order-win pace than the "40-50% CAGR" defense/marine aspiration would imply.
- **Geographic spread:** export countries expanded to 17 in FY26; export revenue share 20% of FY26 revenue, targeted to reach 30-35% in FY27; export pricing carries a 10%+ premium over domestic.

### 4D. Concall red flags

1. **HIGH — Broken quarterly-disclosure promise.** Management explicitly committed to quarterly earnings updates in the H2 FY25 call (an analyst thanked them for this voluntary initiative), but only one further call occurred a full year later, with no acknowledgment of the gap. This is a governance/communication-discipline miss and it also degrades external ability to track the company quarter to quarter.
2. **MEDIUM — Aerospace AS9100D certification slipping quietly.** Implied "next quarter" update in the H2 FY25 call; still not certified 12 months later ("within two months" as of the Q4 FY26 call), with no acknowledgment that the original timeline was missed.
3. **MEDIUM — Free cash flow remains negative with no committed timeline** ("tough to say"), despite continuous heavy capex across multiple new facilities (Supa, Sanand, stamping acquisition), funded via a mix of fresh equity (Rs.43.3cr preferential issue) and debt.
4. **MEDIUM — Non-automotive order book declined nominally** (Rs.250cr in the H2 FY25 call → Rs.230cr in the Q4 FY26 call) even as total order book grew ~65% (automotive-led); the "auto→65%, non-auto→35% within 2-3 years" mix target announced a year earlier looks behind pace (still ~80-85%/15-20% after year one).
5. **MEDIUM — Margin-expansion ambition quietly cut**, from a stated medium-term target of +4-5pp over 3-4 years (H2 FY25 call) to guidance of just ~1% for FY27 (Q4 FY26 call). Externally explained (tariffs, Iran/West Asia conflict, shipping costs) rather than deflected, which is a mitigating factor, but the scale of the cut is significant.
6. **LOW-MEDIUM — Rising customer concentration.** Tata AutoComp moved from 10th to 2nd largest customer within one year, increasing single-relationship and indirect Tesla-demand dependency; not discussed as a risk by either management or the questioning analyst.
7. **LOW — Vague answer on competitive differentiation** when asked directly (Vardhman Lunia, Q4 FY26 call) — a messaging weakness rather than a fundamental risk.
8. **LOW — Imprecise "surpassed guidance on both fronts" claim** in Q4 FY26 opening remarks; the specific margin metric originally guided (+2pp gross margin) does not cleanly reconcile against the reported EBITDA-margin outcome (19.5%) from the disclosed FY25 base figures (17.8% ex-other-income or ~19% incl. other income).
