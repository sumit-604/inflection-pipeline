AI-hardware supply-chain event or DSSL-specific?

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Claim | DSSL blames a ~290bp sequential margin decline in Q4 FY26 entirely on an unquantified AI-hardware/GPU-driven global supply-chain cost escalation, called a "temporary blip" |
| Verdict | **PARTIALLY VERIFIED** |
| Peer evidence | (1) TVS Electronics (Q4 FY26 call, 25-May-2026, A.K. Velu, CFO): explicit, unprompted acknowledgment that "the overall supply chain challenges which the industry is facing... there is an increase in memory prices," forcing higher inventory and causing customers to delay/postpone purchase decisions for one to two quarters; TVSE's FY26 revenue growth decelerated sharply from 17.6% (FY25) to 5.7% (FY26), partly attributed to this. (3) Aurionpro Solutions (Q4 FY26 call, 12-May-2026, Ashish Rai): the single most comparable peer (banking-tech + data-centre TIG business) missed its own FY26 growth/margin plan, but attributes the miss entirely to the Middle East war (deal-closure delays, project-execution slowdowns) and a hyperscaler data-centre project's revenue "slipp[ing] from Q4 into Q1... because of planning delays," explicitly stating "these are not necessarily low margin orders... we just got the timing wrong" — Aurionpro's FY26 EBITDA margin (20.02%) stayed within its guided 20-21% band, showing no AI-hardware-driven compression at all. |
| Peers silent | 3i Infotech: all four transcripts (Jul-2023 to May-2025) predate the claim window by 7-24 months; no evidence either way. |
| Net read | Two of three relevant peers (TVSE, ADSL) independently confirm that *some* real, dated hardware/component cost shock hit the sector in the same quarter — but neither frames it as "AI-hardware/GPU demand"; TVSE calls it a generic memory-price increase, ADSL calls it a war-driven equipment-cost spike. ## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

### 2A. Demand environment
Across all three currently-active, comparable peers (ADSL, Aurionpro, TVS Electronics),
demand commentary is broadly bullish and consistent with DSSL's framing: India digital
infrastructure, sovereign/AI data centres, cybersecurity, and BFSI digital transformation are
repeatedly cited as structural tailwinds (ADSL, Q3 FY26 call, citing the Union Budget's
data-centre/AI/data-security incentives; Aurionpro citing "hundreds of thousands of crore"
data-centre TAM). The complication DSSL's own concalls never surface: three of the four
active peers (ADSL, Aurionpro, and indirectly TVS Electronics via component pricing) report
a *geopolitical* shock — the Middle East/Iran-Israel war — as a material, named driver of Q4
FY26 revenue and cost disruption. DSSL's management frames its Q4 miss purely as an
"AI-hardware supply chain" event and never mentions the war or any comparable geopolitical
exposure, even though DSSL's own hardware-dependent business (servers, networking gear
for data centres and RBI infrastructure) plausibly carries similar sourcing exposure. This is a
disclosed-topic gap worth flagging for synthesis.

### 2B. Pricing and input costs
No single peer's framing matches DSSL's exactly. ADSL's persistent theme across all four
calls is *customer* pricing pressure (competitive bid discounting), not input-cost inflation.
TVS Electronics' Q4 FY26 call is the closest match to an input-cost story (memory-price
increases), but TVSE still expanded margin by walking away from unprofitable orders rather
than absorbing the cost, unlike DSSL. Aurionpro reports no material input-cost pressure at all
in FY26; its own margin stayed inside its guided band. The peer set therefore shows margin
resilience achieved through commercial discipline (TVSE, Aurionpro) rather than the passive
absorption DSSL describes, which is a meaningful point of contrast for synthesis.

### 2C. Capex cycle
This is an industry-wide capex/investment race, not a lone-expander situation. Aurionpro is
pouring "Rs150-200cr" into AI R&D and data-centre/transit capacity in FY27, deliberately
running negative near-term cash flow to fund it; ADSL is investing in Europe/US sales
expansion and AI tooling; TVS Electronics is scaling its EMS/SMT line capacity. DSSL's own
PPE build (Rs8cr to Rs68cr) and net-debt increase (Rs17cr to Rs68cr) funding its As-a-Service
pivot fits squarely inside this same sector-wide investment cycle rather than standing out as
an anomaly — this is a capacity race across the whole peer set, and DSSL is a small
participant in a pattern every peer in this set is also running.

### 2D. Competitive mentions
No peer transcript names Dynacons/DSSL or gives an unmistakable description of it. This
silence is expected and not itself informative: DSSL is a micro-cap relative to all four peers
and would not be expected to register on their competitive radar. No evidence either way on
DSSL's specific competitive standing from this channel.

### 2E. Risks peers raise that DSSL does not
- **Geopolitical/war exposure**: ADSL and Aurionpro both name the Middle East war as a
  material, quantified Q4 FY26 revenue/cost risk (ADSL, Q4 FY26 call; Aurionpro, Q4 FY26
  call). DSSL never raises this despite plausible comparable hardware-sourcing exposure.
- **Agentic-AI/AI-native disintermediation risk to the core services stack**: Aurionpro devotes
  extensive management time across all four calls to the risk that AI-native software
  and agentic execution could structurally disrupt the traditional enterprise-software/services
  model, requiring a "short-term spurt" of Rs150-200cr in defensive reinvestment. DSSL's
  concalls do not address whether its own SI/managed-services model carries comparable
  AI-native disintermediation exposure.
- **Audit-qualification/governance remediation cycles**: ADSL disclosed and spent two years
  resolving auditor qualifications on inventory verification, GST reconciliation, and ECL
  provisioning on receivables/unbilled revenue (Q3 FY26 and Q4 FY26 calls); 3i Infotech
  carried a multi-year forensic-audit legacy overhang. DSSL has no disclosed equivalent, but
  this flags a category of governance risk that a two-call-old management track record (per
  B05) has not yet been tested against.
- **Structural cash-conversion seasonality tied to project go-lives**: Aurionpro explicitly
  discloses negative H1 operating cash flow every year for the last several years, converting
  only an estimated 60-90% of EBITDA to cash by year-end, and frames this as a structural
  feature of project-based IT-infra revenue recognition (Q2 FY26 call, 04-Nov-2025). DSSL
  does not volunteer a comparable cash-conversion framework despite a similar project/O&M
  revenue-recognition model.

**The single most consequential contradiction/complication**: Aurionpro, the peer with the
closest comparable business mix (BFSI banking-tech plus data-centre-heavy TIG segment),
missed its own FY26 growth guidance in the identical Q4 FY26 quarter but attributed the miss
entirely to the Middle East war and a hyperscaler project-timing slip — and its EBITDA margin
stayed inside its guided 20-21% band, showing zero AI-hardware-cost-driven compression.
If the AI-hardware supply-chain shock DSSL describes were genuinely industry-wide and
material enough to cut ~290bp off DSSL's margin in one quarter, the best-matched peer would
be expected to show at least some comparable margin damage from the same cause. It does
not.

**The single strongest independent confirmation**: TVS Electronics' unprompted disclosure, in
its Q4 FY26 call, of "supply chain challenges which the industry is facing and there is an
increase in memory prices," causing customer order delays and working-capital deterioration
in the identical quarter DSSL cites — a genuine, dated, real cost-shock event confirmed by an
otherwise unrelated peer with no incentive to align its story with DSSL's.

**Overall**: the peer set complicates rather than cleanly supports or undercuts DSSL's
narrative. It confirms that a real, industry-wide hardware/component-cost and geopolitical
disruption occurred in the exact quarter DSSL cites — so DSSL is not fabricating an event out
of nothing. But it does not confirm DSSL's specific causal chain ("AI-factory/hyperscaler GPU
demand"), which no peer independently names; the two peers that do report a comparable
disruption attribute it to a generic memory-price cycle (TVS Electronics) and a war-driven
equipment-cost spike (ADSL), not to AI compute demand specifically. And it shows that at
least one well-matched peer navigated the same quarter with no margin damage at all,
attributing its own miss to entirely different, project-execution causes — which weakens the
"purely external, unavoidable, blip" framing DSSL's management gave to four separate
analysts in the same call. On working capital, peer gross-DSO levels (75-120 days) make
DSSL's underlying gross-day exposure look sector-typical rather than alarming, but no peer
discloses a "net working capital" framing to validate or invalidate DSSL's specific presentation
choice. On the sectoral growth narrative (India IT spend, bid-to-win ratios, RBI competitive
share), peers are simply silent on the specific figures DSSL cites — an evidentiary gap, not a
contradiction, but one synthesis should weight as a genuine data hole rather than corroborated
fact.

## PART 5: CROSS-PEER HYPOTHESIS

Across Aurionpro, ADSL, and TVS Electronics, FY26 — and Q4 FY26 specifically — shows a
recurring pattern: each company describes a quarter where **project execution and revenue
recognition lagged behind order-book/demand strength**, not because demand weakened, but
because deal sizes have scaled faster than the balance-sheet and working-capital processes
built to handle them. Aurionpro states this almost verbatim: "even a small leg up in
investments actually means hundreds of crores" now that the company is four times its size
of four years ago, and explicitly plans for elevated working-capital drag (~Rs200-250cr) in
FY27 as a consequence of taking on individually larger contracts. ADSL spent two consecutive
years remediating auditor-flagged inventory/receivable/ECL qualifications that trace to the
same underlying issue — control and reporting processes not scaling with deal size. TVS
Electronics' Q4 FY26 debt-to-equity jump (0.34x to 0.43x) was driven by a deliberate
inventory build to buffer against the same component-cost volatility that hit the whole peer
set that quarter.

**The testable hypothesis**: this is not a set of independent, company-specific execution
misses. It is a sector-wide "scaling pain" phenomenon in which India's mid-cap IT-infrastructure
and systems-integration peer group entered FY26 taking on individually much larger,
more capital-intensive contracts (hyperscaler data centres, metro-scale system integration,
national-bank-scale private-cloud/core-banking infrastructure) than their historical balance
sheets, working-capital discipline, and internal controls were built for — producing a
predictable pattern of quarter-to-quarter margin/cash volatility that is a function of *scale-up
speed*, not of demand quality, competitive position, or (in DSSL's specific case) a genuinely
unique AI-hardware shock. DSSL's own PPE/net-debt inflection in the same year (Rs8cr to
Rs68cr PPE; Rs17cr to Rs68cr net debt funding the As-a-Service pivot) is consistent with this
same pattern rather than a company-specific anomaly.

This is testable: if the hypothesis holds, DSSL's FY27 cash-conversion (operating cash
flow/EBITDA) and receivable/payable-day metrics should show the same "temporary strain
during scale-up, recovery as projects mature into O&M" trajectory that Aurionpro and ADSL
describe for their own businesses — rather than a persistent, structural deterioration. If DSSL's
working-capital and margin metrics fail to normalize over FY27 while true peers' do, that would
argue the AI-hardware framing was cover for a company-specific execution or disclosure
problem rather than a shared sector dynamic.
