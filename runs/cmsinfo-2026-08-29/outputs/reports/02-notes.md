## PASS 3: PATTERN PASS

Re-read across all notes looking for contradictions, mismatches, deliberate vagueness, restatements, subsequent events, and going-concern language. Not a "no material new findings" pass — several cross-note patterns emerge that Pass 1 and Pass 2 captured individually but did not connect end to end.

### Pattern 1 — The dominant pattern: clean headline cash conversion sits on top of deteriorating, under-reserved receivables in a contracting core segment

Four facts, each independently anchored in Pass 1/2, combine into one coherent and material story:

1. Cash Management services revenue **fell** -4.5% SA / -6.6% CON (Note 19, p.91/147 SA, p.124/147 CON), the core legacy line (ATM cash replenishment, retail cash management, cash-in-transit).
2. Cash Management segment result **fell** 25.0% (₹4,013.79m → ₹3,012.14m, Note 38, CON p.133/147), a segment carrying ~66% of Group segment result.
3. The 1-2 year overdue receivables bucket **grew** 8.5x CON (₹177.94m → ₹1,516.32m) and 16.2x SA (₹92.14m → ₹1,490.59m) (Note 12, p.131/147 CON, p.99/147 SA), while "not due" receivables fell 29-33%.
4. The SA loss allowance, at the entity where the deterioration is concentrated, **fell** 14.1% (₹533.94m → ₹458.80m) — a net ₹75.14m release, not an addition (Note 12/37, SA p.99/147) — in the same year the ageing profile worsened 16.2x.

Read together, this is not four separate watch items; it is one story. The segment generating most of Group profit is shrinking and its receivables are aging out uncollected, yet the entity closest to that deterioration released provisions rather than building them. The Group-level LBF-1 cash-conversion thesis (CFO ₹3,895.94m vs PAT ₹3,033.92m, CON) is arithmetically correct but is not evidence of earnings quality on its own: strong CFO this year is achieved despite receivables quality, not because of it, and the provisioning trend runs the wrong way to be reassuring. This is the single highest-priority reconciliation item for Halt 1.

### Pattern 2 — A presentation change sits directly inside the contradiction, and cannot be fully separated from it

In FY25, part of what would have been loss-allowance was reclassified into a separate "ATM cash shortage and claims" provision (₹577.49m reclassified, Note 12 footnote / Note 17, CON p.131-132/147; SA loss-allowance rollforward, p.99/147). This mechanically lowers the loss-allowance base against which FY26's movement is measured. The 14.1% SA release (Pattern 1, item 4) is therefore not cleanly comparable to a pre-reclassification loss-allowance base — some of the apparent "improvement" in coverage optics is presentational, not credit-risk driven. This does not resolve Pattern 1; it removes one avenue (a genuine, explained credit-risk improvement) as the explanation, leaving under-reserving as the more likely reading pending management clarification.

### Pattern 3 — Deliberately thin disclosure on a material forward capital obligation

The audited capital-commitments note (Note 31(b)) shows **₹0** at FY26 close vs ₹93.63m FY25 (SA p.96/147, CON p.128/147). At the same balance date, the Note 10 capital-advances footnote confirms a signed agreement to acquire FSS's ATM Managed Services assets for ₹1,150m, with ₹600m already paid on 30-Mar-2026 and ₹550m outstanding, payable on completion (SA p.89/147, CON p.121/147). A reader of the commitments note alone would see no forward obligation; the ₹550m only surfaces in a different note entirely. Whether this is a technical compliance choice (the "agreement to acquire assets" not meeting the note's definition of a "contract on capital account") or a disclosure gap, the effect is the same: the note that investors check for forward capex/M&A obligations under-states them. This is the clearest example in the AR of one note being materially less informative than a related note on the same transaction.

### Pattern 4 — Multiple favourable, non-repeating items land in the same weak-earnings year

Three distinct items, none individually flagged as earnings management, land together and all point the same direction (cushioning a real PAT decline): the ATM useful-life extension (+₹47.65m to profit, Note 4, effective mid-year), the ESOP compensation-cost collapse (-87.3% CON, ₹307.45m → ₹38.95m, driven by the CEO's option exercise, Note 30/38-39), and the inventory write-back vs prior-year write-down swing (Note 11, SA, small in absolute terms). Individually each is explained and disclosed. Together, in a year PAT still fell 18.6% despite all three tailwinds, they indicate the underlying operating deterioration in Cash Management (Pattern 1) is understated by the reported PAT figure, not overstated.

### Pattern 5 — Capital returned upward while cash and receivable quality moved the other way

Dividend paid rose 39.2% (₹1,063.16m → ₹1,479.58m) against an 18.6% PAT decline (Note 46, Statement of Changes in Equity), cash and cash equivalents nearly halved (SA -58.3%, CON -51.4%, Note 13/Cash Flow), and a ₹1,679.30m buyback at ₹340/share was approved the same day results were signed (14-May-2026, now materially underwater at CMP ~₹243 per company memory). None of these are notes-level red flags in isolation (each is disclosed, and the dividend increase is explained as a timing artefact of a Special Interim Dividend). Set against Pattern 1 (deteriorating core segment, under-reserved receivables) and Pattern 3 (an undisclosed forward M&A obligation), the aggregate picture is a company returning cash to shareholders and funding two acquisitions (Securens + FSS) in the same year its core segment's cash-generating receivables quality worsened — a capital-allocation sequencing question, not an accounting-fraud signal.

### Pattern 6 — Growth is arriving via acquisition onto a base with internal stress signals

Securens (acquired 16-Sep-2025) carries its own CARO clause (xvii) cash-loss qualification and was loss-making in its stub period (Note 45, SA p.102-103/147; CARO Annexure A, CON p.107/147). Two further wholly-owned subsidiaries — Quality Logistics Services Pvt Ltd (negative net worth, ₹-52.09m) and CMS Securitas Limited (net loss ₹-6.31m) — surface only in the Schedule III consolidated breakup (Note 40, CON p.134/147), not in the main subsidiary/investment note. Group-level "growth" (Managed Services +32.5%/+39.1%) is arriving substantially via acquisition (Securens, and the pending FSS deal) layered onto an organic base that includes both the Pattern 1 core-segment contraction and standing subsidiary-level stress. No single item here is individually alarming; the pattern across four entities in one Schedule III table is a coherent signal that organic Cash Management strength cannot be assumed to extend across the whole Group.

### Restatements / reclassifications found
- ATM cash shortage and claims provision separated from loss-allowance treatment: ₹577.49m reclassified in FY25 comparatives (Note 12 footnote / Note 17, CON p.131-132/147; SA p.99/147). Affects YoY comparability of loss-allowance and receivables-ageing ratios (Pattern 2).
- Authorised share capital reclassified: 173,000,000 → 188,000,000 equity shares (+₹150.00m), exactly offsetting cancellation of 1,500,000 OCCRPS (₹150.00m) that existed at FY25 close (Note 14, SA p.90/147). Mechanical, not an earnings item.

### Going concern language
NONE. Explicitly searched; no substantive hits beyond the standard audit-opinion assessment paragraphs (Note 52, SA p.104/147; CON p.136/147: "There are no significant subsequent events that would require adjustments or disclosures... as on the balance sheet date").

### Events after balance sheet date not treated as audited subsequent events in the notes
The notes' own Note 52 subsequent-events disclosure is boilerplate ("no significant subsequent events"), signed 14-May-2026. Two materially relevant post-period-end items sit outside that note, disclosed instead in the Board's Report / MD&A: the ₹1,679.30m buyback (approved same day as signing) and, per company memory, its underwater execution against CMP ~₹243 subsequent to year end. This is a scope boundary of the audited notes, not a misstatement, but means the "clean" subsequent-events note should not be read as covering the buyback's economics.

### E. NOTES-BASED RED FLAGS

- Provisioning: SA loss allowance fell 14.1% while the 1-2yr overdue bucket it should track grew 16.2x — the clearest under-reserving signal in the notes (Note 12/37, SA p.99/147).
- Receivables quality: 1-2yr overdue bucket up 8.5x CON / 16.2x SA; net receivables grew 9.5% CON against 2.6% revenue growth (Note 12, p.131/147 CON).
- Core-segment concealment at the headline: Cash Management revenue and profit both fell materially while Group headline figures showed growth, entirely from Note 19/38 disaggregation not visible in the P&L face or MD&A summary alone.
- Recurring cash embezzlement, third consecutive year, ₹125.35m FY26, with an audit trail limitation landing the same year (Board's Report p.34/147; Auditor's Report SA p.72-73/147).
- Distressed-receivable workout: a customer's overdue receivable converted into a 3-year secured term loan, ₹580.12m (Note 7/48).
- Disclosure-completeness gap: ₹550m FSS forward obligation absent from the capital-commitments note (Note 10/31(b)).
- Comparability break: loss-allowance/provision reclassification (₹577.49m, FY25) makes YoY provisioning ratios not strictly like-for-like (Note 12 footnote/Note 17).

No evidence of revenue-recognition manipulation, related-party extraction, or off-book liabilities was found; the red flags above are concentrated in receivables provisioning, core-segment disclosure, and operational controls, not in headline revenue or profit recognition mechanics.

### F. ONE-LINE NOTES VERDICT

The notes reveal concerning accounting practices concentrated in receivables provisioning and core-segment disclosure. Key concern: the SA loss allowance fell 14.1% while the 1-2yr overdue receivables bucket it should track grew 16.2x, in the same year the core Cash Management segment's revenue fell 4.5-6.6% and its profit fell 25%. Key strength: routine disclosures (related parties, borrowings, contingent liabilities, deferred tax, CSR, goodwill) are clean, well anchored, and show no promoter-extraction or off-book signal. Overall accounting quality: 4/10.
