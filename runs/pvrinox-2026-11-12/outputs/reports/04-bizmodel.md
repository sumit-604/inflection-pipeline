## SECTION 1: THE BUSINESS MODEL IN PLAIN ENGLISH

### 1A One line
PVR INOX rents mall space, fits it with cinema screens, and sells tickets, popcorn and ad slots to people who come for a film that someone else made; it keeps about 55 of every 100 ticket rupees after paying the film's owner (AR PDF p.89, p.96).

(55 = 100 less film-hire cost of 45.3% of box office revenue, FY26 standalone; derived.)

### 1E The chai-stall-uncle version
Think of a chai stall inside a busy railway station. The station owner charges rent. The stall owner sells tea at the same price whether the day is busy or dull. The rent stays the same. On a busy day the stall earns a lot. On a dull day it loses money. PVR INOX runs about 1,800 such stalls, called screens. Its rent is the biggest bill and it does not move with crowds. The film is the tea leaf, and the film owner takes about 45 paise of each ticket rupee. The snacks are the biscuits, and they carry the best profit. The new plan is to let others pay for the stall and keep a fee, which costs less cash but earns less per stall.

### 2D Industry lifecycle
Indian multiplexing is in late growth: screen count grew 9,527 (2019) to 10,003 (2025), about 0.8% a year (derived), while multiplex screens rose 3,400 to 4,800 and single screens fell 6,600 to 5,200 (AR PDF p.83). Growth is substitution of single screens, not a net screen boom. PVR INOX is the lead operator in a mature, content-cyclical format whose growth lever has moved from owning screens to operating them for others.
