## BUSINESS UNDERSTANDING NARRATIVE

Ramkrishna Forgings heats steel bars until they glow, presses them into shaped parts, and machines most of those parts to finished size. Auto forgings make up about 74% of standalone FY26 revenue: front axle beams, steering knuckles, shafts and gears for trucks, buses and tractors, with 47% sold in India and 27% exported. These are safety parts made to the customer's own drawing, so a truck maker cannot change supplier without qualifying the part again. Railway bogie frames (7.5%), mining and farm equipment parts (5.9%) and other industrial parts (8.0%) make up most of the rest, and new casting and aluminium forging lines started between January and March 2026. Tata Motors alone bought 33.4% of consolidated revenue in the nine months to December 2025, and two customers each take more than 10% of sales. Abroad the buyers are US Tier 1 suppliers and European truck makers, and North America took 58% of FY26 exports and 71% of exports in the September 2026 quarter. Customers stay for more than ten years, but they buy against delivery schedules, they can split volume between suppliers, and management declines to give the customer or product split on its calls. Demand today follows truck builds, so the signals to watch are Tata Motors CV volumes and orders, India CV production and wholesale (CV production rose 13% in FY26), and North America Class 8 orders and build, which Bharat Forge and M M Forgings also describe as recovering. US tariffs on steel and aluminium auto parts cut FY26 exports by about 20%, so that signal sits beside the truck numbers. Forward growth rests first on filling the capacity added in FY26, which ran at 72% total utilisation in the September 2026 quarter. Two more drivers sit behind it: a European recovery that EU truck and van registrations will show, and Indian Railways forged wheel procurement and RDSO approval for the joint venture wheel plant, in a country that imports 60 to 70% of about 2,00,000 wheels a year. ACMA auto component industry turnover is the sector cross check on both domestic and export demand. Domestic and export auto forgings hold a moderate moat from part qualification, ten year customer retention and large press lines, but the emerging moat scan scored that qualification lock-in as weak and found no new advantage forming in these lines. Railway bogie frames show no moat, and their share of sales fell from 7.5% in FY26 to 5.5% in the first half of FY27. Forged rail wheels are the only line the scan scored as a moderate emerging advantage, a policy tailwind from import substitution, and it is unproven: the plant has not started commercial production, and the annual report and the October 2026 call disagree on whether RDSO approval is needed. Castings and aluminium forgings carry no moat and earn below the group margin, at 15 to 16% and 14 to 15% on management's own figures. The scan found no talent asymmetry and no cannibalisation barrier, and its total of 9.0 classifies as NONE.

# Forging capacity built, cash and controls lag

Ramkrishna Forgings runs 20 plants: 16 in Jamshedpur, one each in Howrah, Manesar and Pune, and one in Monterrey, Mexico. FY26 consolidated revenue was Rs 4,238 Cr, up 5%. Consolidated profit fell to Rs 72 Cr from Rs 415 Cr, and the FY25 figure carried a Rs 184 Cr tax credit and a discontinued operations gain. Installed capacity is 3,95,800 tonnes. Steel passes through to customers with a lag of up to three months on a large part of volume, so the company earns on the forging and machining work, not on the steel.

The transition is a capacity build paid for with debt. Capex from FY24 to FY26 was Rs 2,491 Cr, and FY26 alone added 89,800 tonnes, 29% more capacity, most of it commissioned between January and March 2026. The September 2026 quarter shows the first return: revenue of Rs 1,405 Cr, up 55% on the year, and an EBITDA margin of 18.2% against 13.5% a year earlier. Against that, two year growth is 33%, in line with peers at 32 to 39%, so most of the jump is a weak base. The diversification story has not shown up yet. Sales outside auto fell from 26% of revenue in FY26 to 22% in the first half of FY27, the rail wheel joint venture slipped on three successive calls, aerospace has zero orders, and Mexico started about two quarters late. Casting did deliver, at 88% utilisation against a promise of 85 to 90%.

Gate 0, the backward screen, scores 47 of 160: core 40 of 100, moat 7 of 60, and 2 of 12 moats confirmed. It lands at AVOID on one deal breaker: FY26 net debt of 3.65 times EBITDA with interest cover of 1.4 times. Three softer caps also fire: a weak Block A, a median ROCE of 9.5%, and 16.2% of promoter shares pledged. Verifier C found that Gate 0 stripped other income out of EBIT; on the formula as written, median ROCE is at least 10.2% and the ROCE cap does not fire, but AVOID stands on leverage. The leverage reading comes from a trough year. Quarterly operating profit has risen from Rs 118 Cr to Rs 256 Cr, and interest cover over the twelve months to September 2026 is 2.0 times. If "latest" in the Gate 0 rule means the latest reported period, and September 2026 net debt sits at or below about Rs 2,516 Cr, the label would be AVERAGE instead. That needs an operator ruling and a number the run does not hold.

Cash is where the bull and bear readings split. FY26 operating cash flow was Rs 840 Cr against Rs 33 Cr in FY25, and the rating agency expects its adjusted leverage to fall from 4.6 times to 3.5 to 4 times in FY27. But about Rs 154 Cr of the standalone figure came from more supplier acceptances and more receivables sold to banks, and cash tax paid fell to Rs 6 Cr from Rs 124 Cr. Receivables older than six months rose to 21.7% of gross from 8.0%, the expected credit loss provision rose to 5.5% from 0.6%, and foreign currency receivables doubled to Rs 556 Cr while export revenue fell 20%. Acceptances of Rs 650 Cr and receivables sold of Rs 400 Cr add up to Rs 1,050 Cr, 59% of standalone borrowings, outside the borrowings line. India Ratings puts the working capital cycle at 193 days and expects 180 to 200 days, which it calls in line with history and with the industry. Free cash flow was negative in FY25 (Rs 944 Cr) and FY26 (Rs 85 Cr). In the first half of FY27, receivables rose from Rs 795 Cr to Rs 1,025 Cr and inventory from Rs 1,246 Cr to Rs 1,469 Cr, both faster than revenue. Contingent liabilities reached 37% of equity, 2.3 times the prior year, most of it guarantees for subsidiaries and the wheel joint venture.

The accounts carry a recent scar. The FY25 stock count found inventory overstated by Rs 220.5 Cr at March 2025 and Rs 50.2 Cr at March 2024, FY24 profit was restated down 15.4%, and the FY25 opinion was qualified. The FY26 opinion is unmodified on both sets of accounts, the fact finding closed with no further adjustment, and the company reports no fraud up to the interim report date. The control signals are still live, though. Stock statements given to banks differed from the books in all four quarters, by up to Rs 206 Cr in December 2025, and the stated cause (one unit left out) cannot explain the quarters where the bank figure ran higher. One loan covenant was breached and waived, the SAP audit trail had a gap, and the Directors' Report says the auditor made no qualification while the auditor reports a modification and adverse CARO items. The notes score 5 of 10 for accounting quality.

Management delivers on the profit line and slips on capital and projects. Across 17 promises on the last four calls, 6 were delivered, 4 partly and 7 missed, a C grade that Verifier B confirms. The margin rose every quarter, from 14.9% to 18.2%, and North America recovered as promised. FY26 growth came in at 5% against a double digit promise, FY27 capex moved from Rs 300 to 400 Cr to about Rs 550 Cr inside one quarter, and the first half cut only Rs 126 Cr of a Rs 500 Cr debt reduction plan. After missing a gross debt target of below Rs 2,000 Cr, the call moved to a net measure. On the October call the MD stepped back from the Rs 8,000 Cr FY29 revenue target and deferred all multiyear guidance until after FY27 results. No call discussed the inventory restatement, the warrants or the promoter holding. The promoters have put Rs 405 Cr into warrants and sold nothing, and the Rs 202 Cr received in FY26 went to repay debt. The fall in their holding to 40.9% is the reclassification of one group entity to public. Against that, 1.23 Cr promoter shares, 16.2% of their holding, have been pledged since August 2025, and the promoter verdict is CAUTION.

The 12 peer transcripts confirm the cycle and question the efficiency claims. Bharat Forge and M M Forgings confirm the North America Class 8 recovery, and all three peers bore the same energy and freight shock. No company claim is contradicted, 2 of 11 are verified, 8 are partly verified and 1 cannot be checked. The difference is in recovery: peers got lagged or partial compensation from customers, while Ramkrishna's customers refused and the company absorbed Rs 8 to 9 Cr of shipping cost in the September quarter. Inventory days of 108 are the longest of the four companies. Management's 2.4 to 2.5 times asset turn at full capacity sits above every peer's own stated ceiling, and M M Forgings puts its own best case at 1.2 times gross block. The capacity race is industry wide, so the new tonnes face peers that also build ahead of orders. M M Forgings names Ramkrishna as a direct competitor in axle, beam, crankshaft and connecting rod parts.

The market is large enough, and capacity and execution set the limit. The Indian forging market measures Rs 53,000 to 63,000 Cr, growing about 9% a year in dollar terms, and the named peer floor for the reachable market is Rs 19,700 Cr, of which Ramkrishna holds an indicative 21.5%. Revenue headroom is 4.65 times and the runway is GOOD. The market sizing stage gives a three year revenue path to Rs 6,220 Cr, a 13.6% annual rate, below management's 23.6% and below the first half annualised pace. The market figure management cites is 2.03 times the conservative estimate. The Rs 8,000 Cr FY29 target would need 92.7% of installed capacity at today's price per tonne, and management has now stepped back from it.
