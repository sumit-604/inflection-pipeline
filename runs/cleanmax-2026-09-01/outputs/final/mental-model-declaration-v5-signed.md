## PART 0 — THE FOUR QUESTIONS
### 0.1 What is the competitive advantage?
Three things, ranked by how well they survive scrutiny.
**Secured evacuation on the state grid. Real, and it is the whole moat.** 2,549 MW operational, 99.35% uptime, receivable days 25. Grid connection is the scarce, regulator-gated resource in this business, and CleanMax has secured it years ahead of demand across seven states. This is 87% of run-rate EBITDA and it works exactly as described.
**Customer lock-in through the group-captive structure. Real, but narrower than claimed.** Indian law requires the customer to hold at least 26% equity in each project company. Once inside, a customer cannot leave without unwinding an equity position. Weighted PPA tenor is 23.17 years and 74% of new volume comes from repeat buyers. But this is a **switching cost, not a barrier to entry.** The rule binds Amplus, Fourth Partner, O2, Serentica and every new entrant identically. It keeps existing customers; it does not keep competitors out.
**Counterparty quality and execution record. Real, and undervalued in the analysis so far.** Named hyperscalers, Apple, Toyota Tsusho. Over 80% of contracted volume is with AA, AAA or multinational counterparties. Projects delivered at 97.20% of budget. Credit rating A+ to AA in about a year, cost of debt 8.7% to 8.4%.
**What is NOT an advantage:**
- Pricing power. New-PPA tariff fell Rs 4.12 -> Rs 3.76 -> Rs 3.57 (FY24–FY26). Scored weak in the corpus.
- Scale. Serentica commissioned 3 GW by Jul-2026 against CleanMax's 3.49 GW and targets 5 GW by FY27 with ~$5bn committed. CleanMax's "largest C&I provider" claim has a shelf life measured in quarters.
- Technology. Nothing proprietary. Solar panels and turbines bought on the open market.
- The claimed 12%->14% market share. Defensible on flow (about 1.4 GW of roughly 8 GW annual national additions), not on stock (about 7-8% of the 32.9 GW cumulative open-access base plus wind). The company does not say which.
### 0.2 What is the transition?
**FROM: R1 commodity price-taker.** Median ROCE 5.83%, FY26 ROCE 4.2–5.1%. Free cash flow minus Rs 4,023 Cr and widening. Operating profit before other income negative for three straight years. Falling tariffs, no pricing power.
**TO: R1+.** On management's own guided FY28 state — EBITDA Rs 3,000 Cr, net debt Rs 16,000 Cr — capital employed lands near Rs 26,000 Cr and EBIT near Rs 1,925 Cr. That is **ROCE of roughly 7 to 9%.**
R2 requires durable mid-teens ROCE. R3 requires 20 to 25%. Neither is reachable.
**So the transition is from a 5% return builder to an 8% return builder with twice the fleet.** Absolute earnings multiply. Return on capital barely moves. This is scale, not a climb up the quality ladder, and the original Claude Code declaration of R1->R3 overstated it by two rungs.
**What actually changes, mechanically:** young assets season. In FY26, 38% of net debt sat against construction in progress producing nothing while the interest ran. As that Rs 5,339 Cr of construction converts to operating plant, the same interest bill starts being matched by revenue. That conversion is the transition.
### 0.3 Why does it become an investment thesis?
Not on the quality climb. On four other things.
**One. The bottom line multiplies far faster than the top line.** Revenue roughly doubles FY26 to FY28. Profit multiplies five to six times, because profit is a thin residual sitting under a large and partly non-earning cost base. See 0.4.
**Two. The crossover is real and dated.** Operating profit before other income: minus Rs 62.4 Cr (FY25), minus Rs 33.6 Cr (FY26), **plus Rs 51.6 Cr (Q1 FY27)**. The first genuine positive, needing no help from other income. Two identified tailwinds carry into FY27: Rs 599 Cr of 11.5% debentures repaid saving Rs 68.89 Cr a year, and cost of debt falling.
**Three. The demand is contracted, not narrated.** Data and AI is 42% of contracted capacity. Indian data centre capacity is around 1.8-1.9 GW, growing about 30% a year, with over $126bn of cumulative sector commitments. Open-access solar additions grew 170% year on year in Q1 2026. The runway is 13.4x revenue headroom on an independently derived market size.
**Four, and this is the actual entry mechanism. Forced supply.** 33.93 million shares, about 29% of capital, came free on 26 August 2026. Brookfield's 21.31% follows around February 2027. The stock is already 53% off its high and the promoter's pledge cover sits near its trigger. A price driven to Rs 700 by supply rather than by deteriorating fundamentals is the setup this framework exists to catch.
**The thesis in one line:** buy an infrastructure compounder with contracted 23-year cash flows and genuine demand, at a price created by a supply event rather than a business event, and accept 8% ROCE as the ceiling.
**What makes it not a thesis today:** the price. At Rs 1,247 the market pays roughly 27-37x guided FY28 earnings. The FY28 state is already in the price.
### 0.4 How do the top line and bottom line grow?
**Top line: megawatts x generation x tariff.**
| Rs Cr | FY26 actual | FY28 implied |
|---|---|---|
| RE Power Sales revenue | 1,399 | ~3,000 |
| RE Services revenue | 497 | ~700 |
| **Total revenue** | **1,913** | **~3,700–4,000** |
Driven almost entirely by average operational capacity rising from about 2,400 MW to about 5,000 MW. Revenue per MW is roughly Rs 58 lakh and the guidance assumes it holds. Tariffs on the book still to be built are Rs 4.00/kWh against Rs 3.57 commissioned in FY26, so pricing is a mild tailwind, not a drag.
**Bottom line: the residual widens because the cost base is partly non-earning today.**
| Rs Cr | FY26 actual | FY28 implied | Multiple |
|---|---|---|---|
| Operating EBITDA | 1,132 | ~2,750 | 2.4x |
| Less finance cost (P&L) | (786) | ~(1,360) | 1.7x |
| Less depreciation | (380) | ~(800) | 2.1x |
| Operating PBT before other income | (34) | ~+590 | — |
| Add other income | 162 | ~150 | 0.9x |
| PBT | 135 | ~740 | 5.5x |
| **PAT attributable** | **94** | **~400–550** | **4–6x** |
| **EPS (Rs)** | **~8** | **~34–47** | — |
**Why profit multiplies five times while revenue doubles.** EBITDA grows 2.4x while interest grows only 1.7x. Three reasons. New capacity is funded partly with equity — the mandatory 26% customer stake plus IPO proceeds — so debt does not grow in step with the fleet. Cost of debt is falling. And most importantly, the Rs 5,339 Cr of construction in progress that carried interest and produced nothing in FY26 becomes revenue-producing plant.
**Other income does not grow, and that matters.** FY26's Rs 162 Cr included Rs 49 Cr of bank interest on Rs 626 Cr of unspent IPO proceeds. That money is being deployed. The base wastes.
**What breaks the arithmetic:** per-MW EBITDA falling below Rs 50 lakh; curtailment spreading beyond Bikaner; capitalisation reverting sharply and pushing more interest into the P&L at once; or the deferred tax asset of Rs 1,215.77 Cr being written down if FY28 disappoints.

## PART A — THE FROM STATE
### A1. Archetype
**RE Power Sales (73.2% of revenue, ~87% of run-rate EBITDA): licence/scarcity business holding a partial licence, with a product-shape gap.**
**The licence works on the state grid.** 2,549 MW operational, 99.35% uptime, 25 receivable days, 23.17-year weighted PPA tenor.
**It does not work in the Rajasthan central-grid corridor.** Bikaner, 525 MW, the entire operational central-grid book, sits on temporary connectivity, is curtailed 70%, and corporate customers are not liable for undelivered power. About Rs 170 Cr a year. Sector data is worse than management's figure: curtailment on temporary connectivity runs 50-60% in solar hours nationally and close to 100% between 11am and 2pm in Rajasthan. The corridor upgrade underdelivered, the next scheme is deadlocked, and Rajasthan still took 39% of Q1 2026 national additions.
**And the licence does not cover the shape of the power.** This is new in v5. Data centres run 24 hours. Solar delivers 17-25% utilisation, wind 35%, hybrid 47%. The market has moved to storage-linked and hybrid PPAs as the standard structure for 24-hour buyers, with data centres named first. Firm round-the-clock power now clears at about Rs 5.25/kWh, below every coal contract signed in 2025. CleanMax sells intermittent power at Rs 3.93-4.00 and greenlit its **first** battery project in the June 2026 quarter. Serentica advertises 55-85% round-the-clock backed by 1,500 MWh of contracted pumped hydro and 2,000 MWh of battery under construction.
Grid access and long contracts remain necessary. For 42% of the contracted book they are no longer sufficient.
**RE Services (26.0%): order-book business liquidating backlog.** Contracted-yet-to-execute fell 215 MW -> 147 MW while Q1 FY27 revenue rose 7.3x. Book-to-bill well below 1.
### A2. The analogy
CleanMax builds solar and wind farms and rents the electricity to large companies on twenty-five year leases. Indian law makes each tenant own at least a quarter of the building. CleanMax sells further slices to partners to fund the next farm, so it never fully owns what it consolidates. It spent FY26 building faster than the rent arrived: Rs 12,411 Cr of debt, Rs 4,023 Cr of negative free cash flow, Rs 135 Cr of profit that was mostly bank interest on IPO money. The state-grid farms work. One farm in Rajasthan is throttled most of the day because the wires out of the state are full, and nobody pays for power that never leaves. Half of what is being built next connects to that same national network. And the biggest tenants increasingly want power at night as well as at noon, which is a different product from the one CleanMax sells.

## PART B — THE TRANSITION
### B1. FROM -> TO
Covered in 0.2. R1 -> R1+. Guided FY28 ROCE 7-9%. Scale, not a quality tier.
### B2. The engine
**Primary: MW commissioned x EBITDA per MW.** Run-rate Rs 1,870 Cr on 3,088 MW is Rs 60.6 lakh/MW; 4.6 GW at that rate plus services is approximately the Rs 3,000 Cr guided. Realised has run below: Rs 62.6 lakh (FY25), Rs 51.3 lakh (FY26), Rs 56.0 lakh (Q1 FY27 annualised).
**New in v5: EBITDA per MW now depends on product shape, not just utilisation.** Firmed power earns a higher tariff and costs more to build. A competitor selling 55-85% round-the-clock at Rs 5.25 may earn more per MW than CleanMax at Rs 4.00, not less. Storage is now a driver of the engine, not an option.
**Tailwinds:** NCD repayment saving Rs 68.89 Cr a year from FY27; cost of debt 8.7% -> 8.4%; under-execution tariff Rs 4.00 vs Rs 3.57 commissioned.
**Two clocks:** revenue stabilisation 3-6 months post-COD; financial stabilisation 9-12 months. H2 FY27 commissioning will not be seasoned through FY28.
### B3. The proof gate
**Metric:** quarterly operating profit before other income, as reported.
**Threshold:** >= Rs 0, sustained two consecutive quarters.
**Status:** FY25 (62.4) -> FY26 (33.6) -> **Q1 FY27 +51.6**.
**Next print:** Q2 FY27, late October 2026.
The FY26 capitalisation flag was tested and cleared in v3: Rs 185.51 Cr capitalised on at least Rs 2,670 Cr average CWIP is 6.9% or less against an 8.4% cost of debt. Conservative, not aggressive. Quarterly capitalisation is NOT DISCLOSED, so the print cannot be independently rebuilt.
> **§B3 CAPITALISATION RECONCILIATION — operator restatement, 2026-09-02 (supersedes the earlier clearance).**
> The two ratios answer different questions and both are correct. 28.43% = capitalised borrowing cost / total
> borrowing cost incurred, a COMPOSITION measure that rises mechanically as construction grows relative to
> operating assets (exactly what a doubling fleet produces); it is not an aggressiveness test. 6.9% = capitalised
> interest / average CWIP, the Ind AS 23 COMPLIANCE test (rate-on-CWIP against cost of debt).
> The earlier clearance was too clean: it used Rs 185.51 Cr interest-only as the numerator and excluded
> Rs 126.76 Cr of letter-of-credit and bank-guarantee charges, which are arguably capitalisable borrowing costs.
> Put them back and the numerator is Rs 312.27 Cr:
>
> | Average CWIP assumption | Interest only | Interest + fees |
> | --- | --- | --- |
> | Rs 2,670 Cr (zero opening, floor case) | 6.9% | 11.7% |
> | Rs 3,670 Cr (opening ~Rs 2,000 Cr) | 5.1% | 8.5% |
>
> Cost of debt 8.4%. On interest alone it is conservative on any assumption. On interest + fees it is at the cap
> or above, depending entirely on the opening balance. So the aggressive-accounting reading is NOT supported on
> interest alone; once fees are included it becomes MARGINAL and cannot be settled without one number:
> consolidated construction in progress, opening AND closing, FY25 and FY26 (flagged since v3, still unresolved).
> **BLOCKING: Stage 11 must not set a destination multiple without that CWIP number.**
> Ledger: (i) LC/BG charges grew 6.2x while capex doubled, disproportionate and unexplained in all three calls;
> (ii) if fully expensed rather than capitalised, FY26 operating PBT before other income moves from -Rs 34 Cr to
> roughly -Rs 160 Cr, not the -Rs 263 Cr full stress test. None of this changes Q2 FY27 as the live test.
### B4. The recognition gap
**Resolved against, provisionally.** ~27-37x guided FY28 attributable earnings at Rs 1,247. The market prices the FY28 state. PRICED NARRATIVE is the live branch. Stage 11 confirms.
### B5. The ugliness test
**ARTIFACT-OF-CLIMB.** Revenue never declined, 25.2% five-year CAGR, operating margin widened every year since FY23, capex/depreciation 15x, CFO/EBITDA 1.35x, accounting conservative.
Structural concerns remain: other income 58.8% one-off or non-cash; current tax 169% of book PBT; 93 of 103 CARO entities in two-year cash losses; NCI in loss three years; CARE opening net debt/EBITDA 5.7x against its own 5.5x downgrade trigger.
### B6. The transition falsifier
Any one of:
(a) Q2 FY27 operating PBT before other income prints below zero.
(b) Koppal commissions on temporary connectivity, or its substation slips past December 2026.
(c) The next unnamed central-grid site is located in the Rajasthan corridor.
(d) **New in v5:** FY27 Data and AI contracting falls materially short of guidance while Serentica, NTPC or Adani announce firmed data-centre PPAs. This is the product-gap test.
(e) FY27 NCI share of profit remains negative.
(f) FY27 EBITDA per MW falls below Rs 50 lakh (nameplate) while the fleet grows.

## PART D — DISCLOSURE CONFLICTS ON RECORD
1. **Pledge.** AR Note 19(g) shows 11,597,866 shares pledged to 360 One as at 31-Mar-2026. A separate promoter-group declaration states no encumbrance was created on promoter shares from the 2-Mar-2026 listing date to 31-Mar-2026. Both can be technically true if the re-pledge predates listing, but the prospectus said the shares were released as at its date and would be re-pledged after lock-in creation. **Practical consequence: anyone monitoring the encumbrance declaration alone would read "no encumbrance" while a fifth of promoter holding is pledged.** Needs the dated re-pledge filing.
2. **Net debt.** AR Note 37.1 gives Rs 11,208.80 Cr at 31-Mar-2026; management states Rs 9,684 Cr. Gap Rs 1,524.80 Cr, no corpus bridge, about Rs 130/share of enterprise value.
3. **FY28 guidance basis.** Labelled both "reported EBITDA" and "run-rate EBITDA" on the same slide; other-income treatment NOT DISCLOSED. About Rs 150/share on the entry price.
4. **Koppal capacity.** 529 MW (AR), 543 MW (Aug call), ~530-560 MW (Mar/May calls).
5. **Operational capacity.** 3,088 MW nameplate vs 3,643.94 MW/MWp on the C&I basis at the same date.
6. **CARE PAT.** The Oct-2025 and May-2026 releases disagree on audited FY24 and FY25 profit. May matches the restated accounts.
7. **Compliance record.** Unable to comply with certain BSE rules for listed NCDs; contravened Companies Act provisions on director appointment terms; 15 delayed charge filings with the RoC.
