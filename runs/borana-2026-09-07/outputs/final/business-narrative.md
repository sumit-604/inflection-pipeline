# Borana Weaves Ltd (BORANA) — Business Narrative
Run 2026-09-07. Phase 1 (evidence). Stages 10 and 11 have not run, so this file carries no valuation, no entry zone and no decision.

## BUSINESS UNDERSTANDING NARRATIVE

Borana Weaves buys polyester filament yarn, texturises part of it in house, and weaves it on water jet looms into unbleached grey fabric. Grey fabric is 91 percent of revenue. It is a raw material and not a finished good: the buyer dyes, prints and finishes it before it becomes dress material or home furnishing cloth. The customer cannot skip that step, because every printed polyester length sold in the Surat trade starts as woven greige cloth someone had to make. Texturised yarn is 5 percent of revenue and beam, downgrade and waste sales are another 4 percent, and both are by products of spare capacity rather than separate businesses. The buyers are traders and dyeing and processing houses, about 85 percent of them in south Gujarat, and export revenue is nil. No customer is named in the annual report, but the offer document shows the largest single buyer of grey fabric in every disclosed period from FY22 to the first nine months of FY25 was Borana Industries LLP, a promoter group firm, at 5.54 to 8.34 percent of grey fabric revenue, with no disclosed price basis. Buying behaviour is loose: the offer document states there are no long term agreements with either customers or suppliers, and greige cloth carries almost no specification lock in, so switching costs are low. Concentration is moving the wrong way, with the top ten grey fabric buyers rising from 30.82 percent of that revenue in FY22 to 40.27 percent in the first nine months of FY25. Present demand comes from Surat processing houses buying cloth to dye and print for the domestic market, and the two signals that track it are the Borana Industries LLP grey fabric offtake and China POY, PFY and PTA import volumes and pricing into India, which set the input cost floor for every weaver in the cluster. Forward demand rests on three externally checkable things and one that is not checkable yet. The Ministry of Textiles PLI Scheme for MMF fabrics granted the company a Round 3 approval letter, but no incentive amount or milestone appears anywhere in the corpus. Gujarat Textile Policy 2024 subsidy notifications run the other way, because the power and interest subsidy windows are five years per unit and Unit 1 starts lapsing in 2026. The BIS QCO litigation status on polyester yarn imports is the live one, since a High Court stayed the November 2025 removal and ordered the quality control order to remain in force until further orders. The defined market grows about 7.3 percent a year and the company holds 7.1 percent of the addressable slice, which leaves roughly 14 times headroom on revenue. On competitive advantage the run found none it could score. The emerging moat scan returned 3.1 on a scale whose ceiling is near 90, classification NONE, with zero categories reaching even moderate strength; the only two rows to score anything were the renewable power build and the PLI approval, both weak. The grey fabric line has no moat: the looms are off the shelf machines at about USD 9,500 each, the cost advantage is a time limited state subsidy, and the yarn line is a by product with no moat claimed for it.

## Grey fabric scale without a durable moat

Borana Weaves is a Surat weaver that turns polyester yarn into unbleached cloth and sells it to the dyers and printers next door. FY26 revenue was Rs 389 crore, EBITDA Rs 92 crore at a 23.6 percent margin, and profit before tax Rs 78 crore. Loom count went from 700 to 1,212 in about 20 months. The company listed in May 2025 and has been public for fifteen months. Almost everything in this record is a capacity story, not a price story: production has scaled with looms, and the offer document shows the machines are bought off the shelf.

The transition on offer is a climb from commodity converter to something with pricing power, built on scale, on captive renewable power, and on a bigger value added mix. The evidence stages did not find that climb. The emerging moat scan scored 3.1 with zero active categories. Customer concentration is worsening, not improving. There are no long term contracts on either side of the business. The margin gap against the listed yarn makers is mostly a value chain step artefact, weaving against upstream polymerisation, and reading it as superior converter economics would be a mistake. The nine quarter margin record also rules out the single cause policy story: EBITDA margin ran 27.18 percent in Q4FY24, fell to 16.49 percent in Q2FY25 and recovered to 25.63 percent in Q1FY27, and the December policy change management cites cannot explain a swing that predates it by two years.

Gate 0 scored 106 of 160, classification GOOD. The raw matrix gave GOOD+ and a cash deal breaker pulled it down one tier. The moat score is 27 of 60 and reads FORTRESS, but it is narrow rather than broad: four of twelve moat metrics score the full 5 and the other eight score zero, so scale, regulation, distribution and float contribute nothing. Accounting quality scored 5 of 10 with eight red flags, and the annual report deep dive scored 4 of 10, with governance the weakest component at 3.

Cash is the deal breaker. Cumulative free cash flow is minus Rs 195 crore across FY22 to FY26, and only one of five years was positive. FY26 operating cash flow was Rs 37 crore against Rs 179 crore of capex. Cumulative operating cash flow to profit is 0.548 against a 0.50 floor, so it clears by 0.048. Working capital days went from 39.4 in FY24 to 66.5 in FY26. Receivables are not the cause and are clean: ageing beyond six months is 0.32 percent and turnover is 17.10 times. The lengthening comes from an 86.2 percent build in raw material inventory and a 93.8 percent collapse in trade payables, best explained by the shift to imports settled by letter of credit as imported raw material went from zero to 32.7 percent of the input bill. Cash stood at Rs 1.68 crore against Rs 69.51 crore of gross debt at the FY26 close. Of Rs 80.94 crore sitting in capital work in progress, Rs 8.24 lakh was capitalised in FY26, so the capex has not started earning.

Management says more than it shows. Credibility graded C. Four investor decks and one earnings call in fifteen months as a listed company, including four months of complete silence after listing. On promises the record is four delivered, one partial and one missed. The 19.79 MW hybrid power project slipped four documented times across seven months and is still uncommissioned. The 160 loom tranche at Unit 4B was never confirmed complete and was quietly replaced by a 192 loom tranche with no reconciliation. The one live margin explanation given on the call never appears in the annual report that covers the delivering quarter.

Three findings surfaced only in verification, and each sits inside a document the pipeline already had open. The renewable power saving management stated twice on the call, Rs 18 to 20 crore a year, is 92 to 102 percent of the company's entire audited Factory Power Expenses (Net) of Rs 19.56 crore, while the stated substitution is only 70 to 80 percent and the hybrid is a third party power purchase project; the claim exceeds a ceiling it cannot reach, and it anchors the payback arithmetic on a Rs 125 crore investment. The CARO annexure records Rs 10.59 crore of loans and advances outstanding to five parties at 31 March 2026, including Rs 3.00 crore to promoter group entity R&B Denims Ltd, the company SEBI searched three weeks later, against the CFO on the record saying "we will invest in our running business only. This is just a purely equity investment. Nothing else". And the Q4FY26 investor deck restates FY25 operating metrics as FY26: average capacity utilisation of 80.93 percent carries the FY25 label into an FY26 label unchanged to two decimals, unit figures repeat across longer periods, and loom count stays at 1,208 after 160 looms were added. On page 26 of the same deck every margin on the half year profit and loss page is exactly twice the correct figure, with EBITDA margin printed at 49.75 percent against an actual 24.85 percent, gross at 86.0 against 42.9, and profit after tax at 33.74 against 16.86. The rupee figures are right; every percentage runs on half the denominator.

Governance carries the same pattern of gaps rather than any single event. All three independent directors and both non Chairman executive directors also sit on the board of R&B Denims Ltd, so every related party approval on record was voted by directors who are at the same time R&B Denims fiduciaries. The factory sits on land owned by directors and a relative, with no title deed in the company's name and rent paid to them, and Rs 29.89 crore of building stands on it. SEBI searched R&B Denims and the premises of certain promoters and its CFO in April 2026 under Section 11C(9), and the subject is still undisclosed four and a half months later; a 2017 SEBI Takeover Regulations settlement of Rs 2 lakh names the same promoters over an R&B Denims disclosure lapse. Internal audit reports were not made available to the statutory auditor for FY26, and six exchange observation letters were not furnished to the incoming secretarial auditor. The annual report names Brickwork and omits CARE's ISSUER NOT COOPERATING status, held since October 2024 and continued in December 2025. Hedging is disclosed as Nil twice against a first year import book of about Rs 120 crore. There are no Key Audit Matters. Against all that, two things are clean: promoter shareholding is 65.24 percent with zero pledge and no selling, and the July 2026 secretarial auditor sequence is far less alarming than the run's own opening record framed it. There was one resignation, not two, on 24 July 2026; the 29 July filing is the Board noting that same resignation. A reason was given, pre-occupation with professional commitments, with a written confirmation that there were no disputes, differences or pending issues. The mandate was two years, FY2025-26 and FY2026-27, not five.

Peers complicate the story rather than settle it. All twelve transcripts across three yarn makers were read and used. The BIS quality control order mechanism is real and the yarn price move is corroborated, but Borana's CFO dated the removal to December and treated it as one action with anti dumping duty, where two peers and a CRISIL analyst date it to 12 and 13 November 2025 and treat the two as separate policy tracks. One peer puts the asset turn on new capacity at 0.8 to 0.85 times against the company's claimed two to three times, with the caveat that the peer was talking about cotton spindles, a different machine class. The closest thing in the corpus to a read on weaving economics went unused: the Sangam call of August 2024 gives yarn margin around 10 percent as a long run average on a 3 to 20 percent range, fabric value addition adding another 4 to 5 points over the yarn base, and garmenting at 15 to 20 percent at full utilisation. No stage engaged it, and the peer stage stated in four places that no peer discloses any fabric margin.

The market has room and the growth arithmetic does not settle cleanly. The addressable market runs Rs 13,637 crore on the conservative chain and Rs 19,965 crore on the realistic one, the serviceable slice is Rs 5,455 crore, and the company holds 7.1 percent of it, which is about 14 times headroom. Runway class is STRONG. The implied revenue growth that the share gain would require is 20.6 percent at three years and 19.3 percent at five on the conservative chain, and 26.2 percent and 23.7 percent on the realistic one, so 20 to 26 percent is a band and not a point, and it straddles rather than clears the 25 percent the strategy looks for. Three year capacity is sufficient only if the Rs 350 to 400 crore doubling to 2,000 looms is funded, and it is not funded as of the FY26 close. Five year capacity falls short even at the full announced plan, by roughly 207 further looms nobody has announced. And the input cost tailwind behind the whole recent margin base has a live legal risk that appears in no corpus document: the BIS quality control order removal was stayed by a High Court, which ordered the order to remain in force until further notice.
