# Business narrative: Divgi Torqtransfer Systems (DIVGIITTS)

## Business understanding narrative

Divgi Torqtransfer Systems makes the parts that send engine power to the wheels. Its largest product is the transfer case, half of sales, the gearbox that splits drive between the front and rear axles in a four wheel drive vehicle. Transmission components, including precision machined export parts, are 28% of sales. An EV transmission line called E-Gear Drive is 8%, synchronizer systems 6%, and a legacy automatic locking hub 2%. A carmaker cannot fit a four wheel drive platform without a qualified transfer case supplier, and qualifying a new one takes years of tooling and testing.

The customers are vehicle makers, not consumers. The annual report names eight OEMs, with Mahindra and Tata Motors the anchor accounts. These buyers run multi year platform programs. Once a supplier wins a platform and passes the parts approval process, the OEM rarely switches mid life because re qualifying costs time and money. The company does not disclose how much revenue any single customer or program carries, so the concentration is real but unquantified.

Present demand tracks a small set of vehicle programs. Transfer case volume follows Mahindra Scorpio and Thar class 4x4 SUV sales at home. The single largest confirmed export program is the Indonesia Scorpio Pik Up, a 35,000 unit order for CY2026. Tata Motors and Force Motors 4x4 and UV volumes are the diversification beyond Mahindra.

Forward demand rests on four external drivers. India EV passenger vehicle sales and the PLI Auto policy drive the E-Gear Drive line. A China plus one sourcing shift, where global OEMs move precision work out of China, drives the components export book. The BorgWarner technology licensing relationship governs access to AWD technology and the royalty cost. A named North American OEM automatic transmission platform decision, still not contracted, is the binary catalyst behind the largest claimed opportunity, and Project Mayflower, the planned US subsidiary, signals how far that relationship goes.

The competitive advantage sits with the mechanical driveline lines, not the new ones. The transfer case and transmission components carry a switching cost from OEM qualification and platform lock in, the best evidenced moat in the scan. The post IPO cash pile and the China plus one export position add to it. The EV transmission line has no proven moat yet, and its launch has slipped three quarters running. The automatic transmission opportunity has no signed contract, so it carries no moat today, only optionality.

## Cash heavy driveline maker on an upcycle

Divgi makes driveline and transmission parts for carmakers from four plants, and listed in March 2023. FY26 revenue from operations was Rs 353 Cr, up 61% on the year. Total income was Rs 375 Cr. PAT was Rs 47 Cr, up 92%. The shares trade at Rs 1,175, a market value of about Rs 3,594 Cr.

The company is climbing out of a FY24 to FY25 trough. Operating return on capital swung from a 4.2% low back to double digits. It is pushing three transitions at once: exports beyond India, an EV transmission line, and a claimed automatic transmission program with a North American OEM. Q1 FY27 kept the pace, revenue Rs 142 Cr, up 85%, led by transfer case volume up 93%.

The quality screen scores the business AVERAGE, 47 of 160. The cap comes from return on capital, not from the operating business. Median ROCE reads 7.68% across the two years that can be computed. A Rs 293 Cr net cash pile sitting on the balance sheet inflates the capital base and drags the ratio down. Strip out the treasury interest and operating ROCE was 12.7% in FY26. Margin has still compressed about 7.9 points over eight years even as revenue grew, and three of the last eight years showed a revenue fall.

The balance sheet is debt free with interest cover of 94.8 times and a current ratio of 5.0. Net cash rose about Rs 9 Cr in FY26 to Rs 293 Cr, which contradicts the Rs 275 Cr net cash fall stated in the run brief. Free cash flow still turned negative, minus Rs 3 Cr, because capex of Rs 44 Cr ran just ahead of operating cash flow of Rs 41 Cr. Working capital days improved by 14. Receivables are clean, all inside the six month bucket, and cumulative cash conversion is 97% of profit. No credit rating rationale is available, so the cash quality cannot be independently certified this run.

Management earns a B on delivery. It hit its financial guidance and delivered the flagship Indonesia ramp on schedule. Against that, the EV transmission launch slipped three straight quarters on the same external excuse, an automatic transmission contract milestone was dropped after the deadline passed, cash was never quantified on any call, and management could not split Indonesia driven growth from organic when asked directly. Governance carries three verified problems. The chairman and MD letters describe a US subsidiary as already operating when the Board's report states none existed in the year. A related party, Tejal Transmission, linked to a sitting executive director, is missing from the related party list. MD pay appears in three different figures across the same annual report.

Peers complicate the story rather than confirm it. Management calls the India EV market flat to justify the EV launch delay. Peer SONACOMS reports Indian electric car and two wheeler sales up 65% and 45%, and calls India the fastest growing EV market in the world. The China plus one sourcing shift that underpins the export thesis is corroborated by three of four peers, but none names Divgi as a beneficiary. The transfer case market size, the sole Tier 1 claim, and the Indonesia tender savings figure could not be verified against any peer.

The runway is large. The served market is about Rs 2,160 Cr and Divgi holds roughly 16% of it, leaving about six times its current revenue as headroom. A three year capture path implies revenue growth near 16% a year. Management's Rs 1,000 Cr FY29 to FY30 target sits 1.4 times above that path, which reads as reachable rather than a stretch. The Indonesia program that anchors near term growth is a single confirmed 35,000 unit order, not the 70,000 units the run brief carried, and follow on volume is not yet confirmed.
