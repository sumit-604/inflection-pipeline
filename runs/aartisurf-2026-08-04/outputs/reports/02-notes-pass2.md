## PASS 2 NEW FINDINGS (not covered, or covered only partially, in Pass 1)

### 1. Redeemable Preference Shares are unlisted almost two years post-issuance because the Company cannot obtain the required credit rating — a material gap on the very instrument Pass 1 flagged as quasi-equity-in-debt-clothing

Secretarial Audit Report (Form MR-3, Annexure B to Directors' Report, p.21) records a
qualified observation: the 10,82,387 0% Redeemable Preference Shares (carrying value ₹1,941.88
Lakhs, Note 11.1(b), p.72) issued 20 August 2019 pursuant to the Composite Scheme of Arrangement
"are not yet listed on the BSE and NSE as per the requirements of SEBI Circular No. CIR/IMD/DF/50/2017."
Director's Report clarification (p.17–18) gives the reason: **"there is also a requirement for
the Redeemable Preference Shares to be rated by the Credit rating agency with a minimum credit
rating of AA-. Since our Company has recently incorporated and does not have its own independent
past track record, getting this rating at this juncture is not possible inspite of having an
absolute clean credit record… there has been delay in listing of these Redeemable Preference
Shares. The Company is considering possible options to address the issues in this regard."**
Cross-referencing the Corporate Governance Report's credit-rating disclosure (Section 6.xx,
p.36): CARE Ratings actually assigned the Redeemable Preference Shares **CARE BBB- (RPS),
Stable** (upgraded 7 October 2020 from CARE BB+ (RPS), Stable) — four full notches below the
AA- minimum needed for listing. 🔴 Red Flag — a debt-classified, 4%-accreting instrument
(Note 11.1(b), already flagged Watch in Pass 1 for its debt/equity classification optics) has
now been shown to also carry an actual, quantified, structural listing impediment: the
Company's own credit quality is roughly 7 notches short of what regulation requires for these
securities to trade. This is a harder fact than Pass 1's classification-optics point and
belongs in the consolidated Top 15.

### 2. Secretarial Audit Report catalogues four further compliance lapses in the listing/post-demerger process, none captured in Pass 1

Same Secretarial Audit Report (Form MR-3, p.21, items (i)–(iv)):
- **Delayed listing of equity shares**: trading in the 75,84,477 equity shares issued under the
  Scheme commenced on BSE/NSE only on 14 July 2020, beyond the 60-day window prescribed from the
  NCLT sanction order date (20 June 2019) under SEBI Circular CFD/DIL3/CIR/2017/21 (as amended).
- **IEPF Form 4 not filed**: the Company has not been able to file the statutory Form IEPF-4 for
  29,914 equity shares transferred to the Investor Education and Protection Fund Authority
  (transferred 12 September 2019, statement due within 30 days of the corporate action), due to
  an unresolved "technical issue," still being followed up as of the report date (9 July 2021).
- **Late share certificate issuance**: one 120-share certificate (name deletion) and one
  120-share certificate (transmission) were issued after the SEBI LODR-prescribed 15-day and
  21-day windows respectively (covered by a subsequent SEBI relaxation circular).
- **Quarterly results resubmission**: Q2/H1 FY2020-21 financial results were initially submitted
  to the exchanges in the wrong format and had to be resubmitted in prescribed XBRL format on
  9 December 2020, following a stock-exchange query.
🟡 Watch, in aggregate — none individually material, but four distinct compliance friction
points in the Company's first full year as a listed entity is a pattern worth tracking, not
just an isolated slip.

### 3. Large intra-year promoter-family shareholding increase, concurrent with an institutional investor's exit, during the year the stock price rose roughly 3x

Note 9.4 (Details of shareholders holding more than 5% shares, standalone FS, p.71):

| Shareholder | FY2019-20 shares (%) | FY2020-21 shares (%) | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jaya Chandrakant Gogri | 3,99,449 (5.27%) | 16,05,708 (21.17%) | +12,06,259 shares (+301.9%) |
| Nikhil Holdings Private Limited | Not a 5%+ holder | 4,26,374 (5.62%) | New entrant |
| Rashesh Chandrakant Gogri | 3,83,438 (5.06%) | 3,83,438 (5.06%) | Unchanged |
| HDFC Trustee Company Ltd. | 7,13,681 (9.41%) | Not disclosed (below 5%) | Fell below 5% threshold |

Jaya Chandrakant Gogri is, per the Corporate Governance Report (p.26), the spouse of
Non-Executive Director and promoter Mr. Chandrakant V. Gogri (who separately holds 1,85,302
equity shares directly, per Annexure A to the Notice, p.133, and the CGR shareholding table,
p.26). Her holding more than quintupled during the year, taking her alone to 21.17% of the
company — the single largest individual shareholder line item disclosed. Simultaneously, HDFC
Trustee Company Ltd, previously the fourth-largest disclosed shareholder at 9.41%, dropped out
of the >5% disclosure table entirely. This reshuffling occurred against a monthly BSE share
price range (Corporate Governance Report, p.34) that ran from a July 2020 low of ₹246.50 to a
December 2020 high of ₹1,211.00 — roughly a 3x-4x move within the same financial year the shares
listed. The relationship of Nikhil Holdings Private Limited to the Company (whether promoter
group, MD Nikhil Desai's family entity, or unrelated) is **NOT FOUND IN DOCUMENT** — Note 9.4
states only the shareholding, not the relationship, and this entity is not listed in the Related
Party note (31.2). 🔴 Red Flag — a promoter-family member's shareholding increasing five-fold
while a large institutional holder exits, inside the same year as an extreme share-price run-up
and the Company's very first (voluntary, first-time) dividend declaration, is a pattern an
equity analyst should put directly to management; it was not surfaced in Pass 1 because Pass 1
did not examine the >5% shareholder note.

### 4. Post-year-end special resolution seeks to raise the borrowing ceiling to ₹225 Crores — roughly 48% above current Gross Debt

Notice of 3rd AGM, Item No. 7 (Special Resolution, p.122/131): shareholder approval is sought
under Section 180(1)(c) of the Companies Act, 2013 to raise the Board's borrowing-powers ceiling
to **₹225 Crores**, against Gross Debt of ₹151.94 Cr as at 31 March 2021 (Note 32.1, p.82) — i.e.
headroom for roughly 48% further debt increase is being requested at the same AGM that will
also vote on the Company's first-ever dividend. The stated purpose (Annexure to Notice, Item
7 explanatory statement, p.131) is "to augment long term funds which may be required for growth
opportunities in the existing operations, various products under development and other corporate
requirement." 🟡 Watch — this is a forward-looking capital-structure signal, not a backward-facing
number, and should be read together with Pass 1's already-flagged 1.09x Net Gearing Ratio and the
working-capital deterioration (receivable/payable days); it indicates the Board is deliberately
keeping leverage optionality open rather than paring debt down.

### 5. CFO turnover in the same year the Company became listed

Directors' Report (p.14): "there were no changes in the Directors / Key Managerial Personnel of
the Company except Mr. Nitesh Harakchand Medh was appointed as Chief Financial Officer (Key
Managerial Personnel) of the Company in the Board Meeting... held on June 04, 2020" — i.e. the
CFO signing FY2020-21's accounts (and certifying them, p.38) had been in the CFO seat only from
the first quarter of the very year under review, itself the Company's first full year as a
listed entity (listed 14 July 2020). 🟡 Watch — not inherently a red flag, but a new-CFO/new-listing
combination in a year with a >10x profit jump, a first-ever borrowing-cost capitalisation policy,
and a first-ever dividend is a relevant context data point for management-quality assessment,
not previously noted in Pass 1.

### 6. Marketing narrative claims "R&D competence"; Annexure C discloses Nil R&D spend

"About Aarti Surfactants" (Corporate Overview, p.4) states the Company's "vast expertise,
**technology and R&D competence**... underpin our operations." Annexure C to the Directors'
Report (Conservation of Energy, Technology Absorption, Foreign Exchange Earnings and Outgo,
p.24), Item 3, "Expenditure Incurred on Research and Development," discloses: **Nil**. 🟡 Watch
— a minor rhetoric-versus-disclosure gap; the marketing positioning (echoed in the "Key
Competitive Advantages" graphic, p.10, listing "Established Long-standing Relationships" and
"Efficient Raw Material Sourcing" rather than R&D as the differentiators) is arguably more
consistent with the Annexure C disclosure than the "About" page copy is — worth noting for
calibrating how much weight to give the Company's own qualitative claims about its moat
elsewhere in the annual report.
