## SECTION 1: THE BUSINESS MODEL IN PLAIN ENGLISH

### 1A. One-line description

CleanMax builds, part-owns and runs solar and wind power plants for large Indian and global companies, selling them electricity on 20-25 year contracts, and separately builds similar plants that customers own outright, then maintains those plants for a fee (AR p.44, "Business Background").

### 1B. Money flow chain, for EACH revenue stream

**Stream 1: RE Power Sales (73.2% of FY26 revenue)**

[Input: land + solar panels/wind turbines/BESS, funded by project debt plus equity from CleanMax and, in many projects, a minimum 26% equity stake from the customer itself] → [CleanMax designs, builds through its own EPC arm, and operates the plant inside a dedicated project company (SPV) for the 20-25 year contract] → [delivers physical electricity through the grid (open access) or, for pure environmental-attribute deals, only the "green" certificate while the power itself is sold on the open market] → [payer: the C&I customer that signed the Power Purchase Agreement (PPA) or Energy Attribute Purchase Agreement (EAPA)] → [how they pay: monthly billing at the pre-agreed tariff for units delivered, average collection cycle 25 days FY26 (Inv. Pres. p.27)] (AR pp.45-47, Note 55 p.469).

**Stream 2: RE Services (26.0% of FY26 revenue) — CAPEX/EPC construction + O&M + Carbon Services**

[Input: CleanMax's land-sourcing, engineering, procurement and construction expertise, funded by the CUSTOMER's own capital this time] → [CleanMax designs, procures and builds a plant the customer owns, then runs O&M for the asset's life, plus sells RECs/I-RECs/carbon credits and VPPA advisory] → [delivers a commissioned turnkey plant plus ongoing operations and carbon-market services] → [payer: the customer that is investing in its own asset, or a corporate buying carbon/environmental attributes] → [how they pay: milestone billing recognised on a percentage-of-completion basis during construction (lumpy, judgment-heavy — flagged as a Key Audit Matter, AR p.144), then an annual O&M fee once operating; RE Services receivable days 93 in FY26, down from 136 in FY25 but still far higher and more volatile than Power Sales (Inv. Pres. p.27)] (AR p.45, "Renewable Energy Services Segment").

### 1E. The chai-stall-uncle version

Think of CleanMax as a landlord who builds solar farms and wind farms, then rents the electricity out to big companies for 20-25 years at a fixed price, like a long lease. For every farm, the law says the tenant (the customer) must also own a slice of the building, at least 26%, so CleanMax's own share of the rent shrinks a bit each time it signs a new tenant this way. Most of the money (73 out of every 100 rupees) is this steady rent business. The rest (26 rupees) is a separate, lumpier job: CleanMax also builds solar farms for OTHER companies who want to own the whole thing themselves, gets paid in construction milestones like a contractor, and then keeps the lights on (O&M) after handover. The rent business is boring and predictable, like a fixed deposit. The construction business is choppy, like a builder's income, some quarters huge, some quarters small.

### 2D. Industry lifecycle stage

Growth stage, inside a broader secular growth industry. India added a record 55.3 GW of non-fossil capacity in FY26 (AR p.141); global renewable capacity additions hit a record 692 GW in 2025 (AR p.140). Within that growth industry, a newer, faster sub-cycle is forming around AI/data-centre power demand ("Age of Electricity," AR p.140-141): every 1 GW of data-centre IT load needs close to 6 GW of renewable power behind it (AR p.11). CleanMax is positioned as an early leader in the CTU-connected, hyperscaler-focused segment of this sub-cycle (525 MW Bikaner CTU project, first of its kind for the company, AR p.143).

### 3D. Unit economics — the physics of the business

- **One unit**: 1 MW (MWp for solar) of operational RE Power Sales capacity, seasoned to steady state.
- **Revenue per unit**: not a single disclosed Rs/MW figure — derived from weighted average tariff (~₹4.20/kWh operational, Inv. Pres. p.27) multiplied by generation, which is PLF-dependent (solar offsite ~24.6% PLF, wind ~35.1% PLF, Inv. Pres. p.26). Flagged as derived, not directly disclosed.
- **Cost per unit**: capex ₹3.5 Cr/MWp solar, ₹7.8 Cr/MW wind upfront (Inv. Pres. p.10); ongoing cost = project-debt service (blended 8.5%, Inv. Pres. p.19) plus O&M.
- **Volume drivers**: MW commissioned (backlog-to-operational conversion) + PLF (weather/technology dependent) + grid/plant availability (98-99%, Inv. Pres. p.26).
- **Price drivers**: PPA tariff locked at signing; new-contract tariffs falling (₹4.12/kWh FY24 → ₹3.57/kWh FY26 trailing-12m newly commissioned, Inv. Pres. p.27) — a function of module/turbine cost deflation and competitive tendering, not CleanMax pricing power.
- **Cost drivers**: module/turbine/BESS capex, land, evacuation infrastructure, project-debt cost, O&M.
- **Incremental margin and operating leverage**: once seasoned, each new MW carries close to portfolio-average EBITDA margin (83-84%, Inv. Pres. p.10), and corporate SG&A leverage further lifts consolidated margin as scale grows (18%→9% of income, AR p.142). But each new MW also adds project debt, and a shrinking parent equity share per project (51%-100% range vs. historically higher stakes) means consolidated EBITDA growth can overstate CleanMax-shareholder economic growth. The metric to watch is PAT attributable to owners growth vs. consolidated EBITDA/PAT growth (Inv. Pres. p.28: owners' PAT ₹941.32 Mn FY26 vs. ₹278.43 Mn FY25 — currently a positive signal, but must be tracked per new project as ownership % varies).

### 4C. Quarterly monitoring checklist

2. Contracted-yet-to-be-executed capacity trend — good: stable/growing backlog; trouble: shrinking without matching commissioning.
3. Segment EBITDA margin, Power Sales and Services separately — good: Power Sales ≥80%, Services holding double digits; trouble: either compresses.

6. Net Debt/Run-rate EBITDA and Debt(net)/Adjusted EBITDA (annualised) — good: within 5.0x-5.5x band; trouble: breach and staying above.

9. RE Services DSO — good: declining toward double digits; trouble: re-widening past ~130 days.
