## Business Understanding Narrative

Venky's is three businesses in one listed company, and only one of them is any good.

The first is poultry. Venky's breeds, hatches and grows chickens, then sells day old chicks
and grown birds. Revenue is birds times price. Price is set by a spot market nobody
controls. FY26 revenue was Rs 1,897 crore and profit before interest and tax was Rs 65
crore, a 3.5% margin (Results p.3).

The second is Animal Health Products. Venky's makes medicines, supplements and nutritional
products for poultry farms, its own and other people's. FY26 revenue Rs 392 crore and PBIT
Rs 99 crore, a 25.3% margin (Results p.3). This division earns more profit than poultry on
a fifth of the revenue.

The third is oilseed. Venky's crushes soybean into oil and meal, feeds the meal to its own
birds and sells the rest. FY26 revenue Rs 1,554 crore and PBIT Rs 54 crore, a 3.5% margin
(Results p.3). Management describes part of that profit plainly: "a certain amount of
profit come from the inventory, which is a speculative profit" (Concall p.18).

What is changing: nothing that management is claiming. There is no declared transition. The
quiet fact is that Animal Health has been compounding, with margins rising from "under 17%,
18%" a decade ago to 29% in the March quarter (Concall p.15), while poultry volume has not
grown since FY20 (Concall p.12).

Why now: because FY26 looks like a turnaround year and is not one. Full year PAT was Rs 139
crore, and Rs 101 crore of it came in the March quarter alone (Results p.2).

What must be true for a thesis: either Animal Health gets separated and repriced, or
poultry realisations stay high.

What breaks it: they already broke. By mid May, broiler prices had fallen from Rs 114 to
about Rs 90, and day old chicks from Rs 55 to Rs 35 against a Rs 32 cost (Concall p.12).

## 2. Business model and archetype
Three segments, reported separately, with only Rs 115 crore of inter-segment revenue on
Rs 3,843 crore of gross segment revenue (Results p.3). The divisions barely trade with each
other on the P&L, which is why two shareholders on the call asked for the Animal Health
business to be demerged (Concall p.13).

Archetypes, one per segment, because one label would lie:
- Poultry: commodity converter. Section 1B v3.7 Amendment 17 binds. Feed and chick go in,
  bird comes out, and the spread is the business. 80% of broiler volume runs through
  integration, 20% through the open market (Concall p.5).
- Animal Health: value added supplier moving toward brand. Products are specified by
  veterinarians, distribution reaches "Trivandrum to Guwahati or Agartala ... within 6 to 7
  days", and market share is 24% to 25% with a 40% to 45% target (Concall p.15, p.17).
- Oilseed: commodity converter. The economics are capacity utilisation, 65% to 70% today
  going to 80%, plus inventory positioning (Concall p.14).

## 3. Competitive advantages
Segment by segment. Poultry has none that survive a price cycle; the one structural edge is
group parentage, and the CFO closed that door himself: parent chicks are sold at "a uniform
price for all the customers" (Concall p.5). Oilseed has scale and a procurement network
across Maharashtra, which is a cost position, not a moat.

Animal Health has the real advantage: a national cold and dry distribution chain, a new
therapeutic plant running at 35% to 40% utilisation, natural growth promoters positioned
against antimicrobial resistance rules, and technical support to farmers (Concall p.15).
That is spec-in plus service. Moat verdict: absent in two segments, forming to present in
the third.

## 4. The promoters
The Rao family, through Venkateshwara Hatcheries Pvt Ltd, the unlisted group parent.
B. Balaji Rao is Managing Director (Results p.6). The only insider records held are 2023
transfers between the MD and Venkateshwara Hatcheries, not open market dealing (market
file). No insider transaction is recorded in FY25, FY26 or FY27.

Group dependence is the question, not family experience. Trade receivables are Rs 544
crore, and the CFO states they include "external as well as the group associates" without
splitting the two (Concall p.18). The oilseed division sells most of its meal inside the
group and is trying to get outside sales to 60% to 65% of revenue (Concall p.9). A CCI
antitrust notice reached the group over practices at the parent breeding farms, and the
company secretary says Venky's itself does not deal in those products (Concall p.10).
Promoter holding and pledge are NOT FOUND.

## 5. Financial trajectory
FY26 against FY25 (Results p.2): revenue Rs 3,727 crore against Rs 3,307 crore, up 13%.
PBT Rs 186 crore against Rs 163 crore. PAT Rs 139 crore against Rs 117 crore. EPS Rs 98.85.

The two quarters (Results p.2): December quarter PAT Rs 49 crore, March quarter PAT Rs 101
crore against Rs 13 crore a year earlier. The March quarter carried 73% of the full year
profit.

Segment PBIT for FY26 tells the true story (Results p.3). Poultry earned Rs 65 crore for
the year and Rs 81 crore of it in the March quarter, so the first nine months of poultry
lost about Rs 15 crore (derived). Animal Health earned Rs 99 crore, up from Rs 72 crore.
Oilseed earned Rs 54 crore, up from Rs 19 crore.

Balance sheet is sound. Equity Rs 1,596 crore, total borrowings Rs 163 crore, cash and
investments about Rs 304 crore (Results p.4). Cash from operations Rs 191 crore against
Rs 139 crore of profit (Results p.5), so cash conversion is genuine. Dividend Rs 10 a share.

Classify the inflection: cyclical, not structural. One good quarter of realisations on top
of a steadily improving Animal Health and a recovering oilseed division.

## 6. The transition and the quality ladder
The company claims no transition, and the ladder has to be read per segment.
Poultry sits at R1 and is not moving; volumes are flat over six years (Concall p.12).
Oilseed sits at R1 to R2, moving on utilisation, not on pricing.
Animal Health sits at R3 and is arguably climbing toward R4, on 25% segment margins, a 24%
share and a widening product line.

Management's claim, quoted once, and note that it is about balance rather than climb:
"majority of the expansion and majority of the production and majority of the EBITDA is
coming from those divisions, though principally, we call ourselves as a poultry company"
(Concall p.5).

That is the finding. A real rung climb exists inside a company whose reported identity, and
whose multiple, belong to a commodity.

## 8. Proof check
The proof point that matters is not in the corpus, and its absence is itself informative.
On 15 May 2026 the CFO said broiler prices "ranging up to INR105 to INR110" in the March
quarter had continued through April and the first ten days of May, then started falling
(Concall p.8), and by page 12 he puts the current level at "INR90 or INR93" against a Rs 89
cost of production. Day old chicks fell from Rs 55 to Rs 35, against a Rs 32 cost, and to
Rs 21 or Rs 22 in parts of the north.

So the March quarter that produced 73% of the year's profit was a price spike, and the
spike was already over when management described it. The June quarter results would settle
this in one line. Bull AI does not carry them.

Animal Health passes its own proof check: segment PBIT rose from Rs 72 crore to Rs 99 crore
with margin expanding, and the driver named is product mix and a new plant, not price
(Results p.3, Concall p.15).

## 12. Verdict card
**PASS as a transition alpha name. WATCH as a sum of the parts situation.** Venky's does not
fit the strategy. There is no rung climb at company level, the cycle has already turned
down, and the multiple prices a peak quarter.

The idea that is actually here is different, and honest to name: an Rs 392 crore Animal
Health business earning 25% segment margins sits inside a commodity holding company, and
two shareholders asked for it to be separated on the same call. That is a value and
catalyst idea, not a GARP transition, and this framework is not built to price it.

Load-bearing fact to verify first: the June 2026 quarter. It tells you how much of FY26 was
price. Verify next: (2) the split of Rs 544 crore of receivables between external and group
parties, from the related party schedule; (3) Animal Health capital employed and return on
it, to test whether the 25% margin is a real franchise; (4) any board discussion of a
demerger; (5) the CCI matter's scope.

What would change the view: a board resolution to demerge Animal Health, or two clean
quarters showing Animal Health above Rs 110 crore of EBITDA while poultry breaks even at
trough prices. Falling below the FY25 pattern in the June and September quarters would
confirm the PASS. No price here.
