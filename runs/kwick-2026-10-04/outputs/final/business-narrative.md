# KWICK: Kwick Forensic Solutions Ltd (BSE SME 544895). Business narrative, Phase 1

## Business understanding narrative

Kwick supplies the tools that police and forensic labs use to collect and test evidence. Physical evidence kits, 37% of FY26 sales, are fingerprint, evidence and DNA collection kits, light sources and fuming chambers, packed into 14 standard kits at its Chennai office. Cyber and digital forensics, 33% of sales, is the resale of imagers, write blockers and triage kits that copy and read seized phones and drives. DNA forensics, 11% of sales, resells rapid DNA systems, genetic analysers and DNA database software to state labs. Van kits that dealers and vehicle builders fit into mobile crime scene vans make up 10%, and handheld scanners rented to the Government of Bihar for liquor detection make up 9%. Police find these tools hard to skip because BNSS, the new criminal procedure law, makes forensic examination mandatory for offences punishable by seven years or more.

The buyers are state and central police, state forensic labs, fingerprint bureaus and training academies, who buy by tender and on GeM at the lowest compliant price and pay after they accept the goods. The top customer took 24% of FY26 sales and the top ten took 78%, the customer count fell from 128 to 95 in two years, and maintenance contracts that could lock buyers in are under 1% of sales.

Present demand is scheme money, tracked by MHA and DFSS approvals and releases under SMFC and successor scheme, and by GeM bid and order awards for forensic kits, DNA and cyber tools. The Government of Bihar equipment rental contract renewal is a signal on its own, because that one customer is the whole rental line.

Forward demand rests on State notifications under BNSS section 176(3), each of which starts a five year clock in that state, on NFIES CFSL and NFSU campus tenders including the Varanasi campus, on the Union Budget and Demand for Grants, MHA forensic lines, and on NCRB and I4C cybercrime registrations for the cyber line. Against this, the SMFC scheme that paid for vans and state lab kits ended in March 2026, and the run did not find its successor.

The run found no moat in any line. The emerging moat scan scored 11.6, below the 12 point line for even a modest moat, with no category of the 22 at Strong or Moderate, and categories 21 and 22, talent asymmetry and cannibalisation barrier, at zero. The kit line has non exclusive access to OEMs such as Sirchie, Thermo Fisher and Smallpond, which is the nearest thing to an edge. The cyber and DNA lines are resale with no moat, the van line is moving to dealers with no moat, and the rental line is one contract, not a moat. The only Moderate item is the BNSS and scheme tailwind, and every competitor shares it. The in house E-Forensics software and handheld devices are unproven, and the run did not establish any revenue from them.

Police forensic reseller rides shared policy tailwind

Kwick sells forensic kits, cyber tools, DNA systems and van kits to Indian police and forensic labs, and rents scanners to one state. Revenue was Rs 30 Cr in FY24, Rs 65 Cr in FY25 and Rs 106 Cr in FY26. Profit after tax rose from Rs 2.8 Cr to Rs 8.6 Cr to Rs 13.5 Cr. The company listed on BSE SME on 3 September 2026, so the restated prospectus is the only audited record. There is no annual report for FY26, no results filing since listing, no earnings call and no credit rating. The whole business runs on 33 payroll staff and a six person sales team.

Two things changed fast. Government buyers fell from 87% of sales in FY24 to 55% in FY26, and the mix moved toward cyber and DNA resale, with DNA going from Rs 0.4 Cr to Rs 11 Cr in two years. The van line also changed shape: Kwick now sells kits for vans through dealers and vehicle builders, Rs 20 Cr of them in FY26 for 95 vehicles. That dealer revenue is the same rupee amount as the second and fourth largest customers added together. Those two customers match the Maharashtra and Rajasthan state totals almost to the lakh, and the prospectus says the end client inspects each vehicle before dispatch. The most likely reading is that state police are the real buyers behind two dealer invoices. On that reading the government share is about 75%, and the customer shift is mostly a billing route. About Rs 27 Cr of non government sales still has no named buyer. The proof that a climb up the quality ladder has started has not fired.

The screen scores Kwick 85 of 160, capped at AVERAGE, because cash from operations covered only 46% of profit over FY21 to FY26. Growth and the balance sheet score near the top: zero debt and Rs 13 Cr of cash at March 2026. The margin story cuts the other way. Gross margin fell from 37% to 31% to 27% of revenue, while operating costs fell from 19.5% to 9.1% of revenue, so the EBITDA margin sat flat at 18%. That cost cushion is close to spent. The four moat points the screen awards come from mechanical tests that flip on the choice of years, and the separate moat scan finds none. Accounting quality scores 5 out of 10.

Cash from operations was minus Rs 2.6 Cr, then Rs 4.7 Cr, then Rs 7.6 Cr against the profits above, or 56% of profit in FY26. Rs 1.4 Cr of the FY26 figure came from GST timing, and FY25 leaned on Rs 7.2 Cr of extra supplier credit. Debtor days look better than they are. The 83, 89 and 74 days in the prospectus use average receivables, a basis the ratio note prints. At year end the days were 144, 111 and 79. Both bases improve, and the share of dues older than six months fell to 12%. Rs 1.3 Cr has sat for two to three years with only a fifth of it provided. Suppliers now want faster payment, the top supplier takes 41% of purchases, and the FY27 plan needs Rs 44 Cr of working capital, of which the IPO funds Rs 31 Cr. One reading says growth ate the cash and it comes back as growth slows. The other says a tender reseller with weak supplier terms never converts much better. The first half FY27 cash flow separates the two.

Management claims get a C. Of the prospectus promises the run could test, 4 were delivered, 3 partly and 1 missed, with 4 untestable. The miss matters: domestic sourcing was meant to lift gross margin, and gross margin fell 10 points. The investor deck says 130 customers against 95 in the audited figures, prints profit before tax Rs 1 Cr higher than its own arithmetic, and shows a Rs 1,852 crore scheme outlay where the prospectus says Rs 185 Cr. On 26 September the company told BSE it had nothing to disclose. On 27 September it filed a note on a third party Rs 150 Cr NFSU campus with no Kwick order named. The promoter read is CAUTION at the upper end. Two group companies sell 48% and 62% of their turnover to Kwick, promoters took Rs 9.7 Cr out in the IPO, equal to 72% of FY26 profit, and three independent directors left within ten months. On the other side, promoter loans are fully repaid, nothing is pledged, and no regulator has acted against the promoters.

No listed company does forensics, so the peers are Zen Technologies, Allied Digital and Dynacons, which sell to the same government buyers on the same budget cycle. They confirm that resale lines carry the price risk: hardware prices rose 25% to 50% and pushed Allied Digital to walk away from tenders. They also show where Kwick is unusual. March quarter sales were 51% of Kwick's FY25 against 26% to 33% at the peers. Peers fund working capital with supplier credit while Kwick moves the other way. Kwick's 27% gross margin sits above the peer resale band of single digits to 18%, which makes convergence the obvious bear question. No peer contradicted a Kwick claim, and three questions had no peer answer at all.

The market Kwick fishes in is modest and policy funded. The run sized the central pool at Rs 921 Cr a year from the FY27 budget, a secondary source, and a scheme floor at Rs 416 Cr a year. Kwick's scheme linked sales are about 19% of that floor, which leaves 3.9 times headroom and a runway the run calls MODERATE. If the pool stays flat, Kwick reaches about Rs 126 Cr in three years, about 6% a year. If its non scheme sales grow at the 40% digital forensics rate, growth runs near 18% a year. Management's Rs 30,000 crore figure is a five year Centre and States announcement, 6.5 times the conservative five year pool, and not an equipment market. The search for the successor scheme and the primary government texts was blocked, so this sizing ran partial.
