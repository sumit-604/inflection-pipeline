Rationale 
The composite scheme of arrangement between Hari & Company Investments Madras Private Limited (HCIMPL), erstwhile 
Vivriti Capital Limited (eVCL), Vivriti Next Limited (VNL), erstwhile Vivriti Asset Management Private Limited (eVAM) and Vivriti 
Funds Private Limited (VFPL) became effective on April 1, 2026. VNL holds a 100% stake in HCIMPL and VFPL at present. As per 
the scheme of arrangement, eVCL’s non -banking financial company (NBFC) operations, along with the corresponding 
assets/liabilities and other und ertakings, have been transferred to HCIMPL (barring its investment in Credavenue Private 
Limited and VAM) while eVAM’s asset management business was transferred to VFPL. HCIMPL  was renamed Vivriti Capital 
Limited (VCL), effective June 13, 2026, and VFPL was renamed Vivriti Asset Management Private Limited (VAM) effective May 
11, 2026. 
 
About 99% of the total assets , as on March 31, 2026 (barring Rs. 50-crore investment in Credavenue Private Limited  and Rs. 
136-crore investment in eVAM), was transferred to HCIMPL  (now VCL) from eVCL , effective April 1, 2026 . For the current 
exercise, ICRA has assessed eVCL’s performance till FY2026 and VCL’s pro forma balance sheet as on April 1, 2026. 
 
The rating considers the company’s adequate and stable profitability (profit after tax/average managed assets; PAT/AMA) of 
2.2% in FY2026 vis-à-vis 2.2-2.3% in FY2025 and FY2024. This was supported by range-bound margins and operating expenses 
while credit costs increased slightly with higher write-offs in the co -lending segment. VCL’s capitalisation profile is adequate 
for the near term  as the pro forma managed gearing was 4.2 times , as on April 1, 2026, after the effect of the scheme of 
arrangement. ICRA notes that the company  is expected to receive Rs. 200 -crore equity capital 1 from VNL (infused by the 
promoter into VNL in Q4 FY2026) in Q1 FY2027. The assets under management  (AUM) stood at Rs. 11,851 crore as of March 
2026 (27% YoY growth) and is expected to expand at a compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of 20 -25% over the next three 
years. 
  
The rating considers VCL’s exposure to borrowers with a moderate risk profile, largely comprising small and mid -sized NBFCs 
and enterprises (other than financial sector entities). ICRA notes that portfolio concentration has declined with the top 20 
exposures reducing to 1 0% of the AUM as of March 2026 from 12% in March 2025 (15% in March 2023). The gross stage 3 
(GS3) increased to 2.2% as of March 2026 from 1.9% as of March 2025 (1.1% as of March 2024) mainly due to the change in 
the reporting of delinquencies in the co-lending/partnership segment, following the revised digital lending guidelines. As per 
the management, the current AUM does not include credit exposures with significant dependence on exports for their 
business. However, the adverse impact of the disruptions caused by the West Asia conflict on borrower cashflow, in general, 
remains monitorable and could pose downside risks to the overall asset quality performance in the near term.

The Stable outlook reflects ICRA’s opinion that VCL would continue to benefit from its established clientele and partnerships 
in the financial sector and its adequate capitalisation, which would help maintain its performance over the near -to-medium 
term. 
 
ICRA has reaffirmed  and withdrawn the long -term rating for the  company’s Rs.  25.00-crore non -convertible debentures 
(NCDs), in accordance with its policy on the withdrawal of credit ratings, as the instruments have matured and have been fully 
repaid. 
Key rating drivers and their description  
Credit strengths 
Adequate near -term capitalisation – VCL is adequately capitalised for its current scale of operations. It is expected to be 
further supported by the capital infusion by VNL in Q1 FY2027. ICRA, however, notes that the company is expected to raise 
further capital, which is crucial for meeting its CAGR target of 20-25% over the next three years. 
Prior to the demerger, the company had reported a managed gearing 2 of 3.9 times as of March 2026 (3.8 times as of March 
2025). Adjusted for exposure to associates and subsidiaries 3, the managed gearing  stood at 4. 4 times as of March 2026 (4.2 
times as of March 2025). VCL’s pro forma managed gearing as on April 1, 2026, after the effect of scheme of arrangement, was 
4.2 times. ICRA notes that the company is expected to extend support its fellow subsidiary (VAM), when required, to the extent 
of 10% of its net worth. The materialisation of the capital infusion in the near term is crucial for keeping the managed gear ing 
below 4.5 times.  
Adequate earnings profile  – Prior to the demerger, profitability (PAT/AMA)  was 2 .2% in FY2026 (2.2 -2.3% in FY2025 and 
FY2024). The revision in the provision guidelines for the loan book covered by default loss guarantees ( DLGs) resulted in the 
release of some provisions in Q4 FY2026 , leading to improved profitability for FY2026 (2.2%) vis-à-vis 9M FY2026  (1.9%). 
However, it will be critical for the company to control credit costs and augment margins in view of the expected increase in 
the cost of funds. 
Credit challenges 
 
Moderate track record of operations – VCL’s loan book was built over the last nine years. It initially gave loans to financial 
sector entities and gradually diversified over the years into lending to non -financial enterprises (predominantly in the mid -
market segment), exposures via co-lending/partnership arrangements, supply chain financing (SCF), factoring and leasing. The 
loan book AUM increased at a 5 -year CAGR of 44% to Rs. 11,851 crore as of March 202 6 (Rs. 8,071 crore as of March 2024) . 
However, growth had moderated to 15% in FY2025 and 27% in FY2026 due to slowing NBFC credit growth and pressure on 
unsecured loan asset quality. VCL is looking to expand its AUM at a CAGR of 20-25% over the next three years, with the share 
of non-financial sector entities projected to improve steadily over the medium term.  
ICRA notes that exposures with longer tenors (original tenor of more than three years) have increased over the years. However, 
the majority of the exposures have an original tenor of less than three years and are monthly amortising in nature. Maintaining 
the established underwriting standards and risk management practices (including for single party and group exposure limits, 
sectoral exposure limits, etc) would be key as the portfolio expands and the exposures season. 
Moderate risk profile of wholesale exposures; retail book performance is monitorable – The AUM consists of exposures to 
financial service s entities (~3 2% as of March 2026), co -lending/partnership arrangements (~3 2%), non -financial enterprise 
finance (~24%), and SCF, leasing & factoring ( ~10%). Its credit exposures are predominantly to small and mid -sized

NBFCs/corporates with moderate risk profiles. Exposure to small business loan financing entities continued to account for the

About 20% of the AUM, as of March 2026, was towards entities in the non -investment grade rating or unrated category, up 
from about 16% in March 2025. Further, 30% of the exposures, as of March 2026, were in the BBB rating category. ICRA notes 
that the share of non-financial enterprise finance (including SCF) in the overall AUM increased to 3 4% as of March 2026 (28% 
in March 2025) from 27% as of March 2024 (~30% as of March 2023) and is targeted to rise to 50% over the medium term. 
Given its intention of raising its share further in the enterprises segment, the performance of this segment would remain 
monitorable.  
The co -lending/partnership arrangements are mainly for personal/consumer loans, followed by small business loans  and 
vehicle finance. ICRA notes that while the underwriting criteria for this segment are decided by VCL, it depends on its partners 
for the origination and servicing of exposures. The reported GS3 in the co -lending book has been on an increasing trend as it 
stood at 4.0% as of March 2026 and 4.3% as of March 2025 vis-à-vis 1.5% as of March 2024, post the change in the reporting 
requirements. However, the ultimate credit losses are largely offset by the combination of higher yields and the first loss 
default guarantee (FLDG) agreed upon with the partners. ICRA notes that the FLDG coverage is currently limited to 5% of the 
disbursement in some cases, pursuant to the revised digital lending guidelines. As such, managing the partnerships over cycles, 
including adequate diversification (partners/sectors), stepping in to service the loan portfolio  by the company’s designated 
collections team, if necessary, etc, would be critical for scaling up this segment on a sustainable basis over the long term.   
Further, AUM concentration has moderated with the top 20 exposures reducing to 1 0% as of March 2026 from 12% as of 
March 2025 (15% in March 2023). The overall GS3 increased to 2.2% in March 2026 (2.4% as of December 2025) from 1.9% as 
of March 2025 (1.1% as of March 2024), mainly due to the change in accounting for the co -lending segment following the 
revised digital lending guidelines. VCL carried a provision of 1.6% of the  on-book AUM as of March 2026 (1.9% as of March 
2025). Going forward, the ability to keep credit costs under control, considering the wholesale nature of the exposures and 
the increasing share of retail exposures which are largely unsecured, would be monitorable from a rating perspective.  
 
Liquidity position: Adequate 
VCL had free cash and liquid investments of Rs. 3 50 crore and undrawn bank lines of Rs. 50 crore as of March 2026 and 
scheduled average monthly collections of ~Rs. 1,589 crore during April-September 2026 against debt repayment obligations 
of Rs. 3,142 crore during this period. Further, the asset -liability maturity (ALM) profile, as of March 2026, reflected positive 
cumulative mismatches across all buckets. The liquidity coverage ratio ( LCR) was 115% as of March 2026. The company has 
more than  70 lenders with banks comprising ~50% of the overall borrowings followed by NBFCs  and financial institutions  
(~24%). Private wealth managers, mutual funds, corporates and other foreign investors accounted for the balance.

Rating sensitivities 
Positive factors – A sustained improvement in the earnings profile, while scaling up the portfolio and maintaining tight control 
over delinquencies, could have a positive impact on the rating. 
Negative factors – A sustained d eterioration in the asset quality (90+ days past due ( dpd)/AUM beyond 3%), impacting 
earnings, would put pressure on the rating . Continued increase in the managed gearing above 4.5  times or a deterioration in 
the liquidity profile would also impact the rating.

About the company 
Pursuant to the composite scheme of arrangement between HCIMPL, eVCL, VNL, eVAM and VFPL , which became effective 
from April 1, 2026 , eVCL’s NBFC business was transferred to HCIMPL.  HCIMPL was renamed Vivriti Capital Limited  (VCL), 
effective June 13, 2026, and VFPL was renamed Vivriti Asset Management Private Limited (VAM) effective May 11, 2026. 
 
VCL was a registered non -deposit taking systemically important non -banking financial company (NBFC -ND-SI), promoted by 
Mr. Vineet Sukumar and Mr. Gaurav Kumar in June 2017. It provided diverse debt financing solutions including loans, working 
capital finance and trade finance to NBFCs and other enterprises across sectors including manufacturing, infrastructure and 
financial services. It also expanded its presence in the retail segment through various co-lending partnerships with other NBFCs. 
  
eVCL reported a net profit of Rs. 264 crore in FY2026, on a standalone basis, on total managed assets of Rs. 13,162 crore while 
it reported a net profit of Rs. 220 crore on total managed assets of Rs. 10,868 crore in FY2025.
