### Claim 1 — GCC ecosystem revenue: "$100 million" vs "$100 billion"

**Claim (verbatim):** Is India's GCC ecosystem generating nearly $100 million or nearly
$100 billion in revenue (Awfis cited both, three months apart, for the same 2,100+ GCC

**Peer evidence:** DevX's Q3 FY26 call (02-Feb-2026, p.6) states: "India today has 1,850

"nearly $100 billion" the (approximately) correct figure. This resolves the internal
inconsistency B05 flagged, though it does not excuse Awfis for never catching or

### Claim 3 — Managed Aggregation share drift: market-wide or Awfis-specific?

**Claim (verbatim):** Are peers also seeing Managed Aggregation share drift down toward
straight lease and developer partnerships, or is this Awfis-specific?

**Verdict: UNVERIFIABLE**, with an informative negative finding.

**Peer evidence:** None of the three peers operate anything resembling Awfis's
Managed Aggregation (co-invested, shared-capex) supply format. All three run a straight
lease-and-build model in which the operator itself bears fit-out capex end to end:
Smartworks (100% self-funded fit-out capex, "Smartworks spend about 60,000 rupees per
seat... all of this is spent by Smartworks itself," Q1 FY26 call p.13); Indiqube (self-
funded interiors plus a "Cornerstone" renovation-asset-management line and a rising
institutional-landlord share, now ~20% of portfolio, Q1 FY27 call p.13-14); DevX (self-
funded fit-out on standard centers, but its "Development Management" model pushes
construction capex entirely onto the landlord, DevX pays only a design/execution fee of
Rs400-600/sqft plus its own fit-out, Q3 FY26 call p.7-8).

**Peers silent:** All three, on the specific MA:straight-lease ratio question, because the
category does not exist in their reporting.

**Net read:** The question cannot be verified as posed because no peer discloses an MA:SL
ratio. The informative finding is structural: the entire peer set already sits at, or is
moving toward, either pure self-funded straight lease (Smartworks) or landlord-funded
development management (DevX, and increasingly Indiqube's institutional-supply and
Cornerstone lines). Awfis's MA share decline (65%->57% across three quarters) reads less
like an anomaly and more like convergence toward the industry-standard supply structure —
though the peer set gives no evidence either way on whether Awfis's newly announced
developer-partnership deal (Malpani) will scale the way DevX's DM model has.

### Claim 4 — Occupancy and churn: market-wide plateau or Awfis-specific stall?

**Claim (verbatim):** How does blended occupancy and churn compare across peers versus
Awfis's ~75-76% blended / 83-84% mature-cohort plateau and its large single-client exit?

**Verdict: CONTRADICTED** — all three peers report materially higher occupancy than
Awfis's plateau.

**Peer evidence:**
- Smartworks: blended/operational occupancy 83% (Q1 FY26, p.9) -> 81% (Q2 FY26, p.9,
  "denominator effect" from new capacity) -> committed occupancy 92% (Q3 FY26, p.3) ->
  81% operational / mature committed 92% (Q1 FY27, p.6). Mature-centre occupancy runs
  88-93% throughout. Retention swings sharply — 94.5% (Q1 FY26) -> 74% (Q2 FY26, "healthy
  churn" from repricing COVID-vintage leases) -> 93% (Q3 FY26) -> 74% again (Q1 FY27,
  "deliberate portfolio rebalancing... mark-to-market repricing at renewal"). Smartworks
  frames its own 74% retention dips as self-inflicted and strategic, not demand-driven.
- Indiqube: portfolio occupancy 87% (Q2 FY26, p.4) -> 84% (Q3 FY26, p.4) -> 81%
  end-of-month / 84% average (Q4 FY26, p.5, management explicitly corrects an apparent
  4-point dip to a real 1-point dip) -> 86% (Q1 FY27, p.14). Steady-state/mature centres
  85-90% throughout. Client retention >90-95% (Q3 FY26 call, p.11-12); monthly churn cited
  as low as 0.01% in one quarter (Q2 FY26, p.4).
- DevX: occupancy 88% (Q2 FY26, p.4) -> 88.4% (Q3 FY26, p.10) -> mature-centre definition
  changed mid-year from an 85%+ to a 100%-occupancy benchmark (Q4 FY26 call, p.8-9,
  flagged by analyst Urmish Shah as a metric redefinition, not a real occupancy gain) ->
  91.93% (Q1 FY27, p.3, up from 88.6% a year earlier). Net churn negative (more seats added
  by staying clients than lost), 0.6-1.2% depending on the quarter cited; one Noida centre
  was closed outright in Q4 FY26 due to landlord facility-maintenance failure (p.9-10), a
  supply-side churn event rather than a demand-side one.

**Net read:** All three peers, on three different business models (large-campus,
enterprise-diversified, and Tier-2 built-to-suit), run blended occupancy 6-16 points above
Awfis's ~75-76% and comparable-or-higher mature-cohort occupancy. This weighs against a
"market-wide plateau" reading and toward the plateau being Awfis-specific. One qualifier
cuts the other way: Smartworks shows that occupancy/retention dips can be self-chosen
(deliberate repricing churn) rather than involuntary, which is a genuinely different
mechanism from Awfis's disclosed involuntary 3,000-seat client exit — the two are not the
same kind of event even though both show up as an occupancy or retention dip.

### Claim 7 — Landlord rent-reset lag vs client pass-through

**Claim (verbatim):** Do peers face a similar multi-quarter lag between landlord rent
resets and client-side pass-through on older-vintage leases?

**Verdict: UNVERIFIABLE** — no peer names an active lag mechanism matching Awfis's
disclosed 2021-vintage reset drag.

**Peer evidence:** No peer describes a multi-quarter timing gap between when it re-prices
with a landlord and when it can pass that through to clients as an active margin
mechanism. The closest adjacent data point is Indiqube's Q2 FY26 call (p.6): "we have an
annual escalation in our client contracts... 5% to 6%, whereas most of our landlords are
on a three-year escalation, more like 14%, 15% every 36 months" — i.e., landlord
escalations (14-15% per 3 years, ~4.5-5%/year annualized) run roughly in line with, not

across calls) without naming a lag.

**Peers silent:** All three, on a specific multi-quarter reset-to-pass-through lag as an
active P&L item.

**Net read:** No independent confirmation exists for Awfis's specific 2021-vintage
lease-reset mechanism as a sector-wide phenomenon. Indiqube's escalation math is broadly
consistent with landlord and client repricing running at similar annualized paces across
the peer set, which if anything argues against this being a structural industry-wide gap —
though it does not rule out a company-specific timing mismatch tied to Awfis's particular
2021 lease vintage and lock-in structure, which none of these transcripts can speak to.

## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

### 2A. Demand environment

All three peers report strong, broadly consistent demand data for the review window:
record CY2025 office leasing (~82-82.6 million sq ft, cited independently by Smartworks
and Indiqube, consistent with Awfis's own 82/82.6msf figures), accelerating Q1 CY2026
leasing (Smartworks 24.6msf quarterly; Indiqube 20.7msf; both above the CY2025 run-rate),
and GCC-led absorption in the 34-44% range depending on the source quarter. No peer
reports a demand slowdown. The one split signal: DevX's own standalone revenue growth
decelerated sharply from 34-80% YoY (Q2-Q4 FY26) to 7.8% YoY (Q1 FY27, DevX call p.2-3),
which management attributes entirely to the timing of its Capital One asset ramp-up
(company-specific execution/timing) rather than to softening demand — worth noting as a
split signal even though it is not framed by DevX as a demand issue.

### 2B. Pricing and input costs

Indiqube's Q4 FY26 call (p.16-17) discloses a genuine external cost pressure Awfis's three
calls never name: a 5-7% material-cost bump attributed to "the war" (geopolitical/tariff
disruption), pushing its blended capex from Rs1,550 to Rs1,650/sqft. No Awfis call
attributes any part of its capex or margin story to input-cost inflation; Awfis instead
names a lease-reset timing lag (client-side) as its active margin drag, a different
mechanism entirely. This is a genuine gap between what one peer discloses and what Awfis
discloses — feeds 2E below.

### 2C. Capex cycle

All three peers are expanding capex, not holding or cutting: Smartworks stepped its capex
guide up sharply, from ~Rs388cr (FY26 actual, Q1 FY27 call p.14) to Rs550-600cr (FY27
guide, same call, p.8) — a ~45-55% step-up, driven by a fresh capex cycle (Eastbridge,
Eastside, the Singapore Work Studio acquisition). Indiqube holds steady at ~Rs350-414cr
with 25-30% growth guidance maintained across all four calls. DevX is stepping up sharply
too, guiding Rs220-230cr over FY27-28 (Q1 FY27 call, p.4-5) against a much smaller revenue
base, funded via a fresh Rs100cr NCD raise. This is an industry-wide capacity race, not a
lone-expander story — all four companies (including Awfis, at ~Rs200-210cr guided for
FY27) are capex-expanding through this window. Relative to peers, Awfis is arguably the
most capex-disciplined of the four on an absolute-rupee basis, though its revenue base is
also the largest, so capex intensity (capex/incremental revenue) is not directly
comparable from these transcripts alone.

### 2D. Competitive mentions

No transcript names Awfis directly. The one notable near-miss: on DevX's Q2 FY26 call
(12-Nov-2025, p.11), an individual investor asks "who are the match-to-match players for
us?" and Umesh Uttamchandani answers "Nationally, if you ask me, there are few listed
players like SmartWorks, Indiqube, which are in a similar offering of providing managed
office space solutions" — Awfis, the largest listed player in the sector by revenue, is
not named in this direct competitor-identification question. This could reflect DevX's
Tier-2 focus versus Awfis's Tier-1/metro orientation, or could be an incomplete answer; it
is named here as a notable absence rather than as a substantive competitive signal.

### 2E. Risks peers discuss that Awfis does not

- **Input-cost/material inflation** (Indiqube, Q4 FY26 call, p.16-17): a 5-7% material-cost
  bump from tariff/geopolitical disruption, pushing capex per sqft up. Never named on any
  Awfis call as a factor.
- **AI-driven hiring impact on office demand** (DevX, Q4 FY26 call, p.17-18; Indiqube,
  Q4 FY26 call, p.7-8): both peers proactively address analyst questions about AI reducing
  IT-sector headcount and therefore office demand, with detailed (if self-serving)
  rebuttals citing campus-hiring data. Awfis's three calls do not substantively address
  AI-driven headcount risk to its enterprise/GCC client base, despite GCC demand being
  Awfis's largest cited growth trigger.
- **Landlord/CAM (common-area-maintenance) failure risk** (DevX, Q4 FY26 call, p.9-10): a
  center was closed outright because the landlord failed to maintain elevators and AC
  systems. A landlord-side operational risk never surfaced on any Awfis call.
- **Promoter share encumbrance for debt security** (DevX, Q1 FY27 call, p.4-5): DevX
  proactively discloses that 19.65% of company equity was encumbered as security for its
  Rs100cr NCD raise, with explicit promoter-holding-floor covenants named. This is a
  different kind of governance disclosure than Awfis's gaps (GST notices, the D&B related-
  party transfer, both never raised on calls), but it illustrates that at least one peer
  proactively surfaces capital-structure mechanics investors would otherwise have to dig
  for — a disclosure practice Awfis's calls do not match for its own live governance
  items.

**Overall: the peer set complicates the main company's narrative more than it supports or
undercuts it.** Two of Awfis's most positive framings — the GCC demand thesis and flex
penetration trajectory — are directionally corroborated by all three peers (claims 1, 2),
which supports the sectoral tailwind story. But the two items most tied to near-term

land against Awfis relative to peers, not for it. The peer set does not undercut the sector
story; it complicates the company-specific execution story, sharpening rather than
resolving the occupancy-recovery and cash-EBITDA-delivery triggers already flagged in B05
as near-term confirm/kill signals.

## PART 5: CROSS-PEER HYPOTHESIS

**Hypothesis:** the sector's disclosed cash-margin leaders sit at the two ends of the
capital-ownership spectrum — either fully self-funded (Smartworks, 100% operator-borne
fit-out capex, cash margin 19.6% by Q1 FY27) or fully landlord-funded (DevX's development-
management model, landlord bears construction capex entirely, DevX standalone cash EBIT
margin 21.4% FY26) — while the peer running a mixed model (Indiqube: self-funded core plus
