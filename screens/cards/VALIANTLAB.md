## Business Understanding Narrative

**What does it do, and how does it make money?** Valiant makes paracetamol API, the bulk drug inside fever and pain tablets, at one plant in Tarapur, Maharashtra (AR26, p.9). It buys para-aminophenol, acetylates it with acetic anhydride, and sells the powder to formulators, 98% of it in India (AR26, p.117). It earns the spread between paracetamol and its inputs, times volume. It is part of the Aarti group. Since November 2025 a wholly owned subsidiary, Valiant Advanced Sciences (VASPL), makes acetic anhydride at a new plant in Saykha, Gujarat (press release 07-Nov-2025, p.2).

**What is changing?** Two things. First, the paracetamol plant came back from two bad years: utilisation rose from 55% to 85% in FY26 (IndRa). Second, Saykha started selling. In Q1 FY27 the group made Rs 21.10 crore of profit after tax. The parent alone made Rs 9.48 crore (Q1R, pp.6 and 11). The difference, about Rs 11.6 crore, came from Saykha and consolidation.

**Why now?** Q1 FY27 PAT of Rs 21.10 crore equals 73% of FY23's full year, the last good year. Revenue rose 146% year on year to Rs 115.29 crore. EBITDA margin was 25.4% against 3.7% for FY26 (Q1R, p.11; AR26, p.23).

**What must be true for the thesis to work?** Saykha must keep earning at a 35% average utilisation, and the paracetamol margin must hold well above its FY26 level once cheap inventory is used up. Saykha must also find buyers outside the group.

**What breaks it?** The rating agency that holds the numbers says the Q1 spike came from "higher historical inventory levels and improving spot prices" and that these are "unlikely to sustain" (IndRa). Paracetamol prices are back in the long run band of Rs 200 to 300 a kilogram (IndRa). And a restructuring among Valiant, its holding company and associates is under evaluation, on terms nobody has seen (press release 16-May-2026, p.2).

## 2. Business model and archetype

One reported segment: "a single segment company, engaged in manufacturing of chemicals business" (Q1R, p.11). Two engines underneath. The parent sells paracetamol API in IP, BP, USP and EU grades (AR26, p.9). VASPL sells acetic anhydride, to the parent and to group companies (IndRa). FY26 consolidated revenue of Rs 237.47 crore was 97% manufactured goods (AR26, p.23).

Margin comes from spread and cost position. The MD&A says so: selling prices are "largely externally determined" (AR26, p.22). Top five customers were 23.4% of standalone revenue (AR26, p.24). No customer reached 10% (AR26, p.117).

**Archetype: commodity converter.** Section 1B v3.7 Amendment 17 binds both slices. Read spread, utilisation and cycle position, never spot ROCE.

## 3. Competitive advantages

Absent on price. Forming on cost. Both starting materials, para-aminophenol and acetic anhydride, are now made inside the Aarti group (AR26, p.11). Valiant claims about 15% of India's paracetamol capacity and a top five rank (IndRa). None of this gives pricing power. India dropped anti-dumping duty on Chinese paracetamol in 2019, so domestic prices track imports (AR26, p.19). Moat: absent; cost advantage forming through group integration.

## 4. The promoters

Promoter and promoter group held 4,07,01,487 shares at 31 March 2026, 74.94%, with no encumbrance declared (Reg 31(4) declaration 06-Apr-2026, pp.1 and 2). The annual report gives 4,06,41,487 shares, 74.83%, for the same date (AR26, p.63). The 60,000 share gap is unexplained. Inside the block: Dhanvallabh Ventures LLP 2,54,37,500 shares (46.84%), the Vora family about 18.7%, Paresh Shah and his wife about 9.4% (declaration, p.2). Valiant Organics (VOL) holds 73.15% of Dhanvallabh and so controls it (AR26, p.202).

On 19 May 2026 VOL gave notice that it would buy 1,86,07,531 shares (34.26%) from Dhanvallabh off market, as "part of the restructuring exercise being undertaken by VOL." The reference price was a 60 day average of Rs 62.71 a share (VOL Reg 10(5) intimation, pp.1 to 3). Completion is NOT FOUND in the corpus.

The group backs the business with money and supply, and the dependence is deep. The parent bought Rs 107.40 crore of goods from VOL in FY26, 63% of its Rs 171.53 crore material cost, and owed VOL Rs 54.96 crore at year end (AR26, p.119; Q1R, p.6). For FY27 the AGM notice seeks Rs 466 crore of VOL transactions, 196% of FY26 consolidated turnover (AR26, pp.192 and 204). VASPL seeks Rs 117 crore with VOL, Rs 187 crore with Aarti Pharmalabs and Rs 59 crore with Alchemie Speciality Chemicals (AR26, pp.194 and 195).

**Classification: margin led, with an asset event behind it.** The asset event is Saykha. The margin is a price and inventory gain the rater expects to fade.

## 6. The transition and the quality ladder

Management's claim: VASPL is "a second engine of growth alongside our core Paracetamol business," and the year took the subsidiary "from pilot stage into commercial production" (AR26, p.5).

What Saykha makes today is acetic anhydride. The press release files it "under the Ketene and Diketene derivatives category" (press release 07-Nov-2025, p.2). That is a commodity reagent and a paracetamol input. The AR says the plant has "secured approvals for several products," with more in customer qualification, and names no product or revenue line (AR26, p.8).

FROM R1 commodity price-taker. TO R2 cost-advantaged converter, underway through integration. One rung inside the base rate window is plausible. The R3 specialty claim is stated, not evidenced.

## 8. Proof check

The Saykha trigger moved the numbers. Consolidated Q1 FY27 minus standalone gives about Rs 41.9 crore of revenue, Rs 16.8 crore of EBITDA and Rs 11.6 crore of PAT (Q1R, pp.6 and 11, derived; intercompany eliminations not disclosed). The proof is one quarter old and the rater discounts it. IndRa ties Q1 to cheap inventory and spot prices. The paracetamol volume recovery is real over four quarters; its Q1 margin step rests on the same inventory effect. Proof point: present for the integration, absent for the earnings run rate. The Q2 FY27 result separates the two readings.

## 9. Flags from the documents

- **Three NSE queries in a year.** July 2025: the FY25 auditor's report missed the balancing figure note. January 2026: no consolidated segment data; the company promised it "from the subsequent quarter onwards." June 2026: both lapses again for FY26, cured by a revised auditor's report (NSE replies 04-Jul-2025, 15-Jan-2026, 01-Jun-2026). Q1 FY27 still reports one segment (Q1R, p.11).
- **Auditor note quotes the wrong figures.** The "Other Matters" paragraph gives the subsidiary's Q1 income and PAT as Rs 11,528.51 and Rs 2,110.30 lakhs, which are the consolidated totals (Q1R, pp.10 and 11). The June, September and December 2025 reports repeat the pattern. AOC-1 puts VASPL assets at Rs 279.18 crore, not the Rs 454.91 crore stated (AR26, p.39).
- **Restructuring without terms.** A Restructuring Committee has sat since 7 March 2026 (AR26, p.32). Minorities cannot yet see a swap ratio.
- **Rs 100 crore corporate guarantee for VASPL at Rs 10,000 a year,** filed under transactions "not at arm's length basis" (AR26, p.40).
- **Rights issue was mostly a loan swap.** Of Rs 81.47 crore, Rs 58.01 crore converted promoter loans; Rs 1.80 crore was earmarked for capex (AR26, p.29).
- **"Debt-free" is the parent only.** Group borrowings were Rs 44.95 crore at March 2026 (Q4R, p.12).
- **Small items.** Two GST orders (Rs 57.25 lakhs; Rs 18 lakhs plus equal penalty), an MCA penalty, exchange fines for a late intimation (AR26, pp.36 and 38). Non executive commission of 2% of consolidated profit, double the 1% limit, is up for approval (AR26, p.200).
- No pledge. Audit opinions unmodified; the key audit matter is PPE capitalisation (AR26, p.72).

## 10. Independent check: the credit rating

India Ratings affirmed IND BBB/Stable/IND A2 on Rs 56 crore of bank lines on 3 September 2026, on a consolidated view with explicit credit for Aarti group support. It credits FY26 for utilisation and backward integration, and states the Q1 FY27 performance "is unlikely to be sustained in the near term." It expects net leverage under 2.0x and FY27 capex of Rs 25 crore (IndRa). A negative trigger is "a delay in the ramping-up of operations at VASPL."

CRISIL holds the company as issuer not cooperating. On 30 June 2026 it cut the rating to Crisil BB/Stable from BB+, after the company did not answer requests made from May 2026 (`credit-ratings/02-CRISIL.txt`). The active agency is constructive on the balance sheet and sceptical on the quarter.

## 12. Verdict card

**WATCH.**

The integration and the volume recovery are real. The quarter that makes the screen look cheap is half new plant, half cheap inventory, and the rater says it will not hold. A restructuring is coming whose terms decide what a minority share is worth.

**Load-bearing facts a /step1 must verify first**
1. Q2 FY27 consolidated EBITDA margin and VASPL's share of it. Near 20% on normal inventory means the climb is structural. A fall toward 5% means Q1 was the cycle.
2. Saykha's sales outside the Aarti group, and its acetic anhydride price against market.
3. The restructuring scheme: structure, swap ratio, and whether VOL's 34.26% purchase closed and at what price.
4. The 60,000 share gap between the two promoter holding filings.

**What would change the view.** PROCEED: two quarters near 20% margin with Saykha above 50% utilisation, or a restructuring term that is fair to minorities. PASS: margin back in single digits, or a scheme that values the listed company at or near the Rs 62.71 reference.

No Role 1 valuation was run and no target price is set. This verdict decides only whether the name earns a full /step1. It is not a position.
