## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

**2A. Demand environment.** No peer describes a demand boom anywhere close to
explaining ASL's FY26 standalone revenue growth of +30.3% YoY or its ~26%+ Q1 FY27
growth (per B05). GALAXYSURF's India volumes were flat-to-low-single-digit for most of
FY26 (3% Q1, flat H1, 8% Q4 after GST-driven destocking reversed); its long-run
guidance band is only 6-8% volume growth. ROSSARI's growth (13-28% YoY across
quarters) is the closer comparable in magnitude, but Rossari is explicit that its growth
is driven by new capacity coming online, export geography expansion (Southeast Asia,
MENA), and new product launches — not underlying end-market growth, which it
repeatedly describes as "soft"/"muted" in India across FY26. AMET/Egypt is in
structural, multi-year volume decline (down ~35% from peak per Galaxy, Feb-2026 call)
due to backward-integrated local competition. Consensus across the peer set is a
subdued-to-mixed demand backdrop through FY26, with growth wherever it appears
attributed to company-specific share/capacity/geography actions rather than a rising
tide — which complicates, rather than corroborates, a market-driven reading of ASL's
own growth.

**2B. Pricing and input costs.** Peer commentary matches ASL's FY26 margin-miss
narrative on direction (elevated fatty-alcohol/oleochemical costs through H2 FY25-H1
FY26, GALAXYSURF) but adds an important complication B05 could not have surfaced from
ASL's own documents alone: **a fresh wave of raw-material and freight inflation hit the
sector in Q4 FY26/Q1 FY27** — the exact window ASL reports its margin recovery —
driven by the West Asia conflict. GALAXYSURF, May-2026 call: "the price of feedstocks
has increased significantly. The sharp and simultaneous escalation across input raw
materials created a highly challenging operating environment" (PDF page 3). ROSSARI,
May-2026 call: "March saw some raw material price increases, some of them were to the
tune of 25% to 30%" (PDF page 5); Jul-2026 call: a single raw material (phenol) cost
Rs 5cr in one quarter from a shipment-timing/price spike, plus rising freight from
renewed Red Sea/Strait of Hormuz disruption (PDF page 14). This directly undercuts a
"raw-material relief" explanation for ASL's Q1 FY27 margin recovery — peers were
seeing the opposite in the same window.

**2C. Capex cycle.** As detailed in Q5: ROSSARI is actively rephasing/cutting capex and
targeting debt-free status within 18 months; GALAXYSURF has paused growth capex since
Aug-2025; only FCL is expanding aggressively, cash/equity-funded, in an unrelated
US oilfield-chemicals business. **This is a lone-expander situation for ASL within the
peer set on debt-funded surfactants capacity**, not an industry-wide capacity race —
the opposite of what B05's framing anticipated.

**2D. Competitive mentions.** No peer transcript across all 12 files mentions Aarti
Surfactants, Aarti Industries' surfactants business, or any unmistakable description of
the company by name, product, or plant location. This silence is unremarkable given
ASL's scale relative to the peer set (Rossari and Galaxy operate at several multiples
of ASL's revenue) but is noted per protocol.

**2E. Risks peers discuss that ASL's own documents do not.** (1) Explicit,
quantified customer-concentration risk management as a stated strategic priority
(Rossari's <2%-per-customer discipline; Galaxy's active Tier 2/3/D2C diversification) —
ASL's AR never discusses concentration risk at all, and the ~92% figure comes only from
CARE, not the company (already flagged in B05, sharpened here by peer contrast).
(2) Reformulation/demand-destruction risk when oleochemical feedstock prices spike
relative to petrochemical substitutes (Galaxy, multi-quarter theme) — a structurally
relevant risk type for any oleochemical-based surfactants maker that ASL's AR does not
raise. (3) Explicit debt-reduction targets and timelines disclosed to investors
(Rossari's 18-month debt-free target) as a governance norm — ASL has no comparable
articulated deleveraging plan despite CARE's own flagged Debt/PBILDT concern (per B05).
(4) Live, named geopolitical raw-material/freight risk (West Asia war, Strait of
Hormuz) disclosed as an active Q4 FY26/Q1 FY27 issue by both Galaxy and Rossari — not
mentioned anywhere in ASL's Q1 FY27 filing per the B05 handoff. (5) Backward-integrated
local competition as a named, multi-year structural risk (Galaxy/Egypt) — a risk
category (channel/competitive disintermediation) absent from ASL's own disclosures.

## PART 4: TRIANGULATION SUMMARY

- Claims verified: 1 of 6 (Q3, US tariff impact)
- Claims partially verified: 1 of 6 (Q2, elevated RM pricing into H1 FY26)
- Claims contradicted: 3 of 6 (Q1, margin recovery as sector tailwind; Q5, industry-wide
  debt-funded capacity race; Q6, customer concentration as standard peer practice) —
  these go to synthesis as priority items
- Claims unverifiable: 1 of 6 (Q4, market TAM/CAGR figure)

**Single most consequential contradiction:** Q1. B05's #1 priority trigger is margin
recovery sustaining above ~7.5-8%. The peer set shows the opposite of the tailwind
that would make that recovery structural: ROSSARI's own margin was flat-to-down in the
identical Apr-Jun 2026 quarter, and both ROSSARI and GALAXYSURF describe a *fresh*
raw-material/freight cost spike (West Asia war) hitting the sector in that exact
window. If ASL's Q1 FY27 recovery is real, the peer evidence says it was earned against
a rising-cost sector backdrop, not with a tailwind — which is either a genuinely
positive company-specific signal (mix/backward-integration/pricing power) or a
one-quarter data point vulnerable to reversal once the same cost pressure peers are
already absorbing catches up with ASL. Either way, B05's own "confirm signal" (two or
more consecutive quarters at/above 7.49%) becomes the load-bearing test, since the
sector context does not do the work of confirming durability on its own.

**Strongest independent confirmation:** Q3. Three independent peers, across a full
year of quarterly calls, corroborate a US-tariff mechanism hurting Indian
specialty/surfactant-adjacent exporters from mid-2025 through early 2026, with Galaxy
quantifying a 3-5% FY25 EBITDA hit and a clean, dated tariff cut (50%→18%) restoring
conditions from Feb-2026. This gives ASL's tariff excuse real sector grounding — though
the magnitude of read-through to ASL specifically cannot be sized because ASL's own
export mix is not disclosed anywhere in the documents reviewed for this run.

**Overall assessment.** The peer set complicates ASL's narrative more than it supports
it. It genuinely corroborates two of the six claims (tariff impact fully; elevated
oleochemical pricing partially) — real, useful confirmation that ASL was not inventing
a macro backdrop. But on the three claims that matter most for the forward
thesis — whether the margin recovery is durable, whether the debt-funded expansion sits
inside an industry-wide capacity build, and whether the undisclosed customer
concentration is ordinary sector practice — independent peer evidence points the
opposite way from what a supportive reading of ASL's own filings would suggest. The
peer set does not accuse ASL of anything; it simply shows two of its closest domestic
comparables making the opposite capital-allocation and disclosure choices in the same
window, which is exactly the kind of cheap, independent evidence this stage exists to
surface.

## PART 5: CROSS-PEER HYPOTHESIS

Across all three peers, a pattern emerges that none of them states explicitly: **the
2025-26 shock sequence (US tariffs, India GST rationalisation, the fatty-alcohol price
spike, and the West Asia war) is pushing Indian specialty/surfactant-adjacent chemical
makers toward a convergent risk posture of customer/product diversification plus capex
and leverage discipline, rather than toward consolidation around fewer large accounts
or debt-funded single-site scale.** Rossari explicitly treats <2%-per-customer exposure
and an 18-month debt-free target as investor-facing badges of resilience; Galaxy
explicitly built out Tier 2/3/D2C accounts as a hedge against Tier 1 reformulation
losses and paused growth capex through the same period; even FCL's one large expansion
was funded to stay debt-free rather than lever up. No single peer frames this as an
industry-wide shift — each describes only its own choices — but the combined pattern
across three independently-run companies in the same 12-month window is a forming
sector norm, not three coincidences. This is testable: watch whether other small/mid
Indian surfactant and specialty-chemical names disclose comparable capex deferrals or
customer-diversification metrics over the next 2-3 quarters. If the pattern holds, a
company running the opposite playbook in this exact vintage — concentrated customer
base, debt-funded single-site expansion, no articulated deleveraging plan — is not
merely more aggressive than peers; it is out of step with a risk-management norm that
appears to be forming in response to a specific, dated shock sequence, which is a
different and more time-bound kind of flag than "aggressive capital allocation" in the
abstract.
