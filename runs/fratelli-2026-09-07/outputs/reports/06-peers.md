The magnitude Sula discloses (table grape prices more than doubling, 350-400bps of margin impact) is large enough that a same-belt neighbour claiming no major impact merits real scepticism absent Fratelli disclosing distinct long-term contract terms that fully insulated it — a specific claim never evidenced in the corpus available to this stage. |

| Field | Content |
|---|---|

Crucially, however, Sula's "capex behind us" statement applies to core winery capex only: in the same breath, Sula is redirecting a growing capital budget into a new vertical, Wine Tourism, including a fresh acquisition (the Chandon estate, Rs20cr, announced Q4FY26 call, closed Q1FY27) and states "over the next 2 years, a lion's share of our total capex will be allocated to Wine Tourism" (SULA-Concall_Feb_2026_Transcript.pdf, p.5) and "our capex in terms of Wine Tourism in FY '27 is going to be something like 3x our capex on the winery front" (SULA-Concall_May_2026_Transcript.pdf, p.15). |

SDBL is the clear counter-example: it is mid-cycle on a large, multi-year greenfield brewery in Uttar Pradesh (~Rs570cr total, ~Rs250-300cr invested as of Q1FY27, not yet at meaningful utilisation and guided to take "another three to four years to achieve... peak capacity" — SDBL-Concall_Aug_2026_Transcript.pdf, p.7), i.e., an active capacity-race expander, not a "behind us" story. |

### Bonus item (task-brief directed, not a numbered B05 question) — EU FTA duty-cut reading

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Claim | Fratelli treats the EU FTA duty phase-down as a manageable risk because of a price gap above Rs2,000 protecting its luxury range |
| Verdict | PARTIALLY VERIFIED / COMPLICATES |
| Peer evidence | Sula, facing the identical policy change, gives a far more granular and hedged account. The negotiated minimum import price is EUR2.50 CIF per 750ml bottle; at the first duty step (75%, expected roughly a year after the FTA's Feb-2026 disclosure), Sula calculates the landed retail price at "about INR1,700 MRP in Maharashtra" (SULA-Concall_Feb_2026_Transcript.pdf, p.6) — a price point BELOW Rs2,000, not above it. | Peers silent | TI (India-UK FTA, scotch import duty 150% to 75%) and SDBL discuss FTA effects only in their own IMFL/spirits categories, not wine; not informative for the wine-specific price-gap question beyond confirming that FTA-driven duty cuts are a live, quantified, multi-peer theme this period, each managing it with explicit multi-year phase-in assumptions. |
| Net read | Sula's own arithmetic is broadly consistent with a "manageable, not immediate" risk in the near term (first-cut landed price ~Rs1,700, still below Fratelli's cited ~Rs2,000 threshold, and cushioned further by rupee weakness), so Fratelli's framing is not contradicted. But Sula's treatment is materially more detailed (specific CIF math, decade-long phase-in horizon, explicit acknowledgment that its own >Rs1,600 wines will "come squarely into contention with these imports") than what the task brief describes as Fratelli's summary framing. The risk compounds over years, not quarters; a "manageable because of the price gap" statement that does not name the 7-10 year phase-in or the currency dependency understates the multi-year trajectory that the market leader itself treats as a multi-year planning problem. |

## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

**2A. Demand environment.** All three peers describe a genuinely soft demand backdrop across FY26, each blaming a rotating mix of state-level regulatory disruption and weather, closely mirroring Fratelli's own excuse pattern (Stage 5, 2B). Sula: Telangana retail-licence transition (Q2FY26), Karnataka destocking (Q3FY26), broad wine-category stagnation confirmed directly by management — "the last 2 years have not seen much growth in the overall wine industry, neither in domestic nor in imports as per IWSR... low single-digit growth" (SULA-Concall_Aug_2026_Transcript.pdf, p.8). SDBL: unusually cold/wet weather (Q3FY26), Karnataka excise disruption, an MP plant licence suspension that erased roughly half its beer volume for two full quarters. TI is the outlier, showing strong, consistent growth throughout — but TI's growth is substantially inorganic (Imperial Blue acquisition) and market-share-gain-driven within a flat-to-modestly-growing category, not evidence of a booming alco-bev demand environment. Net: the peer set is broadly consistent with Fratelli's own account of a difficult FY26 operating environment, but Sula's explicit confirmation that the wine category itself has barely grown in two years complicates any part of Fratelli's growth story that leans on category tailwind rather than share gain.

**2B. Pricing and input costs.** Beyond the grape-specific finding in Claim 3, input cost inflation is a broad theme late in the period across all three peers, undercutting any implicit "stable cost environment" framing. Sula flags coming packaging cost pressure "because of the West Asia conflict" (SULA-Concall_May_2026_Transcript.pdf, p.10). TI reports the identical driver hitting Q1FY27 margins: "ongoing geopolitical tensions led to inflationary pressures across packaging inputs, particularly glass" (TI-Concall_Aug_2026_Transcript.pdf, p.2). SDBL reports barley and glass bottle cost increases of "5% to 6%" and "3% to 4%" respectively in Q3FY26 (SDBL-Concall_Feb_2026_Transcript.pdf, p.7) and "about 7.5% to 8%" average packaging/can/barley inflation in Q1FY27 (SDBL-Concall_Aug_2026_Transcript.pdf, p.16). Three independent alco-bev peers, three different input baskets, the same direction and the same geopolitical driver in the same window is a strong, convergent signal that FY26-27 was not a benign cost environment for the sector.

**2C. Capex cycle.** Mixed, and the split maps cleanly onto sub-segment maturity rather than the sector as a whole, which answers the "lone expander vs. industry-wide capacity race" question directly: this is NEITHER a uniform industry-wide race NOR a uniform pause. Wine (Sula) and branded IMFL/spirits (TI) are both in a post-peak, moderating-organic-capex phase, redirecting growth capital into adjacent verticals (hospitality for Sula; M&A-led scale for TI) rather than pure greenfield expansion. Beer (SDBL) is mid-cycle on a genuine multi-year capacity race (~Rs570cr UP greenfield brewery, 3-4 years to peak utilisation, explicitly benchmarked against a competing UBL brewery investment in the same state — SDBL-Concall_Aug_2026_Transcript.pdf, p.11-12). Fratelli's "capex behind us" claim sits closest to the Sula/TI pattern (mature-segment, post-peak) rather than the SDBL pattern, which is a point in its favour, subject to the hospitality-vertical caveat raised in Claim 5.

**2D. Competitive mentions.** None. Across all 12 transcripts, a full-text keyword search for "Fratelli," "duopoly," "Shotgun," and "RTD" returns zero matches. No peer names Fratelli or refers to it by unmistakable description in any call. This silence is itself informative given Fratelli's own repeated framing of an 85-90% two-player duopoly with the market leader: if that framing were as central to Sula's own competitive picture as it is to Fratelli's, some trace of it (a direct mention, a veiled reference, an analyst question naming Fratelli) would be a reasonable expectation across four quarters and roughly 60 analyst questions. None appears.

**2E. Risks peers discuss that Fratelli's calls do not.** Sula and TI both proactively and repeatedly discuss government-corporation receivables and DSO risk on their own calls (Sula: the Telangana "250-day" government receivable cycle, discussed unprompted by both management and analysts across three calls; TI: "Telangana dues... increased receivables from the government," raised in three of four calls). This is the same category of risk Stage 5 flagged as never once raised on any of Fratelli's five calls despite a 212-day DSO — the peer comparison shows this is a normal, expected disclosure topic in this industry, which makes Fratelli's total silence on its own DSO more conspicuous, not less. Sula also names "unsustainable," possibly financially unsustainable discounting by domestic competitors as a live industry risk, flagging that "I would not be surprised if there were some... casualties among the industry" (SULA-Concall_Feb_2026_Transcript.pdf, p.13) — a structural competitive-intensity risk in the category Fratelli's calls do not appear to name as such. SDBL's calls surface governance-adjacent risk directly (a promoter share-attachment litigation question, a manufacturing licence suspension, an internal auditor's resignation), again illustrating that Indian alco-bev peers do face direct governance questions on calls, even if answered evasively — a contrast with Fratelli's five clean calls despite AR-disclosed items (Section 270 penalty, Rs114.50cr guarantee, warrant forfeiture) that would be analogous prompts.

None of this changes Stage 5's credibility grade, which rests on reconciliation failures against Fratelli's own filed numbers, not on these outward claims; but it removes some of the benefit of the doubt that might otherwise attach to Fratelli's growth-story inputs (grape costs, RTD momentum) specifically.

## PART 5: CROSS-PEER HYPOTHESIS

Across three structurally different alco-bev sub-segments in the identical FY26-FY27 window — wine (Sula), branded IMFL/brandy via acquisition (Tilaknagar), and beer (Som Distilleries) — every visible margin-expansion story in the peer set is either (a) inorganic/M&A-driven (TI's Imperial Blue, delivering scale but guided at only 150-350bps of margin expansion over 24-36 months even with real cost synergies identified and quantified), (b) mix-shift-driven into a structurally different, higher-margin adjacent vertical rather than the core product (Sula redirecting capex 3x toward Wine Tourism rather than squeezing more margin from core wine, which itself just absorbed a 350-400bps grape-cost hit), or (c) explicitly multi-year and not yet delivered (SDBL's UP brewery, 3-4 years to peak utilisation, no near-term margin guidance offered beyond "let's wait"). In no case does any peer in this set claim, or show evidence of, an organic, single-product, single-fiscal-year jump from a loss-making or thin-margin core into a double-digit EBITDA margin driven by volume operating leverage alone. This is a specific, testable pattern that no individual peer states explicitly (each simply describes its own situation) but that emerges only from comparing all three: the observed sector base rate for a credible margin transition is multi-year and either capital-event-assisted (M&A) or mix-assisted (a new higher-margin vertical), never a single-year organic leap. Fratelli's Q4FY26-call guidance — FY27 revenue growth of 30% to ~Rs240cr AND PAT breakeven in the same single year, from a base of two consecutive loss-making years, with no announced M&A and no new higher-margin vertical yet contributing meaningfully (hospitality explicitly deferred) — sits outside every margin-transition pattern observed elsewhere in the Indian alco-bev peer set this period. This does not prove the guidance wrong, but it identifies Fratelli's implied trajectory as an outlier against the sector's own recent behaviour, which is itself a testable proposition for FTTCP: track whether Fratelli's FY27 prints show any of the three peer-observed mechanisms (acquired scale, vertical diversification, or a multi-year phased path) actually emerging, or whether the single-year organic-leap claim continues unaccompanied by any of them.
