## PATTERN PASS

A third read of the same notes, this time hunting for contradictions,
mismatches against the primary statements, deliberately thin disclosure,
restatements, subsequent events, and going-concern language, rather than
reading note-by-note. Pass 1 and Pass 2 between them already did two
source-verified passes and surfaced the material items; this pass adds
genuine new connective findings rather than repeating them.

**P1. The funding chain is one story told across four notes, and no single
note shows the tail risk.** Note 7 (Rs 108.24 cr loan), Note 44 (Rs 447.22
cr guarantee), Note 55 (Rs 145.30 cr unutilised QIP, mostly earmarked for
the same subsidiary's facilities), and consolidated Note 45 (Rs 298.43 cr
capital commitments, Rs 208.18 cr of the gap over standalone sitting at
subsidiary level) are four separate notes about four separate balance
sheet lines. Read together they describe ONE capital structure: the parent
is funding, guaranteeing, and pre-committing capital to a single pre-revenue
subsidiary (Orchid Bio-Pharma Ltd), and the one instrument that carries
open-ended downside (the guarantee) has no visible Ind AS 109 fair-value
or amortising-liability treatment anywhere in the notes (Notes 23/24, 44,
49 all silent on it). No individual note is deficient in isolation; the
composite exposure is not disclosed as a composite anywhere in the
document. This is a genuine pattern-pass finding, not a repeat of any
single Pass 1/2 item.

**P2. A contradiction inside one filing, not between notes and statements.**
The BRSR section of this same annual report states "Nil" for monetary
penalties (Pass 2, B8) while the Corporate Governance Report of the same
filing discloses Rs 88,000 + Rs 10,000 in paid FY2024-25 exchange fines.
Both sections are prepared by the same company for the same reporting
period under the same board sign-off; this is an internal disclosure
control gap, worth naming as a pattern finding in its own right (small in
rupee terms, but a control-quality signal).

**P3. Numbers checked against the face statements, both clear.** Note 52(a)
CWIP ageing (Rs 2,769.14 lakhs) ties exactly to the standalone balance
sheet CWIP figure. Note 51 gratuity OCI remeasurement (Rs 69.95 lakhs) ties
exactly to the P&L OCI line. Note 45 standalone segment geography, once
read from source rather than the corrupt OCR layer, reconciles to the
stated total (Rs 90,914.39 lakhs) with only a Rs 12.78 Cr residual gap
against total revenue from operations (Rs 92,192.59 lakhs, Note 31),
plausibly export incentives not geographically allocated but NOT FOUND IN
DOCUMENT as a stated reconciling item. No other face-statement mismatch
was found on this pass.

**P4. No restatement, no going-concern language.** Notes 57-58 disclose
only a generic "previous year figures regrouped/rearranged where
necessary," unquantified — standard closing boilerplate, not a material
restatement. One genuine reclassification exists and was already
identified in Pass 1/2: the Rs 7.89 Cr FY2024 CURRENT portion of the
Orchid Bio-Pharma loan (Note 15-19) was rolled into the new Rs 108.24 Cr
NON-CURRENT loan balance (Note 7) rather than repaid — a reclassification,
not a restatement, and already captured under Finding 3 below. No
going-concern language, emphasis of matter, or covenant breach was found
anywhere in the notes, the standalone auditor's report, or the
consolidated auditor's report (re-confirmed by direct read on this pass,
consistent with Pass 1 and Pass 2).

**P5. Subsequent events, both already captured, restated here for
completeness.** (i) Joint Memo of Compromise dated 8-Apr-2025 settled the
post-CIRP portion of the lease dispute for Rs 7.62 Cr, fully provided
(Note 44). (ii) NCLT order dated 29-Apr-2025 directed shareholder/creditor
meetings for the Dhanuka Laboratories-into-Orchid amalgamation (Note 56).
Per the run's own corrections file (binding on this stage), that scheme
became effective 10-Jul-2026 — outside this corpus's notes record, carried
here only so the Note 56 disclosure is read as a leading indicator that
later resolved, not as an open question this stage can close.

No further material new findings emerged from this pattern re-read beyond
P1-P5 above.

## D. FIVE QUESTIONS FOR MANAGEMENT

1. What is the pricing and transfer-pricing basis for the Rs 230.72 Cr FY2025 GCLE purchase from Otsuka Chemical (India) Pvt Ltd, a KMP-controlled sole-source supplier, and why is no arm's-length benchmarking disclosed in Note 50 itself?
2. What interest rate, tenure, security, and repayment schedule apply to the Rs 108.24 Cr loan to Orchid Bio-Pharma Ltd, and why does Note 7 describe it as the company's only related-party loan without fixed repayment terms?
3. Has the Rs 447.22 Cr corporate guarantee for Orchid Bio-Pharma's borrowings been recognised and measured under Ind AS 109 (initial fair value, amortising liability), and if not, what is the accounting basis for omitting it, given the guarantee is 34.0% of standalone net worth?
4. What is the basis for removing the Rs 38.72 Cr pre-CIRP contingent claim from FY2025 disclosure, was an independent legal opinion obtained and on what date, and how does that reconcile with the counterparty's continuing dispute of the same matter?
5. What is the stated per-equity-share conversion price or ratio for the Rs 143 Cr promoter OCDs, and why do Notes 22 and 41 show no dilutive EPS effect from an instrument convertible at any time over a ten-year tenor?

## E. NOTES-BASED RED FLAGS

- No Ind AS 109 fair-value or amortising-liability recognition is visible anywhere in the notes for the Rs 447.22 Cr financial guarantee, even though guarantee commission income (Rs 5.28 Cr) is recognised on the other side of the same relationship — an incomplete application of the applicable standard, or at minimum an undisclosed policy choice (Notes 23/24, 44, 49).
- A Rs 38.72 Cr contingent claim was removed from disclosure based on management's own reading of the Resolution Plan, with no independent legal opinion evidenced in the notes, while the counterparty actively disputes the underlying matter (Note 44) — a disclosure judgment call that favours the company's own balance sheet presentation.
- A related-party purchase equal to roughly a quarter of standalone revenue in value terms, from a sole-source, KMP-controlled supplier, carries no arm's-length or benchmarking disclosure anywhere in Note 50 (undisclosed related-party pricing risk indicator).
- ECL coverage on trade receivables loosened from 30.2% to 22.1% of gross in the same year both gross and net receivables grew faster than revenue (Note 13) — a provisioning-adequacy concern that also has the mechanical effect of supporting reported profit relative to a stable coverage ratio.
- An intercompany loan of Rs 108.24 Cr carries charged-and-received economics (interest income, guarantee commission) but no disclosed rate, tenure, security, or repayment terms (Note 7) — a material related-party funding flow with an undisclosed term structure.
- Internal inconsistency within the same filing: the BRSR Penalty/Fine table states "Nil" while the Corporate Governance Report of the same annual report discloses two paid FY2024-25 exchange fines.

## F. ONE-LINE NOTES VERDICT

The notes reveal moderate accounting practices with concentrated related-party and provisioning weaknesses. Key concern: an uncompensated Rs 447.22 Cr subsidiary guarantee with no visible Ind AS 109 treatment, sitting alongside a Rs 230.72 Cr sole-source purchase from a KMP-controlled supplier and a loosening receivables provisioning ratio. Key strength: a clean unqualified standalone audit opinion, conservative deferred-tax policy, and financial-statement arithmetic that ties out cleanly once read from source. Overall accounting quality: 5/10.
