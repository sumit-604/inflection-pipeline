## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

**2A Demand environment.** No peer describes an industry-wide demand boom matching Zaggle's
framing. Capillary shows the strongest organic momentum in the set (organic platform NRR
114-115%, 9-month new order book INR66cr vs INR53cr prior year, Q4 FY26 revenue +26% YoY,
Fortune 50 win — CAPILLARY Q3/Q4 FY26 calls). RateGain shows record Q2 revenue and strong
new contract wins (36-37% quarter-on-quarter) but a materially weaker organic guidance band
(6-8% organic for FY26, later beaten modestly) and an explicit distribution-segment drag from
a churned large OTA client (RATEGAIN Q2/Q3 FY26 calls). Tanla shows a gradual within-year
recovery — Q1 FY26 revenue up only 3.8% YoY, enterprise-ex-OTT still declining through Q2,
finally turning double-digit YoY by Q3 FY26 (TANLA Q1/Q2/Q3 FY26 calls). None of the three
describes anything resembling the environment implied by Zaggle's 40-45% FY26 revenue growth
guidance; even Capillary, the fastest organic grower of the three, tracks well below that pace
on a comparable organic basis. This does not contradict Zaggle's own reported numbers, but the
silence on an industry-wide boom is consistent with Zaggle's growth being substantially
company-specific/inorganic rather than sector-tailwind driven — noted as informative silence
per protocol.

**2B Pricing and input costs.** No peer discloses a cashback/incentive ratio (see Q2). More
generally, RateGain describes an active shift to "performance-linked pricing, where customers
pay based on outcomes delivered" (RATEGAIN Q3 FY26 call, Feb-2026, Bhanu Chopra) and prices its
new VIVA voice-booking product below the 15-20% OTA take-rate it displaces. Tanla reports
persistent pricing pressure in commoditized enterprise SMS ("continuous pricing pressure...
that remains" — TANLA Q3 FY26 call, Jan-2026, Anubhav Batra) alongside volatile Meta/WhatsApp
pricing that has both cut and raised prices within the same year. Capillary's pricing is stable
subscription/per-member with NRR-driven margin expansion. None of this maps directly onto
Zaggle's margin narrative, but it shows a sector-wide move toward outcome/performance-linked
pricing models that loosely resonates with (without confirming) Zaggle's framing of program-fee
economics.

**2C Capex cycle.** Mixed, not uniform. Capillary and RateGain are both actively expanding via
M&A roll-up: Capillary has done five acquisitions (most recently SessionM from Mastercard,
~$35mn, closed May 2026) with a disciplined 4-5 year cash-payback framework and 0.5-1.5x
revenue purchase multiples (CAPILLARY Q3/Q4 FY26 calls); RateGain completed the large Sojern
acquisition (~INR1,090cr utilized) with $12mn of annualized cost synergies already executed
within 100 days (RATEGAIN Q2/Q3 FY26 calls). Tanla, by contrast, is capital-return oriented in
this period — a INR175cr buyback and "nearly INR1,000 crores over the past five years through
dividends and buybacks," a zero-debt balance sheet, and no M&A executed in the three quarters
read (TANLA Q1 FY26 call, Jul-2025, Uday Kumar Reddy). This is not an industry-wide capacity
race; it is two aggressive platform-consolidators (Capillary, RateGain) against one
capital-return name (Tanla). Zaggle's own M&A activity (Rio.Money, DICE, abandoned EffiaSoft)
places it in the consolidator camp by intent, but its execution (valuation cuts, an abandoned
deal) looks less disciplined than either Capillary's or RateGain's stated playbooks.

**2D Competitive mentions.** None. No peer transcript mentions Zaggle by name or by
unmistakable description anywhere across all seven calls.

**2E Risks peers discuss that Zaggle does not.** This is the most consequential cross-read
finding. RateGain openly discloses a churned large OTA client dragging its distribution segment
for multiple quarters, quantifies gross revenue retention/churn ("our GRR or the churn on the
local side has been consistent at 10%" — RATEGAIN Q2 FY26 call, Nov-2025, Bhanu Chopra), and
discusses a separate lost MHS customer by name-context. Tanla discloses a client-concentration
risk crystallizing overnight — "overnight with one regulation, the entire [gaming] business has
gone," naming Dream11 as a former top customer wiped out by a single regulatory change (TANLA
Q2 FY26 call, Oct-2025, Deepak Goyal/Uday Kumar Reddy) — plus a stalled RBI approval on a
pending international acquisition and a contested government tender. Capillary discloses a
churn-indemnity clause triggered on the Kognitiv acquisition because the seller "failed to meet
certain agreed commitments" (CAPILLARY Q4 FY26 call, May-2026, Anant Choubey), and reports
inorganic-side NRR of 94-96% (i.e., quantified customer attrition during platform migration).
Zaggle, per B05, never discloses churn or attrition figures across three tracked quarters, and
explained its own abandoned EffiaSoft acquisition with a materially thinner rationale than any
of these three peers gave for comparable setbacks. The contrast in disclosure candor, not any
single factual contradiction, is the standout finding here and should carry into the
missing-risks section of synthesis.

## PART 5: CROSS-PEER HYPOTHESIS

Across the three peer sets, a pattern emerges that none of the individual peers states
explicitly: platform-migratability appears to determine which of these adjacent SaaS-linked
businesses can safely pursue an aggressive M&A roll-up strategy, and which cannot. Capillary
and RateGain both describe converting acquired, low-margin (20-30% gross margin) revenue onto
a single common technology platform within a known window (Capillary: 4-5 year cash payback,
migrating acquired customers to 65-70% gross margin; RateGain: $12mn of annualized Sojern cost
synergies executed within 100 days of closing) — and both are consolidating aggressively as a
direct result, because the migration economics are predictable and repeatable. Tanla, whose
CPaaS stack is inherently more channel-fragmented (SMS, WhatsApp, RCS, voice, each with
different unit economics and third-party dependencies like Meta), has executed no acquisitions
in the three quarters read and instead returns capital via buybacks — consistent with an
absence of a comparably clean single-platform migration path. Zaggle's own multi-product
structure (Propel, Save, Zagg.money, DICE, the abandoned EffiaSoft) has not been shown, in any
disclosure to date, to migrate onto one common technology stack the way Capillary's acquisitions
do; its M&A outcomes (a cut valuation on DICE, an abandoned EffiaSoft deal, a still-unrecovered
Propel margin) look more like Tanla's fragmented-stack experience than like Capillary's or
RateGain's disciplined roll-up. This is testable: if Zaggle's acquired units (DICE in
particular) fail over the next several quarters to show margin convergence toward its core
business the way Capillary's acquisitions converge toward 65-70% gross margin, that would
support platform-non-migratability, rather than generic "M&A execution risk," as the structural
explanation for Zaggle's inorganic-growth stumbles.
