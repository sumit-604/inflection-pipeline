## The picture in one paragraph

On Wednesday the market broke the floor it had held all autumn. Only 720 stocks still trade above their 200 day line, against 838 at the 8 October low. The count has not been this low since mid April. 384 stocks fell four and a half percent or more in one session, and only 18 rose that much. That makes it the heaviest down day since 12 May. Volume was heavy too, so this looks like forced selling, not a quiet markdown. 237 stocks made new yearly lows, the most since late March. The Nifty fell only about a third of a percent, because IT and a few large names held it up. Under the index, about two stocks in three are in long downtrends. The market now shows lower peaks above a lower floor. That is the shape of a downtrend in breadth, and it holds until a broad day of buying says otherwise.

## Where the market stands

Picture the market as about 2,250 stocks. Each stock has three trend lines under its price: the 20 day, the 50 day and the 200 day average. A stock above a line is in an uptrend on that clock. On Wednesday 720 stocks sat above the long line, about one in three. 408 sat above the 50 day line, about one in five. Only 319 sat above the 20 day line, about one in seven. That short count fell by about 250 stocks in a single session. It has not been this low since 30 March.

The year's story shows why the number matters. The market made its low in late March. On 23 March only 291 stocks held the long line, and 956 stocks fell hard that day. Then two days of broad buying changed the mood. On 1 April more than 1,100 stocks rose four and a half percent. On 8 April 880 did. Traders call days like these thrust days, because they push a whole market into a new advance. The long count climbed through spring and summer. It peaked at about 1,370 on 7 August, the highest in this record.

Autumn has been a slide in steps. The count fell through August and into September. On 15 September a shock day hit, and a repair followed that peaked at 1,186. A smaller repair peaked at 980 on 6 October. On 8 October a second shock took the count to 838. A third repair reached 939 on 16 October. Monday pulled it back to 853. Wednesday pulled it to 720. The long count last sat lower on 13 April, two weeks into the spring recovery. Most of the broad market's summer gain is gone.

The shape of the chart has changed. Until Monday the market had lower peaks on top and a flat floor below. A falling top over a flat floor can still resolve either way. Wednesday stepped the floor down by more than a hundred stocks. Lower peaks over a lower low is the plain definition of a downtrend in breadth.

Inside the day, sellers controlled every hour. About half of all stocks traded at least four percent below their prior close at some point. About half of those finished near their low. About one stock in eleven traded four percent above its prior close. Only one in seven of those kept the gain into the close. That is the weakest finish for early risers in this whole record. Anyone who bought a strong opening lost money by the close.

Volume is the most important change. Turnover reached about 558 crore, against about 457 crore a year ago and about 514 crore on Monday. The share of stocks on heavy volume, against those on light volume, more than doubled from Monday. On a quiet down day, sellers simply step back and prices drift. On a heavy down day, holders are pushed out. The earlier brief named heavy volume on a down day as the sign of forced selling. Wednesday delivered it.

Damage has spread through the middle, while the far end stays thin. About one stock in eleven is down ten percent or more over five days, one of the highest shares in this history. Only six stocks are down twenty percent or more over five days, and nine are up that much. New yearly highs fell to 23, the fewest since 7 April. New yearly lows rose to 237, the most since 30 March.

Fear has reached the index. The India VIX measures what traders pay to protect against big swings in the Nifty. It rose about six percent on the day to about 15.7. It is about twelve percent higher than a week ago and about a third higher than three months ago. Gold funds gave no shelter either. They fell about seven percent over the month, while the Nifty fell about four. The usual safe asset fell harder than the stocks.

## Thirty points

### For the trader

**1. The autumn floor broke.** The long count fell to 720, well under the 8 October low of 838. The earlier brief named that level as the line that mattered. The market closed more than a hundred stocks below it. A floor broken by this margin is rarely reclaimed in a day or two.

**2. Wednesday was the heaviest down day since 12 May.** 384 stocks fell four and a half percent or more. Only 18 rose that much, about one for every twenty fallers. The two earlier autumn shocks were smaller. This one sets the scale for the next test.

**3. Heavy volume came with the selling.** Turnover rose above Monday and above the same day a year ago. The share of stocks on heavy volume more than doubled. A markdown on light volume can reverse quickly. Selling in size usually needs time to finish.

**4. Strong openings failed.** Only one in seven stocks that traded four percent above its prior close kept the gain. That is the weakest reading in this record. About half the stocks that fell four percent closed near their low. Buying strength during the day was penalised.

**5. The short trend is at a spring low.** Only 319 stocks hold the 20 day line, about one in seven. The count has not been this low since 30 March. Swing trades in small names run on this clock. That clock now works against them almost everywhere.

**6. No thrust day since 12 June.** The last day with 250 or more stocks up four and a half percent came on 12 June. The spring advance began with two such days, on 1 April and 8 April. Real breadth bottoms usually show one. None has come this autumn.

**7. The index hid the damage.** The Nifty fell only about a third of a percent while 384 stocks fell hard. IT rose almost three percent and held up the large cap indices. A trader who watches only the Nifty saw a quiet day. The stock count saw a shock.

**8. IT carries the only fresh momentum.** A third of the stocks crossing into fresh weekly momentum are IT names. TCS, HCL Tech, L&T Technology Services and Persistent all show it. Only about one IT stock in four holds the four week line. The move lives in the large names, and the sector setup favours size within IT.

**9. State banks against private banks is the clearest pair.** The PSU bank index is up about two percent on the week, the best of any stock index. The private bank index is down about three percent. The spread between the two is the widest clean pair in the data.

**10. Pharma large names stand apart from healthcare breadth.** The pharma index barely moved. Divi's, Gland, Ipca and Dr Reddy's sit on the strength lists. Only about one healthcare stock in thirteen holds the four week line. The large drug makers are a pocket, not the whole sector.

**11. The smallest names gave no shelter.** The microcap index fell about 2.6 percent and is the worst index of the week. The small cap indices fell almost as much. Size protected traders on Wednesday. The setup penalises the smallest names while selling is this broad.

**12. Bounce attempts were few.** Only 26 stocks turned up from a deeply sold daily reading on a day with 384 hard fallers. The largest were Power Grid, Godrej Consumer and NHPC. A bounce needs breadth behind it, and this one had none.

**13. Shorting deep falls is still thin ground, but it is thickening.** Only six stocks are down twenty percent or more in five days. About one stock in eleven is down ten percent or more, one of the highest shares in this history. The middle of the damage is growing faster than the extreme end.

**14. The sugar mills are the cleanest small group.** Uttam Sugar, Mawana, Avadh, DCM Shriram and KM Sugar all show on the leader or momentum lists. Several trade on heavy volume. A group that rises on a shock day deserves attention on the next session. Crowded small groups also tend to fall together.

**15. The jewellers are crowded.** Sky Gold, TBZ, Shanti Gold, DP Abhushan, Shringar and PC Jeweller lead the return lists. Several trade on very heavy volume. Gold funds fell about seven percent in a month. A group that runs against its own raw material tends to stop abruptly when it stops.

**16. Read the middle of each screen, not the average.** The CCAVENUE figure lifts the average return of several lists. The middle stock on the trend template list is down almost four percent on the week. The middle stock on the breakout list is flat. Those plain figures give the honest read.

**17. Froth is absent.** Only 12 stocks rose ten percent or more. Only 29 rose five percent or more. Without a speculative top to unwind, the risk tonight is a lack of buyers, not a blow off.

**18. The VIX crossed 15.** It closed at about 15.7, up about six percent on the day and about twelve percent on the week. The earlier brief named a close above 15 with weak breadth as the sign that worry had reached the index. That sign fired on Wednesday.

## What would change this read

The read is a broken floor on heavy volume, under a staircase of lower peaks. It turns worse if the long count falls under about 600, below its mid April level. New yearly lows above about 400 would say the same. A second day with 300 or more hard fallers inside a week would turn a break into a slide. A VIX close above about 17, with the Nifty finally falling as hard as the small names, would say the large company shelter has failed. If IT turns down while breadth stays this weak, the index loses its last support.

The cautious read fails if buying returns broad and fast. The first test is a thrust day, with about 250 or more stocks up four and a half percent on heavy turnover. The last one came on 12 June. The second test is the long count back above about 840, the old floor, within a few sessions. That would make Wednesday a washout and not a breakdown. The third test is new lows back under about 100 while new highs climb past about 50. A VIX close back under about 14 would support all three. Tonight the state banks and the sugar mills are the only signs of that kind of buying, and two groups do not make the case.
