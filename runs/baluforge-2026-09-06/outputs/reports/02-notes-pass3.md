## PART 1: PATTERN SCAN

### 1.1 Notes that contradict each other (within FY2025 AR)

- Note 25/24 Borrowings ("quarterly returns... generally in agreement with the books... minor difference on
  account of provisional figures") directly contradicts the auditor's own CARO Annexure A table two notes
  away, which shows a Rs 979.72 Lk (Sep-24) and Rs 940.34 Lk (Jun-24) gap on trade receivables reported to
  Union Bank of India - roughly 10% of the quarterly base, not "minor." [Note 25 p.135 vs Annexure A p.107]
- Note 15/14 (Trade Receivables Turnover ratio, Note 48(l)) reports the ratio *improving* 179 -> 138 days,
  while the ageing schedule in the SAME note shows the >6-month bucket rising from 32.8% to 58.8% of gross.
  Both are true; the ratio is revenue-denominator-dominated and structurally cannot see what the ageing
  table shows. A reader using only the headline ratio would reach the opposite conclusion from a reader
  using the ageing table.
- Note 37/36 Income Tax: the rate-reconciliation table's own bottom line (Rs 4,348.56 Lk) does not equal the
  total tax expense actually charged to the P&L (Rs 5,008.72 Lk, standalone) - an internal arithmetic gap
  inside the SAME note, not merely a cross-note or cross-year issue.
- Note 6 PPE (consolidated): the narrative text states NBV "Rs 14,197.85 Lakhs" (the standalone figure) two
  paragraphs below a table that shows consolidated NBV of Rs 14,680.26 Lakhs. Narrative contradicts its own
  table.
- Note 5.3 (standalone) dates the Business Succession Agreement "3 August 2022"; Note 1 (Corporate
  Information) and every other reference date it "3 August 2020." Same document, same agreement, two dates.

## PART 2: DOES THIS FORM ONE MECHANISM OR SEVERAL?

**Question 1: receivables ageing + ECL reversal + bank discounting + CARO stock-statement gaps + the
FY2026 cash-conversion collapse - one mechanism, or several?**

One mechanism, with high confidence. All five facts sit on the same balance sheet line (trade receivables)
and each is consistent with the same underlying story: receivables are being recognised and carried at a
pace the company's own operating cash generation is not matching.

- The ageing schedule (Note 15) shows the >6-month bucket nearly doubling as a share of gross receivables in
  one year (32.8% -> 58.8% standalone).
- In the SAME year, the ECL allowance protecting against exactly this risk was cut, not raised (Rs 576.48 Lk
  net P&L credit standalone), which is the wrong direction if the ageing data is being taken at face value.
- The CARO stock-statement table shows the discrepancy between books and what is reported to the secured
  lender is confined to the RECEIVABLES line specifically (inventory differences are trivial, near-zero,
  every quarter) - the one line already flagged as deteriorating in quality.
- Rs 64.78 Cr of receivables are then discounted with banks and removed from the receivables note entirely,
  with the recourse question never addressed and the facility invisible in the Borrowings note - a channel
  that converts a weakening receivables book into reported cash and reported "no growth in debt" in the same
  move.
- Stage 1's Gate 0 findings (cross-stage context, not re-derived here) show cash conversion (CFO/PAT)
  collapsing from 72.7% to 12.2% in FY2026 while PAT still grew 27%, and a cumulative FY2021-FY2026 CFO/PAT
  of 0.21x against a 0.50x threshold. The FY2026 figures are unverified screener data with no annual report
  behind them yet, but they are exactly the outcome this note-level pattern would predict if it continued:
  profit booked on receivables that do not convert to cash.

Taken together, these are not five independent findings that happen to touch the same account. They read as
one earnings-quality mechanism, observed from five different vantage points: an ageing schedule, a
provisioning line, a bank-facing reconciliation, an off-balance-sheet financing channel, and (one year later,
unverified) the cash-flow statement. Each vantage point corroborates the others. This is the single most
important finding of the triple pass.

**Question 2: do the tax items form a single transparency pattern?**

Yes. Three separate tax-note facts point the same direction: (a) ~27% of consolidated PBT is taxed near-zero
in the Dubai subsidiary, a proportion growing 2.6x YoY; (b) the rate-reconciliation table in both the
standalone and consolidated notes bridges only to current tax, silently excluding Rs 660.16 Lk of deferred
tax and prior-year true-up; (c) the FY2024 AR's explicit "Effective income tax rate" line is dropped entirely
from the FY2025 AR with no explanation. None of these is a fabrication (the total tax number the P&L bears is
correct and traceable); each independently narrows what a reader can see about the group's TRUE and
SUSTAINABLE tax rate, and all three move in the same direction (less visibility) in the same year the
low-tax offshore share grew fastest. This looks like a single transparency pattern rather than three
coincidental gaps: the underlying number the company would least want emphasised (a rising true effective
rate once the current-tax-only bridge and the offshore mix are both stripped away) is precisely the number
that got harder to see this year.

**Question 3: do the related-party movements form a single pattern?**

Largely yes, and it is narrower than "all RPTs" - it concentrates on ONE relationship: Safa Otomotiv FZ-LLC.
The Trade Receivable-Dr balance differs 66% between the two ARs for the same comparative date; the Machinery
Advance (Rs 2,695.39 Lk) has sat unchanged for two years; the Loan-Dr balance moved Rs 73.92 Lk in FY25 with
no matching "loan given"/"loan repaid" line disclosed. Three separate balance lines in the same related-party
relationship each show a reconciliation weakness, while the DOMESTIC related-party items in the same note
(Hotel Imperial Palace rent, director remuneration, the promoter revolving loan facility) are internally
consistent year to year, just growing quickly and without an arm's-length benchmark. The pattern is not "RPT
discipline is weak everywhere"; it is "RPT discipline is specifically weak for the fastest-growing, most
profitable, least-taxed, least-asset-backed related entity in the group" - which is the entity where weak
reconciliation matters most.

**Question 4: do the disclosure regressions between FY2024 AR and FY2025 AR form a direction of travel?**

Yes. At least four items got LESS transparent between the two most recent annual reports, none more
transparent: the effective tax rate line (dropped), the customer-confirmation caveat (dropped), the Kelmarsh
subsidiary (disappeared without explanation), and the CARO bank-statement gap (present both years but grew
in absolute size). No item in the notes moved the other way (more disclosure, more granularity, a new
reconciliation added) in this comparison. This is notable because FY2025 is also the year the company raised
Rs 162 Cr from the public and Rs 334.8 Cr from promoter warrants - a year that broadened the public
shareholder base and, ordinarily, would invite MORE disclosure rigor, not less. The direction of travel this
pass identifies is: disclosure depth moved against the direction the company's own capital-raising activity
would suggest it should.

## PART 3: PASS 3 NEW ITEMS FROM THE PATTERN RE-READ

Beyond the four synthesis questions above, the pattern sweep surfaces two further, narrower items not
separately called out in Pass 1 or Pass 2:

- The Rs 0.50 Lk standalone-vs-consolidated deferred tax mismatch (Pass 2, item 10) and the Rs 864.54 Lk

  (FG, deferred tax, Safa Otomotiv receivable) sit under this one unspecific licence clause. It is one
  drafting-discipline weakness expressing itself in three places, not three unrelated errors.

  Business Succession Agreement date conflict (2020 vs 2022, appearing identically in both standalone and
  consolidated policy text) both trace to the same 2020 reverse-merger transaction. Neither is new in
  substance, but together they show that some administrative loose ends from the 2020 succession remain
  un-tidied five years on, which is a modest corroborating data point for the broader "disclosure discipline
  is uneven" pattern above rather than a fresh red flag in its own right.

No further material items emerged from the pattern sweep beyond what is captured in Part 1 and Part 2 above.

**Justification (one paragraph):** The overall score is 3 out of 10. The single most important fact this
triple pass surfaced is that the weakest dimension, provisioning adequacy, is not an isolated note-reading
quibble: it is corroborated from five independent angles (the ageing schedule, the ECL P&L line, the
deferred tax rollforward, the CARO bank-statement gap, and the auditor's own Key Audit Matter selection), and
it is directionally consistent with the unverified FY2026 screener collapse in cash conversion carried in
Stage 1's Gate 0 (72.7% to 12.2% CFO/PAT). Layered on top, the tax-transparency pattern and the
disclosure-regression pattern both move in the SAME direction as the receivables pattern, not in opposing
directions, in the same annual report, in a year the company also raised meaningful public capital. Three
things keep the score from falling further: the underlying total tax number and the FG/PPE tables themselves
are arithmetically correct once cross-footed (the gaps are presentation and reconciliation failures, not
fabrications the auditor missed); the auditor's opinion is unmodified with no going-concern language in
either year; and several dimensions (revenue recognition policy, the equity/borrowings rollforwards, the
gratuity actuarial note) are genuinely clean and fully disclosed. This is a corpus-only, notes-based score;
it does not incorporate any live verification of Safa Otomotiv's actual tax status, the discounting

### E. NOTES-BASED RED FLAGS

- **Earnings management indicator:** ECL allowance reduced in the same year the company's own ageing
  disclosure shows material deterioration in receivables quality - profit inflated by a provisioning
  direction that contradicts the underlying evidence in the same note.
- **Aggressive/opaque accounting:** income tax rate-reconciliation tables (both statements) truncated to
  bridge only to current tax, omitting deferred tax and prior-year adjustments from the visible bridge, with
  the FY2024 AR's "effective tax rate" disclosure line dropped entirely and without explanation.
- **Undisclosed risk indicator:** Rs 64.78 Cr of receivables discounted with banks with no recourse
  disclosure and absent from the Borrowings note; true receivables-based leverage is invisible in the
  company's own reported gearing ratio.
- **Undisclosed risk indicator:** CARO-mandated stock-statement comparison shows a recurring, growing gap
  between books and what is reported to the secured lender, confined to the receivables line, described in
  the Notes as "minor" against the auditor's own larger figures.
- **Related-party reconciliation weakness:** three separate Safa Otomotiv balance lines (Trade Receivable,
  Machinery Advance, Loan-Dr) show reconciliation failures or unexplained movements, concentrated in the
  single largest, least-taxed, fastest-growing related entity in the group.

### F. ONE-LINE NOTES VERDICT

The notes reveal concerning accounting practices. Key concern: an ECL reversal against worsening receivables
ageing, compounded by undisclosed receivables-discounting recourse risk and a tax-reconciliation disclosure
regression, all pointing the same direction in the same year. Key strength: an unmodified audit opinion, no
going-concern language, and internally correct (if imperfectly reconciled) total tax and PPE/inventory
figures once cross-footed. Overall accounting quality: 3/10.
