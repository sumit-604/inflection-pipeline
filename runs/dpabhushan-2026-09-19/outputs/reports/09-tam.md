## SECTION 1: MARKET DEFINITION

### 1A. Precise boundaries

- **Product scope (IN):** gold jewellery (plain and studded), diamond-studded gold jewellery, silver jewellery/articles, and platinum (a disclosed but immaterial line). Together these map to the FY26 AR's own material-type breakup of the Indian retail gems & jewellery market: Gold 80%, Diamond 10%, Silver 5%, Others 5% (AR PDF p.75, printed p.143, chart "Gems and Jewellery Industry Breakup by Material Type (% share) in CY25", source cited on the chart as IMARC Group / CareEdge Research). DPAL's own FY26 mix (~91% gold-value, 6-7% diamond-studded, balance silver/platinum, per B04/step1 brief) sits inside this national mix, skewed slightly more gold-heavy than the national average.
- **Product scope (OUT):** loose/cut-and-polished diamonds and coloured gemstones as a standalone B2B trade (India's diamond-cutting/export trade is not DPAL's business), bullion/investment coins as a standalone SKU (DP Swarn Plus is a savings scheme that converts to jewellery purchase, not a bullion-investment product), and the "Others" 5% national material-type slice (fashion/imitation jewellery, coloured gemstones sold loose) that DPAL does not carry.
- **Geographic scope:** India, domestic retail only (no export). Within India, the AR's own regional breakup (AR PDF p.75, chart "Indian Retail Gems and Jewellery Industry Breakup by Region (% share) in CY25") splits South 40.1%, West & Central 25.5%, North 21.3%, East 11.4%, North-East 1.7%. DPAL's current footprint (Madhya Pradesh, Rajasthan) and every named expansion market (Gujarat — entered; Chhattisgarh, Maharashtra — named next) sits inside the "West & Central" bucket. This bucket, not all-India, is the company's realistic geography and is used as the Section 3A SAM geography filter.
- **Customer scope (IN):** B2C retail consumers buying for weddings, festivals, gifting and self-purchase — AR MD&A names this explicitly as the demand base (AR PDF p.75-76). **Customer scope (OUT / unresolved):** "Revenue from Non-Retail Operations," 12.3% of FY26 revenue (-5.96% YoY), has no composition narrative anywhere in the AR (B04 input gap, carried forward unresolved here). If this line is wholesale/bulk trade to other jewellers rather than end-consumer retail, it sits outside this TAM's customer scope; the corpus cannot resolve which, so it is left inside DPAL's reported revenue base for the SAM-share calculation in Section 3B (the conservative, revenue-inclusive treatment) but flagged.
- **Channel scope (IN, for SAM purposes):** organized, branded, large-format showroom retail (COCO and the new FOCO format), plus the newly launched (May-2026) e-commerce channel. **Channel scope (OUT of SAM, but IN of TAM):** the unorganized/family-jeweller channel, which the AR itself states still holds 62-64% of the national market (Section 2 Method 3). TAM, by the stage's own definition (100% capture), includes this channel; SAM excludes it via the organized-retail filter in Section 3A.
- **Price segment:** mass and mid-premium gold-led jewellery bought for occasion-led and everyday-wear purposes, not the ultra-luxury solitaire/bespoke-diamond boutique segment.

### 1B. Management's own TAM claim

**Management does not state an explicit market-size (TAM) figure anywhere in this corpus.** The FY26 AR's MD&A section cites third-party industry data (the IMARC/CareEdge-sourced Rs 7,204 billion CY25 domestic retail gems & jewellery figure, Section 2 Method 1 below) as macro context, but never frames it as "our addressable market" or attaches a company-specific TAM claim to it. None of the four concall transcripts (Q2 FY26 through Q1 FY27) or the two investor decks contain a TAM/market-size statement either (search performed inside the corpus: no hits on "TAM," "addressable market," "market opportunity of Rs").

The closest analog to a management claim is a **revenue target, not a market-size claim**: Rs 15,000 Cr revenue by FY30 alongside 51 stores. This figure does **not** appear in the AR, the investor decks, or any of the four concall transcripts in this corpus — it is sourced to a single media report, confirmed live via web search: "DP Abhushan eyes 51 stores in Tier 2-3 cities, ₹15,000 cr revenue by FY30," Business Standard, early September 2026 (https://www.business-standard.com/companies/news/dp-abhushan-eyes-51-stores-in-tier-2-3-cities-15-000-cr-revenue-by-fy30-126090600350_1.html). The article also states a modest Rs 50 Cr capex plan funded by internal accruals for the expansion — a figure that reconciles with B07's finding of small per-store capex brackets (Rs 2.5-7 Cr/store) but says nothing about working-capital/inventory funding, the actual binding constraint identified in Section 3C. Credibility read: **broad and unverified-in-filings** — a specific number (Rs 15,000 Cr, not a range) attributed to management but never repeated or reconciled in any primary filed document this corpus holds, sourced only to press coverage of an analyst meet or interview not itself in the corpus. Because it is a revenue target rather than a TAM claim, Section 2's TAM-triangulation ratio test does not apply to it directly; it is instead tested against the constructed SOM in Sections 3B and 5E, which is the economically meaningful comparison and the one B00's LBF3 asks for.

Cross-check (secondary, web, IBEF industry data, accessed via search 19-Sep-2026, specific report page not directly opened — flagged as a lower-confidence secondary source): India's Gems & Jewellery market size stated at Rs 7,31,255 Cr (~USD 85 billion) as of a January-2026 dataset, projected to Rs 11,18,390 Cr (~USD 130 billion) by 2030 (ibef.org, "India's Gems and Jewellery Industry and its Market Size," https://www.ibef.org/industry/gems-jewellery-india). This is within 1.5% of the AR's own CY26P figure (Rs 7,99,200 Cr is somewhat higher; Rs 7,31,255 Cr sits between the AR's CY25 actual and CY26P) — consistent order of magnitude, different vintage/definition, not a material divergence.

### Method 2 — Bottom-up (unit: 1 gram of gold jewellery consumed in India)

Source: World Gold Council / Metals Focus, "Gold Demand Trends" data, cited via Tribune India and Retail Jeweller India (web search, 19-Sep-2026): India's total gold jewellery demand for calendar 2025 was 430.5 tonnes (down 24% YoY from 563.4 tonnes in CY2024, tonnage falling as gold prices rose), with the **value** of that jewellery demand up 12% YoY to **Rs 4,54,390 crore** (vs Rs 4,04,510 Cr in CY2024).

- Addressable unit: 1 gram of gold jewellery.
- Total units, CY2025: 430.5 tonnes = 430,500,000 grams.
- Revenue per unit (implied, back-calculated): Rs 4,54,390 Cr / 430,500,000 g = **~Rs 10,555/gram**, a full-year average across a year in which gold ran from roughly Rs 88,000 to Rs 1,18,000 per 10 g (per concall commentary, Concall_Dec_2025_Transcript p.738-746) — i.e. Rs 8,800-11,800/gram — so the back-calculated average sits inside the observed price range, a sanity check that passes.
- Current market (gold jewellery only, CY2025): **Rs 4,54,390 Cr**.
- Overlay diamond-studded and silver (10% and 5% of the *total* retail market, applied to the AR's Rs 7,20,400 Cr CY25 total, since WGC's figure is gold-specific): Diamond = Rs 72,040 Cr; Silver = Rs 36,020 Cr.
- TAM (Method 2, bottom-up, national, CY2025) = 4,54,390 + 72,040 + 36,020 = **Rs 5,62,450 Cr**.

**Divergence flagged, not averaged silently:** Method 1 (Rs 6,84,380 Cr) sits ~22% above Method 2 (Rs 5,62,450 Cr) for the same CY25 year. Likely cause: WGC/Metals Focus values gold jewellery demand at close to bullion market price times fabricated tonnage (a near-metal-value proxy), while IMARC/CareEdge's retail-industry estimate (Method 1) captures full retail sale value — making charges, GST, and retailer margin on top of metal content, which typically run 10-25% above raw metal value in Indian organized retail. This is a real, explainable methodological gap, not noise. Per the stage's conservative-bias rule, **Method 2's Rs 5,62,450 Cr is carried as the CONSERVATIVE TAM estimate; Method 1's Rs 6,84,380 Cr is carried as the REALISTIC TAM estimate.**

- Implied total organized segment (using 37% of the AR's CY25 Rs 7,20,400 Cr total) = **Rs 2,66,548 Cr**.
- The five named organized players above already account for **~79%** of that implied organized total (Rs 2,10,397 / Rs 2,66,548) — plausible, since Titan/Malabar/Kalyan are the three largest organized national chains; the remaining ~21% (~Rs 56,000 Cr) covers PC Jeweller, Joyalukkas, Reliance Jewels, GRT, Lalithaa, Bhima, TBZ, hundreds of smaller regional organized chains, and DPAL itself.
- Implied unorganized segment (62-64% of Rs 7,20,400 Cr) = **Rs 4,46,648 - 4,60,896 Cr**, stated explicitly as an estimate per the method's own instruction, not a filed fact.
- Reconstructed total (organized Rs 2,66,548 Cr + unorganized Rs 4,46,648-4,60,896 Cr) = **Rs 7,13,196 - 7,27,444 Cr**, which lands almost exactly on Method 1's Rs 7,20,400 Cr AR anchor. This is a partly circular check (the organized total was derived FROM the same 37% share applied to the same AR base), but the independent input — five named companies' own reported FY26 revenue, none of it from the AR or DPAL's own filings — cross-validates that the organized share assumption (37%) is internally consistent with real, disclosed peer revenue, which is the genuinely new information this method adds.

### Method 4 — Import substitution

**Not applicable.** DPAL is a domestic retailer selling finished jewellery fabricated largely from imported bullion; it does not compete against imports of finished jewellery, and there is no import-substitution dynamic of the classic kind (domestic production displacing imports) at the retail level. The adjacent and genuinely material fact — the May-2026 gold/silver import duty hike from 6% to 15% (AR p.76; B04 flag) — is a cost/margin risk, captured in Section 4B, not a TAM-sizing method.

### Method 5 — Global benchmark

Source: FY26 AR PDF p.74, chart "Global Gems and Jewellery Market Size (CY20-CY30P)," attributed to IMARC Group / CareEdge Research: global market CY25 ~**USD 254 billion**, growing at ~5.5% CAGR CY20-CY25 and an implied ~6% CAGR to reach **USD 332 billion by CY29P**.

- India's domestic retail G&J market (Rs 7,20,400 Cr ~ USD 85 billion per the IBEF cross-check, implying an ~Rs 85-86/USD rate) is therefore **~33% of the entire global gems & jewellery market by value** — already a very large share for one country, consistent with India (with China) being one of the two anchor gold-jewellery markets worldwide.
- Per-capita read: India's population is ~1.44 billion (2026 estimate). Rs 7,20,400 Cr / 1.44 billion = **~Rs 5,000 per capita annual jewellery spend**, against a rough global average of USD 254bn / ~8.2 billion people = **USD ~31/person (~Rs 2,650 at the same rate)** — India already spends roughly **1.9x the global per-capita average** on jewellery.
- China comparison (web search, 19-Sep-2026): India's gold jewellery consumption **overtook China's in tonnage terms in CY2024** — 563.4 tonnes (India) vs 479.3 tonnes (China), two countries of comparable population (India ~1.44bn, China ~1.41bn) (Retail Jeweller India / The Federal, reporting WGC data). **This is the opposite of the standard "India has huge per-capita headroom versus China" TAM argument used across Indian consumer-discretionary theses; on gold jewellery specifically, India is already at or above China's per-capita consumption.** The per-capita growth driver named in Section 4A below is therefore weighted LOW for this specific market, not high — a finding the conservative-bias rule requires surfacing rather than defaulting to the generic "India is under-penetrated versus China" template.

## SECTION 3: SAM & SOM

### 3A. SAM — five filters applied to TAM

Starting point: TAM conservative Rs 5,62,450 Cr / realistic Rs 6,84,380 Cr (CY25 baseline, national, product-scope-adjusted).

1. **Product fit** — already applied in Section 2 (Gold + Diamond-studded + Silver = 95% of the national material mix; DPAL carries no material product line outside this).
2. **Geography** — apply the AR's own "West & Central" regional bucket, 25.5% of the national market (AR p.75 chart). This is the single largest cut: it discards the South (40.1%, India's largest jewellery-consuming region, where DPAL has zero presence) and the North and East (32.7% combined).
3. **Channel** — apply the organized-retail share, 37% (CY24/25, IBEF-sourced, Section 2 Method 3). This discards the unorganized/family-jeweller channel, which by definition DPAL cannot capture as a branded showroom retailer.
4. **Customer** — no further cut: DPAL's B2C wedding/festival/gifting/self-purchase customer base is the same customer base the national retail figures already describe; no niche-customer exclusion applies.
5. **Capability** — **not applied as a further SAM-level cut.** DPAL's execution capability (guidance credibility grade C, Emerging Moat score 10/92 = NONE, negative CFO three years running, per B05/B07) is real and material, but it is a probability-of-CAPTURE constraint, not a market-BOUNDARY constraint — the West & Central organized market genuinely exists and is genuinely reachable in principle. Discounting it again here, on top of the below-normal share-gain assumption used in 3B, would double-count the same weakness through two mechanisms (a construction CLAUDE.md's rules forbid for quality improvements and, by the same logic, for quality weaknesses). The capability constraint is instead carried entirely into the SOM share-gain assumption in 3B.

**SAM (current, CY25 baseline) = TAM_conservative x 25.5% x 37% = Rs 5,62,450 Cr x 0.09435 = Rs 53,080 Cr.**
(Realistic-basis SAM, for reference: Rs 6,84,380 Cr x 0.09435 = Rs 64,571 Cr.)

**SAM as % of TAM = 53,080 / 5,62,450 = 9.4%.**

### 3B. SOM at 3 and 5 years, with share-gain rules shown arithmetic

**Current share of SAM:** DPAL FY26 revenue Rs 4,065.13 Cr / SAM (CY25 baseline) Rs 53,081 Cr = **7.66%**, rounded 7.7%.

Share-gain rule selection: the standard bands are 1-2pp/3yr (normal), 3-5pp/3yr (aggressive, needs capacity+execution), >5pp (only on competitor exit/acquisition), with faster gains possible where unorganized share exceeds 40% and is formalizing (it is: 62-64% unorganized here). DPAL-specific facts pulling in opposite directions:
- FOR faster gains: capex-embedded near-term growth signal of ~23% (B07, FY27 only, from Rs 13.75 Cr of documented+guided store capex against a 12-store base), a genuine formalization tailwind (hallmarking expanded to 385 districts, AR p.76), and DPAL's own trailing FY23-FY26 revenue CAGR of ~27.3% (4,065.13/1,971, cubed root - though B04 flags this trailing figure as substantially gold-price-inflated, not a clean volume/share signal).
- AGAINST faster gains: Emerging Moat score 10/92 (NONE, B07), concall credibility grade C with guidance whipsawed three times on the store-count target itself (B05), CFO negative three straight years funded entirely by fresh bank debt, and the Rs 600 Cr QIP — the stated funding vehicle for the FY30 buildout — indefinitely postponed and unmentioned in the most recent (Q1 FY27) call.

**Base case selected: +2.0pp of SAM share over 3 years (top of the "normal" band, not into "aggressive"), reflecting the formalization tailwind offset by the execution/funding weaknesses above.** Extended to +3.5pp cumulative over 5 years (still inside a straight-line extension of the normal band, not the aggressive band).

- **3yr (FY29) SOM share = 7.7% + 2.0pp = 9.7%.**
  SOM_3yr = SAM(CY28P proxy) Rs 82,835 Cr x 9.7% = **Rs 8,035 Cr**.
  Implied revenue CAGR, FY26-FY29: (8,035 / 4,065.13)^(1/3) - 1 = 1.977^(0.333) - 1 = **25.5%**.
- **5yr (FY31) SOM share = 7.7% + 3.5pp = 11.2%.**
  SOM_5yr = SAM(CY30P proxy) Rs 1,15,834 Cr x 11.2% = **Rs 12,973 Cr**.
  Implied revenue CAGR, FY26-FY31: (12,973 / 4,065.13)^(1/5) - 1 = 3.191^(0.2) - 1 = **26.1%**.

Bear/bull brackets around the base case, shown for context (not carried into the YAML handoff, which uses the base case only): bear (+1pp/3yr, credibility-grade-C execution) gives SOM_3yr ~Rs 7,207 Cr (~21% CAGR); bull (+4pp/3yr, aggressive band, would require the store pace to actually deliver rather than continue slipping) gives SOM_3yr ~Rs 9,692 Cr (~33% CAGR).

**Cross-check against management's own guidance:** the base-case SOM's implied 25.5%/26.1% CAGR sits almost exactly inside the "25-30%" revenue growth range management has repeated across calls (B05) — a genuinely reassuring triangulation, not a coincidence engineered by this calculation (the SOM was built bottom-up from market share mechanics, not reverse-engineered from the guidance number). It also sits close to DPAL's own FY26 actual (23% YoY) and above the implied FY27 guide (Rs 4,800 Cr = 18% YoY) and FY28 guide (Rs 5,500 Cr = 14.6% YoY) given on the Q4 FY26 call — those two explicit rupee figures, if taken at face value, are actually BELOW this SOM base case, not above it.

**Direct test of the Rs 15,000 Cr-by-FY30 media-reported target (LBF3):** interpolating the SOM base-case share path to FY30 (CY29P proxy, SAM Rs 99,096 Cr, share ~10.45%) gives a base-case SOM of **Rs 10,355 Cr** at FY30 — a Rs 4,645 Cr shortfall against the Rs 15,000 Cr target. For the company to reach Rs 15,000 Cr on this SAM path, it would need **15.1% SAM share by FY30** — a +7.4pp gain in four years, which breaches even the "aggressive" 3-5pp/3yr band on its own, without any named competitor exit or acquisition to justify the >5pp threshold. Read as a straight revenue CAGR from the FY26 base (4,065.13 -> 15,000 over 4 years): **38.6% CAGR** — above every other guided or constructed figure in this analysis, including management's own "25-30%" ceiling and the Rs 4,800/5,500 Cr FY27/FY28 figures from the same investor call. **Read against the SOM (Rs 15,000 / Rs 10,355 = 1.45x), the claim is not implausible purely on market-size grounds** — the SAM has room for it structurally — **but it is internally inconsistent with every other growth figure management itself has put on the record**, and it was never repeated inside any filed document in this corpus. This is the single most load-bearing finding of this stage for LBF3.

### 3C. Capacity cross-check

Using B07's capacity-embedded-growth figure (23%, FY27 only, from Rs 13.75 Cr of documented+guided store capex against the 12-store FY26 base, explicitly caveated in B07 as a "statistical artefact" of an asset-light model rather than a clean capital-efficiency signal):

- **Physical store capex is NOT the binding constraint.** At the disclosed bracket (Rs 2.5-3 Cr small-format, Rs 5-7 Cr larger-format per store, Concall Jan-2026 p.758-762), even the full 39-store net addition implied by "51 by FY30" (51 - 12) would cost only ~Rs 98-273 Cr in pure fit-out capex — well inside the company's stated Rs 50 Cr internal-accrual plan for the near-term slice (Business Standard, Sep-2026) plus normal operating cash generation, IF operating cash generation existed. It largely does not (see below).
- **Working-capital/inventory funding is the real gap, and it is unquantified in any disclosed plan.** FY26 inventory was ~24.7% of revenue (B04) = ~Rs 1,004 Cr for a 12-store base, or **~Rs 84 Cr average inventory per store**. Even at a conservative ~Rs 40-45 Cr average inventory intensity for smaller-format new stores (below the current blended average, since several new stores are the smaller Rs 2.5-3 Cr bracket), 39 net new stores by FY30 imply an incremental inventory/working-capital need of roughly **Rs 1,560-1,755 Cr** — against a Rs 600 Cr QIP that was the stated funding vehicle and is now indefinitely postponed (B05), and FY26 total borrowings of only Rs 290 Cr. No document in this corpus names an alternative funding source for a gap of this size.
- **capacity_check: gap of approximately Rs 1,150-1,750 Cr in disclosed working-capital/inventory funding for the SOM's implied FY30 store count** (using the lower end of the range against the base-case SOM's more modest implied store count, and the upper end against the full 51-store target). **The SOM (and even more so the media-reported 51-store/Rs 15,000 Cr target) is the optimistic side of this pair; the store-opening capex plan is not the constraint, the inventory-funding plan behind it is, and that plan is not disclosed anywhere in the corpus.**

### 4C. Market structure

- **Competitor count / concentration:** thousands of unorganized/family jewellers plus a growing tier of organized national and regional chains. Top-3 by revenue among named organized players (Titan ~Rs 79,660 Cr, Malabar ~Rs 63,000-78,000 Cr, Kalyan ~Rs 39,063 Cr) sum to roughly **Rs 1,82,000-1,97,000 Cr**, i.e. **~68-74% of the entire implied organized segment** (Rs 2,66,548 Cr, Section 2 Method 3) — a genuinely concentrated top tier, with DPAL (Rs 4,065 Cr) a small regional player by comparison, ~1.5% of the organized segment and ~0.56% of the entire national retail TAM.
- **Consolidating or fragmenting:** consolidating, gradually — organized share up from 22% (FY19) to 36-38% (FY25), projected 43-47% by CY29; the unorganized base is still the majority (62-64%) but shrinking in relative terms.
- **Price vs differentiation competition:** organized retailers compete on trust, hallmark certification, transparent making charges and showroom experience (differentiation); the unorganized sector competes on price/flexibility and lower apparent making charges (AR p.77). DPAL's own management (Q4 FY26 call, B05) frames Titan/Kalyan competition as "healthy" without offering comparative numbers — a low-credibility, unsubstantiated claim per B05.
- **Entries and exits:** no major organized-player exits identified in this corpus; entries are ongoing (Malabar targeting a stock-market debut by FY28 per web search, itself pursuing aggressive national expansion) — the competitive set is intensifying, not thinning.
- **Import share trend:** effective import duty on gold/silver raised from 6% to 15% (May-2026), raising landed cost sector-wide (AR p.76); not an import-competition dynamic in the classic substitution sense (Method 4, N/A) but a real industry-wide cost headwind.

## SECTION 5: SUMMARY & RUNWAY

### 5A. Funnel

TAM (conservative, CY25, national) **Rs 5,62,450 Cr** / TAM (realistic) **Rs 6,84,380 Cr**
-> SAM (West & Central geography x organized channel, CY25 baseline) **Rs 53,080 Cr** (9.4% of conservative TAM)
-> SOM 3yr (FY29, base case) **Rs 8,035 Cr**
-> SOM 5yr (FY31, base case) **Rs 12,973 Cr**
(current FY26 revenue for reference: Rs 4,065.13 Cr, i.e. 7.7% of current SAM)

### 5B. Runway assessment

- **Revenue headroom** = SAM / current revenue = 53,080 / 4,065.13 = **13.1x**.
- **TAM growth rate** = 12.2% CAGR (CY25-CY30P, AR/Method 1).
- **Company CAGR vs TAM:** DPAL's FY23-FY26 revenue CAGR was **~27.3%** (Rs 1,971 Cr -> Rs 4,065.13 Cr, per step1 brief/screener), more than double the 12.2% national TAM growth rate — the company has been gaining share, not merely riding the market (consistent with the SOM's own share-gain construction in 3B), though B04 flags this trailing figure as substantially gold-price-inflated rather than a clean volume/share signal, so treat the MAGNITUDE with caution even while the DIRECTION (gaining share) is likely genuine.
- **Years to saturate SAM at current growth:** with a ~13.1x headroom and a company growth rate (~25-27%) running roughly 8-10 percentage points above the blended SAM growth rate (TAM growth + organized-share-gain tailwind, ~15-19%/yr), saturation is on the order of **three decades out** at current relative growth differentials — i.e., market headroom is not the binding constraint on this thesis over any investable horizon; execution and funding (Section 3C) are.

### 5C. Runway classification

**STRONG.** Rationale: 13.1x SAM headroom and a double-digit (12.2%) TAM growth rate with a genuine, sourced formalization tailwind (organized share 37% -> 43-47%) argue against LIMITED or MODERATE. Not classified MASSIVE because SAM is already a meaningfully filtered subset (geography x channel, not the full national TAM) and DPAL's current 7.7% share of that already-filtered SAM is not a negligible starting point (this is not a business with "everything still to prove" against an unpenetrated market) — the ceiling on classification here is that the runway is a genuine, sizeable opportunity, not an unlimited one.

### 5D. SAM expansion levers actually being pursued

- **E-commerce/omnichannel** (launched May-2026): management's own disclosed target is 3-5% of topline over 3-5 years, ~Rs 25-30 Cr incremental profit/year (Q4 FY26 call, per B05) — modest in absolute terms, but genuinely SAM-expanding in kind (a digital channel can in principle reach beyond the West & Central 25.5% physical-showroom geography into the other 74.5% of the national organized market). No delivered revenue disclosed yet (B07 flag); too early to add to the SAM base with any confidence.
- **Diamond-studded mix (6-7% -> 12-15% by Mar-2028):** improves realizable value per customer within the existing SAM rather than expanding its boundary; currently moving the wrong direction (B05: diamond revenue fell YoY in both FY26 and Q1 FY27).
- **FOCO franchise format** (2-3 franchisees/year guided): a capital-light SOM-capture-speed lever, not a SAM-boundary lever — it affects how fast DPAL can reach stores inside the already-counted West & Central geography, not the size of that geography.
- **Geographic entries within the counted bucket** (Gujarat entered; Chhattisgarh, Maharashtra named next): these do not expand SAM beyond the 25.5% West & Central figure already used, since that AR regional bucket already spans these states; they represent DPAL executing INSIDE its existing SAM, which is exactly what the SOM share-gain assumption (3B) already prices in.
- **Revised headroom:** none of the above materially revises the Rs 53,080 Cr current-year SAM figure; e-commerce is the only lever with genuine SAM-boundary-expansion potential, and it is not yet quantifiable.

### 5E. Final output card

- TAM: Rs 5,62,450 Cr (conservative) / Rs 6,84,380 Cr (realistic), India domestic retail gold-led jewellery (gold + diamond-studded + silver), CY25.
- SAM: Rs 53,080 Cr (West & Central India x organized-retail channel), 9.4% of conservative TAM.
- SOM 3yr: Rs 8,035 Cr (25.5% implied CAGR). SOM 5yr: Rs 12,973 Cr (26.1% implied CAGR).
- Current SAM share: 7.7%. Revenue headroom: 13.1x. Runway class: STRONG.
- Management's Rs 15,000 Cr-by-FY30 target (media-sourced, not in filings): plausible on pure SAM/TAM grounds (1.45x the base-case SOM projected to FY30) but requires a +7.4pp SAM-share gain in 4 years — beyond even the aggressive band — and is not reconcilable with management's own Rs 4,800 Cr (FY27) / Rs 5,500 Cr (FY28) figures from the same investor call (B05).

**Valuation implication line:** At **25.5%** revenue CAGR implied by the SOM base case (3-year), with a margin trajectory of roughly **7.6%** (FY26 actual EBITDA margin, per B04/B05) moving toward management's own **8-8.5%** FY30 "long-term vision" (Q4 FY26 call, unproven against delivery — guidance has whipsawed on every other metric this year), the earnings growth embedded here is approximately **27-28%** CAGR, which **on its face SUPPORTS** the current trailing P/E of **13.8x** (screener, 19-Sep-2026) against the strategy's 25% CAGR target — **but this support is conditional, not clean**: it depends on cash conversion turning structurally positive (LBF2, currently unresolved — FY26 CFO -Rs 97.31 Cr on PAT +Rs 211.84 Cr, B02) and on the Rs 1,150-1,750 Cr working-capital funding gap identified in Section 3C actually closing through a source not yet disclosed (the QIP that was meant to fund it is indefinitely postponed). Absent both, this stage's own capacity cross-check names the funding side of the growth story, not the market side, as the optimistic assumption — a finding for Section 1B (destination-PE) and the Halt 1 gate to weigh directly, not this stage to resolve.
