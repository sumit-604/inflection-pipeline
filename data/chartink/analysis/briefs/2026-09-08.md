# Market read, 8 September 2026

Written from the 34 Chartink table files for the 8 September trade date. All
34 of 34 were present, so no section below is short of data. The full figures

The previous brief covered Monday, 7 September. This one covers one new

buy or sell anything. The operator decides.

## The picture in one paragraph

The market spread out today and the index did not notice. Nineteen of
thirty-five indices rose. The Nifty still fell about half a percent, because
the selling sat in the largest banks and lenders. Underneath, the repair that
began last week carried on. Nine hundred and forty-six stocks now sit above
their twenty-day line, up from a low of 801 six sessions ago. More stocks sit
within fifteen percent of a one-year high than at any point in this data.
Defence became the clear leader today, on the index and in its breadth at the
same time. But six in ten stocks still sit below their twenty-day line, and
the fast tail is the fullest it has been all month. This is a market healing
at its edges while its middle stays empty. The index will pay you little. The
right group will pay you a lot.

## Where the market stands

The three layers have not changed shape. The largest companies sit below
their long-term trend line. The Nifty closed at 23,635, down about six tenths
of a percent, and it sits a little more than two percent under that line. The
mid-sized companies sit slightly above theirs. The small and very small
companies sit well above theirs, close to nine and a half percent up, and the
microcap index rose again today.

The day itself was much broader than Monday. Nineteen indices of thirty-five
rose. Yesterday only eight did. Defence led by a distance, up two and a half
percent. Media, pharma, chemicals and healthcare followed. The falls were
concentrated and they were all financial. Private banks fell one percent,
financial services fell nine tenths, and the bank index and the Nifty each
fell about half a percent. Money left the biggest lenders and went almost
everywhere else. That is what a broad day with a red index looks like.

The base held the test the last brief named. About forty-nine stocks in every
hundred sit above their long-term average, and that reading has stayed
between roughly forty-five and fifty-two since early July. The raw count
above the 200-day line is 1,230 against 1,031 below. The last brief said the
constructive view fails if that count breaks under about 1,200. It did not
break. It also has not recovered. The count peaked near 1,370 on 7 August, so
about 140 stocks have dropped out of the long-term uptrend in a month.

The short-term layers are repairing. Nine hundred and forty-six stocks sit
above their twenty-day line against 1,315 below. That is only four in ten, so
the level is still weak. The direction matters more than the level. The count
bottomed at 801 on 2 September and has climbed in four of the six sessions
since. The floor of this market stopped falling about a week ago.

The five-day tape has come back to even. About forty-six stocks rose for
every fifty-three that fell over five sessions, against thirty-nine to sixty
on Monday. Today 112 stocks jumped four and a half percent or more, and only
36 fell that hard.

There has still been no thrust day. In this history a new leg announces
itself when three hundred or more stocks jump four and a half percent in one
session. The last time was 12 June, with 345. Before that came 15 April with
375, 8 April with 895, and 1 April with 1,141, the pair that ended the March
crash. The last washout was 12 May, when 507 stocks fell that hard in a day.
Almost three months have passed with neither event.

One quieter number improved. Of all the stocks that traded four percent above
their previous close at some point today, forty-four percent closed up. That
is the highest share in a month, and it has risen twelve points in a week. On
the other side, the share of intraday breakdowns that closed down fell to
thirty-four percent. Buyers held the breakouts into the close and bought back
the breakdowns.

Fear is cheap, gold disagrees with stocks, and money is not spreading. The
India VIX closed at 11.23, up about five percent on the week and eighteen
percent below its own thirty-week average. Gold is up about eight percent
over the last month while the Nifty is down about one. That gap was six
points a session ago and it is nine now. Turnover is about 1.2 times a year
ago, yet light-volume stocks outnumber heavy-volume ones roughly two to one.
Nearly half the market held a daily range under three percent. Most of the
market is standing still while a small list runs.

## What the sectors say

Healthcare is still the strongest sector in the market. About seven in ten

Twenty-two healthcare names sit on the near-breakout list, the largest single
group there, and eighteen closed at a one-year high. This is the sector where
index and breadth agree most completely, and that has held for two weeks.

Aerospace and defence took the lead today and it took it properly. The index
rose two and a half percent, the best of the day, and it sits nine and a half
percent above its thirty-week line, further than any other index. The breadth
moved with it. Seven in ten defence stocks now sit above their four-week

Midhani rose almost fourteen percent on twenty-nine times its usual volume.
Data Patterns, Unimech, Centum and Cyient DLM all sit on six or seven screens

two.

Energy is the quiet new turn and nobody would see it from the index. Six in
ten energy stocks sit above their four-week line, the second-highest
short-term reading in the table, against fifty-nine percent above the
forty-week one. Short-term breadth above long-term breadth is what an early
sector turn looks like. The index rose only a third of a percent today and is
down four percent over three months. Antelopus Energy trades at nearly twenty

Banks are strong over the long run and weak right now, and the gap is not

Chemicals had a good day and the breadth is genuine. The index rose about

Plastic products still has the widest short-over-long gap in the table.

Realty repeated its trap for a third session. The index was flat today and is

Metals stayed unresolved. The index was flat today and is

Media is the day's second-best index and among the weakest sectors in the

One number covers the whole table. In 20 of 26 sectors the short-term breadth

Across all sectors, about forty-eight percent of stocks sit above their

The market is still losing its short-term grip nearly everywhere, and losing

## What is leading

three are small companies and only one in five is large. The share of the
market sitting within fifteen percent of a yearly high, at 28.3 percent, is
the highest reading in this entire data set. That is the strong half of the
barbell, and it explains why the index feels so much duller than the market
looks from inside.

Several groups moved as groups rather than as single names. Dye and pigment
chemical makers are the clearest. Bodal Chemicals remains the strongest name
in the file, up about eighty-six percent in a week and more than one and a

Cables and wires are the second group. Cords Cable rose nineteen percent

persistent leaders in the file and is up more than seven times over six

Defence electronics is the third group and it has money behind it. Midhani,
Data Patterns, Unimech, Centum and Cyient DLM all sit on five screens or

Jewellery keeps running with the metal. Tribhovandas Bhimji Zaveri is up

The names on the most screens at once are the strongest facts in the file.

Large companies are not absent. Seventy-four large caps trade above their

The demanding screen loosened a little. The modified relative strength scan

There is froth and it grew again. Twenty-four stocks rose ten percent or more

calls a top. It says money has reached the fast end of the market and is
still sitting there.

## Who is buying

One caveat first, in plain words. These shareholding files carry many rows
where the older quarters read zero. A zero usually means a new listing or a
gap in the record, not a real jump from nothing. Only names with a real
three-quarter history are counted below. For foreign money that is 777 rows
of 1,000. For domestic institutions it is only 210 rows of 918, so the
domestic list remains the thinnest evidence of the three.

Foreign money has two themes and both are unchanged. The first is Indian
lenders. Yes Bank, ICICI Bank and IDFC First Bank all show large
three-quarter increases. The second is the hard-asset and equipment complex.
MTAR Technologies in defence electronics, Adani Green in power, Shriram
Pistons in auto parts, Foseco in industrial chemicals and Coforge in IT
services all appear near the top. The typical increase across the list is
about half a percentage point. The large increases are concentrated, and they
point at banks and at things that get built.

Domestic institutions are buying two different things. One is the recently
listed platform companies. Swiggy, PolicyBazaar, Eternal and Delhivery all
show large three-quarter increases, and Delhivery also had one of the largest
single-quarter rises. The other is banks that have gone nowhere. Ujjivan,
Bandhan and City Union sit high on the list, and Kotak and Bandhan appear
among the largest single-quarter additions. Domestic buying into flat bank
prices was flagged a week ago. It has still not paid.

Forty-four names appear on both the foreign and the domestic list. Data
Patterns, Deepak Nitrite, Neuland, Sona Comstar, ICICI Bank, Kronox,
Schneider, Goodluck and IRFC are among them. Two independent institutions
adding to the same name over three quarters is a stronger fact than either
list alone. Data Patterns is the only one of the forty-four that also has
promoters adding.

Promoters raised their stake in 511 of the 980 names with a real record. The
typical rise is small, about a third of a percentage point. The very large
rises sit in very small companies, several above twenty percentage points. A
jump that size is rarely open-market buying. It is usually a preferential
issue or a reclassification, and this file does not say which happened.

Retail holding rose in every one of the 995 names with a real record, by
about one percentage point at the middle of the list. That is a structural
fact about who owns the Indian market now, not a signal about any one
company. The sharper point is where retail crowded most. Shrenik, IRB,
Dharan, Allcargo, Gensol and Kaynes all show double-digit increases. The
framework does not treat low institutional holding as a risk, and that rule
stands. This is a different point. Retail that arrives after a large move is
the float that sells first when the mood turns.

## Thirty-eight points

### For the trader

**1. There is still no thrust.** Almost three months have passed without a
session where three hundred stocks jumped four and a half percent. Today's
112 is a good day, not a launch day. Until that number arrives, every rise is
a bounce inside a range.

**2. The broadest day in a fortnight came with a red index.** Nineteen of
thirty-five indices rose while the Nifty fell about half a percent. Every
large fall was financial. When the index and the count of rising sectors
disagree this hard, the index is the worse guide.

**3. The twenty-day count has turned up.** It bottomed at 801 on 2 September
and sits at 946 tonight. Half the market would be near 1,130, and crossing
there turns a bounce into a trend. Back under 850 and the repair failed.

**4. The five-day tape came back to even.** About forty-six stocks rose for
every fifty-three that fell over five sessions. On Monday it was thirty-nine
to sixty. The quiet drift of late August has stopped. It has not reversed.

**5. Breakouts are holding into the close.** Forty-four percent of stocks
that traded four percent above their previous close finished the day up. That
is the highest share in a month and it has climbed twelve points in a week.
Sellers are no longer taking back every intraday gain.

**6. Breakdowns are being bought.** The share of intraday breakdowns that
closed down fell to thirty-four percent, down six points in a day. Weak opens
are finding bids. This is the mirror of point five and it points the same
way.

**7. Shorting still does not pay.** One hundred and twelve stocks rose four
and a half percent or more today against 36 that fell that hard. A day where
hard fallers outnumber hard risers would be the first real change since May.

**8. The parabolic tail is the fullest this month.** Thirty-six stocks are up
more than twenty percent over five days, against five that are down that
much. Almost three percent of the market trades more than ten percent above
its ten-day line.

**9. Defence is the cleanest sector setup in the file.** The index rose two
and a half percent and sits further above its long-term line than any other.
Seven in ten defence stocks hold their four-week line, the best short-term
reading in the table. Index and breadth agree here, which is rare tonight.

**10. Energy breadth turned before the energy index did.** Six in ten energy
stocks hold the four-week line while the index is down four percent over
three months. Antelopus, Deep Industries and Asian Energy carry the move.
This setup stays favoured while the short reading holds above the long one.

**11. Banks funded the rest of the market today.** Private banks and
financial services were the two worst indices, and only a third of bank
stocks hold their short-term line. If banks steady, the broad move gets
easier.

**12. The IT counter-signal faded.** Monday showed eight IT names turning up
from oversold, the second-largest group on that list. Tonight IT is not among
the leading groups there, and IT had the worst week of any index.

**13. Auto is still a spread, not a sector.** Parts makers fill the momentum
screens while only four in ten auto stocks hold the short line. Steel Strips
Wheels rose the daily limit on twenty-seven times normal volume. Escorts sits
on the oversold list instead. The gap closes when one side moves to the
other.

**14. Realty is the clearest index-versus-breadth trap.** The index is up
more than seven percent over three months while only three in ten realty
stocks are in an uptrend on any horizon. A few large developers carry the
index. The screens fill with small names that are not in it.

**15. Nearly half the market is coiling.** Forty-six percent of stocks held a
daily range under three percent, and the share sitting inside a four percent
five-day range is the highest in a month. Compression this wide usually ends
in an expansion.

**16. Fresh momentum stays a small-cap event, and the strict screen
loosened.** Two thirds of the daily momentum crossers and seven in ten of the
weekly ones are small caps. The strict relative strength scan went from nine
names to thirteen. Size the trade for the liquidity you actually have.

**17. Volume is arriving and not spreading.** Turnover is about 1.2 times a
year ago, while light-volume stocks outnumber heavy-volume ones roughly two
to one. Concentrated money in a narrow list produces sharp reversals. That is
a position-size condition, not a direction one.

**18. Media rose the most after defence and it means little yet.** The media
index gained one and a third percent today and still fell on the week. One in
five media stocks holds its four-week line. Watch it. Do not trust it.

## What would change this read

The constructive view fails if the count above the twenty-day line rolls back
under about 850, giving up the repair that started on 2 September. It fails
if the count above the 200-day line finally breaks under 1,200 while the
yearly-high list stays full, because that is the barbell snapping at the weak
end. It fails on any session where hard fallers outnumber hard risers, which
has not happened since May. It also fails if healthcare or defence loses its
short-term breadth, since those two are now the only sectors where the index
and the breadth agree.

The cautious view fails if a real thrust day arrives, with three hundred or
more stocks jumping four and a half percent in one session. It fails if more
than half the market, near 1,130 stocks, reclaims the twenty-day line while
heavy-volume names stop being outnumbered two to one. It fails if the Nifty
closes back above its thirty-week line and the banks stop leading the falls,
because the large-cap end is the missing piece. It also fails if energy and
plastic products hold their short-over-long shape for another two weeks,
since two confirmed sector turns from the bottom would mean the base is
rebuilding rather than merely holding.
