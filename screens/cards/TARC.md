## Business Understanding Narrative

TARC develops luxury homes in Delhi and Gurugram. It sits on a land bank of 550+
prime acres, wholly owned and paid for, over 300 acres inside New Delhi municipal
limits (IP FY22 Q3 p.7). It launches a project, sells apartments off-plan
(pre-sales), collects cash over the build, and books revenue when it hands over.
Margin comes from luxury pricing on cheap, legacy land: Tripundra carries ~45%
embedded gross margin and luxury EBITDA of 60-65% (guidance). Three live projects,
Tripundra, Kailasa, Ishva, carry a combined GDV of Rs 9,000 cr (IP FY27 Q1 p.9).

What is changing is a turnaround. FY26 total income jumped to Rs 672 cr from Rs 39
cr as Tripundra handovers began; EBITDA turned positive Rs 78 cr from minus Rs 128
cr; PAT was Rs 19 cr against a Rs 231 cr loss (IP FY26 Q4 p.6, p.17). Pre-sales
hit Rs 1,373 cr and cash flows Rs 1,132 cr (IP FY26 Q4 p.7). The company is
deleveraging: it refinanced high-cost Bain Capital NCDs into bank debt and targets
net-debt-zero by FY27-28 (Concall FY26 Q3 p.5; guidance).

Why now: revenue recognition has started, the balance sheet is being repaired, and
the project pipeline gives multi-year cash visibility.

What must be true: Kailasa and Ishva must sell and complete on time, the Rs 1,950
cr debt must come down as guided, and reported "income" must convert to real cash
profit, not intra-group interest.

What breaks it: a luxury-demand slowdown, execution or approval delay, or the debt
staying high while interest eats the margin.

## 2. Business model and archetype
Luxury residential developer on a fully paid legacy land bank (IP FY27 Q1 p.18).
Revenue is lumpy and recognition-driven; cash comes in through pre-sales and
collections across the build. Archetype: order-book / project business, valued on
NAV and embedded value, not PE. The economic engine is luxury price realisation on
low-cost land, geared with debt. This is not a compounding operating business; it
is a project pipeline plus a balance-sheet repair.

## 3. Competitive advantages
Location and land. The 550-acre NCR land bank, largely inside New Delhi, is scarce
and fully paid, which supports margin and capital efficiency (IP FY22 Q3 p.7; FY27
Q1 p.18). Brand and a curated luxury sales model add some pricing discipline (IP
FY27 Q1 p.19). But real estate is cyclical and local; the advantage is an asset
position, not a durable operating moat. Moat: asset-based, cycle-exposed.

## 4. The promoters
Sarin family; Amar Sarin is MD & CEO, building on Anil Sarin's 40-year NCR record
(IP FY22 Q3 p.7). TARC came out of the Anant Raj demerger. Governance points to
check: subsidiary shares (50,000 each across Elevator Properties, Fabulous
Builders, Gadget Builders, Grand Buildtech, Green View Buildwell, High Land
Meadows and more) are pledged to the debenture trustee (AR FY25 p.111), and the
company carries interest-free related-party borrowings of Rs 96 cr (AR FY25
p.131). Promoter holding and any promoter-share pledge were not in the held
corpus. Verify before deeper work.

## 5. Financial trajectory
FY26 consolidated (IP FY26 Q4 p.17): revenue Rs 329.84 cr, other income Rs 341.94
cr, total income Rs 671.78 cr; EBITDA Rs 77.51 cr (11.54%); finance costs Rs 52.61
cr; PBT Rs 14.80 cr; PAT Rs 19.03 cr (helped by a tax credit). FY25 was deeply
negative (EBITDA -Rs 128 cr, PAT -Rs 231 cr). The single most important line: more
than half of "total income" is other income of Rs 342 cr, and management explains
the standalone version of this is interest charged to subsidiaries that knocks off
on consolidation (Concall FY26 Q3 p.5). Read the operating revenue of Rs 330 cr,
not the Rs 672 cr headline. Debt is heavy: borrowings ~Rs 1,950 cr, net financial
liabilities ~Rs 2,130 cr (AR FY25 p.177); net debt Rs 1,688 cr on the deck (IP
FY25 Q3 p.21). The inflection is a revenue-recognition and deleveraging event, not
an operating-quality climb.

## 6. The transition and the quality ladder
FROM R0-R1, accounting-driven and cyclical earnings, TO a cash-generative,
low-debt developer. The transition is balance-sheet repair plus pipeline
monetisation, not a rung climb in pricing power. Management's claim, quoted once:
"Company targets to become Net Debt Zero" by FY27-28 (guidance). This is a
special-situation deleveraging thesis, valued on NAV, and the quality-ladder lens
does not fit it cleanly.

## 7. Growth-trigger register
- FY27 Tripundra balance revenue recognition ~Rs 730 cr. Source guidance. Status: underway.
- Kailasa & Ishva revenue recognition ~Rs 8,000 cr from FY29-30. Source guidance/IP FY27 Q1 p.9. Status: stated.
- ~Rs 10,000 cr cashflows over 5 years; FY27 inflows Rs 1,600-1,800 cr. Source guidance. Status: stated.
- Net-debt-zero by FY27-28; FY27 debt repayment ~Rs 900 cr. Source guidance. Status: underway.
- Debt refinanced ~17-18% to 12.75%; ~Rs 1,000 cr moved to bank debt. Source Concall FY26 Q3 p.5. Status: delivered.

## 8. Proof check
The last year shows the turnaround starting: EBITDA and PAT turned positive, cash
flows doubled, and pre-sales were strong (IP FY26 Q4 p.6-7). But Q4FY26 EBITDA was
only Rs 1.05 cr and Q4 PAT Rs 1.61 cr (IP FY26 Q4 p.17), so quarterly operating
profit is thin and lumpy. Proof of pre-sales and refinancing: yes. Proof of clean,
recurring operating profit and of debt actually falling to zero: not yet.

## 9. Flags from the documents
- Other income Rs 342 cr is larger than operating revenue Rs 330 cr; part is
  intra-group interest (IP FY26 Q4 p.17; Concall FY26 Q3 p.5). Read revenue, not
  total income.
- High debt ~Rs 1,950 cr; net financial liabilities ~Rs 2,130 cr; other equity
  fell on the FY25 loss (AR FY25 p.177).
- Subsidiary share pledges to the debenture trustee; interest-free related-party
  borrowings Rs 96 cr (AR FY25 p.111, p.131).
- Rating only ACUITE BBB- (rating watch), up from BB+ (IP FY25 Q3 p.21; Q1 p.24).
  Sub-investment-grade history.
- NCD redemption is back-ended; execution and demand risk carry into later years.

## 10. Independent check: the credit rating
No full rationale in Bull AI, but the grade is quoted: ACUITE BB+ (stable) moving
to BBB- (rating watch) (IP FY25 Q1 p.24; Q3 p.21). That is a low, improving grade
consistent with a levered turnaround. Get the full ACUITE rationale before any
deeper work; for a debt-heavy developer the rating is central, not optional.

## 11. Posture: Transition Decision Matrix
- Proof gate: partially fired (pre-sales and refinancing), NOT fired on recurring
  operating profit and net-debt-zero.
- Ugliness: structural for now (debt load, lumpy recognition), improving.
- Recognition gap: hard to read on PE; this is an NAV name.
A levered turnaround with real pre-sales but unproven recurring earnings maps to a
special situation, not a transition-alpha compounder. It is off-archetype for the
strategy.

## 12. Verdict card
**PASS for this strategy; a separate special-situations mandate at most.** TARC is
a Delhi-NCR luxury developer running a deleveraging and NAV-monetisation
turnaround. It is debt-heavy, lumpy, and valued on land and pipeline, not on an
operating-quality climb, so it does not fit transition-alpha. Load-bearing facts
to verify first if pursued separately: (1) operating revenue and cash profit
excluding intra-group other income, (2) the actual debt-reduction path against the
FY27 Rs 900 cr repayment target, (3) promoter holding and any promoter pledge,
(4) the full ACUITE rationale. What would change the view: a couple of quarters of
clean recurring operating profit with debt visibly falling would make it a real
NAV re-rating case. No price here.
