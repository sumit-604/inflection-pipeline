### 1B. Quantified guidance (chronological)

**Q4 FY25 call (04-Jun-2025):**
- Order book Rs 1,018 Cr as of 30-May-2025; execution window 12-18 months (longest leg ~18 months).
- FY26 order inflow guidance: Rs 600-750 Cr (same level as FY25).
- FY26 revenue growth guidance: 25%.
- FY26 EBITDA margin guidance: 9%-11%; margin profile expected flat for "next 2-3 years."
- Confirmed order book target: Rs 1,500-1,600 Cr by end of FY26 (on 25% inflow growth).
- Current-capacity revenue ceiling: Rs 1,200-1,400 Cr (capex already done "about a year back").
- Full optimal capacity utilization guided for FY27.
- Working capital target: ~30% of revenue within 2-3 years, from ~50% currently.
- Capex rule of thumb: ~Rs 600 Cr revenue per new SMT line; ~Rs 30 Cr capex per line; ~Rs 2-3 Cr/year for other critical equipment.
- With current balance sheet/capacity, without incremental working capital: Rs 1,200-1,300 Cr revenue achievable.
- Opportunity pipeline: Rs 9,000-10,000 Cr over next 4-5 years.
- Export mix: ~22%-23% of orders, driven by licences/approvals.

**H1 FY26 call (10-Nov-2025):**
- H1 FY26 revenue Rs 214.41 Cr (total income, +43% YoY); EBITDA Rs 23.90 Cr, margin 11.15%; PAT Rs 9.35 Cr (+33% YoY); order inflow H1 Rs 411 Cr; order book Rs 1,062 Cr as of 30-Sep-2025.
- Growth guidance reiterated/clarified under analyst pressure: 30%-35% every year, "no fluctuation," for the next 3-4 years (management called the earlier 25% figure "conservative").
- Rs 150.04 Cr capital raise; ~Rs 61 Cr first tranche received Sep-2025, earmarked for working capital and capacity expansion.
- Capex timeline: expansion takes ~18 months, "operational by post-March 2027" i.e. April 2027.
- Current base capacity: Rs 1,100-1,200 Cr revenue; new capex round expected to "double that capacity."
- Margin: 9%-11% currently; internal target of "at least 3 bps EBITDA margin uptake in about 4 years."
- Capacity utilization: currently 35%-40%; target 50%+ by end of FY26 (55%-60%), 70%-75% by end of FY27.
- 5-year internal target: cut defence/aerospace revenue mix from current level to ~60%, other 4 segments (medical, industrial, commercial aerospace, railways) to ~40%.
- Receivable days: CFO target — "should be able to reduce the receivable days under 4 months" if revenue lumpiness is avoided.
- Order book mix: defence/aerospace 70%-75% of revenue in the period; industrial ~15%; balance medical/others.
- New capex CWIP of Rs 21.06 Cr disclosed by an analyst (Jalaj Manocha), confirmed by management.

**Q4 FY26 call (29-May-2026):**
- FY26 revenue Rs 518 Cr (consolidated); EBITDA Rs 64.77 Cr, margin 12.5%; PAT Rs 30.87 Cr; H2 FY26 revenue ~Rs 302 Cr.
- Order inflow FY26 ~Rs 960 Cr; order book Rs 1,309 Cr as of 31-Mar-2026.
- NADCAP accreditation and Rs 150 Cr preferential raise both confirmed complete.
- Israeli defence-prime JV signed (early stage, "primarily for manufacturing of defence systems in India"); wholly-owned US subsidiary incorporated for 2 shortlisted US manufacturing programs.
- 25,000 sq ft additional manufacturing infrastructure initiated for "Class 3 integration and system integration intensive programs."
- Order book mix: 85%-90% defence.
- Capacity utilization: 35%-40% (stated as the level "after the capacity enhancement"); capex of Rs 30 Cr; capacity "available in about two to three months from now" (~Aug-Sep 2026).
- Peak revenue potential post capacity doubling: Rs 2,000-2,100 Cr, on a "Rs 500-600 Cr revenue per SMT line" rule of thumb.
- FY27 guidance: order inflow +25%-30% vs FY26; revenue growth 30%-35% sustained (management confirmed "Yes" to sustaining this beyond FY27 too); EBITDA margin 11%-12%.
- Export: "almost crossing 50% of our revenue" in FY26, expect ~50/50 domestic/export going forward; within exports, Europe larger than US.
- Israel supply-chain dependency reframed as "10%-15% of our supplies" (a different metric than the Q4 FY25 call's "27%-30% of our programs").
- Medical: targeting ~5% of FY27 revenue; a newly onboarded customer expected to ramp by end of CY2026.
- Promoter holding 30%, no plans to increase.

### 1C. Trigger evolution across the three calls

- **Capacity/peak-revenue ceiling: STRENGTHENING IN RUPEE TERMS BUT INCONSISTENT (flag).** Q4 FY25: current-capacity ceiling Rs 1,200-1,400 Cr. H1 FY26: current-capacity ceiling restated down to Rs 1,100-1,200 Cr, with new capex expected to "double" it (implying ~Rs 2,200-2,400 Cr). Q4 FY26: peak post-expansion capacity restated again as Rs 2,000-2,100 Cr, built bottom-up from "Rs 500-600 Cr per SMT line." None of the three restatements is reconciled against the prior call's number by management, and no analyst asked why the base moved. This is the capacity-ceiling thread named in the task brief; the two "bases" (pre-expansion ~Rs 1,100-1,400 Cr, post-expansion ~Rs 2,000-2,100 Cr) are directionally consistent (roughly a doubling) but the pre-expansion number itself is not stable across calls.
- **Capex timeline: MATERIALLY INCONSISTENT (flag).** H1 FY26 call: capacity online "post-March 2027" (18-month gestation from Nov-2025). Q4 FY26 call, 7 months later: capacity "available in about two to three months" (~Aug-Sep 2026) for a Rs 30 Cr capex tranche. These may be two different capex programs (the larger SMT-line expansion vs. a smaller near-term addition), but management never distinguishes them across calls, so from the transcript record alone this reads as a large, unexplained timeline acceleration.
- **Capacity utilization target: WEAKENING / REDEFINED.** H1 FY26: explicit numeric path (35-40% now -> 50%+ by end of FY26 -> 70-75% by end of FY27). Q4 FY26: utilization stated at 35-40% "after the capacity enhancement" — the FY26-end 50% milestone is not confirmed as met, and the ambition is reframed: "peak utilization is something that we would not intend to reach on the aerospace and defence side" (Q4 FY26 call, response to Achuth). This is a walk-back from a numeric target to a qualitative disclaimer.
- **Export share: NEW AND UNCORROBORATED, LARGE SWING (flag).** Q4 FY25: exports ~22-23% of orders. Q4 FY26 (12 months later, no intermediate data point in the H1 FY26 call): exports "almost crossing 50% of our revenue." No segment note in either results PDF corroborates this (the company reports a single ESDM segment under Ind AS 108 in both FY26 and H1 FY26 filings). No analyst questioned the size of the jump.
- **Faster-execution-of-defence-programs trigger: DROPPED.** Raised by Vikram Sharma at Q4 FY25 call (possible compression of 5-7 year program timelines to 3-4 years, post "this war scenario," via an emergency procurement process). Management called the enquiries "very fresh" and promised an exchange announcement if anything converted. Never mentioned again in either the H1 FY26 or Q4 FY26 call.
- **Commercial aerospace Tier-1 OEM validation: WEAKENED / STALLED.** Q4 FY25: "one of the foreign OEMs has cleared the plant, done the audit... placed the first purchase order for the validation of the manufacturing process," with an update promised "during the first half of this year." H1 FY26: describes aerospace customers generically as still in "first article inspection," no update on the specific Tier-1 OEM named six months earlier. Q4 FY26: no specific aerospace-program update given at all (folded into general system-integration commentary). Reads as a stalled or quietly shelved specific claim.
- **Margin range: STRENGTHENING but re-guided down.** Q4 FY25 and H1 FY26 both guide 9%-11%, "no material change for 2-3 years." Actual FY26 margin came in at 12.5% (H2 ~13%), beating that range. For FY27, management guides down to 11%-12% despite having just beaten the prior band — not explained as conservatism or as a genuine expected deceleration.
- **Israel supply-chain exposure: METRIC REDEFINED.** Q4 FY25: "27% to 30% of our programs have some dependency on the supply chain coming from Israel." Q4 FY26: "we have a dependency on Israel, about 10% to 15% of our supplies." Different denominators (programs vs. supplies) make this impossible to compare directly; it reads as either a real de-risking or a more favourable way of stating the same exposure — undetermined from the transcripts alone.

### 2B. Excuse pattern analysis

Every miss or shortfall traced across the three calls is attributed to an external or structural factor, never to an internal execution shortfall:
- H2 FY25 lower margin: attributed to "mixed change... larger volume of slightly lower margin boards" (Q4 FY25 call) — external/structural (customer mix), not an admission of pricing or cost-control weakness.
- FY25 receivables/inventory spike: attributed to "Middle Eastern conflict... exposure to the Israeli supply chain" (Q4 FY25 call).
- H1 FY26 inventory rise: attributed to "revenue tranche every month has also gone up" — a volume/mechanical explanation, not a working-capital-discipline admission.
- H1 FY26 gross margin dip: attributed again to "product mix," the identical word used to explain both margin declines and (a year later) margin increases.
- Order book shortfall vs. the Rs 1,500-1,600 Cr target: not explained at all — a silence rather than an excuse.
- Receivable-days miss vs. the CFO's own 4-month target: not explained at all on the following call — also a silence.

Classification: **external-blame-heavy**. There is no instance across three calls, roughly 9,000 words of Q&A, where management says a variant of "we underestimated," "we should have," or "that was our miss." Hard topics (cash conversion, the missed order-book target, the missed receivable-days target) are raised only when a specific number forces the topic (e.g., an analyst asking about inventory), and are never raised proactively by management in the following call once a promise has lapsed.

### 2D. What they are NOT saying

- **FY26 operating cash flow was negative Rs 32.30 Cr against a positive PAT of Rs 30.87 Cr** (computed from the audited FY26 cash flow statement: net cash from operating activities (Rs 3,229.65 lakh), vs. net profit Rs 3,086.83 lakh). This is the single most decision-relevant number in the FY26 result set and it is never mentioned, explained, or asked about anywhere in the Q4 FY26 (29-May-2026) call. No analyst raised it either. Likely reason for silence: the story on that call is capacity, order book, exports and margin — all positive — and neither side had an incentive to surface a cash-conversion problem sitting underneath a "record year" narrative.
- **Customer concentration** (a SPEAR load-bearing fact) is never quantified. Asked directly at the H1 FY26 call (Agam Shah: "who will be our largest customer or top 5 customers?"), management answers only categorically ("defence public sector undertakings... second would be the private defence companies in India"), with no percentage, across all three calls.
- **Repeat vs. new revenue split** is never discussed in any of the three calls.
- **Material cost as a stated percentage of revenue** is never given as a hard number; management only says raw material costs are "significantly higher" than peers (Q4 FY25 call, response to Vikram Sharma). Computed from the audited results, cost of material consumed was ~73.0% of revenue in FY25 and ~78.9% of revenue in FY26 — both derived from filed numbers, neither spoken on any call.
- **The reconciliation between the Rs 1,500-1,600 Cr order-book target (Q4 FY25 call) and the Rs 1,309 Cr actual (Q4 FY26 call)** is never addressed.

## SECTION 3: COMPETITIVE INTELLIGENCE FROM CONCALLS

### 3A. Competitor commentary
Management makes no specific named-competitor comparisons in any of the three calls. The closest reference is qualitative differentiation language: "we are what we call a high-mix, high-complexity assembly house... not like a different EMS where we cater to the low mix, high volume manufacturing" (Q4 FY25 call, response to Piyush Arora). Credibility check: unverifiable from the transcripts; no peer margin, market-share, or win/loss data is cited to support the differentiation claim.

### 3B. Industry/market intelligence
- Opportunity pipeline "close to Rs 9,000 crores to Rs 10,000 crores over the next four to five years" (Q4 FY25 call), self-reported, tied to "the programs that we are part of," not an independent market-sizing figure.
- NADCAP: "globally we have hardly maybe a little over 30 companies which are NADCAP accredited and 2-3 companies on the electronic side which are NADCAP accredited in India" (H1 FY26 call, response to Priyankar Sarkar) — a specific, checkable claim about certification scarcity.
- Defence programs generally described as having a "24-36 months" gestation/qualification period, cited as the structural entry barrier (H1 FY26 call, response to Bijal Shah).
- Semiconductor/logistics supply-chain disruption described as short-term ("ease out over the next three to four months," Q4 FY26 call) across two separate questions (Shubham Goyal, Pranay), with a consistent message.
- India positioned as "an increasingly important manufacturing destination" amid "trade realignments" and "national security" focus (Q4 FY26 call opening remarks) — generic macro framing, not company-specific evidence.

### 3C. Toughest analyst questions
- **Vikram Sharma (Q4 FY25 call):** why does VINYAS's margin (9-11%) sit at the same level as lower-complexity EMS peers (also 10-11%) despite claiming higher complexity? Management's answer (raw material costs "significantly higher," value-add ends up at 10-11% regardless of complexity) is a real, structural point but does not fully resolve why the complexity premium does not show up in margin — **a real risk to the value-added narrative**, not fully answered.
- **Agam Shah (H1 FY26 call):** company guided 30-35% growth but delivered 43% in H1 — "are we under-grading?" Answered directly ("Correct," a conservative guide) — satisfactory, and later corroborated by the FY26 outturn landing near the top of the revised band.
- **Ravi Mehta (Q4 FY25 call):** does the "next five years" pipeline framing already price in program delay risk, or is delay risk incremental? Management: delays are "over and above" the pipeline sizing, citing programs "delayed by two, three years" historically — a real, acknowledged risk, satisfactorily flagged rather than dismissed.
- **Sanket Gupta / implicitly the FY26 receivables question that was never asked (Q4 FY26 call):** the most consequential unasked question of the entire three-call record — nobody asked management to reconcile the FY26 operating cash outflow against the reported profit. This is a real risk that remains completely unexamined in the primary source material.

### 3D. Customer and order book signals
- Order book progression: Rs 1,018 Cr (30-May-2025) -> Rs 1,062 Cr (30-Sep-2025) -> Rs 1,309 Cr (31-Mar-2026).
- Order book mix shift: "close to 80%" Make-in-India defence programs (Q4 FY25 call) -> defence/aerospace ~70-75% of revenue mix, industrial ~15% (H1 FY26 call) -> order book (not revenue) 85%-90% defence (Q4 FY26 call). The 70-75%/85-90% figures are not the same metric (revenue mix vs. order-book mix) so are not directly comparable, but both calls confirm defence dominance.
- New relationships: NADCAP accreditation opens "regulated commercial aerospace and export control defence programs" (H1 FY26 call); onboarded "new Tier-1 customers in commercial aerospace and industrial electronics" (H1 FY26 call) — unnamed; Israeli JV and US subsidiary (Q4 FY26 call) — both very early stage, no financial terms disclosed.
- No customer losses, renewals, or pricing renegotiations are discussed in any of the three calls.

### 4B. Questions for peer verification

1. **Question:** Is VINYAS's claimed export share of "almost 50% of FY26 revenue" (Q4 FY26 call) consistent with how comparable defence-EMS peers report export mix, given VINYAS itself reported only "22%-23% of orders" as export twelve months earlier (Q4 FY25 call)? **Why it matters:** unverified, one-year near-doubling, no segment filing corroborates it; a materially different true export mix changes margin and currency-exposure assumptions. **Check peers:** CENTUM, ASTRAMICRO, DATAPATTNS, KAYNES, SYRMA.
2. **Question:** Do comparable high-mix, low-volume defence PCB/subsystem EMS players report EBITDA margins in the 9%-13% range, or is VINYAS's margin profile (raw material cost "significantly higher... value add ends up 10-11%," per management) below what the complexity claim would imply? **Why it matters:** management's own answer (Q4 FY25 call, Vikram Sharma) concedes peers earn similar margins despite VINYAS's claimed complexity premium — a direct test of the differentiation thesis. **Check peers:** CYIENTDLM, AVALON, KAYNES, ASTRAMICRO.
3. **Question:** What receivable-days/DSO levels do peer defence-EMS companies report, and do they attribute long DSO to "milestone/project timelines" the way VINYAS does? **Why it matters:** VINYAS's computed FY26 year-end receivable days (~161) missed the CFO's own "under 4 months" target (H1 FY26 call) and the miss was never revisited; benchmarking tests whether this is sector-wide or company-specific. **Check peers:** CENTUM, DATAPATTNS, ASTRAMICRO, CYIENTDLM.
4. **Question:** Do peers size a similar Rs 9,000-10,000 Cr multi-year defence/aerospace opportunity pipeline for overlapping programs, or is this figure double-counted across bidders? **Why it matters:** it is an industry-growth/market-size claim (Q4 FY25 call, Piyush Arora) used to underwrite the long-dated trigger list, and is self-reported. **Check peers:** CENTUM, ASTRAMICRO, DATAPATTNS, KAYNES.
