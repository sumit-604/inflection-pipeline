## Business Understanding Narrative

**What does it do, and how does it make money?** Credent moves blood samples and supplies people for diagnostic labs. It runs home sample collection, stationed phlebotomy teams, lab technicians and temperature-controlled sample logistics (Pros, p.119). It had 2,589 riders and 97 vehicles at 30-Jun-2026 (Pros, p.119) and 6,338 workers at 31-Mar-2026, 2,286 of them on contract (Pros, p.130). FY26 consolidated revenue was Rs 214.16 crore: logistics 25.26%, operations and supply-chain staffing 24.93%, healthcare services 43.04% (Pros, p.126). It bills labs per service on credit. The money is a spread over wages, conveyance and subcontracted logistics.

**What is changing?** Scale and shape. FY25 standalone revenue was Rs 77.94 crore with a 6.41% EBITDA margin (Pros, p.121). In FY26 the company bought three promoter-owned companies: Credent Healthcare (CHPL) in April 2025, Credent Team in December 2025 and Alltrak Technologies in February 2026 (Pros, p.148). FY26 consolidated revenue became Rs 214.16 crore at a 13.29% margin (Pros, p.121). The IPO raised Rs 93.90 crore in August 2026, Rs 63.80 crore of it for working capital (H1-27, p.26).

**Why now?** H1 FY27 revenue was Rs 138.09 crore against Rs 90.03 crore a year earlier; profit Rs 14.65 crore against Rs 7.53 crore (H1-27, p.11). The stock trades at Rs 380, twice the issue price (MD; Pros, p.2).

**What must be true?** The H1 growth must be organic demand, not pass-through subcontracting. Receivables must turn to cash. Unbilled revenue estimates must bill out. Margins must hold without a related-party customer.

**What breaks it?** H2 FY27 operating cash flow stays negative while receivables keep rising. A top-10 customer leaves; ten customers gave 81.76% of FY26 revenue (Pros, p.235). Or the unbilled revenue estimate reverses, as it did when the restatement cut audited FY25 profit from Rs 5.53 crore to Rs 2.25 crore (Pros, p.189).

## 2. Business model and archetype

FY26 revenue by service (Pros, p.126): logistics Rs 54.11 crore, operations and supply chain Rs 53.38 crore, healthcare services Rs 92.18 crore, marketing Rs 5.90 crore, IT services Rs 6.01 crore, software product Rs 2.05 crore. Healthcare services rose from Rs 9.49 crore in FY25, mostly from consolidating CHPL (Pros, pp.121, 231). Maharashtra 29.40% and Uttar Pradesh 26.83% lead the state split (Pros, p.122).

The standalone company also changed. Its operations-and-supply-chain revenue rose from Rs 10.24 crore to Rs 53.38 crore; its logistics expenses rose from Rs 7.47 crore to Rs 43.44 crore (AR26, p.85). [INFERENCE] Most new standalone revenue rides on bought-in logistics, a thin pass-through layer. Top-10 suppliers rose from 22.70% of purchases in FY24 to 46.97% in FY26 (Pros, p.120).

**Archetype: Outsourcing partner** (manpower and logistics to diagnostic chains). Track client concentration, wallet share, contract stickiness and price per sample. No capacity metric exists (Pros, p.129).

## 3. Competitive advantages

**Scale in a fragmented niche: forming.** 2,530 associated labs at March 2026, from 1,675 in FY24 (Pros, p.120). The company says no listed peer exists (Pros, p.130). **Switching costs: weak.** No firm commitments from top customers (Pros, p.24); 38 customers have stayed three straight years (Pros, p.122). **Technology: claimed.** LogiTrak, a SaaS tool, runs through the Alltrak subsidiary (Pros, p.128). **Pricing power: NOT FOUND.** No price or per-sample data in the corpus. **Cost position: labour-bound.** Employee cost was 39.46% of FY26 income (Pros, p.230).

**Verdict: no moat yet. A labour-and-routing operator with a concentrated client book; network density is the only forming edge.**

## 8. Proof check

The company reports half-yearly, so this is a two-half test on H2 FY26 and H1 FY27.

**The proof point is present on earnings.** H1 FY27 revenue of Rs 138.09 crore beat H2 FY26 by 11%, though H1 is the seasonally weaker half (H1-27, p.11; Pros, p.28). PAT before the tax credit rose to Rs 12.87 crore from Rs 10.84 crore in H2 FY26 and Rs 7.53 crore in H1 FY26 (H1-27, pp.11, 13). Margin held at 13.2% to 13.6% across three halves. H2 FY26 against H2 FY25 is not like-for-like, because CHPL entered in April 2025.

**What the halves do not prove.** Cash. Receivables rose Rs 20.02 crore in six months against Rs 18.75 crore of operating cash before working capital (H1-27, p.12). Organic growth by client is NOT FOUND. Without a transcript or deck, no filed reason for the H1 jump exists.

## 11. Posture: the Transition Decision Matrix

- **Proof gate: FIRED on earnings, not on cash.** Two halves of rising revenue and profit at a stable margin (step 8). Cash flow negative in both periods.
- **Ugliness: ARTIFACT-OF-CLIMB, contested.** The receivable build matches a growth spurt, and the IPO funds it (Pros, p.88). The unbilled-revenue judgment and 82% top-ten concentration are structural strands.
- **Recognition gap: CLOSED.** Trailing PE 27.1 (MD). Forward PE 25.1x on the base reading, 21.4x on the seasonal reading (step 13). Both sit above the R2 neighbourhood of 15x to 17x and the R3 neighbourhood of about 19x.

**Posture: EARNINGS-ONLY.** Returns ride EPS growth alone. If H2 FY27 operating cash flow is negative again, ugliness turns STRUCTURAL and the cell becomes AVOID.

## 12. Verdict card

**WATCH.**

Profit growth of 50% or more at a stable 13% margin is real on the filed halves. The re-rating is already paid for, and every rupee of profit since listing sits in receivables. The family sold its own companies to the listed entity. It lent the proceeds back and converted them at Rs 30.90 a share. The IPO price was Rs 189.

**Load-bearing facts to verify first**
1. FY27 operating cash flow and receivable days in the audited annual results, against minus Rs 10.55 crore over FY26 and H1 FY27 combined (H1-27, p.12).
2. The size of unbilled revenue inside the Rs 78.85 crore of receivables, and its billing rate after September (AR26, p.95; H1-27, p.10).
3. Who the top five customers are, and whether any related or ex-related entity remains among them (Pros, pp.24, 60).
4. The H1 FY27 Reg 23(9) disclosure and any flows with Credent Foundation or Vsanskriti LLP (Pros, p.171).

**What would change the view**
- Up, to PROCEED: positive H2 FY27 operating cash flow, receivable days at or below 95, H2 revenue above Rs 160 crore.
- Down, to PASS: a second negative half of operating cash flow, a write-off of unbilled revenue, or a top customer loss.
