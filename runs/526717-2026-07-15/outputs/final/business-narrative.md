# HCP Plastene Bulkpack Ltd (526717)

FIBC turnaround real, cash conversion still unproven

HCP Plastene Bulkpack makes woven plastic bulk packaging out of Gujarat, close to the Kandla and Mundra ports. The Woven Sacks division, which covers FIBC jumbo bags, small PP sacks, AD Star bags and PP fabric, is 99.46% of revenue; a Jacquard label division is the remaining half a percent. It sits inside the Champalal group, one of the larger HDPP granule importers in India. The subsidiary KP Woven, bought in February 2022, runs at about 3.2 times the parent's revenue, so the consolidated numbers are the ones that matter.

The company came out of insolvency. The listed entity took over a distressed HDPP facility through the NCLT process, and FY21 profit of Rs 63.6 cr was mostly a Rs 53 cr one-off other-income gain, not operating profit. FY22 to FY24 were loss years while the subsidiary was consolidated and the plant ran on a job-work model. From FY25 the business shifted to selling directly, with exports at 53.7% of revenue.

The turnaround itself is real. Consolidated revenue grew 57% in FY25. PAT moved from a Rs 1.2 cr loss owned by shareholders in FY24 to Rs 9.6 cr in FY25 and Rs 23.2 cr in FY26. Consolidated EBITDA margin rose from about 5.7% to 7.6% and reached roughly 9.55% in FY26, above the 7% to 9% band the rating agency projected.

The scorecard is harsh on the balance sheet. Gate 0 scored the company 27 out of 100 on the core and 48 out of 160 overall, landing in the AVOID tier. The binding reason is leverage: net debt to EBITDA of 4.10 times sits with interest coverage of only 2.37 times in FY26. That is a current-state fact, not just a legacy of the loss years.

Cash is where the story splits from the profit. Operating cash flow was negative in five of the six years to FY25, cumulatively about minus Rs 80 cr. FY25 was negative at both the parent (minus Rs 5.8 cr) and the group (minus Rs 18.5 cr) despite the profit. FY26 consolidated cash flow finally turned positive at plus Rs 16.2 cr, but standalone cash flow worsened to minus Rs 7.6 cr, and no source explains the divergence. Capex is near zero, so the drain is working capital, not investment, and much of the receivable base runs through related parties and sits outside the bank's drawing power.

Related-party dependence runs through the whole model. Related parties account for 46.6% of standalone revenue, up to 70.4% of consolidated COGS, and up to 49.2% of consolidated receivables. Plastene India Ltd alone is about 35% of revenue and is also a 4.68% promoter-group shareholder. This year the subsidiary took a fresh Rs 33.2 cr interest-free loan from that same customer, which sits against the filing's own arm's-length claim, and cross-guarantees across the group exceed two times consolidated net worth.

On credibility the record cuts both ways. FII holding rose from near zero to 4.5% and DII to 4.8% over 2025, the promoter pledge has been zero for more than five straight quarters, the 2022 raise was a pro-rata rights issue rather than a promoter allotment, and the managing director forwent his own pay. Against that, three company secretaries came and went in about fifteen months, the latest resigning in January 2026 citing unresolved matters requiring clarification, a SEBI disclosure lapse recurred within nine months of a written assurance, and a Malaysia JV that was pitched as a growth opportunity was quietly dissolved. The stage graded credibility C and the promoter check returned CONCERN.

Peers sharpen the picture rather than settle it. Emmbi and Kanpur Plastipack both run FIBC capacity at 75% to 90% utilisation, including through their own expansions, against HPBL's 31% in FY25, which makes the low utilisation look company-specific rather than an industry ramp curve. Kanpur is also spending Rs 105 cr on new capacity in the same window HPBL frames as capex-free, so the "no major capex" stance is a lone-operator choice, not a sector pause. Both peers confirm that raw material pass-through is genuine but lagged by 30 to 45 days, not automatic. The FIBC market runway is large, roughly US$12.6bn by 2033 at about 5.4% growth per the company's own unnamed source, and the modelled path implies close to 30% revenue growth over three years, but HPBL holds only about 0.8% share today and its own capacity ceiling caps the five-year opportunity.
