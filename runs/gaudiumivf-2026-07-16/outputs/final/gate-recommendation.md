## FLAG-CASH

Metric and direction: trade receivable days rose from 7 in FY23 to about 156 in FY26 and receivables reached 51% of revenue; standalone receivables turnover fell from 6.34x to 3.17x, a 50% decline; CFO was about 45% of PAT in FY25; FY26 free cash flow was negative. Direction is deteriorating (B01, B02, B03, B05).

Determination: INDETERMINATE.

The affordability-model explanation argues growth induced: revenue is recognised at embryo transfer while patients pay across the treatment cycle, and management has doubled the upfront payment from Rs 40,000 to Rs 80,000 and added a Rs 40,000 late-transfer fee to shorten collection (B05 guidance). Three things prevent confirming that reading. Peer triangulation contradicts the claim that a roughly 150 day cycle is sector-normal: HCG collects in 105 to 115 days and Kaya collects prepaid (B06 contradicted). The "zero bad debt" claim was itself contradicted by the first-ever ECL allowance of Rs 32 lakh in the same-week audited cash flow (B05 promise_delivery). And receivables grew 62% in FY26 against 48% revenue growth, still outpacing the top line rather than tracking it (B04). No rating agency working capital commentary is available to characterise the book independently, because no rating rationale was provided to the pipeline.

Missing evidence that would resolve the determination: the credit rating rationale with its verbatim working capital commentary (obtain from the rating agency press release), a receivables ageing schedule for FY26, and one post-fix quarter of collection data.

Resolving metric to watch: Q1 FY27 trade receivable days. A fall from the roughly 150 to 156 day base, with the ECL allowance flat or lower, would support growth induced; days holding or rising with a growing ECL would push the read toward structural.
