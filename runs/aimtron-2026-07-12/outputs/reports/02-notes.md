## PATTERN PASS — NOTES RE-READ FOR CONTRADICTIONS, MISMATCHES, VAGUE DISCLOSURE, RESTATEMENTS,
## SUBSEQUENT EVENTS AND GOING-CONCERN LANGUAGE

Most pattern candidates (cross-note contradictions, consolidation-mechanics gaps, restatement
traces, a blank reconciliation table) were already surfaced across Pass 1 and Pass 2. Re-reading
against the source PDF for this pass sharpened two items and added AGM-notice corroboration on a
third; no entirely new red flag category emerged beyond what Pass 1/2 identified.

1. **Note 38 "Reasons for Variances" contains a factually incorrect driver, not merely a
   low-specificity one.** Re-checked against the primary Balance Sheet (p.88) and Note 14
   (p.102): Inventories actually **declined** from ₹3,573.70 lakh (FY24) to ₹3,507.63 lakh (FY25),
   a **−1.85% decrease**. Note 38's mandatory explanation for the 103.96% inventory-turnover
   improvement reads "**Higher proportionate increase in inventories**" — this is the opposite of
   what happened; the ratio improved because the numerator (Total Turnover) rose 70.3% while
   inventory fell, not because inventory rose. Pass 1 flagged this as an "inconsistency" between
   the note's stated reason and Note 14's data; on source re-verification this is better
   characterised as a **factual error in a Schedule III–mandated disclosure**, elevated
   accordingly. (Note 38, p.111; Note 14, p.102; Balance Sheet, p.88) 🔴

2. **AGM Notice RPT annexure (pp.152–153) adds hard percentage-of-turnover context not visible
   from the Notes alone.** The SEBI-mandated RPT disclosure table states the proposed ₹100 crore
   Aimtron Corporation USA ceiling represents "**Approximately 18%**" of that counterparty's own
   annual consolidated turnover, and the ₹20 crore Aimtron Systems LLC ceiling represents
   "**Approximately 6%**." This confirms the counterparties are themselves smaller entities for
   which Aimtron is a material trading partner, reinforcing (not merely restating) Pass 2's
   finding that the ₹120 crore combined FY26 ceiling is not incidental group housekeeping but a
   structurally significant, continuing revenue relationship. (AGM Notice p.152)

3. **Chairman's "Non-Executive" designation is now doubly confirmed against a real remuneration
   line, via the Director's Report annexure (p.155) as well as Note 35.** The Regulation 36(3)
   annexure independently labels Mr. Mukesh Vasani "**Non-Executive Director & Chairman**" and
   separately confirms he is Chairman of Aimtron Foundation and a director of Aimtron
   Technologies Private Limited — the same two related parties already flagged for CSR routing
   (Note 3 item 15) and the new ₹404.99 lakh Services Availed line (Note 35(ii)). This
   independently sourced confirmation strengthens, rather than merely repeats, Pass 2's finding
   #3. (AGM Notice p.155; Note 35, p.109)

4. **Going concern**: Re-read of the full Auditor's Responsibility sections in both the
   standalone (p.81) and consolidated (p.115) reports, plus CARO clause (xix) (p.85) — all
   contain only standard template language ("if we conclude that a material uncertainty exists,
   we are required to draw attention... we neither give any guarantee nor any assurance that all
   liabilities falling due... will get discharged"). No material uncertainty is flagged and no
   exception is taken in either opinion. Confirmed: **going_concern_language: NONE.**

5. **Restatement pattern re-confirmed at source**: Note 2 (Reserves and Surplus, p.98, quoted in
   Pass 1) shows a FY24 "Restatement adjustment" of (₹406.94) lakh against Surplus and a Bonus
   Issue capitalisation of ₹1,058.41 lakh, consistent with SEBI ICDR-mandated restatement of
   pre-IPO financials — never narratively named as such anywhere in the Notes themselves. No
   additional restatement lines were found on the pattern re-read beyond what Pass 1 already
   identified.

No other new pattern-level contradictions were found on this pass. The remainder of this document
is the required consolidation of all three passes.

### D. FIVE QUESTIONS FOR MANAGEMENT

1. What is the nature of the ₹404.99 lakh "Services Availed" from Aimtron Technologies Private Limited that appears for the first time in FY25 with no FY24 comparative and no description in the Notes, and why did the balance with Aimtron Corporation USA flip from a ₹1,061.76 lakh payable (FY24) to a ₹2,152.54 lakh receivable (FY25) even as sales to that entity declined YoY?
2. What drove the 93.9% collapse in closing finished-goods inventory (₹688.30 lakh to ₹41.65 lakh) at the same time trade receivables rose 417% — is there a shift in revenue-recognition or shipment timing concentrated at year-end, and can the underlying Q4 invoicing profile be shared?
3. Why did salaries and wages fall 15.8% YoY despite 70.3% revenue growth and an active-hiring narrative in the MD&A — was production labour reclassified out of this line into cost of materials or inventory, and if so, by how much?
4. Given the AGM Notice seeks a combined ₹120 crore FY26 related-party transaction ceiling with Aimtron Corporation USA and Aimtron Systems LLC (76% of FY25 revenue), what is the intended trajectory for related-party revenue as a share of total revenue over the next two to three years, and what specific arm's-length pricing benchmarking is applied to these transactions?
5. Can the company clarify Mr. Mukesh Vasani's "Non-Executive Director" classification against the ₹24.00 lakh FY25 "Remuneration to Directors" paid to him (up 3.0x YoY, versus ₹0.45–0.60 lakh sitting fees paid to the company's genuinely independent non-executive directors) — is this commission under Section 197, and if so, where is the specific disclosure required for that?

### E. NOTES-BASED RED FLAGS

- Related-party revenue concentration of 27.68–31.53% of FY25 revenue, plus a forward FY26 RPT ceiling request of ₹120 crore (76% of FY25 revenue, 6.3x/1.3x the statutory materiality threshold) — Note 35; AGM Notice Items 5&6.
- Trade receivables +417% vs revenue +70.3%, 46.3% related-party, thin bad-debt coverage (~2.3% of the at-risk pool), and a receivable/payable flip on a single related counterparty — Notes 15, 35, 38.
- New, unexplained related-party expense of ₹404.99 lakh with nil prior-year comparative and no description of the underlying services — Note 35(ii).
- Contingent liabilities up from ₹4.04 lakh to ₹20.91 crore (13.48% of net worth), designated a Key Audit Matter, with no provision made — Note 30.
- A Schedule III–mandated ratio-variance disclosure (Note 38) states a factually incorrect driver for the inventory-turnover improvement — inventory declined 1.85% YoY, not increased as stated.
- Note 3 item 3 (no loans to related parties) is directly contradicted by Note 35(ii)'s disclosure of a ₹28.33 lakh loan given to a related party in FY24.
- Note 36's bank-reconciliation table is blank in the "as per books" row for all four quarters, unable to substantiate CARO's own assertion that quarterly bank returns agree with the books.

### F. ONE-LINE NOTES VERDICT

The notes reveal concerning accounting practices. Key concern: related-party revenue and receivable concentration (up to 31.53% of revenue, 46.3% of receivables) compounded by a forward FY26 RPT ceiling equal to 76% of trailing revenue and a factually incorrect mandatory ratio-variance disclosure. Key strength: conservative capitalisation policy (Schedule II depreciation, fully expensed R&D), fully traceable and utilised IPO proceeds, and no going-concern exception. Overall accounting quality: 4/10.
