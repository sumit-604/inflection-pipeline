## Business Understanding Narrative

**What does it do, and how does it make money?** Monolithisch makes premixed silica ramming mass: crushed quartzite with a boric acid or boron oxide binder. Induction furnace mills ram it in as a lining and break it out every 45 to 60 hours (Oct-2025 call, p.17). It sells at about Rs 8 a kg, delivered (Feb call, p.18; May call, p.22). Stone costs Rs 2.2 to 2.6 a kg and additives Rs 1 to 1.7 (Feb call, p.18). Freight is the swing cost. The money is the spread between the delivered price and landed stone, additive and freight. FY26 consolidated revenue was Rs 135.29 crore and profit after tax Rs 23.03 crore (FY26R, p.7).

**What is changing?** Scale, supply chain and product. Group capacity went from 1,32,000 tonnes a year in April 2025 to 5,76,000, with the greenfield plant producing from 10 October 2026 (greenfield filing 17-Sep-2026, p.1). The company bought its promoter family's ramming mass company, Mineral India Global (EGM, p.15-17). It won a five-year quartzite block in Bihar for Rs 157 crore (mine filing 10-Sep-2026, p.1). A premium grade, SGB Limited, was about 50% of Q1 FY27 revenue (press release 25-Jul-2026, p.3).

**Why now?** Two record quarters. Profit after tax was Rs 8.11 crore in Q4 FY26 and Rs 10.07 crore in Q1 FY27, against Rs 4.29 crore in Q1 FY26 (Q1R, p.2).

**What must be true for the thesis to work?** The new capacity must fill. Q1 FY27 volume was 52,000 tonnes (press release 25-Jul-2026, p.2), about 36% of the new capacity annualised. The 28% margin must survive without trading gains and cheap old inventory.

**What breaks it?** Oversupply and fixed commitments. An analyst noted the listed peer is also expanding to about 8 lakh tonnes; management declined to comment (Oct call, p.14). About 70% of the market is unorganised and half of customers are "very, very sensitive" to price (Jul call, p.22-23). If prices give, the Rs 157 crore mine bill does not.

## 2. Business model and archetype

Three plants: Sarbari, West Bengal, 2,50,000 tonnes; Ranchi (Mineral India Global), 72,000; Purulia greenfield (Metalurgica, 99.95% owned), 2,52,000 to 2,54,000 (Q1 deck `screener/Investor_Presentation_1.pdf`, p.5; FY26R, p.10). One reported segment (FY26R, p.10). SGB-777 is the old flagship and SGB Limited the new premium (Q1 deck, p.6).

The engine is freight. The plants sit in the eastern induction furnace belt. Outward freight to Raipur is Rs 1,500 to 1,700 a tonne; a Rajasthan maker pays at least Rs 3,000 (Oct-2025 call, p.19). Plant is Rs 24.93 crore against Rs 135.29 crore of revenue (FY26R, p.8), so return on capital is high.

Some revenue is trading: bulk additives resold and lower grades bought in. Management put Q1 FY27 trading at about Rs 4.5 to 5 crore of Rs 47.19 crore (Jul call, p.20).

**Archetype: commodity converter.** Spread over landed stone, additive and freight; utilisation; cost-curve rank set by location. Section 1B v3.7 Amendment 17 binds. SGB Limited adds a thin build-to-spec layer.

## 3. Competitive advantages

Partial, and mostly location. Safety makes mills cautious: a lining failure can stop a plant for 15 to 20 days (Oct-2025 call, p.8). But large mills keep two or three vendors at 80-20 or 60-40 (Feb call, p.9-10). The Executive Director put the cost gap between vendors at about Rs 2 a tonne of steel (Oct-2025 call, p.9). The top two groups are 24% to 25% of sales (Feb call, p.26). The mine adds a cost edge from early 2027 if it runs. **Moat: present in freight geography, absent in product.** The SGB Limited premium of Rs 700 to 800 a tonne, costing Rs 250 to 300 to make (Jul call, p.8), is one quarter old.

## 4. The promoters

The Tekriwal family's "Mineral Group". Prabhat Tekriwal, Chairman, has been in furnace consumables since 1983 (Jul call, p.7). Harsh Tekriwal is Managing Director, Kritish Tekriwal Executive Director. Control sits in a holding company: Kargil Transport Private Limited holds 64.04% and Prabhat Tekriwal 9.89% (AR, p.111-112). The family bought after the IPO: Prabhat Tekriwal added 70,250 shares and Sharmila Tekriwal 59,000 (AR, p.112). Promoters held 74.57% at September 2026 (screener.in service record, not a filed document). Pledge: none disclosed in the AR; a pledge filing is NOT FOUND.

Related-party dependence is high, and money runs both ways. The company paid Rs 17.99 crore cash for 100% of Mineral India Global: Rs 16.99 crore to Prabhat Tekriwal HUF and Rs 1.00 crore to Prabhat Tekriwal (EGM, p.16, p.32; AR, p.128). The stated basis was net asset value, yet the target's capital plus reserves was Rs 14.92 crore (EGM, p.15). Goodwill on consolidation rose from Rs 0.09 crore to Rs 3.56 crore (FY26R, p.8).
- Harsh Tekriwal lent the company Rs 18 crore, interest-free and unsecured, to fund that purchase. Rs 14.70 crore was repaid; Rs 3.30 crore was outstanding at 31 March 2026 (AR, p.56, p.127). The 2026 AGM was asked to ratify it.
- The company lent Prabhat Tekriwal Rs 1.55 crore and got it back within the year (AR, p.127). CARO records loans "repayable on demand or without specifying any terms" (AR, p.96). Mineral India Global separately borrowed Rs 5.65 crore from him and repaid it (AR, p.166).
- The company guarantees Rs 4.40 crore of Mineral India Global's bank facility (AR, p.126).
- Ongoing trade with promoter entities: Rs 3.91 crore of purchases from Mineral Resources, a director's firm; sales to Metalaid Industries; Rs 0.14 crore of transport from Maa Ambey Logistics, Harsh Tekriwal HUF's firm (AR, p.128).
- Kargil Transport, the 64% holding company, is absent from the related-party list (AR, p.127). Any freight it carries for the group is NOT FOUND.

**Classification: earnings-led, with an asset event (the related-party acquisition) and a margin step that is part mix, part inventory and trading gain.**

## 6. The transition and the quality ladder

FROM R2, cost-advantaged converter: margin from location and freight, not price. TO R3 claimed: a spec'd supplier with a warranty-backed premium grade. Management goes further: "we are transforming Monolithisch from a high-volume market leader into a high-margin value compounder" (Q1 deck, p.16). The AR sets dates: high-purity quartz in about two years, tile adhesives in one, silica bricks in three (AR, p.59).

The R3 mechanism is SGB Limited: a 52 to 55 hour heat assurance against 45 to 50 hours for SGB-777 (May call, p.15). The mine strengthens R2 cost position; it does not lift pricing power. **Reading: a real R2 business, an R3 claim one quarter old, and an R4/R5 narrative with no evidence.**

## 8. Proof check

The company reports quarterly by choice; SME rules require only half-yearly (EGM, p.2). So the two-quarter test applies. On the headline, Q4 FY26 and Q1 FY27 both moved the way the triggers claim: revenue up 35% and 64%, EBITDA margin 28.1% and 27.8%.

Underneath, the proof is thinner. Organic growth was 7.7% and 22.9%. Q1 missed its guide by 9% to 14%. Q1 group utilisation was about 70% (Jul call, p.15), before the greenfield added 2,52,000 tonnes. To reach Rs 250 crore, H2 FY27 needs about Rs 143 to 148 crore, near Rs 72 crore a quarter against Q1's Rs 47 crore. **Proof present for earnings; absent for the climb.** The separating observation: Q2 and Q3 FY27 standalone gross margin with freight added back and trading shown apart, and greenfield tonnes sold.

## 9. Flags from the documents

- **Mine commitment.** Rs 157 crore over five years, payable regardless of volume (Oct call, p.17-18): 6.8 times FY26 profit and 11.4 times FY26 operating cash flow. The first Rs 17 to 18 crore falls due January or February 2027, funded by cutting a Rs 20 to 30 crore stone inventory (Oct call, p.8). Management calls it a prepayment, "not a capex" (Oct call, p.16).
- **Treasury in listed shares.** Rs 6.96 crore put into quoted equity in FY26, worth Rs 6.17 crore at year end; the Rs 0.79 crore loss was not provided for (AR, p.116). The holdings are not named. A Rs 0.29 crore gain on listed shares came in H1 FY26 (clarification, p.18).
- **Related parties.** Acquisition above book, the target's margin then rising, and promoter loans both ways without terms (steps 4, 5).
- **CFO change.** On 4-Sep-2026 the promoter Chairman left the CFO post to a chartered accountant with three years' experience, promoted from Finance Executive (board outcome 04-Sep-2026, p.2-3), as the mine and two plants start.
- **Guidance record.** FY26 revenue below its floor; Q1 FY27 below its range; FY27 top end dropped; capacity dates one to four months late.
- **Accounting.** Depreciation moved from WDV to SLM from 1-Jan-2026, adding Rs 0.21 crore to profit; auditors drew attention (FY26R, p.6). Q3 EBITDA was reported with other income (Rs 8.94 crore, Q3 deck p.9), Q4 without.
- **Exchange query.** NSE flagged the H1 FY26 results as not in SEBI format and not machine-readable (clarification, p.1-2).
- **Promotional cadence.** Many of the 38 filings are awards, summits and PR or IR agency hires, including EY (filing 23-Sep-2026, p.1). Management claimed "over 80% of integrated steel plants" as customers (Oct-2025 call, p.4) and a 10% to 12% national share (Feb call, p.24).
- **Audit.** Opinion unmodified, no key audit matters (AR, p.91). CARO is clean on statutory dues, defaults and fraud (AR, p.96-97). Its clause (xviii) says "There has been resignation of the statutory auditors during the year", then calls the clause not applicable (AR, p.98). The wording is unexplained. The contingent note shows no capital commitments while the greenfield was being built (AR, p.126).

## 11. Posture: the Transition Decision Matrix

- **Proof gate: FIRED on earnings, NOT FIRED on the rung climb.** Premium mix, margin base and organic growth are not yet separated from trading, inventory gains and the acquisition.
- **Ugliness: ARTIFACT-OF-CLIMB in the balance sheet, governance question open.** Inventory and capex are the cost of a 4x capacity build. The related-party pattern is not proven structural, and not cleared.
- **Recognition gap: CLOSED.** Price basis, not a corpus document: screener.in service record, 2026-10-18, market capitalisation Rs 2,663 crore, price Rs 1,225, trailing PE 92.5. FY26 PE is 115.6x.

**Posture: EARNINGS-ONLY.** The climb is partly real and already priced. Returns must ride earnings growth alone, and the growth needed is the FY28 guide.

## 12. Verdict card

**WATCH.**

A real business with quarterly data to track it, but the market cap does not pass the earnings test. At the FY27 guide the stock is near 60x; only full delivery of FY28's Rs 500-550 crore brings it to 27x to 34x (step 13). The company missed its last two revenue guides. It has just taken on a Rs 157 crore fixed mine bill, Rs 45 crore of plants and a junior CFO. A `/step1` now would spend effort to confirm a price that already assumes success.

**Load-bearing facts to verify first, when it returns**
1. Q2 and Q3 FY27 revenue against Rs 55-60 crore, greenfield tonnes, and gross margin with freight added back and trading out.
2. Nawada auction terms: payment schedule, offtake, landed stone cost after royalty, raising and freight.
3. Mineral India Global's margin once old inventory is gone, and repayment of the Rs 3.30 crore MD loan.
4. Kargil Transport's dealings with the group, and the names of the listed shares held.

**What would change the view**
- To PROCEED: two quarters near Rs 60-70 crore with margin above 24% after freight and without trading, and positive operating cash flow; or a price fall that reopens the gap.
- To PASS: margin back to 20% as the peer's capacity lands, or a deferred mine payment.

No Role 1 valuation was run and no target price is set. This verdict decides only whether the name earns a full `/step1`, and it is not a position.
