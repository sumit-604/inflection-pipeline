## SECTION 1: THE BUSINESS MODEL IN PLAIN ENGLISH

### 1A. One-line description

Titan Biotech buys animal and plant protein raw material, ferments and extracts
it into biological ingredients (peptones, culture media, collagen peptides, and
related biological goods), and sells them as consumable inputs to pharma,
diagnostic, food, and nutraceutical manufacturers who use them to grow microbes,
formulate supplements, or run lab tests (AR FY26 p.71, p.100, p.119).

### 1B. The money flow chain

The company discloses only ONE reportable business segment (AR FY26 p.100, p.142;
Q1FY27 filing note 3) and no product-wise revenue split. The chain below is
therefore drawn at the level the filings actually support: one production engine,
two customer geographies.

**Domestic stream**
[Animal/plant protein raw material, 37.1% bought from related parties (AR FY26
p.145)] → [fermentation, extraction, drying into peptones/culture
media/collagen/biological extracts at four Bhiwadi-Chopanki-Kaharani plants, AR
FY26 p.119] → [delivers packaged biological ingredients] → [pharma, diagnostic,
food, veterinary, cosmetic and agriculture manufacturers in India] → [pays on
invoice; MSME/trade payable and receivable terms; no channel or distributor
structure disclosed, NOT FOUND] = Rs 125.86 cr, 61.0% of FY26 revenue (AR FY26
p.142).

**Export stream**
[Same raw material and production base] → [same manufacturing process, product
specs presumably built to buyer/regulatory requirement, though no certification
is named in the filings, NOT FOUND] → [delivers to overseas pharma/biotech/food
buyers] → [pays in foreign currency, imports of Rs 8.63 cr against Rs 80.33 cr of
exports make the company a large net foreign-exchange earner, per prior-stage
figures cross-checked against this run's AR FY26 p.142 export total] = Rs 80.33
cr, 39.0% of FY26 revenue, up 49.0% from Rs 53.90 cr in FY25 (AR FY26 p.142).

Both streams sit inside the same "Sale of Products (Net of Discount)" revenue
line (AR FY26 p.135, Note 23); FY26 Rs 20,619.03 lakh includes a freight
gross-up of roughly Rs 5.84 cr neutralised in other expenses, not restated in
the FY25 comparative (per prior-stage finding; the freight note recurs verbatim
in the FY26 audited results and the Q1FY27 filing note 5). Reported FY26 growth
is 31.8% on this basis; like-for-like growth is closer to 28%. Every reading of
this business's revenue momentum below uses the like-for-like framing.

### 1E. The chai-stall-uncle version

Titan Biotech is like a spice grinder for biology labs. It buys animal and plant
protein (its raw "spices"), grinds and cooks it down through fermentation, and
sells the result as the base ingredient other people use to grow bacteria, make
supplements, or run medical tests. It does not sell a finished medicine or food
product to you or me; it sells the "stock" that other kitchens (pharma
companies, diagnostic labs, food companies) use to cook their own final dish.
Four grinding units, all clustered in one small patch of Rajasthan (AR FY26
p.119), feed both Indian and export customers. The catch: like a spice
wholesaler competing with other wholesalers, Titan cannot always pass on a
spike in the cost of its own raw ingredients, and it says so itself (AR FY26
p.101). Roughly a third of what it grinds is now bought from businesses linked
to its own promoters (AR FY26 p.145), which is worth watching the way you would
watch a shopkeeper buying stock mostly from his own brother-in-law.

### 2D. Industry lifecycle stage

The global culture media and biological-ingredient market is in a growth phase:
USD 6.03 billion in 2025, projected to USD 14.26 billion by 2035, an 8.99% CAGR
(AR FY26 p.100, source cited as Precedence Research, updated June 2026). Titan's
own revenue path does not track a smooth growth curve: it fell 4.6% in FY25
before rising 31.8% (reported) in FY26 (AR FY25 p.130; AR FY26 p.103, p.135).
The company's own MD&A language — "tried to maintain its market share" (AR FY26
p.102) — reads as a follower holding position inside a growing market, not a
share-gaining leader.

**Cycle stage that matters for valuation:** FY25 was a margin-and-revenue
trough (revenue down 4.6%, AR FY25 p.130) and FY26/Q1FY27 (+31.8% reported,
+27.2% Q1FY27 standalone, this run's Q1FY27 filing) is a recovery. Any relative
or DCF valuation must state explicitly which year's earnings and growth rate it
anchors to, and should not treat the single-year FY26 or Q1FY27 rebound as the
new run-rate without more evidence that it is durable value-added mix, not a
post-FY25 restock (a question this run's evidence does not settle).
