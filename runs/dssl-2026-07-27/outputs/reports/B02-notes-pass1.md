### 1. ACCOUNTING POLICIES & CHANGES

- **Basis of preparation**: Ind AS, historical cost except certain financial
  instruments at fair value. No first-time Ind AS adoption this year (company has been
  Ind AS since prior years). 🟢 (Note S-1.2.1/C-1.2.1, p.101-102 / p.157)
- **Ind AS 116/117/21 amendments (FY25)**: management concluded "no impact" on
  standalone/consolidated financials for all three — boilerplate, no quantification
  since genuinely nil impact stated. 🟢 (Note S-1.2.4, p.104-105; Note C-1.2.4, p.159-160)
- **Revenue recognition policy — a genuine industry-relevant finding**: sale of goods
  is recognised **point-in-time on dispatch/delivery**; services on **output basis**
  (units delivered/efforts expended). There is **no percentage-of-completion /
  milestone accounting** disclosed anywhere in the revenue note, despite DSSL's
  business being described (Board's Report / Note S-1.1) as "turnkey systems
  integration" of "large Network and Data Centre infrastructures" — work that in
  practice is billed on customer-acceptance milestones. The Notes explicitly state
  **"there are no unbilled dues receivable from any customers"** (Note S-9.6, p.119;
  Note C-8.6, p.174), i.e. no contract-asset/unbilled-revenue line exists at all. 🟡
  This combination (delivery-triggered revenue recognition + contractual 90-120 day
  terms + management-stated milestone-based collection per the credit-rating context)
  is a structural source of the receivables ageing tail found below — revenue is
  booked on delivery but customers pay only after milestone sign-off, so the
  balance-sheet risk shows up entirely in trade receivables ageing rather than in a
  separately provisioned contract-asset line.
- **Deferred contract costs — NEW capitalisation this year**: Rs 3,819.71 lakh
  (**Rs 38.20 Cr**) recognised for the first time as "Other Non-Current Assets" (was
  Rs 0 in FY24). Policy note S-1.2.2.4/C-1.2.2.4 (p.105/p.161) describes these as
  "upfront costs incurred for the contract...amortised on a systematic basis
  consistent with transfer of goods/services." No amortisation schedule, no
  description of which contract(s) these relate to, and no disclosure of the expected
  amortisation period is given anywhere in the Notes. 🟡 Watch — this is a new,
  unexplained, material (4.9% of standalone total assets) capitalisation with no
  amortisation-period disclosure; an investor cannot assess whether this represents
  genuine future economic benefit or is deferring what would otherwise be a current
  expense. (Note S-7, p.117; Note C-6, p.173)
- **Useful lives**: computers (desktop/laptop 6 yrs, servers/storage 3 yrs, others 2
  yrs), furniture 10 yrs, vehicles 8 yrs, office equipment 5 yrs, intangibles 6 yrs —
  all at or below Schedule II norms (management uses lower-than-statutory lives, i.e.
  more conservative/faster depreciation). Assets <Rs 5,000 fully depreciated in year
  of purchase. 🟢 (Note S-1.2.7, p.106; Note C-1.2.8, p.162-163)
- **Capitalisation threshold**: Rs 5,000, disclosed. 🟢
- **Impairment test assumptions**: policy describes CGU/value-in-use methodology in
  generic terms; **no specific discount rate, growth rate, or CGU is disclosed**
  anywhere, and no impairment loss was recognised in either year. NOT FOUND IN
  DOCUMENT (quantified assumptions). 🟢 (immaterial — no goodwill on either balance
  sheet, no impairment charge either year)
- **ECL matrix / provisioning**: the impairment policy (Note S-1.2.18/C-1.2.21,
  p.111-112/p.166-168) commits to lifetime-ECL for trade receivables, but the actual
  **allowance for expected credit loss on trade receivables is frozen at Rs 13.68 lakh
  in BOTH FY24 and FY25** (Note S-9.1, p.118; Note C-8.1, p.173) despite (a) trade
  receivables growing 8.7% YoY and (b) the >180-day / 1-2 year ageing buckets growing
  far faster than total receivables (see Trade Receivables section below). 🔴 Red
  Flag — the ECL charge has not moved in two years even as the ageing profile of the
  book has visibly deteriorated; this is the single clearest under-provisioning signal
  in the Notes.
- **Employee benefit plan (gratuity) discount rate**: cut from 7.19% (FY24) to 6.73%
  (FY25), salary escalation flat at 5.00%. Lower discount rate increases the DBO —
  consistent, no cherry-picking evident; sensitivity table disclosed in full. 🟢 (Note
  S-32.2, p.130-131; Note C-31.2, p.186-187)
- **Ind AS 116 (leases)**: incremental borrowing rate used for lease liabilities is
  **NOT FOUND IN DOCUMENT** (the Company discloses ROU/lease-liability amounts and
  movements but never states the discount rate applied) — see Leases finding below

  year" of **Rs 9,549.85 lakh = Rs 95.50 Cr** (Note S-32.11/C-31.9, p.140/p.195-196).
  Critically, this new lease liability is **NOT** matched by a commensurate ROU-asset
  increase — the Right-of-Use asset for leasehold premises actually *fell* from Rs
  2.30 Cr to Rs 1.56 Cr (depreciation only, no additions, per Note S-2). Instead, the
  balance sheet shows a new **"Lease Receivable"** asset of Rs 58.56 Cr (non-current,
  Note S-5/C-4) plus Rs 24.30 Cr (current, Note S-13/C-12) = **Rs 82.85 Cr total**,
  appearing from zero in FY24. The lease accounting policy (Note S-1.2.16/C-1.2.17,
  "Company as a lessor," p.109-110/p.164-165) explains the mechanism: "the company has
  entered into lease agreements for IT hardware & same are deployed to customers under
  service contracts & under the service contracts the customers have full control and
  use of the assets during the term and are entitled to acquire the assets at the end
  of the term, the Company derecognises the Right-of-Use assets as Financial Assets."
  **In plain terms: DSSL is taking IT hardware on lease from a financier (recognising
  a large lease liability as lessee), then sub-leasing/deploying that hardware to end
  customers under arrangements that qualify as finance leases (recognising a Lease
  Receivable as lessor), effectively acting as a financing intermediary for customer
  hardware deployments.** This single transaction structure explains almost the
  entire ~Rs 187 Cr (32%) growth in the standalone balance sheet this year (total
  assets Rs 587.03 Cr → Rs 774.07 Cr) and is also the stated reason for the sharp fall
  in the Debt Service Coverage Ratio (Section 7). 🔴 Red Flag — this is the most
  significant, least-explained structural change in the entire Notes set: no
  counterparty/vendor/financier is named, no interest rate implicit in the lease
  liability is disclosed, no discount rate used to measure the lease liability is
  disclosed, and no maturity/credit-quality profile of the Lease Receivable book is
  given beyond the current/non-current split. An investor cannot assess the margin,
  credit risk, or sustainability of this new ~Rs 95.5 Cr financing-lease business line
  from the Notes as drafted.
