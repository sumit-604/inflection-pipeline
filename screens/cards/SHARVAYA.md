## Business Understanding Narrative

**What does it do, and how does it make money?** Sharvaya remelts aluminium scrap and
ingot and sells alloyed ingots, billets, slabs, sheets and circles. It also machines EV
battery housings (AR p.5). The company names its own segment plainly: "manufacturing of
Aluminium Commodity" (FYR p.10, note 6). The money is the spread between metal bought
and metal sold. FY26 revenue was Rs 229.83 crore, all of it in India (AR p.42, note 37).
Imports at CIF value were Rs 83.58 crore (AR p.41, note 29). The plant runs on 49
employees (AR p.25) and a gross block of Rs 15.43 crore (AR p.37).

**What is changing?** Two things. A 10 inch extrusion press was commissioned on 5-Oct-2026
with IPO money (PR p.1). On 14-Oct-2026 the board approved the purchase of Success
Electronics, a die casting company owned by the promoter family, paid for in new shares
(BO p.2). Management sells this as a move from alloy to finished component.

**Why now?** The IPO raised Rs 49.00 crore of fresh money in Sep-2025 (MA-Jun p.7). The
press is the first asset it built. The acquisition came nine days later.

**What must be true?** The extrusion line and the die casting unit must earn a margin the
remelting business never earned. Before the IPO it earned about 5% at the operating line
(CARE p.1). FY25 and H1 FY26 showed about 16% to 18%. H2 FY26 showed 2.9%. The thesis needs
the middle number to be the real one.

**What breaks it?** H2 FY26 already looks like the break. Revenue rose and profit almost
vanished. A related party share swap prices the target at about 30 times trailing profit. With
the warrants, the share count rises 29%. If H1 FY27 repeats H2 FY26, the IPO years were the
exception.

## 2. Business model and archetype

One plant at Ahilyanagar, Maharashtra. Stated capacity: 3,500 MTPA ingots and scrap
processing, 500 MTPA billets, 2,000 MTPA EV components, 1,000 dies a year, and 10,000
MTPA extrusions (AR p.9). The monitoring agency describes the extrusion object as 8,000
MTPA and the old plant at about 89% utilisation (MA-Jun p.13 to p.14). The two
extrusion figures do not agree.

Customers per CARE: authorised raw material suppliers of utensil brands Hawkins, Prestige
and Nirlep, plus automobile buyers (CARE p.2). No product mix, volume or customer share is
disclosed. Revenue sits in one line, "Sales of Aluminium Products" (AR p.38, note 17).

The engine is a remelter's spread. Material is 94% of revenue in a weak half and 80% in
a strong one (FYR p.8). A few points of spread decide the year.

**Archetype: commodity converter**, so Amendment 17 binds at any later stage. The EV
housing and extrusion lines aspire to build to spec component maker. No separate revenue
for them is disclosed.

## 3. Competitive advantages

Absent today. CARE calls the industry "highly competitive and fragmented" (CARE p.2).
The company passes metal prices to buyers (CARE p.2), which caps the downside of a price
move and also caps pricing power. Scale is small. Cost advantage is not evidenced.

Possibly forming. The AR claims 2xxx and 7xxx series extrusions, which it says few Indian
makers can produce (AR p.10). Customer qualification in defence or aerospace would bring
switching costs. No qualification, order or revenue for these alloys is in the corpus.
Moat: absent, with one claimed seed.

Skin in the game is real at 61.52%. The new issue adds to it at Rs 144 (step 9).

## 6. The transition and the quality ladder

FROM **R1, commodity price taker.** CARE's FY23 and FY24 data show 5% operating margin,
1.4% net margin and gearing of 1.70 times (CARE p.1). The company calls its own segment
a commodity.

TO a claimed **R3, spec'd supplier**: extrusions and die cast parts for defence,
aerospace, railways and EV. Management's claim, quoted once: "we control every step from
the alloy to the finished component" (PR p.1).

That is a two rung jump. The ladder base rate is one rung per two to three years, and a
multi rung leap is itself a red flag. The two readings:

- Reading A: FY25 to H1 FY26 margins came from EV housings and better alloys. H2 FY26 was a
  metal price or ramp hit. The press lifts the mix from here.
- Reading B: the remelter earns about 5% in normal years. The IPO window margins were the
  outlier. The press has not yet sold a tonne.

The one observation that separates them: the H1 FY27 EBITDA margin, due by mid Nov-2026.
Ladder reading: **R1, claim unproven.**

## 8. Proof check

SME reporting is half yearly, so the proof test here is two halves, not two quarters.

H1 FY26 against H1 FY25: revenue up 118%, PAT up 129% (H1R p.3). H2 FY26 against H2 FY25:
revenue up 96%, PAT down 72% (FYR p.8). Volume grew in both halves. Margin held in one and
collapsed in the other.

No half yet contains the extrusion press, which started in Oct-2026. The proof point is
absent, and the latest half points the other way.

## 9. Flags from the documents

- **Related party acquisition at a rich multiple.** Success had FY26 revenue of Rs 10.36
  crore and PAT of Rs 1.01 crore (BO p.7). Sharvaya pays Rs 30.24 crore in 21,00,000 new
  shares at Rs 144 (BO p.8). That is 29.9 times trailing PAT and 2.9 times sales.
  Success revenue was Rs 9.24, 11.92 and 9.47 crore in 2023 to 2025 (BO p.9): flat.
  H1 FY27 PAT of Rs 0.89 crore on Rs 4.48 crore revenue (BO p.4) is a 19.8% net margin,
  double FY26's 9.7%, in the half before the sale.
- **Sellers.** Promoter group sellers get 11,31,375 shares; Katariya relatives classed
  as public get 9,68,625 (BO p.11 to p.12).
- **Dilution.** 21,00,000 swap shares plus 8,11,000 warrants on 1,00,30,000 shares is a
  29.0% increase. Fully diluted count 1,29,41,000. Promoter group goes to 62.46% (EGM p.42 to p.43).
  On FY26 numbers, Success's PAT adds 8.7% to earnings while the swap adds 20.9% to
  shares. EPS falls about 10%.
- **Price.** Rs 144 sits at the SEBI floor, the 10 day VWAP of Rs 143.92 (EGM p.22). MKT
  shows Rs 204. Only 25% of the warrant price is paid upfront (BO p.14). The warrant money
  has no monitoring agency (EGM p.39).
- **IPO proceeds.** No deviation reported (DEV p.2). General corporate purposes, Rs 7.30
  crore, were fully spent by 31-Mar-2026, before most capex (MA-Mar p.12). The CA
  certificate comes from "Rishabh S. Katariya & Associates", the tax auditor (MA-Jun p.13).
  The agency notes it is "neither by the statutory auditor nor the peer reviewed firm"
  (MA-Jun p.8). Link to the promoter family: NOT FOUND. The agency's cumulative figures
  do not roll forward: 28.53 at 31-Mar (DEV p.2) against an opening 27.33 at 1-Apr (MA-Jun
  p.12). MA-Mar prints 28.89 as the sum of 25.30 and 3.23 (MA-Mar p.13).
- **Audit.** Unmodified opinion. Accounting software audit trail "has not been preserved"
  (AR p.27). Auditor is Pukhraj & Associates, Raipur, fee Rs 3.00 lakh (AR p.39).
  Secretarial auditor notes delayed MCA filings (AR p.20).
- **Restated history.** AR shows FY24 PAT Rs 153.57 lakh (AR p.8). H1R shows FY24 PAT Rs
  99.71 lakh after a Rs 81.63 lakh prior period item (H1R p.3). AR's P&L shows FY25 PAT Rs
  1,222.98 lakh (AR p.31); FYR shows Rs 1,250.93 lakh (FYR p.8).
- **Exports.** The company describes international customers (PR p.2). Foreign exchange
  earnings were NIL (AR p.18).
- **Price queries.** BSE asked about price moves in Apr and Jun-2026. The company replied
  the moves were "purely market-driven" (`filings/2026-04-28_reg30-announcement.pdf` p.1;
  `filings/2026-06-24_price-movement-clarification.pdf` p.1). MKT shows a 52 week range

- **Filing error.** The EGM warrant table misstates the pre issue holdings of three

## 10. Independent check: the credit rating

CARE carried the bank lines as Issuer Not Cooperating from Apr-2021: CARE B, then CARE B
minus from Jun-2022. The company asked for withdrawal, granted on 25-Oct-2024 (CARE p.1,
p.3). The agency's view:
"modest scale", "moderate profitability", gearing 1.70 times, and margins exposed to
scrap prices in a fragmented market (CARE p.1 to p.2). Strength: experienced promoters
and repeat utensil supply chain buyers (CARE p.2).

The rating predates the IPO, so today's filings win on leverage. On margin it still
speaks: 5% operating and 1.4% net before the IPO window. H2 FY26 sits near that base.

## 11. Posture: the Transition Decision Matrix

- **Proof gate: NOT FIRED.** No half contains extrusion output. The latest half shows a
  margin collapse.
- **Ugliness: STRUCTURAL, provisional.** The margin fell in the old business, before any
  climb asset ran. The pre IPO record says 5% is the norm. An artifact reading needs the
  new assets to be the cause. They were not running.
- **Recognition gap: CLOSED.** MKT market cap Rs 205 crore is 17.6 times FY26 PAT and
  45 times the H2 run rate. The price already pays for R2 to R3 earnings.

**Posture: VALUE-TRAP RISK, with the gap CLOSED plus STRUCTURAL overlay pointing to
AVOID.** H1 FY27 is the test that could reclassify the ugliness.

## 12. Verdict card

**PASS.**

The case for a /step1 rests on one new press and one unproven margin. Against it stand a
2.9% margin half, minus Rs 33.53 crore of operating cash, a related party swap at 29.9
times trailing profit, and a family issue at the SEBI floor. The corpus holds no evidence
that the climb has started.

**Load bearing facts, if the name returns**
1. H1 FY27 EBITDA margin and any extrusion revenue in it.
2. The EGM vote of 6-Nov-2026, and the Success valuation method on the company website.
3. Whether Rishabh S. Katariya & Associates is a promoter relative's firm.
4. Pledge and the full promoter group map, from the RHP and shareholding filings.

**What would change the view**
- H1 FY27 EBITDA margin above 10%, with extrusion sales shown, reopens this as WATCH.
- The swap price raised to market, or minority shareholders voting it down.
- H1 FY27 margin near 3% confirms PASS and moves the posture to AVOID.

No Role 1 valuation was run and no target price is set. The verdict decides only whether
the name earns a /step1. It is not a position.
