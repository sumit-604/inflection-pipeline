Pharma distributor roll up turns cash positive

Entero distributes pharmaceutical and MedTech goods to pharmacies, hospitals and institutions across India, booking the full value of the goods as principal revenue rather than a distribution fee. FY26 revenue was Rs6,591 Cr, up 29% on the year, with PAT of Rs146 Cr on a 2.2% net margin, a 10.3% gross margin and a 4.0% EBITDA margin. The company listed in February 2024 and has completed 51 acquisitions since inception, run from 136 warehouses across 523 districts. It reports as a single segment, so the pharma and MedTech splits are not separately audited. The shares trade around Rs1,273 for a market capitalisation of Rs5,537 Cr. This is a thin margin, working capital heavy distributor scaling on debt funded M&A.

The transition is a mix shift toward MedTech and toward a higher margin agency model. Seven of the FY26 acquisitions were MedTech (IVD, cardiology, orthopedics), taking the segment past a Rs1,000 Cr run rate and above 15% of revenue, with an aspiration toward 20% over two to three years. The commercial and agency model, where Entero acts for manufacturers rather than buying and reselling, runs at 10 to 15% of sales at roughly twice the normal distribution margin, and management frames its expansion as the main gross margin lever. FY26 gross margin rose 78bps and EBITDA margin 67bps, inside the guided MedTech contribution range, though management attributes the gains to many factors and will not isolate the MedTech piece.

The cash statement turned for the first time in FY26. Consolidated operating cash flow was positive Rs96 Cr against negative Rs77 Cr in FY25, the first positive year in a seven year series. Net operating working capital days fell to 59 in the March 2026 quarter from 70 at FY25 close. The receivables and inventory build that drove the earlier cash burn was largely acquisition led. Set against that, the FY25 annual report claimed operating cash flow turned positive in the second half of FY25, a claim the audited full year negative figure contradicts, receivables days rose from 59 to 67 while payables stretched to about 95 days, and management refused a direct analyst request for the debtor ageing beyond six months on the December 2025 call.

Gate 0 scores AVERAGE at 74 of 160, with a core score of 53 of 100. Two deal breakers cap it: seven year median ROCE of 3.85%, below the 10% line, and cumulative operating cash flow to profit of negative 1.05, below the 0.50 line. Both are driven backward, by the pre-IPO FY20 to FY23 low return years and the FY20 to FY25 working capital funded cash burn, not by the current business. FY26 shows a genuine multi metric inflection across ROCE, EBITDA margin and cash. The confirmed moat count is 5 of 12, graded STRONG, and the forward emerging moat scores 25 of 80 in the STRENGTHENING band, resting on working capital improvement and industry consolidation but on only one guided fiscal year.

Management delivered or beat five of six tracked FY26 guidance items and grades out at B for credibility. It hit the 31.5% like for like growth guide, the 4.0% EBITDA floor and the 59 day working capital target, and landed the OCF headline marginally short at Rs96 Cr against Rs100 Cr plus wording. Against that, accounting quality scores 5 of 10. Three subsidiaries carry an auditor confirmed fraud finding under CARO clause xi with Section 143(12) ADT-4 reports filed and zero narrative in the annual report, a formal audit trail exception sits in the standalone auditor report, and 83% of subsidiaries carry at least one CARO qualification. A Rs46 Cr intercompany loan was waived to the Curever Pharma subsidiary, and goodwill stands at 43% of equity across 43 acquired cash generating units.

The promoter picture is a CONCERN with real offsets. The founder chief executive is a former Alkem Laboratories chief executive with a CA, CFA and ISB background, promoter pledge is zero, the KPMG affiliate auditor was freshly reappointed for a five year term, and OrbiMed anchors a credible institutional base. Domestic institutions bought in through FY26, with ICICI Prudential taking a bulk position the same day a US fund exited, explaining the foreign to domestic ownership rotation. The countervailing weight is the unresolved subsidiary fraud findings, a pending Drugs and Cosmetics Act criminal complaint naming both promoters, and a non controlling interest step up guided to persist at 25 to 27% of pre minority profit into FY27.

Twelve peer transcripts partly confirm and partly complicate the story. Redington and RPTech corroborate Entero's claim of insulation from cross border trade shocks, since import exposed Redington reports Middle East disruption while domestic RPTech reports minimal impact. The claimed acceleration of the Indian pharma market to about 12% has zero peer corroboration; MedPlus supports only the 7 to 8% baseline. The MedTech 10% plus EBITDA margin claim has no direct peer analog, and the available thin margin comparators run below Entero's own 4%. A Redington investor day does disclose services gross margin above 20% against a 5.5 to 6% core and a 60% margin uplift from attaching services, which independently supports the logic behind Entero's agency margin premium.

The addressable market is large on a gross distribution value basis, sized at Rs2.5 lakh Cr to Rs2.9 lakh Cr with a serviceable slice of Rs1.75 lakh Cr. Entero holds about 3.8% of that serviceable market today, leaving 26.6 times revenue headroom and a MASSIVE runway class. The binding constraint is not physical capacity but working capital and debt funding: gross borrowings grew about 90% in FY26 against 29% revenue growth, and roughly Rs1,700 Cr to Rs2,500 Cr of incremental working capital funding is implied by the three year share gain path. Organic growth ran at 1.4 times the pharma market multiplier in the last two quarters, down from a historical 1.7 to 1.9 times, and management deflected the question of why on two consecutive calls.

The valuation says avoid at Rs1,273. On the operator approved basis, a 15 times forward exit multiple on FY30 owners' earnings gives a base fair value of Rs1,112, below today's price, so three years of holding compounds at minus 4% a year. The hurdle ratio comes out at 0.74 on the base case and 0.90 even on the bull case, both a stop against the 25% bar, because a stock at 48 times trailing owners' earnings has to de-rate to 15 times forward and no credible earnings path outruns that. Entry opens only in the Rs506 to Rs569 zone, roughly 55% below the current price, with a margin of safety price of Rs455. Owners' earnings do compound 33% on the base ladder and 43% on the bull, revenue is FIRING and margin is STARTING, so the eight factor scan reads plus 5 of 8, a buy candidate on the business. The price is not there. This is an investable business at an un-investable price, held on watch rather than bought on the next dip.
