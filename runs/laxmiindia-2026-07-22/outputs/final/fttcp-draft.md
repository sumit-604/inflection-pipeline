# FTTCP DRAFT — LAXMI INDIA FINANCE (LAXMIINDIA)

Company: Laxmi India Finance Ltd. Ticker LAXMIINDIA. CMP: not populated in
manifest (0.0). Run date 2026-07-22. First workup, not a refresh. Concalls
available, so this is NOT no-concall mode: all four post-IPO transcripts (Q1 to
Q4 FY26) were read for the concall stage. This is a lender, so it runs the
LENDER TRANSITION SET (AUM growth, NIM, asset quality, RoA and RoE) in place of
the four standard transitions.

One line up front. This is a small Rajasthan NBFC that grew fast and repaired
its balance sheet at the IPO, but it missed every number it promised in its
first public year, its reported profit leans on assignment gains the pipeline
could not fully reconcile, and its one critical transition, asset quality, is
going the wrong way. The forward call lands at DEEP WATCH.

Every call below is made. None is a question. Each carries a confidence tag and

   lender, which it plainly is.

   Section 1B destination PE as the secondary check (Amendment 7). Confidence:
   sure. Wrong only if Section 1B added a dedicated small-NBFC row, which it has
   not.

5. **AUM growth transition: backward FIRING, forward FIRING (+2).** AUM
   compounded 33.4% over three years (686.8 to 1,626.3 cr, IP p.7) and grew
   27.4% in FY26, both above the 20% firing line, and the growth is funded by
   documented IPO capital and low leverage (CRAR 26.12%, D/E 2.87x, huge

   wrong: FY27 AUM growth printing below 20%. Note the stricter read: growth
   decelerated from a 35-40% maiden guide to 27% and disbursements are not
   clearly accelerating, so a disciplined reader can round this to STARTING
   (+1), which drops the composite from +3 to +2. Both land in the DEEP WATCH
   family. I hold FIRING because the 20% threshold is cleared with a wide margin
   and the capital to sustain it is already raised.

6. **NIM transition: backward improving, forward STARTING (+1).** NIM rose to
   11.26% in FY26 from 10.47% in FY25 and cost of borrowing fell to 10.80% from
   11.48% on the March 2026 rating upgrade (IP p.5, p.10). I round down from
   firing to STARTING for two reasons: the FY26 NIM is flattered by roughly
   8.66 cr of upfront assignment gain booked in Q4 on a single 41 cr pool sale
   (IP p.6), so core spread is thinner than the headline, and management missed
   its own cost-of-borrowing guide (delivered 68 bps against 100 to 125 guided).
   Confidence: fairly sure. Wrong if FY27 shows NIM holding above 11% with the
   assignment-gain contribution to income falling, which would make the
   expansion core rather than one-off.

7. **Asset quality transition (the critical one): backward DETERIORATING,
   forward DECLINING (-1).** Gross NPA rose every year, 0.58% to 0.73% to 1.07%
   to 2.13% FY23 to FY26 (Note 75/93(d); IP p.11), collection efficiency fell
   from about 95% to 89%, provision coverage sits at 49.43% which is below the
   50% line, and management promised NPA improvement in three straight calls
   while it worsened each time. Confidence: fairly sure it is declining. The
   single fact that would prove it wrong: two consecutive quarters of falling
   gross NPA with the Up Money pool resolved. Read both ways honestly: the bull
   read is that 2.13% is almost entirely one pool (Up Money, roughly 19 cr,
   about 60% provisioned, under legal recovery) and that gross NPA excluding it
   is a best-in-class 0.80%; the bear read, which the numbers I used follow, is
   that the organic gross NPA was already climbing before Up Money and
   collection efficiency is slipping, so there is a softening underneath the one
   pool.

8. **Catalyst strength on asset quality: WEAK, not NONE, so the Kernex cap does
   not mechanically engage, but this is the call the whole verdict turns on.**
   There is one documented mitigant, the roughly 60% provision on the Up Money
   pool plus legal and servicer action, so I do not score the catalyst as NONE.
   But that mitigant addresses a past loss, not the forward trajectory, and the
   organic slippage and falling collection efficiency have no catalyst at all.
   Confidence: genuinely uncertain. Both readings: if the operator judges the
   provisioning to be remediation rather than a forward catalyst, catalyst
   strength is NONE and the Kernex cap engages at DEEP WATCH; the numbers I used
   score it WEAK, and the composite already lands at DEEP WATCH either way, so
   the cap changes nothing here. The single fact that resolves it: whether the
   Q1 and Q2 FY27 prints show gross NPA turning down.

9. **RoA and RoE transition: backward mixed, forward STARTING / RECOVERING

   healthy, but it is partly lifted by the same assignment gains. RoE fell to
   13.73% in FY26 from 15.63% in FY25 because the IPO roughly doubled net worth
   (257.9 to 465.5 cr), so RoE is depressed by fresh equity, not by decay, and
   management has room to re-lever from D/E 2.87x. Confidence: fairly sure it is
   recovering, not firing. The single fact that would prove it wrong: RoE
   staying flat or falling through FY27 while leverage rises, which would mean
   the capital is not earning. I do NOT credit this recovery into the Pillar 1
   ROE input (I use current ROE), to stay conservative and to keep the single-

10. **Cash / earnings-composition determination: INDETERMINATE, so the

    transition is asset quality, but the assignment and ARC accounting sits on
    top of it and the pipeline could not clear it: upfront gain on sale drove

    FY26 against 31% loan growth, and two notes disagree by about 180x on how

11. **Composite score +3, position DEEP WATCH.** AUM +2, NIM +1, asset quality

12. **Pillar 2 asset-quality multiplier: 0.80x (Stressed), not 0.65x.** Gross

## THE CATALYST STORY IN PLAIN WORDS

**AUM growth.** The catalyst is capital. The company raised roughly 165 cr of
fresh equity, deployed about 152 cr of it into onward lending exactly as
promised, and now carries CRAR of 26.12% and debt to equity of only 2.87x, so it
can grow the book 25-30% for two to three years without new equity. That is a
documented, funded catalyst, not a promise. What confirms it firing: FY27 AUM
above about 2,050 cr. What kills it: a fresh capital constraint or a credit-cost
shock that forces the company to slow lending to protect the balance sheet.

**NIM.** The catalyst is the rating. Acuite moved the company to A Stable in
March 2026, and a further notch or wider bank-line access would push incremental
borrowing below 10%. What confirms it: incremental borrowing cost crossing into
single digits with NIM holding above 11% on core spread. What kills it: the
rating stalling at A Stable and cost of borrowing plateauing above 10.5%, or the
assignment-gain contribution to income being stripped out and NIM falling with
it.

**Asset quality.** This is where the catalyst search comes up short. The one
real mitigant is the Up Money pool: about 19 cr of exposure, roughly 60%
provisioned, under legal action, with the company trying to become servicer so
it can collect directly. If that pool recovers, the headline gross NPA drops back
toward the 0.80% ex-pool level. But that is remediation of a past loss, not a
catalyst that improves the forward trend, and the forward trend has no catalyst:
the organic gross NPA was already rising before Up Money, collection efficiency
is falling, and management promised improvement three times and delivered
deterioration three times. Peers make this worse, not better. Management blamed
Rajasthan floods and heavy commercial vehicle stress for the Q2 slide, but all
four peers show flat to improving asset quality through FY26 and none names a
flood, so the excuse does not survive contact with the peer set. What confirms a
turn: gross NPA falling for two straight quarters with Up Money resolved. What
kills it: gross NPA rising past 2.13% again, or the ex-pool figure climbing.

**RoA and RoE.** The catalyst is re-leveraging. RoE is low only because the IPO
capital has not been fully levered yet, and management has room to take debt to
equity from 2.87x back toward 4x, which lifts RoE mechanically if RoA holds.
What confirms it: RoE climbing back toward 15% over four to six quarters as the
book grows. What kills it: RoA falling as the assignment-gain contribution
normalises, which would offset the leverage benefit.

Compact evidence table:

| Transition | Best catalyst | Evidence tier | Strength |
|---|---|---|---|
| AUM growth | IPO capital deployed, CRAR 26.12%, D/E 2.87x | Documented | Strong |
| NIM | Rating upgrade to A Stable, NCDs at 10.50% | Documented | Moderate |
| Asset quality | Up Money ~60% provisioned + legal/servicer action | Rating note (scanned) / claim | Weak |
| RoA / RoE | Re-leveraging headroom from D/E 2.87x | Documented | Moderate |

## THE VERDICT

The composite is +3 out of 8, which is DEEP WATCH: wait for confirmation. The
Kernex cap is a live question because the critical transition, asset quality, is
declining, but I scored its catalyst as Weak rather than None on the strength of
the Up Money provision, so the cap does not mechanically engage; if the operator
reads that provision as remediation rather than a catalyst, the cap engages and
the answer is still DEEP WATCH. The TRIM rule does not apply because not all four
transitions are firing backward. The whole call turns on one print: whether
gross NPA turns down over the next two quarters while the reported profit stops
leaning on assignment gains. If both happen, this re-rates toward a BUY-candidate
quickly; if gross NPA rises again while another assignment gain props up profit,
the DEEP WATCH hardens toward AVOID.

## THE P/E BASE CARD (for operator approval)

This is a preview of the exit multiple, not the valuation. Role 1 still runs the
full dual-track exercise in Phase 3. For a lender, P/B against sustainable ROE is
the PRIMARY method and the PE below is the cross-check.

- **Pillar 1 (ROE base).** ROE used: 13.73% (current FY26, IP p.9). Normalization
  route: NONE. The lender uses ROE, not ROCE; the post-IPO ROE dip is real but I
  do not credit a forward recovery into the base, to stay conservative given the
  grade D delivery record. Base PE = 0.5 x 13.73 + 7.5 = **14.4x**.
- **Pillar 2 (asset-quality multiplier).** **0.80x** (Stressed: gross NPA rising,
  PCR 49.43% below 60%, cost-of-borrowing/asset-quality guidance missed). Not
  0.65x, because ex-Up-Money underwriting is 0.80% gross NPA; flagged as
  INDETERMINATE with downside to 0.65x.
- **Pillar 3 (growth and moat premium).** **+1x.** Component 3a growth-visibility
  pays +0x because management delivery grade is D; 3b moat-formation pays +1x for
  the STRENGTHENING funding-cost and capitalisation moat; 3c duration pays +0x
  (a lender has no order book).
- **Strategic premium.** **+0x.** No ROE re-rating optionality credited; the
  recovery is leverage-driven and unproven, and grade D does not support it.
- **Undiscovered Alpha.** **Not applied** (listed under 12 months; FII+DII 6.72%
  above 3%).
- **Sector cap.** 18x. Not binding on the numbers below.

Destination (exit) PE, both tracks, anchored to the Section 1B lines:

- **Additive track:** (14.4 x 0.80) + 1 + 0 = **~12.5x**. UA not applied. Under
  the 18x cap.
- **RRM track:** at a cost of equity around 15%, RRM = 1 + (13.5 - 15) x 0.12 =
  0.82 (Amendment 4.4 percentage-point reading, bounded 0.70-1.60). Applied to
  the quality-adjusted base this lands around **10.5 to 11x**.
- **Primary method (P/B):** theoretical P/B = ROE / cost of equity = 13.73% / 15%
  = **~0.9x**; on a recovered ROE near 15% it is about **1.0x**. This is the
  number Phase 3 should lead with.

**Provisional-on-CMP note.** The manifest CMP and market cap are 0.0, so the
current PE and P/B, and therefore the Hurdle Ratio, cannot be computed here. The
card is provisional until the operator supplies CMP. The exit base above is
independent of CMP and stands.

**The earnings-basis question (operator decides, I do not).** One-year-forward
PE applies the multiple to next year's EPS; trailing PE applies it to the last
reported EPS. For this company the trailing EPS is flattered by the FY26
assignment gains, so a trailing multiple prices in a one-off, while a forward
multiple depends on a guidance record that is grade D. Both have a defect here.
This is the operator's call at the valuation gate, and it is recorded there.
