## BUSINESS UNDERSTANDING NARRATIVE

Two unrelated businesses sit inside one listed company. The Extrusion Machinery Division made 69.8 percent of FY26 revenue, Rs 314.89 Cr of Rs 451.05 Cr (AR FY26 Note 38 p.160-161). It designs and assembles the machines that turn plastic granules into pipe, blown film, sheet and compounds, and a pipe maker cannot make pipe without one (AR FY26 p.35-36). The Battery Division, called Geon and formerly Battrixx, made 30.2 percent of FY26 revenue, Rs 136.11 Cr (AR FY26 Note 38 p.160-161). It buys lithium ion cells from global suppliers, designs the pack, the battery management system and the housing, and assembles at Chakan near Pune, but it does not make cells (AR FY26 p.36). Extrusion customers are pipe, film and flexible packaging makers, mostly Indian, buying engineer to order machines against an advance plus milestone payments (AR FY26 p.35-36). Exports were Rs 57.52 Cr, 12.8 percent of FY26 revenue (AR FY26 Note 38 p.160-161). Battery customers are electric two and three wheeler OEMs, plus retail buyers of a direct to consumer inverter battery launched in FY26 (AR FY26 p.4-5, p.36). Concentration has already cost money: one customer was 19.11 percent of FY26 revenue, and Hero Electric, owing Rs 30.39 Cr, went into NCLT insolvency in December 2024 (AR FY26 Note 38 p.106; Note 9 p.87-88). Neither annual report gives churn, retention or qualification lock in data, so switching costs are not established.

Machine demand today rides on water infrastructure spending, and the company names Jal Jeevan Mission disbursement delays as the cause of its FY26 decline, tracked here as the downstream candidate Jal Jeevan Mission 2.0 fund disbursement pace (AR FY26 p.36). Battery demand today rides on India EV retail registrations, up 24.6 percent to 2.45 million units in FY26, tracked as the downstream candidate India EV retail registrations by category (AR FY26 p.32-33). Forward, the two markets size to Rs 6,135 Cr on the conservative basis and Rs 10,480 Cr on the realistic basis, growing about 15.9 percent a year, against a served share of 7.35 percent and 13.6 times revenue headroom. Three outside signals test that runway: Union Budget capex allocation on the Jal Jeevan Mission and PLI-ACC lines, PLI-ACC and PM E-DRIVE disbursement milestones, and the lithium carbonate and cell price index. The company is not capturing it yet, because consolidated revenue fell 5.45 percent in FY26 while both cited markets grew, and battery revenue grew 7.2 percent against the 24.6 percent industry figure printed in the same report (AR FY26 Note 38 p.160-161; p.32-33). The emerging moat scan scored 9 of 92 and classified NONE, and the machine line carries no moat: the ~40 percent market share claim appears only in the FY25 report, and gross margin fell from 38.9 percent to 35.64 percent (AR FY25 p.37; AR FY26 p.37). The battery line carries none either, because management calls its model technology agnostic and asset light, a design any entrant can copy, and the Dec 2023 deck claims of ARAI AIS-156 accreditation, an IATF approved facility and a 90 percent plus design win rate appear in neither annual report (AR FY26 p.36; Dec 2023 deck p.16, p.22). The last two scan categories agree: category 21, cannibalisation barrier, found no evidence, and category 22, regulatory tailwinds, scored weak because JJM 2.0, PM E-DRIVE and PLI-ACC reach every competitor equally.

## Machine business funds a widening battery loss

Kabra Extrusion Technik made Rs 451.05 Cr of revenue in FY26, down 5.45 percent, and lost Rs 5.37 Cr on a consolidated basis and Rs 2.44 Cr standalone (AR FY26 p.37, p.122). The 43 year old extrusion machinery business still earns: segment result Rs 50.75 Cr on Rs 314.89 Cr of segment revenue, or 16.1 percent, down from 19.3 percent (AR FY26 Note 38 p.160-161). The six year old battery business does not: segment result minus Rs 43.35 Cr on Rs 136.11 Cr of segment revenue, a loss ratio of 31.9 percent against 20.1 percent the year before (AR FY26 Note 38 p.160-161). The transition on offer is a machine tool maker turning into a battery pack maker. About Rs 250 Cr is sunk into the roughly 7 GWh Chakan plant, and management puts its revenue ceiling at Rs 1,500 Cr plus at optimal levels, which the FY26 run rate fills to about 9 percent (AR FY26 p.36, p.37). Capex has been commissioned: capital work in progress fell from Rs 50.35 Cr to Rs 12.21 Cr and depreciation rose 89.9 percent to Rs 29.57 Cr (AR FY26 p.83; Note 28 p.94).

Gate 0 scored the backward record 22 of 100 on the core and 25 of 160 grand total, and classified it AVOID. This is the backward scorecard class, not a valuation decision, and no valuation has run. Two things force it. The core score sits far below the 40 floor. Deal breaker 6 fires on its own: FY26 net debt to EBITDA of 4.19 times with interest coverage of 0.317 times. Revenue fell in 6 of the last 9 years, and FY26 is the loss year of the last three. Two lines sat just barrier side of a further deal breaker: median ROCE 10.31 percent, 0.31 percentage points above the AVERAGE cap line, and cumulative CFO to PAT of 0.543, 0.04 above the floor. Both would read worse if FY26 repeats. The cause is narrow and traceable, not a broad quality collapse. Take the battery segment out and the machine business is still profitable.

Cash is the tightest fact in the file. Standalone cash was Rs 1.97 Cr against Rs 140.92 Cr of secured short term borrowings repayable on demand (AR FY26 Notes 10 and 17). Consolidated cash flow from operations fell 79.4 percent to Rs 8.96 Cr; standalone fell 71.7 percent to Rs 12.82 Cr. Free cash flow was negative in both FY25 and FY26. The mutual fund buffer fell 57.8 percent from Rs 53.49 Cr to Rs 22.59 Cr, and bank margin money fell 86.4 percent in the same year bank guarantees nearly doubled (AR FY26 Notes 8, 10A, 41a). Working capital days rose from about 154 in FY24 to about 244 in FY26. Gross receivables shrank 8.5 percent to Rs 90.52 Cr while the ageing worsened: the over 3 year bucket nearly doubled to Rs 19.35 Cr, and half the gross book is now more than 6 months overdue (AR FY26 Note 9 p.87-88). The company raised Rs 15.30 Cr of net new secured borrowing in FY26 and paid a Rs 8.74 Cr dividend on the prior year's profit in a year it made a loss. FY26 dividend was cut to nil. CRISIL moved the long term rating twice in about 13 months, from A+/Negative to A/Negative and then to A-/Stable on 13-May-2026, with the short term rating from A1 to A2+ (AR FY26 p.51).

Management says one thing and the same document shows another. The no concall delivery check graded credibility D: 0 of 5 testable annual report promises fully delivered, 2 partial, 3 missed. Research and development spend was cut 67.1 percent in extrusion and 78.6 percent in Geon in the year the report says Geon accelerated its research (AR FY26 p.28-29). The ~40 percent market share claim was dropped with no replacement figure and no acknowledgement. The chairman's letter reports EBITDA of Rs 10 Cr while the management discussion table in the same report says Rs 13.05 Cr. The Hero Electric receivable note is still dated as at 31-March-2025 in a 170 page FY26 report. The two step downgrade appears only in the corporate governance table and is never named as a risk or a subsequent event. Accounting quality scored 4 of 10 on seven plus independently anchored numeric and label inconsistencies: a warranty provision that misses its own roll forward by Rs 10.24 Cr, a tax reconciliation that misses by Rs 0.36 Cr, three different CSR figures on one page, and a dividend per share label of Rs 3.50 where the arithmetic gives Rs 2.50. Against that, the audit opinion is clean and unqualified on both statements, CARO reports no fraud and no borrowing default, there is no going concern paragraph, related party transactions run at about 0.48 percent of revenue, and expected credit loss cover rose with the receivable deterioration rather than lagging it. Nothing found points to deliberate earnings management; it points to a close process that does not re check its own totals.

Peers do not confirm the company's story; they undercut it. Of the claims tested, none was verified, three were contradicted and five could not be tested. Rajoo Engineers, the closest direct comparator, reported an order book above Rs 200 Cr with a Rs 1,000 Cr pipeline and never once mentioned Jal Jeevan Mission across four calls, which sits badly against the company's explanation that infrastructure disbursement delays caused a 13.2 percent segment revenue decline. Rajoo's export share rose to 73 to 74 percent of first half FY25 revenue and it called exports the margin source, against the company's framing of exports as a sector wide weakness that cut its own export revenue 11.6 percent. Rajoo expected to fill its installed capacity in FY26 while the company halved capex from Rs 61.81 Cr to Rs 29.51 Cr. The caveat matters as much as the finding: the newest peer call is 22-Oct-2024, about 17 months before the FY26 close, so every contradiction tests framing plausibility, not FY26 figures. The one corpus source that does reach FY26, the Windsor Machines screening file showing sales up 72.9 percent to Rs 566.52 Cr, was never mentioned by the peer stage at all, and Windsor's own net profit stayed negative.

The market is large enough and the company is not taking it. Total addressable market sizes to Rs 6,135 Cr conservative and Rs 10,480 Cr realistic, serviceable market Rs 6,135 Cr, obtainable market Rs 677.8 Cr over three years and Rs 963.9 Cr over five, with the runway class recorded as STRONG and revenue headroom of 13.6 times. Both management market claims failed their own consistency test. The FY26 report cites an India EV battery pack market of Rs 507.5 Cr, which is smaller than Geon's own Rs 136.11 Cr of segment revenue implies at any credible share. The FY25 ~40 percent share claim back calculates to a machinery sub market of Rs 787.5 Cr, between 0.19 and 0.47 times the independent estimates of Rs 1,685 Cr to Rs 4,200 Cr. Rajoo's own quantified domestic sizing of Rs 1,500 Cr to Rs 2,000 Cr puts the company's Rs 314.89 Cr of extrusion revenue at 16 to 21 percent of the market, not 40 percent.

The promoter read came back CAUTION, on partial status because web fetches were blocked. One old adverse item exists: a February 2021 SEBI consent settlement against Ekta Anand Kabra, now Vice Chairperson and Managing Director, disgorging Rs 72,500 plus Rs 10,679 interest, settled without admission of guilt. Five senior management and key managerial exits landed within about six months, covering the CFO twice over, the Geon chief operating officer, the head of strategy and the company secretary, alongside the downgrades and the loss year. The chairman and managing director's pay rose 6.6 percent from Rs 205.02 lakh to Rs 218.64 lakh in the year the standalone result swung from a Rs 33.87 Cr profit to a Rs 2.44 Cr loss, and the report's own remuneration annexure states that increase as zero. On the other side, promoter pledge is nil across FY24 to FY26, related party dealing is small and clean, the patriarch's personal stake was wound down from 7.86 percent to zero into a family trust with the handover disclosed year by year, and a new independent director appointed in August 2025 runs a battery casings maker, an appointment that fits the pivot. A 2026 preferential issue of about Rs 120 Cr to Rs 141 Cr at Rs 375 a share to Singularity Large Value Fund III and Nitish Mittersain is media reported only, sits outside the corpus, and its extraordinary general meeting reportedly fell on 2-Sep-2026, three days before this run.

The run carried four verification priorities and closed three of them. The ~40 percent market share claim is self reported, appears once in the FY25 report at p.37 with no source and no defined denominator, was dropped from FY26 entirely, and is contradicted by the peer's own market sizing. Geon is confirmed as both the growth story and the loss carrier: revenue grew 7.2 percent, the loss widened 69.8 percent to Rs 43.35 Cr, and the division ties up Rs 311.80 Cr of net segment capital against the machine division's Rs 242.31 Cr. The FY26 consolidated loss is confirmed at Rs 5.37 Cr and sits alongside the two step CRISIL downgrade, but the fourth leg of that priority failed: the rating rationale is not in the corpus and no stage could obtain it. Borrowings, cash and working capital all moved the wrong way, with borrowings up from Rs 73.98 Cr in FY23 to Rs 145.06 Cr in FY26, cash at Rs 3.08 Cr on the screener consolidated basis, and working capital days up 90 in two years.
