### 1E Chai-stall-uncle version
Think of a courier plus a nurse-on-hire. A lab chain wants blood drawn at people's homes and carried to its lab fast and cold. It does not want to hire hundreds of nurses and riders itself. Credent hires them and charges the lab each time, or each month for a nurse posted at the lab. Credent wins when each nurse and rider stays busy. The money problem is the lab pays slowly: for every Rs 100 of work, about Rs 27 is still owed after a year (derived: 5,882.73 / 21,416.18). Credent pays salaries every month, so it borrows to bridge the gap. Four-fifths of its work comes from ten customers, so one lab leaving would hurt.

### 3D Unit economics
Per-sample revenue and cost are NOT FOUND in AR, listing deck, H1 deck or RHP. Pricing is "fixed tenure, SLA-linked" (RHP p.27) with no price per pickup or per collection given. The business has no single natural unit because 68% of revenue is deployed staff. So the unit is one deployed field head (payroll or contract), with derived proxies. Also the model's physics: revenue is price x volume per field head; cost is that head's wage plus vehicle and fuel; margin is what is left after the lab's slow payment is financed.

Volume drivers: lab outsourcing, home-collection adoption, new labs signed (2,530). Price drivers: client renegotiation at contract renewal (RHP p.27). Cost drivers: wages (largest), fuel, contract-labour mix, vendor couriers. Operating leverage: modest. The cost base is mostly people and per-trip costs that scale with volume; fixed items are 97 vehicles, 2 warehouses, 4 branch offices (AR p.11). The FY26 margin step came from mix and from other expenses falling 10.3 points of revenue (derived: 12.87% vs 23.12%), which is a one-time reclassification risk, not proven operating leverage; the reason is NOT FOUND in the documents. Key lever: utilisation of field heads, and collection days.
