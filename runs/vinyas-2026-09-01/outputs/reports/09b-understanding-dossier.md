## SECTION 2: MENTAL MODEL DECLARATION (DRAFT — PENDING OPERATOR SIGN-OFF)

### PART A — THE FROM STATE (the anchor)

**A1. Archetype.** Single business line (one reportable segment, per AR Note 34, B04). Primary archetype: Outsourcing partner (CDMO/EMS/IT services) — client concentration undisclosed, wallet share undisclosed, capacity fill and order-book based, contract stickiness via certification lock-in (B04). Secondary/transitional overlay: Build-to-spec component maker — the company is explicitly climbing from pure board-stuffing (Build-to-Print, B2P) toward design-and-test (Build-to-Spec, B2S) and full system integration (B04, citing AR "Our Objectives," p.5). The B2P-versus-B2S revenue split is not disclosed anywhere in the AR or investor presentation (B04); customer concentration is also NOT DISCLOSED (B01, B04, B05). Both gaps are named spear facts that remain unresolved in this corpus.

**A2. The simple analogy.** Vinyas is a tailor who does not design clothes. Customers bring their own patterns — circuit diagrams and parts lists — and Vinyas's job is to stitch them perfectly, on time, every time, using the right thread and fabric, except the "clothes" go into fighter jets, radars, tanks and dialysis machines, so a bad solder joint matters more than a loose stitch (B04, 1E). Most work today is build-to-print: the customer hands over the exact design. Increasingly, Vinyas is also learning to design some of these patterns itself (build-to-spec), which pays better but is a newer skill. The tailor buys most of the cloth itself, so raw material is by far the largest cost, nearly 79 paise of every FY26 revenue rupee (AR Note 20, B04). Because defence customers pay slowly and Vinyas must stock components for months before payment, the business needs a lot of cash tied up in unbilled work and warehouse stock even while it grows fast and profitably on paper (B04).

### PART B — THE TRANSITION (the model)

**B1. FROM to TO.** One line (single segment). FROM: R2 COST-ADVANTAGED CONVERTER — a sub-scale, job-shop EMS assembler whose margin comes from cost position on commodity board-stuffing work, not from price-setting power. TO (claimed): R3 VALUE-ADDED / SPEC'D SUPPLIER — a certified, spec'd-in defence/aerospace/medical electronics partner where program qualification and design capture give partial pricing power and stickier ROCE (CLAUDE.md QUALITY LADDER). Evidence for the FROM anchor: pricing power reads "moderate" and cost advantage is "not evidenced" in the current model — the material cost ratio worsened, not improved, year on year (72.98% to 78.90%, B04). Evidence for the claimed TO direction: the NADCAP AC7120 accreditation achieved in FY26, the B2S design layer, and management's stated intent to move up into system integration and box-builds (B04, B07).

**B2. The engine.** Two things must physically change to move FROM to TO: (1) the revenue mix must shift from pure build-to-print toward build-to-spec and system integration, where Vinyas captures design value rather than passing through a customer's design at commodity margins; (2) the certification stack (NADCAP AC7120, AS9100-D, ISO 13485, IATF 16949, Defence Industrial License) must convert into qualified, higher-value, harder-to-switch programs rather than sitting as unconverted capability (B04 2C, B07 A1/B2). Neither is separately quantified in the corpus: the B2P/B2S split is undisclosed, and NADCAP/JV/subsidiary claims rest on concall disclosure only, not on a filed programme-level breakdown (B04, B07 flags).

**B3. ## SECTION 3: BUSINESS UNDERSTANDING NARRATIVE

Vinyas builds electronics for other companies. It makes printed circuit board assemblies, wire harnesses, sub-systems, and full box-builds. These parts sit inside defence radios, avionics, missiles, and medical devices. The customer sends a design or a specification. Vinyas buys the components, assembles the boards, tests them, and ships. Two work types run side by side: build to print, where the customer owns the design, and build to spec, where Vinyas adds engineering. Products and services together are the whole business, and services are about 1% of sales (B04).

The customers are defence public sector units, defence primes, and aerospace and medical OEMs. HAL Korwa signed a five year agreement for electronics inside head up displays and IFF units (operator-ferried downstream note, weighed not anchored). Fresenius Kabi anchors a small medical line (B05, B07). Buying runs through long qualification cycles, so a customer cannot easily move a running defence programme to another vendor part way through its life. That lock-in is the customer relationship, though customer concentration is not disclosed for FY23 to FY26 (B01, B04, B05).

Demand today rests on an order book of about Rs 1,309 Cr, which is 2.5 times revenue, and 85% to 90% of it is defence (B04, B05). Forward drivers tie to named downstream signals: Positive Indigenisation List updates from the MoD and DDP, the Union Budget defence capital outlay, and NADCAP and PRI accreditation status for Vinyas and its rivals (B09 downstream_candidates). Growth beyond that leans on named Global A&D Tier 1 OEMs and the Israeli JV partner, on medical device OEM localisation of the Fresenius Kabi type, and on independent EMS and A&D market trackers (B09).

The competitive advantage is not spread evenly. The moat sits on qualified defence and aerospace programmes, where the NADCAP AC7120, AS9100-D, ISO 13485, IATF 16949, and Defence Industrial License bundle plus programme qualification create switching costs (B04, B07). The general build-to-print work, where the customer owns the design IP, carries little pricing power (B04). The emerging moat scan reads the forward moat as real but shallow, MODEST, and resting mostly on management claims rather than filed evidence: 13 of 23 rows are management-claim evidence, only 2 are documented (B07). The run did not establish an independent, filed basis for the NADCAP-driven programme mix, JV, or US subsidiary claims (B07 input_gaps).

## SECTION 5: PLAIN-LANGUAGE SUMMARY

1. Vinyas builds circuit boards, wire harnesses, and full electronic sub-systems for other companies' products.
2. Its customers give it a design or a specification. Vinyas buys the parts, assembles them, tests them, and ships.
3. Nearly all of FY26 revenue, 99%, comes from selling these built products. Services are about 1% and shrank in FY26.
4. Buyers are defence public sector units, defence primes, and aerospace and medical device makers.
5. Once a part is qualified into a customer's certified defence or aerospace programme, the customer cannot easily switch vendors. That is the main reason customers stay.
6. Vinyas never discloses how much revenue comes from its largest customer or its top five customers.
7. Demand today rests on an order book of Rs 1,309 Cr, about 2.5 times FY26 revenue, and 85% to 90% of it is defence work.
8. Growth beyond the current order book depends on things that can be checked from outside the company: defence budget outlays, indigenisation policy, and named accreditation and OEM approvals.
9. The moat sits on hard-to-get certifications (NADCAP, AS9100-D, ISO 13485, IATF 16949, and a Defence Industrial License) and on qualification lock-in for defence and aerospace programmes.
10. The moat does not sit on cost. Raw material cost, the company's biggest expense, actually rose as a share of revenue in FY26, not fell.
11. The mental model in one point: Vinyas is trying to climb from a low-margin, cost-driven contract assembler toward a certified, spec-qualified supplier that captures design value, and the corpus shows real certification progress but no proof yet that this shows up in real margin or cash.
12. The fragility read: seven separate things need to go right for the growth story to hold, most of them can only be checked against management's own word for now, and one single variable, whether operating cash flow turns positive, can break the whole read on its own.
13. The corpus could not establish the split between build-to-print and build-to-spec work, or the true export share of revenue, because the company has never disclosed either.
14. The corpus also could not establish a pre-listing financial baseline: no prospectus is in the file set, and the file labelled as a 2015 annual report turned out to be a five-page AGM notice with no financials.
15. The two biggest open questions: why did FY26 operating cash flow turn sharply negative in the same year profit hit a record, and why do two related-party associates worth a combined Rs 550 Cr in proposed transaction ceilings appear nowhere in the audited FY26 accounts.
