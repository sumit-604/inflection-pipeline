NEW FINDING 4 — Interest on late payment of income tax jumped 10x (sixth compliance-timing gap)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
Source: Details of Finance Cost as Restated, Annexure XXX, F-27; Statement of Tax Shelters, Annexure
XLI, F-39.

- Finance Cost note shows "Interest on Income Tax" of **₹94.47 Lakh (FY26)** vs ₹9.30 Lakh (FY25) vs
  ₹6.82 Lakh (FY24) (Annexure XXX, F-27) — a >10x jump. Cross-referenced in the Statement of Tax Shelters
  (Annexure XLI, F-39), the same amount appears under Permanent Differences as "Interest on Late payment
  of taxes" — a tax-disallowed expense confirming this is interest paid to the tax authority for LATE
  payment, not merely an accrual-timing artefact.
- 🔴 Red Flag — Pass 1 identified five distinct compliance-timing gaps (Section 185 breach, unregistered
  charges, stock-statement discrepancies, MSME late-payment interest, and CSR under-spend). This is a
  SIXTH, and it grew faster in percentage terms (10x) than any of the others in FY26: the company is now
  also paying materially and rapidly growing interest for its own late payment of income tax, in the same
  year it went public. This strengthens, rather than stands apart from, the pattern Pass 1 already
  identified as a "compliance-discipline" concern.
- Minor, related data point in the same Tax Shelters table: a new "Bonus u/s 43B" timing-difference line
  of ₹41.54 Lakh (FY26 only) — bonus payable to employees not paid within the statutory timeline and
  therefore disallowed until paid, the same late-payment-to-counterparty mechanic already seen with MSME
  dues (Annexure XLI, F-39). 🟡 Watch, additive.

NEW FINDING 5 — Company's own mandatory ratio-variance table independently corroborates, and adds to,
Pass 1's red flags
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
Source: Additional Regulatory Information as per Schedule III Para Y, Annexure XLIX item (xii), F-43
("Significant Accounting Ratios," a statutory disclosure requiring the company itself to explain any
YoY ratio variance exceeding 25%). Pass 1 did not extract this table; it independently computed
receivable/payable days manually instead.

- **Trade Receivables Turnover Ratio** fell from 5.09x (FY25) to **2.05x (FY26)**, a (59.72)% variance.
  The company's own stated reason: "The variance is due to increase in average trade receivables of the
  company." This is the company's OWN formally mandated admission of the same deterioration Pass 1 flagged
  as its #1 finding, from an entirely separate disclosure line — strong corroboration.
- **Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR) was only 0.33x in FY25**, before jumping to 5.52x in FY26 (a
  1572.73% variance, explained by the company as "increase in profitability of the company during the
  year"). 🔴 Red Flag, NEW — a sub-1x DSCR means FY25 cash generation available for debt service did not
  cover FY25 debt service obligations; Pass 1 did not identify this ratio or this year at all. Given FY26
  operating cash flow was itself negative (per Pass 1's own headline finding), the FY26 DSCR of 5.52x
  should be understood as calculated off an EBITDA/PBT-style numerator rather than actual operating cash
  flow — the two facts (negative FY26 OCF vs a nominally healthy 5.52x FY26 DSCR) are not contradictory
  once the ratio's construction is understood, but the combination is worth flagging for a methodology
  question, since a DSCR built on accrual profit can mask the cash reality Pass 1 already surfaced.
- **Net Capital Turnover Ratio** was 57.65x in FY24, collapsing (107.98)% to 4.60x in FY25 (company's
  reason: "increase in average working capital of the company"). A turnover ratio of 57.65x on such a
  small revenue base (FY24 revenue ₹938.60 Lakh) implies FY24 net working capital was close to zero (or
  the ratio is distorted by a very small denominator) — i.e., the company was operating on almost no net
  working capital cushion in FY24, before working capital expanded sharply in FY25 and then again,
  severely, in FY26 (per Pass 1's receivables finding). 🟡 Watch — new context for how thin the FY24
  working-capital base was before the FY25/FY26 blowout.
- **Current Ratio** fell from 1.99x (FY25) to 1.43x (FY26), a (28.14)% variance, company's own reason:
  "increase in current liabilities as compared to previous year" — directly corroborates Pass 1's finding
  that trade payables stretched to ~347 days in FY26 (Pass 1 Section 8), now with the company's own
  formal acknoweldgment that current liabilities, not just payables specifically, drove liquidity down.
- **Return on Equity, per Annexure XLIX**, is reported as 144.46% (FY24), 40.94% (FY25), 69.94% (FY26) —
  materially different figures from the "Return on Net Worth" of 83.88% (FY24), 22.52% (FY25), 44.83%
  (FY26) shown in the separate Accounting Ratios note (Annexure XL, F-38), for what appear to be the same
  underlying concept (profit ÷ equity base). 🟡 Watch — an unreconciled internal inconsistency between two
  different Annexures computing what should be the same or a closely related ratio, using undisclosed
  differing methodologies (e.g., average vs year-end equity base); neither note cross-references the
  other. Worth a methodology question, though this is a presentation issue rather than a cash or earnings
  concern.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
Source: Annexure XLIX item (vii), F-42, "For Financial Year 2023-24" table (a third, separate table from

  full length of the restated period covered by this RHP. This strengthens the control-weakness reading:
  every single year for which the company has ever filed a quarterly stock statement with a lender shows
  at least one quarter of material discrepancy against the books.

- FY26: Net cash from Operating Activities = **(₹1,076.29) Lakh**; Net cash used in Investing Activities

  funding round already substantially spent by year-end.
