## SECTION 1: MARKET DEFINITION

### 1A. Precise Market Boundaries

**Product scope (included):** RDSO-spec and MoRTH-spec elastomeric/rubber engineered components sold to Indian Railways and highway/bridge agencies —
- Railway: Composite Grooved Rubber Sole Plates (CGRSP) / rail pads, UIC Rubber Vestibules, Rubberized Level Crossing Pads.
- Infrastructure: Elastomeric Bridge Bearings, POT-PTFE Bearings, Spherical Bearings, Strip Seal / Compression Seal / Slab Seal Expansion Joints.

**Product scope (excluded):**
- New conveyor-belting line (RHP Objects of Issue, ₹925.56 lakh of new machinery; installed capacity 4,942.08 MT, "N/A — not yet operational" utilisation) — a different end-market (mining/cement/steel), explicitly flagged "unproven" in task scope. Treated as a SAM-expansion lever (Section 5D), not core TAM.
- Generic commercial rubber (Moulded/Long Rubber Sheets, Cow Mats, Gym Mats, Rubber Beadings, Extruder Items) — ₹516.07 lakh / 5.49% of FY25 revenue (RHP, Our Business, product-wise revenue table, p.103-104) — not railway/infra-specific, low certification barrier, effectively a different, undifferentiated market.
- Commodity natural/synthetic rubber production and trading (raw material, not finished certified component).
- Non-rubber bridge components (steel bearings, mechanical/finger joints).

**Geographic scope:** India (domestic). Company exports to 5+ countries and has incorporated a US subsidiary (Ameenji Rubber Inc., 2024, per Investor Presentation) but export revenue is not separately quantified in extracts available to this stage (NOT FOUND) and is treated as a SAM-expansion lever, not core domestic TAM.

**Customer scope:** Indian Railways / RDSO / DFCCIL / RVNL / IRCON / metro-rail corporations (railway segment); NHAI, state PWDs, highway and bridge EPC contractors, private infra developers (infrastructure segment). FY25 order-book mix as of 31-Aug-2025: ~89% Indian Railways/allied entities, ~10% infrastructure, ~1% other industrial/export (RHP Risk Factor #25, p.34).

**Channel scope:** Direct tender/empanelment supply requiring RDSO vendor approval (railway) and MoRTH source approval / client-wise qualification (infrastructure) — not a retail/distributor channel. This is a structural entry barrier (certification lead time, testing cost).

**Price segment:** Certified OEM-spec components only; excludes generic uncertified rubber goods.

**Why this definition matters:** the RHP itself states, in Basis for Issue Price (p.82-84): *"There are no listed companies in India that engage in a business similar to that of our Company... it is not possible to provide an industry comparison in relation to our Company."* This is a genuinely niche, certification-gated market with no single published India-specific market-size report (searched; NOT FOUND — see Search Log). Every number below is therefore built up from primary physical/financial anchors, not read off an industry report.

## SECTION 2: TAM ESTIMATION, MULTIPLE METHODS

### Method 1 — Top-down (outer-bound sanity check only)

Indian Railways' safety-related capex bucket for FY26 — which the source explicitly states "includes track renewal, signalling and telecom upgrades, and construction of new railway points and crossings" — is **₹1,16,514 cr** (Ministry of Railways Year-End Review 2025, published Dec-2025; corroborated by a second figure of ₹1,16,470 cr from the same review — fresh, current FY). This is the outer envelope within which ALL track-renewal materials spend (rail, sleepers, ballast, fastening/fittings including rubber pads) must sit.

No India-specific, sourced percentage breakdown of this bucket by material category (rail steel vs. concrete sleepers vs. ballast vs. fastening/elastomeric fittings) could be located (searched; NOT FOUND). Applying an illustrative, unsourced 3% share for elastomeric/rubber fastening components would imply a ₹3,495 cr ceiling — this number is **not usable as a headline estimate** because the 3% multiplier itself is not anchored to any source; it is presented only as an order-of-magnitude ceiling, confidence **LOW**, and excluded from the triangulated conservative/realistic figures below.

### Method 2 — Bottom-up (primary method, most defensible)

**Railway rail-pad sub-segment (fully sourced inputs):**
- Indian Railways broad-gauge network: **65,977 km** (Wikipedia, citing IR data; static infrastructure stock, low volatility).
- Sleeper density, plain broad-gauge track: **1,540 sleepers/km** (RDSO M+7 standard, cited via IIT Kanpur SATHEE railway-engineering reference).
- Elastomeric pads per sleeper: **2** (one per rail seat) — company product literature basis (CGRSP fitted under each rail seat).
- Annual track-renewal target: **5,000 km/yr** used (conservative end; RE target per Budget 2025-26 reporting), vs. actual pace reported at up to 7,500 km/yr in FY26 (newsonair.gov.in, 2025) — conservative bias applied, lower figure used.
- Renewal-driven pad demand = 5,000 km × 1,540 sleepers/km × 2 pads/sleeper = **15.4 million pads/year** (excludes new-line construction, gauge conversion and doubling — annual new-track-km NOT FOUND sourced, so this understates true demand; conservative).
- Realised price/unit, Ameenji FY25 Railway Rubber Pad line (from RHP Objects-of-Issue capacity table and product-revenue table): installed capacity 1,35,47,520 units/yr × 61.23% FY25 utilisation = 82,95,674 units produced; FY25 revenue for this line ₹2,890.04 lakh (RHP p.103-104) → **₹34.84/unit**.
- **National renewal-only railway rail-pad market ≈ 15.4mn units × ₹34.84 ≈ ₹53.6 cr/year.**

UIC Vestibules and Level-Crossing Pads have no independently sourced national demand driver (coach-build rate, level-crossing count — NOT FOUND) and are excluded from bottom-up quantification (this line is ~10% of Ameenji's mix; exclusion understates TAM, consistent with conservative bias).

**Scaling to full product mix:** Railway Rubber Pad is 30.73% of Ameenji's FY25 revenue (RHP product-wise table). If this mix proportion is assumed representative of the addressable market's composition nationally (assumption, flagged, MEDIUM confidence — no independent verification), implied total addressable market (railway + infrastructure) ≈ ₹53.6 cr ÷ 0.3073 ≈ **₹174.4 cr/year**.

**Sanity cross-check:** company-wide installed capacity across the four core product lines, run at 100% utilisation, would generate ≈₹180 cr revenue (see Section 3C) — of the same order of magnitude as this ₹174 cr bottom-up estimate for a fragmented market served by roughly 5 identified organised players (Section 4C). This internal consistency supports Method 2 as the anchor estimate.

### Method 3 — Peer revenue aggregation

RHP Basis for Issue Price (p.82-84) confirms: *"There are no listed companies in India that engage in a business similar to that of our Company."* Per explicit task instruction, **GRP Ltd is excluded** (reclaim-rubber/tyre sector, not a rail-components comparable) — the RHP's own silence on any peer independently corroborates this exclusion.

RDSO-vendor web search identified other organised players: Rahee Infratech Ltd (listed, but primarily a track-machinery/EPC company with GRSP as one sub-line, not a pure-play comparable), Holdwell Components Pvt Ltd, Deesawala Rubber Industries, FutureLayers — none disclose product-line revenue publicly (private/unlisted, or diversified-listed with no segment split). **Peer revenue figures: NOT FOUND.** Method 3 cannot produce a quantitative aggregate; it contributes only the qualitative finding that the organised field is fragmented across roughly 5 named players plus an unquantified regional/unorganised tail. Excluded from the triangulation average.

### Method 4 — Import substitution

Not applicable in the standard sense: Ameenji exports to 5+ countries and has set up a US subsidiary (Ameenji Rubber Inc., 2024), indicating India is a net exporter / self-sufficient in this specific certified niche rather than import-dependent. The RHP's only import statistic (~40% of India's 1.1 million-tonne Natural Rubber consumption is imported) concerns commodity raw rubber, not finished RDSO-spec components, and is not usable for this product-category TAM. No finished-component import data for elastomeric rail/bridge parts was found (searched; NOT FOUND). **Method 4 = not applicable**; the analogous lever here is export-market access (captured under Section 5D), not import substitution.

### Method 5 — Global benchmark

- Global elastomeric bridge bearings market: **$1.37bn (2024) → $2.29bn (2033), CAGR 5.8%** (bridge-bearings market research aggregator, 2024 data — approaching the 2-year staleness threshold from run date 2026-07-30; confidence lowered).
- Global POT-PTFE bearings market: **$1.2bn (2023) → $2.4bn (2032), CAGR 7.5%**; market composition roughly elastomeric 45% / POT 30% / spherical 25% (2023 data — >2 years old, **STALE**, informs direction only per staleness rule).
- Global/APAC rail fastening system market: global **$4.12-4.5bn (2024)**; **APAC $2.5bn (2024) → $2.7bn (2025) → $3.8bn (2033), CAGR ~5.8%** (2024 data, approaching staleness threshold).
- India's share of APAC rail-fastener demand is not separately published (NOT FOUND). Applying an illustrative 15-20% India allocation of the APAC figure (assumption, flagged — based on India's rail network/capex scale being meaningful but smaller than China's within APAC, not independently sourced) gives 15% × $2.5bn ≈ $375mn ≈ **₹3,125 cr** (at ~₹83.3/US$) for the full rail-**fastening-system** category (clips, plates, springs, insulators, pads combined — broader than rubber pads alone). If rubber/elastomeric pads are ~20-30% of fastening-system value (illustrative, unsourced), the rubber-pad-equivalent share ≈ **₹625-940 cr**.

This method involves two to three stacked, unsourced allocation assumptions (India-share-of-APAC, sub-component share of fastening-system value) that individually are plausible but compound into wide uncertainty. **Confidence LOW.** It is retained as a directional upper-bound cross-check, not the headline estimate.

**SAM = 90% × ₹475 Cr = ₹428 Cr.** `sam_pct_of_tam` = 428/475 ≈ **90%** (of realistic TAM; against conservative TAM of ₹175 Cr, the filter set is not separately meaningful since SAM is defined off the realistic base).

### 3B. SOM at 3 and 5 Years

- **Current SAM share:** FY25 revenue ₹94.05 Cr (RHP restated, revenue from operations ₹9,405.18 lakh) ÷ SAM ₹428 Cr = **22.0%** current share. This is plausible for a certification-gated market with only ~5 identified organised players (average share per player, if evenly split, would be ~15-20%) — passes an internal sanity check.
- **Share-gain assumption:** "aggressive" tier (+3-5pp in 3 years) applied, justified by: (a) IPO-funded modernisation of existing machinery underway, (b) large spare capacity in the bearings/vestibules lines (20-37% utilised, see 3C) giving room to win share without needing new capex, (c) no competitor exit/acquisition assumed (so capped below the >5pp tier). Applied: **+4pp over 3 years, +2pp further over years 4-5 (total +6pp by year 5)**.
- **TAM/SAM forward growth rate:** no India-specific CAGR for this niche is sourced; blended from Method 5's global bearing-market CAGRs (5.8-7.5%) plus a premium for India's faster capex growth trajectory (Union Budget 2025-26 infra capital outlay +normalising at high single digits) → **8% TAM/SAM CAGR assumed** (flagged, MEDIUM confidence).
  - SAM (yr3, 2029) = ₹428 Cr × (1.08)^3 ≈ **₹539 Cr**.
  - SAM (yr5, 2031) = ₹428 Cr × (1.08)^5 ≈ **₹629 Cr**.
- **SOM 3yr** = (22.0% + 4pp = 26.0%) × ₹539 Cr ≈ **₹140 Cr**.
- **SOM 5yr** = (22.0% + 6pp = 28.0%) × ₹629 Cr ≈ **₹176 Cr**.

**Implied revenue CAGR (arithmetic, off FY25 actual revenue ₹94.05 Cr):**
- Yr3: (140/94.05)^(1/3) − 1 = (1.489)^(0.333) − 1 ≈ **14.2%**.
- Yr5: (176/94.05)^(1/5) − 1 = (1.872)^(0.20) − 1 ≈ **13.4%**.

These sit close to the company's own historical FY23-25 revenue CAGR of 12.7% (₹74.10 Cr FY23, derived from Railway-segment ₹3,246.64 lakh at 43.82% of total per RHP Risk Factor #11, to ₹94.05 Cr FY25) — a modest, not heroic, acceleration, consistent with the "aggressive-but-not-extreme" share-gain tier chosen.

### 3C. Capacity Cross-Check (using B07 capex-embedded-growth figure, 60.4%)

**Critical finding:** the RHP's Objects-of-Issue capacity table shows **"No Change"** in installed capacity for all EXISTING core product lines (Railway Rubber Pad, Bearings/POT-PTFE/Slab Seal/LC Pads, UIC Vestibules, Expansion Joints) after the IPO-funded capex — the ₹1,668.96 lakh capex is a **modernisation** (efficiency/quality) spend for existing lines plus an entirely new **conveyor-belting** line (₹925.56 lakh of machinery, 4,942.08 MT capacity, pre-operational). The **60.4% capex-embedded-growth figure (B07) is therefore driven almost entirely by the new conveyor-belting line — a different end-market outside this TAM/SAM definition — not by expansion of core railway/infrastructure capacity.**

**Result:**
- **SOM 3yr (₹140 Cr) fits comfortably within the ₹180.1 Cr core-capacity ceiling** — ₹40 Cr / 29% headroom remaining. Capacity is **sufficient** for the 3-year SOM.

Because the committed capex plan does **not** add capacity to the core lines (it is allocated to the unrelated conveyor-belting line), any core-business growth beyond ~FY31 would require **further, currently undisclosed and uncommitted capital expenditure**.

**`capacity_check`: sufficient for SOM 3yr (₹40 Cr headroom on a ₹180 Cr core ceiling); tight for SOM 5yr (₹4 Cr / ~2% headroom) — the capex plan, not the SOM, is the optimistic-lagging side beyond year 5, since committed capital is directed at the conveyor-belting diversification rather than core-segment capacity.**

### 5B. Runway Assessment

- **Revenue headroom** = SAM ÷ current revenue = ₹428 Cr ÷ ₹94.05 Cr ≈ **4.55x**.
- **TAM growth rate (assumed):** ~8%/year (see 3B methodology; blended from global bearing-market CAGRs of 5.8-7.5% plus an India infra-capex premium; MEDIUM confidence, no direct India-specific CAGR sourced).
- **Company CAGR vs. TAM:** company FY23-25 revenue CAGR ≈ **12.7%** (₹74.10 Cr → ₹94.05 Cr) vs. assumed TAM growth of 8% — company has been **gaining share**, not merely riding the market, consistent with the SOM share-gain thesis above.
- **Years to saturate SAM at current trajectory:** solving 94.05×(1.142)^t = 428×(1.08)^t (using the SOM-implied 14.2% company growth rate against the 8% SAM growth rate) gives (1.142/1.08)^t = 4.551 → t ≈ **27 years** — a long nominal runway, but this ignores the binding constraint found in 3C: **existing core-segment capacity, not market size, becomes the effective ceiling around year 5** (SOM5yr ₹176 Cr vs. ₹180 Cr ceiling). The true near-term runway constraint is capacity, not market size.

### 5C. Runway Classification

Applying a standard headroom/growth matrix (MASSIVE: >10x headroom & >15% TAM growth; STRONG: 5-10x & 10-15%; GOOD: 3-5x & 6-10%; MODERATE: 1.5-3x & 3-6%; LIMITED: <1.5x or <3%/declining) — at **4.55x headroom** and **~8% TAM growth**, this sits squarely in the **GOOD** band (bordering STRONG on headroom alone, but capped at GOOD given the capacity ceiling identified in 3C, applying conservative bias).

**`runway_class` = GOOD.**
