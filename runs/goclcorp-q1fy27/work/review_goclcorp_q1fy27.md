## MANDATORY FINAL SECTION — PLAIN-LANGUAGE BRIEF

### 1. Summary narrative (plain language)
GOCL earned Rs 40.40 Cr consolidated net profit this quarter, down 96.7% from Rs 1,222.55 Cr a year earlier. That fall looks alarming and is not: last year's number was almost entirely a one-time Rs 1,371 Cr gain from selling Kukatpally land, which does not repeat. Stripping that out, the ongoing business made Rs 43.85 Cr of continuing profit, down 18% year on year. The more important fact is what that profit is made of. Revenue from actual operations was Rs 4.29 Cr; treasury and other income was Rs 58.84 Cr. In other words, once you remove interest, dividends and a foreign-exchange gain on a derivative, the continuing business loses money. The company has become a holding vehicle sitting on cash and land, not an operating business. Three things changed this quarter that matter more than the P&L. First, the auditors flagged, on both the standalone and consolidated accounts, that Rs 1,316 Cr of corporate guarantees to group companies (HNPCL and Hinduja Energy) were given without the related-party approvals the law requires, and that these guarantees are secured against the company's own property. Shareholders approved this after the fact in a July postal ballot. Second, the plan to merge the HNPCL power plant into GOCL was refused by the NCLT on July 30, 2026, and the company is appealing. That refusal, for now, keeps roughly Rs 6,400 Cr of external debt out of GOCL. Third, land monetisation paused: no Kukatpally land was sold this quarter, and the Rs 815 Cr Ecopolis sale to Tata Realty has taken only a Rs 1 Cr advance with no deed executed. Twenty percent of consolidated profit comes from a UK subsidiary that the main auditor did not review. There was no earnings call and no presentation, so management gave no explanation of any of this beyond the filing notes. For the position: it is HELD on momentum and governed by a daily Chandelier Exit, not by these fundamentals. Nothing here converts it to a value buy; the fundamental verdict stays AVOID, and the new governance flag makes the story worse, not better. This review informs exit framing only.

### 2. Sector intelligence
- [Notion/prior] The thesis is a group-restructuring/holding-company special situation, not a clean sector exposure: a Hinduja-group conglomerate exiting a 60-year explosives business, monetising urban land, and (attempting to) absorb a 1,040 MW thermal plant. The "sector" that matters is Indian regulated thermal power via HNPCL, where reported profits are non-cash LPSC (late-payment-surcharge) regulatory accruals and interest coverage ran below 1.0x in FY24/FY25.
- [Filing Q1FY27] The thermal-power leg did NOT enter GOCL this quarter: the NCLT refused the merger on Jul-30-2026 (ADDM item 8). The regulated-power risk (including the APTEL ~Rs 2,000 Cr capital-cost disallowance) therefore stays outside the consolidated numbers for now, pending the NCLAT appeal.
- [Filing Q1FY27] The two disclosed operating "sectors" are immaterial: Electronics Manufacturing Services (loss-making every period, result Rs (1.11) Cr this quarter) and Realty (pure lumpy land-sale events, Rs (0.05) Cr this quarter with no sale). ### 3. Business-model intelligence
- [Filing Q1FY27] The company makes almost no money from operations. Continuing revenue Rs 4.29 Cr vs Other Income Rs 52.55 Cr standalone (12.3x); operating EBITDA excluding other income is negative in every period on both standalone and consolidated bases (Q1FY27 −Rs 5.25 Cr consol, Q1FY26 −Rs 6.00 Cr consol). The model is: hold cash and land, earn treasury income, and realise lumpy one-off gains when land parcels sell.
