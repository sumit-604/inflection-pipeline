# CMS Info Systems, Business Narrative (Phase 1 Lite)

## Business Understanding Narrative

CMS Info Systems moves and manages physical cash for banks and retailers across India. Its largest line, ATM Management Solutions, is about 54% of revenue. This line replenishes ATMs, moves cash in transit, and runs brown label ATMs and banking automation for banks. Banks outsource this work because armoured fleets, vaults, and cash reconciliation are costly and risky to run in house. The second line, Retail Solutions and Currency Logistics, is about 24% of revenue and collects and carries retail cash and bullion. The third line, Technology and Payment Solutions, is about 15% of revenue and sells HAWKAI Vision AI remote monitoring, ALGO ATM software, and payment cards, mostly on recurring terms.

Customers are named banks: State Bank of India, HDFC Bank, Axis Bank, ICICI Bank, Punjab National Bank, and Hitachi Payment Services. They buy through managed services contracts that run four to seven years, which raises switching costs. Revenue concentrates in three large bank contracts, so a single renegotiation matters.

Demand today rests on cash still in use. The signals to track are RBI currency in circulation, the total India ATM count with its outsourced share, and the national cash fulfilment rate. Demand should grow as banks outsource more ATMs, as ATM penetration rises in semi urban and rural areas, and as the AGS Transact insolvency vacates share. The verifiable forward signals are the AGS Transact CIRP resolution, the SBI, ICICI, HDFC, and IPPB contract ramp, and non BFSI Vision AI tender wins. UPI transaction volume is the counter signal, because digital payment growth is the structural threat to cash volume.

Competitive advantage sits unevenly across the lines. ATM Management holds the clearest moat: a national cash in transit network with about 97% geographic coverage and a 16 year route and incident dataset across 150,000 touchpoints. Technology and Payment Solutions carries an emerging moat that is live and scaling but not yet dominant. Retail and Currency Logistics is mostly fixed fee logistics with thin differentiation, and it fell about 8% in FY26. The hardware trading slice has no moat and is pass through. The emerging moat scan scored 23, MODEST, just below the strengthening threshold.

## Cash logistics leader building a technology layer

CMS is India's largest cash logistics operator, managing about 150,000 business points with more than 40% revenue market share. It carries cash for the country's biggest banks and holds an [ICRA]AA+ (Stable)/A1+ rating with a net cash balance sheet. FY26 revenue was about Rs 2,487 Cr and PAT about Rs 303 Cr.

The transition thesis is a shift from cash logistics toward technology revenue. Technology and Payment Solutions has more than doubled its revenue share since FY22, and management guides it past 20% of services revenue by the end of FY27. Three major bank contracts signed inside 18 months feed the physical network, and HAWKAI Vision AI adds a recurring layer on top of it. The shift is real and multiply corroborated, but the core cash segment still drives most of the profit.

Gate 0 scored GOOD at 85 of 160, corrected down from 88 by a verifier. The moat score was 12 of 60, MODERATE, held down by missing peer data rather than by an absent moat. FY26 was a real step down. Post tax ROCE fell from about 25% to 16.6%. EBITDA margin eased from 26.1% to 24.1%. PAT fell 18.6%. Cash conversion stayed above 1.2x for a third straight year, with CFO to PAT at 1.28x, but free cash flow flipped negative as capex nearly tripled to about Rs 409 Cr.

The cash conversion headline sits next to a receivables problem. The 1 to 2 year overdue receivables bucket grew 8.5x on a consolidated basis and 16.2x standalone. In the same year the standalone loss allowance was released 14.1%, not built. Core Cash Management revenue fell 4.5% to 6.6% and its segment profit fell 25%. DSO rose from 116 to 126 days. One overdue customer receivable of Rs 58 Cr was converted into a three year secured term loan.

Management credibility grades B. In the August 2026 call, management cut FY27 revenue guidance and raised margin guidance to about 27% in the same breath, backed by a printed 27.2% margin quarter. CEO Rajiv Kaul holds 6.43% and bought through the private equity exit and the price fall rather than selling. Against that, two promises were dropped without comment: the 74,000 to 75,000 ATM target and the pledge to normalise DSO by March 2026. Segment reporting was retired as the cash segment weakened.

Peers cut against the receivables story. RADIANTCMS DSO improved from 70 to 65 days and SIS held 63 to 67 days across the same window CMS worsened. The receivables stress is CMS specific, not sector wide. AGSTRA's receivable drift preceded its 2025 insolvency in this same sector, which raises the stakes of the CMS trend rather than lowering them.

The market opportunity is large. TAM sits at about Rs 17,850 to Rs 27,000 Cr, revenue headroom is 9.1x, and the runway class is STRONG. SOM implied revenue CAGR of 11.7% to 12.2% sits just below management's 13% to 14% guide, so the claim is reasonable, not inflated. The spear pass read a small pond; the runway work revised that. The small pond applies mainly to ATM Management, where CMS already holds 58% to 61% of the outsourced base, while Retail and Technology carry the larger open runway.
