## PASS 3: PATTERN RE-READ FINDINGS

Genuine new material surfaced on this pass — not a "NO MATERIAL NEW FINDINGS" pass.

### 1. NEW: CARO cross-reference reveals cash losses across nearly the entire subsidiary base
🔴 Annexure "A" to the Independent Auditor's Report on the Consolidated Financial Statements
(p.162) lists 11 subsidiaries whose own standalone auditors flagged remarks under paragraph
3(xxi) of CARO 2020 in their respective standalone CARO reports: Jindal Urban Waste
Management Ltd, Quality Iron and Steel Ltd, JWIL Infra Projects Ltd, JITF Urban Waste
Management (Ferozepur) Ltd, JITF Urban Waste Management (Bathinda) Ltd, JITF Urban Waste
Management (Jalandhar) Ltd, Jindal Urban Waste Management (Bawana) Ltd, JITF Urban
Infrastructure Ltd, Jindal Urban Waste Management (Kakinada) Ltd, Jindal Urban Waste
Management (Nellore) Ltd, and Tuticorin Desal Private Ltd. **All 11 are flagged under CARO
paragraph 3(xvii) — cash losses incurred in the current and/or immediately preceding
financial year.** Two of them — JWIL Infra Projects Ltd and JITF Urban Infrastructure Ltd —
carry an additional flag under paragraph 3(ix)(e), indicating funds raised on a short-term
basis were utilised for long-term purposes. This is a materially new, primary-source-anchored
finding neither Pass 1 nor Pass 2 captured (both worked from the Notes; this is drawn from
the Auditor's Report Annexure). It quantifies, subsidiary by subsidiary, exactly what the
negative consolidated net worth and going-concern flags already implied qualitatively: the
Group's operating subsidiary base is broadly cash-loss-making, and at least two entities show
a textbook short-term-funds-for-long-term-use liquidity mismatch. (Annexure A to Independent
Auditor's Report — Consolidated, p.162.)

### 2. VERIFIED AT SOURCE: KMP loan contradiction confirmed word-for-word
🔴 Direct read of Standalone Note 18(iii), "Additional Regulatory Information — Loans or
Advances" (p.153): *"The company has not granted any loan to promoters, directors, KMPs and
the related parties (as defined under Companies Act, 2013), either severally or jointly with
any other person."* This confirms, verbatim, Pass 2's finding that this statement directly
contradicts Note 24.11.7/24.11.8 (p.147, 149), which discloses a ₹13.50 lakh loan given during
the year to Dr. Amarendra Kumar Sinha (Whole-Time Director/CEO, a disclosed KMP), with ₹10.13
lakh outstanding at year-end. This is a genuine, unreconciled, same-document contradiction
between a specific transaction note and a regulatory compliance assertion — not resolved
anywhere else in the notes reviewed. (Note 18(iii) p.153; Note 24.11.7/8 pp.147,149.)

### 3. VERIFIED AND SHARPENED: going-concern language, read at source
🔴 The Independent Auditor's Report on Consolidated Financial Statements (p.156) states, under
"Material uncertainty relating to Going concern related to Subsidiary Company": *"Attention is
invited to Note no. 42.26(a) ... in respect of JITF Water Infra (Naya Raipur) Limited
(JITFWIL/NRDA), whose auditors have drawn attention in their audit report regarding
non-extension of the Concession Agreement for Operation and Maintenance beyond 4th January,
2018 by the local authority. Therefore, it indicates that a material uncertainty exists that
may cast significant doubt on the JITFWIL's ability to continue as a going concern. Our
opinion is not modified in respect of this matter."* A separate "Emphasis of Matter" paragraph
covers JITF Urban Waste Management (Bathinda) Ltd (JUWMBL) on the same going-concern basis
(Municipal Corporation default; Bathinda arbitration; management's assessment based on
"Arbitration Award and additional support from promoters"). This confirms Pass 1's Top
Finding #2 at the primary-source level. It also bears on Pass 2 finding #11 (the "NRDA vs.
grantor" drafting-ambiguity point): the Auditor's Report itself appends "/NRDA" directly to
"JITF Water Infra (Naya Raipur) Limited," i.e. the audit report's own drafting uses NRDA
ambiguously as if interchangeable with the company name, even though NRDA more naturally
denotes the local grantor authority (Naya Raipur Development Authority) referenced elsewhere
in the notes for the same concession dispute. This is not a new risk, but it reinforces — at
the highest level of the document (the signed audit opinion, not just a note) — that the
Group's own disclosure drafting around this specific going-concern matter is imprecise enough
to create genuine ambiguity about which entity is under going-concern doubt. Substance is
clear (it is the Company/subsidiary, not the grantor) but the drafting-quality point stands.
(Auditor's Report p.156; Note 42.26(a),(d) pp.240-241.)

### 4. VERIFIED AND SHARPENED: NCI dividend is not incidental, it is the entire NCI dividend line
🔴 The Consolidated Statement of Changes in Equity (p.168) shows a single "Dividend on Equity
Share Capital" line in the Non-Controlling Interest column for FY26 of **₹(373.73) lakh** —
and this is the *only* NCI-column dividend entry in the FY26 equity roll-forward. Cross-read
against Pass 2's finding that Samarpan Infra Holdings Private Limited (a promoter-group
entity, per Note 42.14 p.225) holds the 45.40% non-controlling stake in JWIL Infra Ltd and
received a dividend of ₹373.73 lakh in FY26 (Note 42.14 p.229), this is now confirmed at the
primary-statement level: **the entire Group's non-controlling-interest cash dividend outflow
for FY26 went to a promoter-group entity.** This materially sharpens (not merely repeats)
Pass 2's finding — it is not one line among several NCI payouts, it is the whole of it.
(Statement of Changes in Equity p.168; Note 42.14 pp.225,229.)

### 5. Minor: an unexplained revenue-figure gap between two notes
🟡 Note 32/P&L "Revenue from operations" (consolidated) totals ₹2,80,802.29 lakh (P&L, p.166),
while Pass 1's Section 11 revenue-mix breakdown (EPC ₹2,20,868.51 lakh + Sale of finished
goods ₹58,482.18 lakh + other lines) summed to a stated "total operating revenue" of
₹2,79,360.50 lakh — a gap of ₹1,441.79 lakh. This is most likely other operating income (e.g.
scrap sales, EPR-certificate sales) captured within Note 32's total but not separately broken
out in the mix Pass 1 quoted; it is not flagged as a contradiction of substance, but the two
"total revenue" figures used in different sections of the notes are not visibly reconciled to
each other, which is itself a minor disclosure-presentation observation consistent with this
pass's broader pattern (see item 6, Note 29 vs Note 42.29 customer-advance mismatch, already
identified in Pass 2). (Note 32 p.187-188; P&L p.166.)

### 6. No formal "restated" prior-year figures found
🟢 No note in either the standalone or consolidated financial statements is labelled
"restated" or discloses a prior-period error correction. The only prior-year figure
adjustments identified across all three passes are ordinary reclassification-type items
already covered: the NCI stake dilution in JUISL (100%→75%, equity statement footnote "@",
p.168/178) and the FY25 discontinued-operations presentation of the Rail Freight Wagon
business (Note 42.31). Neither is a restatement in the Ind AS 8 sense.

### 7. Post-balance-sheet events: nothing beyond what Pass 2 already found
🟢 Financial statements were approved by the Board on 12-May-2026 (Note 42.39, p.257;
corroborated by the signature block dates on pp.154, 161, 167-168). No subsequent event
beyond the routine subsidiary dividend proposals already identified in Pass 2 (Note 42.37) is
disclosed.

**Net effect of Pass 3:** the pattern re-read did not overturn any Pass 1/Pass 2 finding; it
verified the two most serious governance/disclosure findings (KMP loan contradiction,
going-concern language) directly against primary source text, materially sharpened the NCI
promoter-dividend finding using the equity roll-forward, and added one significant new,
independently sourced finding (the CARO cash-loss cross-reference across nearly the entire
subsidiary base) that neither prior pass surfaced because it sits in the Auditor's Report
Annexure rather than the Notes proper.

### D. FIVE QUESTIONS FOR MANAGEMENT

1. Please reconcile the direct contradiction between Note 24.11.7/8 (a ₹13.50 lakh loan given
   during the year to Whole-Time Director/CEO Dr. Amarendra Kumar Sinha, ₹10.13 lakh
   outstanding) and Note 18(iii)'s statement that "the company has not granted any loan to
   promoters, directors, KMPs and the related parties." Which disclosure is accurate?
2. What is the quantified impairment test (discount rate, growth assumptions, sensitivity)
   supporting the standalone company's unimpaired ₹32,083.16 lakh carrying value for its
   investment in JUISL, given the consolidated Group in which that subsidiary sits reports a
   negative net worth of ₹(51,327.81) lakh?
3. What is the basis for expensing the entire ₹4,592.17 lakh JWIL Infra ESOP fair value in the
   grant year despite a stated 1-year vesting period, and how were the 14.29% volatility and
   1-year expected-life assumptions derived from unnamed "JWIL peers"?
4. Given that all 11 CARO-reported subsidiaries recorded cash losses in their own standalone
   auditor reports (paragraph 3(xvii)), what is management's plan to restore subsidiary-level
   cash generation, and what covenant headroom exists on the ₹3,94,547.85 lakh of consolidated
   borrowings, most of which are secured by promoter-pledged shares and guarantees rather than
   the Group's own balance sheet?
5. Why does loss-allowance coverage remain at just 0.12% of gross trade receivables when
   receivables overdue beyond one year nearly tripled to ~11.2% of the book this year — what
   customer-level credit assessment supports this provisioning level, and does it fully capture
   the risk embedded in the ₹51,375.89 lakh of unbilled EPC revenue?

### E. NOTES-BASED RED FLAGS

- **Front-loaded/assumption-driven earnings management risk:** the ₹4,592.17 lakh ESOP fair
  value was fully expensed in the grant year on a 1-year vesting schedule with a 40x
  strike-to-fair-value discount and unbenchmarked volatility input — technically compliant
  but the single largest swing factor in the year's results.
- **Disclosure-accuracy failure:** a verified, same-document contradiction between two
  standalone notes on whether a loan was made to a KMP.
- **Related-party role concentration:** Siddheshwari Tradex Private Limited is simultaneously
  the Group's largest related-party lender, principal guarantee/collateral provider, and a
  top-4 (11.68%) shareholder of the listed parent; a second promoter-group entity (Samarpan
  Infra Holdings) is the entire NCI-dividend recipient at the key profitable subsidiary.
- **Thin provisioning against deteriorating receivables:** 0.12% ECL coverage against a book
  where >1yr overdue exposure nearly tripled.
- **Broad, previously undisclosed cash-loss pattern:** CARO cross-reference shows cash losses
  at all 11 listed subsidiaries — invisible from the consolidated notes alone, only surfaced
  by reading the Auditor's Report Annexure against the subsidiary list.
- **Unreconciled figures across notes:** two different "advance from customer" balances (Note
  29 vs Note 42.29) with no cross-reference, and an ambiguous "NRDA" reference in going-concern
  language repeated in the signed Auditor's Report.

### F. ONE-LINE NOTES VERDICT

The notes reveal concerning accounting practices. Key concern: the Group's solvency, a
negative ₹(51,327.81) lakh consolidated net worth, 115.63% gearing, two going-concern
subsidiaries, and a CARO-confirmed cluster of cash-loss-making subsidiaries, rests on
undisclosed-scale promoter-family funding, guarantees and even minority capital, compounded
by deteriorating receivables/retention cash conversion and a verified note-level contradiction
on a KMP loan. Key strength: a genuinely conservative deferred-tax non-recognition policy, a
correctly (if aggressively timed) expensed ESOP charge rather than a capitalised or hidden
one, and a real, if partly debt-funded, improvement in absolute cash balances. Overall
accounting quality: 4/10.
