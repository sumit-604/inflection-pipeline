## SECTION 1: MARKET DEFINITION

### 1A Precise boundaries

- **Product scope (in):** proprietary digital map data licensing (2D/3D/HD/LiDAR),
  embedded automotive navigation/ADAS software (NCASE suite), enterprise APIs/SDKs/SaaS
  platforms (mGIS, Workmate, InTouch, RouteNet), government GIS/digital-twin project
  delivery, IoT fleet-telematics SaaS (fleet tracking, video telematics), and IoT hardware
  (dashcams, trackers, telematics devices) — i.e. the full scope of B04's three revenue
  streams (Map data & software licensing 66.5%, IoT SaaS 22.4%, IoT hardware 11.1%,
  Inv. Pres. slide 7).
- **Product scope (out):** satellite/earth-observation data capture (MMI licenses/uses
  such imagery but does not operate satellites), raw land-surveying/photogrammetry
  services as a standalone business (MMI's Prashant Advanced Survey LLP stake is a
  minority-stake optionality, not core revenue — B07 optionality register), drone/UAV
  hardware manufacturing (Indrones stake is a minority optionality), and pure GIS
  consulting/systems-integration work done by non-map-data-owning IT services firms.
- **Geographic scope:** India, domestic. The Terra Link Technologies (Indonesia) JV and
  the wider Southeast Asia ambition (AR p.32) are explicitly excluded from the TAM/SAM
  figures below and treated only as a SAM-expansion lever in Section 5D, per the
  definitions rule that TAM/SAM must reflect what the company can realistically serve
  today, not aspirational geography.
- **Customer scope:** automotive OEMs (4W/2W/CV/EV), enterprises (BFSI, e-commerce,
  logistics, FMCG, retail — the "~5,000 companies" universe AR cites, p.29), and
  government bodies (local/state/central — AR p.30-32). Individual retail/consumer-only
  demand is included only via the IoT hardware aftermarket (dashcams/trackers) and the
  Mappls app's monetisation-adjacent halo effect, not as a standalone consumer-subscription
  market (Mappls app itself is not separately monetised, per B04 2C).
- **Channel scope:** direct B2B/B2G contracts, OEM design-in licensing, API/SDK
  self-integration, and enterprise/government tender-based project delivery.
- **Price segment:** the full range from per-vehicle-per-module OEM licensing through
  enterprise subscription/consumption pricing to fixed-price government contracts — no
  price-tier exclusion applied.
- **Exclusions restated:** foreign global mapping providers' India-specific revenue
  (Google, HERE, Esri, TomTom) are not summed as "MMI's TAM" — they compete for the same
  demand but under the data-localisation regime cannot access the same high-resolution
  domestic data (AR p.27, "not permitted for foreign players"); this is a competitive
  dynamic within the TAM, not a scope exclusion.

### 1B Management's own TAM claim
"...projections estimating the Indian geospatial market will reach ₹1 lakh crore by 2030"
(AR FY2024-25, Management Discussion & Analysis, Industry Overview section, p.35, dated
report published for FY ended 31.03.2025). This is corroborated externally by Amitabh
Kant (IAS Board Member, L&T) at GeoSmart World 2025, cited as "India's geospatial market
to double to ₹1.06 lakh crore by 2030" from a current base of roughly ₹50,000 Cr, at a
13.45% CAGR (2022-2030) sourced to Geospatial World research (Convergence Now,
dailypioneer.com, communicationstoday.co.in — all November 2025).

**Credibility read: broad.** Management's figure is the entire national "geospatial
market" as defined by an industry body/consultant — it is not MMI's own addressable
slice (no product/geography/channel/customer filter applied by management), and it
bundles segments MMI does not participate in today (satellite/earth-observation capture,
surveying-instrument hardware, land-survey services). It is directionally credible
(externally corroborated, dated within the staleness window) but is a market-wide claim,
not a company-specific TAM — held for the Section 2 comparison below.

## SECTION 2: TAM ESTIMATION, MULTIPLE METHODS

### Method 1 — Top-down (industry report, with relevance haircut)
Base: Indian geospatial market ≈ ₹50,000 Cr (2025) → ₹1,00,000-1,06,000 Cr (2030), 13.45%
CAGR (Amitabh Kant/GeoSmart World 2025, Geospatial World research, corroborated by AR
p.35's independent ₹1 lakh Cr/2030 figure — two independently-sourced numbers converge,
raising confidence).

Subtraction: this headline "geospatial market" figure, as commonly scoped by Indian
industry bodies, bundles satellite/earth-observation data capture and analytics,
surveying-instrument manufacturing, drone/UAV hardware, and raw land-survey services —
none of which MMI monetises as a core, scaled revenue line today (all are, at most,
sub-19.8%-stake optionality per B07). Applying a 30-40% haircut for these non-relevant
sub-segments (judgement estimate, explicitly flagged as such, not a disclosed split):
- Conservative (40% haircut, 60% relevant): ₹50,000 Cr × 0.60 = **₹30,000 Cr** (2025)
- Realistic (30% haircut, 70% relevant): ₹50,000 Cr × 0.70 = **₹35,000 Cr** (2025)
2030 equivalent at the same haircut and 13.45% CAGR: ₹56,000-64,000 Cr (conservative-realistic
range), broadly a doubling by 2030.

### Method 2 — Bottom-up (unit economics, automotive sub-segment cross-check)
Addressable unit: one new vehicle sold per year in India (all categories). Total units:
25.60 Mn (FY25, SIAM data via Autocar Professional, dated 2025) — this matches the AR's own
FY28 target denominator implicitly (6 Mn vehicles at a 25% attach-rate target implies a
~24 Mn-vehicle addressable base, AR p.27-28, internally consistent with the SIAM figure).
Current penetration: 11% attach rate (AR p.27) / 3.0-3.5 Mn vehicles built-in (AR p.36;
Inv. Pres. slide 8-9).
Revenue per unit: **NOT FOUND** as a disclosed per-vehicle ARPU (flagged in B04 3D). Proxy
derived: A&M segment revenue ₹230.0 Cr (FY26, Inv. Pres. slide 8) ÷ 3.5 Mn new
vehicles built-in FY26 = **≈₹657/vehicle** blended ARPU. This proxy mixes NCASE per-module
licensing with fleet/logistics revenue booked in the same A&M line, so it is a rough
blend, not a clean per-module figure — flagged as lower confidence.
Full-penetration (100% attach, all 25.6 Mn vehicles/year) automotive-only TAM = 25.6 Mn ×
₹657 = **≈₹16,800 Cr**. This is an automotive-only subset, not a standalone TAM estimate —
used as an internal cross-check: it represents ~48-56% of Method 1's ₹30,000-35,000 Cr
total, closely matching MMI's own Automotive & Mobility revenue share (48.5% of FY26
revenue, Inv. Pres. slide 8) — a reasonable internal-consistency signal, not proof of
accuracy given the ARPU proxy's weakness.

### Method 3 — Peer/segment aggregation (external market-research reports)
Given B04/B07's confirmed gap — no named competitors disclosed in the AR or investor
presentation — a classic "sum of named competitors + unorganised estimate" cannot be
built from company documents. Instead, two independently-published, distinctly-scoped
external market reports are summed as non-overlapping segments matching MMI's business
model:
- India Location-Based Services market: **USD 1.89 Bn (2025)** → USD 4.61 Bn (2030),
  19.52% CAGR (Mordor Intelligence, "India Location-based Services Market," report dated
  2025). At ≈₹95.5/USD (spot rate 13-Jul-2026, per Federal Reserve H.10/x-rates data) =
  **≈₹18,050 Cr**. This covers the map-data/enterprise-API/consumer-location layer,
  closely matching MMI's Map-led licensing stream (66.5% of revenue).
- India Fleet Telematics Platforms market: **USD 1.68 Bn (2025)** → USD 4 Bn (2032)
  (MarkNtel Advisors, "Fleet Telematics Platforms Market — India," dated 2025). At
  ₹95.5/USD = **≈₹16,050 Cr**. This matches MMI's IoT-led (SaaS + hardware) stream (33.5%
  of revenue).
Sum: ₹18,050 + ₹16,050 = **≈₹34,100 Cr (2025)**.
(A third, more narrowly-scoped "India Digital Map Market" figure — USD 726 Mn 2025,
≈₹6,930 Cr — was also found via search aggregation but the primary publisher could not be
cleanly identified from the search result set; it is noted here for completeness but
excluded from the headline sum given the attribution uncertainty — flagged, not used.)

### Method 4 — Import substitution
Not separately quantified as a Rs Cr method. The National Geospatial Policy 2022's
data-localisation clause functions as a de facto domestic-reservation mechanism — foreign
players are "not permitted" access to high-resolution India geospatial data (AR p.27) —
which is conceptually the inverse of classic import substitution (a policy-created
domestic-share floor rather than a measured consumption/production/import statistic). No
consumption-vs-domestic-production Rs Cr breakdown for geospatial data specifically was
found in the AR, investor presentation, or web search. Treated qualitatively as a growth
driver (Section 4A, R1) rather than forced into a numeric method.

### Method 5 — Global benchmark (per-capita, directional only)
Global geospatial analytics market: USD 93.5-103.75 Bn (2025) (Mordor Intelligence /
Fortune Business Insights / GII Research, all dated 2025-2026). India's share of this
global market: **4.5%** (Grand View Research, "India Geospatial Analytics Market," dated
2025), i.e. ≈USD 4.2-4.7 Bn against a ~17.7% share of world population (India ≈1.45 Bn of
≈8.2 Bn global). Global per-capita spend ≈USD 11.5-12.8; India per-capita spend
≈USD 2.9-3.2 — India runs at roughly a quarter of the global per-capita intensity. If
India reached global-average per-capita intensity, the implied full geospatial-analytics
market would be ≈USD 17-18.5 Bn ≈**₹1,62,000-1,77,000 Cr** — a theoretical ceiling, not a
3-5 year SOM input, flagged as directional (scope is "geospatial analytics" globally,
broader than MMI's product set, and a global per-capita convergence assumption is a
multi-decade proposition, not a near-term one). Used only to illustrate long-run headroom
in Section 4, not fed into the conservative/realistic TAM figures below.

| Method | Estimate (current, ~2025-26) | Confidence | Staleness |
|---|---|---|---|

| 2. Bottom-up (automotive unit economics) | ₹16,800 Cr (auto-only subset, cross-check) | Low (ARPU is a derived proxy, NOT FOUND disclosed) | Not stale |
| 3. Peer/segment aggregation (LBS + fleet telematics reports) | ₹34,100 Cr | Medium | Not stale (2025 sources) |

| 5. Global per-capita benchmark | ₹1,62,000-1,77,000 Cr (theoretical ceiling) | Low (scope mismatch, directional only) | Not stale, but definitional mismatch |

Methods 1 and 3 converge closely (₹30,000-35,000 Cr vs ₹34,100 Cr) despite being built from
entirely independent sources (a national industry-body projection vs two segment-specific
commercial market reports) — this convergence is the primary basis for the headline
figures below. Method 2 is materially lower because it only covers the automotive
sub-segment, consistent with automotive being roughly half of MMI's revenue mix — not a
divergence to be averaged away, but an expected subset relationship, explained rather than
blended. Method 5 is excluded from the headline range on scope-mismatch grounds, per the
"flag rather than average silently" rule.

**Conservative TAM (current) = ₹30,000 Cr. Realistic TAM (current) = ₹34,000-35,000 Cr.**

**Management's claim vs conservative estimate:** management's ₹1,00,000 Cr (2030,
undiluted whole-market figure, AR p.35) compared against this stage's conservative TAM
grown to 2030 at the same 13.45% CAGR (₹30,000 Cr × 1.1345⁵ ≈ ₹56,400 Cr) gives a ratio of
**1.77x**. This sits between the "reasonable" (≤1.5x) and "likely inflated" (>2x) bands.
Read: **moderately optimistic, not clearly inflated** — the gap is structurally expected
because management's figure is the entire undiluted national geospatial market (including
segments MMI doesn't serve), while this stage's conservative estimate already nets those
out; it is not evidence of deliberate TAM inflation by management, but it does mean
management's own market-size framing should not be read as "MMI's addressable market"
without the haircut applied here.

**SAM ≈ ₹18,590 Cr (≈62% of conservative TAM).**

### 3B SOM at 3 and 5 years

Current SAM share = FY26 revenue ₹474.1 Cr ÷ SAM ₹18,590 Cr = **2.55%**.

Share-gain rules applied: given (a) the FY26 execution stumble (revenue growth fell to
+2.3% vs a 24% 5-year IPO-to-date CAGR, government-order deferrals across three
consecutive quarters — B07 F2, a flagged adverse signal that must propagate here, not
resolve upward), but (b) genuine signed-contract catalysts in the pipeline (Survey of
India national platform, IOCL ₹110 Cr/5-year IoT contract, DMRC MoU — B07 H2, Strong) and
(c) the market is actively formalising post-NGP-2022 liberalisation (the "faster possible
where unorganised share >40% is formalising" condition plausibly applies to a market this
young in its liberalised form), the trajectory used sits at the boundary of normal and
aggressive, not deep into the aggressive band:

- **3-year SOM share:** current 2.55% + 1.45pp gain (within the 1-2pp "normal" band, tempered by the FY26 stumble) = **4.0% of SAM**
  SOM 3yr = 4.0% × ₹18,590 Cr = **₹744 Cr**
- **5-year SOM share:** current 2.55% + 3.0pp cumulative gain (at the boundary of the 3-5pp "aggressive" band, justified only by the multiple *signed* — not merely announced — catalysts reaching maturity in the 24-36m+ window per B07's own moat-evolution timeline) = **5.5% of SAM**
  SOM 5yr = 5.5% × ₹18,590 Cr = **₹1,022 Cr**

**Implied revenue CAGR, arithmetic shown:**
- 3yr: (₹744 / ₹474.1)^(1/3) − 1 = (1.5694)^0.333 − 1 = **16.2%**
- 5yr: (₹1,022 / ₹474.1)^(1/5) − 1 = (2.1558)^0.20 − 1 = **16.6%**

Both paths cluster at ~16-17% — well below the 24% 5-year historical CAGR and dramatically
below the ~45% CAGR management's FY28 ₹1,000 Cr target implies over the two years FY26→FY28
((1000/474.1)^(1/2) − 1 = 45.3%). This gap is the formal cross-check handed to Stage 11.

### 3C Capacity cross-check
B07's capex-embedded-growth figure: `capex_embedded_growth_pct = 40.3%`, derived as
FY26 capex ₹42.40 Cr × fixed-asset-turnover 4.50x = ₹190.8 Cr implied incremental revenue,
i.e. an implied near-term capacity ceiling of ₹474.1 + ₹190.8 = **≈₹665 Cr** (B07 Section
2C, itself flagged there as a mechanical FAT-multiplier proxy, likely distorted upward by
contract-specific IoT hardware capex that is already substantially locked into signed
orders rather than general-purpose capacity).

Comparing to this stage's SOM 3yr (₹744 Cr): a gap of **₹744 − ₹665 = ₹79 Cr**, i.e. the
3-year SOM sits ≈12% above the capex-embedded ceiling. **Read: the SOM is the modestly
optimistic side of the two, not the capex plan** — but the gap is small and closeable: MMI
holds ₹685 Cr of net cash/treasury (Inv. Pres. slide 15) available for further capex
deployment without external financing, and B07's own capex proxy is flagged as likely
understating true capacity for a business with near-zero marginal cost on its core
map-licensing line (₹31.1 Cr PP&E against ₹941.8 Cr total assets, AR p.38 — this is not a
capacity-constrained manufacturing business in the way the FAT-multiplier method assumes).
**capacity_check: gap of ₹79 Cr, SOM modestly optimistic, closeable from existing treasury.**

### 5B Runway assessment
- **Revenue headroom = SAM ÷ current revenue** = ₹18,590 / ₹474.1 = **39.2x**
- **TAM growth rate** = 13.45% CAGR to 2030 (Geospatial World/GeoSmart World 2025 data,
  corroborated by AR p.35)
- **Company CAGR vs TAM:** MMI's 5-year IPO-to-date CAGR is 24% (Q4 FY26 concall, per B07)
  — well above the 13.45% TAM growth rate, i.e. historically **gaining share**. But the
  single most recent year (FY26, +2.3%) fell *below* TAM growth — a real, recent
  deceleration that must be weighed, not smoothed away, alongside the multi-year trend.
- **Years to saturate SAM at current growth:** highly sensitive to which growth rate
  persists — at the FY26 actual rate (2.3%) saturation would not occur within any
  realistic horizon (>100 years, i.e. the question is moot at that pace); at the 5-year
  historical rate (24%) it is ≈17 years; at the SOM-implied rate (≈16%) it is ≈25 years.
  All scenarios point to **decades**, not years, of headroom at the SAM level.

### 5C Runway classification
**runway_class: MASSIVE** — revenue headroom of ≈39x against a double-digit-growing TAM
(13.45% CAGR) with multiple independently-evidenced structural tailwinds (regulatory
formalisation, EV/connected-car proliferation, AI-product monetisation of the existing
data asset, signed government-platform contracts). This classification describes
market-size headroom, not the probability of capturing it quickly — the SOM already
discounts for execution (Section 3B), and the FY26 execution stumble (B07 F2, adverse,
propagated here rather than resolved upward) is the reason SOM captures only 4.0-5.5% of
SAM by year 3-5, not a reason to downgrade the market-size classification itself.
