## Business Understanding Narrative

**What does it do, and how does it make money?** Metalic Technoforge forges and
machines metal components at Veraval (Shapar), Rajkot, Gujarat. It sells to
domestic and global original equipment manufacturers across automotive and
non-automotive industries, and exports to Germany, Finland, the USA, Italy,
China and Turkey (PROSPECTUS, FY2027, Q2, page 321). It employs 191 people. It
was incorporated in 2016 and listed this quarter.

**What is changing?** Scale and mix, both fast. Revenue went from Rs 50.85 crore
in FY24 to Rs 74.37 crore in FY25 to Rs 95.55 crore in FY26. Exports went from
Rs 9.44 crore to Rs 28.05 crore to Rs 33.82 crore across the same three years,
so from 18.6% of revenue to 35.4% (PROSPECTUS, FY2027, Q2, page 287).

**Why now?** Profit is compounding faster than revenue. Restated profit after
tax went from Rs 4.26 crore in FY24 to Rs 9.03 crore in FY25 to Rs 12.36 crore
in FY26 (RHP, FY2027, Q2, page 269). That is 70% a year on 37% revenue growth.
Operating leverage is visible and it is the opposite of what Tirupati Forge, its
own named peer, shows.

**What must be true for the thesis to work?** The export share must keep rising
and the customers must stay. Exports nearly doubled margin contribution, and the
company has no long-term contract with anyone.

**What breaks it?** Customer concentration without contracts. The top customer
is 22.94% of FY26 revenue and the top five are 45.35%, and the company's own
risk factor says the "absence of long-term contractual arrangements with such
customers exposes us to customer concentration, demand volatility and credit
risks" (RHP, FY2027, Q2, page 37).

## 2. Business model and archetype

One engine: buy steel, forge and machine it, sell the component. Revenue is
Rs 95.55 crore in FY26, of which sale of goods is Rs 93.57 crore and sale of
services Rs 1.98 crore (PROSPECTUS, FY2027, Q2, page 287). Domestic goods are Rs
59.75 crore and exports Rs 33.82 crore.

The company's own peer table is the most useful document in this corpus, because
it puts Metalic against three named competitors on identical definitions for
FY24 (RHP, FY2027, Q2, page 140):

| FY24 | Metalic | Amic Forging | Tirupati Forge | Paramount Speciality |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| EBITDA margin | 14.34% | 13.07% | 11.62% | 12.54% |
| PAT margin | 8.39% | 10.97% | 6.04% | 5.26% |
| Return on equity | **76.27%** | 32.73% | 15.42% | 26.77% |
| Return on capital employed | **37.01%** | 27.84% | 16.84% | 19.78% |
| Debt-equity | 1.40 | 0.06 | 0.22 | 1.09 |
| Current ratio | 1.00 | 1.97 | 2.22 | 1.20 |

Metalic had the best margin and by far the best returns, and it got there on the
most leverage and the thinnest liquidity in the group. A current ratio of exactly
1.00 and debt-equity of 1.40 is how a 76% return on equity is manufactured.

**Archetype: build-to-spec component maker**, with a converter slice. Customer
capex cycle, design-win pipeline, content per unit and input pass-through bind.

## 3. Competitive advantages

Forming, on the evidence available, and the evidence is the issuer's.

The return profile is the argument. Sustaining 37% return on capital employed
while growing revenue 37% a year is not what a pure price-taker does. Something
about the product mix, the machining content or the export qualification is
earning above the cost of capital.

The counter-argument is leverage. Strip debt-equity of 1.40 back to the peer
median and the return on equity falls a long way. High ROE on high gearing is a
financing outcome as much as an operating one.

The company names no patent, no exclusivity, no long-term contract and no
switching cost. What it has is export qualification with German, Finnish, US and
Italian OEMs, which takes years to earn. Call it **forming, resting on
qualification**, and note that the durability test cannot be run on a company
with no post-listing quarter.

## 4. The promoters

Five individual promoters, all operators rather than financiers. Gajipara Keyur
Dhirajlal is Chairman and Managing Director. The Prospectus states that the
promoters "have experience of more than nine years each in the business of
manufacturing forged and machined metal components", and that promoters and
senior management personnel oversee forging, machining, quality control, human
resources, finance and administration directly (PROSPECTUS, FY2027, Q2, page
321).

Pre-issue holding is precise (RHP, FY2027, Q2, page 108): Gajipara Ronakkumar
Mansukhbhai 15.06%, Trambadiya Dhaval Vrajlal 12.63%, Vadodariya Satish
Rameshbhai 9.72%, Kapadiya Vipul K 9.72%, Rupapara Jay Rameshbhai 9.72%, total
promoter group 83.56%, and 83.62% with Rameshbhai Ukabhai Rupapara.

Post-issue, "our Promoters and Promoter Group is expected to hold 61.00% of the
Post Issue Equity Share Capital" (PROSPECTUS, FY2027, Q2, page 61). The
per-person post-issue table is left blank in the document read.

**This is the Rule G building ledger, and it is favourable.** Five working
promoters built a Rs 95 crore export-capable forging business from 2016, funded
it with debt at 1.40 debt-equity, earned 37% on capital employed, and only then
sold shares to the public. They deployed under constraint and delivered. No
pledge is disclosed in what was read. The ledger does not lift any cap the
concentration risk imposes, and 61% post-issue promoter control with five
holders is itself concentrated.

## 6. The transition and the quality ladder

FROM R2 COST-ADVANTAGED CONVERTER. TO R3 VALUE-ADDED / SPEC'D SUPPLIER.

The mechanism is the export mix. Domestic forging sold to Indian OEMs on price
is R2, and the 14.34% EBITDA margin against peers at 11.6% to 13.1% says Metalic
holds a cost position rather than a price position. Exports to German, Finnish
and Italian OEMs require qualification, drawing conformity and traceability, and
they doubled their revenue share in two years. That is the R3 mechanism.

Management makes no quoted transition claim in this corpus. The offer documents
state strategy as "expanding our business and geographical footprint, focusing
on enhancing our brand image and purchase of machinery" (RHP, FY2027, Q2, page
60 series). That is a capital-deployment plan, not a rung claim.

One rung, consistent with the base rate. The rung is inferred from the mix data,
not asserted by the company, and it is therefore untested.

## 11. Posture: the Transition Decision Matrix

- **Proof gate: NOT FIRED.** Not because the proof failed, but because no
  post-listing period exists to fire it. The historical record is strong; the
  gate is untested.
- **Ugliness: ARTIFACT-OF-CLIMB.** The ugly facts are high gearing and a current
  ratio of 1.00. A company compounding revenue at 37% and funding it with debt
  because it had no equity access until July 2026 looks exactly like this. The
  IPO proceeds are the resolution, which is the definition of a climb artifact.
- **Recognition gap: OPEN, with a caveat on the arithmetic.** Market
  capitalisation of Rs 355.93 crore against FY26 restated PAT of Rs 12.36 crore
  is 28.8 times. But the market capitalisation reflects the post-issue share
  count and the profit is pre-issue, so the true comparison is flattered. The
  fresh-issue shares raised money the FY26 profit never had. On the ladder, R2
  sits near 15-17x and R3 near 19x, so 28.8x on an unadjusted basis already
  prices the R3 destination.

**Posture: RESEARCH / WATCH.** Proof not fired, ugliness an artifact, gap open
on the unadjusted number. The framework reads this as narrative until the gate
fires. Watch the gate.

## 12. Verdict card

**WATCH, upgrading to PROCEED on the first post-listing result.**

The history is the best in this run on returns: three years of profit
compounding faster than revenue, 37% return on capital employed, an export mix
that doubled, five working promoters who built it under constraint. The reason
this is not a PROCEED today is arithmetic, not judgement. There is no
post-listing quarter, no current balance sheet and no independent credit view,
so a full `/step1` would be run entirely on the issuer's own offer document.

Load-bearing facts a deep run must verify first:
1. **The first post-listing result** (Q2 FY27, due around November 2026).
   Whether the 37% growth rate survived listing is the whole question.
2. **The FY26 balance sheet**: current ratio, debt-equity and working-capital
   days after the issue. FY24's current ratio of 1.00 must be re-measured.
3. **Customer concentration at FY27**, and whether any long-term contract now
   exists. 45.35% in five names with no contracts is the standing risk.
4. **The use of issue proceeds** against the stated machinery purchase, and the
   monitoring agency report if one is appointed.
5. **The operator's revenue figure**, which conflicts with the filed Rs 95.55
   crore and should be reconciled before anything else.

What would change the view to PASS. A first post-listing quarter showing margin
compression, or a current ratio still at or below 1.00 after the issue, which
would say the growth was funded rather than earned.

No price. No position.

## 13. Forward view

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| FY26A restated PAT | Rs 12.36 crore |
| FY27E PAT | Rs 16.1 crore to Rs 17.3 crore |
| Growth on FY26 | +30% to +40% |
| Market capitalisation | Rs 355.93 crore |
| **Forward PE** | **20.6x to 22.1x** |
| **Basis tier** | **C — Trend** |

Derivation. Bull AI holds no management guidance records for this company and
the offer documents state no forward number, so tiers A and B are both
unavailable: there is no guided figure and no post-listing quarter to annualise.

The tier C basis is the delivered multi-year rate with a mechanism behind it.
PAT compounded from Rs 4.26 crore in FY24 to Rs 12.36 crore in FY26, a two-year
compound rate of 70.3%. That rate is not carried forward, because FY26 growth
had already decelerated to 37%. The estimate holds FY27 growth at 30% to 40%,
bracketing the most recent delivered year. The mechanism is the rising export
mix and the machinery purchased with issue proceeds.

Market capitalisation is the Bull AI company record at 22 September 2026, a
service record, not a filed document.

**The warning this step exists to carry.** Tier C is the weakest usable basis
and this estimate is a trend line, not a plan. Two specific distortions apply.
First, the forward PE compares a post-issue market capitalisation with an
earnings estimate grown off a pre-issue equity base, so it understates the
multiple per share; the fresh issue adds shares without adding a full year of
the profit they fund. Second, a company with a current ratio of 1.00 and
customer concentration of 45% in five unconcracted names can lose a year of
growth to one customer's inventory correction. Read this step with steps 8, 9
and 11, never instead of them.
