## Business Understanding Narrative

**What does it do, and how does it make money?** DelaPlex is an offshore IT services and supply chain consulting firm. It has 350+ associates in four Indian cities and sells mainly into the United States (DECK-JUL26, p.12, p.14; RHP, p.133, issuer claim). The money comes from billing engineer time. Most of that time is billed to delaPlex INC, the US company that owns 34.34% of DelaPlex Ltd (AR25, p.135). DelaPlex invoices it "on Cost Plus Model as per regulations of transfer pricing" (AR25, p.24). The August 2025 AGM notice put that one customer at about 60% of FY25 consolidated turnover (AR25, p.25). So the Indian listed company is, in large part, the captive delivery arm of a US front end owned by the promoter's family. delaPlex INC is 60% owned by QualiPlex Software LLC and 40% by Alcantara Holdings LLC (RHP, p.167). Manish Sachdeva, the MD's brother, lists QualiPlex as his interest (RHP, p.166).

**What is changing?** Management says it is moving from staff augmentation to products and owned infrastructure. It bought Blueberry Systems in the UK through a new subsidiary (from 13-Apr-2024) and 70% of Celestia Crew in Bengaluru (AR25, p.68, p.69). It launched Micro Data Centres (MDC) with BSNL in June 2025 (PR-FEB26, p.2). Domestic sales rose to 38.7% of revenue in FY26, up 49.3% (DECK-JUL26, p.8). In H1 FY26 the new "solutioning" line was 16% of revenue (PR-NOV25, p.3).

**Why now?** The stock sits at Rs 173, below the Rs 192 IPO price, at 12.3x trailing earnings (MKT, service record). (Fresh issue Rs 34.56 crore for 18,00,000 shares, CLAR-APR26, p.19.) The price ran from a 52 week low of Rs 80 to a high of Rs 180, and NSE surveillance asked about it on 14-Oct-2026 (PRICE-OCT26, p.2). Two weeks earlier the UK subsidiary bought Bisham Ltd, a WMS consultancy (BISHAM, p.1).

**What must be true for the thesis to work?** Direct revenue (domestic, UK, MDC) must grow faster than the parent channel shrinks, at a margin no worse than the cost plus channel. SME issuers report half yearly, so this card works on a two half year proof test.

**What breaks it?** The parent channel. If delaPlex INC buys less, standalone revenue falls and no rate card protects it. FY26 standalone profit after tax already fell 8.6% (RES26, p.6).

## 2. Business model and archetype

One reported segment, "IT & Business Consultancy" (RES26, p.16). The H1 FY26 mix was supply chain 43%, software development and cloud 36%, solutioning 16%, other 5% (PR-NOV25, p.3).

Three revenue pools sit under that one segment.
- The parent channel: standalone billing to delaPlex INC at cost plus. The prospectus showed related parties (delaPlex INC plus Xperity LLC) at 83.35% of H1 FY24 revenue, 4,230.33 of 5,400.46 in FY23 (RHP, p.35). The top five customers were 99.37% (RHP, p.31).
- The UK subsidiary: FY26 revenue 1,694.43 and profit after tax 322.54, against 1,439.63 and 201.04 in FY25 (RES26, p.12; AR25, p.69).
- Domestic: BSNL MDC work, the Hyderabad consulting division, Celestia. 38.7% of FY26 revenue (DECK-JUL26, p.8).

The margin engine in the parent channel is the transfer pricing mark up. Price is set by a related party agreement, not by a market.

**Archetype: Outsourcing partner.** Client concentration and contract stickiness dominate, with the twist that the main client is a related party.

## 3. Competitive advantages

Absent in the parent channel, forming at best elsewhere. A captive cost plus supplier earns what the transfer pricing report allows. The prospectus calls the parent arrangement an "exclusivity agreement" (RHP, p.32, issuer claim), which protects volume but caps margin. Partner tiers with Blue Yonder, Microsoft, Oracle and AWS are memberships, not moats (DECK-JUL26, p.33). The MDC line has a channel edge: BSNL fronts the sale and holds the exchange real estate (CALL, p.9). Contract values are NOT FOUND. The Blueberry low code platform is the one owned IP named, and its revenue is not split out (CALL, p.14).

## 4. The promoters

Nitin Sachdeva, MD, a chartered accountant, director since 2004 (RHP, p.153). His wife Preeti Sachdeva is whole time director. His brother Manish Sachdeva is non executive director and CEO of delaPlex INC (DECK-JUL26, p.17).

Holdings: promoter group 67,64,000 shares at 31-Mar-2026, no encumbrance during FY26 (PROM-31(4), filings/team_sandeshc_07052026121013_45.pdf, p.3). That is 74.25% of 91,10,000 shares. Nitin Sachdeva bought 6,000 shares on the open market on 12-Mar-2026, taking him to 36,23,895 shares, 39.78% (SAST-MAR26, p.4). delaPlex INC holds 31,28,100, 34.34% (AR25, p.135). It sold 6,00,000 shares in the IPO offer for sale (RHP, p.76).

Related party dependence is the whole question. Rent flows to Nitin Sachdeva, his HUF and Manish Sachdeva; sister concern Delastream is a supplier (AR25, p.147).

Skin in the game is real: 39.78% direct plus the parent's 34.34%. The conflict is real too: the parent's owners sit on both sides of the main contract.

## 6. The transition and the quality ladder

FROM R2, cost advantaged converter: margin comes from India cost position under a cost plus contract, with mid teens operating returns. TO R3, value added supplier, claimed through productised IP, MDC infrastructure and direct client ownership. The claim in management's words: "we have pivoted and there is a plan to pivot to products" (CALL, p.15).

The mechanism has three legs. Direct domestic revenue, UK direct accounts, and owned MDC capacity sold through BSNL. Management targeted MDC at "34% to 40% of the EBITDA... in the short run" (CALL, p.14). No rupee number backs it.

## 8. Proof check

The triggers claimed H2 FY26 would carry the year as MDC proofs of concept converted (CALL, p.6). The two half years say the opposite. H2 FY26 revenue was 3,171.69 against 3,717.75 in H1, and 46.0% of the year (RES26, p.14). H2 FY25 was 46.8% of its year too. The prospectus itself said "Our business is not seasonal in nature" (RHP, p.220).

The proof point is absent. One positive sits beside it: domestic share went from 26.42% to 38.7% in a year (DECK-NOV25, p.35; DECK-JUL26, p.8). That is a mix shift. It has not yet shown up as growth or margin.

## 9. Flags from the documents

- **NSE sought clarification on results twice.** On 4-Jul-2025 the company admitted FY25 results filed on 30-May-2025 were "inadvertently not in the format prescribed" and refiled them (RES25, p.1). On 13-Apr-2026 it answered three NSE queries on the H1 FY26 results: format and missing disclosure of IPO fund use; an XBRL error that left out EPS, including consolidated EPS; and missing Schedule III comparatives, including the cash flow (CLAR-APR26, pp.1-2). The revised H1 FY26 numbers match the November filing (CLAR-APR26, p.12). The reply cites "Ind AS", yet the results are prepared under AS 17 and AS 21 (RES-H1, p.7, p.12). Two refilings in ten months point to weak reporting control, not to restated numbers.
- **Undisclosed parent revenue.** The FY25 related party note lists sales to Blueberry (287.91) but gives no rupee figure for sales to delaPlex INC or Xperity (AR25, pp.147-148). Only the AGM notice's "~ 60%" exists (AR25, p.25).
- **Related party receivables.** At 30-Sep-2025, 1,273.00 of 2,607.10 standalone receivables were due from related entities, including the US promoter company (RES-H1, p.6).
- **Managerial pay above the limit.** The FY25 report states remuneration was paid "in excess of the limit as stipulated under Section 197" (AR25, p.114). The MD drew 77.00 (AR25, p.113). The 2026 AGM approved MD and WTD pay by special resolution; promoter votes were ruled invalid as interested, so 7,560 public votes carried it (AGM26, pp.6-7, 11-12).
- **Section 185 limit raised** at the 2026 AGM (AGM26, p.7, p.12). Section 185 governs loans to entities in which directors are interested. Standalone long term loans stood at 1,258.63 (RES26, p.5). FY25 loans went to the UK subsidiary at 10% (AR25, p.126).
- **Capitalisation.** Intangibles up 634.53 in FY26, including goodwill and an in house platform (CALL, p.15). Other non current assets rose 807.03 with nature NOT FOUND.
- **Deck versus filing.** The MD's FY26 comment cites PAT of Rs 13.8 crore (DECK-JUL26, p.6). The filed figure is 1,283.90 lakh (RES26, p.14).
- **Dilution.** 48,480 ESOP shares at Rs 10 listed 25-Jun-2026; 44,400 sit in one distinctive range (LIST-JUN26, p.3). About 73,320 options remain from the 1,21,800 pool [INFERENCE from CALL, p.12 and ESOP-JUN26, p.3].
- IPO proceeds: 2,414.29 of 2,822.50 used by 30-Sep-2025; 408.24 unused, mostly APAC marketing (CLAR-APR26, p.20). No deviation reported.

## 11. Posture: the Transition Decision Matrix

- **Proof gate: NOT FIRED.** Guided H2 strength missed by 9 to 14 points of annual share. Standalone profit fell.
- **Ugliness: STRUCTURAL, provisional.** The ugly optic is captive dependence on a customer owned by the promoter family at cost plus. It comes from ownership design, not from a climb. The margin dip from MDC hardware (materials cost 294.27 against 7.95, RES26, p.14) and ESOP cost is artifact. The concentration is not. It would turn ARTIFACT if direct revenue passed half of the total at an operating margin of 19% or better.
- **Recognition gap: OPEN.** 12.3x trailing sits below the R2 neighbourhood of about 15 to 17x and the R3 neighbourhood of about 19x. (Multiple: MKT, service record. Neighbourhoods: CLAUDE.md quality ladder.)

**Posture: VALUE-TRAP RISK.** Proof not fired, ugliness structural. The cheap multiple prices a captive vendor correctly until the mix shift shows in margin.

## 12. Verdict card

**WATCH.** Not PASS, because domestic revenue grew 49%, the UK subsidiary grew profit 60%, there is no debt, and the stock trades at 12.3x trailing. Not PROCEED, because the core channel is opaque, the guidance missed, and the filings needed NSE correction twice.

**Load bearing facts to verify first**
1. Rupee sales to delaPlex INC for FY25 and FY26, and the cost plus mark up. Source: a readable FY26 annual report, related party note.
2. H1 FY27 results (due by mid-Nov-2026): revenue against 3,717.75, operating margin against 19.7%.
3. MDC economics: contract values, capital owned, revenue share with BSNL.
4. The FY26 auditor's report, CARO and the nature of other non current assets of 1,973.22.

**What would change the view**
- H1 FY27 revenue growth with operating margin at or above 19% moves this to PROCEED.
- A disclosed parent channel decline without a matching direct gain moves it to PASS.
- Any loan under the new Section 185 limit to an entity linked to a promoter moves it to PASS.

No Role 1 valuation was run and no target price is set. This verdict decides only whether the name earns a full `/step1`, and it is not a position.
