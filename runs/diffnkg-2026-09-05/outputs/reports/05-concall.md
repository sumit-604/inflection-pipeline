### 1C Trigger evolution and slippage flags

- **Heavy engineering (Unit 4) capacity doubling — the single most decision-relevant, load-bearing
  promise.** Timeline has slipped at every call: implied Q4 FY26 (background Nov-2025 call, per
  operator note) → "by end of FY26" (Q3 FY26 call, Feb-2026, [PAGE 4]) → "end of Q1 2027 (calendar)"
  (Q4 FY26 call, May-2026, [PAGE 3-4], described as "progressing well and as planned" despite being a
  slip of roughly a full quarter or more) → "phase-wise", no committed date, only partial floor space
  in use (Q1 FY27 call, Aug-2026, [PAGE 9]). Each slip is presented in confident, forward-looking
  language rather than as a miss. TREND: WEAKENING / repeatedly slipping, never acknowledged as a
  miss.
- **Railway (Vande Bharat) order conversion** — timeline has stretched at every call: 3-5 months
  (Q3 FY26) → 6-9 months (Q4 FY26) → 9-12 months (Q1 FY27), with only "one or two small orders"
  materialised by Aug-2026 despite three LOIs having been received back in Feb-2026. TREND:
  WEAKENING.
- **FY27 revenue-growth guidance** — 25% (Q3 FY26 call) walked down to >20% (Q4 FY26 call) before
  FY27 even began, then Q1 FY27 actual growth printed 33%-36.5% YoY, beating the lowered guide by a
  wide margin. Read together with management's own words ("we always believe in overperforming and
  undercommitting"), this looks like deliberate sandbagging rather than a genuine miss — a
  credibility-positive pattern on delivery, but it also means the initial 25% figure carried
  no real information. TREND: guidance lowered, then beaten.
- **EBITDA margin improvement** — promised +100-200bps "by FY27" (Q3 FY26 call), narrowed to
  +80-100bps "this year" (Q4 FY26 call). Q1 FY27 actual margin (12.85% consolidated) came in BELOW
  Q1 FY26 (13.12%), the opposite direction of the promise, attributed to raw-material volatility
  (tungsten, nickel, cobalt) and the West Asia conflict. Management again pushed the promise out to
  "next year, year and a half" (Q1 FY27 call, [PAGE 16]). TREND: WEAKENING, promise now three calls
  old and still unmet.
- **Tejorup/VSHORADS defence optionality** — introduced Q3 FY26 with detailed strategic framing
  (manufacturing rights, ₹40-45cr valuation); by Q4 FY26 management pre-emptively lowered
  expectations ("I don't foresee any revenue... nor anything significant next year either"); by Q1
  FY27 reduced to a one-line "progressing well" update with no new specifics. TREND: narrative
  intensity DROPPING even though the underlying prototype timeline (1-2 years, stated Feb-2026) has
  not itself changed — this looks like deliberate de-emphasis of a trigger once the near-term
  optionality reality set in.
- **Leasing-of-equipment model** (disclosed in the IPO prospectus) — never mentioned by management in
  Q3 or Q4 FY26 calls; when an analyst asked directly in Q1 FY27, management admitted the model has
  NOT gained new traction because the pilot cement customer preferred an upfront-purchase-plus-AMC
  structure over leasing (Q1 FY27 call, [PAGE 18]). A DROPPED TRIGGER that quietly disappeared from
  the narrative and was revealed, on direct question, to have stalled.
- **New product pipeline (VRM rollers, steel mill rollers, shredder parts)** — mentioned
  consistently across all three calls with growing specificity (from generic "new products" in Q3
  FY26 to named wins — VRM roller order from a "leading cement manufacturer" — by Q4 FY26, and
  reiterated in Q1 FY27 as an ongoing focus). TREND: STRENGTHENING, the one trigger showing
  consistent forward progress with growing specificity.
- **Order book growth** — INR200cr (Dec-2025) → INR200cr (Apr-2026, essentially flat) → INR209cr
  (Jun-2026, +20.4% QoQ vs the INR174cr figure given for Mar-2026 in the Q1 FY27 call). Note the
  Mar-2026 order book figure (INR174cr, cited Q1 FY27 call [PAGE 3]) sits below the "~INR200cr as of
  30-Apr-2026" figure cited a month later at the Q4 FY26 call [PAGE 3] — the two management-supplied
  order-book figures for adjacent dates are not fully reconcilable from the transcripts; flagged, not
  resolved (data gap, not accused as false).

### 2B Excuse pattern analysis

- Every margin miss is attributed to raw-material price volatility (tungsten, nickel, cobalt,
  molybdenum, ferro-alloys) and, from Q4 FY26 onward, the West Asia conflict — classified as
  EXTERNAL-BLAME. The underlying price moves are independently plausible (tungsten cited up
  300%+ vs 2024 average, Q3 FY26 call [PAGE 12]) and management does document a pass-through lag
  mechanism consistently across all three calls, so this reads as a genuine, recurring
  operating condition rather than a fabricated excuse — but it is never balanced by an admission
  that the medium-term margin guidance itself might be too optimistic.
- The Unit 4 capacity slippage is NEVER framed as a miss. Both the May-2026 and Aug-2026 language
  ("progressing well and as planned"; "phase-wise... started utilizing") is constructed to avoid
  the word "delay" even though the completion date has visibly moved. Classified as DEFLECTION
  (soft), not silence — management does answer directly when pressed (e.g., Kunal Mehta's direct
  question in Q1 FY27, [PAGE 9]) but never volunteers the slippage unprompted.
- Standalone Q4 FY26 PAT declined YoY (-3.05%) while consolidated PAT rose strongly; management
  proactively addressed this when an analyst raised it (Neil Bahal, Q4 FY26 call, [PAGE 7-8]),
  attributing it to capacity being maxed out at 85% utilisation — a semi-self-inflicted
  explanation (capacity planning), delivered candidly rather than deflected. This is the closest
  the calls come to an HONEST-ADMISSION register.
- No instance across the three calls of management saying "we made a mistake" or similarly direct.
  Hard topics (standalone/consolidated divergence, raw-material volatility, margin trajectory) are
  mostly raised by analysts first, then answered candidly; management does not proactively flag the
  Unit 4 slippage or the walked-down FY27 guidance before being asked.
- Guidance philosophy is explicitly stated as deliberately conservative twice ("we always believe
  in overperforming and undercommitting", Q4 FY26 call [PAGE 9]; repeated near-verbatim in Q1 FY27
  call [PAGE 16]) — a candid, self-aware admission of sandbagging, which is credibility-positive but
  also means headline growth numbers should be read as a floor, not a real estimate.
