## 1. Tax-rate reconciliation is internally inconsistent between standalone and consolidated notes — same total, different reconciling items (NEW — cross-note pattern)

Both the standalone (Note 38(F), p.177) and consolidated (Note 36(F), p.239) effective-tax-rate reconciliations bridge to the identical total tax expense of ₹374.92 Mn / ₹37.49 Cr, but allocate the bridge completely differently:

| Reconciling item | Standalone (Note 38F, p.177) | Consolidated (Note 36F, p.239) |
|---|---|---|
| Expected tax at statutory rate | 367.00 | 361.00 |
| Expenditure disallowed | 7.30 | 7.04 |
| Capital-gains rate effect | — | — |
| **Tax effect of earlier years** | **0.44** | **19.69** |
| Others | 0.18 | (12.81) |
| **Total** | **374.92** | **374.92** |

The consolidated table's "Tax effect of earlier years" line (₹19.69 Mn / ₹1.97 Cr) exactly matches the P&L's separately disclosed "Tax expense pertaining to earlier year" current-tax line item (both standalone and consolidated P&L, p.127/191) — i.e., the consolidated reconciliation treats the earlier-year tax charge as a clean, full one-off. The standalone reconciliation instead nets almost all of that same ₹19.69 Mn earlier-year charge into a tiny ₹0.44 Mn "earlier years" figure and a ₹0.18 Mn "Others" residual — implying it was substantially offset by something else within the standalone P&L that the consolidated table does not show as offsetting. 🟡 Watch — this appears connected to the DTA/DTL movement table's newly-created, unexplained "Other timing difference" deferred-tax line of ₹(19.14) Mn / ₹(1.91) Cr in FY25 (Note 38 standalone p.177 / Note 36 consolidated p.239), which has no narrative explanation anywhere in the notes and roughly offsets the ₹19.69 Mn current-tax earlier-year charge in the standalone reconciliation but is NOT used the same way in the consolidated reconciliation. **REFINES PASS 1**: Pass 1 (§10, Deferred Tax) cited the ₹1.97 Cr earlier-year tax charge as simply "a one-off" without noting (a) the standalone rate-reconciliation table shows its true net P&L effect as only ₹0.44 Mn, not ₹19.69 Mn, because of an offsetting, unexplained deferred-tax credit, and (b) the consolidated reconciliation table categorizes the same overall tax expense completely differently. Neither the standalone nor the consolidated notes explain the "Other timing difference" line or reconcile the divergence between the two rate-bridge tables. Worth a management question.

## 2. CSR: FY24 was a zero-spend year, not a small-shortfall year — corrects Pass 1's characterization (NEW / REFINES PASS 1)

Note (ii) to Other Expenses, CSR expenditure detail (standalone p.162; consolidated equivalent p.224-225): the FY24 column shows "Amount spent during the year — on purpose other than [construction/acquisition of asset]" as "–" (nil), against a gross requirement of ₹4.72 Mn (₹0.472 Cr) that year. The Company spent **nothing** on CSR in FY24, carrying forward a shortfall of ₹4.72 Mn, on top of the ₹0.81 Mn (₹0.081 Cr) already brought forward from FY23 — a combined ₹5.53 Mn (₹0.553 Cr) obligation entering FY25. In FY25 the Company spent ₹15.29 Mn (₹1.529 Cr) against a current-year requirement of ₹8.19 Mn, clearing the full ₹5.53 Mn brought-forward obligation plus the current year in full, leaving a ₹1.57 Mn (₹0.157 Cr) excess carried forward. 🟡 Watch. Pass 1 stated the Company "cleared the FY24 shortfall of ₹0.081 Cr plus current year in full" — this understates the FY24 shortfall roughly 7x (actual brought-forward obligation was ₹0.553 Cr, not ₹0.081 Cr); the underlying conclusion (FY25 was a genuine catch-up/over-spend year) still holds, but the correct characterization is that FY24 was a complete CSR-spend miss, not a partial one.

## 3. Capital advances outstanding nearly tripled YoY (NEW)

Note 8, Other Non-Current Assets, standalone (p.148) / Note 6 consolidated (p.211): Capital advances ₹384.26 Mn (₹38.43 Cr) FY24 → ₹1,084.32 Mn (₹108.43 Cr) standalone / ₹1,108.04 Mn (₹110.80 Cr) consolidated FY25 — a +182% increase, well ahead of the capex/PPE addition growth rate. Consistent with the facility build-out (GE Vernova/Mitsubishi plants) and the CWIP ramp, but the magnitude of cash tied up in advances-not-yet-capitalised was not separately quantified in Pass 1. 🟡 Watch — a large share of the QIP proceeds appears to be flowing out as capital advances ahead of actual asset capitalisation; worth tracking conversion of these advances into productive assets in FY26.

## 4. Government-authority receivables balance more than doubled YoY (NEW)

Note 12, Other Current Assets, standalone (p.151) / Note 10 consolidated (p.214): "Balance with government authorities" (GST input credit/duty drawback/export-incentive receivables) rose from ₹262.54 Mn (₹26.25 Cr) FY24 to ₹526.33 Mn standalone (₹52.63 Cr) / ₹554.83 Mn consolidated (₹55.48 Cr) FY25 — standalone +100.5% YoY, roughly 3x the pace of revenue growth (+32.9%). 🟡 Watch — increasing working capital is tied up in government-authority balances (consistent with the export-revenue mix rising to 91.9%, which generates more GST/duty-drawback receivables), a genuine but non-obvious drag on operating cash conversion not separately flagged in Pass 1's working-capital discussion.

## 5. Rising non-audit fees paid to the statutory auditor (NEW — governance/independence watch item)

Note 29(i), Other Expenses, Auditors' remuneration breakdown, standalone (p.161) / Note 27 consolidated (p.224): "As auditor" fee ₹3.90 Mn FY25 vs ₹3.50 Mn FY24 (+11.4%); "In other capacity" (non-audit) fee ₹1.10 Mn FY25 vs ₹0.30 Mn FY24 — more than tripled, raising the non-audit fee ratio from 8.6% to 28.2% of the audit fee. A footnote further discloses total auditor fees EXCLUDING IPO/QIP-related fees capitalised to share-issue expenses: ₹10.10 Mn FY25 (₹1.01 Cr) and ₹12.50 Mn FY24 (₹1.25 Cr) — meaning total auditor compensation across all engagements was actually ₹15.30 Mn (₹1.53 Cr) FY25 and ₹16.40 Mn (₹1.64 Cr) FY24 once combined, materially higher than what appears in the P&L "other expenses" auditor-remuneration line alone. 🟡 Watch — same audit firm (M S K A & Associates) earning a rising share of non-audit fees plus substantial capital-markets-transaction fees is an audit-independence-adjacent item worth monitoring, though not itself disqualifying.

## 6. A slice of "other financial assets" deposits are bank-lien-restricted, not free liquidity (NEW — minor, refines liquidity picture)

Note 7 standalone (p.147) / Note 5 consolidated (p.211) footnote: of the >12-month fixed deposits classified as non-current "other financial assets," ₹167.93 Mn (₹16.79 Cr) as at FY25 (₹183.50 Mn / ₹18.35 Cr FY24) are held as LIEN by banks against various fund facilities sanctioned — i.e., not freely available despite sitting in a financial-asset line that reads as liquid. This is a small slice relative to the Company's overall ₹695.92 Cr cash/bank position (Note 11) and does not change Pass 1's net-cash conclusion, but it was not previously surfaced as a nuance in the liquidity/gearing discussion. 🟢 Minor.

## 7. CWIP ageing schedule confirms no overdue projects (NEW — confirmatory, minor)

Note 4, Capital Work-in-Progress, standalone (p.145) / consolidated (p.210): ageing schedule shows ₹95.70 Mn (₹9.57 Cr) of CWIP sitting in the 2-3 year bucket as at FY25 (down from ₹236.56 Mn/₹23.66 Cr FY24), and an explicit management representation (Note 4, point 1) that "there are no projects as capital work in Progress...whose completion is overdue or cost of which exceeds in comparison to its original plan." 🟢 Clean, supplementary granularity not separately extracted in Pass 1.

## 8. Promoter-shareholding 3-year table contains an anomalous "2517%" change figure (NEW — minor disclosure-quality nit)

Note 13(v) standalone (p.152) / Note 11(v) consolidated (p.216): the FY23→FY24 promoter shareholding reconciliation shows Rakesh Chopdar's holding falling from 14,64,435 shares (88.66%) to 3,83,30,255 shares (64.84%), with the table's own "% change during the year" column showing "2517%" — an arithmetic artifact of the bonus issue and share-split changing the share count base mid-period, not a real economic change. Not a red flag, but a table-construction quirk that could confuse a reader skimming the shareholding-pattern disclosures.

## 9. Schedule III Note 45 quantifies precisely how much the subsidiaries drag consolidated profit (NEW — quantifies/refines Pass 1)

Note 45, "Additional information pursuant to paragraph 2 of Division II of Schedule III" (consolidated, p.244): Azad Engineering Limited (parent, standalone) contributes **101.85%** of consolidated FY25 profit (₹881.34 Mn / ₹88.13 Cr) and **98.71%** of consolidated net assets (₹13,751.39 Mn / ₹1,375.14 Cr); Azad Prime Private Limited contributes -0.84% of profit (₹-7.27 Mn loss) and Azad VTC Private Limited -1.01% (₹-8.73 Mn loss), combining for -1.85% of consolidated profit (-₹16.00 Mn / -₹1.60 Cr) against 1.28% of consolidated net assets. **REFINES PASS 1**: Pass 1 (§6, Investments) stated qualitatively that the subsidiary losses "drag consolidated PAT below standalone PAT" without this precise Schedule III quantification; this note is the authoritative, pre-calculated source for that gap.

## 10. IFC (internal financial controls) opinion is unqualified/clean despite the audit-trail exception (NEW — context that tempers a Pass 1 flag)

Annexure B to the consolidated auditor's report (Report on Internal Financial Controls, p.188): the auditor's opinion states the Group has "in all material respects, an adequate internal financial controls with reference to consolidated financial statements and such internal financial controls...were operating effectively as at March 31, 2025" — i.e., a clean IFC opinion, notwithstanding the audit-trail (edit-log) database-level exception flagged separately under Rule 11(g)/CARO. 🟢 Context. This does not remove Pass 1's 🟡 Watch on the audit-trail gap (finding #9 in Pass 1's top 10), but clarifies that the auditor did not treat it as rising to a material weakness in internal financial controls — worth noting when weighing the severity of that flag.

## 11. New "QIB expense payable" line item (NEW — minor, procedural)

Note 20 standalone (p.157) / Note 18 consolidated (p.221), Other Financial Liabilities: a new ₹136.03 Mn (₹13.60 Cr) "QIB expense payable" line appears in FY25 (nil FY24), alongside "IPO expense payable" declining from ₹137.69 Mn to ₹44.78 Mn. Routine QIP-transaction-cost accrual, not a red flag; noted only because it is a new, sizeable payable line not itemised in Pass 1.
