## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

**2A Demand environment.** Consensus is upbeat-to-resilient in the peer set, not
depressed. DMCC reports domestic Indian demand (pigments, fertiliser, paints,
polymers, boron) recovering "across the board" from FY25 lows, with the one
structural weak spot being Europe (DMCC Nov-2024, p.5-6/15; May-2025, p.6-7/16).
Neogen frames "demand resilience in Pharma, Flavors and Fragrances" even
through a fire-related supply disruption (Neogen Feb-2026, p.2/24). TANFACIND
describes booming solar-grade demand (6-8x expected over 5 years) and locked
long-term contracts covering the bulk of new HFC-32 and solar-DHF capacity
(TANFACIND May-2026, p.4-5,13/19). No peer describes anything resembling
Kronox's implicit "difficult demand year." This split complicates rather than
supports Kronox's framing.

**2B Pricing and input costs.** Universal theme: a real, geopolitically-driven
input-cost shock (sulfur via the Middle East/Strait of Hormuz; lithium and
bromine volatility at Neogen; fluorspar logistics costs at TANFACIND) hit the
sector in the same window. All three peers describe successfully passing this
through with a 30-90 day lag, generally preserving absolute (if not percentage)
margins (DMCC May-2026, p.3-4/12; TANFACIND Aug-2026, p.4-5/22). This
corroborates the existence of Kronox's cited macro shock but does not corroborate
it as an excuse for flat revenue — see Q1/Q3 above.

**2C Capex cycle.** Not a uniform industry-wide capacity race — a clean
bifurcation. NEOGEN and TANFACIND are aggressively expanding with capex in the
hundreds of crores, both financed partly through fresh external capital raises
(Neogen: Rs 600 cr QIP approved, Rs 161 cr promoter preferential already
completed, Aug-2026; TANFACIND: Rs 250 cr QIP completed + Rs 100 cr preferential
approved, Aug-2026) and both anchoring new capacity to pre-signed long-term
customer contracts (Neogen's salt capacity "completely take care of the 5,500
metric tonne" via signed agreements, Feb-2026, p.9/24; TANFACIND's R-32 capacity
65% pre-contracted rising toward 80-85%, May-2026 p.14/19 and Aug-2026 p.14/22).
DMCC is the deliberate non-expander — "no major CAPEX," debt-paydown focus,
repeated for six straight quarters across the four transcripts held. Indo Borax
is mid-transition: capex "still in discussion," no announced figure as of its
one call. Kronox's Dahej Unit IV — restarted after an unexplained multi-year
gap, with reset deadlines but zero capex/CWIP/capital-commitment trail in the
same AR (per B05 red flag) — fits neither peer pattern cleanly: it lacks the
external-capital discipline of the growth peers and lacks the plain
"we are not expanding" clarity of the conservative peers.

**2D Competitive mentions.** None. See Q4.

**2E Risks peers discuss that Kronox does not.**
- Geopolitical/single-region raw-material dependency: Indo Borax explicitly
  names Turkey/South America/North America concentration in boron ore sourcing
  and a live de-risking programme (INDOBORAX Jun-2026, p.13-14/28). Kronox's AR
  MD&A contains no raw-material sourcing-geography risk discussion despite
  presumably importing key inorganic inputs.
- Regulatory/quota risk disclosed in granular detail: TANFACIND devotes several
  Q&A exchanges to HFC-32 quota-allocation mechanics under the Montreal
  Protocol/Kigali Amendment, including penalty-clause structure in customer
  contracts (TANFACIND May-2026, p.7-9/19). Kronox discloses no comparable
  regulatory-dependency detail for its own product lines.
- FX mark-to-market risk: TANFACIND quantifies a one-off Rs 2.5-3 cr M2M loss
  from rupee depreciation (TANFACIND May-2026, p.12/19). Not discussed by
  Kronox despite an export mix rising to 32.39% (per B05).
- Insurance/business-continuity risk disclosure standard: Neogen's detailed,
  quarter-by-quarter insurance-claim reconciliation (Feb-2026 through Aug-2026
  transcripts) illustrates a disclosure norm for a major operational disruption
  that Kronox's own unexplained multi-year Dahej construction delay never
  receives in its AR.
- Governance/promoter-pledge scrutiny: an Indo Borax analyst directly probes
  promoter share pledging and loan-to-value ratio (INDOBORAX Jun-2026, p.20/28)
  — the kind of scrutiny Kronox's 100% OFS IPO and promoter-remuneration pattern
  (B00 load-bearing facts) could plausibly invite once it has an active
  analyst base.

## PART 5: CROSS-PEER HYPOTHESIS

The two peers running large, credible capacity expansions (Neogen, TANFACIND)
are also the two simultaneously tapping external capital markets (QIP,
preferential issue) for that expansion, and both anchor the new capacity to
majority pre-signed, long-term customer offtake before commissioning. The two
peers NOT raising external capital for their (much smaller or absent) capex
programmes — DMCC, which is self-funding modest debottlenecking from internal
accruals, and Indo Borax, whose capex is still "in discussion" — are also the
two giving the vaguest, undated, uncosted forward guidance, closely resembling
Kronox's own Dahej Unit IV disclosure style.

Testable hypothesis: in this Indian specialty/fine-chemicals peer set,
disclosure discipline on capex (rupee figure, capacity, commissioning quarter,
contracted offtake) is not a governance choice but a market-imposed
precondition of raising external growth capital — QIP/preferential investors
require it, so companies that need their money supply it; companies funding
capex (or non-capex) purely from internal accruals or promoter equity face no
such external discipline and stay vague. If this holds, Kronox's Dahej Unit IV
vagueness may say less about management's willingness to disclose and more
about the fact that Kronox has not yet needed (or has chosen not) to tap
external capital markets for it. Confirm signal: any future Kronox capex
announcement accompanied by a QIP/preferential/rights issue should immediately
jump to Neogen/TANFACIND-grade disclosure specificity as a condition of the
raise; absence of such a jump even after an external raise would falsify the
hypothesis and point back to a genuine governance/opacity problem.
