Bearings maker grows fast, receivables grow faster

Menon Bearings makes engine bearings and auto components in Kolhapur, run by the Menon family, with Nitin Menon as executive chairman and more than 30 years of manufacturing behind it. Three divisions carry the business: bi-metal engine bearings, bushes and thrust washers at 72% of revenue, aluminium die cast and machined parts through Menon Alkop at 24%, and brake linings and shoes through Menon Brakes at 3 to 4%. Exports run near 34% of sales. Customers include John Deere, Tata Motors, International Tractors and Brakes India, and Menon is the sole or major supplier to most of them. 80 to 90% of revenue comes from the auto sector, concentrated in the cyclical commercial vehicle and tractor segments.

FY26 consolidated revenue rose 23% to Rs 294 Cr, EBITDA reached Rs 65 Cr at a 22.2% margin, and PAT grew 53% to Rs 38 Cr. ROCE was 29.8% on the annual report Note 33 basis, about 24% on a closing operating basis. CFO to PAT was 0.61. The shares trade near Rs 190 for a market cap of about Rs 1,073 Cr, roughly 24 times trailing earnings. Debt is low at 0.25 times equity, and CRISIL rates the group BBB+ with a stable outlook.

The company is midway through a debt funded expansion across all three divisions, adding bi-metal capacity from 486 to 580 lakh units, roughly doubling Alkop capacity, and lifting brake capacity. The export push into the United States runs through Allison and Federal-Mogul DRiV and rides the China plus one sourcing shift. Menon Alkop moved from an internal division to a wholly owned subsidiary in January 2024, which reshaped the standalone accounts. Two operational unlocks sit ahead: converting export terms to ex works, now at 80% against a 90% target, and a railway and OEM brake approval gated on a single testing dynamometer.

Gate 0 scored the company 70 of 160 and labelled it AVOID, and that label is a backward score artifact rather than a distress signal. Core scored 54, an average band, then took a mandatory one tier downgrade for limited history, since only four continuous years exist, FY23 to FY26, after a 15 year filing gap disconnects the older record. A non binding cash deal breaker in Block B pulled it further. Block A scored 19 of 20 and Block D 18 of 20, and the moat reads MODERATE with three confirmed sources. The forward emerging moat scan scored 11, which is the NONE band, so the combined read stays AVOID.

The cash question sits at the center of the story. Standalone trade receivables jumped 72% while revenue grew 28.5%, the ageing bucket beyond six months tripled from 6.8% to 22.2%, DSO stretched from 85 to 114 days, standalone cash conversion fell to 35.3%, and standalone cash dropped 69.7%. On the consolidated books the picture is calmer: ageing held broadly stable near 11%, though consolidated CFO to PAT fell to 61%, the first reading below 100% in four years. CRISIL, reaffirming the rating in April 2026 after year end, called liquidity adequate, noted a 2.45 times current ratio and cash accrual above Rs 31 Cr against term debt near Rs 9 Cr, and attributed the balance sheet pressure to debt funded capex. The weight of that evidence points to a growth and capex build rather than distress, so the cash flag reads GROWTH-INDUCED. The standalone deterioration is real, DSO sits exactly on the 114 day line, and the ex works fix management promised for it has not yet arrived.

Credibility grades C. Core revenue and margin guidance landed within about 2% of target across the year. The transition promises did not. The brake dynamometer that gates railway and OEM approval slipped four straight quarters, its ETA moving from September 2025 to August 2026 while the stated reason degraded to reasons that cannot be explained here, and railway revenue is still zero. The Alkop revenue target was stated three different ways, Rs 120 Cr by FY27, then over two years, then Rs 95 Cr by FY27 in a same period investor presentation, and never reconciled. The brake division Rs 100 Cr FY27 ambition shrank to a Rs 10 Cr pipeline over two years. Four named initiatives, a Canada OEM deal, an 800 part US aftermarket offer, a Menon Ventures EV charging plan, and a distributor push from 35 to 150, were introduced and then dropped without closure.

Governance carries watch items rather than a red verdict, graded CAUTION. The new CFO appointed in March 2026, Chandrakant Ghatge, lists only one prior employer, Mani Auto Components, which is the chairman's own related party sales counterparty, not an external finance hire. The MD and CFO both changed on the same date, 4 March 2026, four weeks before year end, with the outgoing MD near 82 and the incoming MD 70 at the start of a fresh five year term. A Rs 29 Cr corporate guarantee to Menon Alkop sits outside any contingent liabilities table, Alkop itself carries negative reserves of Rs 2.07 Cr against the parent unimpaired Rs 28.24 Cr carrying value with no impairment taken, and an interest free advance to Menon Brakes grew 45% to Rs 4.57 Cr. Promoter holding is stable at 68.44%, but the pledge percentage could not be confirmed because the primary BSE fetches returned 403 errors and web sources conflicted.

Three peer calls, from HARSHA, NRBBEARING and PRECAM, confirm the China plus one sourcing shift and the copper and aluminium input inflation with a lagged and partial pass through. They contradict one Menon claim directly: the framing that heavy capex is behind it and it is now sweating assets toward 2 to 2.5 times asset turns. All three peers are mid expansion in the same period, and NRB explicitly rejects the sweat the assets model. Peers describe export and Europe demand as slow and fragile, with one peer subsidiary sliding into insolvency, a more cautious tone than Menon confident structural shift narrative. Domestic market sizing puts TAM at Rs 8,500 to 10,100 Cr and SAM at Rs 1,858 Cr, with Menon holding 10.4% of SAM and 9.6 times domestic revenue headroom, a runway classed STRONG. The domestic SOM implies only a 7.5% three year revenue CAGR, against management 25% aspiration toward Rs 500 Cr by FY28. That 3.3 times gap rests almost entirely on the export leg, which this analysis could not size independently.

Valuation lands the stock in the WATCHLIST bucket. The destination PE is 25x on the governing additive track, which equals the Manufacturing and Industrial sector cap, and 22.4x on the more conservative Return Reversion track. Current trailing PE is 24.3x, so there is no re-rating room and the whole return must come from earnings. The Hurdle Ratio fails on base earnings at 1.73 against the 25% Tier A bar and scrapes a pass only on capped bull earnings at 1.96, which fixes the disposition at CONDITIONAL. The entry zone is Rs 132 to 148 with a margin of safety price of Rs 118, so CMP Rs 190 sits 28 to 44% above the buy zone. The probability weighted three year CAGR is 13.4%. The call is BUY-ON-DIPS in the Rs 132 to 148 zone and AVOID-on-valuation at Rs 190.

The 25x destination and the CONDITIONAL verdict both rest on one operator override. On the mechanical framework build, Pillar 3 pays +0x on an emerging moat score of 11 in the NONE band, the destination falls to about 23.75x on the reported ROCE or about 20.7x on the 24% operating ROCE, and the Hurdle STOPs on both base and bull. The honest framework build is therefore AVOID or STOP; the WATCHLIST label exists because the operator added one growth point and set the exit multiple to the cap. The devil pressure test rates the thesis WEAKENED BUT ALIVE: growth triggers, moat durability and management trust each weakened, and valuation safety destroyed, with the shared catalyst confirmed, since in FY26 exports rose about 50% while receivables grew 2.4 times faster than revenue and the expected credit loss allowance stayed frozen. Cash firing and cash breaking arrived together, which is the single fact the thesis has to survive.
