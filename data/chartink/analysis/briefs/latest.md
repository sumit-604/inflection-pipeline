## The picture in one paragraph

Tuesday was the widest buying day since 12 June, and the Nifty closed flat. 270 stocks rose four and a half percent or more, against 41 that fell that hard. About seven stocks in ten closed up. The small company indices rose almost three percent while the banks and IT slipped. The buying was wide and it was not heavy. Turnover stayed near Monday's level, below Friday's selling day, and the heavy volume share stayed low. The new lows shrank from 304 to 93. The long trend count rose to 658 stocks above the 200 day line, about three in ten. That is still far below the 1,357 of early August. Two up days in a row now sit on top of a falling staircase. The spring showed that days of this size can fail. A real turn needs a third day, heavier volume, and a long count back above the autumn floor.

## Where the market stands

Picture the market as about 2,240 stocks. Each stock has three trend lines. The short line averages the last 20 days. The middle line averages 50 days. The long line averages 200 days. A stock above a line is in an uptrend on that clock. On Tuesday 658 stocks sat above the long line, about three in ten. 404 sat above the middle line, about one in six. 413 sat above the short line, also about one in six. On Friday only 178 held the short line. In two sessions the short count more than doubled. The long count rose by about 80 in the same two days.

The year gives these counts their scale. The spring bottom came on 23 March, when the long count fell to 287. That day 950 stocks fell hard. The climb out began on 1 April, when more than 1,100 stocks rose hard in one session. A second huge day came on 8 April. After those days the long count climbed for four months, to 1,357 on 7 August. Autumn has been a staircase down from that peak. A shock came in mid September. Another came in early October. A repair lifted the long count to about 935 on 16 October. Then last week broke the floor, with three heavy down days, and the count fell to 577 on Friday.

Tuesday's 658 is about where the market stood in the second week of April. It is more than double the March low. The direction is the difference. In April the count rose off a giant thrust. Tonight it is rising for the second day off a fresh low, after a long fall. The count of stocks in an uptrend on the long clock is low for this record. It is not at the extreme.

The day itself was strong in width. 270 stocks rose four and a half percent or more. Since mid April the record has only one other day above 250. It came on 12 June. About one stock in six rose four percent or more by the close, the highest share in the past twenty sessions. Nearly four stocks in ten traded four percent above their prior close at some point in the day. A little under half of those kept the gain into the close. On last Wednesday only one in seven did. Few stocks traded four percent below the prior close, about one in thirteen.

The volume did not match the width. Turnover reached about 524 crore. Monday had about 519 crore and Friday about 611 crore. So the selling day still carried more money than either buying day. The share of stocks trading on heavy volume, against those on light volume, sits in the lower half of its history. Turnover is above the year ago level of about 416 crore, so the market is not quiet by long standards. Wide buying on ordinary volume says sellers stepped back. It does not yet say large buyers stepped in.

The far end of the damage repaired fast. On Monday more than one stock in four was down ten percent or more over five days. Tonight about one in nine is. About one stock in forty trades more than ten percent below its 10 day average. 14 stocks are down twenty percent or more over five days, against seven up that much. New yearly lows fell to 93 from 304 on Monday and 453 on Friday. 18 stocks made a new yearly high. Still, close to four stocks in ten sit within fifteen percent of a yearly low. Fear eased and stays high. The India VIX measures what traders pay to protect against big swings in the Nifty. It fell more than four percent on Tuesday and closed near 16.4. ## What would change this read

The read is a two day bounce, wide and light, on top of a falling staircase. It turns worse if the selling returns before volume confirms. Another day of 300 or more hard fallers within the next week would repeat the March pattern, when wide days of this size failed. A long count back under about 580, Friday's low, would erase the bounce. New yearly lows back above about 300 would say the same. A Bank Nifty slide that drags the Nifty down while the small names fade would show the bounce had no floor under it. A VIX close back above about 18 would say fear has returned.

The cautious read fails if the buying turns heavy and keeps going. The first test is a wide day on turnover above Friday's 611 crore. The second test is the long count back above about 840, the old autumn floor. The third test is new highs past about 50 while new lows stay under 100. Tuesday passed the new low half of that test, at 93. It failed the volume test, at about 524 crore. New highs reached only 18. A VIX close under about 15 would support the turn. A third wide day on heavier volume would start to change the read. A quiet fade on light volume would leave the staircase in place.
