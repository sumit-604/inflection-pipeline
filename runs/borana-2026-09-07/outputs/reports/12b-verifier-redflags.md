## PART 0: WHAT COULD NOT BE TESTED BECAUSE THE CALLS DO NOT EXIST

Borana held exactly one earnings call in its listed life (27-Jan-2026, Q3 FY26,
its "maiden" call). It has held none since, across two subsequent results. The
following standard tests are therefore unavailable and I make no finding on
them:

- **Cross-quarter tone drift in management's own voice.** One transcript.
- **Repeated-question tracking.** A repeated evasion needs the same question in
  two or more quarters. No topic can qualify. I record **zero repeated
  evasions**, and that is a limit of the record, not a clean bill.
- **Story-consistency to different analysts across calls.** Not testable across
  calls. It IS testable *within* the one call, and I found a contradiction
  there (Flag M4).
- **Whether the Q4 FY26 sequential revenue decline, the Q1 FY27 flat quarter,
  the SEBI search at the promoter-group entity, the secretarial auditor's
  mid-term exit, or the Rs 5 cr third-party loan would have survived analyst
  questioning.** All post-date the only call. Nobody has ever asked management
  about any of them.

What I substituted, per the degradation rule: the Annual Report FY2026 (the
document the company is legally most accountable for), the six results filings,
the four investor decks, the nine exchange announcements, the three rating
documents and the IPO monitoring agency report. Twelve peer transcripts were
used to test management's claims about input prices, import policy and industry
conditions.

**One structural observation before the findings.** The company promised
continued engagement in its closing remarks — "We look forward to engage with
you in the quarter ahead" (Concall, p.16). Seven months and two results later,
there has been no second call. In the same window the company disclosed a SEBI
search at a promoter-group entity, a fourth slip on its flagship power project,
a scaled-down solar component, an unsecured loan out to a third party, and a
sequential revenue decline. Every one of those was disclosed at the bare
regulatory minimum, in a form no one could question. The absence of the second
call is itself the largest single red flag in the record, because it is what
makes every other flag below untestable against management.

## PART 1: INDEPENDENT RED-FLAG LIST (33 items, found before reading B05/B06)

### GROUP A — CLAIMS THAT THE COMPANY'S OWN FILINGS CONTRADICT

**A1. [CRITICAL] The Rs 18-20 crore renewable power saving is arithmetically
impossible against the company's own audited power bill.**

Management stated the figure twice, unprompted and under challenge:
- To Disha (Sapphire Capital): "how much power savings are we expecting?" —
  "It will be around nearly INR18 crores to INR20 crores" (Concall, p.7).
- To Madhur Rathi (Counter Cyclical), who explicitly challenged the payback:
  "we will get a benefit of 20 on an average. So, we will be free in 6 years"
  (Concall, p.11).

Audited FY26 accounts, Note 28 Other Expenses: **Factory Power Expenses (Net)
Rs 1,955.77 lakh = Rs 19.56 crore** for the full year FY26, against Rs 1,628.44
lakh in FY25 (Annual_Report_FY2026, p.114). The accounting policy note confirms
this line is stated net of the power subsidy: "Power subsidy to be reduced from
Factory Power Expenses" (Annual_Report_FY2026, p.104).

A claimed annual saving of Rs 18-20 crore therefore equals **92% to 102% of the
entire annual factory power cost**. The stated substitution rate is only 70-80%
("Approximately 70%-80% of the company's current power requirements are
expected to be met through renewable energy sources", Concall, p.4), and the
19.79 MW hybrid is a third-party developer project (Clean Max Enviro Energy
Solutions, per Concall p.4 and the Reg 30 filing of 29-Aug-2026) — meaning the
company will pay a PPA tariff for that power, not receive it free. Even at 80%
substitution and a zero replacement tariff, the ceiling on the saving is about
Rs 15.6 crore. The claim exceeds a physically unreachable ceiling.

The number was never repeated in any presentation, so it appears exactly once,
in the one forum where it was challenged and held. It is also the number that
carries the payback and IRR arithmetic on a Rs 125 crore investment.

**A2. [MAJOR] The renewable benefit is credited twice — once as margin
expansion, once as the offset to the subsidy cliff.**

Management disclosed a hard sunset on both subsidies, per unit: "in power, we
are getting around 20% to 22% of the subsidy... It's INR2 per unit. And it will
be for 5 years. So, for the unit 1, it will be still 2026. Then in unit 2, it
will be '27. Unit 3, it will be for 2028" (Concall, p.8). In the same answer:
"But meanwhile, we will be doing these renewables, so that will not affect our
margin at all."

The same renewable saving is used to (a) neutralise the subsidy expiry and (b)
expand margins — the Q3 deck's own framing, "Capacity expansion and higher
renewable energy usage are expected to improve operating margins"
(Investor_Presentation_Q3FY26, p.33). One benefit, two mechanisms.

**A3. [MAJOR] The subsidy sunset appears in no deck and no annual report.** The
Unit 1 / Unit 2 / Unit 3 expiry dates of 2026, 2027 and 2028 exist in exactly
one document in the corpus: the one transcript (p.8). Other income was Rs 8.21
crore in FY26 against PBT of Rs 78.40 crore (Investor_Presentation_Q1FY27,
p.15), and management confirmed the interest subsidy runs through other income:
"the interest subsidy that goes in the quarter is on other income... in July, we
got the permission for unit 3... So at that time, we booked it in that quarter"
(Concall, p.9). A time-limited item worth ~10% of PBT, with a stated expiry
schedule, is disclosed only orally and only once.

**A4. [MAJOR] "Net debt free" does not survive the balance sheet two months
later.** Opening remarks: "this has resulted in a very strong balance sheet that
is net debt free" (Concall, p.4). Audited position at 31-Mar-2026: borrowings
Rs 55.66 crore non-current plus Rs 13.85 crore short-term = **Rs 69.51 crore
gross debt against Rs 1.68 crore cash** (Investor_Presentation_Q1FY27, p.16).
Brickwork's annual review of 11-May-2026 shows sanctioned facilities rising from
Rs 73.30 crore to **Rs 122.32 crore**, including a new Rs 40 crore term loan and
cash credit up from Rs 25.50 crore to Rs 45.50 crore (Credit_Rating_2026-05-11,
p.1). The claim was made 63 days before a year-end showing Rs 67.8 crore of net
debt.

### GROUP B — WHAT MANAGEMENT TOLD TWO ANALYSTS IN THE SAME CALL

**B1. [MAJOR] Within-call contradiction on imports, told to two different
analysts twenty minutes apart, and contradicted by the company's own IPO
monitoring report.**

To Raman KV (Sequent Investments), p.6: "from December, the government has freed
the anti-dumping duty, the raw material which is coming from China, and **we are
profiting the same**... many of the Chinese companies are now offering our raw
material. And that is cheaper from the Indian raw materials."

To Madhur Rathi (Counter Cyclical), p.10: "the raw material that we make from
China **is not imported. It has very heavy duties. So, this thing is not
imported from there.**"

Both statements cannot be true. The company either buys cheaper Chinese raw
material and profits from it, or it does not import it because duties are heavy.

The filed record settles it against the second answer. The IPO Monitoring Agency
Report for the quarter ended 31-Dec-2025 lists the General Corporate Purposes
breakdown: **nine separate "Raw Material Import" transactions between
30-08-2025 and 16-09-2025 totalling Rs 7.93 crore**
(2025-12-31_IPO-monitoring-agency-report, p.8).

**B2. [MINOR] Yarn share understated to an analyst against the same week's
deck.** Asked "what is the percentage of texturized yarn sales in our total
sales?", management answered "It is around 7%" (Concall, p.13), and framed the
segment as incidental: "whatever extra yarn is being manufactured is being sold,
but it is not in part and part of the business to sell the yarn" (Concall,
p.13). The deck filed three days earlier states **PTY Yarn 9M FY26 revenue
contribution 9%, FY25 revenue contribution 12%** (Investor_Presentation_Q3FY26,
p.13). A twelfth of revenue is not a by-product.

**B3. [MAJOR] Blended utilisation of "82% to 83%" is not supported by the
company's own utilisation table or by its own output.** Management: "Blended
utilisation consisting of all the four units? — Yes, it is around 82% to 83%",
with a stated ceiling: "What is the maximum that we can achieve? — Up to 90%"
(Concall, p.14-15). The deck filed the same week gives, for the nine months to
31-12-2025: Unit 1 **76.62%**, Unit 2 **74.73%**, Unit 3 **82.94%**, Unit 4
**78.11%** (Investor_Presentation_Q3FY26, p.16) — a set whose blend cannot reach
82-83%. Separately, 9M production was 16.31 crore metres (Concall, p.5) against
total installed capacity of **39,21,12,000 metres** (Investor_Presentation_Q3FY26,
p.6), i.e. **~55%** of pro-rata capacity. The company has never reconciled its
utilisation percentage to its installed-capacity headline.

**B4. [MINOR] Production figure stated two ways in one call.** Opening remarks:
"In FY '25, the total fabric manufactured by us was 18.6 crore metres"
(Concall, p.5). Q&A twenty minutes later: "Last year, we had 18,36,00,000 for
the entire year" = 18.36 crore metres (Concall, p.8).

### GROUP C — DISCLOSURE INTEGRITY IN THE FILED DECKS

**C1. [CRITICAL] The Q4 FY26 deck presents FY25 operating metrics relabelled as
FY26.**

- Q3 FY26 deck, p.6: "**80.93%** Average Capacity Utilization % (**FY 25**)".
- Q4 FY26 deck, p.6: "**80.93%** Average Capacity Utilization % (**FY 26**)".

The identical figure, to two decimal places, relabelled to a different financial
year. Two of the four unit-level utilisation rows are likewise unchanged across
a period extension of three to four months
(Investor_Presentation_Q3FY26 p.16 vs Investor_Presentation_Q4FY26 p.16):

| Unit | Q3 deck | Q4 deck |
|---|---|---|
| Unit 3 | 82.94% for 09 months to 31-12-2025 | **82.94%** for 12 months to 31-03-2026 |
| Unit 4 | 78.11% for 05 months to 31-12-2025 | **78.11%** for 08 months to 31-03-2026 |

Units 1 and 2 did move. Two rows did not, to two decimals, over a longer period.
That is not a coincidence; it is a stale table filed as current. The headline
"Total Installed Capacity 39,21,12,000 Meter" and "1,208 High Speed Water Jet
Looms" are also identical in both decks — despite the intervening addition of
160 looms said to bring "approximately 160 high-speed waterjet looms... over
5 crore meters of incremental annual capacity" (Concall, p.5).

**C2. [MAJOR] The Q4 deck's own arithmetic implies utilisation above the ceiling
management stated three months earlier.** Deriving Q4 from the deck's 9M and 12M
figures: Unit 1 = 4(82.50) − 3(76.62) = **100.1%**; Unit 2 = 4(85.70) −
3(74.73) = **118.6%**. Management's stated maximum achievable was "Up to 90%"
(Concall, p.15). Either the ceiling was wrong or the utilisation table is.

**C3. [CRITICAL] Every margin on the H2 FY26 profit-and-loss page of the Q4 deck
is exactly double the true value.** Investor_Presentation_Q4FY26, p.26:

| Line | Printed | Correct (from the deck's own figures) |
|---|---|---|
| Gross profit margin H2 FY26 | 86.0% | 90.99 / 212.10 = **42.9%** |
| EBITDA margin H2 FY26 | 49.75% | 52.70 / 212.10 = **24.85%** |
| PBT margin H2 FY26 | 41.00% | 43.45 / 212.10 = **20.49%** |
| PAT margin H2 FY26 | 33.74% | 35.76 / 212.10 = **16.86%** |
| EBITDA margin H2 FY25 | 44.58% | 35.02 / 157.10 = **22.29%** |

The rupee figures are correct; every percentage is computed on half the correct
denominator. An entire filed page overstates profitability by 2x, in the
quarter with no earnings call to catch it.

**C4. [MAJOR] Promoter experience roughly doubled between two consecutive
decks, against the company's own offer document and an independent rating
agency.**

| Source | Mangilal Borana | Ankur Borana | Rajkumar Borana |
|---|---|---|---|
| RHP, May-2025, p.202 | "over 24 years" | "13 years" | "13 years" |
| Brickwork rating rationale, 11-Feb-2025, p.1 | "more than 24 years" | — | — |

The Q1 FY27 deck headlines the total: "Promoter-led leadership combines **101
Years of Experience**". The RHP total is 50 years. A biographical fact filed
with SEBI fifteen months earlier, and independently corroborated by a rating

**C5. [MAJOR] Tone and format shift with the results.** The Q3 FY26 deck (a
growth quarter) carries a signed "Management Commentary" slide from the CFO
(p.23) and a contact page naming the IR advisor (Adfactors, two named contacts)
and the Company Secretary (p.35). The Q4 FY26 deck — the quarter revenue fell
**9.5% sequentially**, from Rs 111.36 crore to Rs 100.73 crore
(Investor_Presentation_Q4FY26, p.23) — has **no management commentary slide at
all**, and its contact page (p.34) is reduced to a website address. The named IR
advisor and the Company Secretary were removed in the down quarter. No document
anywhere explains the sequential decline.

The Q1 FY27 deck then headlines "**Q1 FY27 accelerated growth and operating
leverage**" (p.4) on revenue of Rs 100.84 crore — flat against Q4's Rs 100.73
crore and still 9% below Q3 FY26. Three quarters of no sequential growth
presented as acceleration, by selecting the year-on-year frame.

**C6. [MAJOR] The capacity-doubling plan disappeared from the deck without a
walkback.** On the call, with rupee and unit specificity: "presently, we have
1,000 looms. So, we are planning to make it double by 2,000"; "we are going to
be around INR350 crores to INR400 crores"; "our planning is completely till
March 28"; "By 2027, we are planning to make it up to 1500" (Concall, p.7, 9,
16). The Q3 and Q4 decks both carry "Medium-term vision: To Double Production
Capacity in next 24 months" (p.34 / p.33). The Q1 FY27 deck carries **no
doubling target, no Rs 350-400 crore figure and no 2,000-loom target**. Its
"Expansion Programme" page offers "+192 Water-Jet Looms... Targeted Commercial
Production: December 2026" (p.19).

Measured against the plan, delivery is: loom count 1,208 (Q4 deck, p.6) → 1,212
(Q1 FY27 deck, p.4). **Four looms in two quarters**, against a stated path to
1,500 by March 2027. Note also that management's own base ("presently we have
1,000 looms") contradicts both decks, which say 1,208 — so it is not even clear
what "doubling" means.

**C7. [MAJOR] The rooftop solar project vanished for a quarter; the hybrid's
solar component shrank by 23%.** The 3.55 MW rooftop plant, promised for
"Commissioning: February 2026" (Investor_Presentation_Q3FY26, p.33), is
**absent from the entire Q4 FY26 deck** — removed from both the renewable page
(p.32) and the Future Outlook page (p.33), where the Q3 deck had read "3.55 MW
solar and 19.79 MW hybrid projects". It reappears without a date in the Q1 FY27
deck: "3.55 MW rooftop solar is operational" (p.17). A project promised for
February is missing from the March-quarter deck and asserted as done in the
June-quarter deck, with no commissioning date ever given.

Separately, the hybrid's solar component: every company document states "Solar
9.89 MW & Wind 9.90 MW" (Q3 deck p.33, Q4 deck p.32, Q1 FY27 deck p.17). The
Reg 30 filing of 29-Aug-2026 and the developer's attached letter both describe
"1 WTG location with Solar (**7.65 MW**)" and title the whole project "the 9.9
MW Hybrid Power Project", not 19.79 MW. A 2.24 MW reduction in the solar block,
unexplained, on the project carrying the savings claim in A1.

### GROUP D — GOVERNANCE AND THE ANNUAL REPORT

**D1. [CRITICAL] Rs 10.58 crore of loans out to five parties at 31-Mar-2026,

The Rs 5 crore unsecured 12% loan to Win Star Industries Private Limited

## PART 4: CREDIBILITY GRADE

**B05 grades C. I would grade lower.**

B05's grade rests on a fair reading of what it examined: the testable delivery
record is mixed rather than bad. Unit 4 and the working-capital object were
delivered on the money and on time under an independent monitoring agency; the
one specific margin guidance given on the call was met.

I grade lower because of a category of finding B05 did not reach: the company's
own filed documents contradict its own filed documents, on numbers a reader
would rely on.

**Is this a first-year listed company learning to report, or a company
concealing something? The evidence supports both readings on different items,
and I will not collapse them.**

*Learning to report* explains: the doubled margins on an entire filed page (C3);
the copy-pasted utilisation rows and the FY25 metric relabelled FY26 (C1); the
three different CINs (D12); the limited-review report filed against annual
results (D4); the incoherent related-party loan table (D6). These are the
signature of no controls and no review layer, not of design — nobody
concealing anything gains from printing an EBITDA margin of 49.75%. The Rs 1.50
lakh audit fee (D4), the exchange observation letter on Audit Committee
composition, the inactive Smart ODR registration and the un-updated Structured
Digital Database (D2) tell you exactly why there is no review layer.

*Something being managed* explains: an impossible Rs 18-20 crore savings figure
stated twice and held under direct challenge (A1); "we will invest in our
running business only... Nothing else" said while Rs 10.58 crore sat out on loan
to five parties including the promoter-group entity SEBI would search three
months later (D1); "this thing is not imported from there" said while the
company's own monitoring report listed nine import transactions (B1); promoter
experience doubled from the figure in the SEBI offer document (C4); a rating
agency's non-cooperation notice omitted from the Annual Report's rating
disclosure while the investment-grade rating is printed (D3); the Management
Commentary slide removed in the quarter the numbers fell (C5).

The distinguishing test is direction. Copy-paste errors point randomly.
**Every one of the second group points the same way — toward a better-looking
company.** That asymmetry is what moves my grade below C. What cannot yet be
told is intent, and I make no finding on it. What can be told is that no number
in this company's investor communication should be used without tracing it to a
primary filing, and that the absence of any earnings call since January 2026
means no one has been able to ask.
