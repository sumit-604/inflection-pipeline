# Indo Rama Synthetics: Q2 FY27 in plain words

Sources: the committed review (work/review_indorama_q2fy27.md, after loop 2), the A5 audit (COMPLETE) and work/notion-thesis.md. No agent was re-run.

1. **What the company does.** Indo Rama makes polyester at one large site in Nagpur. It buys oil linked raw materials and turns them into fibre, yarn and plastic resin for bottles. It earns the gap between what it pays for inputs and what it gets for its products. Revenue can rise just because oil prices rise, so the gap matters more than sales.

2. **What happened this quarter.** Profit was Rs 74 cr, three times last year's Rs 25 cr. Sales rose about 10% to Rs 1,341 cr. That sounds very strong, but last year's quarter was a weak one. Against an ordinary quarter, profit before tax rose about 41%. That is good, not dramatic.

3. **What improved.** Costs outside raw materials fell, and the currency loss shrank. Interest cost came down a little because the company owes less. These cost savings, not better selling prices, drove most of the profit gain.

4. **What got worse.** The gap between product prices and raw material costs did not widen. It was slightly worse than a year ago and clearly worse than last quarter. The first half of the year was weak overall: sales in the first quarter fell by more than a quarter, and management blames trouble in the Middle East. In the parent company alone, gross profit fell even though sales rose.

5. **Cash and the balance sheet.** Net debt fell by about Rs 261 cr in six months. That is real progress. About half came from cash the business earned. The other half came from collecting money from customers faster and paying suppliers slower. That second half can reverse. The company now collects from customers in about a week but pays suppliers in about five months. The filing does not say if it sells its bills to banks, which can make debt look smaller than it is.

6. **The capex decision.** The board approved about Rs 200 cr to build capacity for special fibre and yarn at the same Nagpur site. Output should start between October 2027 and April 2028. It will be paid for by a bank loan plus the company's own cash, but the filing does not say how much of each. It also does not say how the money splits between the company and its subsidiary.

7. **What management said or promised.** The chairman said Middle East events pushed up oil and raw material costs. Management says the new plant will widen the product range and move the company into higher value products. It also promises to keep improving results, but gives no numbers, no capacity figure and no expected sales from the new plant. The press release calls total income "revenue" and counts other income inside its profit figure, which makes the headline look better.

8. **Forensic red flags in plain words.** First, the company pays no tax. A normal tax rate would cut profit by about a quarter, and nobody outside knows how long this tax shield lasts. Second, the subsidiaries made between a quarter and half of group profit, but the filing gives no split. They seem to do a lot of trading, and they owe the parent at least Rs 183 cr. Third, a tax credit carried on the books makes up about 40% of the company's net worth. If that credit is worth less, the balance sheet is weaker than it looks.

9. **Flagged open items.** Cash quality cannot be judged yet, because the filings leave out factoring, the make-up of supplier credit and a large block of other assets. The audit closed every error after two correction rounds. Seven small wording notes remain, and none changes a number or the verdict. The verdict is "proceed with flags".

10. **What this means for the thesis.** This is a first look, with no earlier analysis on record. There is no Notion page, no thesis, no entry price and no tripwires to compare against. So no tripwire moved and no thesis is tested. The quarter shows a cheaper, less indebted business, but not yet a better one. The step up the quality ladder needs a wider price gap, and that has not shown up.

11. **What to watch next quarter.** The single best test is the gross margin in the December quarter, due by mid February 2027. Above about 24% would say the gap is widening. Below about 22% would say this quarter's gain was mostly cost cutting. Also watch whether tax starts to appear, whether customer collections stay this fast, and whether the bank loan for the new plant is signed and for how much.

12. **Before any deeper work.** The name has no spear line, so the full pipeline cannot run on it yet. A Step 1 intake would come first. The 20 questions for management, led by the tax shield, the price gap per tonne and profit by subsidiary, are the fastest way to settle the open points.
