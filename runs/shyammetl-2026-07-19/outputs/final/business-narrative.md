Transition real but price already exceeds value

Shyam Metalics is an integrated metals producer in eastern India, making sponge iron, wire rod, TMT bars, ferro alloys, stainless steel, aluminium foil and iron ore pellets across backward integrated plants with captive power. FY26 consolidated revenue was Rs 18,552 Cr and consolidated PAT to owners was Rs 1,070 Cr. It sells commodity and semi commodity long steel, ships intermediate products on merchant terms, and earns a growing slice from value added stainless and aluminium. Raw material runs about 74% of revenue by design, and the company is a price taker in a cyclical industry. The shares trade at Rs 1,022 for a market value near Rs 28,541 Cr.

A Rs 13,902 Cr capex programme is shifting the mix from commodity long steel toward stainless steel, aluminium foil and specialty products, where EBITDA per tonne runs roughly 5 to 10 times the carbon steel base. Through FY26 only about Rs 3,285 Cr had been executed. Seventy six percent remains, with commissioning dates stretching to FY29. The balance sheet is net cash and carries CRISIL AA plus with a stable outlook, so the build has been self funded so far. Four named global technology partners sit behind the new lines.

The screening posture is weak. Return on capital employed fell from 23% to 13% over FY18 to FY26, EBITDA margin compressed from 20.85% to 13.67%, and free cash flow was negative Rs 614 Cr in FY26 against active heavy capex. Gate 0 scored AVOID at 34 of 100. Part of that is a data gap: the block covering shareholding and contingent liabilities scored zero only because those documents were absent from the file. Underneath the gap the deterioration is genuine.

Cash conversion is the sharpest concern. At the standalone parent, operating cash flow to profit fell to 0.57 times in a year profit grew 39%, and standalone free cash flow was negative Rs 223 Cr. The mechanics trace to a roughly Rs 924 Cr swing in trade payables, an inventory build, and cash pumped into the core subsidiary. At the consolidated level working capital days improved, but finished goods inventory grew 54% against 15% revenue growth and the writedown expense jumped ninefold. The read is growth and timing rather than a broken model, yet the core subsidiary's own profit contribution roughly halved even as capital went in.

Management delivers the capex it promises on the quarter it promises it. The Ramsarup blast furnace and the CRM Phase II line both commissioned on schedule, and every call tracks capex spend line by line. Against that, the PLI stainless benefit and a flange beam line were quantified then dropped without explanation, a 90 MW power plant guided for a specific quarter was never confirmed commissioned, FY27 growth guidance jumped from a repeated 15 to 20% band to about 30% with no reconciliation, and management deflected outright on an Enforcement Directorate coal notice. The credibility grade is a B.

Accounting quality grades 5 out of 10. The parent holds equity in three entities that are themselves promoter shareholders owning 35% of the company, a circular cross holding the annual report never explains. The group reports a single business segment while its own notes show sharply different profitability by product line. About 23% of pre tax profit growth came from treasury gains. And 10 of 13 group entities carry an auditor cash loss qualification, with one small aluminium foil subsidiary flagged going concern adjacent.

Two live April 2026 actions sit over the promoter. The Enforcement Directorate provisionally attached Rs 160 Cr of assets at the material subsidiary under money laundering law tied to alleged illegal coal mining, and a pollution regulator ordered a plant cluster closed with conditional three month remediation relief. The Chairman and Managing Director roles were combined in one person in 2025. Offsetting that, a SAIL and NMDC calibre independent director joined in 2024, a Rs 1,385 Cr institutional placement explains the promoter stake drop as pure dilution and not a sale, pledge is zero, and succession passed to the second generation in an orderly way.

Peers independently confirm the demand backdrop and the safeguard duty story, though the duty lapsed in late 2025 before being reimposed, so sustained pricing is only partly supported. Every listed peer is expanding capex at the same time, so this is an industry wide capacity race and not a lone bet. The market runway is genuinely large, with a bottom up five year revenue path near Rs 33,800 Cr that sits about Rs 8,500 Cr below management's own FY31 target of Rs 42,500 Cr. Peer coverage has a real hole: there was no stainless, aluminium or nickel comparator in the set, the exact segments the growth thesis leans on.

The valuation concluded AVOID at Rs 1,022. Two destination multiples were built from the framework pillars, an RRM track anchored to return on capital at about 15.7 times and an additive track at about 18.5 times. The operator elected the sector ceiling of 20 times flat, applied to forward FY27 earnings of about Rs 45.67, above both self derived tracks. Even at that generous 20 times the base fair value is about Rs 913, with bear Rs 867 and bull Rs 1,006, all below the current price. The Hurdle Ratio stops, base 1.24 and bull 1.66 against a 1.953 pass mark, so a 25% compounding return is not reachable even on bull earnings. Probability weighted expected return is minus 3.3%. The 25% hurdle entry zone sits at Rs 416 to 468 with a margin of safety price of Rs 374, roughly 54% below today.

The devil's advocate attacked the constructive transition read and the AVOID survived. It argued the depressed 13% return on capital may be structural rather than a capex cycle trough, since capital work in progress is tiny at 0.85% of capital employed. It argued the core subsidiary shows falling returns on the fresh capital it absorbs, with its profit contribution roughly halved. It argued the single shared catalyst already under delivers, the first commissioned value added line printing lower realization quarter on quarter. And it argued standalone cash may be structural, the parent bankrolling a weakening subsidiary. Growth, moat and management all weakened under that attack, while valuation safety held, which is what keeps the call an avoid rather than a watch.
