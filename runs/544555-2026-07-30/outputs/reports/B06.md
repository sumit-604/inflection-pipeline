## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

**2A. Demand environment.** GRP's four calls describe a bifurcated demand picture: sustained U.S./Europe tyre-export weakness through most of FY26 driven by tariffs and macro softness, a sharp but incomplete recovery starting February 2026 once U.S. tariffs on Indian imports fell "from a potential high of 50% to about 18%" (GRP Ltd, Q3 FY26 call, 13-Feb-2026, p.2, Harsh Gandhi), and a consistently stronger domestic Indian market: "Domestic trends... have, however, been more supportive. Consumption rose on a year-on-year basis and GRP's market share improved by 200 basis points" (same call, p.3-4). By Q4 FY26, "our order book as of now is much stronger and higher than what we've had for most part of FY '26" (GRP Ltd, Q4 FY26 call, 18-May-2026, p.10, Harsh Gandhi). None of this speaks to Railways or rail-linked infrastructure demand — GRP's domestic-demand strength is tyre-sector and non-tyre rubber-goods demand, an entirely separate driver from any rail-capex cycle. This is a genuine gap for the triangulation exercise: the one industrial data point available (domestic Indian demand for a rubber sub-product improving through the year) is directionally consistent with "Indian domestic industrial activity is not weak," which is weak, indirect, non-corroborating background support at best for a broader "India infrastructure spend is picking up" narrative, but says nothing about Railways specifically.

**2B. Pricing and input costs.** Covered in Q5 above. Summary: GRP's key raw material (butyl inner tubes) saw 37-45% YoY cost inflation persisting three-plus quarters, while other virgin rubber grades were flat-to-down YoY (GRP Ltd, Q2 FY26 call, p.3). Pass-through to institutional/OEM customers lagged the cost increase by 2-3 quarters and was only "entirely obtained" from April 2026 pricing resets (GRP Ltd, Q4 FY26 call, p.9-10). Separately, recycled-plastics economics were hit by a ~30-45% YoY decline in virgin polypropylene prices and rising low-cost Chinese imports (GRP Ltd, Q3 FY26 call, p.4; Q4 FY26 call, p.14-15) — a reminder that "rubber and plastics raw material cost" is not a single monolithic trend; direction varies sharply by grade and end-market. This nuance matters for any pass-through assumption Ameenji's management may be making about its own input basket.

**2C. Capex cycle.** GRP is an aggressive, multi-year expander (INR250cr capex program across FY24-27 for pyrolysis, recovered carbon black, crumb rubber and reclaim-rubber technology, plus solar/renewable investment), but with a consistent pattern of timeline slippage on every major milestone (see Q6). With only one peer transcript-covered, it is not possible to characterize this as either "industry-wide capacity race" or "lone expander" — that call requires visibility into Rubfila's and Elgi Rubber's capex plans, which this stage does not have (screening-CSV only, no concalls). This should be recorded as a coverage gap rather than a finding either way.

**2D. Competitive mentions.** No mention of Ameenji Rubber, or any unmistakable description of it, appears anywhere across the four GRP transcripts. GRP's named competitive/customer references are entirely tyre-industry and non-tyre rubber-goods entities (tyre OEMs, ITTAC, bitumen modifiers, cement/steel buyers, petrochemical value-chain players) — no overlap with a Railways-components supplier's customer or competitor set.

**2E. Risks peers discuss that the main company does not (feeds missing-risks).** Two risk themes recur at GRP that are structurally relevant to weighing any Indian small-cap rubber-sector capex story, Ameenji's included, even though GRP itself has zero Railways exposure: (i) **execution/guidance-delay risk** — GRP's capex and margin-improvement targets slipped in three consecutive quarters (Q1→Q2→Q3→Q4 FY26, detailed in Q6 above), a pattern worth testing against Ameenji's own capacity or margin guidance if such guidance exists; (ii) **raw-material sourcing concentration risk** — GRP attributes its worst input-cost squeeze to new reclaim-rubber capacity additions in Pakistan and Egypt diverting butyl inner-tube supply away from India ("over the last year and a half, we have seen new manufacturing capacity for butyl reclaim coming up in countries like Pakistan and Egypt. And this has created a significant shortage in availability of tubes in India," GRP Ltd, Q1 FY26 call, 28-Jul-2025, p.10, Harsh Gandhi) — a reminder that rubber-sector raw-material risk in India can originate from geographically concentrated, policy-sensitive foreign supply, a general risk class worth checking against Ameenji's own natural/synthetic rubber sourcing, even though the specific corridor (Pakistan/Egypt butyl tubes) does not apply to Ameenji directly.
