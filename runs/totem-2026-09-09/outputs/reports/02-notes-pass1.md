**The Rs 5.9 cr Labour Codes charge — FOUND, but not classified as exceptional. Note 39 (PDF p.126-127):**
"On November 21, 2025, the Government of India notified four Labour Codes... In accordance with IND AS 19,
these changes constitute a plan amendment requiring immediate recognition of past service cost, resulting in
an incremental impact of ₹590 Lakhs (comprising gratuity and compensated absences) which has been recognized
as an employee benefit expense in the current reporting period." That is **₹5.90 cr**, matching the claim's
figure precisely — but it sits inside **Employee Benefits Expense (Note 24)**, not as a separately flagged
exceptional item. Cross-check against Note 31 (Employee Benefits, gratuity roll-forward, PDF p.109-110):
gratuity "Past Service Cost" for FY26 = ₹262.69 lakh (₹2.63cr), against nil in FY25; total gratuity expense
recognised in P&L jumped from ₹67.90 lakh to ₹310.66 lakh. Compensated-absences provision also newly appears
at ₹64.68 lakh non-current-service-linked current provision (Note 15B) versus nil FY25. The gratuity piece
(₹2.63cr) plus the compensated-absences piece together are what Note 39 sums to ₹5.90cr; the notes do not
give the compensated-absences component in isolation, so the ₹2.63cr/₹3.27cr gratuity/leave split cannot be
independently rebuilt from the disclosed tables to the last lakh, but the ₹5.90cr aggregate is explicit and
sourced.

**Accounting-quality read:** treating a one-time, externally-driven ₹5.90cr regulatory catch-up (2.4% of
revenue, 15% of FY26 PBT) as ordinary employee-benefit expense rather than tagging it exceptional is a
legitimate policy choice under Ind AS 19 (a plan amendment is a P&L item, not automatically "exceptional")
but it does reduce a reader's ability to see FY26's underlying operating margin without adjustment. Employee
benefits expense rose 14.6% YoY (₹44.03cr to ₹50.44cr) partly on this one item; without it, the underlying
increase is closer to 8.6%, roughly tracking revenue growth. 🟡 Watch — legitimate treatment, but an investor
comparing FY26 opex ratios to FY25 or to FY27 guidance should strip this ₹5.90cr out first, and the AR gives
no adjusted/non-GAAP reconciliation to make that easy.
