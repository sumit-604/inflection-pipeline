### 1C. Trigger evolution across the three quarters

- **Value-led group offers / daypart campaigns**: introduced/scaling in Q2 FY26 (explained as a deliberate, "not blanket" pricing/offer strategy, contrasted with a prior failed blanket-discount attempt ~18-24 months earlier per Manjeet Buaria's question, p.13) → credited as the primary SSSG driver in Q3 FY26 → validated at scale in Q4 FY26 with explicit "no price hike, entirely volume-led" framing. **Trend: strengthening, consistent.**
- **Marketing spend**: flagged as a temporary "measured investment" in Q2 FY26 (+1.2% YoY) → reframed in Q3 FY26 as "the new steady state... a structural step-up, not a temporary spike" (Rahul, p.9) → quantified precisely in Q4 FY26 as a permanent step to 3% of revenue from 1.9% (+110bps). **Trend: strengthening and being institutionalised — watch for whether 3% ceiling holds.**
- **Restaurant count target**: an implied ~270-275 by FY26 end (referenced retrospectively by analyst Viraj Mehta in the Q3 FY26 call, p.11, as guidance given "end of last year and even in first quarter" — outside this stage's 3-quarter window and therefore not independently verified here) → explicitly revised down to ~265 in Q3 FY26 → actual close was 262 (35 gross/32 net additions) per Q4 FY26 results. **Trend: timeline slippage, modest (~3-13 restaurant shortfall depending on which baseline is used), with explanation ("these are just accounting dates," commissioning timing).**
- **BBQ India mature ROM target**: historical aspiration of "20%+ " referenced by an analyst in Q3 FY26 (Pritesh Chheda, p.15) → management resets expectations to a staged "first 18%, from current 16%" → by Q4 FY26, guidance is restated at the consolidated (not BBQ India-specific) mature-portfolio level as 17-18% for FY27, with FY26 exit mature-portfolio ROM at 14.4% (Q4FY25: 13.8%). **Trend: target down-shifted from historical levels but consistently reiterated; not yet delivered within the 3-quarter window.**
- **Gross margin recovery**: guided at 67-68% in all three calls without ever being reached; Q4 FY26 management discloses gross margin "bottomed out in February 2026" and improved ~100bps in March/April. **Trend: consistent aspirational band, consistently missed on a quarterly basis, but internally-tracked monthly recovery evidence offered for the first time in Q4 FY26 — a new, more granular disclosure that partially substitutes for the still-unmet annual target.**
- **South India / Bangalore performance**: flagged as a "struggling market" historically; Q2 FY26 management declines to give a Bangalore-specific number but says South is performing similarly to non-South; by Q3 FY26, management says South has "meaningfully improved" and "converged with national performance." **Trend: strengthening, but never quantified at city level in any of the three calls (persistent specificity gap).**
- **No triggers were found to have quietly disappeared** across the three quarters; all major themes introduced in Q2 FY26 recur through Q4 FY26.

### 2B. Excuse pattern analysis

- **Gross margin miss**: framed largely as a deliberate strategic choice ("this compression is a result of deliberate choices," Q4 FY26, Rahul, p.6) — an honest-admission pattern, not deflection — but partially blends in external blame ("inflation in few input items, specifically in the Middle East business related to the West Asia crisis," same page). Classification: **mostly honest-admission, with a minority external-blame component.**
- **Back-end cost step-up (6.1%→7.1%)**: framed transparently as "deliberate structural investment" in central capabilities (COO, Chief Culinary Officer hires) rather than an unplanned cost overrun. Classification: **honest-admission.**
- **12 consecutive quarters of negative SSSG (Q2 FY26, analyst Mathur Rathi)**: management's response — "We don't see it obviously in that fashion... the negative numbers... are broadly in line with where the industry is" — leans toward **deflection/minimisation** rather than direct acknowledgment of the underlying problem, though it does not blame external parties by name.
- **Restaurant-count target slippage**: explained as timing/accounting ("these are just accounting dates"), a **deflection-leaning explanation** that avoids naming any operational or site-execution issue behind the delay.
- Pattern check: management does **proactively raise hard topics** in prepared remarks (e.g., the mature-ROM bridge disclosure in Q4 FY26 was volunteered, not extracted under pressure) but response tone to blunt questions in Q2 FY26 (the trough quarter) shows more defensiveness ("A negative 2% number doesn't mean that there is fire all around," Rahul to Mathur Rathi, p.11-12) than in Q3/Q4 FY26 once results improved. Management rarely says "we made a mistake" in plain terms; the closest is the Q4 FY26 acknowledgment of FY23 store closures ("some of the restaurants didn't work out... we closed some of these," p.14), offered only when directly pressed by an analyst (Rushabh Sharedalal) about the risk of repeating a prior over-expansion mistake.

## SECTION 3: COMPETITIVE INTELLIGENCE FROM CONCALLS

### 3A. Competitor commentary and credibility check

Management's consistent posture across all three calls is to decline specific competitor commentary while asserting industry-level observations:
- Q2 FY26 (Jay Vora question, Rahul, p.17): "Unfortunately, there is no reported number by the competition... we have not seen any increase in competitive intensity, at least from the competition in the same all-you-can-eat buffet category restaurants."
- Q3 FY26 (Pritesh, Rahul, p.16): "Not much... we have not seen the competitive intensity change. We have definitely not seen this to have increased."
- No competitor commentary volunteered in Q4 FY26.

Credibility check: the claim is repeated consistently (a positive credibility signal — no shifting story), but it is qualitative, unverifiable from these transcripts, and self-serving (supports the narrative that margin investments are company-specific choices, not defensive discounting against rising competition). This is precisely the kind of claim the peer-verification stage should test (see 4B).

### 3B. Industry and market intelligence

- GST rate reduction cited once as a possible industry-level demand tailwind, mentioned by analyst Devanshu Bansal and not disputed by Rahul (Q2 FY26, p.9) — never revisited or quantified in Q3 or Q4 FY26.
- Competitive intensity in the "all-you-can-eat buffet" category described as flat/unchanged across Q2 and Q3 FY26 (see 3A); general restaurant openings continue in trade areas but are not characterised as a threat.
- West Asia geopolitical crisis cited as driving input-cost inflation specifically in the Middle East business (Q4 FY26, Rahul, p.6, p.11-12); LPG price increases also cited as a general inflationary pressure (Q4 FY26, p.10-11).
- No import/export, technology-shift, or regulatory-pipeline commentary of substance beyond the GST mention.
