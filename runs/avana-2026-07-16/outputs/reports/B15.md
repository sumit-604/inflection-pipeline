## 0. THE ONE FINDING THAT MATTERS MOST (read this first)

**The 25% hurdle clears only because the exit is priced at a multiple that assumes NO de-rating — and that assumption silently breaches the 25x sector cap in trailing terms.**

B11 applies destination PE 20.9x to FY30 **forward** EPS Rs 14.82 to get an FY29 exit price of Rs 322 (B11 Section 3). Decompose that exit:
- Exit price ex-cash = 20.9 × 14.82 = Rs 309.8 (B11).
- FY29 **trailing** EPS at exit = Rs 11.40 (B11 Section 2 ladder).
- Implied **trailing** PE at exit = 309.8 / 11.40 = **27.2x**.

So the "conservative destination PE of 20.9x" is, in the currency the market actually quotes (trailing), **27.2x at exit** — essentially the same rich 28.1x trailing multiple the stock carries TODAY (146/5.19, B11 Section 1B Sanity). The base case therefore assumes **zero de-rating over a 3-year hold** for a Cables/Industrial business whose framework-earned multiple is ~21x and whose sector cap is an ABSOLUTE 25x (B10 sector_cap_row; Section 1B Sector Cap Table).

Two consequences the pipeline did not surface prominently:
1. **The pass is method-manufactured.** On the literal trailing-exit basis the Hurdle Ratio is 1.63 — a FAIL (B11 hurdle_ratio.literal_trailing_exit_base). It only becomes 2.12 PASS by pricing the FY29 exit on FY30 earnings, i.e. buying one extra compounding year you have not held (B11 hurdle_ratio.base). This is precisely the SFL failure mode named in my mandate: mixing a trailing current PE (28.1x) with a forward-exit destination to fabricate a pass.
2. **The sector cap is breached in trailing terms.** 27.2x trailing at exit > 25x absolute cap (Section 1B: "The Sector Cap is ABSOLUTE — UA can never breach it"). Override 2 authorises the forward method and the injected framework/deliberation wins — but the operator must SEE that the authorised method delivers an exit multiple above the sector ceiling it was told is inviolable.

**Honest de-rating math.** Price the FY29 exit at 20.9x **trailing** on FY29 EPS 11.40: FV = 20.9 × 11.40 + 12 = Rs 250. CAGR from 146 = (250/146)^(1/3) − 1 = **19.6%** — below the 25% hurdle. That is the same answer as the trailing-exit HR of 1.63. The 25% case exists only in the forward-priced frame.

Everything below is downstream of this.

## 3. VALUE TRAP TEST

**What makes it LOOK good:** 40% ROCE, net cash, 25.8% historical revenue CAGR (B10), a "reasonable" 20.9x multiple, 6.9x asymmetry, a grid-modernisation tailwind.

**What is actually underneath:**
- The 40% ROCE is **ex-cash and forward-unproven**; deployed-capital ROCE once the Rs 22 Cr funds a 245-day WC cycle and 6-years-late capex is likely closer to the headline 27.8% (Section 1). A 245-day cash cycle in a business trying to grow 30% is a **working-capital furnace**: 245 days on Rs 84 Cr revenue already ties ~Rs 56 Cr; grow revenue to ~Rs 200 Cr and net WC balloons past ~Rs 130 Cr, far beyond the Rs 22 Cr cash cushion — the growth self-funds a cash drain, not free cash flow.
- The 20.9x is a **forward** multiple = 27.2x trailing at exit (Section 0). Not reasonable — rich, and above the sector cap in trailing terms.
- The 25.8% history spans an IPO-year inflection off a sub-scale FY21-22 base (revenue 22.56 → 14.95 → 20.43 Cr, B01 FLAG-GATE0). Extrapolating 30% forward from a rebased micro-cap is exactly the trap.

**Is it a melting ice cube?** No — it is genuinely growing. It is the OTHER trap: a real grower whose entire incremental value is staked on one binary, twice-priced event (KIADB) and held aloft by SME momentum rather than institutional validation or primary-document confirmation. **A hope-priced-as-certainty trap, not a melting-ice-cube trap.**

## 5. CATALYST DEPENDENCY — the SHARED CATALYST single point of failure

This is the structural heart of the case and it is worse than the flag implies.

**The SHARED CATALYST (B11 shared_catalyst_flag: true; FTTCP verdict; B10 SHARED-CATALYST-EXECUTION-RISK):** KIADB facility commissioning drives BOTH Pillar 1 (operating ROCE holding ~40% as the cash deploys productively) AND the revenue ladder (30% vs SOM 15%). One event, two pillars, no diversification.

**Why it is a circular single point of failure — the point the pipeline under-stated:**
- Override 1 strips the Rs 22 Cr cash from the ROCE denominator BECAUSE it is about to be deployed into KIADB — that is what produces 40% and base PE 26.1x.
- But if KIADB slips, the cash is NOT deployed — it stays idle in deposits earning ~7% pre-tax. Idle cash left in the business drags ROCE toward the headline 27.8%, and the strip that produced 40% becomes unjustified.
- **So in the exact scenario where the catalyst fails, the 40% ROCE that anchors the 26.1x base evaporates simultaneously with the revenue ladder.** Pillar 1 and Pillar 3 do not just share a catalyst — they share a FAILURE mode. There is no state of the world where growth disappoints but the ROCE base survives.

**History and status:** original deadline 1-Jun-2020; extended to 22-May-2026 — a ~6-year slip; the DRHP flags KIADB action risk if missed again (B08). As of run date 2026-07-16 the extended deadline has **already passed with zero primary confirmation of commissioning** (B08 searches_skipped: "Confirmation of whether KIADB 22-May-2026 commercial-production deadline was actually met"). We are not waiting for a future catalyst; we are waiting to find out whether an already-due catalyst failed.

**What happens to the price while you wait:** If commissioning slips 2-3 more years (consistent with its own history), revenue holds ~15% (SOM), the cash sits idle, ROCE narrative resets to ~21x base, and the 28x trailing multiple de-rates toward the sector-real ~18-20x on lower growth. The cash multiplier that had been generously held at 0.80x was really 0.65x, and CFO turned negative again as the company funded the working-capital furnace to chase growth — echoing FY23's minus Rs 0.32 Cr CFO (B01). The single strong FY26 CFO print proved to be an IPO-year collection artifact, not a trend.

And the price simply gave back its listing momentum. The stock had doubled from Rs 77.50 to Rs 146 in under six months on a thin SME float and had already been queried by the NSE (B08); when the growth story stalled, there was no institutional holder base to defend it (FII/DII never confirmed, B10), and it round-tripped. Nothing here required fraud or catastrophe — only that a factory 6 years late stayed late, that a 245-day cash cycle behaved like a 245-day cash cycle, and that a richly-priced micro-cap normalised to its sector. Every one of those was visible in the analysis as a flag.
