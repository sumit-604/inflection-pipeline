**Six mandatory diagnostics:**

1. **Did revenue grow YoY?** Yes, +7.66% (Rs 286.73 → 308.69 Cr). Modest; roughly in line with the Notion FY28E implied ~6.5% CAGR path. Annualised Q1 run-rate ~Rs 1,235 Cr vs FY28E target Rs 1,265 Cr — the top line is already near the destination, so the thesis is a MARGIN story, not a revenue-growth story.

2. **Did operating EBITDA margin expand?** Yes, materially: Q1 FY27 14.5% vs Q1 FY26 ~0.0% = ~+1,450 bps. In Q1 FY26 core operations were break-even at EBITDA level; the pivot to shorter-cycle, higher-margin services is showing in the numbers, not just the narrative.

3. **Did core operating PBT (ex-OI) grow YoY?** Yes — and this is the headline. Core operating PBT swung from Rs (9.08) Cr LOSS to Rs +36.94 Cr profit, a +Rs 46.02 Cr swing. Unusually for a small-cap, reported PAT growth is BACKED by core improvement rather than treasury.

4. **What drove the gap between core-operating-PBT growth and reported-PAT growth?** The gap runs the OPPOSITE of the usual failure mode. Core PBT improved +Rs 46.02 Cr, but Other Income FELL Rs 21.22 Cr (a drag), finance costs helped +Rs 1.07 Cr, depreciation +Rs 0.16 Cr, tax was a Rs 2.83 Cr drag (ETR rose 0% → 4.1%), and discontinued-ops loss widened Rs 1.03 Cr. Net continuing-PBT change +Rs 24.80 Cr = core +46.02 less OI 21.22. The headline PAT growth UNDERSTATES the operational improvement because Other Income shrank.

5. **Are D&A and finance costs scaling faster than revenue?** No. Depreciation −4.7% and finance costs −19.2% against revenue +7.66%. No capex-absorption gap; consistent with the lighter-working-capital, shorter-cycle pivot and the Durgapur depreciation halt (note 2(i)).

6. **Is Other Income concentration changing?** Yes, favourably for earnings quality. Other Income fell 40% YoY (Rs 53.10 → 31.88 Cr) and dropped from 120.6% of PBT to 46.3%. The quarter is LESS reliant on non-operating income than a year ago. Still, at 46.3% of PBT, Other Income remains a large block worth watching (Q1FY27 total Rs 31.88 Cr; source not disclosed in a bare results filing — routed to Questions Q6/Q7).

## PLAIN-LANGUAGE BRIEF (mandatory standing deliverable)

### 1. SUMMARY NARRATIVE

GE Power India put up a clean, operationally-driven quarter. Revenue from continuing operations was Rs 308.69 Cr, up 7.7% year-on-year, but the story is margin, not top-line. A year ago core operations barely broke even; this quarter they earned Rs 36.94 Cr of profit before tax excluding other income, and the operating margin (stripping out treasury income) reached 14.5%. Net profit rose 66% year-on-year to Rs 52.55 Cr. What makes it credible is that the growth is real: other income actually FELL 40%, so the profit came from the business, not from interest on the cash pile. This is the first genuinely clean quarter since the BHEL settlement, because the earlier quarters were flattered by one-off provision reversals and this one has none. Management says the pivot to shorter-cycle, higher-margin services is working, and for once the numbers agree with the words. The company also confirmed it is exiting the low-return Durgapur unit by demerging and transferring it to JSW Energy, targeted to complete by mid-2027, and the loss-making FGD (pollution-control EPC) contracts have been terminated, which is exactly what the thesis wanted. The cautions: order backlog dropped 41% to Rs 1,545 Cr, mostly from the FGD exit, and we cannot yet see whether new services orders are replacing it. The tax rate is only 4% because of past losses, so reported profit is flattered and will fall when those losses run out. Being a Q1 filing, there is no balance sheet, so we cannot check cash, receivable days, or related-party lending — the three things most likely to hide a problem. The stock is Rs 907 against a re-entry ceiling of Rs 500, so despite a good quarter there is no action: it stays on the watchlist, and the next real test is the September-quarter balance sheet and the concall.

### 2. SECTOR INTELLIGENCE

The Indian thermal power-equipment cycle is bifurcating: legacy long-cycle EPC and flue-gas-desulphurisation (FGD) work is fading as regulatory FGD mandates stall and utilities defer capex, while the higher-margin aftermarket — services, upgrades, and spares on the installed base — is the durable pool. This quarter's evidence fits: the company terminated two FGD EP contracts (Jaypee Bina, Nigrie, Rs 774.9 Cr of backlog removed) and is leaning into services (this quarter's filing, media release L590-592). [Provenance: this quarter's filing.] The structural tailwind the thesis leans on — power-plant life-extension and emissions retrofits on an ageing Indian thermal fleet, plus optionality in data-centre power and industrial cooling via GE Vernova IP — is NOT quantified in this filing; those remain a Notion/prior-work narrative pending concall confirmation. [Provenance: prior Notion thesis; NOT disclosed this quarter.] Regulation cuts both ways: the FGD mandate timeline drives the services opportunity, and the four Labour Codes (notified 21 Nov 2025) already cost a one-off Rs 42.57 Cr provision in FY26 (note 3/4) — a sector-wide cost step that is now behind the P&L. [Provenance: this quarter's filing.]

### 3. BUSINESS-MODEL INTELLIGENCE

GE Power India makes money two ways: (a) equipment and erection services for power generation (the cost-heavy, long-cycle legacy), and (b) services, upgrades and spares (shorter-cycle, higher-margin, lighter working capital). The deliberate model drift this quarter is TOWARD (b): cost of materials and erection was Rs 187.37 Cr (61% of revenue) vs Rs 194.97 Cr a year ago on higher revenue, employee costs fell, finance costs fell 19%, and depreciation is low and falling — partly because depreciation on the Durgapur assets was halted once they were classified held-for-sale (note 2(i)). [Provenance: this quarter's filing.] The consolidation tells you the business is essentially the standalone entity: the subsidiary (GE Power Boilers Services) is dormant — consolidated cost lines match standalone to the rupee — and the only consolidation dynamic is the NTPC GE joint venture, whose contribution collapsed to Rs 1.18 Cr from Rs 10.60 Cr last quarter (A3-F2-01, A3-F3-01). [Provenance: this quarter's filing.] Two model caveats the filing exposes: reported profit is under-taxed (4.1% ETR on carried-forward losses, so real cash-tax economics are worse than they look), and unit economics on a cash-conversion basis cannot be verified until the half-year balance sheet. The Notion model assumes FY28 revenue Rs 1,265 Cr at 12% EBITDA and Rs 100-110 Cr PAT; the clean 14.5% operating margin this quarter is running AHEAD of that assumption. [Provenance: prior Notion thesis vs this quarter's filing.]

### 4. COMPETITION INTELLIGENCE

The company competes in Indian power-generation equipment and services against BHEL (the dominant domestic OEM and, notably, its former FGD counterparty and now-settled litigant), Thermax and ISGEC in boilers and environmental/emissions equipment, and Siemens Energy and other multinationals in the high-technology services and upgrades niche. Where GE Power India wins is the installed-base aftermarket on GE-origin and multi-OEM (oOEM) equipment, backed by GE Vernova's technology library and (per the thesis) an implicit parent corporate guarantee. [Provenance: prior Notion thesis; parent linkage confirmed by IR contact GE Vernova at L28 this quarter, but the Rs 4,000 Cr guarantee status is NOT disclosed this quarter.] Where it is structurally weaker: scale and balance-sheet heft versus BHEL, and dependence on the parent for technology, guarantees, and — until the "supply-chain independence" timeline firms up — sourcing. The competitive risk to watch is order intake: a 41% backlog decline (this quarter's filing) is tolerable if it is the intended FGD exit being replaced by services wins, but dangerous if peers are taking the services and upgrades share the pivot depends on. The non-GE (oOEM) order percentage — the cleanest measure of whether the company can win beyond captive GE work — is a Notion checklist item (green ≥50%, red <40%) and was NOT disclosed this quarter; it is the sharpest competitive question for the concall. [Provenance: prior Notion checklist; NOT disclosed this quarter.]
