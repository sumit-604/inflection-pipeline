## Business Understanding Narrative

**What does it do, and how does it make money?** Solara makes active pharmaceutical ingredients (APIs) for drug makers. About 75% of sales go to regulated markets (AR26, p.22). It was demerged from Strides Shasun in 2017 (01-CRISIL). Management splits it in two. The base business sells 60-plus catalogue APIs (AR26, p.6) from Cuddalore, Mangalore and Ambernath (Jul-26 call, pp.12-13). In FY26 it earned Rs 1,041.40 crore at a 23.5% EBITDA margin. The Ibuprofen business runs one dedicated plant at Puducherry. It earned Rs 333.74 crore with an EBITDA loss of Rs 52.96 crore (AR26, p.4).

**What is changing?** The company is leaving Ibuprofen. Puducherry and Vizag hold 10,000 to 12,000 tonnes of capacity against about 3,000 tonnes sold (Feb-26 call, p.3). Bankers run a strategic review. Base revenue crossed Rs 300 crore a quarter in Q4 FY26 and held for two more quarters (Q2 deck, p.7).

**Why now?** Q2 FY27 was the best quarter in five years. Revenue was Rs 417.34 crore, up 33%. PAT was Rs 28.46 crore against a Rs 10.10 crore loss a year earlier (Q2 results, p.4). Ibuprofen EBITDA was near breakeven, a Rs 0.2 crore loss (Q2 deck, p.8). All Rs 446.62 crore of rights money is in (Q2 results, p.7).

**What must be true?** The Ibuprofen review must end without another hit to the balance sheet. The base gross margin must hold in the low 50s. Sales to Strides, the promoter's other company, must be arm's length trade.

**What breaks it?** The Ibuprofen price war returns after the West Asia solvent shortage ends. The base gross margin keeps sliding. Or the exit costs more than expected. The CFO calls full recovery of the roughly Rs 700 crore deployed in Ibuprofen "slightly farfetched" (Jul-26 call, pp.7 and 9).

## 2. Business model and archetype

|---|---|---|

Source: AR26, p.4. The split is "management estimated proforma" and gets restated (Q4 FY26 deck, filings/SOLARA_15052026132136, p.4). The statutory accounts report one segment (AR26, p.169).

The base engine is a catalogue API written into a customer's own drug filing. Switching means re-filing, so orders repeat. The top 15 to 20 products make about 75% of base sales (May-26 call, pp.7-8). Ibuprofen is the opposite. Solara buys its starting material and is not backward integrated (May-26 call, p.6). In July 2025 the CEO agreed that current Ibuprofen prices do not cover the cost of manufacturing (filings/SOLARA_29072025162637, p.12).

**Archetype, decided on the evidence: not an outsourcing partner.** In July 2025 the founder spoke of a Rs 400 to 500 crore CRAMS business in three to four years (filings/SOLARA_29072025162637, p.15). In May 2026 the CFO put CRAMS at Rs 10 to 12 crore a year with no strong pipeline (May-26 call, p.8). The Synthix demerger is on hold (AR26, p.11). **Ibuprofen is a commodity converter, so Section 1B v3.7 Amendment 17 binds that slice. The base is a build-to-spec component maker:** design wins are customer filings, content per unit is the API, and input cost is passed through (Jul-26 call, p.6).

- Puducherry (Ibuprofen): four observations on an unannounced inspection, VAI, EIR 24-Apr-2026 (filings/SOLARA_28042026165601, p.2).

The moat has a gap. No meaningful new DMF was filed in four years (May-26 call, p.10). The first went in April 2026 (May-26 call, p.14). New products add revenue only from FY29 to FY30.

The group gross margin did not recover; it sits at 47%. The base gross margin fell 12.4 points in six quarters. The CFO blames Q1 FY27 on about Rs 17 to 18 crore of cost pass-through and puts the adjusted figure at 54.5% (Jul-26 call, p.7). EBITDA rose on revenue over a flat cost base and a smaller Ibuprofen loss.

Debt fell from Rs 999 crore at March 2024 to about Rs 500 to 530 crore at May 2026 (01-CRISIL). Net debt was Rs 614 crore at March 2026 (AR26, p.22) and Rs 500 crore at September 2026. Of the Rs 114.1 crore H1 cut, Rs 100 crore was rights money and Rs 14.1 crore came from operations (Q2 deck, p.10). Net debt rose in Q2, from Rs 479.5 crore in June (Q2 deck, p.7).

**Classification: margin-led through operating leverage, not gross margin, plus an asset event: equity-funded deleveraging.**

## 6. The transition and the quality ladder

FROM R1, commodity price-taker, at company level: FY26 ROCE was 4.7% (AR26, p.24) and Ibuprofen sells below cost. TO R3, spec'd supplier, as claimed for the base: 50% gross margin, 23% to 25% EBITDA, supply locked in by customer filings.

The founder's claim, quoted once: "the non-ibuprofen business now trades at a significant gross margin of 56% and EBITDA of 25%" (Feb-26 call, p.4).

Two readings. One: the climb is a mix shift; cut the R1 line and an R3 business is left. Two: the base margin is itself drifting, from 62.7% to 50.3%, and base capital employed is NOT FOUND, so its ROCE cannot be checked. A two-rung jump is a red flag under the base rate. **The observation that separates them is base gross margin and base ROCE after the Ibuprofen exit.** Record the climb as claimed R1 to R3, evidenced as a mix shift only.

## 8. Proof check

The triggers claim base growth and deleveraging. Both show. Base revenue rose 37% in Q2 FY27 and 30% in H1 (Q2 deck, p.6). PAT rose three quarters running: Rs 9.60, 16.31 and 28.46 crore (step 5 table). H1 EBITDA of Rs 140.4 crore covers finance cost of Rs 39.42 crore 3.6 times (Q2 deck, p.9; Q2 results, p.4). That clears CRISIL's upgrade trigger of 3 times (01-CRISIL).

**Name the absence.** Group gross margin has not recovered. The Q2 Ibuprofen turn rests on prices during a solvent shortage, which the CFO called "tactical" (Jul-26 call, p.15). One quarter near breakeven is not a reset.

## 9. Flags from the documents

- **Promoter-group customer.** FY26 sales to Strides Pharma Science were Rs 328.97 crore, 24% of revenue, up from Rs 214.97 crore. Receivables from Strides were Rs 160.45 crore, 37% of all receivables (AR26, p.168). The AGM seeks approval for up to Rs 450 crore of FY27 sales to Strides, 35.07% of FY26 turnover (AR26, p.253). Flag, load-bearing.
- **Unaudited, restated split.** Q3 FY26 base EBITDA was Rs 608 Mn in the February deck and Rs 520 Mn after restatement (filings/SOLARA_06022026140941, p.5; Q4 FY26 deck, p.5).
- **Exit loss risk.** Ibuprofen capital is about Rs 700 crore (Jul-26 call, p.7). Vizag CWIP of Rs 182.49 crore is overdue (AR26, p.143). Goodwill of Rs 364.90 crore is a key audit matter (AR26, p.115). A Rs 4.50 crore loss on Vizag CWIP was booked in Q2 (Q2 results, p.7).
- **Tax litigation.** Rs 300.36 crore is before CIT(A) and Rs 105.08 crore before ITAT (AR26, p.123). The Rs 161.92 crore AY2019-20 demand was rectified to nil on 08-Aug-2026 (filings/SOLARA_28082026144034, p.2).
- **Untaxed profit.** No tax is charged. Unrecognised deferred tax assets are Rs 156.36 crore, plus Rs 607.24 crore of disputed goodwill depreciation claims (AR26, p.148).
- **Liquidity history.** At March 2026 net current liabilities were Rs 35.81 crore, and the board's going-concern note leaned on the final call (filings/SOLARA_15052026131424, p.11). By September 2026 net current assets were Rs 243 crore (Q2 results, p.5). The audit opinion is unmodified with no emphasis of matter (AR26, pp.114-115). CARO reports no loan default (AR26, p.123).
- **A one-off most years.** The November 2023 Puducherry fire, its insurance claim still unrecognised (AR26, p.178). The Q2 FY26 Mangalore shutdown (Nov-25 call, p.3).
- CEO pay was Rs 6.05 crore in a loss year (AR26, p.168).

## 10. Independent check: the credit rating

CRISIL upgraded to BBB+/Stable/A2 on 22-Jun-2026, from BBB/Negative in 2024. Reasons: debt cut from Rs 999 crore to about Rs 500 to 530 crore, FY26 interest cover near 2 times, and cash accrual of Rs 120 to 150 crore against debt service of Rs 30 to 60 crore a year. The main weakness named is Ibuprofen's negative margin. Bank limit use was 93%. Downgrade trigger: operating margin below 9% (01-CRISIL). The agency backs the balance sheet and stays neutral on the transition. Its text gives March 2026 gearing as 1.9 times, which conflicts with AR26 p.24; treated as an error.

## 11. Posture: the Transition Decision Matrix

- **Proof gate: FIRED on base growth and deleveraging, NOT FIRED on the Ibuprofen exit,** which is the transition itself.
- **Ugliness: ARTIFACT-OF-CLIMB, conditional.** The losses sit in a line being exited. If Ibuprofen stays, they become structural.
- **Recognition gap: CLOSED on earnings.** Price basis: screener.in service record, 18-Oct-2026, not a filed document. Rs 3,381 crore is 30.5x to 39.3x untaxed FY27 profit (step 13), above the R3 neighbourhood of about 19x.

**Posture: EARNINGS-ONLY.** If the review fails, it slides to PRICED NARRATIVE (TRAP).

## 12. Verdict card

**WATCH.**

The climb is real in parts. Base revenue jumped a level, debt halved, interest cover passed 3 times and CRISIL upgraded. The stock went from a Rs 422 low to Rs 697 (market-data, service record). The market prices the base without Ibuprofen losses, and the step that removes them has slipped twice. A `/step1` today would value an outcome not yet announced.

**Load-bearing facts to verify first**
1. Ibuprofen review outcome, due within 12 weeks of 16-Oct-2026: exit price against about Rs 700 crore deployed, and the impairment on Vizag CWIP and goodwill.
2. What Solara sells to Strides, at what price, and in which line: Rs 328.97 crore in FY26 and Rs 160.45 crore receivable.
3. Base gross margin net of pass-through, Q3 FY27, against the 62.7% of Q4 FY25.
4. Base capital employed and ROCE.
5. Cuddalore USFDA status.

**What would change the view**
- A clean Ibuprofen exit with a contained write-off, base gross margin back at 53% or more, and the Strides share falling moves this to PROCEED.
- Ibuprofen losses returning to Rs 10 to 15 crore a quarter, or receivables from Strides rising again, moves it toward PASS.

No Role 1 valuation was run and no target price is set. This verdict decides only whether the name earns a full `/step1`. It is not a position.
