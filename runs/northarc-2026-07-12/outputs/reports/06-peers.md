### Q2. Cost of funds "bottomed" at 8.5%-8.6% in Q4 FY26, no further reduction expected

|---|---|

| Verdict | **CONTRADICTED** |

CGCL (May-2026, Q4 FY26 call): cost of borrowing fell a further 18-20bps in the quarter to ~9.0%-9.1%, and management guides "10 to 20 bps reduction this year" plus another 20bps on a hoped-for rating upgrade — deceleration in pace, but explicitly not a hard stop. ## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

**2A. Demand environment.** Three of four peers (MASFIN, UGROCAP, CGCL) describe a gradual MSME demand recovery building through H2 FY26 after a stressed H1: MASFIN's Kamlesh Gandhi repeatedly frames the "overleveraging cycle" as "coming to a logical end" (Nov-2025, Jan-2026 calls) with sequential growth acceleration each quarter (4%→6% QoQ); UGROCAP tightened underwriting through mid-FY26 on overleverage signals before pivoting fully to a strategic realignment; CGCL's secured, gold-heavy book showed no demand softness at any point in the period. FEDFINA is the outlier and the most cautious voice — its Oct-2025 commentary on "rural India... incomes have been stagnant" is the only peer voice explicitly flagging rural softness rather than recovery, in tension with NORTHARC's more bullish rural/MFI framing.

**2B. Pricing and input costs.** Consistent across all four peers: cost of funds declined through FY26, decelerating in pace toward year-end but not flattening to zero in any peer's own guidance (see Q2 above). No peer reports a funding-cost "bottom" concurrent with NORTHARC's claimed Q4 FY26 plateau.

**2C. Capex cycle (branch/network expansion).** Genuinely split, not industry-wide: CGCL is an aggressive, sustained expander throughout the period (adding branches every single quarter, guiding 700-800 more over two years) in gold loans specifically. FEDFINA is a moderate, disciplined expander (100-150 gold branches/year while consolidating LAP branches into gold premises to hold total count roughly flat). MASFIN is explicitly conservative — branch count stabilized, growth "at a lesser pace than anticipated." UGROCAP's branch build is now complete and deliberately halted in favor of productivity ("no new incremental opex... expansion phase is now complete"). NORTHARC's single-quarter 23% rural/MFI branch surge is therefore a **lone-expander situation relative to the peer closest in borrower profile (MASFIN)**, not evidence of an industry-wide capacity race into rural/underserved lending.

**2D. Competitive mentions.** No peer transcript mentions Northern Arc Capital, NORTHARC, or an unmistakable description of it by name across all 14 transcripts reviewed.

**2E. Risks peers discuss that NORTHARC does not (per B05).** (i) Rural income stagnation and MFI-spillover stress into adjacent secured lending (FEDFINA, Oct-2025; UGROCAP, Nov-2025) — NORTHARC frames the rural/MFI segment as a recovery story rather than flagging spillover risk into its own book. (ii) Structural, capital-inefficiency of co-lending/DA income — gains do not accrete to regulatory net worth until realized, creating repeat capital-raise pressure (UGROCAP, Apr-2026: "this business requires more capital... if you keep raising more capital and keep diluting shareholders at low value, the value creation actually becomes destructive") — a risk theme directly relevant to NORTHARC's placement/fee franchise that is absent from its own disclosure per B05. (iii) New co-lending guideline (effective Jan-2026) causing "2 quarters of disruption to reset the new process" industry-wide (UGROCAP, Aug-2025) and a temporary co-lending volume slowdown as existing contracts expire and are migrated (CGCL, May-2026) — a transition-risk theme in the exact regulatory window NORTHARC's FLDG/ECL claim (Q4) sits in, which NORTHARC's disclosed materials do not address. (iv) Gold-price-volatility / LTV-breach management as an active, quantified risk discipline (CGCL, FEDFINA) — sector risk-management theme with no NORTHARC analog but illustrative of the granularity peers disclose that NORTHARC's materials (per B05) increasingly do not (segment credit-cost disclosure narrowed to annual-only).

## PART 4: TRIANGULATION SUMMARY

- **Claims verified:** 0 of 7
- **Claims partially verified:** 0 of 7
- **Claims contradicted:** 4 of 7 (Q2 cost-of-funds plateau, Q3 industry-wide MSME stress framing, Q5 AUM growth outpacing industry, Q6 co-lending "moat" framing) — priority items for synthesis
- **Claims unverifiable:** 3 of 7 (Q1 MFI sector growth/contraction figures, Q4 RBI FLDG/ECL guideline impact, Q7 rural/MFI branch re-acceleration) — no peer runs a comparable MFI book, and one claim (Q4) concerns a regulatory interpretation no peer has yet disclosed comparably

**The single most consequential contradiction:** the cost-of-funds "bottomed, no further reduction expected" claim (Q2) is contradicted by two peers (UGROCAP explicitly: "cost of borrowings will continue improving," fifth consecutive quarterly decline; CGCL: guided 10-20bps more plus a rating-upgrade kicker) in the same Q4 FY26 window. This matters because NORTHARC's FY27 margin and RoA guidance has already been flagged in B05 as diluted and repeatedly pushed out across three consecutive calls — a sector-wide continued cost-of-funds tailwind that NORTHARC is declining to bank on (or has genuinely exhausted ahead of peers) is directly load-bearing for whether the RoA trajectory story holds.

**The single strongest independent confirmation:** none of the seven specific claims achieved two-peer corroboration and therefore none is VERIFIED under this stage's rules. The closest thing to positive triangulation is indirect: the broad MSME demand-recovery backdrop through H2 FY26, corroborated by three of four peers (MASFIN, UGROCAP, CGCL) independently describing an improving credit environment after a stressed H1 FY26 — this is directionally consistent with, and lends some plausibility to, NORTHARC's general growth optimism, even though it does not confirm any of the seven specific numeric claims tested.

**Overall assessment — supports, complicates, or undercuts:** the peer set **complicates** NORTHARC's narrative on balance. On the positive side, the general MSME demand-recovery story is broadly corroborated and NORTHARC's rural/MFI focus is not directly contradicted (peers simply don't operate there, so it cannot be settled either way). On the negative side, four of seven testable, specific claims are contradicted by peer evidence: the funding-cost tailwind may be more idiosyncratic to NORTHARC than sector-wide as framed; the "industry-wide" characterization of Q3 MSME stress is disputed by the peer with the most directly comparable experience; the "outpacing the industry" AUM growth claim does not hold against at least one directly comparable secured lender growing 2-3x faster; and the co-lending fee franchise "moat" framing is directly undercut by the peer running the most similar model, which characterizes that income line as structurally thin and is actively retreating from it. None of this proves NORTHARC's underlying numbers are wrong — peers may simply differ in segment, strategy, or timing — but it removes independent support for several claims management used to frame the investment case, and surfaces two risk themes (rural/MFI spillover stress, co-lending capital-inefficiency) that NORTHARC's own disclosures did not raise.
