## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

**2A DEMAND ENVIRONMENT.** All three peers describe steady-to-strong demand across FY26-FY27, driven by structural shifts (organized-vs-unorganized consolidation for Metropolis/Krsnaa; pharma/biologics R&D shift to India for Vimta), NOT a demand boom. Vimta and Metropolis both flag a temporary Q1 FY27 (Apr-Jun 2026) softness tied to the "Middle East crisis"/geopolitical disruption affecting food-testing import/export volumes (Vimta) and general seasonality (Metropolis notes weather-driven demand variability). None of the three peers describes an industry-wide demand boom of the kind that would corroborate an unstated Qualitek "demand surge" narrative; where growth outpaces the cited market CAGR, management attributes it explicitly to market-SHARE gains from unorganized-to-organized migration or brand execution, not sector-wide acceleration, and Vimta explicitly ties its OWN electronics-segment growth to capacity/local-testing-mandate share gains against a slow-growing (4.5%) underlying market (see Q4 above), not to demand acceleration. This is a mild caution against reading Qualitek's growth as riding an industry tailwind rather than execution/M&A.

**2B PRICING AND INPUT COSTS.** See Q6 above. Consistent theme across all three peers: modest, manageable input-cost inflation (reagents, chemicals, manpower, and a named geopolitical/consumables-lead-time channel at Vimta), partially offset by GST relief (Metropolis, Krsnaa) and productivity/automation initiatives; none of the three shows a large gross-margin expansion. Pricing power is limited and calibrated: Metropolis explicitly delayed a routine annual price increase in FY26 to pass GST savings to customers; Vimta states margins are being "maintained," not expanded, via pricing; Krsnaa has embedded 2-5% contractual rate escalations in PPP contracts but no discretionary pricing power.

**2C CAPEX CYCLE.** All three peers are EXPANDING capacity, not holding or cutting — this is an industry-wide capacity build, not a lone-expander situation. Vimta: ~INR100 Cr FY26 capex, ~INR80 Cr FY27 guided, new Genome Valley facility, biologics/CRADS build-out, and separately about INR40-45 Cr specifically on the electronics/electricals testing segment (Jul-2026 call). Metropolis: INR55-65 Cr annual capex, network expansion to 750+ towns, "100 mini hubs" plan, genomics platform build. Krsnaa: cumulative ~INR5,000 million capex across Rajasthan PPP + MRI expansion, one of "the largest diagnostic infrastructure expansions undertaken in the country." This is an INDUSTRY-WIDE CAPACITY RACE, not a lone Qualitek build-out — Qualitek's FY27 Rs 63 Cr capex plan and new-lab strategy sits within a sector-wide pattern of aggressive capacity investment across testing/diagnostics peers, which somewhat de-risks the "is Qualitek overbuilding relative to demand" question, though all three peers ALSO report utilisation lag and margin drag from their own capacity builds (see Q3), so the capacity race itself does not guarantee near-term returns.

**2D COMPETITIVE MENTIONS.** No peer transcript mentions Qualitek by name or by unmistakable description. Vimta names its competitors as SGS, Eurofins, Intertek, Bureau Veritas, Covance, Charles River — all large multinational TIC/CRO players, none Indian small-caps. Metropolis and Krsnaa's competitive references are healthcare-diagnostics specific and do not touch industrial/TIC testing. Corrected from cycle 1: this is not simply a segment silence. Vimta operates a genuine electronics/EMI-EMC/defence-adjacent testing division out of Hyderabad that overlaps Qualitek's largest revenue line (see Q4); Vimta names no competitor in that specific sub-segment and never references Qualitek, but the overlap itself is a finding worth carrying forward — it means at least one part of the peer set is a real, not merely adjacent, comparator for growth-rate and capacity-cycle purposes in that vertical, even without a direct competitive mention.

**2E RISKS PEERS DISCUSS THAT QUALITEK DOES NOT.**
- Manpower attrition, quantified and named as an industry-wide norm on the call: Vimta states "attrition is quite high across the industry... that's going to be the norm" (Feb-2026 call). Corrected from cycle 1: Qualitek's FY26 AR DOES name attrition as a category risk — the Threats table lists "Skilled Manpower: Dependence on specialised technical and scientific personnel creates risks relating to availability and attrition" (FY26 AR, page 71) and a separate "Human Capital Risk" line in the Risks and Concerns section (page 74, addressed only generically via "employee training, development, engagement and retention initiatives"). The finding narrows: Qualitek names the risk category but gives no quantification, no discussion of magnitude, and no on-record acknowledgment that attrition is currently elevated (the way Vimta volunteers on-call), so the gap is one of DEPTH and CANDOUR, not existence.
- Leadership/key-person departures disclosed with specific framing and reassurance: Krsnaa lost its CFO, CBO mid-period and addressed it directly on-call with continuity framing (Feb-2026, May-2026 calls) — Qualitek has had company-secretary cessations (per B00, three in about 17 months) but no equivalent open discussion of the departures' business impact, and it holds no earnings call to be asked.
- Geopolitical/tariff pass-through risk to input costs and logistics, named specifically and quantified in direction: Vimta names "war" impact on food-testing import/export and consumables lead times with a stated (if unquantified) revenue exposure split (50% domestic / 50% import-export in food testing). Corrected from cycle 1: Qualitek's FY25 AR chairman's letter DOES name a version of this risk generically ("even in the current uncertain global trade scenario"; "Global Trade uncertainty, inflation and other macro-economic challenges," FY25 AR page 20-21), but at a macro, unquantified level, with no tie to specific input costs, lead times, or segment exposure the way Vimta discloses. The finding narrows to: Qualitek names the general category, peers name the specific mechanism and magnitude; the gap is in specificity, not total absence.
- Explicit government-payment-system transition risk (SNA-SPARSH, RBI-link account rollouts) as the NAMED mechanical cause of receivable delays (Krsnaa) — Qualitek's own receivables disclosure (per B00 LBF2) does not name any comparable payment-system mechanism, leaving the debtor-day story less evidenced than Krsnaa's, and this claim now rests on a single-peer comparison (see Q1 above).
- Regulatory/pricing-benchmark risk from CGHS rate resets flowing through to competitor pricing (Metropolis, Krsnaa both discuss CGHS price-benchmark dynamics for existing and future tenders) — not applicable to Qualitek's segment but illustrates that peers routinely name specific regulatory pricing mechanisms as forward risks/opportunities, a level of specificity absent from Qualitek's disclosure.

## PART 5: CROSS-PEER HYPOTHESIS

Across all three peers, a common pattern emerges that none of them states explicitly: the sector's margin story in FY26-27 is being told almost entirely through OPERATING LEVERAGE AND INTEGRATION MECHANICS (network densification, lab-to-center ratios, barcoding/vendor consolidation, "productivity" programs), NOT through gross-margin or pricing improvement. Vimta explicitly caps its own margin ambition ("plus/minus 1% or 2%"); Metropolis attributes margin gains to "material productivity" and network ratios, not price; Krsnaa's margin recovery is entirely a function of new-project utilisation maturing, not price or gross-margin gain. Combined with the observation that ALL THREE peers are simultaneously in an active, large-scale capex/capacity-expansion phase (Part 2C above) that is itself DEPRESSING near-term margins even as it is billed as the future margin driver — this suggests the testing/diagnostics services sector in India, as a whole, is currently in a phase where reported margins UNDERSTATE steady-state economics across nearly every listed name, because everyone is simultaneously building capacity ahead of utilisation. This is testable: if the hypothesis holds, sector-wide EBITDA margins across Vimta, Metropolis, and Krsnaa should show a visible step-up in FY28-29 (once FY26-27 capex cohorts mature to 3-4 year utilisation, per the Core Diagnostics and Rajasthan timelines both peers themselves name), even without further capex acceleration. If margins instead plateau or compress further in FY28-29 despite this capex build maturing, the hypothesis is falsified and the sector's capacity race would be better read as a structural feature (perpetual reinvestment need, not a temporary J-curve) — which would matter directly for how Qualitek's own new-lab drag (Rs 3.3 Cr H1 FY26 loss across 9 labs) should be read: as a temporary phase (bullish) or a permanent feature of the growth model (bearish, since Qualitek would then never "graduate" out of a state of margin-depressing new capacity).
