### 2C. Growth embedded in capex — arithmetic shown

The standard formula (total capex under execution × historical fixed asset turnover = implied
incremental revenue) does not cleanly apply to this asset-light business, because the only
disclosed capital deployment (Rs 24 Cr into Fabtech Technologies LLC/FT Institutions) is
subsidiary equity capitalization, not fixed-asset capex. Shown for completeness, with the caveat
stated explicitly:

- Capex under execution: Rs 24 Cr (subsidiary capitalization approved, Q1 FY27 call p.3)
- Historical fixed asset turnover (FY26, per B01-gate0): 14.2x
- Rs 24 Cr × 14.2x = Rs 340.8 Cr implied incremental revenue
- As % of FY26 revenue from operations (Rs 410.77 Cr, AR p.22): **83%**

**This number should be treated as a low-confidence, methodologically-inappropriate artefact** —
FAT of 14.2x reflects a nearly-fixed-asset-free turnkey model (B01 data_notes flags this FAT as
itself a "post-IPO-rebase distortion"), and Rs 24 Cr is working capital, not plant/equipment. The
more meaningful embedded-growth proxy for this business model is **order book as a multiple of
current revenue**: order book Rs 900+ Cr (31-Mar-2026, AR p.10) ÷ FY26 revenue from operations Rs
410.77 Cr = **~219%**, i.e. roughly 2.2x current annual revenue sits in signed/LC-backed backlog
with an 18-24 month conversion window (Q3 FY26 call p.8-9; Q4 FY26 call p.10-11). 📄. Order book
progression across the three calls, however, is close to flat once new inflows and deliveries are
netted: Rs 926 Cr (31-Jan-26) → Rs 900+ Cr (31-Mar-26) → Rs 900+ Cr / ~Rs 920 Cr FX-adjusted
(30-Jun-26) (Q3/Q4/Q1 calls). 🔍 Analyst inference: net backlog growth is modest — new order
intake is roughly offsetting execution/delivery rather than compounding the book.

## SECTION 3: THE 20-CATEGORY SCAN

### FAMILY A — Product & Technology

**A1 — Rare manufacturing capability.** Management repeatedly claims to be "the only turnkey life
sciences platform with in-house process air and water manufacturing" in its markets (Q3 FY26 call
p.3), and cites "firsts": first Oncology facility in the GCC (Saudi), first biosimilar in Algeria,
first COVID vaccine project in Egypt, largest IV solution plants in the Middle East (Q3 FY26 call
p.22). 🎙️ — self-reported, unverified, and flagged by B05 as a peer question worth testing
("Fabtech claims no direct/indirect listed Indian peers... do any compete for the same MENA/Africa
turnkey pharma tenders?"). No global-manufacturer-count, no 12-36 month qualification-timeline
disclosure, no long-lead-equipment specifics. **Strength: Weak.**

**A2 — Patent and IP pipeline.** NO EVIDENCE FOUND. The AR's own Technology Absorption section
states explicitly: "There is no technology absorption by the Company... [R&D] Not Applicable" (AR
p.33). No patents, no filing trend, no licensing revenue, no lab partnerships disclosed anywhere.

**A3 — Process innovation.** Execution-timeline compression claim (24-27mo → ~18-24mo, Q4 FY26
call p.11) is 🎙️ only. Contribution margin improvement (37.6% → 46.7%, Q1 FY26 to Q1 FY27) is 📄
in the disclosed quarterly financials (Inv. Pres.), but management's own attribution — "better
project selection, procurement efficiency, execution quality, scope mix" (Inv. Pres. "Why the
margin matters") — is narrative, not a disclosed yield or unit-cost metric. **Strength: Weak.**

**A4 — Product platform / modular architecture.** NO EVIDENCE FOUND. No SKU count, launch
frequency, or shared-architecture data disclosed.

### FAMILY B — Supply Chain

**B1 — Backward integration and RM security.** Evidence found points the *other* way: related-party
procurement dependence is ~26% of current procurement (FTCL cleanroom panels ~11% + Advantek air
handling ~15%, Q4 FY26 call p.16-17; flagged MEDIUM severity in B05). These are minority-owned or
independent affiliated suppliers (FABL 49%, TSA ~16.33% and being exited, per AR p.4 ecosystem
chart), not captive/wholly-owned backward integration. No RM% decline vs. peers, no 5+ year supply
agreements disclosed. 📄 (the 26% figure) but the underlying signal is concentration risk, not a
moat. **Strength: Weak** (and this is really a risk flag more than a moat — see 6B).

**B2 — Qualification lock-in.** cGMP/WHO/EU-GMP/USFDA references exist but describe the *client

reinvestment cycle noted by management (Q1 FY27 call p.12) is a soft relationship signal, not a
qualification-lock-in mechanism. **Strength: Weak.** NO EVIDENCE of formal multi-year post-
qualification agreements.

**B3 — Supply chain network effect.** NO EVIDENCE FOUND.

## SECTION 4: REGULATORY & POLICY TAILWINDS (R1)

**4A. Regulatory approvals in pipeline.** SACE's Saudi incorporation itself functions as the
regulatory unlock: local-content/in-Kingdom presence is what qualifies Fabtech for a wider set of
tenders under Vision 2030 (Q1 FY27 call p.5). 📄 — entity formed, capital approved, first order
won. No other specific Fabtech regulatory filing (e.g., an FDA application in its own name) is
disclosed; the WHO/EU-GMP/USFDA references throughout the AR and presentations describe the
*client facilities* Fabtech designs to those standards, not Fabtech's own regulatory status.

**4B. Government policy tailwinds.** (i) Saudi Vision 2030 GCC-wide localisation mandate — real
macro policy, described by management as shifting "from preference to hard qualification criteria"
(Q1 FY27 call p.5); Fabtech's specific capture is the SACE entity (📄) plus narrative framing
(🎙️). (ii) Djibouti government-funded IV solutions/dialysis project, single-project, fully
documented (Q1 FY27 call p.22). (iii) India PLI schemes and import-substitution measures are
referenced only as generic industry background in the AR's MD&A ("Government initiatives such as
Production Linked Incentive (PLI) schemes... are expected to support future demand," AR p.42) — no
company-specific PLI capture, amount, duration, or enrolment status is disclosed. **NOT FOUND** for
company-specific PLI benefit. Competitors share the benefit in all cases: Saudi local-content rules
apply to any foreign EPC entrant willing to incorporate locally, and PLI-type schemes are
sector-wide, not Fabtech-exclusive.

**4C. Regulatory moat assessment: EMERGING, not yet active.** Time to kick in: near-term (SACE
already converted one order). Sustainability: **moderate, not high** — because the underlying
qualification requirement is non-exclusive, durability depends on Fabtech's execution speed and
relationship depth during the current window, not on a structural barrier competitors cannot
cross. This directly informs why E1/R1 were scored Moderate rather than Strong.
