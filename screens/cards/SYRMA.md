## Business Understanding Narrative

Syrma builds electronics for other companies. It is an EMS or ESDM firm: it makes
printed circuit board assemblies, box builds, and RFID modules to customer
designs, and increasingly its own designs (ODM). It sells to five verticals:
consumer, industrials, auto and EV, railways and IT, and healthcare (IP FY27 Q1
p.9). Revenue is volume times content per unit; margin comes from mix (more
engineering-heavy work), design content, and now backward integration into PCBs
(IP FY26 Q1 p.8). It runs 16 plants and 300+ customers with low concentration,
top 5 at 43.5% (IP FY26 Q1 p.12).

What is changing is a deliberate climb up the value chain. Management is walking
away from high-volume, low-margin consumer work to concentrate on industrial and
healthcare, accepting slower top line for better margin (Concall FY25 Q4 p.7).
EBITDA margin moved from 6.9% (FY24) to 8.6% (FY25) to 11.3% (FY26) (IP FY26 Q1
p.8; guidance). A 75:25 PCB joint venture with Shinyup starts commercial
production in April 2027, meant to be margin-accretive (IP FY26 Q1 p.8; guidance).

Why now: FY26 delivered 27% revenue growth and 87% PAT growth, the balance sheet
turned net cash, and the order book is Rs 6,770 cr (guidance).

What must be true: the mix shift and PCB integration must keep lifting margin, and
the swelling receivables must not choke cash.

What breaks it: a working-capital blowout, a demand slump in one big vertical, or
paying up so much at entry that even good growth does not clear the hurdle.

## 2. Business model and archetype
Build-to-spec electronics manufacturer moving to design-led. Services FY25:
EMS/PCBA 91.7%, RFID 8.3% (IP FY26 Q1 p.12). Backward integration into PCB and
components via a 75:25 JV with Shinyup (IP FY26 Q1 p.8). Archetype: outsourcing
partner / build-to-spec component maker. The economic engine is high-mix flexible
volume manufacturing, where margin rises with engineering content and captive PCB
supply. Management is explicit that high-margin work carries higher working
capital and lower asset turns (Concall FY25 Q4 p.10).

## 3. Competitive advantages
Forming. The edge is scale (one of India's largest non-consumer ESDM firms),
end-to-end capability from design to lifecycle support, a 40-year record, low
customer concentration, and now backward integration (IP FY26 Q1 p.8, p.12).
Switching costs exist once Syrma is designed into a customer's product, which is
the ODM prize. But EMS is competitive and pricing power is limited: margins are
mid-teens EBITDA at best. Moat: forming, on spec-in and PCB integration, not yet
durable.

## 5. Financial trajectory
Revenue FY25 Rs 38,361 Mn (CAGR FY22-25 44%), FY26 Rs 48,569 Mn, up 27%
(IP FY26 Q1 p.8; guidance). EBITDA margin 6.9% (FY24), 8.6% (FY25), 11.3% (FY26).
PAT FY26 Rs 3,458 Mn, up 87%, 7.1% margin. Q4FY26 revenue Rs 1,477 cr up 56%, PAT
margin 8.1% (guidance). Balance sheet: debt cut to Rs 3,531 Mn from Rs 6,111 Mn,
net cash, net debt/EBITDA (1.2) in H1FY26 (IP FY26 Q3 p.10). ROCE H1FY26 11.7%,
14.9% adjusted for surplus IPO cash and goodwill (IP FY26 Q3 p.10). The
inflection is margin-led, driven by mix.
One real flag sits inside a good picture: trade receivables jumped 59% (Mar24 to
Mar25) to Rs 14,775 Mn while revenue grew ~20%, pushing receivable days 77 to 115
(IP FY25 Q4 p.29). Payables also rose, so net working capital held near 69 days.
The mix shift to engineering-heavy work is consuming receivables, exactly as
management warns (Concall FY25 Q4 p.10). Watch this.

## 6. The transition and the quality ladder
FROM R2, a cost-advantaged converter (low-margin consumer EMS). TO R3, a
value-added spec'd supplier with partial pricing power (industrial, healthcare,
ODM, captive PCB). Management's claim, quoted once: the shift to high-margin
verticals is "a very cautious call ... to recalibrate its focus on the high-margin
segments" (Concall FY25 Q4 p.7). The rung climb is visible in the margin series.
PCB integration in FY28 is the next rung lever.

## 8. Proof check
The last quarters confirm the thesis. Margin rose to 11.3% for FY26 and 11.9% in
Q4, PAT grew 87%, and the balance sheet turned net cash (guidance; IP FY26 Q3
p.10). The proof gate has fired on the margin climb. The one unproven leg is PCB,
which contributes only from FY28. The one counter-signal is receivables (step 5).

## 11. Posture: Transition Decision Matrix
- Proof gate: FIRED on the margin climb (Part B3).
- Ugliness: an artifact of the climb (top-line slowed as consumer was dropped).
- Recognition gap: likely CLOSED at ~80x, if the CMP screen holds.
Proof fired plus artifact ugliness plus a closed gap maps to EARNINGS-ONLY, and
at ~80x it edges toward PRICED NARRATIVE. From here returns must come from EPS
growth alone, with no help from re-rating and little room for error.

## 12. Verdict card
**PASS for now; revisit only on a large drawdown.** The business is a genuine
value-chain climb with a net-cash balance sheet and a real PCB option. Two things
keep it off the buy list: size (this is a large cap, off the small/micro-cap
mandate) and price (~80x on the current screen leaves the recognition gap closed).
Load-bearing facts to verify first if revisited: (1) live CMP and the true
multiple, (2) receivable-days trend into FY27, (3) PCB ramp and its margin. What
would change the view: a drawdown that re-opens the gap, or PCB proving
accretive while receivables normalise. No price here.
