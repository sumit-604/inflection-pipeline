| Net read | Pass-through as a contractual/policy mechanism is genuinely industry-standard — both peers confirm it exists and both use language close to Modison's ("complete pass-through," "price variation clause"). But neither peer's experience supports the idea that pass-through makes margin immune to a commodity cycle. Both report real, quantified margin compression (SBCL: transit/inventory exposure event; Salzer: 200bps then a further 400-450bps over two quarters) precisely because pass-through has a lag of one to two quarters, and Salzer states this lag risk explicitly as industry-wide. That divergence is worth flagging, not waving through as a company-specific strength. |

## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

**2A Demand environment.** Both peers describe FY26 as a choppy, not a boom, year: SBCL spent three to four quarters explaining muted volumes (a large US customer's inventory overhang, tariff-driven order deferrals, a flat Indian switchgear/MCB market), turning constructive only from Q4 FY26/Q1 FY27 onward. Salzer's core industrial-switchgear and wires-and-cables businesses grew at healthy double digits throughout (infrastructure/capex-led), but its two "story" growth businesses (smart meters, EV chargers) badly underdelivered against guidance for the entire period covered (see 2E). Neither peer describes a broad, unqualified demand boom; both describe pockets of real strength (smart-meter relay manufacturing at SBCL, industrial switchgear capex at Salzer) sitting alongside pockets of real softness (US tariff-hit exports, flat Indian MCB demand, smart-meter execution delays). This is a more mixed picture than a simple "industry tailwind" narrative would suggest.

**2B Pricing and input costs.** This is the most consequential cross-read finding (see Claim 1). Silver and copper prices rose sharply through FY26 into Q1 FY27 at both peers (Salzer: silver "gone up by close to 3x," copper roughly 800 to 1,300-1,400/kg; SBCL: describes the same silver spike coinciding with its plant-move exposure). Both peers report real, quantified EBITDA margin compression from this spike specifically because pass-through has a structural lag (Salzer: one to two quarters; explicitly named an "industry-wide challenge... not a Salzer-specific issue"). Modison's FY26 AGM claims a *record* 16.2% EBITDA margin in the same window. That divergence does not match the industry pattern peers describe and should be treated as a priority verification item, not dismissed as company-specific execution strength.

**2C Capex cycle.** Neither peer is running an aggressive capacity race. SBCL's Pune facility is a modest ~Rs 20-25cr incremental capex riding on an already-built high-capex base (Solan), targeting Rs 250-350cr in eventual busbar/PCBA assembly revenue over three years — a forward-integration bet on assembly value-add, not raw capacity expansion. Salzer explicitly says no major capex is planned for FY27 beyond ~Rs 15-16cr of maintenance/balancing spend, deferring larger switchgear capacity expansion to FY28 and citing "dynamic," low-capex-intensity capacity additions in its core industrial-switchgear line. Its Saudi plant, the one greenfield project either peer is running, has been delayed roughly six months by the Middle East conflict. This is a **lone-expander pattern, not an industry-wide capacity race**: if Modison's LV/HV brownfield/greenfield capex (targeted for FY23 completion per B05, status still unconfirmed as of the AGM) represents a larger or more aggressive build than either peer's programme, that asymmetry is worth naming explicitly in synthesis rather than assuming Modison is simply keeping pace with sector investment.

**2D Competitive mentions.** No peer mentions Modison by name or by an unmistakable description. This is notable specifically for SBCL, which operates a silver-contacts subsidiary in the same product category as Modison's core business and does name a bimetal-segment competitor by name elsewhere (EMS Bimetal, a US/European company) when discussing competitive dynamics. SBCL's silence on Modison, despite naming a competitor in an adjacent line, suggests either non-overlapping customer bases/end-markets or that Modison does not register as a visible competitor to SBCL's analyst base — a data point for, not against, a smaller/more niche positioning than Modison's own market-share claims imply.

**2E Risks peers discuss that Modison's own sources do not (per B05).**
- Pass-through *lag* as a structural, quarter-plus margin risk during a commodity spike (both peers, explicit and quantified) — B05 records no discussion of this lag mechanic in Modison's own sources, only the pass-through *policy* itself.
- Bidirectional commodity risk: Salzer notes silver falling from ~Rs 400/g back to ~Rs 250/g by Aug-2026, meaning a pass-through-linked revenue/margin tailwind can reverse just as fast as it built — a symmetry risk not addressed in Modison's sources per B05.
- Inventory/transit exposure specifically *during* a precious-metal price spike (SBCL, tied to a plant relocation) — directly analogous to Modison's own FY26 inventory build, but Modison's sources (per B05) do not name this mechanism as an explanation.
- Tariff and geopolitical disruption to export plans (both peers, extensively, across all four quarters) — Modison's export-push narrative in B05 does not engage with US tariff policy or geopolitical risk to its 23-country export base.
- Guidance credibility risk in adjacent "story" growth lines: Salzer's smart-meter guidance collapsed from an implied ~Rs 700-1,000cr multi-year opportunity to under Rs 100cr delivered over roughly two years, drawing a sharp analyst rebuke on-call ("do you think you have misled investors") — a cautionary parallel worth weighing against Modison's own credibility grade (C, per B05) and its history of guidance/market-share figures that have moved sharply between sources without explanation.

## PART 5: CROSS-PEER HYPOTHESIS

**Hypothesis:** In the Indian electrical-contacts/switchgear-components sector, "commodity pass-through" is a genuine, widely-used contractual practice, but it operates with a systematic one-to-two-quarter lag between an input-price spike and the point at which price increases are fully realised in the P&L. This means any company reporting a *record* margin during the same commodity up-cycle that its direct peers report as margin-negative is showing either (a) a genuinely superior contractual/costing structure worth independently verifying, or (b) a timing artifact — selling finished/in-transit inventory costed at pre-spike input prices while invoicing at post-spike (partially pass-through-adjusted) prices, a one-off inventory-costing tailwind that reverses when the commodity price itself reverses (as Salzer notes silver already has, falling from ~Rs 400/g to ~Rs 250/g by Aug-2026).

This is testable and specific: it predicts that if Modison's FY26 margin strength is largely a timing/costing artifact rather than a structural pass-through advantage, Modison's EBITDA margin should compress in the first one-to-two quarters *following* any sustained decline in silver prices, mirroring the bidirectional exposure Salzer explicitly names for itself. Neither peer states this hypothesis about Modison or about the sector generally — SBCL frames its own pass-through as essentially costless to margin, and Salzer frames its own lag as a temporary headwind it is actively correcting — but combining SBCL's "no margin impact" claim with Salzer's explicit "the lag cuts both ways" mechanic, and holding both against Modison's simultaneous record-margin claim in the same commodity window, produces a testable prediction about Modison specifically that no single peer's disclosure states on its own. Downstream stages (FTTCP, Role 1) should treat "stable margin through a full commodity down-cycle, not just the up-cycle" as the real confirm signal for Modison's Trigger 1, not stability during the up-cycle alone.
