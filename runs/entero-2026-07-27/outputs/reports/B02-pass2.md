## New Findings

### 1. 🔴 Standalone auditor's opinion carries an actual exception tied to the audit-trail gap, not just a self-disclosed note
The Independent Auditor's Report on the Standalone Financial Statements states books of account were properly kept "**except for the matter stated in paragraph 2(h)(vi) below on reporting under Rule 11(g)**" (Auditor's Report on Standalone FS, para 2(b), p.90), and paragraph 2(h)(vi) then describes the audit-trail (edit log) deficiencies across the Company's four accounting softwares (p.91). Pass 1 treated the audit-trail issue as a company-disclosed control gap (Note 57(xii)); this pass confirms the statutory auditor formally carried it through as an exception on the "proper books of account" opinion itself — a harder form of auditor pushback than a disclosure note. Same language repeats for the Group at consolidated level implicitly via the CARO/Annexure B mechanics already covered in Pass 1.

### 2. 🟡 Standalone Key Audit Matter #1 is "Impairment of investments and loans (including interest) given to subsidiaries" — a forward-looking risk designation, not a one-off
The standalone Independent Auditor's Report identifies this as a formal Key Audit Matter (p.88-89), citing "significant judgement" in assumptions such as discount rates, revenue growth, future business plans and recoverability, and confirms the auditors involved internal valuation specialists and performed sensitivity analysis. This reframes the Curever/CPD impairments (already flagged in Pass 1) as symptomatic of a structural, recurring judgment area the auditors themselves flag as high-risk for the Holdco's ~₹9,551.54m of subsidiary investments and loans (net of ECL) going forward — not simply a one-time exceptional item.

### 3. 🔴 Standalone retained earnings remains structurally NEGATIVE despite two consecutive profitable years
Standalone Statement of Changes in Equity (p.100, Note 23 p.121-122): Retained Earnings opening balance 1 April 2023 ₹(839.94)m → closing 31 March 2024 ₹(454.49)m → closing 31 March 2025 ₹(255.05)m. This is despite net profit of ₹384.40m (FY24) and ₹191.81m (FY25). Large pre-IPO accumulated losses have not yet been fully absorbed at the listed-entity level. Total "Other Equity" is positive (₹16,068.72m) only because of the enormous Securities Premium Reserve (₹16,281.42m) built from the IPO/CCPS conversion — not from retained operating profits. Relevant to distributable-reserves capacity and dividend policy (Board cited "conserving resources" for the no-dividend decision; negative retained earnings is a more concrete constraint than resource conservation language suggests).

### 4. 🟡 Consolidated retained earnings, in contrast, swung from negative to solidly positive — showing where "profit" actually originates in the group
Consolidated Statement of Changes in Equity (p.166, p.169): Retained Earnings opening 1 April 2023 ₹(726.99)m → closing 31 March 2024 ₹(338.07)m → closing 31 March 2025 **+₹638.28m**. The turnaround is driven almost entirely by subsidiary-level profits consolidating in (Group PAT ₹1,074.34m FY25), not by the listed Holdco's own economics, whose retained earnings stayed negative per Finding 3. Useful lens for separating "where the reported profit lives" (subsidiaries) from "where the listed shareholder's claim sits" (still working off historical losses at Holdco level).

### 5. 🔴 Standalone operating cash flow negative for two consecutive years; Holdco funds itself from financing/investing cash, not operations
Standalone Cash Flow Statement (p.101): Net cash **used** in operating activities ₹(1,090.16)m FY25 vs ₹(756.50)m FY24. Standalone cash & other bank balances fell from ~₹7,923.52m (FY24) to ~₹2,233.86m (FY25) (cross-referenced in Note 47(A)(iii), p.132) as the Holdco deployed ₹3,034.86m into fresh intercompany loans and ₹1,916.07m into subsidiary equity investments during the year (Standalone Cash Flow Statement, investing activities, p.101). The Holdco's own operations do not generate cash; the entire funding model runs on IPO-proceeds cash being drawn down for subsidiary funding.

### 6. 🔴 Consolidated (Group) operating cash flow was ALSO negative for the full year and worsened YoY — a direct tension with the MD&A/CEO narrative
Consolidated Cash Flow Statement (p.167): Net cash **used** in operating activities ₹(768.66)m FY25 vs ₹(366.12)m FY24 — i.e., the full-year figure got *more negative*, not better, year-on-year. This sits in direct tension with the MD&A/CEO's Message claim that "in the second half of the year, we turned operating cash flow positive" (p.15, MD&A p.24 risk section language echoes the same framing). Whatever happened in H2 alone, the FULL YEAR consolidated operating cash flow deteriorated versus FY24. This is a genuine narrative-vs-financials discrepancy that should be surfaced prominently (feeds FLAG-CASH; also a strong pattern-pass candidate for Pass 3).

### 7. 🟡 Holdco's intercompany loan book shifted from ~100% current to majority non-current within one year
Standalone Balance Sheet / Notes 9 and 18 (p.114-118): FY24 all ₹5,891.69m of loans to subsidiaries were classified current (non-current portion nil). FY25 the split is ₹6,715.70m non-current vs only ₹1,845.47m current (of ₹8,561.17m net of the Curever impairment). This is an implicit extension of the expected repayment horizon on roughly ₹892.59cr of subsidiary funding — a maturity-profile signal not discussed in Pass 1 that is relevant to how quickly the Holdco could realistically recall this funding if needed.

### 8. 🟡 Secretarial Audit Report (standalone) lists three specific compliance lapses not previously flagged
Annexure-II A, Secretarial Audit Report (p.36-37): (a) a Risk Management Committee meeting was "conducted with delay of a few days"; (b) related-party-transaction disclosures were filed with the Stock Exchanges on 31 May 2024, two days after the due date, "because of technical glitches"; (c) the Company intimated the exchanges about Nominee Director Mr. Vipul Indravadan Desai's resignation within the prescribed timeframe, but "the resignation letter... was submitted later" to the exchanges. Individually minor, but a set of governance-process slippages worth naming.

### 9. 🟡 CSR: a disclosure gap between the front-matter narrative and the audited Notes
Standalone Note 55 (p.149) confirms s.135 CSR provisions apply to the Company but it is not required to spend in FY25 due to insufficient average profits under s.198. Separately, the Corporate Overview section ("Group CSR Initiative," p.20) discloses ₹4.92 million spent on CSR activities in FY25, attributed by name to specific subsidiaries (Sai Pharma Distributors, Chirag Medicare Solutions, Chethana Pharma, R S M Pharma) via named NGO partners. However, **no consolidated financial-statement Note anywhere in Notes 1-59 (consolidated) quantifies which Group subsidiaries were individually required to spend under s.135, the amount required vs. actually spent, or any unspent-amount/shortfall disclosure** — a search of the full document confirms "CORPORATE SOCIAL RESPONSIBILITY" appears only twice (the front-matter heading and the standalone Note 55), with no consolidated equivalent note. This is a disclosure gap relative to normal Schedule III practice for group entities with CSR-obligated subsidiaries.

### 10. 🟡 Unexplained related-party item: commission expense paid BY the Holdco TO a subsidiary
Standalone Note 48B (p.137, p.145): "Commission Expense — Novacare Healthcare Solutions Private Limited" ₹8.95m (FY24) / ₹8.26m (FY25). The direction is unusual — typically the parent charges subsidiaries a business-support fee (as it does, per the "Business Support Charges" line items already in Pass 1's aggregate); here the Company appears to be the payer to a subsidiary. No commercial rationale for this specific commission relationship is disclosed in the note. Worth a direct management question (added to Section D candidates for Pass 3 consolidation).

### 11. 🟢 IPO idle-funds precision: a sharper number than Pass 1's utilisation table alone
Standalone Auditor's CARO Annexure B, clause x(a) (p.94): "the maximum amount of idle/surplus funds invested during the year was Rs. 6,406.10 million, of which Rs. 1,584.50 million was outstanding at the end of the year." This confirms (and sharpens) the IPO-proceeds utilisation picture already in Pass 1's Note 52 coverage — the bulk of the IPO warchest sat idle in fixed deposits for much of FY25 before being substantially drawn down (cash and bank balances fell from ~₹7.9bn to ~₹2.2bn over the year, per Finding 5 above).

### 12. Housekeeping / anchor correction (no rating — accuracy note only)
Pass 1 cited "Note 45 Fair value measurements" for the standalone financial statements. On this re-read, the correct standalone note numbering is: Note 45 = Capital Management (p.129), Note 46 = Fair Value Measurements (p.129-131), Note 47 = Financial Risk Management (p.132-133). The substance of Pass 1's finding (Level 3 valuation via external experts for call/put options) is unaffected; only the note number needs correcting in the consolidated analysis.

### 13. 🟢 Minor governance nuance: PE investor reclassified as "Promoter"
Standalone Note 21(f) (p.120): Orbimed Asia III Mauritius Limited (38.01% holder) is footnoted as "During the year ended March 2024, classified as promoter in the Annual Return of FY'24." A financial/PE investor holding the largest single stake is formally a "Promoter" for regulatory purposes rather than a public/institutional shareholder — relevant colour for related-party and control-classification purposes, not previously noted.

## PASS 2 NEW FINDINGS SUMMARY

Thirteen new items surfaced on the second pass, none of which duplicate Pass 1. The two most investor-relevant are: (i) the standalone auditor's opinion carries an actual qualification (not just a disclosed note) tied to the audit-trail gap, and the standalone Key Audit Matter on subsidiary-impairment risk was not previously surfaced as a formal, forward-looking auditor designation; and (ii) both standalone AND consolidated full-year operating cash flow were negative and consolidated cash flow from operations *worsened* year-on-year, directly in tension with the MD&A/CEO narrative of having "turned operating cash flow positive" in H2 FY25 — a material narrative-vs-financials discrepancy that should carry through to the red-flags and FLAG-CASH sections at consolidation. Supporting findings cover the divergent retained-earnings trajectories at standalone (still negative) vs. consolidated (turned positive) level, a maturity-extension signal in the intercompany loan book, three minor Secretarial Audit compliance lapses, a CSR disclosure gap between the front-matter and the audited Notes, and one unexplained related-party commission flow running from the Holdco to a subsidiary rather than the more typical direction.
