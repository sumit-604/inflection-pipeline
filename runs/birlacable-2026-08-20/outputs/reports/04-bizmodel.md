## SECTION 1: THE BUSINESS MODEL IN PLAIN ENGLISH

### 1A. One-line description
Birla Cable makes and sells telecommunication cables — optical fibre cables and copper/structured LAN cables — from a single plant in Rewa, Madhya Pradesh, converting purchased raw materials (glass preform, copper, polymer compounds) into finished cable sold mainly to telecom operators and enterprise network buyers in India and abroad (AR p.40, Industry Structure and Developments).

### 1B. Money flow chain, by revenue stream

**Stream 1: Optical Fibre Cables (OFC)**
[Glass preform, jelly, plastics purchased as raw material] → [drawn/extruded into optical fibre cable at Rewa plant, tested and certified to ISO/TEC standards] → [delivered to telecom operators and OFC distributors, in India and overseas, largely export-weighted] → [telecom operators/OFC buyers pay] → [payment on credit terms, receivables generally settled within roughly 90-120 days per two AR notes that differ (AR p.101 Note 39(c) says 120 days; AR p.128 says 90 days; NOT FOUND which is current — flagged as an internal inconsistency)].

**Stream 2: Structured Copper LAN Cables / Copper Telecommunication Cables**
[Copper rod, insulation compounds purchased as raw material] → [drawn, insulated, jacketed into LAN and copper telecom cable at Rewa plant] → [delivered domestically, in bulk, to enterprise network buyers and telecom operators — one customer alone was ₹34,693.95 lakh, 45.0% of FY26 revenue] → [that customer and other bulk buyers pay] → [on credit, per company-wide receivable terms above].

### 1E. The chai-stall-uncle version
Think of Birla Cable like a workshop that turns raw copper wire and glass fibre into the cables that carry your phone calls and internet. It buys the raw copper and glass, twists and coats it into finished cable at one factory in Rewa, and sells it to phone companies and big offices. Most of the money today comes from copper LAN cable, sold heavily to one big customer, not the glassy optical fibre cable that used to matter more. Raw material is 82 paise of every rupee of sales, so the "workshop margin" it keeps is thin, and rivals often undercut on price. And there's a twist: this workshop is being folded into a sister company, Vindhya Telelinks, so the shop itself may not exist as a separate listed business for much longer.

### 2D. Industry lifecycle stage
Two different clocks are running inside one company. The domestic LAN/copper cable line, which is 86% of revenue, is riding a currently strong up-cycle tied to one customer's telecom rollout (AR p.41). The OFC line, 14% of revenue and export-weighted, is described as coming off a "deep global dearth... historically low levels" with recovery only "since late March 2026" (AR p.40-41) — i.e., OFC is emerging from a cyclical trough while LAN cable is at or near a cyclical peak. India's wider wire-and-cable industry is described (AR p.40) as projected to grow at an 11-13% CAGR through FY2029, an industry-level growth-stage claim, unattributed to a named source in the extracted text.

### 3D. Unit economics — the physics of the business
- Unit: A kilometre of cable (fibre-km for OFC, or cable-km for copper/LAN) is the natural physical unit for this business, consistent with how the AR itself reports industry demand in F-km (AR p.21-22). The company does NOT disclose its own volumes in km or units sold anywhere in this AR — only revenue in ₹ lakh by product line.
- Revenue per unit: NOT FOUND (no company-level volume disclosed to divide into revenue).
- Cost per unit: NOT FOUND, same reason.
- Volume drivers: Telecom operator capex cycles (5G/fiberisation, BSNL rollout), enterprise network buildouts, and specifically one large customer's order flow for the LAN/copper line (AR p.39-41).
- Price drivers: Global OFC pricing cycle (described as at "record levels" entering FY27, AR p.21) for the export-weighted OFC line; competitive underbidding described as compressing prices in the domestic cable market generally (AR p.42-43).
- Cost drivers: Copper price, glass preform/helium/polymer feedstock price and availability (AR p.21-22); raw materials are 82.3% of revenue, so this is the dominant cost driver by a wide margin over labour (employee cost is 4.8% of revenue: ₹3,701.20L / ₹77,111.40L, AR p.75).
- Incremental margin and operating leverage: EBITDA (Earnings before Finance Cost, D&A, Tax) rose from ₹3,594.18L to ₹5,071.30L (+41.1%) on revenue growth of only 16.5% (AR p.20), implying meaningfully positive operating leverage in FY26 off a low fixed-cost base (single plant, PPE turnover ~7x) — but this cuts both ways: a reversal in the LAN cable segment (currently 86% of revenue and customer-concentrated) would hit EBITDA disproportionately hard on the way down too.

### 4B. Valuation method applicability — HANDOFF TO STAGE 11

| Method | Applicable? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| EV/EBITDA (normalized/through-cycle) | YES — PRIMARY | EBITDA swung from ₹3,594.18L to ₹5,071.30L (+41%) in one year on a single-customer-driven segment surge (AR p.20); a through-cycle or normalized EBITDA multiple is more defensible than a spot-year multiple for a business with one segment at a trough (OFC) and another near a peak (LAN cable) |

| Dividend yield/DDM | NOT APPLICABLE | First dividend in this AR after a prior nil year (₹1.25/share, 12.5% of face value, AR p.4) — one data point, not a track record to anchor a DDM |
| Sum-of-parts | NOT APPLICABLE | Single reportable segment ("Cables") under Ind AS 108 (AR p.101); no separable business units to sum |

**Cycle stage that matters for valuation**: OFC is trough-to-early-recovery (AR p.40-41, "revival since late March 2026"); LAN/copper is at or near a customer-driven peak (86% of revenue, up 28.7% YoY on one buyer's ramp). Both cycle positions must be normalized before applying any multiple — using the spot FY26 print risks anchoring on a peak year for the dominant segment.

**Structural override**: none of the above matters if the amalgamation into Vindhya Telelinks completes — at that point BIRLACABLE ceases to exist and the investable question becomes the swap ratio (10 VTL shares per 115 BIRLACABLE shares) versus Vindhya Telelinks' own standalone valuation, not a standalone DCF/multiple on Birla Cable. This is a live structural flag for Stage 11 and Stage 13, not a resolved input.

### 4D. Highest-value questions for management

1. What is the actual capacity utilisation at the Rewa plant today, and is the FY26 LAN cable surge running at full capacity? — Reassuring answer: utilisation has headroom and is scalable without major capex; Worrying answer: already near full utilisation, so further LAN cable growth requires capex the company hasn't guided to.
2. Who is the customer that is 45% of revenue, and what is the visibility on that order book beyond FY27? — Reassuring: multi-year contracted visibility with reasonable diversification plans; Worrying: spot/annual order with no forward commitment.
3. What is the expected timeline for NCLT and exchange approval of the Vindhya Telelinks amalgamation, and is the 10:115 swap ratio final? — Reassuring: clear near-term timeline, ratio locked and fair-value-tested; Worrying: open-ended timeline or contested ratio.
4. How much of the FY26 raw material cost increase was passed through in pricing, versus absorbed in margin? — Reassuring: substantially passed through via formula-linked pricing; Worrying: absorbed, explaining why margins stay thin despite volume growth.
5. Given DSCR of 0.39x, how is the company managing near-term debt service, and is refinancing or equity support planned? — Reassuring: covenant waivers/refinancing already arranged, cash flow trajectory improving; Worrying: no clear plan, reliance on continued short-term borrowing.
6. Is the OFC segment demand recovery (referenced as starting late March 2026) translating into actual order intake for Birla Cable specifically, given it is export-weighted? — Reassuring: firm order confirmations; Worrying: still commentary-level, no bookings yet.
7. What happens to the standalone listed entity's shareholders and the company's employees/plant post-amalgamation? — Reassuring: clear integration plan preserving the Rewa facility's role; Worrying: no operational integration plan disclosed yet.
