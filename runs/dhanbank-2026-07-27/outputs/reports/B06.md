## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

**2A. Demand environment.** Universal FY26 pattern across all four peers: system deposit growth lagged credit growth into H1 FY26 (CD ratios rising system-wide), RBI cumulative ~100bps of repo cuts (Aug-25 through the FY26 window) compressed NIM broadly, then a reversal set in from Q3/Q4 FY26 as deposits repriced down. From ~January-February 2026 (CSB, KTKBANK, SIB, TMB Q3 calls) a new demand-environment shock appears simultaneously in all four transcripts: the West Asia/Gulf crisis, cited as a driver of NRI-deposit uncertainty, gold-price volatility, and renewed caution on SME/export-linked lending. GST rate cuts (South Indian Bank, Oct-25 call) are separately flagged as an auto/consumption demand tailwind. This broad, dated, cross-peer macro backdrop (rate-cut NIM compression → West Asia shock → gold volatility) is directly relevant context against which DHANBANK's FY26 numbers should be read, since DHANBANK operates the same South-India small-bank funding/asset mix.

**2B. Pricing and input costs.** All four peers describe the identical mechanism: EBLR/MCLR-linked assets reprice down immediately on repo cuts while term deposits reprice with a 2-4 quarter lag, compressing NIM through H1 FY26 before recovering in H2 as the deposit book catches up (explicit in KTKBANK, SIB, CSB, TMB transcripts). Gold-loan yields are the one pricing lever moving the *other* way — all three gold-heavy peers (CSB, SIB, TMB) describe deliberately shifting toward larger-ticket, "working-capital" gold loans at higher yields as a way to protect NIM even as consumption-gold yields hold flat or fall slightly.

**2C. Capex/investment cycle.** Not a physical-capex story (these are banks) but a clear parallel: every peer is mid-way through a heavy technology/branch reinvestment cycle simultaneously — CSB's full core-banking (Flexcube) migration and 50+ surround-system rollout (all four CSBBANK transcripts); TMB's Rs.250cr FY26 IT budget (up from Rs.151cr FY25), loan-management-system rollout, and branch expansion (all four TMB transcripts); Karnataka Bank's regional loan-processing-center buildout; South Indian Bank explicitly "restarting" branch/hiring after a 3-year freeze (SOUTHBANK, Jul-26, p.4-5). This is **not a lone expander vs a cautious peer set** — it is an industry-wide, simultaneous reinvestment/tech-catch-up cycle across the entire comparable set, with every peer explicitly guiding that the cost-to-income payoff from this investment will not show up until FY27/FY28. This is the correct frame for judging any DHANBANK claim of near-term cost-income improvement: peers uniformly treat FY26 cost reduction as preparatory, not terminal.

**2D. Competitive mentions of the main company.** No peer transcript names or unmistakably references Dhanlaxmi Bank in any of the 16 calls read. Silence noted and treated as uninformative given DHANBANK's small scale relative to this peer set — not itself evidence for or against any DHANBANK claim.

**2E. Risks peers discuss that DHANBANK's claim set does not raise.**
- **ECL/expected-credit-loss transition (effective 1 April 2027).** All four peers actively quantify and are provisioning for this now — CSB (contingency provision write-back plus SMA-based provisioning-floor analysis, CSBBANK Q4 FY26, p.10-11), Karnataka Bank (~1-1.5% capital impact spread over 4-5 years, KTKBANK Q4 FY26, p.9), South Indian Bank (460bps incremental Stage-II provisioning on <100bps of exposure, deemed immaterial, SOUTHBANK Oct-25, p.7-8), TMB (Rs.210cr rising to Rs.264cr estimated impact, cushioned by an existing Rs.250cr COVID-era contingency buffer, TMB Nov-25 p.5 and Feb-26 p.5). DHANBANK's ECL-transition readiness is not addressed anywhere in the injected claim set — a candidate missing risk.
- **West Asia/Gulf geopolitical exposure to NRI deposits and gold prices**, discussed at length by CSB (NRI vertical build-out, representative office delay, CSBBANK Q4 FY26 p.8-9), Karnataka Bank (limited Gulf deposit exposure, KTKBANK Q4 FY26 p.17), South Indian Bank (FCNR/NRI flow monitoring, credit-cost deterioration expectation, SOUTHBANK May-26 p.9-10 and Jul-26 p.5-6), TMB (limited West Asia export exposure, 50.95bps of book, TMB Apr-26 p.5) — not referenced in the DHANBANK claim set.
- **RBI's April-2025 gold-loan/LAS reclassification circular**, actively reshaping reported gold-loan growth at CSB, TMB and Karnataka Bank (all three describe repledger/loan-against-security books running off into "gold loan" reporting lines) — this mechanically inflates gold-loan growth optics sector-wide and is directly relevant to interpreting any DHANBANK gold-loan number, but is not flagged in the DHANBANK claim set.
- **Legacy technically-written-off (TWO) book recovery as a recurring "other income" source** — material and repeatedly discussed at KTKBANK, TMB and South Indian Bank (TWO recoveries contributing tens of crores per quarter to reported profit) — not mentioned in DHANBANK's claims, though DHANBANK's own 7.45% incl-write-off GNPA figure implies a comparably large legacy TWO book whose recovery economics are unaddressed.
- **Leadership/succession risk** — both Karnataka Bank (new MD taking over amid "significant transitions," senior departures) and South Indian Bank (MD Seshadri's term ending, active succession search) treat management continuity as a live investor concern across multiple quarters. Not directly transferable to DHANBANK, but worth noting as a live theme in this exact peer set that DHANBANK's NO-CONCALL status leaves entirely unaddressed either way.

## PART 4: TRIANGULATION SUMMARY

- **Claims verified:** 1 of 6 fully verified (Q2, NIM trajectory), 3 of 6 partially verified (Q4 CASA direction, Q5 GNPA gap width, Q6 MSME mix-shift), 1 of 6 contradicted on comparability while partially verified on direction (Q3 cost-income), 1 of 6 unverifiable for DHANBANK-specific figures with a peer benchmark band established instead (Q1 gold LTV/yield).
- **Claims contradicted:** Q3 (cost-income) is the priority item for synthesis — DHANBANK's claimed 85.42%→73.45% cost-income band sits structurally outside the entire peer set's experience (44%-65% range), and even the least-efficient peer (CSB) explicitly says its own *sharp* efficiency gains are not due until FY28. This does not disprove the DHANBANK number, but it removes any peer-based support for calling a 12pp one-year improvement "structurally durable."
- **Most consequential contradiction:** The absolute cost-income level itself (Q3) — no South-India peer bank in this set has ever reported an 85% or even a 73% cost-income ratio in the four quarters covered, which either marks DHANBANK as a genuine outlier turnaround story or signals a data/definitional issue that synthesis should probe before crediting the improvement as comparable to peer efficiency gains.
- **Strongest independent confirmation:** Karnataka Bank's FY26 NIM print (2.88% full-year, 3.07% Q4) is numerically identical to DHANBANK's claimed trajectory (Q2), delivered via the same funding-cost mechanism DHANBANK would need (EBLR-lag then CASA-led recovery). This is unusually strong, mechanism-matched corroboration from an independent, directly comparable peer.
- **Overall:** The peer set complicates more than it simply supports or undercuts DHANBANK's narrative. It strongly corroborates the NIM trajectory and the CASA-improvement direction (both are genuine, dated, sector-wide phenomena in FY26), offers approximate support for the wide headline-vs-inclusive GNPA gap, but supplies no comparable precedent for the scale of DHANBANK's claimed cost-income turnaround, and shows that "MSME mix-shift" claims in this exact peer group are unreliable roughly a third of the time even when the strategic intent is genuine and repeatedly stated. Treat the NIM and CASA claims as reasonably well-grounded; treat the cost-income magnitude and any MSME mix-shift narrative with active skepticism pending line-item DHANBANK evidence.

## PART 5: CROSS-PEER HYPOTHESIS

**Hypothesis:** Reported FY26 gold-loan book growth across South-India small/mid-cap banks (CSB, TMB, and to a lesser extent Karnataka Bank) is materially inflated by a one-off, sector-wide regulatory reclassification rather than organic demand — specifically, RBI's April-2025 circular on loan-against-security/repledger products is causing all three banks to migrate existing LAS/repledger exposure into their "gold loan" reporting line simultaneously and independently, mechanically pushing gold-loan mix percentages up (CSB: 44%→53% of book in one year; TMB experienced a similar retail-to-agri gold reclassification in Q3→Q4 FY26) without a matching increase in underlying customer gold tonnage (CSB explicitly reports tonnage growth of only ~4% YoY against reported book growth of 30%+). No single peer frames this as a *sector-wide optical effect* — each describes it only as an internal portfolio-management choice — but reading all three transcripts together reveals a common, dated (April 2025), regulation-driven mechanical inflation of gold-loan growth figures across the peer set in FY26.

This is specific and testable: it predicts that any bank in this comparable set (including DHANBANK, if gold-loan-heavy) reporting sharp FY26 gold-loan mix growth should show a materially smaller increase once LAS/repledger migration is stripped out, and that true organic gold-loan demand growth for the sector in FY26 is closer to CSB's disclosed ~4% tonnage growth than to the 30-50%+ headline book growth rates being reported. Any DHANBANK gold-loan growth claim should be tested against this discount before being taken as evidence of demand strength.
