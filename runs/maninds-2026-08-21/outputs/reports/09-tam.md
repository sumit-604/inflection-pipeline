## SECTION 1: MARKET DEFINITION

### 1A Precise boundaries
- **Product scope (in):** Large-diameter (typically ≥16") welded steel line
  pipe — LSAW, HSAW, ERW — plus external/internal coating (3LPE, FBE) for
  oil & gas transmission, water transmission/desalination, and linked
  infrastructure. Includes the nascent stainless-steel seamless mother-pipe
  line (Jammu, 22,000 MTPA, targeting chemical/defence/marine/nuclear/
  power/refinery applications).
- **Product scope (out):** Small-diameter/structural pipe, piling pipe,
  ductile-iron (DI) pipe, plastic/HDPE pipe, seamless carbon-steel pipe at
  scale (MANINDS has none beyond the nascent SS line), and non-pipe
  activity (Merino Shelters real-estate monetisation — 10.5% of FY26
  consolidated revenue — is explicitly excluded; it is a one-off asset
  sale, not a market MANINDS competes for).
- **Geographic scope:** Global for the top-down TAM number (Method 1);
  narrowed to MANINDS's actual served footprint — India, Saudi Arabia/GCC,
  wider MENA, and Africa — for SAM (Section 3). MANINDS has near-zero
  presence in China, North America, Europe, Russia and most of Southeast
  Asia, the largest blocks of the global market.
- **Customer scope:** National oil companies, EPC contractors on
  oil/gas/water transmission programmes, and water/desalination
  authorities (Aramco, ADNOC, QatarEnergy, KOC, Bapco, Saudi Water
  Authority per AR, PIB/Jal Shakti-funded EPC contractors in India).
- **Channel scope:** Direct tender/bid response to approved-vendor lists
  (Aramco-approved since 2005 per AR) — not a distributor or retail
  channel business.
- **Price segment:** Mid-to-premium (API-grade, coated, certified) —
  MANINDS does not compete in commodity/uncertified small-bore pipe.

## SECTION 2: TAM ESTIMATION, MULTIPLE METHODS

### Method 1 — Top-down (global large-diameter steel pipe market)
- Global large-diameter steel pipes market: **US$13.34B (2025)**
  (cognitivemarketresearch.com industry report) and **US$13.6-13.7B
  projected for 2026** at a 2.7% CAGR off a US$12.3B 2022 base (Global
  Industry Analysts, via PRNewswire, three successive syndicated releases
  301302243/301505638/301617767 — 2022 base year is >2 years old, flagged
  STALE for the base, but the 2026 projection itself is contemporaneous
  with the run date). LSAW alone: ~US$7.6B by 2026 at 2.4% CAGR.
- Subtract non-relevant segments: piling/structural large-diameter pipe
  (not an application MANINDS competes in) is commonly cited in industry
  breakdowns as ~15% of "large diameter steel pipes" volume (oil & gas
  transmission ~45-55%, water/utility ~20-25%, structural/piling
  ~20-30% — no single source gives an exact split for this exact
  product-market cut, so the 15% haircut applied here is a conservative,
  analyst-judged deduction, flagged as an estimate).
- Calculation:
  - Conservative: $13.34B × (1 − 0.15) = $11.339B × ₹95.75 = **₹10,85,700 Cr**
  - Realistic: $13.6B × (1 − 0.15) = $11.56B × ₹95.75 = **₹11,06,900 Cr**
- Confidence: Medium (product definition match is reasonable; the 2022 base
  year and the 15% haircut are both judgment calls, not single-sourced).

### Method 2 — Bottom-up (volume × ASP cross-check)
- Addressable unit: 1 tonne of large-diameter coated line pipe.
- Global welded large-diameter/API-grade pipe realisation: **$680-750/ton**
  bare, higher with coating (web search, multiple LSAW/HSAW/API 5L pipe
  price trackers, H2 2026 pricing). Blended bare+coated midpoint used:
  $700-750/ton.
- Implied global volume at Method 1's post-haircut TAM: $11.34-11.56B ÷
  $700-750/ton ≈ **15.1-16.5 million tonnes/year**.
- Cross-check: known large-diameter capacity among major global and
  regional players (Tenaris, Vallourec, ArcelorMittal, Nippon Steel, JFE,
  EEW, Welspun, Jindal SAW, MANINDS+NPC 1.6mn MTPA, Arabian Pipes 460,000
  MTPA, Saudi Steel Pipe, Global Pipe Company) is directionally consistent
  with a global market in the low-tens-of-millions-of-tonnes range for
  this specific product niche.
- **Limitation, stated plainly:** this method derives volume FROM Method
  1's dollar TAM, so it is a consistency check, not a fully independent
  triangulation. Confidence: Medium, and explicitly not counted as
  independent corroboration in the triangulation table below.

### Method 4 — Import substitution
Not materially applicable in the conventional sense. India is a net
**exporter** of finished large-diameter line pipe (MANINDS itself supplies
30+ countries per its own deck); the AR's "Highest Quantity of Steel
Imports, Deendayal Port Authority, Kandla, FY25" award refers to imported
steel plate/coil as a raw-material **input**, not finished-pipe imports —
worth flagging explicitly since the two are easy to conflate. India's
crude-oil import dependency (88.2% FY25, AR) drives downstream refining
and pipeline capex, but that is captured under Method 1/5, not import
substitution of the product itself.

## SECTION 3: SAM & SOM

### 3A SAM — five filters applied to conservative TAM (₹10,85,700 Cr)
1. **Geography filter (India + Saudi Arabia/GCC + wider MENA + Africa vs
   global):** No single authoritative source gives this exact regional
   split for large-diameter line pipe specifically (**NOT FOUND** as a
   clean sourced number). Built as an analyst estimate from the relative
   scale of regional oil & gas capex (Middle East NOCs ≈15-18% of the
   global $680.85B 2026 oil & gas capex per Mordor Intelligence, Section
   4A) plus India's water-capex weight (JJM 2.0 ≈₹2.9L Cr/yr against a
   global water/desal spend base) — a **22% geography filter** is used,
   flagged explicitly as an estimate, conservative bias applied (not the
   higher end of plausible regional shares).
2. **Product/capability filter:** MANINDS covers LSAW, HSAW, ERW and
   coating — most of the product-scoped TAM already excludes structural
   pipe; a further 85% multiplier accounts for segments requiring
   certifications/approvals MANINDS does not hold everywhere in its
   footprint (e.g., non-Aramco-qualified tenders even within Saudi/GCC),
   and near-zero seamless carbon-steel scale.
3. **Customer/channel filter:** folded into the 85% above — MANINDS sells
   via direct tender to approved-vendor lists; this is the same
   constraint driving the capability filter, not treated as a separate
   multiplicative cut (to avoid double-counting the same approval-status
   barrier).
- **SAM = ₹10,85,700 Cr × 22% × 85% = ₹2,03,000 Cr** (rounded).
- SAM as % of TAM = 2,03,000 ÷ 10,85,700 ≈ **18.7%**.
- **India cross-check (Method 3):** the India-only peer-aggregate proxy of
  ₹53,400 Cr implies India is roughly 26-30% of this SAM figure, which is
  plausible given Saudi/GCC/MENA's larger dollar-denominated capex scale
  (Section 2, Method 3 discussion) — the two methods are not wildly
  inconsistent.

### 3B SOM at 3 and 5 years
Current revenue used: **₹3,593 Cr** (FY26 consolidated total income basis,
Investor Presentation p.20, since SAM already spans MANINDS's
international footprint; the standalone anchor ₹3,563.9 Cr, screener FY26
Sales, is the India-only slice within this).
- Current share of SAM = 3,593 ÷ 2,03,000 = **1.77%**.
- **Share-gain rule applied: aggressive (3-5pp), not the normal 1-2pp
  band.** Justification required by the framework and present here: the
  NPC acquisition (completed 21-May-2026) added 430,000 MTPA of
  Aramco-approved Saudi capacity in one transaction — taking group line
  pipe capacity from ~1.2Mn+ MTPA to 1.6Mn+ MTPA (+33%), with an
  immediate $120M order book and an approved-vendor status held since
  2005, i.e., capacity **and** execution evidence both already exist,
  which is exactly the framework's bar for the aggressive band.
- **3-year SOM:** +3pp → share of 4.77% by yr3.
  SOM_3yr = 4.77% × ₹2,03,000 Cr = **₹9,683 Cr ≈ ₹9,680 Cr**.
  Implied revenue CAGR: (9,680 ÷ 3,593)^(1/3) − 1 = **≈39.2%**.
- **5-year SOM:** +5pp (top of the aggressive band; justified by two
  further committed capacity events targeted for March 2027 — the Dammam
  coating plant (value-add margin capture) and the 22,000 MTPA Jammu SS
  seamless line, a genuinely new product-market entry) → share of 6.77%
  by yr5.
  SOM_5yr = 6.77% × ₹2,03,000 Cr = **₹13,743 Cr ≈ ₹13,740 Cr**.
  Implied revenue CAGR: (13,740 ÷ 3,593)^(1/5) − 1 = **≈30.8%**.

### 3C Capacity cross-check (using B07's 46% capex-embedded-growth figure)
Blended ASP assumption (industry proxy, MANINDS-specific tons-sold figure
**NOT FOUND** in extracted disclosures): line pipe ~₹75,000/ton
(mid-point of the $680-750/ton web-sourced range × ₹95.75), stainless
seamless mother pipe ~₹3,50,000/ton (analyst estimate, specialty-grade
proxy — flagged, not company-sourced).

At **80% utilisation** of committed capacity (1.6Mn MTPA line pipe +
22,000 MTPA Jammu SS, both fully online by the FY27/28 window per the
presentation's Mar-2027 targets):
- Line pipe: 1,600,000 × 0.80 × ₹75,000 = **₹9,600 Cr**
- SS seamless: 22,000 × 0.80 × ₹3,50,000 = **₹616 Cr**
- **Ceiling at 80% utilisation ≈ ₹10,216 Cr**

At 90% utilisation: ₹10,800 Cr + ₹693 Cr = **≈₹11,493 Cr**.

- **SOM_3yr (₹9,680 Cr) fits within the capacity ceiling** (₹10,216-
  11,493 Cr) — tight (requires ~76-95% utilisation across the newly
  combined base within 3 years) but physically producible on committed
  capacity. Sufficient.
- **SOM_5yr (₹13,740 Cr) exceeds the capacity ceiling** by roughly
  ₹2,250-3,520 Cr (midpoint ≈₹2,900 Cr), even at 90% utilisation and the
  full SS line ramped.
  **capacity_check: gap of ~₹2,900 Cr by yr5; the SOM assumption is the
  optimistic side of this pairing, not the capex plan.** To hit SOM_5yr,
  MANINDS would need capacity beyond the currently disclosed
  NPC+Dammam+Jammu envelope — a further expansion that is not yet
  committed in filed disclosures (management has signalled interest in
  scale via the acquisition route, per the "why acquisition beats
  greenfield" slide, but no fourth capacity event is on record as of this
  run date).

### 4C Market structure
- **Global:** moderately concentrated among integrated steel/pipe majors
  (Tenaris, Vallourec, ArcelorMittal, Nippon Steel, JFE, EEW) plus
  regional specialists; price-competitive on standard grades,
  differentiated on approvals/coating/sour-service for premium work.
- **India:** organised-sector dominated given capital intensity —
  Welspun, Jindal SAW, MANINDS, Ratnamani, PSL are the principal listed
  players; unorganised share is estimated low (~15-20%, per the Method 3
  discussion) relative to typical Indian small-cap industries, because
  Aramco/major-NOC-style qualification is a multi-year barrier few
  unorganised players can clear.
- **Saudi Arabia:** simultaneously consolidating (MANINDS's own route —
  buying an existing approved player, NPC, rather than building
  greenfield) and seeing new entry (Welspun's greenfield Dammam LSAW
  facility, targeted mid-2026) — both a consolidation and an entry story
  in the same window, worth tracking for margin/pricing effect.
- **Price vs differentiation:** commodity pricing for bare/standard-grade
  pipe; differentiation (and margin) concentrated in coated,
  high-grade-steel, and approved-vendor segments — exactly where MANINDS
  is positioning via Dammam coating and the SS seamless line.
- **Import/export note:** India (and MANINDS specifically) is a net
  **exporter** of finished large-diameter pipe; do not conflate this with
  the AR's steel-plate/coil import award, which is a raw-material input,
  not a finished-goods import signal.

### 5B Runway assessment
- Revenue headroom (SAM ÷ current revenue) = 2,03,000 ÷ 3,593 ≈ **56.5x**.
- TAM growth rate (Method 1, global blended) = **2.7%** — a mature,
  low-single-digit global average that masks much faster regional pockets
  (Saudi/GCC, India water) captured in the SAM narrowing, not the TAM
  growth figure itself.
- Company CAGR vs TAM: MANINDS's own FY22-FY26 consolidated revenue CAGR
  is **13.4%** (deck p.6) vs global TAM growth of 2.7% — the company is
  **gaining share**, not riding the global market; its growth is
  regionally and M&A-concentrated (Saudi entry, ERW line addition), not a
  broad tailwind story.
- Years to saturate SAM at current growth: even at the CLAUDE.md-cited
  25% CAGR strategy target, ln(56.5)/ln(1.25) ≈ **18 years**; at the
  actual FY22-26 historical 13.4% CAGR, ≈**32 years**. Either way, SAM
  headroom is not the binding constraint on a 3-5 year hold — **capacity
  is** (Section 3C already shows the SOM_5yr case bumping into a capacity
  gap well before SAM saturation would ever bind).

### 5C Runway classification: **MASSIVE**
Driven by the 56.5x revenue-headroom ratio. This classification should be
read with an explicit caveat: it reflects market-size headroom only. The
Section 3C finding — that SOM_5yr already exceeds committed capacity by
~₹2,900 Cr — means the practical constraint on MANINDS's growth over the
thesis horizon is execution and capex, not market size. A MASSIVE TAM
classification here should not be read by Stage 11 as "market growth
alone can carry the thesis"; it is a statement that MANINDS will not run
out of addressable demand before it runs out of capacity or execution
capability.

### 5D SAM expansion levers actually being pursued
1. **Dammam coating plant** (Mar 2027 target) — captures higher wallet
   share per pipe sold (value-add margin layer), does not expand the SAM
   boundary itself; already reflected in the capability filter.
2. **Jammu SS seamless line** (22,000 MTPA, Mar 2027 target) — a genuine
   new product-market entry (stainless mother pipes for chemical,
   defence, marine, nuclear, power, refinery). Potential addition at full
   utilisation (per 3C calc): **≈₹770-880 Cr**, revised SAM
   ≈₹2,03,800 Cr — a small absolute addition relative to core SAM, but
   the qualitative point (diversification away from oil&gas/water-cycle
   dependence) matters more than the ₹ figure.
3. **NPC acquisition itself** is the lever that converts Saudi/GCC from
   "excluded by local-content rules" to "included" in SAM's geography
   filter — i.e., this lever is already priced into the SAM base, not an
   incremental addition on top of it.

**Valuation implication line.** Current market cap ₹5,354.93 Cr
(screener-Data_Sheet.csv) ÷ FY26 consolidated PAT ₹171 Cr (deck p.20) =
**~31x trailing P/E**. Management has not disclosed a forward
consolidated PAT-margin target; NPC's own historical margin band is
disclosed as 15-18% EBITDA / 11-14% PAT (deck p.11, NPC-specific, not a

4.7% PAT margin at a revenue-weighted mix plausibly implies a

of ₹9,680 Cr and a 6.5% PAT margin, implied PAT ≈₹629 Cr, a PAT CAGR from
₹171 Cr of **≈54%** — well above the 39.2% revenue CAGR due to assumed
operating leverage.

**At 39% (yr3) revenue CAGR implied by SOM, with an analyst-constructed
margin trajectory toward ~6-8% consolidated PAT margin (not a disclosed
company target), the earnings growth embedded here is roughly 50-65%
CAGR, which supports the current valuation of ~31x P/E — conditionally.**
The condition is load-bearing: this entire SOM path already sits at the
edge of, and by yr5 beyond, committed physical capacity (Section 3C). The
