## Business Understanding Narrative

Ram Ratna buys copper, enamels it into winding wire, and charges a conversion fee. That is the whole
business model, and the agency says so in one sentence: "The company is merely a converter, where it
charges fixed conversion charges to its consumers" (Rating p.2). Copper is about 95% of the cost of
sales (Rating p.3). What the company sells is not metal, it is the process of turning metal into a
wire that goes inside a motor, a transformer, a pump or an appliance. It sells under the RR Shramik
brand, 70% to 75% direct to large OEMs and the rest through dealers (Rating p.2).

Because copper passes straight through, every margin percentage on this company is misleading.
Revenue rose 40.8% to Rs 5,176.6 crore in FY26 while the EBITDA margin was 5.1% (IP FY26). The number
that means something is rupees of profit per tonne, and CARE gives it: Rs 30,000 to Rs 35,000 per
tonne on copper wires, Rs 37,000 to Rs 52,000 per tonne on copper tubes, and Rs 38,423 on average in
FY25 (Rating p.2). Read that pair carefully. It is the entire thesis.

What is changing is the mix between those two numbers. Copper tubes and pipes went from 14% of
revenue in FY25 to 22% in FY26 to 26% in the June 2026 quarter, while enamelled wires fell from 85% to
76% to 72% (IP FY26; IP Q1FY27 p.6). The company built that shift: a 24,000 tonne per annum copper
tube plant at Bhiwadi, Rajasthan, about Rs 300 crore including a renewable power plant, operations
started June 2025 (Rating p.2). It also absorbed its own former subsidiary Global Copper, which had
7,200 tonnes of tube capacity, by amalgamation effective 23 June 2025 (Rating p.2).

Why now: the government imposed a countervailing duty on copper tubes imported from Vietnam, Thailand
and Malaysia, and air-conditioning and refrigeration demand is rising (Rating p.2). A domestic tube
maker with OEM relationships walks into a protected, growing market.

What must be true: the Bhiwadi tonnes must sell, at tube economics rather than wire economics. The
plant is more than three times the tube capacity the company had before it.

What breaks it: the balance sheet. Total borrowings went from Rs 296.2 crore to Rs 654.1 crore in one
year, inventories doubled to Rs 486.1 crore and receivables rose 64% to Rs 640.6 crore, against equity
of Rs 584.9 crore and cash of Rs 7.8 crore (IP FY26 balance sheet). A converter's working capital
grows with the copper price and with volume at the same time. CARE's own negative trigger was already
tripped at FY25 end (Rating p.3), and FY26 added more debt.

## 2. Business model and archetype
Tonnes in, tonnes out, a fee for the conversion. Copper wire capacity was 41,400 tonnes a year at
March 2025, up from 36,577 in FY21, running at 87% utilisation in FY25 against 84% in FY24
(Rating p.2). Tube capacity was 7,200 tonnes, now joined by 24,000 tonnes at Bhiwadi. Raw material
risk is managed rather than borne: "Majority (over 95%) of the inventory holding remains order-backed,
where raw material is sourced back-to-back from suppliers against confirmed orders" (Rating p.3).

Archetype: **Commodity converter.** This is unambiguous and it is the agency's own word. The dials are
spread, meaning conversion charge per tonne, utilisation, cycle position and cost-curve rank. CLAUDE.md
binds the consequence: Section 1B v3.7 Amendment 17 applies, so a deep run must not feed spot-year
ROCE or rupee working-capital trends into Section 1B or FTTCP for this name. Note that the FY26 ROCE
of 25.1% printed on the deck is exactly the spot-year number Amendment 17 exists to exclude.

## 3. Competitive advantages
Modest, and honestly described by the agency rather than by the company.
- **Scale inside a fragmented industry.** "RRWL operates in a highly competitive and fragmented copper
  winding wire industry characterised by the presence of numerous organised and unorganised players,
  given the low technical expertise requirement and value addition" (Rating p.3). Being one of the
  largest organised players is worth something against unorganised competition, and CARE says the
  long-standing relationships "provide it with a competitive edge over unorganised players".
- **Two to three decades of OEM qualification**, supplying power, auto, consumer durable, transformer
  and construction customers (Rating p.2). Winding wire goes inside a motor; requalification is slow.
- **Group backing.** RR Global carries RR Kabel, RR Parkon, RR Busduct and MEW Electricals
  (Rating p.1). Distribution, procurement and credibility come with that.
- **A policy moat on the new product**, not of the company's making: the countervailing duty on tubes
  from Vietnam, Thailand and Malaysia (Rating p.2). Real while it lasts, and not a durable asset.

Against all of it: "low technical expertise requirement and value addition", and a 4% to 5% margin
band that has held for four years.
**Verdict: no moat in the winding wire business. A forming one in tubes, resting on scale, a duty and
an OEM list rather than on anything proprietary.**

## 4. The promoters
Three generations of the Kabra family, and the corpus is unusually clear on the succession.
Rameshwarlal Kabra founded RR Global and has "over five decades of extensive experience in enamelled
copper winding wire industry". Mahendra Kabra, second generation, is Managing Director with "over
three decades of experience". Hemant Kabra, third generation and Mahendra's son, is Joint Managing
Director and "manages finance, marketing, and industrial and consumer product business"
(Rating p.1). The company was incorporated in 1992 by Rameshwarlal Kabra with sons Tribhuvan Prasad,
Mahendra Kumar and Shreegopal Kabra (Rating p.3).

Governance is the best evidenced of the seven names read in this run, and it is evidenced by a third
party. CARE records: "RRWL's board of directors has ten directors which include five
independent/non-executive directors out of which one is a woman director. Chairman of audit,
nomination and remuneration, stakeholders' relationship and corporate social responsibility committees
are independent directors" (Rating p.3). Half the board independent, and independents chairing all
four key committees, is a materially better structure than the family-only boards seen elsewhere in
this run.

Building, under the Rule G test, is plainly documented. The promoters raised debt, built a Rs 300
crore plant, brought it into production in June 2025, and absorbed a subsidiary by scheme. The board
approved a Rs 700 crore capex outlay on 2 August 2024, of which the Rs 300 crore tube plant is the
first tranche, and sensibly "further capex would be finalised only post stabilisation of this capex"
(Rating p.3). Deploying under constraint, then pausing, is a good sign.

Promoter holding percentage: **NOT FOUND.** Pledge: **NOT FOUND.** Looked for in the rating, the FY26
deck and the July run manifest. The 315-page annual report held on disk carries both and was not
opened in this shallow read. This is the easiest gap on any card in this run to close.

Three things move together, which is what a real transition looks like. Revenue compounded 73.5% over
two years. EBITDA compounded 121.5% and profit after tax 98.9% over the same two years, so profit grew
faster than sales even at a 2% net margin. And the EBITDA margin broke a four-year band: 4.1%, 4.0%,
4.3%, then 5.1%.

That last number matters more than it looks, because an outsider named the threshold in advance. CARE's
stated positive rating factor, written in September 2025, was "improvement in PBILDT margin above 5% on
a sustained basis" (Rating p.1). FY26 printed 5.1%. The company cleared a bar an independent agency had
set, in the year after it was set. Q1 FY27 at 4.8% means "sustained" is not yet established, and that
is the open question, not a failure.

The June quarter reads soft against a very strong March quarter and strong against a year earlier.
Sequentially, revenue rose 5.7% while EBITDA fell 3.9% and the margin went 5.3% to 4.8%. Year on year,
revenue rose 88.6% while EBITDA rose 109.0% and the margin went 4.4% to 4.8% (IP Q1FY27 p.7). The
year-on-year comparison is the fair one, and it says operating leverage is working in rupees.

## 6. The transition and the quality ladder
FROM **R1 COMMODITY PRICE-TAKER.** A fixed conversion charge on a pass-through metal, in a fragmented
industry with "low technical expertise requirement", earning 4% for four years. The agency's word
"merely" is doing real work in that sentence.
TO **R2 COST-ADVANTAGED CONVERTER**, with a reach toward the lower edge of R3. The tube business earns
Rs 37,000 to Rs 52,000 a tonne against Rs 30,000 to Rs 35,000 for wire (Rating p.2), so the same
conversion model at a better rate, protected for now by a countervailing duty and by scale nobody else
domestically has at Bhiwadi.
Mechanism: shift tonnes from the lower-fee product to the higher-fee product, and own enough capacity
that import substitution flows to you.
**Management's claim, quoted once:** "RRWL is strategically positioned to become a leading domestic
supplier in India's rapidly expanding copper tubes & pipes market, enabling large-scale import
substitution while serving global and domestic HVAC demand" (IP FY26, copper tubes slide).
**Ladder ruling: one rung, correctly paced, and the mechanism is arithmetic rather than narrative.**
The honest caution is that a higher conversion fee is still a conversion fee. Tubes do not give this
company pricing power; they give it a better spread. R3 would require spec-in and switching costs, and
the only candidate there is Inner Grooved Tubes, whose production is still "in progress" (IP FY26).

## 8. Proof check
**The proof gate has FIRED**, on the transition the company actually claimed, and it is the only card
in this run where that is true.

The claim was diversification into copper tubes. Test it across five quarters and three years:
- Copper tubes went 14% to 22% to 26% of revenue (IP FY26; IP Q1FY27 p.6). Not a plan, a mix.
- EBITDA margin broke a four-year 4.0% to 4.3% band and reached 5.1% in FY26 (IP FY26).
- Return on capital employed rose 630 basis points, 18.8% to 25.1% (IP FY26). Read as direction, not
  as a level, per Amendment 17.
- Profit after tax doubled in two years, Rs 54.6 crore to Rs 108.6 crore, while EBITDA rose faster than
  revenue in both FY26 and the June quarter year on year.
- A Rs 300 crore plant was funded, built, commissioned and is producing, verified independently by an
  agency that visited the numbers (Rating p.2).

Named proof point: **the margin crossed the threshold an outsider had set in advance.** CARE wrote in
September 2025 that PBILDT above 5% sustained would be a positive rating factor; FY26 delivered 5.1%.
That is as close to a pre-registered test as a shallow read ever gets.

The honest qualifications. Q1 FY27 came in at 4.8%, so "sustained" is not yet proven, and the company
needs two or three more quarters above 5% to close it. Volume in tonnes is not disclosed for FY26 or
Q1 FY27, so the mix shift cannot be separated from the copper price with the corpus held. And the
working capital cycle deteriorated from about 18 days to about 33 days in the same year the margin
improved, which is the cost of the climb showing up somewhere else.

## 9. Flags from the documents
- **The deck's leverage ratio excludes most of the debt.** Net Debt/Equity is printed as 0.46 times for
  FY26, on a definition the deck itself prints: "(Non-current Borrowings + Non-current Lease
  Liabilities - Cash & Cash Equivalents (incl. bank balances) / Total Equity" (IP FY26, financial
  indicators). Current borrowings were Rs 388.8 crore, 59% of total borrowings, and are excluded.
  Including them the same ratio is about 1.13 times. CARE, using its own overall gearing measure, put
  it at 1.28 times as far back as March 2025 (Rating p.3). **The strongest flag on this card.** Not a
  misstatement, the definition is disclosed, but a reader who takes 0.46 at face value will misjudge
  this company badly.
- **CARE's negative sensitivity on capital structure was already triggered at FY25 end** (Rating p.3),
  and FY26 added Rs 358 crore of borrowings.
- **The working capital cycle roughly doubled in FY26**, from about 18 days to about 33 days, computed
  from the deck's own balance sheet. The rating still describes a shortening cycle, because it is a
  year old.
- **Cash is Rs 7.8 crore** against Rs 388.8 crore of current borrowings (IP FY26 balance sheet).
- **FY25 operating cash flow is flattered by financing.** CARE attributes the rise from Rs 108 crore to
  Rs 230 crore to "significant increase in LC backed acceptances" (Rating p.3). Letter-of-credit
  acceptances sit in payables and lift operating cash flow while being, in substance, borrowing.
- **Equity share capital doubled, from Rs 22.0 crore to Rs 46.7 crore**, and the held extracts do not
  say why (IP FY26 balance sheet). The Global Copper amalgamation and a bonus issue are both plausible.
  NOT FOUND, and it changes per-share arithmetic, so verify before any valuation.
- **Exports are shrinking**, 8% to 7% of revenue in FY26 and 8% to 6% in the June quarter (IP FY26;
  IP Q1FY27 p.6). The import-substitution story is domestic, and that is fine, but nobody should read
  this as a global business.
- **The policy moat is a duty.** The countervailing duty on tubes from Vietnam, Thailand and Malaysia
  (Rating p.2) can be withdrawn, and the corpus gives no expiry date.
- **The independent check is twelve months stale.** See step 10.
- **A repository defect, not a company one.** `runs/ramrat-2026-07-29/manifest.yaml` carries
  `sector_cap_row: "Pharma / CDMO"` with the note "auto-picked; verify". That is the wrong sector cap
  row for a copper converter and would corrupt a Section 1B run. Fix before `/step1`.

## 10. Independent check: the credit rating
**COMPLETE BUT STALE.** CARE Ratings, 30 September 2025: CARE A-; Stable on long-term facilities and
CARE A2+ on short-term, with Rs 188.95 crore newly assigned and the rest reaffirmed on materially
enhanced limits, long-term and short-term facilities enhanced from Rs 210 crore to Rs 355 crore and
from Rs 183 crore to Rs 313 crore (Rating p.1). Consolidated view including Tefabo Products (64%),
Epavo Electricals (50%) and RR-Imperial (10%).

On sustainability, the agency is positive and specific: strength from "experienced and resourceful
promoters, prominent position in the copper winding wires industry in India, long-standing association
with reputed clientele and adequate liquidity", plus the benefit expected from the tube capex
(Rating p.1). Liquidity is "Adequate", with gross cash accruals of Rs 95 crore in FY25 expected to
reach Rs 127 to Rs 187 crore across FY26 to FY28 against Rs 35 to Rs 50 crore of scheduled repayments
(Rating p.3). That is comfortable coverage.

On leverage, it is blunt: gearing moved from 0.85 to 1.28 times, TOL to tangible net worth from 1.05 to
1.69 times, the negative sensitivity was triggered, and further term debt drawdown in FY26 would
"further moderate" gearing (Rating p.3).

On the transition, it gives the operator the two numbers the thesis needs: Rs 30,000 to Rs 35,000 per
tonne on wire against Rs 37,000 to Rs 52,000 on tubes (Rating p.2). Everything in step 6 rests on that
pair.

**The freshness caveat is the point.** This rationale is built on FY25 and predates both the FY26
results and the June quarter. Since it was written, the margin cleared the 5% bar the agency named as a
positive factor, and the working capital cycle deteriorated in a way the agency has not yet seen. Both
readings are available to the operator and neither is in the rating. Today's filings win.

## 11. Posture: the Transition Decision Matrix
- **Proof gate: FIRED.** Step 8. Mix, margin and return moved together across three years and five
  quarters, against a threshold an outsider set in advance.
- **Ugliness: split, and the split is the whole reading.** The 2.1% net margin and the 5.1% EBITDA
  margin are a **STRUCTURAL FEATURE** of the converter archetype, not decay and not a phase. A
  conversion business on a copper-inflated revenue base will always print a thin percentage; that is
  why CARE measures rupees per tonne. Reading that thin margin as ugliness is precisely the Amendment
  17 trap. The rising leverage and the stretched working capital, by contrast, are
  **ARTIFACT-OF-CLIMB**: borrowed to build a plant that is now running and already 26% of revenue.
- **Recognition gap: CLOSED, or very nearly.** Rs 4,922 crore of market value against Rs 108.6 crore of
  FY26 profit is about 45 times trailing earnings; against the June quarter annualised it is about 35
  times. That is a re-rated multiple for a business whose own rating agency calls it "merely a
  converter". The climb has been noticed. The July 2026 run recorded a price of Rs 454; the identity
  record implies about Rs 526 today, so it has continued to be noticed.

**Posture: EARNINGS-ONLY.** Proof fired, the transition real, and the market has already repriced it.
The framework: "Climb real but already priced; the re-rating engine is spent, returns ride EPS CAGR
alone. Actionable only if growth clears the Hurdle without help from re-rating."

That makes the whole question arithmetic, and the corpus permits the arithmetic. **[INFERENCE]** The
Bhiwadi plant adds 24,000 tonnes a year on top of 7,200 tonnes of existing tube capacity. At CARE's
disclosed tube economics of Rs 37,000 to Rs 52,000 per tonne, a mid-point of Rs 45,000 on 24,000 tonnes
at full utilisation is roughly Rs 108 crore of incremental EBITDA, against FY26 EBITDA of Rs 263.6
crore. That is a possible uplift of about 41% from one plant filling, before any wire growth. The
inference rests on three unverified links: that the plant reaches full utilisation, that FY25 per-tonne
economics hold at scale, and that the duty stays. A deep run must test all three.

## 12. Verdict card

**PROCEED to `/step1`.**

Ram Ratna is the one name in this run where the transition has already happened rather than being
promised. Copper tubes went from 14% to 26% of revenue in five quarters. A Rs 300 crore plant was
funded, built and commissioned. The EBITDA margin broke a four-year band and cleared the exact
threshold an independent agency had named a year earlier. Return on capital rose 630 basis points.
Profit doubled in two years. Three generations of promoters run a board that is half independent, with
independents chairing every key committee. And the corpus is already on the operator's disk, including
a 315-page annual report and five peer transcripts this shallow read did not open.

The reason it is not a stronger call is the posture, not the business. At roughly 45 times trailing
earnings the market has repriced the climb. The re-rating engine that makes transition alpha work looks
spent here, which means the 25% CAGR would have to come from earnings growth alone. That is possible on
the tube arithmetic above, and it is exactly the sum a `/step1` exists to do properly.

The bear case at the same bar: this remains a converter with 2% net margins, whose agency describes
"low technical expertise requirement and value addition", whose cash is Rs 7.8 crore against Rs 388.8
crore of current borrowings, whose working capital cycle nearly doubled in FY26, whose deck reports
leverage on a definition that excludes 59% of its borrowings, and whose new product is protected by a
duty that a policy change could remove.

4. Promoter holding and pledge. Both NOT FOUND here, both in the annual report on disk.
5. Why equity share capital doubled from Rs 22.0 crore to Rs 46.7 crore. Amalgamation shares, bonus, or

6. The countervailing duty: its scope, rate and expiry.
7. Whether CARE has published since September 2025, and whether FY26's 5.1% margin has triggered a

8. **Before any Stage 11 run: fix `sector_cap_row` in `runs/ramrat-2026-07-29/manifest.yaml`.** It
