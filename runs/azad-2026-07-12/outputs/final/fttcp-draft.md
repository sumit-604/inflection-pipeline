## THE CATALYST STORY IN PLAIN WORDS

Revenue keeps firing because the work is already sold. The order book is roughly 13 times a single year of revenue, filled with named multi-year contracts from the biggest turbine and engine makers in the world, and the factories to build it are being handed over one by one. What confirms it is quarterly revenue holding above 25% growth. What kills it is a marquee customer deferring or an order book that stops growing while new contracts are announced, which would mean old contracts are rolling off as fast as new ones arrive.

Margin has nothing left to prove and no room to climb. At 37% it is already nearly double the nearest peer and above what management itself guides for the long run. The realistic path is a gentle drift toward 33 to 35% as the mix and ramp costs settle. What would change the call is a documented shift into hot-section parts, like the Mitsubishi Nozzle Vane, actually lifting the blended margin rather than holding it.

Cash is the whole story. The business is not losing money, it is funding its own growth through inventory and receivables, and the question is whether that reverses. The evidence that it does is strong on paper: the rating agency attributes the drain to alloy stock for parts under development and to three units mid-commissioning and says it normalises with serial production, and the closest comparable company went through the identical squeeze and came out the other side with cash flow swinging sharply positive. The evidence that it might not is behavioural: management promised this improvement for the back half of FY26, did not deliver, loosened the target, and will not put an operating cash flow number on the table. What confirms the catalyst is a positive operating cash flow print in FY27. What kills it is another negative print with working capital days stuck at 340 plus.

ROCE recovers only if the cash does. The same commissioned factories that release working capital are the ones that lift EBIT on the deployed capital base. The catch is that Azad is not finished spending, so every year of recovery meets a new year of capex on the denominator. This is why the recovery target is mid-teens, not the 20% plus that drives the big re-ratings.
