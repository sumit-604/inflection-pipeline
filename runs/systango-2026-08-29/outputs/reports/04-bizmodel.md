## SECTION 1: THE BUSINESS MODEL IN PLAIN ENGLISH

### 1A. One-line description
Systango is a small, Indore-based digital engineering shop. It builds software, AI systems, and blockchain products for clients in the UK and USA, billing mostly in pounds and dollars while paying its engineers in rupees.

### 1B. Money flow chain, by revenue stream

The company discloses five revenue channels in its investor deck, but gives no rupee split between them (Inv. Pres. slide "Multi Channel Revenue Streams"). The single line item in the audited accounts is "Software & Development Charges," Rs. 6,132.96 lakhs standalone for FY25 (AR Note 16, p.83).

| Stream | Input | What Systango does | What it delivers | Who pays | How they pay |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Staff augmentation | Engineer hours | Embeds developers inside a client's own product team | Dedicated capacity | The client company | Time & materials, billed monthly (Inv. Pres. slide "Multi Channel Revenue Streams") |
| Time & materials / project billing | A defined scope | Builds a product end to end, prototype to launch | A working app, platform, or AI system | The client company | Milestone or T&M invoices (same slide) |
| Managed & maintenance contracts | An already-built system | Runs, patches, and supports it | Uptime and SLA-backed support | The client company | Recurring fee (same slide) |
| Solution licensing | Its own IP (e.g. Swotter LMS, Shootih wealth tool) | Licenses a pre-built product | A branded, white-label platform | A licensee | Licence fee (same slide) |
| BaaS (Blockchain-as-a-Service) | Blockchain infrastructure it built once | Hosts and operates it for a client | A running blockchain platform | The client company | Pay-as-you-go (same slide) |

Read-through: four of the five streams are still tied to a named client relationship (staff aug, project billing, managed contracts, BaaS-per-client). Only solution licensing looks close to true product economics, and its size is NOT FOUND, check investor presentation or concall.

### 1E. The chai-stall-uncle version

Imagine a small but well-regarded workshop in Indore. Foreign shopkeepers in London and New York need someone to build and fix their digital shopfronts, so they hire this workshop's craftsmen by the month. The workshop pays its craftsmen in rupees but bills the foreign shopkeepers in pounds and dollars, so every rupee of local wage buys more foreign revenue than it costs. Lately the workshop has also started selling a few of its own tools (an online-learning product, a wealth-management dashboard) instead of only renting out labour, but that side business is still tiny. The workshop's biggest risk is not a shortage of work; it is that ten shopkeepers supply two-thirds of its orders, and if two or three walk away, the workshop feels it immediately.

### 2D. Industry lifecycle stage

The broad global IT-services industry is mature (India IT industry revenue grew 5.1% in FY25 to USD 282.6bn, AR Annexure D, p.50). The specific niches Systango plays in — Generative AI/LLM development, Web3/blockchain, cloud-native migration — are early-to-growth stage within that mature industry (global IT spending projected to surpass USD 5.5tn in 2025 with services growing 9-10%, AR Annexure D, p.50-51). Systango itself is a small, newly public company (listed NSE Emerge March 2023) showing accelerating growth: consolidated revenue growth stepped up from roughly 18.7% (FY24 to FY25, AR Annexure D p.51) to 34.7% (FY25 to FY26, Inv. Pres. slide "Annual Financial Performance").

### 3D. Unit economics — the physics of this business

**Unit**: one employee (the company does not disclose per-hour billing rates, so revenue-per-employee is the best available proxy for a person-hours-billed business).

**Revenue per employee, consolidated, over time** (revenue: Inv. Pres. "Historical Consolidated Income Statement"; headcount: Inv. Pres. "Key Performance Metrics," "Employee Bifurcation," technical + support):

| Year | Revenue (Rs. mn) | Headcount | Revenue per head (Rs. lakh) |
|---|---|---|---|
| FY23 | 523 | 312 (281 technical + 31 support) | ~16.8 |
| FY24 | 566 | 275 (244 + 31) | ~20.6 |
| FY25 | 671 | 276 (251 + 25) | ~24.3 |
| FY26 | 904 | 308 (285 + 23) | ~29.4 |

This is the single most telling number in the whole file: revenue per head has risen every year, roughly doubling from FY23 to FY26, while headcount barely moved (312 to 308, briefly dipping to 275). Growth is coming from pricing and mix upgrade (more AI/ML/Cloud work at higher realisation), not from adding bodies. That is high-quality, operating-leverage-driven growth, if it holds.

**Cost per unit**: standalone Employee Benefits Expense was Rs. 3,414.93 lakhs in FY25 (AR Note 18, p.84). Against an approximate standalone-linked headcount of ~276 (Inv. Pres. FY25 total), fully loaded cost per head is roughly Rs. 12.4 lakh — well below the consolidated revenue-per-head figure of Rs. 24.3 lakh for the same year, evidencing the offshore-cost arbitrage that drives the margin.

**Volume drivers**: number of active client engagements and headcount deployed.
**Price drivers**: mix shift toward AI/ML/GenAI work (higher-value billing) and GBP/USD versus INR.
**Cost drivers**: Indian tech-talent wage inflation, specifically for AI/GenAI specialists.
**Incremental margin / operating leverage**: strong and visible. EBITDA margin expanded from 30.2% to 37.6% (Inv. Pres. "Annual Financial Performance") over the same period revenue per head rose 75%, consistent with a people-light, high-operating-leverage services model where each new project adds far more to revenue than to headcount cost.

### 4B. Valuation method applicability — handoff to Role 1

| Method | Applicable? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| P/E (normalised, ex-other-income EPS) | Yes | Best-fit primary. Business is profitable, asset-light, single reportable segment; strip the ~15-24% of PBT that is treasury income before comparing multiples |
| EV/EBITDA, net of cash/investments | Yes | Good secondary cross-check. With Rs. 4,602.37 lakhs (AR Note 11, p.82) plus further cash growth through FY26 (consolidated investments of Rs. 676mn plus cash Rs. 157mn per Inv. Pres. "Historical Consolidated Balance Sheet") sitting on the balance sheet, EV (not raw market cap) is the right denominator |
| DCF | Conditional, tertiary at best | Free cash flow is real and growing fast (Rs. 7mn to Rs. 333mn, FY23 to FY26, Inv. Pres. "Consolidated Financial Performance"), but only 3 years of post-IPO public history and high client concentration make multi-year cash-flow forecasting fragile |
| Net-cash-adjusted / SOTP floor check | Yes, as a sanity check only | With ~Rs. 830mn of investments + cash sitting on a small-cap balance sheet (FY26, consolidated), a cash-adjusted floor value is worth checking against the market cap, especially given ~55% of IPO proceeds remain unutilised two years post-listing |
| Dividend Discount Model | Not applicable as primary | Company just started paying (interim dividend Rs. 7/share declared H2-FY26, Inv. Pres. "H2-FY26/FY26 Operational Highlights"); too short a track record and too growth-stage for DDM to anchor value |
| Replacement cost / asset-based (ex-cash) | Not applicable | Asset-light services business; no meaningful hard-asset base to replace |
| Segment-level SOTP | Not applicable currently | Single reportable operating segment disclosed; the licensing/BaaS product lines are not broken out with separate financials |

**Primary**: P/E on normalised (ex-other-income) earnings, cross-checked against the Section 1B destination-PE framework (Section 1B governs the actual multiple — this stage only names the method).
**Secondary**: EV/EBITDA, net of the company's substantial cash and investment balance.
**Tertiary**: Net-cash-adjusted floor check, given the scale of unutilised IPO proceeds and treasury balance relative to company size.
**Cycle stage that matters**: secular/structural growth (AI-services re-rating), not a commodity cycle — use forward, not trailing, growth assumptions, and do not let the current treasury-income cushion inflate the "quality" of the earnings base being valued.

### 4C. Quarterly monitoring checklist

1. Consolidated revenue growth YoY and QoQ (good: sustained above ~20%; trouble: single-digit or negative)
2. EBITDA margin, and specifically whether it is expanding on the operating line or only via other income
3. Top-3 and top-10 client revenue concentration, if disclosed in any subsequent deck (good: stable/falling; trouble: rising further from FY26's 42%/72%)
4. Standalone Trade Receivable Turnover Ratio / DSO (good: recovering toward 6-7x; trouble: further fall below 4.5x)
5. Related-party sales as % of standalone revenue (good: continuing to fall from 32.2%; trouble: rising back toward the 42% FY24 level)
6. Revenue per employee (good: continued rise; trouble: flat or falling)
7. Headcount growth versus revenue growth (good: revenue growth outpaces headcount growth; trouble: the reverse)
8. Other income as % of PBT (good: continuing to fall; trouble: rising back above ~20-25%)
9. Effective tax rate (good: stable ~18-20%; trouble: a step-up signalling SEZ benefit erosion)
10. Doubtful debt provision and bad debts written off (good: stable/falling; trouble: rising)
11. Cash and investment balance and how much of unutilised IPO proceeds has been deployed (good: declining unutilised balance with a stated use; trouble: static balance, no stated use)
12. Revenue mix by technology service line — AI/ML/Data/Cloud share (good: stable/rising from 46%; trouble: falling)
13. Any named client win/loss disclosed in exchange filings or concalls
14. Dividend/capital-return consistency versus stated capital-allocation plan

### 4D. Highest-value questions for management

1. What is the rupee split between staff augmentation, managed contracts, and solution licensing/BaaS revenue, and what share of revenue is genuinely recurring?
 Reassures: a rising, disclosed recurring-revenue share. Worries: a refusal to break this out, implying it is close to zero.

2. Why does standalone-to-related-party sales run 32-42% of standalone revenue through the UK/US subsidiaries, and is this transfer-pricing structure stable and audited?
 Reassures: documented arm's-length pricing, declining trend, no transfer-pricing disputes. Worries: a rising percentage or any adverse tax/transfer-pricing finding.

3. Who are the identified top-3/top-10 clients, and what is their contract renewal status entering FY27?
 Reassures: multi-year MSAs recently renewed at flat or higher wallet share. Worries: month-to-month statements of work, or any top account in run-off.

4. What is utilisation, given headcount grew only ~13.5% (FY25 to FY26) while consolidated revenue grew 34.7%?
 Reassures: utilisation still has headroom (below ~85-90%). Worries: utilisation already near saturation, risking delivery quality and attrition.

5. What is the deployment plan for the Rs. 1,704.75 lakhs of still-unutilised net IPO proceeds (Rs. 3,083.53 lakhs net proceeds less Rs. 1,378.38 lakhs utilised through FY25-end, AR Note 21C.1, p.87), and why does the company hold enough in mutual funds and fixed deposits to generate ~24% of standalone PBT as treasury income?
 Reassures: a specific, funded M&A or capacity plan (the deck says the company is "actively exploring strategic acquisition opportunities," Inv. Pres. "Company Overview"). Worries: indefinite parking with no stated plan.

6. What is the SEZ tax-holiday timeline — which assessment year does the unit's 100%-then-50% deduction step down, and what is the expected normalised effective tax rate afterward?
 Reassures: several years of holiday runway still ahead. Worries: a near-term step-down not yet reflected in market expectations for PAT margin.

7. Is the roughly 50-percentage-point swing in the "IT" client-vertical share (from ~50-55% in FY24-FY25 to ~25% in FY26, per the investor deck chart) genuine diversification into FinTech, Hospitality, and Logistics, or the loss/reclassification of one large IT client?
 Reassures: multiple new logos across the smaller verticals. Worries: a single client roll-off dressed up as diversification.
