Water treatment EPC scaling faster than cash

Apex Ecotech builds turnkey water treatment plants on contract, delivering WTP, ETP, STP, ZLD and membrane recycling systems for industrial and commercial clients. FY26 revenue was Rs 149 Cr, PAT was Rs 17 Cr, and the shares trade near Rs 242 for a market cap of Rs 319 Cr. Products and full plant delivery make up 96% of sales; operations and maintenance make up the remaining 4%. The model is asset light, working capital heavy, and carries no meaningful debt, with a debt to equity ratio of 0.00 and a current ratio of 4.50x at FY25.

Revenue doubled in FY26, up 109.5% off a smaller FY25 base that itself grew about 34%. The company listed on NSE Emerge in December 2024 and is now scaling on the back of a single anchor order from Reliance Consumer Products worth Rs 100 Cr to Rs 125 Cr, which management says accounts for roughly 70% of second half FY26 execution. The stated direction is toward larger ticket ZLD work and a higher margin mix. Two prior growth vectors have gone the other way: the international push into the Gulf and Vietnam was explicitly abandoned by the May 2026 call, and export revenue had already fallen 59% in FY25 with complete customer and geography churn.

Gate 0 scores 86 of 160, which classifies as AVERAGE. The classification is capped there by the cash conversion deal breaker, not by weak returns. Backward looking moat strength reads STRONG with 5 moats confirmed, but the forward looking emerging moat score is 10.1 against a threshold of 12, which reads NONE. Combined that is an AVERAGE business without a proven forward catalyst, and the single strong emerging item, the war chest, is an IPO artefact rather than a growth engine. The AVERAGE cap is flagged as a possible post IPO rebase case for later position sizing review.

Cash conversion is the central issue. FY25 operating cash flow was negative, reported at minus Rs 14.08 Cr in the original filing and restated to minus Rs 5.24 Cr in the audited FY26 comparative, against positive PAT of Rs 8.56 Cr. FY26 operating cash flow recovered to Rs 6.77 Cr but still ran at only 0.40 times PAT of Rs 17.02 Cr. Trade receivables rose 155% while revenue rose 34%, debtors turnover fell from 6.77x to 4.61x, and the ageing table carried zero doubtful debt provision across both years. About 48% of receivables are contractual retention money, which is structural to EPC and released on project completion, and the company held Rs 35 Cr of cash at year end. The evidence does not settle whether the shortfall is a one time listing year build or a structural drag, and the third party check that would settle it, a credit rating rationale with working capital commentary, was not available because SME listed issuers are exempt from a mandatory rating.

Management credibility grades B. Numeric commitments were met or beaten, with FY26 revenue crushing the at least 25% guidance. The governance and communication commitments were not: the ESOP promised for end FY26 was dropped without mention, quarterly numeric reporting was requested on all three calls and delivered only once as a percentage only circular, the H1 to H2 revenue skew widened rather than narrowed, and order book figures could not be reconciled on a live call across values of 55, 62, 145 and 125 Cr. Director remuneration rose 90.9% in the listing year, and two statutory disclosures contradict each other on the same executives, one implying a 169.3% pay rise and the other stating 37.02%.

Peers complicate the story rather than confirm it. Four peers across 16 quarterly calls were read. They corroborate rising input costs but at a smaller magnitude and later timing than the 25 to 40% raw material inflation Apex claimed for second half FY26, and they tie it to a specific Middle East shipping disruption rather than a broad metals move. The core TAM claim on ZLD penetration and the customer concentration question both came back unverifiable against peer disclosure. The wider peer pattern is strong demand talk early in FY26 followed by successive guidance cuts on execution and payment timing. One caveat weakens the peer read further: verification found two peer citations behind the corroborating claims were fabricated or misattributed to the wrong speaker and company.

Market runway is genuinely large. TAM sits at Rs 11,250 Cr to Rs 14,500 Cr, the serviceable slice at Rs 6,375 Cr, and the three year serviceable obtainable market at Rs 277 Cr, giving a runway class of STRONG with about 43x revenue headroom against a current 2.33% serviceable share. The binding constraint is not the market. It is order book replenishment, with a book to bill ratio of 0.84x and an order book that grew far slower than the revenue it fed. Margins are drifting the wrong way, with EBITDA margin down from 16.7% to 14.6% across FY24 to FY26 and cost of material consumed up from 68.9% to 75.6% of revenue.

Governance carries five cautions without a legal red. The promoter record is clean, holding is 69.29% and pledge is 0%. The cautions are three related enterprises sharing the corporate address with zero disclosed transactions, executive pay divergence from employee pay in the listing year, independent director attendance of 33% to 42% with an executive on the audit committee, three company secretary changes in about 16 months around the IPO, and a fourth promoter exiting the board eight months before listing with no reason disclosed.
