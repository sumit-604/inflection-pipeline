## Business Understanding Narrative

**What does it do, and how does it make money?** Sakuma buys sugar from Indian mills and sells it to exporters' buyers abroad and to Indian institutions. It also trades maize, edible oil, pulses and other agri goods (AR26, pp.26-27). Sugar is "majority of its revenue" (INF-26, p.3). Clients include Britannia, ITC, Coca Cola and Parle (INF-26, p.3). It owns no plant and had 42 employees (AR26, p.29). FY26 turnover was Rs 1,708.28 crore and PAT Rs 9.51 crore (AR26, p.16). The margin is the spread between mill price and selling price, on back-to-back contracts (ICRA-25, p.2).

**What is changing?** Two things. First, India banned sugar exports from October 2023, allowed quotas in November 2025, and banned them again on 13-May-2026 to 30-Sep-2026 (INF-26, p.3). The company moved to lower-margin domestic institutional sugar and maize for ethanol (ICRA-25, p.2). Second, the capital base. A Rs 199.83 crore rights issue in April 2024 (Rights-MA, p.2) and a 4:1 bonus (AR25, p.99) took the share count to 156.77 crore. The board now proposes 65 crore more (Board, p.1).

**Why now?** The operator named it. The filings give no business reason. Q1 FY27 PAT rose to Rs 2.82 crore from Rs 1.65 crore on revenue down 11.3% (Q1 res, p.8). Trailing PE is 27.9 and market cap Rs 298 crore against Rs 748.86 crore of net worth (MD; INF-26, p.6).

**What must be true?** Sugar exports must reopen with quotas Sakuma can win. Diversification into ethanol, wheat flour, edible oil and corn must earn a spread (INF-26, p.3). The Rs 107.90 crore must earn more than the mutual funds it sits beside.

**What breaks it?** The export ban persists. Other income stops carrying the profit. The new allottees, not the promoter, set direction after the issue.

## 2. Business model and archetype

One segment, "trading in agro products" (Q1 res, p.8). Export earnings were Rs 265.61 crore FOB in FY26 against Rs 305.31 crore in FY25, standalone (AR26, p.20). Overseas subsidiaries in Dubai, Singapore, the UK and Bahrain added Rs 267.33 crore of turnover in FY26 (AR26, p.19).

The engine is volume times a thin spread. EBITDA margin was 0.42% in FY26 and 0.61% in FY25 (INF-26, p.6). Institutional sugar earns a lower return than exports (ICRA-25, p.2). Maize is priced at mandi plus carrying cost plus a fixed margin (ICRA-25, p.2).

**Archetype: fits none cleanly.** The nearest is commodity converter, because the economics are a spread and cycle position; Amendment 17 binds any deep run. Sakuma converts nothing. It is a policy-dependent trader.

## 3. Competitive advantages

**Relationships: present, not a moat.** Two decades with mills and buyers (ICRA-25, p.1). **Risk process: present.** Back-to-back orders, forward cover, 10% customer advances on maize (ICRA-25, p.2). **Pricing power: absent.** Margins track policy, not the company. **Cost or scale advantage: absent.** No asset base, 42 staff. **Scarcity: absent.** Export quotas go to the trade, not to Sakuma.

**Verdict: moat absent.**

## 10. Independent check: the credit rating

Infomerics assigned IVR BBB/Stable on Rs 133 crore of bank lines on 07-Sep-2026 (INF-26, p.1). It cites comfortable capital structure and a strong net worth. It flags product concentration, falling revenue and profit, and rising working-capital days, now 106 against 78 (INF-26, pp.1-4). The downgrade trigger is PAT margin below 0.50% sustained; FY26 was 0.55% (INF-26, pp.5-6). ICRA reaffirmed BBB (Stable)/A3+ on Rs 165 crore in June 2025 (ICRA-25, p.1). Acuite downgraded to BB+ on non-cooperation and withdrew in November 2021 (ACU-21, p.1).

The agencies see a solvent trader with thin, policy-bound margins. None sees a transition. The 2026 filings, including the dilution, post-date the rating.

## 12. Verdict card

**PASS.**

No climb exists to underwrite. The profit is treasury income, the core spread is near zero, and the board proposes to sell 29.3% of the post-issue company below book.
