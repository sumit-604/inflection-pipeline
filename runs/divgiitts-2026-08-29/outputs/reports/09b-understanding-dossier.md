### PART A - THE FROM STATE

**A1. Archetype.** Single reportable business segment (Note 39, cited in B04) covering all product lines, so one archetype declaration applies across lines. Primary archetype: **build-to-spec component maker** (customer platform/capex cycle, design-win pipeline, content per unit, input-cost pass-through) per the ARCHETYPE LIBRARY. Secondary traits of an **outsourcing partner** (client concentration, wallet share, capacity fill) are present but not primary (B04 `analyst_note`: "not a commodity converter, so Amendment 17 converter restrictions... do not bind this name"). This applies to every current line - Transfer Case, Transmission Components, Synchronizer Systems, ALH, and the newer E-Gear Drive (EV transmission) line.

**A2. The simple analogy.** Divgi makes the mechanical part that lets a Mahindra Thar or a Tata pickup shift into four-wheel drive. Big carmakers do not build this part themselves; they qualify Divgi to design it, manufacture it in Pune-area plants, and deliver it to the vehicle assembly line just in time. Divgi never sells to the end car buyer. It sells to the carmaker, one truckload of parts at a time, at a price mostly fixed years in advance under a supply contract. It earns money by buying steel and aluminium castings, machining them to a tight spec, shipping on schedule, and getting paid in 60-90 days. Because the factories and machines are already built and paid for, every extra rupee of volume above the break-even point drops disproportionately to profit (B04).

## SECTION 3: BUSINESS UNDERSTANDING NARRATIVE

**What the products are and why they matter.** Divgi TorqTransfer designs and manufactures the mechanical driveline hardware that lets a vehicle send power to all four wheels: 4WD/AWD transfer cases, plus transmission components, synchronizer systems, an EV transmission line (E-Gear Drive), and a small legacy automatic-locking-hub line (B04). These parts matter because a carmaker cannot put a vehicle into production without a qualified, tested transfer case or transmission built to its exact specification; the part is engineered into the vehicle's bill of materials years before the vehicle launches, through an OEM qualification (PPAP) process (B04, B07 `B2`).

**Who buys and why.** The customers are vehicle OEMs and global Tier-1 suppliers, not end consumers. Mahindra & Mahindra and Tata Motors are the two dominant, decades-long domestic relationships (50+ years each, B04); Toyota Kirloskar, Force Motors, Ashok Leyland, JCB and MG Motors also buy; BorgWarner is both a 20+ year technology licensor and an export customer for precision-machined components (B04). They buy from Divgi because switching an OEM-qualified driveline supplier mid-platform is costly and slow, and because Divgi's cost base is competitive against global peers for comparable engineering quality (B04 moat table: switching costs, cost advantage).

**Why demand exists.** Demand exists because every 4WD/AWD vehicle sold needs a physical transfer case or equivalent driveline hardware, and Divgi is one of a small number of Indian suppliers qualified to build it at OEM quality standards, including chain-driven and electronic shift-on-the-fly technology the company states it is the sole Indian exporter of (B04). Demand is currently concentrated in a small number of named platforms - Mahindra Scorpio/Thar/XUV700, Tata pickups, and a large one-off Indonesian export order for Tata and Mahindra pickups - rather than broad-based across many customers (B04, B05).

**Why demand grows or does not.** Three separate growth vectors are visible in the corpus, each at a different stage of proof. First, the Indonesia export program is delivering (~30% executed, Q1 FY27 transfer-case revenue +93% YoY, B05) but its organic-versus-program split is unanswered and its unit count has been independently corrected downward (35,000 verified vs the 70,000 management cited, B09). Second, export diversification more broadly is not growing on schedule - export share fell from 18% (FY26) to 16% (Q1 FY27) against a reiterated 20-25% target (B05). Third, the EV transmission (Sigma) and automatic-transmission technology-transfer lines, which represent the highest-value growth optionality, have both stalled: Sigma has slipped SOP three straight quarters and the automatic-transmission "contract by July 2026" milestone was quietly dropped (B05, B07). Structurally, ICE-to-EV substitution is a long-run risk to the core mechanical transfer case itself, which is the reason the company is investing in the EV transmission line in the first place (B04).

**Where the competitive advantage sits, per line.** For the mechanical Transfer Case/Components/Synchronizer/ALH lines, the advantage is switching costs from OEM qualification and tooling sunk cost, plus an India cost position versus global Tier-1s (B04, B07 `B2` scored STRONG). For the EV transmission (E-Gear Drive) line, the claimed advantage is in-house design capability described as unique among Indian suppliers, but this is a narrow-to-medium-durability moat, unproven in scaled production given the SOP slippage (B04, B07). For the export/Tier-1 components line, the advantage is a China+1 sourcing-shift tailwind that the peer set independently corroborates as real and industry-wide, not Divgi-specific (B06: China-to-India desourcing "well corroborated (3/4 peers)," but no peer names Divgi specifically as a beneficiary). No moat is claimed or evidenced for the automatic-transmission opportunity; it remains a non-contracted, evaluation-stage optionality (B07 `optionality_register`).
