## SECTION 1: THE BUSINESS MODEL IN PLAIN ENGLISH

### 1A. One-line description
TAAL Tech rents out engineers by the hour (and occasionally by the project) to
large manufacturers in North America and Europe who need extra design and
engineering capacity but do not want to hire, train, or manage that capacity
themselves.

### 1B. Money flow chain, by revenue stream

**Stream 1: Time & material (T&M) engineering services — 95.5% of FY26
consolidated revenue (AR p.113, Note 24)**
[Client has an engineering workload spike: a new plant, a redesign, a
digitisation push] -> [TAAL Tech assigns named engineers, in Bangalore or at
the client site, against an agreed hourly/monthly rate card] -> [Engineers
deliver plant engineering, aerospace design, product design, BIM/digital-twin,
or embedded/IoT work under the client's direction] -> [Client's engineering or
procurement function pays] -> [Billed monthly against timesheets, in USD/EUR
for the 99.8% of revenue that is exported (AR p.113, Note 42, geographical
segment: Rs19,708.14 lakh outside India of Rs19,742.92 lakh total)].

**Stream 2: Fixed-price, fixed-timeframe contracts — 4.5% of FY26 consolidated
revenue (AR p.113, Note 24: Rs883.91 lakh of Rs19,742.93 lakh)**
[Client defines a bounded scope: a specific design package or deliverable] ->
[TAAL Tech quotes a lump sum and delivers against milestones, revenue
recognised by percentage-of-completion or completion method (AR p.72, Note
2.7)] -> [Deliverable is a drawing set, model, or completed engineering
package, certified by the customer] -> [Client's project office pays] ->
[Billed against milestone certification, not hours].

**Stream 3: Sale of goods — 0.01% of FY26 consolidated revenue (AR p.113,
Note 24: Rs2.40 lakh)**. Immaterial. NOT a business line; treat as rounding.

There is no product line, no licence, no subscription, no platform take-rate.
Every rupee of revenue in Note 24 traces to a person's time or a person's
deliverable (AR p.72 and p.113).

### 1E. The chai-stall-uncle version
Think of TAAL Tech as a very skilled staffing agency, except the "staff" are
aircraft and plant design engineers, not chai-stall helpers. A big foreign
factory owner needs 50 extra engineers for six months to redesign a
production line. Instead of hiring and firing 50 people, the factory owner
calls TAAL Tech, who already has trained engineers sitting in Bangalore. TAAL
Tech sends the engineers, bills by the hour (like a taxi meter, not a fixed
fare), and the factory owner pays in dollars or euros. TAAL Tech keeps the
factory owner happy for ten years so it never has to look for a new customer
each year. The business does not build or sell anything you can touch; it
sells trained people's time, and its only real cost is paying those people.

### 4D. Highest-value questions for management

1. **What offshore-cost ratio, delivery-pyramid structure, or pricing mechanism explains a genuine EBIT margin of approximately 27.8%, roughly double the margin earned by listed ER&D peers of comparable or larger scale?**
   Reassuring answer: a specific, named structural advantage (unique client mix, proprietary tooling, or a delivery model peers do not run) with supporting data. Worrying answer: no clear mechanism, or an answer that relies on one-off contract terms or revenue definitions not comparable to peers.

2. **What is driving the unbilled revenue build (Rs4.64cr to Rs13.75cr) and the fall in cash conversion (CFO/PAT 0.84x to 0.33x), and when does the company expect this to normalise?**
   Reassuring: a specific billing-cycle or milestone-timing explanation tied to the Q4 FY26/Q1 FY27 revenue acceleration, with a stated normalisation quarter. Worrying: no clear cause, or an admission that billing terms have lengthened structurally.

3. **What caused the Q1 FY27 revenue jump to Rs64.81cr (up 41.6% YoY) after three years of roughly 7% CAGR — a new large client, a scope expansion with an existing account, or something else?**
   Reassuring: a named, diversified cause not concentrated in the already-24%-plus top account. Worrying: the jump traces mostly to the top customer, deepening concentration risk.

4. **What is the company's utilisation rate and bench cost, and how does headcount growth track the recent revenue acceleration?**
   Reassuring: utilisation in a normal band with headcount additions ahead of or in line with demand. Worrying: no metric tracked internally, or utilisation falling while revenue accelerates (implying overtime/pricing strain, not capacity growth).

5. **Why does the company hold 85.5% of its Rs143.89cr investment book in unquoted, largely unrated, subordinated NBFC/fintech NCDs and alternative-credit AIFs rather than in more liquid instruments, given the company has no borrowings and no stated capital-deployment plan?**
   Reassuring: a clearly stated treasury policy with credit-risk limits and a rationale tied to yield versus liquidity trade-offs, reviewed by the board. Worrying: no policy exists, or the answer reveals related-party or promoter-linked placement of this capital.
