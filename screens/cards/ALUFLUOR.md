## Business Understanding Narrative

Alufluoride makes aluminium fluoride, AlF3, which smelters add to molten alumina to cut
its melting point. It is "the only Company in Andhra Pradesh producing high purity
Aluminium Fluoride" (AR MD&A p.36). Feedstock is hydrofluosilicic acid, FSA, a waste
by-product two fertiliser majors, Coromandel International and IFFCO Paradeep, supply
under long-term contracts (AR MD&A p.36). Revenue is tonnes times realisation; profit is
the spread over FSA cost, power and logistics.

What is changing is scale and feedstock security, not the model. Capacity rose 12,000 to
16,000 to a stated 18,000 TPA; IFFCO Paradeep now ships over 17,500 TPA against a 7,000
TPA contracted minimum (AR MD&A p.36). FY2026 capital work in progress jumped from Rs
459 lakh to Rs 2,254 lakh, funded partly by a 67% rise in borrowings, to about Rs 37.7
crore (Results FY26Q4 p.8). That is new capex with no named target in this corpus.

Why now: global and Indian aluminium smelting capacity is expanding, pulling AlF3
demand with it (AR MD&A p.36), a cited tailwind, not a self-claim.

What must be true: the new capex must convert to shipped tonnes at a realisation that
holds the spread, FSA supply must keep growing, and the new debt must be serviced from
volume, not from cash, which fell from Rs 166.77 lakh to Rs 38.14 lakh in FY2026
(Results FY26Q4 p.9).

What breaks it: the company withdrew from a 2020 Jordan JV to build a greenfield AlF3
plant, citing "the uncertain political situation in the Gaza area" (AR MD&A p.36). Both
FY2025 and FY2026 carry an exceptional loss for "diminution in value of Company's
investment in its wholly owned foreign subsidiary... considered as 'not temporary in
nature'" (Results FY26Q4 p.1, p.7), a real recurring cost of the abandoned plan.
Q4FY26 revenue, PBT and net profit all fell against Q3FY26 (Results FY26Q4 p.7); whether
that is seasonal or a trend, one filing cannot say.

## 2. Business model and archetype

One business, tonnage-driven: FSA in, AlF3 out, sold to Indian and a few overseas
smelters (AR MD&A p.36). Solar contributed Rs 181.47 lakh of FY2026 revenue against Rs
20,694.93 lakh core, under 1% (Results FY26Q4 p.7). Archetype: commodity converter. Its
scarcity is regional and input-side, the only AP processor at scale, not a brand or
spec moat.

## 3. Competitive advantages

The advantage is captive, contracted feedstock a rival would have to replicate with the
same two fertiliser majors. "Repeat orders due to quality and reliability" is the only
customer-side claim (AR MD&A p.36), soft, not evidence of switching costs. Operating
margin fell from 16.2% to 14% in FY2025, net margin 11.5% to 9.9% (AR MD&A p.37,
13% fall, below the report's own 25% disclosure threshold so unexplained). FY2026's
implied PBT margin recovered to about 16.6% (Results FY26Q4 p.7), suggesting the FY2025
dip was expansion-cost drag, not structural, though no management commentary confirms
this. Management names its own risks: FSA availability, rising Odisha transport cost,
and expansion debt (AR MD&A p.36). Moat: real but narrow, input-side, unquantified for
durability past current contracts.

## 4. The promoters

The Akkineni family: Venkat Akkineni (Managing Director), wife Jyothsana Akkineni
(Executive Director), son Aditya Akkineni (Whole-Time Director and CEO), brother-in-law
Ashok Vemulapalli (Non-Executive, Non-Independent Director) (AR Corp Gov p.38). Disclosed
promoter lines sum to about 59% of equity, unchanged year on year (AR p.140,
bullai-extracts.md). No pledge is stated either way; NOT FOUND is the honest fill.
Related-party disclosure shows remuneration, sitting fees, and dividends on existing
holdings, no loans or guarantees (bullai-extracts.md, Note 5.40). Flag: the Board raised
the MD's remuneration ceiling to Rs 3 crore a year, from Rs 1.57 crore paid in FY2025,
and the CEO son's to Rs 2 crore plus a 1% profit commission (Results FY26Q4 p.1; AR p.42),
near double, against Rs 24.3 crore of FY2026 net profit.

## 5. Financial trajectory

FY2026 standalone: revenue Rs 20,694.93 lakh vs Rs 18,448.61 lakh, up 12.2%. PBT before
exceptional items Rs 3,429.69 lakh vs Rs 2,698.85 lakh, up 27.1%. Net profit Rs 2,429.21
lakh vs Rs 1,831.47 lakh, up 32.6%. EPS Rs 31.06 vs Rs 23.42 (Results FY26Q4 p.7).
Sequentially, Q4FY26 vs Q3FY26: revenue Rs 5,076.29 lakh vs Rs 5,843.11 lakh; PBT Rs
625.08 lakh vs Rs 1,220.02 lakh; net profit Rs 375.76 lakh vs Rs 944.41 lakh (Results
FY26Q4 p.7). The year grew; the latest quarter did not.
Cash conversion is strong: operating cash flow Rs 4,770.73 lakh against net profit Rs
2,429.21 lakh, aided by a receivables release (Results FY26Q4 p.9). Debt rose 67% to fund
a roughly 2.4x step-up in capex, to about Rs 35.5 crore from Rs 14.5 crore (Results
FY26Q4 p.8-p.9); cash fell 77% to Rs 38.14 lakh.
Inflection classification: earnings-led on the full year; the trailing quarter needs
watching before calling it a trend.

## 6. The transition and the quality ladder

Management's claim, quoted once, is about capacity and feedstock, not a value-chain
climb: "enhancing capacity to 18,000 TPA" with FSA "expected to supply up to of 19,000
TPA" (AR MD&A p.36).
FROM R2, cost-advantaged converter: margin from a captive input position, not price or
spec, and Amendment 17 binds.
TO the same rung, larger scale. No spec-in language, no switching-cost claim beyond
"repeat orders," no stated pricing power. The one rung-relevant move is that the company
is "currently exploring the setup of an Alumina Hydrate... manufacturing facility" (AR
MD&A p.36), backward integration into its own input, a real structural change if
sanctioned. It is exploration only, no capex, timeline, or capacity named. This is a
volume-and-security story inside R2, with one un-costed, unsanctioned move toward a
genuine climb.

## 8. Proof check

supply growth are delivered facts, not forward claims.

## 12. Verdict card

**WATCH.** Alufluoride runs a real, narrow input-side moat: the only AP producer of
high-purity AlF3, feeding smelters riding a genuine demand tailwind, with FY2026 revenue
up 12%, profit up 33%, strong cash conversion. But it has not proven a rung climb, only
volume and feedstock security inside its current converter rung. Its one international
diversification, the Jordan JV, was withdrawn and carries a recurring write-down. Its
domestic diversification, Alumina Hydrate, is unsanctioned. The latest quarter softened
on every profit line, and an unquantified capex cycle is already drawing down cash and
raising debt.
Load-bearing facts a deep run must verify first:
1. Target tonnage, cost, and completion date of the expansion behind the FY2026 CWIP
   jump.
2. Whether Q1FY27 and Q2FY27 resume growth or confirm Q4FY26 softening.
3. Carrying value and outlook of the Alufluoride International Pte Ltd Singapore
   subsidiary.
4. Whether Alumina Hydrate moves from exploration to a sanctioned, costed project.
5. Promoter pledge status, confirmed either way.
What would change the view: a sanctioned, costed expansion plan, two quarters resuming
growth, and a sanctioned Alumina Hydrate project would justify PROCEED to `/step1`.
Further sequential softening, or another Jordan-linked write-down, would justify PASS.
No price here.
