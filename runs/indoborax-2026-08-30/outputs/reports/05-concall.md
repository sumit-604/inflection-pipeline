### 2B. Excuse pattern analysis

Only one identifiable "miss" is discussed on the call: the margin
compression from a historical 25%–30% band (per Rahil Desani's question,
p.7) to the current 20%–22%. Management's explanation: "geopolitical
conditions" and raw material inflation (Kalra, p.7; Bharuka, p.4) —
classified **EXTERNAL-BLAME**, though plausible given boron ore is a
globally concentrated, dollar-denominated commodity input. Management
does NOT say "we made a mistake" anywhere in the transcript, and does not
proactively raise the margin-compression topic — it surfaces only because
an analyst (Rahil Desani) pressed it directly, referencing multiple prior
quarters' numbers. Pattern: external-blame on the one topic pressed, no
self-initiated disclosure of weak spots (the pre-exceptional PBT decline,
the boron-oxide capex/CWIP gap, and the affiliate debt/pledge structure
were all left unaddressed until an investor's confused follow-up forced
the debt question onto the table — see 2D and 3C).

### 2D. What they are NOT saying

- **No separation of core vs exceptional PAT.** The Rs 10.15 cr exceptional gain (per company memory/B03) that materially lifts reported FY26 PAT is never named or excluded on the call, even though management repeatedly cites the "18% net profit growth" headline. Likely reason: the core number (per B03, a 3.75% pre-exceptional PBT decline) would contradict the "transformational, strong year" narrative.
- **No R&D department disclosure.** Management states an intent to "spend some more on R&D" (p.13) without acknowledging that B03 finds no R&D department exists in the FY26 AR. Likely reason: naming the current absence would undercut the forward-integration and new-chemistry narrative built across several answers.
- **No quantification of import/FX dependency.** Ore-sourcing de-risking is discussed qualitatively (Turkey/South America/North America) but the current import concentration (55% of FY25 revenue per company memory) and the associated FX exposure are never quantified on the call, even though raw-material risk is one of the most repeated topics (5+ separate analyst questions touch it). Likely reason: a qualitative "de-risking in progress" story reads better than naming how concentrated the exposure still is today.
- **No resolution of the debt/pledge structure.** The one direct question on this (Puran Manglilal Tak, p.19–20) is deflected to an IR email rather than clarified on the call, even though a one-line answer ("that financing sits at the acquirer/HoldCo level, not on Indo Borax's own balance sheet") would have resolved the apparent contradiction cleanly. Likely reason: acquisition-financing detail sits outside what the standalone listed entity's management wants to characterise live, unscripted.
- **No capex rupee figure for boron oxide or debottlenecking**, despite giving a specific production timeline for boron oxide ("3–4 quarters," p.8). Likely reason: per B03, no matching capex/CWIP for the boron oxide facility appears in the FY26 AR — the project may not yet carry a board-approved capital commitment, which would make a firm production date premature to state as confidently as it was stated.

## SECTION 3: COMPETITIVE INTELLIGENCE FROM CONCALLS

### 3A. Competitor commentary

Management names **zero competitors** anywhere in the 27-page transcript,
despite repeatedly asserting "almost 50% market share" of the Indian
boric acid market (Kalra, p.1) and describing the company as a clear
technical-quality leader with the steel industry (p.16). No credibility
check is possible on the market-share figure from this call alone — it is
asserted, not sourced ("as per reports" is used for the market-size/CAGR
claim on p.2, source unnamed). This is itself a gap: a company claiming
half the domestic market gives no view on who holds the other half, or
how concentrated/fragmented that half is.

### 3B. Industry and market intelligence

- Indian boric acid market ~40,000 TPA, projected 8% CAGR to ~53,000 TPA
  by 2030 (unnamed source, "as per reports," Kalra p.2).
- Steel & Refractory segment = 55%–60% of Boric Acid demand (Kalra, p.2).
- Boron ore is geographically concentrated in only three regions —
  Turkey, South America, North America — with historical sourcing skewed
  to Turkey (Kalra/Malhotra, p.12–13).
- No industry pricing index exists for boric acid (unlike LME for
  aluminium); pricing is cost-plus/market-plus-minus, geography-specific,
  with a mix of monthly, quarterly and yearly contracts on both the sales
  and the raw-material purchase side (Kalra, p.22–24).
- India DOT (agri-micronutrient) market ~8,500 tons, "growing," Indo
  Borax ~7%–8% share currently (Kalra, p.19).
- Geopolitical conditions are cited as raising raw material costs
  sector-wide, with management asserting the entire value chain (not just
  Indo Borax) is passing the increase through to end customers (Kalra,
  p.6–7).
