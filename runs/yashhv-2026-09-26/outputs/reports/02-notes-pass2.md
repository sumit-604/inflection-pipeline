## NEW FINDING 1 — FY25 CFO discrepancy between the two Annual Reports is real, undisclosed as a formal reconciliation, and only partially traceable (LBF2 priority item)
- FY25 AR (IGAAP, AS-3 cash flow statement, p.39/[page 39]): Net Cash From Operating Activities FY25 = **₹918.68 Lakh**.
- FY26 AR (Ind AS, restated FY25 comparative, standalone Statement of Cash Flow, p.102/[page 102]): Net Cash from operating activities (A) FY25 = **₹953.13 Lakh**.
- Difference: **+₹34.45 Lakh** (+3.75%). This is NOT a rounding artefact — it traces to a genuine re-presentation, not new cash: "Cash generated from operations" line moved from ₹1,574.51L (IGAAP) to ₹1,608.96L (Ind AS), a +₹34.45L delta, while "Direct taxes paid" is identical (₹655.84L) in both. Above the operating-profit line, "Operating profit before working capital changes" itself moved from ₹3,365.92L to ₹3,402.65L (+₹36.73L), and the working-capital movement lines were reclassified (IGAAP's single "Loans & Advances" and "Non-Current Assets" captions were split into "Other financial assets" and "Other current assets" under Ind AS; "Other Long Term Liabilities" and "Other Current Liabilities" folded into "Other liabilities" and "Other financial liabilities").
- Partial traceability: the Ind AS 116 lease-accounting change (Note 38, p.140, [page 140]) recognises Amortisation Charge on ROU Assets ₹36.49L (FY25, nil under IGAAP) and Finance Charges on Lease Liabilities ₹13.07L (FY25, nil under IGAAP) in place of what was previously a straight rent expense inside "Other Expenses." This is consistent in direction with the Depreciation line moving from ₹271.32L (IGAAP) to ₹307.81L (Ind AS, +₹36.49L — an exact match) and Finance Cost moving from ₹315.26L to ₹322.71L (+₹7.45L, a partial match to the ₹13.07L lease finance charge, the balance likely netting against other finance-cost reclassifications not itemised).
- **Disclosure gap**: Annexure A (Ind AS 101 first-time-adoption disclosure, Notes 1-6, pp.153-157/[pages 153-157]) provides a full balance-sheet and P&L reconciliation but contains **no cash flow statement reconciliation** at all, despite Ind AS 101 encouraging (and the CFO figure itself changing by a non-trivial ₹34.45L). NOT FOUND: any explicit statement anywhere in the FY26 AR that the FY25 CFO figure differs from the one previously reported in the FY25 AR, or by how much, or why.
- 🟡 Watch. The direction and rough order of magnitude are explainable by the disclosed lease and other Ind AS 101 adjustments (i.e., this reads as a genuine presentational effect of first-time adoption, not evidence of manufactured cash), but the absence of a quantified cash flow reconciliation is itself a disclosure-completeness gap on a number (CFO vs PAT) that is central to the operator's cash-conversion test (LBF2). An investor comparing the FY25 AR to the FY26 AR without doing this cross-check would see two different "prior year" CFO numbers with no signposting.

## NEW FINDING 2 — Bank quarterly stock-statement discrepancy is a persistent, three-year, one-directional pattern, not a single-quarter event (escalates Pass 1 finding 7; LBF2 priority item)
Pass 1 flagged only the Mar-26 variance (-₹1,183.95L, -17.76%) as the largest in "the two-year dataset." Note 45 (pp.143-144/[pages 143-144]) in fact tabulates **12 quarters back to Jun-2023**, and the full run reveals a systemic pattern:

| Quarter | Stock variance (books vs bank-reported) | % |
|---|---|---|
| Jun-23 | -₹17.63L | -1.13% |
| Sep-23 | -₹27.80L | -1.48% |
| Dec-23 | -₹119.98L | -5.47% |
| Mar-24 | -₹263.59L | -12.18% |
| Jun-24 | -₹675.83L | -30.01% |
| Sep-24 | -₹597.05L | -20.51% |
| Dec-24 | -₹376.74L | -13.62% |
| Mar-25 | -₹751.94L | -25.35% |
| Jun-25 | -₹114.04L | -4.67% |
| Sep-25 | ₹0 | 0.00% |
| Dec-25 | ₹0 | 0.00% |
| Mar-26 | -₹1,183.95L | -17.76% |

- Every non-zero quarter runs in the **same direction**: books show MORE stock than the amount reported to Axis Bank. The two zero-variance quarters (Sep-25, Dec-25) are the only clean quarters in three years.
- The pattern **recurs and peaks at every fiscal year-end**: Mar-24 (-12.18%), Mar-25 (-25.35%), Mar-26 (-17.76%) are each a local maximum versus the surrounding quarters in their own year (except Jun-24/Sep-24 which run even higher than Mar-24/25, so year-end is not the single worst point, but it is consistently a large negative print every year without exception).
- Of the 12 quarters, only **2** (Jun-24, Mar-25) carry any substantive explanation, both via a single shared footnote: "*The said variance is on account of value of stock in transit & certain other stores & spares related items & valuation of inventories" (p.144/[page 144]). The other 10 non-zero quarters, including the largest-ever Mar-26 print, are labelled simply "Not Material" with **no reason given at all** despite a company-drafted "Reason for Material Discrepancy" column existing specifically for this purpose.
- Trade-debtors variance, by contrast, is near-zero or negative in 9 of 12 quarters, but flips to a **positive** variance (bank-reported EXCEEDS books) uniquely in Sep-24 (+₹256.38L, +14.30%) and Dec-24 (+₹257.24L, +10.28%) — the opposite skew from every other debtor quarter and from the stock pattern throughout, and likewise labelled "Not Material" with no explanation.
- 🔴 Red Flag, upgraded from Pass 1's 🟡 Watch. A three-year, near-universally one-directional discrepancy between book stock and bank-reported stock, growing to double-digit percentages and low-single-digit-crore absolute values, self-certified "Not Material" in 10 of 12 instances with no quantified reason, is a control-design and disclosure-appetite question distinct from — but adjacent to — the cyber-fraud control-design finding already in Pass 1 (rank 3). It directly bears on LBF2: if book inventory has been running structurally ahead of what is reported (and implicitly drawn against) at the secured lender, the true drawing-power-backed liquidity position may be tighter than the balance sheet alone suggests.

## NEW FINDING 3 — Ind AS 101 P&L reconciliation's two largest line items are not individually itemised against the six named drivers (LBF3 priority item)
- The FY25 restated P&L reconciliation (Annexure A, p.155/[page 155]) shows Revenue From Operations adjusted by **-₹56.74L** and Other Expenses adjusted by **-₹93.62L** — together the two largest single-line swings in the entire reconciliation, larger than the Employee Benefit (-₹17.03L), Finance Cost (+₹7.45L), or Depreciation (+₹36.49L) adjustments that ARE traceable to named standards.
- The six qualitative drivers disclosed immediately after (Notes 1-6, pp.156-157/[pages 156-157]) describe ECL (Ind AS 109), employee benefits/OCI (Ind AS 19), security deposit fair valuation and ROU/lease (Ind AS 109/116), deferred tax (Ind AS 12), and forward contract fair valuation (Ind AS 109) — none of these, by their own conceptual description, obviously explains a **downward** revenue restatement of ₹56.74L or a **downward** Other Expenses restatement of ₹93.62L of this magnitude.
- Partial cross-reference is possible: Note 38 (lease disclosure, p.140/[page 140]) shows the straight-line rent expense that would previously have sat inside "Other Expenses" was replaced by ROU amortisation (₹36.49L) and lease finance cost (₹13.07L) — together ₹49.56L, covering roughly half of the ₹93.62L Other Expenses reduction. The remaining ~₹44L of the Other Expenses adjustment, and the entire ₹56.74L revenue adjustment, have **no disclosed rupee-level driver** anywhere in the FY26 AR notes.
- NOT FOUND: a line-item bridge (of the type Ind AS 101 reconciliations commonly provide) tying each of the six named drivers to each affected P&L caption in rupee terms; the AR gives only the aggregate opening/closing reserve bridge (which nets everything to -₹6.93L / -₹15.40L) and the top-line P&L reconciliation table, with no line connecting the two.
- 🟡 Watch. Nothing here contradicts the "clean, standard first-time-adoption" read from Pass 1 (finding 4) — the total reserve effect is small and the six drivers are all legitimate, disclosed Ind AS mechanisms. But the specific claim that this is "fully explained" should be softened: the two largest P&L reconciling lines are not individually reconciled to a named driver in the document, which is a genuine disclosure-completeness gap for a first-time Ind AS filer to leave unresolved.

## NEW FINDING 4 — Forward contract derivative recognised at Ind AS transition has no traceable presence in the FY25 or FY26 fair-value or risk-management notes
- Annexure A driver 6 (Ind AS 109: Forward Contract Liability, p.157/[page 157]) states the Company "recognised the fair value of outstanding forward exchange contracts, resulting in recognition of a derivative liability" at the 1-Apr-2024 transition date, quantified in the reserve bridge as a ₹4.53L (1-Apr-24) / ₹2.48L (31-Mar-25) charge.
- However, Note 44 (Fair Value Measurements, pp.142-143/[pages 142-143]), which tabulates every financial asset and liability category with a Level 1/2/3 fair-value split for FY26, FY25, and 1-Apr-24, shows **no "Derivative" line item at all** in any of the three columns — only Trade Receivables, Cash, Bank Balances, Other Financial Assets/Investments on the asset side, and Borrowings, Other Financial Liabilities, Trade Payables, and Leases on the liability side.
- Note 46(C) (Market Risk / Foreign Currency Risk, pp.144-146/[pages 144-146]) discloses trade receivable and trade payable currency exposure (USD, EUR, CHF, AUD) and a 5% sensitivity table for FY26 and FY25, but states the Company "uses foreign exchange forward contracts to mitigate the risk" **in the general policy sentence only** — no outstanding forward contract notional, fair value, or maturity is disclosed for either FY25 or FY26.
- NOT FOUND: any outstanding forward/derivative position disclosed for FY25 or FY26, despite (a) the company stating in its own risk-management policy that it uses forward contracts, and (b) foreign currency trade receivables growing sharply (USD receivable ₹77.89L FY25 → ₹638.09L FY26, +719%) alongside the export ramp (Pass 1 finding, Note 26).
- 🟡 Watch. Either the forward contract recognised at transition has since matured/unwound with no new contracts taken out (plausible, and not inherently a red flag), or an active hedging position exists but was not disclosed in Note 44/46(C) as it should be. The net effect either way is a rising, apparently unhedged USD receivable exposure feeding into a swing in FX sensitivity: the 5% INR/USD sensitivity impact moved from -₹8.37L (FY25, net payer position) to +₹29.99L (FY26, net receiver position) — a reversal in net USD exposure direction driven by the export ramp, with no evidence of an offsetting hedge in place.
