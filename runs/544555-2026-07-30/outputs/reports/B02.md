## PASS 3: PATTERN-LEVEL FINDINGS

### Pattern 1 — Unexplained/unusual items cluster disproportionately in FY23 and the FY22 opening balance
Cross-referencing Pass 1 and Pass 2 findings by fiscal year shows six distinct unexplained or unusual items anchor specifically to FY23 or the April 1, 2022 opening balance sheet, versus materially fewer such items in FY24 or FY25:
1. ₹571.23 Lakh "Sale of Fixed Assets" in the FY23 Restated Cash Flow Statement is in substance a non-cash CWIP-to-Building capitalisation (Annexure III, p.159; Annexure G, p.177 — Pass 2 Finding 5).
2. ₹63.57 Lakh "Fixed Assets Written Off" in FY23 (18% of that year's PAT) has no matching deduction anywhere in the PP&E rollforward (Annexure W, p.185; Annexure G, p.177 — Pass 2 Finding 8).
3. A constant ₹(12.77) Lakh "cumulative adjustment to opening reserves as on 01-04-2022," the only restatement line in Annexure V with no narrative cause given, dated before the earliest restated year (Annexure V, p.165 — Pass 2 Finding 7).
4. Rent of ₹12.00 Lakhs paid directly to CMD Mufaddal Najmuddin Deesawala in FY23 only, a stream that stops exactly as the ABJ Rub Metalico rent stream of ₹16.50L begins in FY24 — property never reconciled to the Immovable Properties table in either case (Annexure Y §B, p.187 — Pass 2 Finding 1).
5. ₹23.00 Lakhs of "voluntary donations" in FY23 (6.6% of that year's PAT) with no named recipient or purpose, against ₹11.95 Lakhs in FY24 and nil in FY25 (Annexure W, p.185 — Pass 2 Finding 11).
6. The outgoing auditor M/s Sharma & Sastry signed the FY23 audit report on September 5, 2023 and resigned seven days later, September 12, 2023, citing "paucity of time" (Auditor's Examination Report, p.153-154; General Information, p.56 — Pass 2 Finding 9).
🟡 Watch (synthesis) — no single item is individually disqualifying, and none contradicts Pass 1's separate finding that no audit qualifications carried forward. But the concentration of six independent, unexplained anomalies in one year (the earliest restated year, and the year of the auditor transition) rather than an even spread across three years is itself a pattern worth naming: it is consistent with either weaker record-keeping/controls in the pre-restatement period, or a restatement process that surfaced more corrections the further back it reached. Investors should weight FY23 figures with more caution than FY24/FY25 figures for this reason alone.

### Pattern 2 — The three working-capital notes, read together, show a cash conversion cycle that roughly tripled in two years
Pass 1 computed receivable days, inventory days, and payable days separately in three different sections (4, 5, 8) without combining them. Read together (all Pass 1 computations, using Annexures K/L/E/R, cost of material consumed as denominator):

| | FY23 | FY24 | FY25 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Receivable days | 47.9 | 64.8 | 65.9 |
| Inventory days | 150.5 | 214.6 | 272.5 |
| Payable days | 149.0 | 142.5 | 172.5 |
| **Cash conversion cycle (Rec + Inv − Pay)** | **≈49.4** | **≈136.9** | **≈165.9** |

🔴 Red Flag (synthesis, elevated from the individual 🟡/🔴 component findings) — the combined cash conversion cycle roughly tripled from FY23 to FY25. This is the clearest single number in the entire notes section demonstrating that working-capital efficiency deteriorated structurally rather than cyclically, and it independently explains why the same two years (FY24-FY25) also show the promoter short-term loans (Section 2 of Pass 1), the broadened high-cost NBFC borrowing (Aditya Birla Finance, Profectus Capital at 10.75%-14.51%), and the stretched MSME payable book all appearing together. No single note discloses the combined metric; it is only visible by reading inventory, receivables and payables notes side by side.

### Pattern 3 — Compliance gaps span four distinct regulatory domains in the same three-year window, not one isolated lapse
Cross-referencing across both passes: (i) company law — nine consecutive years of unfiled cash flow statements, regularised via NCLT compounding filed the same day as the RHP (Pass 1, Section 12); (ii) FEMA — unpaid share subscription and admitted ODI-filing gap for the US subsidiary, with disclosed RBI compounding-penalty exposure (Pass 1, Section 6); (iii) labour law — contractor worker expense growth of 5.7x over two years against a falling EPF/ESI-registered headcount (Pass 2, Finding 4); (iv) MSMED Act — self-admitted failure to provide interest on delayed MSME payments (Pass 1, Sections 1 and 8). A fifth, disclosure-integrity item sits alongside these: the Group Company section's conclusion contradicts its own stated related-party test for ABJ Rub Metalico (Pass 2, Finding 2).
🔴 Red Flag (synthesis) — taken individually, each item might be dismissed as a discrete, correctable lapse typical of a first-time-IPO SME. Taken together, spanning four separate statutory domains plus a disclosure-drafting contradiction, all unresolved as of the RHP date, the pattern reads as a systemic compliance-function gap rather than isolated incidents, which is directly relevant to the "disclosure transparency" and "consistency" dimensions of the accounting-quality score below.

### D. FIVE QUESTIONS FOR MANAGEMENT

2. Who is the individual or entity that owns or controls ABJ Rub Metalico, what is the arm's-length pricing policy for the simultaneous sales, purchases and rent transacted with it, and why is the leased premises not identified in the Immovable Properties table?
3. Why does the "Our Group Company" section conclude there are no group companies when ABJ Rub Metalico appears to meet the RHP's own stated related-party-transaction criterion for that classification?
4. What explains the 5.7x increase in contractor worker expenses over two years alongside a decline in EPF/ESI-registered employee headcount from 81/74 to 39/29, and has the company assessed any Contract Labour Act reclassification risk?
5. What is the current status of the FEMA Form ODI filing and the unpaid share subscription for Ameenji Rubber Inc., and what is the expected timeline and quantum of any RBI compounding penalty?

### E. NOTES-BASED RED FLAGS

- **Aggressive/unusual accounting presentation:** the FY23 ₹571.23L non-cash CWIP-to-Building capitalisation presented as a cash "Sale of Fixed Assets" in the Restated Cash Flow Statement; the finished-goods/WIP build-up with zero write-downs despite a near-halving of inventory turnover.
- **Undisclosed/under-disclosed risk indicators:** self-admitted MSMED Act interest non-provision; the FEMA/ODI gap for the US subsidiary; the contract-labour-vs-EPF/ESI headcount divergence; the Group Company section's internal contradiction; the unnamed controlling owner of ABJ Rub Metalico; the unexplained constant ₹12.77L opening-reserves adjustment; the untraceable FY23 ₹63.57L fixed-asset write-off.

### F. ONE-LINE NOTES VERDICT

The notes reveal concerning accounting practices. Key concern: an unexplained working-capital cash drain (inventory turnover collapsed from 2.99x to 1.29x, cash conversion cycle roughly tripled to ~166 days) compounded by unresolved related-party, MSMED, FEMA and labour-classification compliance gaps. Key strength: broad, granular disclosure in the RPT, borrowings and actuarial-assumption notes, with the company self-admitting several of its own compliance gaps rather than concealing them. Overall accounting quality: 4/10.
