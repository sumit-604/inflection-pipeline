# Stage 6: Peer Concall Verification — Apex Ecotech Ltd (APEXECO)
Run date: 2026-07-10 | Model: claude-sonnet-5 | Stage: B06-peers

## Scope note on peer set

B05 suggested Ion Exchange, Thermax, Va Tech Wabag and Concord as comparison names. Those are not
the peer set with transcripts available to this pipeline. The actual evidence base is four
water/environmental EPC companies, 16 transcripts total (four quarterly calls each, roughly
Q1 FY26 through Q4 FY26, Aug/Sep 2025 through May/June 2026):

- **CEWATER** (Concord Enviro Systems Ltd) — the closest match to the B05-suggested "Concord."
  Core ZLD/water-wastewater EPC, O&M, some CBG and renewables diversification, Sharjah/Mexico/Africa
  export exposure.
- **EIEL** (Enviro Infra Engineers Ltd) — water/wastewater EPC (AMRUT, Namami Gange, JJM, CETP),
  aggressively diversifying into solar/wind/BESS via subsidiaries in FY26.
- **EMSLIMITED** (EMS Ltd) — sewerage/water supply EPC, largely North Indian government contracts
  (UP, MP, Rajasthan, Uttarakhand, West Bengal), the most execution-troubled of the four in FY26.
- **FELIX** (Felix Industries Ltd) — the most adjacent of the four: core water/wastewater EPC and
  O&M business (~60-75% of revenue depending on quarter) plus a growing hydrocarbon/oil-waste
  recycling business in Oman and new plastic/metal recycling and acid-reclamation verticals. SME
  Emerge listed through FY26, migrating to main board.

Weighting: CEWATER, EIEL and EMSLIMITED are read as close-to-identical segment peers for water/EPC
claims. FELIX is weighted as adjacent — its water segment evidence is used at full weight, but its
oil/metal/plastic commentary is flagged as segment-adjacent, not water-sector-specific, where used.

### Claim 2 — 25-40% raw material (metal) cost inflation in H2 FY26; industry-wide or company-specific; handling on fixed-price contracts

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Claim | Did peer water-treatment EPC players also see 25-40% raw material (metal) cost inflation in H2 FY26, and how did they handle pricing on fixed-price contracts signed before the spike? |
| Verdict | **PARTIALLY VERIFIED** (direction only; magnitude and timing do not match) |
| Peer evidence | Two of four peers corroborate rising input costs, but later and smaller than the claim. CEWATER: "we are already seeing some of our raw material prices going up. We have had to move a lot of freight to air freight to support projects. So, there is some cost increase that we are seeing this year" — stated in the **Q4 FY26 call (May 2026)** discussing FY27 guidance, i.e., the pressure is described as emerging at the FY26/FY27 boundary, not squarely in H2 FY26 (Oct 2025-Mar 2026). EIEL: "Given that we do have a global crisis, I am reducing my EBITDA guidance to somewhere around 21% to 22%, which was earlier 22% to 24%" and "the pricing of the commodities or the raw materials which are we are procuring, that has gone high to an extent" — again in the **Q4 FY26 call (May 2026)**, framed as a 1-2 percentage point EBITDA impact, not a 25-40% raw-material cost move (EIEL, Q4 FY26 call, May 2026, Manish Jain). EIEL separately deferred lithium-ion battery cell procurement for its new BESS orders "so the prices get settled down" (EIEL, Q4 FY26 call, May 2026) — a distinct, narrower input (imported battery cells, not general steel/metal) also cost-affected by the same geopolitical event. |
| Peers silent | FELIX and EMSLIMITED show no evidence of raw-material/metal cost inflation as a driver of their FY26 results across all eight of their combined calls. | Net read | Direction (input costs rising) is corroborated by 2 of 4 peers, but magnitude (peers imply low-single-digit EBITDA impact, not 25-40% raw-material inflation) and timing (peers place the pressure at Q4 FY26/into FY27, not H2 FY26) both diverge materially from the claim as framed. The two corroborating peers tie the cost pressure to a specific geopolitical/shipping disruption (Sharjah, Middle East, lithium-ion imports) rather than a broad metals-market move — this looks like a narrower, later, and smaller effect than what the main company's H2 FY26 framing implies. On fixed-price contract handling: FELIX's Nishant Sharma states peer practice directly — "In maximum of the contracts, it is a price variation clause which is applicable. So a part of the cost increase will definitely get offset with the price variation clause. However, it does not take into account the entire price increase in the market. So to that extent, we are required to lower our guidance" (FELIX, Q2 FY26 call, Nov 2025). This is useful, directly on-point evidence of standard peer practice (partial pass-through via price variation clauses, margin absorbs the rest) even though it does not validate the 25-40% magnitude claim. |

## PART 2: Unprompted cross-read

### 2A. Demand environment

A consistent, sector-wide pattern across three of the four peers (CEWATER, EIEL, EMSLIMITED): strong
headline demand/TAM commentary early in FY26, followed by successive guidance cuts through the year,
driven overwhelmingly by **execution and cash-timing** issues rather than demand destruction:

- **CEWATER**: FY26 guidance cut from 18-20% (Q1, Aug 2025) → 12-15% (Q2, Nov 2025, after an Africa
  project delay and a high Q2 FY25 export base) → ~2% (Q3, Feb 2026, Kenya project delay, CBG
  financial-closure delays, SAP re-implementation) → actual FY26 revenue **down 6.2% YoY** to ₹557.8
  crore (Q4/FY26, May 2026).
- **EIEL**: FY26 guidance held at 35-40% through Q1-Q2 (Aug/Nov 2025), then materially missed by
  Q3-Q4 (Feb/May 2026) on order-book-to-revenue conversion delays, Delhi tender re-bidding, Bihar
  evaluation delays, and unbilled revenue stretching to 195 days.
- **EMSLIMITED**: the most severe pattern — Q2 FY26 revenue down 26-27% YoY on monsoon halting
  underground digging across six states (Nov 2025 call); Q3 further hit by Uttarakhand floods (Feb
  2026 call); full-year FY26 standalone revenue fell ~37% short of the ₹1,200-1,300 crore guided,
  landing at ₹608 crore, with management citing West Bengal state elections halting road-digging
  work and a new central-state government payment-reconciliation portal ("SPARSH") delaying fund
  releases (Q4/FY26, May 2026).
- **FELIX** is the outlier with genuinely strong demand execution through most of FY26 (consolidated
  revenue +178% YoY to ₹102.21 crore, EBITDA +131%, PAT +100% for FY26 — Q4/FY26 call, June 2026),
  but even FELIX flags margin and liquidity headwinds by Q4 FY26/Q1 FY27 tied to the same
  geopolitical/Oman disruption theme as CEWATER and EIEL.

Read against the main company's own narrative: none of the peers describe a demand boom that the
main company's framing would need to be consistent with, but three of four peers independently
confirm that government-payment-timing risk, weather disruption and election-driven work stoppages
are chronic, sector-wide execution risks — not unique to any one company, including the main company.

### 2B. Pricing and input costs

See Claim 2 above. Net: rising input costs are real for 2 of 4 peers but smaller and later than the
main company's H2 FY26 framing implies, and tied to a specific Middle East geopolitical/shipping
disruption rather than a broad metals-market move. FELIX and EMSLIMITED show zero evidence of input
cost inflation driving their results.

### 2C. Capex cycle — industry-wide race, not a lone expander

This is **not** a lone-expander-in-a-cautious-peer-set situation. Three of the four peers
(CEWATER, EIEL, FELIX) are all expanding aggressively into adjacent verticals simultaneously in
FY26, funded by a mix of debt, equity and internal accruals:

- **CEWATER**: HAM project debt build-up (Mathura, Saharanpur, ~₹150-165 crore of additional term
  loans expected in FY27), two US minority equity investments in polymer/membrane technology firms
  ($2 million each), Nala Membranes solar-asset acquisition.
- **EIEL**: the most aggressive pivot — acquired a wind EPC company (Suyog Urja Ltd), secured
  ~₹1,070 crore of NTPC BESS EPC orders, added a 150 MWh BESS acquisition in Bihar, built a >250-person
  dedicated renewables team, and grew total order book to >₹6,814 crore with the renewable segment
  alone at ~₹3,130 crore (Q4 FY26 call, May 2026).
- **FELIX**: Oman Phase 2 oil-processing capacity expansion, acquisition of a metal-recycling unit
  (Mehsana), in-process acquisition of plastic-recycling capacity (three existing licensed units in
  Ahmedabad/Baroda/Surat), and a new acid-reclamation technology pilot.
- **EMSLIMITED** is the conservative counter-example: explicit "we do not want to take any debt"
  philosophy, debt-free by choice, only organic growth, no further capex planned beyond a paper-company
  acquisition already completed (Q1 FY26 call, Sept 2025).

Net: the sector's scaled EPC players are in a genuine capacity/diversification race into adjacent
recurring-revenue verticals (renewables, oil/metal/plastic recycling) — this is industry-wide
behavior among three of four peers, not an isolated bet by any single company.

### 2D. Competitive mentions of the main company

None. No peer transcript across all 16 calls mentions Apex Ecotech, APEXECO, or any unmistakable
description of the main company by name or product. This absence is noted but not over-interpreted —
these are four independent management teams answering their own analyst questions, with no prompt to
discuss competitors by name.

### 2E. Risks peers discuss that the main company's own materials may not

- **Government payment-system risk as structural, not one-off**: EMSLIMITED flags a newly introduced
  central-state fund-reconciliation portal ("SPARSH") as an active cause of Q4 FY26 payment delays
  (Q4/FY26 call, May 2026) — a systemic mechanism risk, not company-specific mismanagement.
- **Election-driven work stoppages**: EMSLIMITED's West Bengal state election halted road-digging
  work for a multi-month stretch (Q4/FY26 call, May 2026) — a recurring, predictable political-cycle
  risk for underground-infrastructure EPC work that a water-tech company like the main company should
  factor into execution planning around any state election calendar.
- **Cyber/impersonation fraud**: EIEL disclosed an ₹11.15 crore cyber fraud loss (impersonation of the
  MD instructing fund transfers), with only partial recovery (EIEL, Q1 FY26 call, Aug 2025) — a
  governance/internal-controls risk relevant to any EPC company handling large milestone payments.
  Promoters voluntarily forewent remuneration until the loss was recovered.
- **Promoter share pledging as a recurring governance flag**: EMSLIMITED's promoter pledge rose from
  ~7% to ~28% across FY26 despite repeated management assurances of near-term reduction, prompting
  direct investor pushback ("it does not take much to be transparent... give a realistic projection
  henceforth" — EMSLIMITED, Q4 FY26 call, May 2026) — a governance-credibility pattern worth watching
  for in the main company's own promoter-holding disclosures.
- **Unbilled revenue / working capital stretch from milestone billing**: EIEL's unbilled-revenue days
  stretched to 195 by Q4 FY26 (from a target of ~90), driven by government invoicing/TDS-matching
  requirements introduced via a GST circular (EIEL, Q4 FY26 call, May 2026) — a sector-wide cash
  conversion risk tied to how government milestone billing works, independent of any one company's
  collections discipline.
- **Persistent competitive fragmentation**: EMSLIMITED's own description of "8, 9, 10 companies of
  our size" in the unlisted tier (Q2 FY26 call, Nov 2025) is a reminder that bid-win-rate assumptions
  (EMSLIMITED cites ~15% historically, rising ambition to ~20%) remain competitive and are not
  guaranteed to improve just because a company scales.

## PART 4: Triangulation summary

- **Claims verified**: 0 of 5
- **Claims partially verified**: 3 of 5 (raw material cost inflation direction + fixed-price contract
  handling; market consolidation direction, weakly; SME voluntary quarterly disclosure, single-peer)
- **Claims unverifiable**: 2 of 5 (ZLD/water-recycling penetration percentage; customer revenue
  concentration and peer disclosure norms)
- **Claims contradicted**: 0 of 5, though see note below — the cost-inflation claim's magnitude and
  timing diverge enough from peer evidence that it functions as a near-contradiction on specifics even
  though direction is corroborated.

**Single most consequential finding for synthesis**: the raw-material-cost claim (25-40% in H2 FY26)
is not supported at that magnitude or in that window by any peer. The two peers that do report input
cost pressure (CEWATER, EIEL) place it later (Q4 FY26/Q1 FY27) and smaller (1-2 percentage points of
EBITDA), and tie it to a specific Middle East geopolitical/shipping disruption rather than a general
metals move. This should be flagged to synthesis as a claim requiring correction or much tighter
sourcing, not just a caveat.

**Strongest independent confirmation**: FELIX's direct, on-point confirmation that an SME-listed peer
voluntarily moved from the SEBI six-month minimum to quarterly reporting for transparency reasons —
clean, unambiguous, and the only claim in this batch with a peer quote that maps almost one-to-one
onto the question asked.

**Overall assessment — supports, complicates, or undercuts**: The peer set **complicates** rather than
cleanly supports or undercuts the main company's narrative. On disclosure and pricing mechanics
(Claims 2 and 5), peers provide real but partial, narrower-than-claimed corroboration. On TAM/
penetration and customer concentration (Claims 1 and 3), peers are silent, which neither helps nor
hurts the main company's claims but means those numbers remain unverified outside the company's own
disclosure. On demand and execution more broadly (Part 2), three of four peers independently show that
government-payment-timing risk, weather/election disruption, and stretched working capital are
chronic, sector-wide conditions — this contextualizes rather than excuses any of the main company's own
execution issues, and it means the main company's execution track record should be benchmarked against
a sector baseline that is itself quite volatile, not against an assumed-smooth peer group.
