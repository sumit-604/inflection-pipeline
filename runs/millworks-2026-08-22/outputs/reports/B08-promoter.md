## SECTION 3: BUSINESS CONDUCT & ETHICAL TRACK RECORD

### 3A. Related-party transaction history — the central governance issue in this file

**V3 Technologies (promoter partnership, RPT circularity).** Per the RPT summary (RHP, p.56–57) and
financial-indebtedness annexures (RHP, p.190–194, p.222):

- **Landlord**: company pays rent to V3 Technologies for Unit-1 (also the registered office); rent
  expense ₹155.76L (FY26) vs ₹99.12L implied prior year.
- **Vendor**: "Purchase of Materials" ₹47.20L (FY26); "Purchase of Property, Plant and Equipment"
  ₹707.74L (FY25) — machinery transferred from V3 Technologies and **settled via a preferential
  equity allotment of 3,723 shares to the four promoters "in lieu of machinery transferred"**
  (RHP, capital structure section, line ~6017), i.e., a non-cash, related-party asset-for-equity swap.
- **Customer**: "Sale of Goods" ₹204.20L (FY25) — V3 Technologies also buys from the company in the
  same period it sells to it.
- **Lender/borrower**: unsecured loan taken from V3 Technologies ₹430.45L (FY24), ₹190.79L
  outstanding at FY24 close, repaid by FY25.
- **Advances**: company gave V3 Technologies advances of ₹503.58L (FY26) and ₹556.58L (FY25), with
  ₹490.00L and ₹316.73L outstanding receivable respectively — a materially large, recurring advance
  balance to a related partnership.
- **Security deposit**: ₹140.00L given to V3 Technologies (FY26).
- **Corporate guarantee**: V3 Technologies has corporate-guaranteed several of the company's secured
  bank facilities (Axis Bank cash credit and term loans), and V3's own Peenya industrial property
  (jointly owned with Rashmi Sridhar Acharya) is pledged as collateral alongside the promoters'
  personal residence, under a cross-collateral group exposure ceiling of ₹1,261L (RHP, p.221–224).
- **Common pursuits**: the RHP itself discloses V3 Technologies as engaged in the same line of
  business as the company (RHP, p.179 "Common Pursuits" table) — a live competing-entity disclosure,
  mitigated only by a non-compete/non-solicit agreement between the company and V3 Technologies
  (the sole "material agreement" disclosed in the RHP, RHP, p.162).

This is landlord + vendor + customer + lender + advance-recipient + guarantor + potential competitor,
all in one related partnership, in the same two fiscal years. Individually, each line item is
disclosed and modest relative to company scale; taken together the pattern is a promoter entity that
is financially and operationally interwoven with the listed company across nearly every transaction
type available. 🔴

**Quick Pay Private Limited (quasi-related party, undisclosed under AS-18).** Quick Pay is the
company's largest customer at **47.02% of FY26 revenue (₹6,992.76L of ~₹14,873L total)**, up sharply
from 33.51% (FY25) and 54.09% (FY24) (RHP, p.28–29). The company separately made a **₹575.06L
("₹5.75 crore") strategic equity investment in Quick Pay**, converting an unsecured loan into equity,
explicitly to enter the drone-component supply chain: Millworks manufactures components, ships them to
Quick Pay, and Quick Pay integrates/markets/sells finished drones (RHP, p.120, 187–188 area; corroborated
📰 MEDIA — insightsbybeatthestreet.substack.com via search snippet, 2026-08-22, full article not
independently fetchable — egress-blocked). The RHP states: "there are some common shareholders of the
Company and Quick Pay Private Limited," while simultaneously asserting "none of the Directors,
Promoters, members of the Promoter Group, Shareholders, or the Issuer Company is, in any manner
whatsoever, related to Quick Pay Private Limited, except for the investment made" (RHP, risk factors,
p.25). This is a company that is simultaneously the issuer's top customer (47%), a captive downstream
integrator in a supply chain the issuer built specifically to serve it, and a company the issuer holds
an equity stake in and has common shareholders with — yet it is formally kept outside AS-18
related-party disclosure. 🔴 This is a materially significant "quasi-related party" structuring
question: the economic substance (concentration + investment + captive supply chain + common
ownership) resembles a related party far more than the accounting classification suggests.
Web search could not independently verify Quick Pay Private Limited's precise corporate identity —
the only "Quick Pay Private Limited" found via public company search is Delhi-registered
(CIN U74140DL2015PTC288488, ₹5L paid-up capital), whose product catalogue does include a drone line
(Qfly series) but whose registered address and capital base do not obviously match a company capable
of absorbing 47% of a ₹149cr-revenue supplier's output. ❓ **NOT FOUND / unverified — flagged as an
identity-and-substance gap that a fresh filing search (MCA/Zauba by exact CIN, if disclosed in a later
filing) should close.**

**Big Bang Boom Solutions Private Limited (BBBS) — independently verified, credible, not
promoter-related.** BBBS is a real, well-capitalized defence-tech company that won a ₹200+ crore
Indian Air Force / Indian Army contract for its "Vajra Sentinel" anti-drone system (📰 MEDIA VERIFIED
— Outlook Business, SP's Land Forces, 2026-08-22). The company depends on BBBS as a sub-assembly
partner for defence projects (assembly occurs at BBBS's facility under a "Bill-to-Ship-to"
arrangement, RHP p.25). This is supply-chain execution risk, not a governance or self-dealing risk —
BBBS is not promoter-related. **Clean, positive counter-finding.**
