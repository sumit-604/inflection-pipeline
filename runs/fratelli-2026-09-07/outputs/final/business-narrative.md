# FRATELLI VINEYARDS LTD (FRATELLI) — BUSINESS NARRATIVE
Run: fratelli-2026-09-07 | Phase 1 (evidence stages only) | Written 2026-09-08

## BUSINESS UNDERSTANDING NARRATIVE

Fratelli grows grapes and turns them into wine. It farms about 400 owned acres and about 1,000 contract farmed acres at Akluj and Jambhali in Maharashtra, ferments and ages the fruit at a winery holding 5.4 million litres, and bottles more than 50 labels across luxury, super premium, premium and value tiers. Bottled still wine is about 90 percent of revenue, and the luxury names Sette and J'NOON carry the brand claim, with 250 awards and five Decanter scores above 90 points behind them. The second product is Shotgun, a canned ready to drink alcoholic drink launched in February 2025, made on the same winery infrastructure and moved by the same field sales team, which with TiLT wine in a can is about 10 percent of revenue. No customer has to buy either product, so demand rests on preference and price, not on need; the honest version of "why the customer cannot do without it" is that the customer can, and wine is under 1 percent of India's alcohol market by volume.

The buyers are private distributors and retailers across 29 states and union territories, government beverage corporations in the states that run a corporation model, hotel and restaurant venues, 29 Canteen Stores Department canteens that supplied about 8 percent of the June 2026 quarter topline, and export buyers in 15 to 17 countries who took about 1.7 percent of FY26 revenue. Buying behaviour is thin on lock in: no long term customer contract is disclosed, no reorder rate or active outlet metric is disclosed against the 31,000 touch point count, and the largest single customer fell from 17.3 percent to 11.6 percent of group revenue in one year. A substantial portion of receivables sits with government corporations that set their own payment cadence, which is one reason collection runs at 212 days.

Demand today comes from premiumisation among urban affluent drinkers and from the fast start of the canned format, and it is gated by rules rather than by taste. The run named six downstream signals that carry that demand: state excise duty and licensing notifications, which control market access and shelf price; CSD empanelment and procurement, the one assured demand channel; the India EU FTA wine tariff schedule; the USD to INR rate; the Nashik and Akluj table grape price index; and the quarterly disclosures of Sula Vineyards and Grover Zampa, the only independent read on share. Each of the six is externally checkable, in state excise gazettes, Ministry of Defence procurement data, DGFT notifications, the RBI reference rate, and APMC Nashik price bulletins.

Whether demand should grow is contested inside this run's own evidence. Management and the annual report cite a wine market of roughly Rs 1,900 cr to Rs 2,300 cr growing near 16 percent a year to the early 2030s, and the independent market data corroborates the size. The market leader told its own investors in August 2026 that the category has shown low single digit growth at best over two years and that its own revenue has been almost flat near Rs 600 cr for three to four years. The pipeline used the realised rate, about 4 percent, not the projected 16 percent, and the India EU FTA cuts the other way by pulling landed import prices toward Rs 2,000, which is the exact boundary Fratelli uses to define the luxury tier it calls its fastest growing engine.

The competitive advantage sits, to the extent it sits anywhere, in the still wine line and not in the canned line. Still wine has the two best evidenced candidates: 15 years of proprietary grape clone work, imported varietals grafted onto Indian rootstock that the company itself calls hard to copy in the near to medium term, and a distribution network of 31,000 touch points across 29 states. The canned line has no moat and should be said so plainly: Shotgun needs no vineyard, and any beverage maker with a canning line and a distributor can enter. The forward moat scan scored 13 out of a possible far higher total and classified the position MODEST, with three moderate categories, product platform reuse, backward integration into grapes, and CSD qualification access, and its two newest categories, tested on a two leg standard and on a named sacrifice standard, both scored zero. Gate 0 scored the moat at NONE, and the wine segment result went from a profit of Rs 19 cr in FY24 to losses of Rs 10.5 cr in FY25 and Rs 10.6 cr in FY26, so none of the three moderate categories has yet converted into segment profit.

## Wine brand claims outrun the audited numbers

Fratelli sells premium Indian wine and, since February 2025, a canned drink called Shotgun. The wine comes off its own vineyards in Maharashtra and a contract farming network, and goes out through 31,000 retail and restaurant touch points in 29 states, through government beverage corporations, and through 29 defence canteens. FY26 revenue was Rs 181 cr, the loss after tax was Rs 25 cr, and earnings per share were minus Rs 10.92. It employs 494 permanent people.

The claimed transition has two legs. The first is a cleanup: the listed company used to be Tinna Trade, an agri commodity and steel abrasives trader, and the wine business came in through that shell. FY24 was 47 percent wine, FY25 was 59 percent wine, and FY26 is the first wine only year. The second leg is premiumisation plus a second engine: luxury wine grew about 15 percent in FY26 with J'NOON up 44 percent, and Shotgun reached about 100,000 cases and about 9,000 outlets in 18 states in its debut year. Management guided FY27 to 30 percent revenue growth and a breakeven profit after tax.

The numbers do not yet support the claim. Gate 0 scored 10 out of 160, classified AVOID, confirmed no moat, and fired seven of its nine deal breakers, with the history downgrade applied because the comparable window is only three years long and its first year mixes wine with the trading book being exited. On the only like for like basis, the wine segment, revenue went from Rs 213 cr in FY24 to Rs 178 cr in FY25 to Rs 181 cr in FY26. Recomputed EBITDA margin was minus 2.5 percent in FY26. Return on equity was minus 12.6 percent. The June 2026 quarter grew 22.5 percent and still printed a net loss.

Cash is the sharper problem. Consolidated cash and equivalents fell 84.5 percent to Rs 7.78 lakh against Rs 119.74 cr of borrowings, and the holding company itself closed FY26 with Rs 0.88 lakh, down 90.5 percent. Against that the holding company issued a new Rs 114.50 cr corporate guarantee to the wine subsidiary, and the going concern note in both the standalone and the consolidated accounts never names it. Working capital days rose from 149 in FY24 to 349 in FY26 while revenue did not grow. Collection sits at 212 days on Rs 105 cr of receivables. FY26 was the one year with positive operating cash flow, Rs 5.0 cr, and it came from a near doubling of rebate and payables accruals, not from profit: operating profit before working capital changes was minus Rs 3.65 cr and free cash flow was minus Rs 7.3 cr.

Management credibility grades D. FY26 revenue guidance was walked down four times inside the year, from 20 to 25 percent at the start, to 15 to 20 percent, to 12 to 15 percent, to about 7 percent, and the year printed about 1 percent. The same question on why EBITDA margin sits so far below gross margin was deflected in three consecutive quarters, the hospitality project economics were deflected in three, and the equity raise question was deflected in two. Five consecutive calls carry no mention at all of the Rs 114.50 cr guarantee, the auditor's going concern emphasis of matter, the 65.1 percent warrant forfeiture, or the Rs 6.63 cr Section 270 misreporting of income penalty for AY 2018-19, all of them filed at or before the call where they belonged. On the delivery side the corrected count is about five promises kept, one part kept and seven missed, and four more unkept promises the stage did not track at all, covering deferred tax, a holding company receivable, an advertising spend reduction, and Shotgun run rate disclosure. What management did deliver, it delivered plainly: Shotgun reached 18 states against a 10 to 15 state target and hit its 100,000 case target.

One correction belongs here, because the first version of this run got it wrong. The stage 5 headline finding, that management's stated EBITDA carried the opposite sign to the filed accounts, does not survive checking. It compared a consolidated recomputation against figures management was quoting on the wine subsidiary basis. On the subsidiary basis the claims largely reconcile: the December 2025 quarter subsidiary profit before tax was plus Rs 11.79 lakh against a claim of roughly Rs 0.1 cr, and subsidiary EBITDA was Rs 5.42 cr against a claim of Rs 5.5 cr. The D grade stands on the guidance record, the evasions, the silence on filed items and the peer contradicted claims, not on a sign flip.

The peers contradict three claims outright. Fratelli prints a one third domestic wine share on every investor deck and its CFO described an 85 to 90 percent duopoly with Sula. Its own audited Rs 181 cr against its own cited market size of Rs 1,917 cr to Rs 2,320 cr is 7.5 to 9.5 percent, and adding Sula's own disclosed Rs 511 cr of own brand revenue gives a combined 30 to 36 percent, not 85 to 90. The 25 to 30 percent EBITDA margin an analyst attributed to the market leader on a Fratelli call, and which management did not correct, is contradicted by Sula's own filed operating EBITDA margins of 17.4 percent in FY26 and 14.7 percent in the June 2026 quarter. The claim that canned wine grows 25 to 30 percent quarter on quarter is contradicted by Sula reporting slow traction on canned wine, with a format mapping caveat that canned still wine is not the same product as an RTD cocktail. Across 12 peer transcripts and six peer decks, the word Fratelli appears zero times. Sula also disclosed that Maharashtra table grape prices more than doubled from Rs 15 to 16 a kilo to Rs 35 a kilo in the 2026 harvest, costing it 350 to 400 basis points of gross margin, over the same harvest and in the same state where Fratelli's commentary named only yield and quality and its March 2026 quarter call mentioned grapes not at all. And Sula's FY26 Maharashtra subsidy accrual of Rs 48 cr exceeded its own profit before tax of Rs 35.4 cr, which makes state subsidy dependence a category wide structural feature rather than a Fratelli specific flaw, and makes Fratelli's silence on its own subsidy after one Rs 8 cr mention harder to read charitably.

The market is real but smaller than the plan. The domestic branded wine and wine RTD market is Rs 2,400 cr to Rs 2,900 cr, the addressable slice is Rs 1,919 cr, and Fratelli holds about 9.4 percent of it, which leaves roughly 10.6 times revenue headroom and a GOOD runway class. The three year attainable share supports about Rs 236 cr of revenue and the five year about Rs 279 cr, a growth rate near 9 percent a year. Management's Vision 2030 target of Rs 500 cr by FY30 needs 28.9 percent a year, roughly three times that, with no incremental winery capacity committed anywhere in the filings. A third organised competitor, Grover Zampa, is reported at 12 to 15 percent share, which is the plainest evidence against the duopoly framing.
