### 2B. Utilisation trajectory per facility
- **Vuyyuru sugar unit**: installed capacity 7,500 TCD. FY21 crushing season ran only 91 days (10/12/2020–10/03/2021) vs 133 days in FY20 (season split across two periods). Cane crushed collapsed to 411,324 MT (FY21) from 784,964 MT (FY20), a 47.6% YoY decline (MD&A §3, AR p.31-32). Recovery % improved marginally (9.27% vs 9.22%). **Utilisation is contracting, not expanding.**
- **Lakshmipuram unit**: crushing operations suspended since sugar season 2019-20 due to inadequate cane supply; company has consolidated (diverted) cane to Vuyyuru and is planning to sell the unit's movable fixed assets (Note 43/AR p.106; consolidated Note 42/AR p.241-242). This is footprint contraction, not emerging capacity.
- **Distillery**: Industrial/Anhydrous Alcohol installed capacity 50,000 BL/day; FY21 production 64.82 Lakh Litres vs 119.29 Lakh Litres FY20, a **45.7% YoY decline** (MD&A §3(ii), AR p.32).
- **Cogeneration**: 15 MW installed; FY21 power produced 19,275,653 KWH vs 32,129,526 KWH FY20, a 40.0% decline (Note 42, AR p.106).

All measurable utilisation trends in this AR point down, not up — the opposite of what an
emerging-capacity moat would show.

### 2C. Growth embedded in capex — arithmetic shown, then caveated
- Total FY21 capital expenditure (standalone, Segment Note 56, AR p.116): **₹15.17 Cr** (₹151,720,763)
- Net fixed assets, standalone, FY21 year-end (PP&E net ₹84.52 Cr + CWIP ₹2.65 Cr, Note 3/Balance Sheet, AR p.80, p.94): **₹87.17 Cr**
- FY21 revenue from operations (Directors' Report §2, AR p.22): **₹299.39 Cr**
- Historical fixed-asset turnover = 299.39 / 87.17 = **3.43x**
- Mechanical implied incremental revenue = 15.17 Cr × 3.43 = **₹52.03 Cr ≈ 17.4% of current revenue**

**Caveat (this number is not decision-useful and should not be treated as a real growth
signal):** ₹13.06 Cr of the ₹15.17 Cr total capex (86%) is the one-time Engineering Division
land/building/registration purchase — an entirely unrelated new business with zero disclosed
revenue, capacity, or commissioning date. Applying the *sugar-business* fixed-asset turnover
ratio to *non-sugar* capex materially overstates the implied revenue. The capex embedded in
the actual core sugar/chemicals/power business is close to zero — no core capacity expansion
was announced, and the Lakshmipuram unit was closed (a contraction). `capex_embedded_growth_pct`
is reported per the mechanical instruction but flagged low-confidence throughout.

## SECTION 4: REGULATORY & POLICY TAILWINDS (R1)

### 4A. Regulatory approvals in pipeline
NO EVIDENCE FOUND — no company-specific pending regulatory approval, licence, or application
is named anywhere in the AR. ISO 14001:2015 / 9001:2015 / OHSAS 18001:2007 certifications are
continuing, not new (Directors' Report §26, AR p.27).

### 4B. Government policy tailwinds
**Ethanol blending mandate.** MD&A states: "Mandatory blending of ethanol with petrol will
boost the revenue and profitability of Sugar Mills" and separately flags growing energy
consumption and Green Fuel status as long-term positives (MD&A §1–2, AR p.30). 
- **Amount:** not quantified in rupee terms specific to KCPSUGIND.
- **Duration:** ongoing national policy (Government of India, multi-year).
- **Enrolment status:** company already operates a 50,000 BL/day distillery producing
  Industrial/Anhydrous Alcohol, but **production fell 45.7% YoY** (64.82 vs 119.29 Lakh Litres,
  MD&A §3(ii), AR p.32) — no dedicated ethanol-capacity expansion capex or EBP supply contract
  is disclosed in this AR.
- **Competitors:** explicitly shared — this is an industry-wide opportunity listed for the
  whole Sugar Industry, not an exclusive KCPSUGIND advantage (MD&A §2(i), AR p.30).

### 4C. Regulatory moat assessment
Active but underexploited: the policy is already in force as of FY21 (not a future trigger),
yet the company's own alcohol output moved in the *wrong* direction during the same year. Time
to kick in for this specific company is uncertain and contingent on future distillery capex
not committed to as of FY21. Sustainability of the policy itself is high (durable, multi-year
GoI programme), but because it is shared equally by all sugar mills with distillery capacity,
it functions as a sector floor rather than a company-specific moat.
