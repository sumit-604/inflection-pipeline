## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

**2A. Demand environment.** All four peers report a consistent pattern across FY26/Q1 FY27: strong domestic Indian demand (2W, PV, CV, tractors) accelerated by the September 2025 GST rationalization, with festive-season strength persisting well beyond the festive months (Endurance, Happy Forgings, Sansera, Sona Comstar all cite this). Export demand to the US and Europe was weak through most of FY26 (tariff uncertainty, EV subsidy cuts, European OEM insolvencies) but all four peers describe a turn toward recovery by Q4 FY26/Q1 FY27, helped by tariff clarity and an accelerating global EV narrative (especially Sona Comstar, whose Q1 FY27 call frames a broad-based recovery "well ahead of our own expectations"). This is a consensus picture, not a split — and it contradicts Divgi management's narrower "flat EV market" framing (see Claim 5).

**2B. Pricing and input costs.** Commodity inflation (aluminum, steel, copper) is a universal theme from roughly Q4 FY26 (Apr-2026) onward across all four peers, driven by the same macro shock (Middle East conflict/energy prices). All four describe raw-material cost pass-through to customers as standard practice but with a lag (typically one quarter domestically, longer for exports), meaning margins compress mechanically (numerator/denominator effect) during inflationary spikes even when absolute EBITDA holds. This matches a margin narrative Divgi's B05 record does not directly engage with — B05 did not surface any commodity-cost or margin-compression discussion for Divgi itself, which is a gap given peers uniformly flag this as a live FY26/FY27 issue.

**2C. Capex cycle.** This is an industry-wide capacity race, not a lone expander. Endurance is running one of its largest-ever capex programs (~₹800cr/year, four new greenfield plants: ABS, disc brakes, alloy wheels, battery packs). Happy Forgings is mid-way through a ₹650cr+ heavy-forging/data-center capex program. Sansera is expanding its ADS (aerospace/defense/semicon) hangar and driveline forging capacity simultaneously. Sona Comstar just announced a Denso JV plus a Robotics/Physical AI vertical, on top of railway-business integration capex. All four are explicitly building capacity ahead of confirmed demand, funded from internal accruals, and all frame this as diversification-driven (non-auto, non-ICE, or geographic). Divgi's own capex program (Indonesia ramp-up, automatic-transmission localization, EV transmission, Project Mayflower US plant) fits this same industry-wide pattern of pre-emptive, growth-linked capacity building — Divgi is not an outlier in pace, though its capex is far smaller in absolute scale than these peers'.

**2D. Competitive mentions.** No peer mentions Divgi Torqtransfer, transfer cases, or automatic-transmission localization for India by name or unmistakable description, across all 16 transcripts. This silence is itself informative: none of the four driveline/forging-adjacent peers treats transfer-case/automatic-transmission localization as a market they are competing in or watching, consistent with Divgi occupying a narrow, largely uncontested niche rather than a contested one with larger listed peers.

**2E. Risks peers discuss that Divgi's B05 record does not.** (1) Rare-earth magnet supply disruption from China — a dominant, recurring theme in every Sona Comstar call (heavy rare-earth ban from April 2025, shift to light rare-earth and then ferrite-based motor designs) — not mentioned anywhere in Divgi's three transcripts, even though Divgi's own EV transmission (Sigma) program sits in the same broad EV-driveline space. (2) New Labour Code one-time PAT hits, disclosed by Endurance (₹15cr, Q3 FY26), Sansera (₹16.2cr, Q3 FY26), and Sona Comstar (₹30cr, Q3 FY26) as a sector-wide cost effective November 2025 — B05 records no equivalent disclosure or discussion for Divgi across its three transcripts. (3) Freight/container-cost spikes (Happy Forgings: container costs rose from ~$2,000 to ~$6,000, partially recoverable from customers) affecting export economics — not raised by Divgi despite its own export/Project Mayflower dependence. (4) Gas availability constraints in North India (Sona Comstar, Apr-2026) forcing a shift to electric heating — a regional supply-chain risk not mentioned by Divgi. (5) Nexperia chip shortage and the Novelis aluminum-plant fire (both flagged by Sona Comstar as live, monitored risks in the Oct-2025 call) — generic supply-chain fragility themes Divgi's calls do not address. These feed the missing-risks analysis for synthesis.

## PART 4: TRIANGULATION SUMMARY

- **Claims verified:** 1 of 7 (Claim 7 — USMCA/RVC content rule). Partially verified: 2 of 7 (Claim 1 — direction yes, magnitude no; Claim 6 — trend yes, Divgi-specific naming no).
- **Claims contradicted:** 1 of 7 (Claim 5 — India's EV market is not flat; three of four peers show 45-90%+ YoY growth across 2W/3W/PV segments through FY26 and Q1 FY27). This is a priority item for synthesis.
- **Unverifiable:** 3 of 7 (Claims 2, 3, 4 — automatic-transmission Tier-1 landscape, transfer-case market size, Indonesia tender savings — all outside what these four peers would ever disclose).
- **The single most consequential contradiction:** Divgi management characterized India's EV market as flat across all three transcripts analyzed in B05, using this to frame the Sigma EV transmission's three-quarter slippage as partly market-driven rather than purely execution-driven. Three of four peers — most emphatically Sona Comstar, whose entire EV order book and BEV revenue mix is the central story of every one of its four calls — show India EV growth accelerating sharply (2W scooters +45-65% YoY, electric cars +65% YoY by March 2026) through the exact period Divgi's calls span. This materially weakens the market-wide-headwind explanation for Divgi's EV transmission delays and should sharpen the synthesis's read on the EV slippage as OEM/execution-specific.
- **The single strongest independent confirmation:** The USMCA/RVC minimum-local-content mechanism (Claim 7) is confirmed independently and in detail by two peers (Sansera and Sona Comstar) across multiple quarters, describing a real, currently binding 65%-rising-to-70-75% domestic value-addition threshold tied to US tariff relief, extended for five years and recently broadened to medium/heavy vehicles. This gives Divgi's Project Mayflower capex rationale a credible regulatory foundation independent of Divgi's own telling.
- **Overall:** The peer set complicates more than it either fully supports or fully undercuts Divgi's narrative. It lends real credibility to two of Divgi's structural theses — the China-to-India de-sourcing tailwind (Claim 6) and the USMCA/RVC rationale for US manufacturing (Claim 7) — both corroborated independently by multiple peers in their own words, not just plausible-sounding assertions. But it directly contradicts Divgi's flat-EV-market framing (Claim 5), which is the specific claim management used to soften scrutiny of a three-quarter EV transmission slippage, and it fails to corroborate the magnitude of Divgi's China-tariff claim (Claim 1), where the peer-cited figures (25-50%) sit meaningfully below Divgi's ~60%. On balance: the macro tailwind claims survive triangulation; the claim doing the most work to explain away Divgi's own execution miss does not.

## PART 5: CROSS-PEER HYPOTHESIS

Across all four peers, two patterns recur that none of them names explicitly but that emerge only from reading them together: (1) every peer's non-automotive/non-ICE diversification push (Endurance's ABS/battery-pack/solar-damper lines; Happy Forgings' data-center/heavy-forging vertical; Sansera's ADS aerospace/defense/semicon business; Sona Comstar's railway acquisition, Denso JV, and robotics/physical-AI vertical) is being funded almost entirely from internal accruals rather than fresh equity or debt, even though each is simultaneously running its largest-ever capex program; and (2) every peer explicitly frames its new vertical as arriving 4-7 years after the first investment before it becomes revenue-material (Sona Comstar states this pattern explicitly and repeatedly: EV differential assemblies 2016-2021, suspension motors 2021-2026, robotics 2025-2030+).

Taken together, this suggests a sector-wide capital-allocation discipline that is not really about diversification per se — it is about using cash-generative legacy ICE/auto businesses as a self-funding bridge to option-like bets on adjacent high-growth verticals (non-auto industrial, aerospace/defense, robotics, EV-adjacent electronics), on a rolling 5-7 year payback assumption, without ever levering the balance sheet to accelerate the timeline. None of the four peers states this as a deliberate shared playbook — each frames its own diversification as its own unique strategic insight — but the pattern (self-funded, patient, multi-year, always framed as "we do not want external capital pressure to force premature commercialization") is identical across all four, independently arrived at. This is testable: if the pattern holds, none of these four (or comparable Indian auto-component peers) will raise dilutive capital specifically to accelerate a new-vertical bet in the next 2-3 years; the moment one does, it signals either a break from this discipline or that the new-vertical opportunity has become large enough to outrun what internal accruals can fund — which would itself be a notable inflection worth tracking across the peer set.
