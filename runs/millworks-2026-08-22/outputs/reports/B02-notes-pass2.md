## NEW FINDING 1 — The restatement is far broader and more severe than Pass 1's summary line captured
(Annexure IV, Note 3 "Reconciliation of Restated Profits", p.206/F14–F15; Note 4 "Reconciliation of
Restated Net-Worth", p.207/F16)

Pass 1's Top-10 #10 mentioned "Restatement of Sales... plus MSME-interest, depreciation-useful-life, and
deferred-tax miscalculations." The actual reconciliation note lists **thirteen distinct restated P&L line
items** plus a separate net-worth reconciliation, each with its own explanatory footnote (a–n):

| # | Item | Explanatory note (company's own words, abridged) |
|---|---|---|
| a | Insurance expense | "failed to recognise the prepaid expense... now restated" (FY24, FY25) |
| b | Electricity expense | "made payments... but failed to record the same" (FY24, FY25) |
| c | Interest income on ICD | "failed to book interest income regarding ICD" (FY25) |
| d | Interest on MSME dues | "failed to recognise interest payable on delayed payments" (all years) |
| e | Rent expense | "inappropriately calculated... lease equalisation" AND "by mistake capitalised excess rent to Capital work in progress" (FY24, FY25) |
| f | Salary expense | "capitalised excess amount to capital work in progress" (FY24, FY25) |
| g | Restatement of sales | "recognised the sales pertaining to next period" — cut-off error (FY24, FY25) |
| h | Changes in inventory | consequential to (g) |
| i | Gratuity expense | "taken revised gratuity report for March 2025" |
| j | Exchange rate gain | "recognised foreign currency translation income/expense inappropriately" |
| k | Depreciation and amortization | "calculated... using incorrect useful life and ignoring the scrap value of the assets" |
| l | Prior period items | booked in wrong period, "now restated" (FY26: ₹95.39L) |
| m | Income tax for previous years | "inappropriately calculated income tax liability" |
| n | Deferred tax | "not calculated correctly the deferred tax impact" |

Net effect, quantified:
- **Net Profit After Tax, restated vs. audited**: FY2024 ₹259.37L (audited) → ₹195.41L (restated), a
  **-24.7% cut**; FY2025 ₹511.54L → ₹524.90L, **+2.6%**; FY2026 ₹3,637.58L → ₹3,706.39L, **+1.9%**
  (driven mainly by the ₹95.39L Prior Period Income item and small tax corrections in FY26).
- **Net worth, restated vs. audited**: FY2024 ₹296.38L (audited) → ₹232.98L (restated), a **-21.4% cut**;
  FY2025 ₹2,411.79L → ₹2,331.25L, **-3.3%**; FY2026 ₹8,248.22L → ₹8,266.99L, **+0.2%**.

🔴 Red Flag (upgraded from Pass 1's 🟡 Watch characterisation). Two items in particular are control
failures, not mere reclassifications: (i) rent AND salary expense were both wrongly **capitalised to
Capital Work-in-Progress** in FY24/FY25 to avoid hitting the P&L — a classic expense-deferral technique,
whatever the intent — and (ii) sales cut-off errors moving revenue between periods recur in both FY24 and
FY25. A first-time filer restating net worth by -21.4% in its earliest presented year, across thirteen
separate line items spanning revenue cut-off, expense capitalisation, tax computation, and deferred tax,
is a materially weaker internal-controls signal than a single "sales restatement" line suggests. This
should inform the Section 1A quality assessment directly, not just sit as a Watch item.

## NEW FINDING 2 — The Quick Pay equity investment was funded by converting an existing unsecured loan,
and the relationship is a captive manufacturing arrangement, not a passive stake
(RHP "Our Business" / "Business Strategies", p.137; cross-refs p.25, p.28/Risk Factor #4)

Pass 1 correctly flagged the ₹575.06L Quick Pay investment and its non-AS-18 treatment. What Pass 1 did
not capture: the company's own narrative states "**The investment of ₹5.75 crore was made by converting
an existing unsecured loan into equity shares of Quick Pay Private Limited**" and describes a captive
supply arrangement: "our Company will manufacture specified drone components... and supply these
components to Quick Pay Private Limited. Quick Pay Private Limited will integrate the supplied
components into finished drone systems." (RHP, p.137)

🔴 Red Flag — this closes Pass 1's implicit gap on *how* the investment arose and *why* it is not a
passive minority stake. Before FY2026, Millworks was already an unsecured lender to the company that
became (a) its dominant customer at 47.02% of FY2026 sales and (b) its equity investee. The commercial
relationship is now: Millworks funds Quick Pay's balance sheet (via the converted loan/equity), Millworks
manufactures components exclusively for Quick Pay to integrate and sell — i.e., revenue, receivables, and
investment carrying value are all concentrated in one counterparty relationship that is deliberately kept
outside AS-18 scope on the technicality of no direct shareholding/directorial overlap. No unsecured-loan
amount, date, or interest terms prior to conversion are disclosed anywhere in the notes — NOT FOUND
(remains open; the notes disclose only the post-conversion equity balance).

## NEW FINDING 3 — CLOSES Pass 1 NOT FOUND: the ₹707.74L "Purchase of PPE from V3 Technologies" (FY2025)
was settled in equity shares, not cash — and Pass 1's other NOT FOUND (nature of the 5,267 non-cash
shares) is answered by the same disclosure
(Capital Structure chapter, "Issue of equity shares for consideration other than cash", RHP p.72–73)

The Capital History table shows an allotment on **01.03.2025 of 3,723 Equity Shares at ₹19,010/share
(₹9.98 crore face-equivalent at that price ≈ ₹707.7L), "Other than Cash", "Preferential Allotment"**, with
the explicit note: "**the said allotment is done in lieu of machinery transferred by M/s. V3 Technologies
(Partnership Firm) to the Company**." The allottees are the four promoters as partners of V3 Technologies
(Rashmi Sridhar Acharya, Sowmya Madhu, Sridhar Acharya, Madhu H K). The value (3,723 × ₹19,010 =
₹707.65L) reconciles to the ₹707.74 Lakh "Purchase of PPE" figure already in Pass 1's RPT table (small
rounding gap only).

🔴 Red Flag — this is a materially different fact pattern than Pass 1's framing of "PPE vendor,
arm's-length pricing not evidenced." The transaction was not a cash purchase at all: the company issued
equity to its own promoters (via their partnership firm) in exchange for machinery, diluting all other
shareholders, at a self-set issue price (₹19,010/share) that was **not disclosed as a related-party item
in the AS-18 note itself** (Annexure XXXVIII shows only the ₹707.74L rupee value, with no flag that
settlement was via share issuance to the promoters personally rather than cash to the firm). A
promoter-controlled non-cash asset-for-equity swap, priced by the company itself, is a governance issue
distinct from and additive to Pass 1's arm's-length-pricing concern.

Separately, this closes Pass 1's NOT FOUND on the "5,267 non-cash consideration shares": **3,723 (V3
Technologies machinery swap, above) + 1,544 (loan-to-equity conversion, see Finding 4) = 5,267**, exactly
matching the Share Capital note's aggregate non-cash-consideration figure (Annexure V, p.208/F17).

## NEW FINDING 4 — A second, separate loan-to-equity conversion to non-promoter individuals, one of whom
is a large pre-IPO shareholder
(Capital Structure chapter, RHP p.73)

On **03.03.2025**, 1,544 equity shares were issued at ₹19,010/share (≈₹293.5L) "**via Conversion of Loan
to Equity**" to two individuals: Sheila Bhaskar Mudbidri (1,289 shares) and Mayur Bhandari (255 shares).
Neither is disclosed as a promoter, director, or KMP, and neither transaction appears in the AS-18
related-party note. Mayur Bhandari subsequently received **5,25,800 bonus shares** in the December 2025
1:200 bonus issue (RHP p.75) — the third-largest bonus allotment after the promoters and Aparna Samir
Thakker — indicating he is a substantial pre-IPO shareholder whose original stake originated, at least in
part, from a loan-to-equity conversion rather than a cash subscription.

🟡 Watch — no interest rate, tenor, or origin of this loan is disclosed anywhere in the notes; the
counterparty is not evidenced as a related party, so this sits outside AS-18 scope by disclosure, but the
mechanism (loan converted to equity at a company-set price, no cash changing hands, to what appears to be
a significant continuing shareholder) parallels the promoter-linked non-cash equity issuance in Finding 3
closely enough to warrant a question for management on the origin and terms of this loan.

## NEW FINDING 5 — Recurring, worsening, multi-regime statutory filing delays (GST, EPF, ESIC) spanning
nearly the company's entire operating history, elevating Pass 1's "isolated compliance" framing to a
genuine cross-cutting pattern
(Risk Factor #14, RHP p.32–36)

Risk Factor #14 discloses three separate delay tables:
- **GST returns**: 10 instances of late filing, Karnataka, spanning FY2021-22 through FY2025-26 (delays
  of 1–19 days each), reason given each time: "Accounts Preparation."
- **EPF returns**: 23 months of delayed filing from Jul-2023 through Mar-2026 (i.e. nearly continuous;
  only a handful of months in the 33-month window show no delay), delays ranging 1–73 days, up to 149
  employees affected in a single month. The three most recent months before the RHP (**Dec-25: 73 days
  late, 145 employees; Jan-26: 42 days; Feb-26: 52 days**) are among the WORST in the entire series, not
  the best — despite the company's own repeated corrective-action statement in every single row:
  "Company has strengthened its accounts department; they review these issues on time-to-time basis."
- **ESIC returns**: a parallel, similarly near-continuous delay pattern (Aug-23 through Mar-26), same
  boilerplate corrective-action language each time.

🔴 Red Flag — Pass 1 flagged bank drawing-power discrepancies (Finding #3) and MSME payment delays
(Finding #5) each as standalone recurring patterns. Read together with this Risk Factor #14 disclosure,
the company shows recurring, unresolved compliance failures across **at least five separate domains**:
GST filing, EPF filing, ESIC filing, MSME supplier payments (Section 43B(h)/interest), and bank
stock/book-debt reporting — plus the Section 185 loan violations and the systemic P&L restatement
(Finding 1 above). The identical corrective-action language repeated dozens of times without the
underlying delays actually stopping (delays are getting worse, not better, into the RHP filing window) is
itself a signal that the "strengthened accounts department" remediation claim has not been effective.
This is now the single most important pattern-level finding across both passes and should anchor the
accounting-quality score's "disclosure transparency" vs. "consistency with prior years" dimensions.

## NEW FINDING 6 — Nearly half of FY2026 "Finance Cost" is self-inflicted penalty interest, not
genuine cost of capital
(Annexure XXX, "Details of Finance Cost as Restated", p.218/F27)

| Component | FY2026 | % of total |
|---|---|---|
| Interest on Borrowings | 156.62 | 46.1% |
| Bank and loan Processing Charges | 27.83 | 8.2% |
| Interest on Income Tax (company's own late tax payment) | 94.47 | 27.8% |
| Interest on late payment of MSME Dues | 61.14 | 18.0% |
| **Total Finance Cost** | **340.06** | 100% |

🔴 Red Flag — new quantification, not in Pass 1. **₹155.61L, or 45.8% of total FY2026 finance cost, is
penalty/interest arising from the company's own late payment of its own income tax and its own MSME
supplier dues** — not the cost of borrowed capital (which is the remaining 54.2%, ₹184.45L). "Interest on
Income Tax" of ₹94.47L (FY26) vs ₹9.30L (FY25) vs ₹6.82L (FY24) is a ~10x jump, tracking the same
deteriorating pattern as MSME interest (also confirmed independently in the Statement of Tax Shelters as
a permanent difference, Annexure XLI, p.230/F39). This is a direct, quantified earnings-quality cost of
the compliance weaknesses in Finding 5: the company is paying real cash penalties for late payment
practices across tax and MSME dues, and this cost is embedded inside "Finance Cost" where a reader
modelling forward interest cost as a function of debt would not isolate it.

## NEW FINDING 7 — "Sundry Balance Written Off" jumped ~10.5x, alongside zero doubtful-debt provisioning
(Annexure XXXII, "Details of Other Expenses as Restated", p.218–219/F27)

Sundry balances written off: **₹69.60L (FY2026)** vs ₹6.61L (FY2025) vs ₹5.86L (FY2024) — a 953% jump vs
prior year, far outpacing the 573% revenue growth. No further break-down of what these balances relate to
(trade receivables, advances, or other) is disclosed — NOT FOUND.

🟡 Watch — read alongside Pass 1's Finding #2 (₹0 doubtful-debt provision across all three years despite
the receivables blow-out), this raises the question of whether some uncollectible balances are being
written off directly through Other Expenses on an ad hoc basis rather than being captured by a forward-
looking provisioning policy. It does not resolve Pass 1's FLAG-CASH concern; if anything it corroborates
that receivables/balances quality is under pressure even before the FY2026 explosion is fully tested.

## NEW FINDING 8 — Boilerplate "no loan-to-equity conversion" statement is narrowly scoped and could
mislead a reader who does not cross-reference the Capital History note
(RHP p.161/F168, "Material Acquisitions" chapter)

The RHP states: "**As of date of this Red Herring Prospectus, there are no defaults or rescheduling of
borrowings from financial institutions or banks or conversion of loans into equity in relation to our
Company.**" Read in isolation this could suggest no loan-to-equity conversions occurred. In fact, at least
**two** loan-to-equity conversions are disclosed elsewhere in the same RHP: the 1,544-share conversion to
Sheila Bhaskar Mudbidri/Mayur Bhandari (Finding 4) and the ₹575.06L Quick Pay unsecured-loan-to-equity
conversion (Finding 2).

🟡 Watch — not a false statement (it is scoped to "borrowings from financial institutions or banks," which
neither conversion was), but the juxtaposition is a disclosure-quality issue: a reader relying on the
single boilerplate line in the Material Acquisitions chapter without cross-referencing the Capital History
note (Capital Structure chapter) and the Business Strategies narrative would miss both conversions
entirely. Flagged for completeness; downstream stages should not rely on any single negative-assurance
statement in this RHP without checking the notes.

# PASS 2 NEW FINDINGS SUMMARY

1. Restatement scope is far wider than Pass 1's headline suggested: 13 P&L line items restated across
   all three years, including two genuine capitalisation-to-CWIP errors (rent, salary) and cut-off errors
   in both FY24 and FY25; net worth cut -21.4% in FY2024 and -3.3% in FY2025 on restatement. 🔴
2. The Quick Pay equity stake originated from converting an existing unsecured loan, and the underlying
   arrangement is a captive component-supply relationship for a new drone product line, not a passive
   investment. 🔴
3. The ₹707.74L "Purchase of PPE from V3 Technologies" (Pass 1 Finding #4) was settled via a non-cash
   equity issuance of 3,723 shares to the four promoters as V3 Technologies partners — closing Pass 1's
   NOT FOUND on the 5,267 non-cash shares and adding a promoter-dilution dimension to the RPT concern. 🔴
4. A second, separate loan-to-equity conversion (1,544 shares) to two non-promoter individuals, one of
   whom is a top-10 pre-IPO shareholder by the bonus issue. 🟡
5. Statutory filing delays (GST, EPF, ESIC) recur across nearly the entire company history and are
   worsening, not improving, in the months immediately before the RHP — a pattern that, combined with
   Pass 1's bank drawing-power and MSME findings, spans at least five distinct compliance domains. 🔴
6. 45.8% of FY2026 Finance Cost (₹155.61L of ₹340.06L) is self-inflicted late-payment interest on the
   company's own income tax and MSME dues, not cost of borrowed capital. 🔴
7. "Sundry Balance Written Off" jumped ~10.5x YoY to ₹69.60L in FY2026, alongside the zero doubtful-debt
   provision already flagged in Pass 1. 🟡
8. A boilerplate "no loan-to-equity conversion" statement elsewhere in the RHP is narrowly scoped and
   does not surface the two conversions found in Findings 2 and 4 without cross-referencing. 🟡
9. Minor: Head of Business Development resigned three weeks before the RHP date. 🟡
10. CLOSED: hedging policy NOT FOUND upgraded to a confirmed explicit negative disclosure ("We do not
    have a formal hedging policy").

No item found in this pass overturns or contradicts a Pass 1 finding; all are additive detail, closed
gaps, or new items in previously under-examined sections (Capital Structure allotment history, Risk
Factors statutory-delay tables, Finance Cost and Other Expenses annexures, Business Strategies
