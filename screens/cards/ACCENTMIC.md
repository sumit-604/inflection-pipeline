## Business Understanding Narrative

**What does it do, and how does it make money?** Accent makes microcrystalline cellulose (MCC). MCC is a white powder made from wood pulp. It is the filler and binder in most pressed tablets (H1 deck, p.10). The company imports pulp, breaks it down with acid, then dries and grades it into 22 grades (H1 deck, p.11). It sells about 85% through distributors to more than 200 customers in over 75 countries (H1 deck, pp.6, 11). Exports were 72.34% of H1 FY27 revenue (H1 deck, p.5). FY26 revenue was Rs 349.03 crore and profit Rs 43.86 crore (AR26, pp.95, 117).

**What is changing?** Two things. First, premium grades (silicified MCC and MCC spheres) rose to 13.12% of FY26 revenue from 3.74% in FY25 (AR26, p.17). Second, a new plant. Unit III at Nayka, Kheda, Phase 1 adds 2,800 MTPA of croscarmellose sodium (CCS), sodium starch glycolate (SSG) and pulp-based CMC. Management targets Rs 650-700 per kg against about Rs 250-260 per kg for today's blended MCC (H1 deck, p.23; May-26 call, p.9).

**Why now?** H1 FY27 printed a 25.11% EBITDA margin against 19.02% a year earlier, and profit of Rs 31.12 crore against Rs 18.07 crore (H1 deck, p.20). Receivables halved and operating cash flow was Rs 78.28 crore in six months (H1 res, pp.4-5). CARE upgraded the company to A-/Stable in March 2026 (CARE, p.1).

**What must be true?** Unit III must get its consent to operate, start, and win customer approvals for CCS. The H1 FY27 margin must come from mix and manufacturing, not from one customer, currency or a change in how bought-in MCC is booked. Phase 2 (12,000 MTPA of MCC) must arrive so the low-margin trading can stop.

**What breaks it?** Unit III stays stuck on approvals. The H1 margin reverts to the FY26 level of 18.18% (H1 deck, p.20). Customer qualification for CCS takes years, as the company itself warns (H1 deck, p.15). Or this year's governance drift continues.

## 2. Business model and archetype

Three units (H1 deck, p.13; AR26, p.18):

| Unit | Capacity | Use | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| I, Pirana | 2,000 MTPA | Spin-flash MCC, domestic | 100% utilised |
| II, Dahej SEZ | 7,200 MTPA | Spray-dried MCC, export | 95% utilised |
| III, Nayka Phase 1 | 2,800 MTPA | CCS, SSG, CMC | Trial runs; approvals pending |
| III, Phase 2 | 12,000 MTPA | MCC | Target H1 FY28 |

The two operating plants sold about 15,000 MT in FY26 against about 10,000 MT of capacity (May-26 call, p.7). The gap is trading: about 25% to 30% of volume, Rs 95.55 crore of stock-in-trade purchases in FY26 against Rs 53.31 crore in FY25 (AR26, p.95). Trading earns 4% to 5% (May-26 call, p.14), and "2 to 3 percentage" for one customer (May-26 call, p.23). Nearly all of it runs through Pirana (May-26 call, p.14).

Where the margin sits: in H1 FY27 Dahej earned segment PBIT of Rs 35.08 crore, Pirana Rs 6.84 crore, and Unit III lost Rs 0.44 crore (H1 res, p.6; Unit III derived from the segment total). In FY26 Pirana earned Rs 4.90 crore on Rs 152.92 crore of revenue (H1 res, p.6).

**Archetype: Commodity converter for core MCC** (pulp in, MCC out; spread, utilisation and cost position decide returns; Amendment 17 binds for any deep run). A **build-to-spec** layer sits on top for premium grades and Unit III products, where customer qualification and DMF filings create stickiness.

## 3. Competitive advantages

**Qualification and certification: present.** EXCiPACT, GMP, FSSC 22000, US-DMF (H1 deck, p.15). The same page states the limit: new-product revenue "depends on customer approvals".

**Cost position: partial.** Dahej is an SEZ unit with export benefits (H1 deck, p.13). Pulp comes mainly from one supplier, SAPPI, which the AR lists as a weakness (AR26, p.39).

**Pricing power: weak on core MCC.** Domestic realisation is Rs 170-190 per kg against about Rs 250-260 blended (May-26 call, p.9). CARE calls the MCC market "fragmented" and the industry "highly competitive" (CARE, pp.1, 3). Raw-material increases were "completely passed on" (May-26 call, p.6).

**Technology: claimed, not proven.** A wood-pulp to CMC to CCS route after "~8 years of R&D", described as a first in India (H1 deck, p.14). Only a 50 kg sample lot has shipped (H1 deck, p.24).

**Verdict: a qualification moat is present on export MCC; the premium moat is forming at best.**

## 6. The transition and the quality ladder

**FROM: R2, cost-advantaged converter.** Core MCC margin comes from cost position and export scale. ROCE in the low twenties. Pirana's domestic line sits closer to R1.

**TO: R3, value-added spec'd supplier.** Premium grades now, then CCS, SSG and pulp-based CMC from Unit III at Rs 650-700 per kg (H1 deck, p.23).

Management's claim, once: "Accent's R&D capability is evolving from incremental product development into process innovation" (H1 deck, p.14).

Ladder check. One rung, so the base rate holds. But the rung is earned through customer qualification, which the company says takes years (H1 deck, pp.15, 24).

## 8. Proof check

**The proof point is Unit III commercial production. It is absent.** The plant has missed three targets in twelve months (step 7). The H1 FY27 deck, filed 15-Oct-2026, still says approvals are pending (H1 deck, p.23). No Reg 30 update followed the 13-May-2026 intimation. Project spending ran over budget: IPO funds earmarked Rs 54.39 crore against Rs 72.48 crore deployed, and rights funds Rs 32.33 crore against Rs 62.80 crore (H1 deck, p.24).

**What did move: profit and cash.** H1 FY27 PAT rose 72.2% and EBITDA 65.6% (H1 deck, p.20). Cash conversion was 1.78x EBITDA (H1 deck, p.22). Most of the cash came from collecting one customer's receivables. This is real delivery, but from the existing plants, and its source is the open question in step 5.

**Revenue fell 16.6% half on half** (H1 deck, p.20). Cutting trading explains part of it.

- **Rs 17 crore inter-corporate deposit** given in H1 FY27 (H1 res, p.5). The FY26 AR said the company gave no loans under section 186 (AR26, p.73). Counterparty NOT FOUND. Short-term loans and advances rose to Rs 28.23 crore from Rs 10.37 crore (H1 res, p.4).
- **Related-party purchases tripled** to Rs 46.20 crore in FY26 (step 4), the year trading purchases rose to Rs 95.55 crore (AR26, p.95).
- **Promoter holding down 4.07 points** with no filed disclosure in the corpus (step 4).
- **Director pay ceilings up 67%** after the NRC chair left (step 4).
- **CRISIL "Issuer Not Cooperating"** since at least 2023, revised to BB-/Stable/A4+ on 22-Oct-2026 on legacy facilities of Rs 20.92 crore (CRISIL). The company moved to CARE. Not a credit signal, but a disclosure-hygiene one.
- **Contingent liabilities.** A new Rs 15.26 crore EPCG duty obligation and a Rs 4.12 crore Gujarat Pollution Control Board appeal (AR26, p.124).
- **Key audit matter on Rs 101.80 crore of CWIP**, for the judgement in what is capitalised (AR26, p.86). Not a qualification.
- **Concentration.** Top ten customers 44.10% of FY25 revenue (CARE, p.2). One multinational customer drove the receivable swing (H1 deck, p.22). One main pulp supplier (AR26, p.39).
- **Disclosure.** NSE cautioned the company for a late board outcome (filings/ACCENT_21022025202621, p.1). An investor said on the May-2026 call that the Unit III delay was first learnt from credit ratings (May-26 call, p.17).

## 10. Independent check: the credit rating

**CARE A-/Stable/A2+**, upgraded from BBB+/Stable/A2 on 25-Mar-2026, on Rs 41.98 crore of facilities (CARE, p.1). CARE cites growth in scale, healthy margin in a niche, gearing of 0.01x and adequate liquidity (CARE, pp.1-3). Its weaknesses: moderate scale, pulp and currency exposure, and a competitive industry (CARE, p.1). It would downgrade below Rs 250 crore of income or on gearing above 0.50x (CARE, p.1). Its Unit III date of Apr-2026 has passed (CARE, p.2).

**CRISIL BB-/Stable/A4+, Issuer Not Cooperating** (22-Oct-2026). CRISIL had no management input and used NSE figures only (CRISIL).

The CARE view is the informed one. It sees a safe balance sheet. It does not rate the transition, and its Unit III date is stale.

## 11. Posture: the Transition Decision Matrix

- **Proof gate: NOT FIRED.** Unit III is not producing (step 8). Premium mix is flat on FY26 (step 7).
- **Ugliness: ARTIFACT-OF-CLIMB.** Low-margin trading is a bridge until Phase 2 adds MCC capacity (May-26 call, p.14). The plant delay is an approvals problem, not a demand one, on the filed evidence (U3, p.1).
- **Recognition gap: CLOSED.** Trailing PE is 27.9 at Rs 662 (MD). The forward PE in step 13 is about 25.5. The R3 neighbourhood is about 19x.

**Posture: PRICED NARRATIVE (TRAP).** The earnings growth is real, but it did not come from the transition engine. The price already pays for the R3 rung. Hard WATCH until the plant runs and the margin source is shown.

## 12. Verdict card

**WATCH.**

A strong half, a clean balance sheet and a CARE upgrade. Against that, the transition plant is not running, the margin jump is unexplained, and four governance items moved the wrong way in 2026.

**Load-bearing facts to verify first**
1. The source of the H1 FY27 gross-margin rise to about 48% from about 40%: manufactured MT, premium MT, realisation, and how bought-in semi-processed MCC is booked (H1 res, p.3; May-26 call, p.7).
2. Unit III consent to operate and first commercial sale, with dated filings (U3, p.1; H1 deck, p.23).
3. Who received the Rs 17 crore inter-corporate deposit, and on what terms (H1 res, p.5).
4. The promoter selling from 55.47% to 51.40%: who sold and whether it was filed (H1 deck, p.25; MD).

**What would change the view**
- Up to PROCEED: a Reg 30 filing of Unit III commercial production, plus an H2 FY27 EBITDA margin above 22% with stock-in-trade purchases still low, plus a named, arm's-length ICD.
- Down to PASS: the ICD goes to a promoter-linked entity; the H1 margin proves to be a booking change; or Unit III is still not commercial at the H2 FY27 results.

## 13. Forward view

**Basis tier: B, run-rate.** Management gives no profit guidance: "we don't intend to give any kind of forward-looking guidance" (May-26 call, p.5). It gave plant-level numbers only (Rs 70 crore Year-1 revenue for Phase 1; margin up 2-3 points), and those depend on a plant that has not started.

- **FY+1 (FY27) bottom line: Rs 62.23 crore.** H1 FY27 PAT of Rs 31.12 crore, doubled (H1 res, p.3). Tax is already in it at an effective 24.8% (H1 res, p.3). The company filed no seasonality warning. FY26 earned more in H2 than in H1 (H1 deck, p.20).
- **Growth:** +41.9% against FY26 PAT of Rs 43.86 crore (AR26, p.117).
- **Forward PE: about 25.5x.** Market cap Rs 1,589 crore (screener.in, 26-Oct-2026 close, a service record) divided by Rs 62.23 crore.
- **The two readings.** If H1 FY27 margin holds, the figure above stands. If H2 FY27 only repeats H2 FY26 profit of Rs 25.79 crore (H1 deck, p.20), FY27 PAT is Rs 56.91 crore, growth 29.8% and forward PE about 27.9x (derived). The observation that separates them is the H2 FY27 gross margin with the stock-in-trade line beside it.
- **Read with steps 5, 8 and 9.** No Unit III profit sits in either number. The run-rate rests on a margin whose source is unverified.
