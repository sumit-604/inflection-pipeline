## NEW FINDINGS

### 1. "Claims receivable" (₹183.96mn) and "Claims Received" (₹54.98mn) are undisclosed in nature — a disclosure-transparency gap larger than Pass 1's footnote treatment suggested
Note 13 (p.98): Other Current Financial Assets — "Claims receivable" ₹183,962,074 (FY21) vs ₹193,973,433 (FY20), unsecured, considered good, with **no note anywhere in the standalone, subsidiary, or consolidated financial statements explaining what these claims are** (no reference to cane/sugar subsidy, insurance, government incentive, or litigation-related claims). Note 28 (p.102): Other Income — "Claims Received" ₹54,980,802 (FY21) vs ₹41,734,729 (FY20, +31.7%), again with no narrative. Combined, this receivable is **3.2% of total assets** and the related income line is **1.8% of revenue** — larger in size than several items Pass 1 did anchor and discuss (e.g., the leave-encashment provision), yet the underlying nature of the claim is never stated in 265 pages. 🟡 Watch — elevated from Pass 1's brief footnote mention to a standalone finding given its size and total opacity; a reasonable investor cannot assess recurrence or collectability risk of a ₹184mn asset with zero qualitative disclosure.

### 2. CARE's "Negative" outlook applies to the Company's Long-Term Bank Facilities rating too, not only the Fixed Deposit programme
Directors' Report Item 25 (p.26): "CARE Ratings Limited has revised the Credit Ratings of your Company as **'CARE A -; Negative' for Long Term Bank Facilities**, 'CARE A2+' for Short Term Bank Facilities and 'CARE A -; Negative' for Fixed Deposit Programme." Pass 1 (Top Finding #7) framed the Negative outlook as being specific to the "Fixed Deposit Programme." In fact the entity's **overall long-term credit rating** — covering its bank borrowings — also carries a Negative outlook, alongside the rising Debt-Equity trend Pass 1 already identified (0.36 vs 0.31 vs 0.16-0.19 three years prior). 🟡 Watch — broadens the credit-deterioration signal from a retail-depositor-only concern to a company-wide one.

### 3. Shareholders were asked to raise the borrowing ceiling to ₹300 crore explicitly to fund further business diversification
AGM Notice Item 4 "Borrowing Powers" (p.5) and its Explanatory Statement (p.13): a special resolution supersedes earlier borrowing-power resolutions and seeks approval to borrow up to **₹300 Crores** (over and above paid-up capital, free reserves and securities premium). The stated rationale: **"Your Company is in the verge of diversification of business so as to augment the business and revenues of the Company. For this purpose, the Company may require prompt financial assistance."** This is a forward-looking capital-allocation signal not captured in Pass 1's Top Finding #4 (the Trichy Engineering land purchase) — management is explicitly seeking headroom for *further* diversification beyond the ₹11.7 crore already committed, potentially debt-funded. 🔴 Red Flag — reinforces and extends Pass 1's Red Flag #4; the pivot away from core sugar operations appears intended to continue, not be a one-off.

### 4. The related-party universe disclosed in Note 52 is wider than Pass 1 captured — three named entities show zero transactions or balances anywhere
Note 52(A) (p.114) "Entity in which Directors of the Company are interested" lists **five** entities: (a) Amethyst Cafe P Ltd, (b) **V M Rao Consultants P Ltd**, (c) Durgamba Investment P Ltd, (d) **Anibrain Digital Technologies P Ltd**, (e) **Sethi Funds Management P Ltd**. Pass 1 discussed only Amethyst Cafe (rent transactions) and Durgamba Investment (38.58% shareholder, no transactions). The other three — V M Rao Consultants P Ltd, Anibrain Digital Technologies P Ltd, and Sethi Funds Management P Ltd — are named as related parties but have **no transaction, balance, or explanation anywhere** in the standalone, Eimco, KCP-ARF, or consolidated notes, nor in AOC-2 (Schedule VII, p.65), which reports only three counterparties (Eimco, Amethyst Cafe, M/s Outhouse). 🟡 Watch — a completeness question: either these three entities genuinely had no dealings with the Company (in which case their inclusion in the related-party list without further comment is unremarkable) or the AOC-2/related-party transaction disclosure is incomplete relative to the related-party universe the Company itself defines. Notably, "Sethi Funds Management P Ltd" shares its name with the Executive Chairman (Vinod R. Sethi); no relationship or dealings are disclosed.

### 5. Pending title-deed transfer covers land *and buildings*, not land alone, and stems from a named demerger scheme
Note 3.1 (p.94) states only: "Cost of Freehold Land includes Land in respect of which the transfer of title deeds in the name of K.C.P. Sugar and Industries Corporation Ltd is pending." Cross-referencing Auditor's Report Annexure A(i)(c) (p.75) clarifies the fuller picture: **"the title deeds of immovable properties are held in the name of the Company, except in respect of certain immovable properties (land and buildings), which have been transferred to the Company as per a scheme of demerger are in the name of the erstwhile demerged company."** AGM Notice item 29 (p.9) confirms the demerged entity is **"The KCP Limited."** Pass 1's footnote list mentioned this only as "unquantified pending land title transfer"; the buildings component and the specific demerger source were not captured. No rupee amount is quantified anywhere. NOT FOUND IN DOCUMENT (quantum). 🟡 Watch — disclosure-quality gap on legacy title risk, now shown to extend to buildings as well as land.

### 6. Eimco-K.C.P.: a ₹10.32mn "profit from partnership firm" — a swing from a ₹1.33mn loss the prior year — drove over a third of the subsidiary's Other Income, with no explanation
Eimco Note 25 "Other Income" (p.159): "Profit or loss from partnership firm" = **₹10,322,488** (FY21) vs **Nil** in the FY20 other-income line; the Eimco Cash Flow Statement (p.141) confirms the adjustment moved from a **₹1,330,809 loss add-back in FY20** to a **₹10,322,488 gain deduction in FY21** — i.e., Eimco's share of Quality Engineering Works' (Trichy) results swung from a loss to a large profit within one year. This ₹10.32mn is **35.7% of Eimco's total Other Income of ₹28.91mn** (Eimco Note 25, p.159) and exactly matches the increase in "Investment in Partnership Firm" carrying value from ₹48,469,191 to ₹58,791,679 (Eimco Note 5, p.155). No narrative anywhere explains what drove the turnaround at the partnership firm. 🔴 Red Flag — this is a *second*, subsidiary-level instance of the group-wide pattern Pass 1's Red Flag #1 identified at the parent (non-operating/investment income dominating reported profit): Eimco's own reported PAT of ₹41.40mn (Pass 1 §12e) is itself inflated by more than a quarter from an unexplained investee profit swing, meaning the group's core-operating-earnings problem is broader than the parent's equity book alone.

### 7. Group-level accounting-policy inconsistency: KCP-ARF elects FVTOCI for its equity book (gains never reach P&L), while the parent and Eimco use FVTPL (gains flow through P&L)
KCP-ARF Note 2(m) (p.188-189) and Note 4 (p.192): KCP-ARF's listed-equity portfolio (Asian Paints, Blue Star, Indraprastha Gas, Marico, Savita Oil, etc., ₹44,178,137 at FY21) is classified as **Fair Value through Other Comprehensive Income (FVTOCI)**, per its accounting policy note: "For all other equity instruments, the Company decides to classify the same either as at fair value through other comprehensive income (FVTOCI) or FVTPL... For equity instruments classified as FVTOCI, all fair value changes... are recognized in other comprehensive income (OCI). There is no recycling of the amounts from OCI to the Statement of Profit and Loss, even on sale." This is the *opposite* election from the parent (Note 2(u), FVTPL, p.91) and Eimco (Note 2(r), FVTPL, p.151). Consequence: KCP-ARF's **₹14,498,354 equity fair-value gain (FY21)** — which alone explains its **swing from a ₹19,032 operating loss to a ₹14,479,321 Total Comprehensive Income** (KCP-ARF P&L, p.183) — never touches KCP-ARF's, or the consolidated Group's, reported Profit After Tax; it appears only in OCI/Total Comprehensive Income. NOT a Red Flag by itself (both elections are Ind AS 109-permitted), but 🟡 Watch — it means the group's consolidated PAT of ₹233.02mn (Note 9(IX), Consolidated P&L, p.208) is *understated* relative to what full FVTPL consistency would show, the mirror image of the parent's earnings-quality concern; readers reconciling consolidated PAT to the sum of standalone PATs need to know this policy split exists.

### 8. No quantified sensitivity is disclosed for equity/securities price risk, despite it being the dominant driver of P&L volatility
Note 48 "Financial risk management" (p.109, standalone) and Consolidated Note 39 (p.236) each state only: "The company's exposure to equity securities price risk arises from Investments held... The company monitors the movement in the value of the Investments by observing the NAV." No quantified sensitivity (e.g., impact of a 5%/10% NAV move on P&L) is given for equity price risk anywhere — in contrast to the **Interest Rate Risk** section in the same note, which *does* provide a quantified 100-basis-point sensitivity table (₹20,284,543 impact, Note 48 p.111). This is an internal disclosure-adequacy inconsistency: the risk factor Pass 1 identified as the single largest driver of reported earnings volatility (Red Flag #1: ₹315.81mn fair-value gain) is the one risk factor for which Ind AS 107 sensitivity quantification is *not* provided. 🟡 Watch.

### 9. The standalone "Others" reportable segment (Note 56) is a blended bucket that now absorbs the new bought-out sugar trading revenue, reducing transparency of that revenue stream's margin
Note 56 Segment Reporting (p.116) footnote states "Others" comprises "Bio Fertilizers," yet "Others" segment **external revenue is ₹207,372,029** (standalone, FY21) — far larger than Bio-Fertiliser product revenue of ₹67,120,243 disclosed separately in Note 38 (p.104). The difference (≈₹140.15mn) matches almost exactly the new "Export Sale of Sugar - Trading" (₹129,701,536) plus "Sale of Sugar - Trading" (₹10,449,000) that Pass 1 flagged in Note 27/38 as a new, lower-margin revenue stream (Pass 1 §11). Because the trading revenue is folded into the "Others" segment rather than disclosed as its own line, the segment result for "Others" (₹39,873,201 FY21 vs ₹34,673,278 FY20, Note 56) mixes Bio-Fertiliser margins with trading margins, and an investor cannot isolate the trading business's profitability from the segment table. 🟡 Watch — a segment-classification transparency gap compounding the revenue-quality point Pass 1 already raised.

### 10. Every primary business segment except "Others" declined at the segment-result level — the deterioration is broader than the Sugar-segment loss Pass 1 highlighted
Note 56 (p.116): Segment Results (profit before depreciation, finance cost and taxation) — **Sugar**: ₹(57,984,138) FY21 vs ₹28,324,870 FY20 (into loss, per Pass 1); **Chemicals**: ₹50,952,719 vs ₹84,847,147 (**-39.9%**); **Power & Fuel**: ₹42,901,155 vs ₹80,377,560 (**-46.6%**); **Others**: ₹39,873,201 vs ₹34,673,278 (+15.0%, the only segment to grow, per Finding #9 above, partly trading-revenue-driven). Total segment result fell 66.8% (already noted by Pass 1), but the composition shows the decline is **broad-based across every core operating segment**, not confined to Sugar. 🟡 Watch — elaborates and sharpens Pass 1's Red Flag #1 framing.

### 11. Eimco-K.C.P.'s export earnings nearly halved even as its order book roughly doubled — order growth appears domestically sourced while the export franchise weakened
Eimco Directors' Report Item 7 "Foreign Exchange Earnings and Outgo" (p.121): **"Our Export earnings during the Financial Year 2020-2021 is Rs.6.24 Crores as against Rs.12.67 Crores for the previous year"** — a **-50.7%** decline — occurring in the same year Eimco's Orders (valued) rose from ₹45.32cr to ₹99.15cr (Eimco Directors' Report Item 1, p.120, already noted by Pass 1) and Eimco's own revenue *fell* from ₹40.51cr to ₹31.94cr (Eimco Directors' Report, p.120). Combined with the Liquidated Damages provision growth (+40.5%, Pass 1 Top Finding #10), this indicates the order-book expansion has not yet, and may not proportionately, translate into delivered/billed revenue, and the export segment specifically weakened. 🟡 Watch.

### 12. Parent's gratuity plan remains underfunded by ~₹10.7mn at FY21-end (narrowed from ~₹25.8mn at FY20-end, largely mechanically via the Lakshmipuram settlement)
Note 49 (p.112): Defined Benefit Obligation at FY21-end ₹122,473,248 vs Fair Value of Plan Assets at FY21-end ₹111,790,878 ⇒ **net unfunded liability ₹10,682,370**. At FY20-end: DBO ₹154,290,397 vs Plan Assets ₹128,461,996 ⇒ net unfunded liability ₹25,828,401. Pass 1 discussed the gross movements (benefits paid, contributions, actuarial assumption changes) extensively but did not compute the funded-status gap itself. 🟢 Clean/informational — the funding shortfall narrowed, but the plan is still not fully funded, and the improvement is substantially a function of the one-off Lakshmipuram unit closure settlement rather than a change in ongoing funding policy.

### 13. Several unflagged current-liability swings worth noting alongside the payables-paydown narrative
Note 24 (p.101): "Outstanding Liabilities for Expenses" fell **37.4%**, from ₹269,126,164 to ₹168,609,924; "Unpaid matured deposits and interest accrued thereon" rose **50.4%**, from ₹10,163,745 to ₹15,285,898. Note 25 (p.101): "Statutory Liabilities" fell **75.9%**, from ₹23,544,994 to ₹5,680,522. None of these are individually flagged in Pass 1. 🟡 Watch on the unpaid matured deposits increase specifically — it is a small but directionally relevant data point alongside the Negative FD-programme rating outlook (Pass 1 Top Finding #7 / Finding #2 above): more depositors appear to be letting deposits lapse unclaimed rather than renewing. 🟢 Clean on the Outstanding Liabilities for Expenses and Statutory Liabilities movements — most likely timing/period-end effects consistent with the broader deleveraging story Pass 1 already documented (Top Finding #6), not separately concerning.

### 14. Capital work-in-progress more than doubled, with no itemization of what is under construction
Balance Sheet (p.80) / AGM Deposit Circular 3-year summary (p.18): Capital work-in-progress rose from ₹10,826,431 (FY20) to **₹26,473,489 (FY21), +144.5%**. PP&E Note 3 (p.94) shows gross additions to Property, Plant & Equipment jumped from ₹5,862,095 (FY20) to ₹134,327,588 (FY21) — a 22.9x increase, driven by the Land addition of ₹116,925,100 (the Trichy Engineering purchase, already discussed) — but the CWIP balance itself (assets not yet ready for use) is not itemized by project anywhere in the notes. NOT FOUND IN DOCUMENT (CWIP composition). 🟢 Clean/informational — directionally consistent with the known capex programme, simply unitemized.
