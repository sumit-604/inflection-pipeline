# KABRAEXTRU, Kabra Extrusion Technik Ltd

Phase 1 evidence narrative. Run date 2026-09-05. Rework coverage rerun 2026-09-09. No valuation has run; no entry zone, decision, destination PE or fair value appears in this file.

---

## BUSINESS UNDERSTANDING NARRATIVE

Two businesses sit inside one listed company. The Extrusion Machinery Division made 69.8 percent of FY26 revenue, Rs 314.89 Cr of Rs 451.05 Cr (AR FY26 Note 38 p.105-106). It designs and assembles the machines that turn plastic granules into pipe, blown film, sheet and compounds, and a pipe maker cannot make pipe without one (AR FY26 p.35-36). The Battery Division, called Geon and formerly Battrixx, made 30.2 percent of FY26 revenue, Rs 136.11 Cr (AR FY26 Note 38 p.105-106). It buys lithium ion cells from global suppliers, designs the pack, the battery management system and the housing, and assembles at Chakan near Pune. It does not make cells (AR FY26 p.36). Extrusion customers are pipe, film and flexible packaging makers, mostly Indian, buying engineer to order machines against an advance and milestone payments (AR FY26 p.35-36); exports were Rs 57.52 Cr, 12.8 percent of FY26 revenue (AR FY26 Note 38 p.106). Battery customers are electric two and three wheeler makers, plus retail buyers of a direct to consumer inverter battery launched in FY26 (AR FY26 p.4-5, p.36). Concentration has already cost money: one customer was 19.11 percent of FY26 revenue, and Hero Electric, owing Rs 30.39 Cr, entered NCLT insolvency in December 2024 (AR FY26 Note 38 p.106; Note 9 p.87-88). Neither annual report gives churn, retention or qualification lock in data, so switching costs are not established.

Machine demand today rides on water infrastructure spending, and the company names Jal Jeevan Mission disbursement delays as the cause of its FY26 decline, tracked here as the downstream candidate Jal Jeevan Mission 2.0 fund disbursement pace (AR FY26 p.36). Battery demand today rides on India EV retail registrations, up 24.6 percent in FY26, tracked as the downstream candidate India EV retail registrations by category (AR FY26 p.32-33). Forward, the two markets size to Rs 6,135 Cr conservative and Rs 10,480 Cr realistic and grow about 15.9 percent a year, against a served share of 7.35 percent and 13.6 times revenue headroom. Three outside signals test that runway: Union Budget capex allocation on the Jal Jeevan Mission and PLI-ACC lines, PLI-ACC and PM E-DRIVE disbursement milestones, and the lithium carbonate and cell price index. The company is not capturing the market yet. Consolidated revenue fell 5.45 percent in FY26 while both cited markets grew, and the battery line is smaller than it was two years ago: segment revenue fell 52.3 percent from Rs 266.16 Cr in FY24 to Rs 126.98 Cr in FY25, then printed Rs 136.11 Cr in FY26 (AR FY25 Note 38 p.104; AR FY26 Note 38 p.105-106). The emerging moat scan scored 9 of 92 and classified NONE, and the machine line carries no moat: the ~40 percent market share claim appears only in the FY25 report and gross margin fell from 38.9 percent to 35.64 percent (AR FY25 p.37; AR FY26 p.37). The battery line carries none either, because management calls its own model technology agnostic and asset light, a design any entrant can copy, and the Dec 2023 deck claims of ARAI AIS-156 accreditation, an IATF approved facility and a 90 percent plus design win rate appear in neither annual report (AR FY26 p.36; Dec 2023 deck p.16, p.22). The last two scan categories agree: category 21, cannibalisation barrier, found no evidence, and category 22, regulatory tailwinds, scored weak because Jal Jeevan Mission 2.0, PM E-DRIVE and PLI-ACC reach every competitor equally.

## Machine cash funds a halved battery business

Kabra Extrusion Technik made Rs 451.05 Cr of revenue in FY26, down 5.45 percent, and lost Rs 5.37 Cr consolidated and Rs 2.44 Cr standalone (AR FY26 p.37, p.122, p.17). The 43 year old extrusion machinery business still earns: segment result Rs 50.75 Cr on Rs 314.89 Cr of segment revenue, 16.1 percent, down from 19.3 percent (AR FY26 Note 38 p.105-106). The six year old battery business does not: segment result minus Rs 43.35 Cr on Rs 136.11 Cr of segment revenue, a loss ratio of 31.9 percent against 20.1 percent the year before (AR FY26 Note 38 p.105-106). About Rs 250 Cr is sunk into the roughly 7 GWh Chakan plant, and management puts its revenue ceiling at Rs 1,500 Cr plus at optimal levels, which the FY26 run rate fills to about 9 percent (AR FY26 p.36, p.37). Capex has been commissioned: capital work in progress fell from Rs 50.35 Cr to Rs 12.21 Cr and depreciation rose 89.9 percent to Rs 29.57 Cr (AR FY26 p.83; Note 28 p.94).

The battery growth number does not mean what the report implies. Battery segment revenue fell 52.3 percent between FY24 and FY25, from Rs 26,616.45 lakh to Rs 12,698.12 lakh, and the segment result swung from a profit of Rs 651.55 lakh to a loss of Rs 2,553.28 lakh (AR FY25 Note 38 p.104). Neither annual report narrates that collapse. The FY25 report instead calls Geon a business "gaining traction from EV OEMs" in the same document (AR FY25 p.38, p.4). The FY26 report's 7.2 percent battery growth therefore runs off a base that had already halved. It is also not a like for like number: the segment revenue basis changed between the two reports without disclosure, because the FY25 comparative of Rs 48,983.14 lakh includes other income and the FY26 figure of Rs 45,099.83 lakh excludes Rs 2,367.55 lakh of it (AR FY26 Note 38 p.105 against P&L p.67 and Note 23 p.95; AR FY25 Note 38 p.104). The same defect makes the 13.2 percent extrusion decline not like for like.

Gate 0 scored the backward record 22 of 100 on the core and 25 of 160 grand total, and classified it AVOID. This is the backward scorecard class, not a valuation decision, and no valuation has run. Two things force it. The core score sits far below the 40 floor. Deal breaker 6 fires on its own: FY26 net debt to EBITDA of 4.19 times with interest coverage of 0.317 times. Revenue fell in 6 of the last 9 years, and FY26 is the loss year of the last three. Two lines sat just barrier side of a further deal breaker: median ROCE 10.31 percent, 0.31 percentage points above the AVERAGE cap line, and cumulative cash from operations to PAT of 0.543, 0.04 above the floor. Both would read worse if FY26 repeats. The cause is narrow and traceable, not a broad quality collapse. Take the battery segment out and the machine business is still profitable.

Cash is the tightest fact in the file. Standalone cash was Rs 1.97 Cr against Rs 140.92 Cr of secured short term borrowings repayable on demand (AR FY26 Notes 10 and 17). Consolidated cash from operations fell 79.4 percent to Rs 8.96 Cr; standalone fell 71.7 percent to Rs 12.82 Cr. Free cash flow was negative in FY25 at minus Rs 17.02 Cr and in FY26 at minus Rs 24.68 Cr. The mutual fund buffer fell 57.8 percent from Rs 53.49 Cr to Rs 22.59 Cr, and bank margin money fell 86.4 percent in the same year bank guarantees nearly doubled (AR FY26 Notes 8, 10A, 41(a)). Working capital days rose from about 154 in FY24 to about 244 in FY26. Gross receivables shrank 8.5 percent to Rs 90.52 Cr while the ageing worsened: the over 3 year bucket nearly doubled to Rs 19.35 Cr, and half the gross book is now more than 6 months overdue (AR FY26 Note 9 p.87-88). The company raised Rs 15.30 Cr of net new secured borrowing in FY26 and paid a Rs 8.74 Cr dividend on the prior year's profit in a year it made a loss. The FY26 dividend was cut to nil. CRISIL moved the long term rating twice in about 13 months, from A+/Negative to A/Negative and then to A-/Stable with effect from 13-May-2026, and the short term rating from A1 to A2+ (AR FY26 p.51). One printed ratio appears to say the opposite, and it is wrong. The management discussion prints an Interest Coverage Ratio change of plus 39.20 percent (AR FY26 p.37). That figure is Note 43's Debt Service Coverage Ratio variance, 14.36 against 10.32, copied under the wrong label; Note 43 carries no interest coverage line at all (AR FY26 p.112). A true EBIT over finance cost reading falls about 84 percent, from 4.03 times to 0.629 times.

Management says one thing and the same document shows another. The no concall delivery check graded credibility D on the widest evidence base the run has assembled: 0 of 5 testable annual report promises fully delivered, 2 partial, 3 missed. Research and development spend was cut 67.1 percent in extrusion and 78.6 percent in Geon in the year the report says Geon accelerated its research (AR FY26 p.28-29). The ~40 percent market share claim was dropped with no replacement figure and no acknowledgement. The chairman's letter reports EBITDA of Rs 10 Cr while the management discussion table in the same report says Rs 13.05 Cr. The FY26 letter drops PAT entirely and never uses the word loss (AR FY26 p.4-5 against AR FY25 p.4). The Hero Electric receivable note is still dated as at 31-March-2025 in a 170 page FY26 report. Two findings added by the rerun change how the comparative year reads. FY25 profit carried an exceptional gain of Rs 848.98 lakh on the Penta divestment, 20.25 percent of reported FY25 pre tax profit of Rs 4,192.26 lakh and 25.39 percent of pre tax profit before exceptional items, and the FY25 chairman's letter reports the resulting PAT without naming it (AR FY25 p.67, Note 32 p.96, p.4). And Rs 421.14 lakh moved silently between employee benefits expense and other expenses in the FY25 comparative, exactly offsetting to the paisa in both the standalone and the consolidated columns, disclosed only by generic regrouping boilerplate at Note 47 (AR FY25 p.67 and p.121 against AR FY26 p.67 and p.122; Note 47 p.113). On that restated base, other expense growth reads 15.1 percent instead of 8.6 percent and employee cost growth reads 20.4 percent instead of 28.9 percent. The prior year's report shows the same failure mode: it prints three different FY25 PAT figures, Rs 34 Cr in the management discussion table, Rs 32 Cr in the prose on the same page, and Rs 33.9 Cr in the chairman's letter, against an audited standalone figure of Rs 33.87 Cr (AR FY25 p.38, p.4, p.67). Accounting quality was cut from 4 of 10 to 3 of 10 on the strength of these two items. Against all of it, the audit opinion is clean and unqualified on both statements, CARO reports no fraud and no borrowing default, there is no going concern paragraph, related party transactions run at about 0.48 percent of revenue, and expected credit loss cover rose with the receivable deterioration rather than lagging it. Nothing found points to deliberate earnings management. It points to a close process that does not re check its own totals.

Peers do not confirm the company's story; they undercut it, and the rerun sharpened two of the readings. Of ten claims tested, none is verified outright, two are partially verified, one is partially contradicted, three are contradicted and four cannot be tested. Rajoo Engineers, the closest direct comparator, reported an order book above Rs 200 Cr with a Rs 1,000 Cr pipeline and never mentioned Jal Jeevan Mission across four calls, which sits badly against the company's explanation that infrastructure disbursement delays caused its segment revenue decline. Rajoo's export share rose to 73 percent of first half FY25 revenue and it called exports the margin source, against the company's framing of exports as a sector wide weakness that cut its own export revenue 11.6 percent. Rajoo expected to fill its installed capacity in FY26 while the company halved capex from Rs 61.81 Cr to Rs 29.51 Cr. The ~40 percent market share claim now has a number against it: Rajoo's own quantified domestic market sizing of Rs 1,500 Cr to Rs 2,000 Cr puts Rs 314.89 Cr of extrusion revenue at 16 to 21 percent of the market, so the claim moved from untestable to partially contradicted (RAJOOENG Q4 FY24, 18-Apr-2024, PDF p.11; Q2 FY25, 22-Oct-2024). The rerun also added the working capital contrast the first pass never tested. Rajoo takes 35 to 40 percent advance on order finalisation, states full payment before dispatch in 95 percent of cases, and reported 22 receivable days, against a company book with 48.9 percent of gross receivables more than one year overdue (RAJOOENG Q2 FY24, 6-Nov-2023, PDF p.10, p.11, p.12; AR FY26 Note 9 p.88). That contrast rests on one peer, so it is recorded as partially verified, not verified. The staleness caveat matters as much as any finding: the newest peer earnings call is 22-Oct-2024, about 17 months before the FY26 close, so every contradiction tests framing plausibility, not FY26 figures. The one corpus source that does reach FY26, the Windsor Machines screening file showing sales up 72.9 percent to Rs 566.52 Cr, is now used with caveats, and the same file shows operating cash flow of minus Rs 59.09 Cr in FY26 and borrowings up 147 percent to Rs 86.04 Cr.

The market is large enough and the company is not taking it. The total addressable market sizes to Rs 6,135 Cr conservative and Rs 10,480 Cr realistic, serviceable market Rs 6,135 Cr, obtainable market Rs 677.8 Cr over three years and Rs 963.9 Cr over five, with the runway class recorded as STRONG and revenue headroom of 13.6 times. Both management market claims failed their own consistency test. The FY26 report cites an India EV battery pack market of Rs 507.5 Cr, smaller than Geon's own Rs 136.11 Cr of segment revenue implies at any credible share. The FY25 ~40 percent share claim back calculates to a machinery sub market of Rs 787.5 Cr, between 0.19 and 0.47 times the independent estimates of Rs 1,685 Cr to Rs 4,200 Cr. Both market sizing stages ran with status partial because web fetches to the primary research houses were blocked.

The promoter read came back CAUTION, also on partial status because web fetches were blocked. One old adverse item exists: a February 2021 SEBI consent settlement against Ekta Anand Kabra, now Vice Chairperson and Managing Director, disgorging Rs 72,500 plus Rs 10,679 interest at 12 percent a year, settled without admission of guilt. Five senior management and key managerial exits landed within about six months, covering the CFO twice over, the Geon chief operating officer, the head of strategy and alliances and the company secretary, alongside the downgrades and the loss year. The chairman and managing director's pay rose 6.6 percent from Rs 205.02 lakh to Rs 218.64 lakh in the year the standalone result swung from a Rs 33.87 Cr profit to a Rs 2.44 Cr loss, and the report's own remuneration annexure states that increase as zero. On the other side, promoter pledge is nil across FY24 to FY26, related party dealing is small and clean, the patriarch's personal stake was wound down from 7.86 percent to zero into a family trust with the handover disclosed year by year, and a new independent director appointed in August 2025 runs a battery casings maker, an appointment that fits the pivot. A 2026 preferential issue of about Rs 120 Cr to Rs 141 Cr at Rs 375 a share to Singularity Large Value Fund III and Nitish Mittersain is media reported only, sits outside the document corpus, and its extraordinary general meeting reportedly fell on 2-Sep-2026, three days before this run. One independent director, Utpal Sheth, is a named personal allottee in that issue, approved by the board he sits on, with the allocation size undisclosed in any available source.

The run carried four verification priorities and closes three of them. The ~40 percent market share claim is self reported, appears once in the FY25 report at p.37 with no source and no defined denominator, was dropped from FY26 entirely, and is now partially contradicted by the peer's own market sizing at 16 to 21 percent. Geon is confirmed as both the loss carrier and, on a longer view, a shrinking one: revenue halved between FY24 and FY25, the FY26 rebound of 7.2 percent runs off that halved base and on a changed basis, the loss widened 69.8 percent to Rs 43.35 Cr, and the division ties up Rs 311.80 Cr of net segment capital against the machine division's Rs 242.31 Cr. The FY26 consolidated loss is confirmed at Rs 5.37 Cr and sits alongside the two step CRISIL downgrade, but the fourth leg of that priority failed again: the rating rationale is not in the corpus and no stage could obtain it. Borrowings, cash and working capital all moved the wrong way, with borrowings up from Rs 73.98 Cr in FY23 to Rs 145.06 Cr in FY26, cash at Rs 3.08 Cr on the screener consolidated basis, and working capital days up 90 in two years.
