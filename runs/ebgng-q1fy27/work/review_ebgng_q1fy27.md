# OPERATOR BRIEF (plain language) — GNG Electronics (EBGNG) — Q1 FY27

## Summary
GNG Electronics had a strong June quarter, its first full year as a listed company. Consolidated revenue grew 32 percent from a year ago to 412 crore and profit rose 56 percent to 29 crore. Gross margin improved to 24.6 percent, well above the 17 percent level that would have broken the investment case. The growth beat the company's own guidance, and management then raised the full year targets, revenue to 30 percent and profit margin toward 1 percent. Most of the growth came from better pricing and mix, with volumes up 18 percent, and almost all of the margin gain came from the overseas refurbishing arms, not the India parent. The real worry is cash. A June filing carries no cash flow statement, but on the call the company said net debt rose about 100 crore in three months to 406 crore. That rise does not square with a small fall in inventory and flat working capital, which means the business most likely still burned cash this quarter despite the strong profit. So the long standing question of whether this business funds itself is still open, and it now leans slightly negative. Management raised guidance freely but would not give the two numbers that matter, blended margin at scale and a working capital or cash target. Governance items from last time, a related party tax demand and unaudited US subsidiaries that hold nearly half the profit, were not addressed on the call. The verdict stays proceed with caveats, and the stock is still not worth buying at today's price, about 55 times earnings against a fair value near 20 times. The one thing to watch is the September half year cash flow statement, which finally settles the cash question.

## Sector and competitive intelligence
The refurbished computer market is in a structural upswing. Memory and component prices have jumped five times since late 2025, pushing a basic new laptop from 40,000 to 48,000 rupees. New PC shipments are expected to fall about 11 percent worldwide this year, so more buyers are choosing tested, warrantied refurbished machines instead. India is the long runway, with only 55 to 60 million PCs against a billion phone users, and similar gaps across Africa and other young markets. GNG says it faces almost no organized competition in its niche and works with the top national distributors, Redington first and Ingram second. Its edge over rivals is trust and a one to three year warranty, which lets it price above a plain commodity refurbisher. The risks are real too, because the whole story rides on one macro tailwind in memory prices, and the big new computer brands, Dell, HP and Lenovo, still own the enterprise buyer. If component prices ever correct, volumes may hold but the pricing power that drives today's margin could fade.

## Business model, what is unique
GNG buys used and off lease laptops in bulk from banks and large companies when they retire their fleets, often at opportunistic prices. It refurbishes them at a hub in the UAE so they look and work like new, then sells them with a warranty through a distribution network across 49 countries. What is hard to copy is the combination of three things built over decades, deep sourcing relationships, in house refurbishment, and a wide global distribution reach. The model is asset light with no factories, but it is heavy on working capital, so cash sits in inventory rather than in machines. It also treats inventory as a weapon, buying ahead of rising prices and being the only player able to ship thousands of a single model in one go, which gives it genuine pricing power. That single supply ability, plus trust and warranty, is what lets it move away from being a commodity seller.
