## PASS 3 — PATTERN RE-READ (contradictions, cross-note checks, going-concern language)

Method: cross-checked Pass 1/Pass 2 figures against each other and against the main restated financial
statements and MD&A/Risk Factor sections for contradiction; searched the full extracted text for
"going concern", "material uncertainty", "qualified opinion", "emphasis of matter"; re-read the Quick
Pay narrative for operational (not just financial) entanglement.

- **Going concern**: NO going-concern qualification or material-uncertainty language found anywhere in
  the auditor's Examination Report or the restated financial statements. The single hit for "going
  concern" outside the slump-sale note (RHP p.16818, "transfer was executed as a going concern" — refers
  to the acquired business, not Millworks itself) is a listing-eligibility criterion statement confirming
  the company has NO audit qualification with respect to going concern (RHP, ~p.21505). 🟢 Clean, no
  going-concern language applicable to Millworks itself.
- **No numeric contradiction found** between the Section III "Summary" tables (pp.54-56) and the Section
  V Annexure tables (pp.191-236) for Contingent Liabilities, RPT, or Accounting Ratios — reconfirmed
  this pass, consistent with Pass 1's method note.
- **NEW (Pass 3) — operational entanglement with Quick Pay goes beyond the financial relationship
  already flagged.** RHP Risk Factor #4 (p.32) discloses the company depends on business partners,
  including Quick Pay Private Limited AND Big Bang Boom Solutions Private Limited ("BBB"), for execution
  of defence-sector projects. Company personnel perform "partial assembly and integration of drones... at
  business partners' facility," and under a "Bill-to-Ship-to" arrangement, "materials/sub-assembled
  products were directly dispatched from Supplier Facility to Customers" — i.e. goods can move supplier
  to customer without transiting the company's own premises. (RHP p.32-34) 🟡 Watch — this is a pattern
  finding, not a new number: it corroborates that the Quick Pay relationship (Pass 1 Finding #1, Pass 2
  Finding #2) is operationally captive, not merely a financial/RPT-adjacent one, and that the company's
  own personnel and inventory routing are embedded inside a counterparty relationship kept outside AS-18
  scope. This strengthens, rather than adds a new dollar figure to, the existing Quick Pay red flag —
  folded into Finding 1 below rather than counted as a fifteenth separate line.
- Restatement pattern (Pass 2 Finding 1) re-checked against the cash flow statement and P&L: the -21.4%
  FY24 net-worth cut and the CWIP-capitalisation-of-opex items (rent, salary) are internally consistent
  with the PPE note's roll-forward — no further contradiction surfaced.
- No prior-year number found restated a SECOND time between Pass 1's and Pass 2's citations (i.e. no
  "restatement of a restatement" detected).

No other material new findings from the pattern re-read. Proceeding to consolidation.

### D. FIVE QUESTIONS FOR MANAGEMENT

1. Why is Quick Pay Private Limited not treated as an AS-18 related party, given common shareholders, an equity investment funded by converting an existing loan, 47.02% revenue concentration, and company personnel working at Quick Pay's facility?
2. What is the commercial rationale, interest rate and repayment schedule for the ₹490.00L advance still outstanding to V3 Technologies at FY2026-end, and does it relate to the promoter-owned Peenya land the company plans to purchase?
3. What remediation has actually reduced the recurring bank drawing-power discrepancies and the worsening EPF/ESIC filing delays, given the pattern continues through the months immediately before the RHP despite repeated "strengthened accounts department" statements?
4. What is the nature, customer and collectability status of the new ₹2,300.00L FY2026 domestic services revenue line, and how much of the ₹13,868.68L FY2026 trade receivables balance relates specifically to Quick Pay?
5. What were the interest rate, tenor and origination date of the unsecured loans converted to equity for Quick Pay (₹575.06L) and for Sheila Bhaskar Mudbidri / Mayur Bhandari (₹293.5L, 1,544 shares) before conversion?

### E. NOTES-BASED RED FLAGS

- Quick Pay Private Limited excluded from AS-18 related-party disclosure despite common shareholders, a loan-to-equity funded investment, 47.02% revenue concentration, and captive operational integration (company personnel at Quick Pay's facility, Bill-to-Ship-to material flow).
- Trade receivables grew 20x in FY2026 to 93% of revenue, DSO ~340 days, zero doubtful-debt provision throughout, CFO -₹10.76cr against PBT +₹50.23cr.
- 13-line-item restatement cutting net worth -21.4% (FY24) / -3.3% (FY25), including rent and salary wrongly capitalised to CWIP — an expense-deferral pattern, not mere reclassification.
- Recurring, multi-year, multi-lender bank drawing-power/stock discrepancies, always attributed to generic "clerical error."
- V3 Technologies promoter-firm circularity: ₹707.74L PPE purchase settled via non-cash equity issuance to the four promoters personally, not flagged as an equity-settlement in the AS-18 note; ₹490-556L advances outstanding, no disclosed terms.
- Section 185 Companies Act violation confirmed by the statutory auditor (loans to related parties, FY24/FY25).
- Recurring statutory compliance delays across five domains (GST, EPF, ESIC, MSME payments, bank DP reporting), worsening into the RHP filing window.
- 45.8% of FY2026 finance cost is self-inflicted late-payment interest on the company's own tax and MSME dues, not cost of capital.
- ₹441.00L unrealised FX translation gain = 95.3% of Other Income, explicitly self-labelled "not related to Business Activity," with no formal hedging policy despite 27.5% export revenue.
- MSME Section 43B(h) disallowance of ₹1,009.68L in FY2026, a ~10x jump, with the company admitting it lacks a system to classify MSME creditors.

### F. ONE-LINE NOTES VERDICT

The notes reveal alarming accounting practices. Key concern: a 47%-concentration customer funded by a
converted loan and operationally captive to the company sits outside related-party disclosure while
receivables balloon to 93% of revenue with zero provisioning. Key strength: granular, largely consistent
note-level disclosure once cross-referenced across chapters, with no going-concern qualification and no
numeric contradiction found between duplicate tables. Overall accounting quality: 3/10.
