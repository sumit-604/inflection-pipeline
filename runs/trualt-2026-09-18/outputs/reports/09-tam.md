# Stage 9: TAM / SAM / SOM — TruAlt Bioenergy Ltd (TRUALT)
Run date: 2026-09-18. All figures in Rs Crore unless stated. Ethanol volumes
in crore litres (cr L) or KLPD as marked. Text route: "[page N]" = PDF page.

## SECTION 1: MARKET DEFINITION

### 1A Precise boundaries

- **Product scope (core TAM).** Fuel-grade ethanol sold under India's
  Ethanol Blended Petrol (EBP) programme, plus Extra Neutral Alcohol (ENA)
  sold to the potable-alcohol industry and ethanol sold for industrial/
  chemical use. This is 97.6% of TRUALT's FY26 revenue, the "Ethanol and
  other products" segment (B04-bizmodel.yaml, sourced AR p.89/109).
- **Excluded from the core TAM, sized separately in Section 2:**
  Compressed biogas (CBG, 2.4% of FY26 revenue) and Sustainable Aviation
  Fuel (SAF, pre-revenue, first cash flow guided FY28 per B07-emoat.yaml).
  Both are real but currently small or zero-revenue adjacencies; folding
  them into one blended TAM would overstate the market TRUALT actually
  monetises today. DDGS, liquid CO2 and dry ice are by-products of the
  ethanol process and are captured inside the ethanol realisation figure,
  not sized as a separate market.
- **Geographic scope.** India only. The EBP programme does not apply in
  Andaman & Nicobar and Lakshadweep (prospectus p.236). OMC allocation is
  distance-linked to the distillery ("economic linkages calculated as per
  the distance of the distillery ... to the OMC location", prospectus
  p.229), so a Karnataka-based producer's realistic addressable share of
  national demand is geographically constrained; this is folded into the
  SAM capacity filter in Section 3, not treated as a separate top-down
  percentage cut (no anchored South-India-specific demand split exists in
  this corpus).
- **Customer scope.** Public-sector OMCs (IOCL, BPCL, HPCL), private OMCs
  (Jio-bp, Nayara, Reliance-BP), the potable-alcohol industry (ENA buyers:
  John Distilleries, InBrew, Amrut, Elite Vintage per prospectus p.165),
  and industrial/chemical buyers.
- **Channel scope.** Government tender/allocation-based OMC supply
  contracts (annual ESY tender cycle, prospectus p.228-229) plus open-market
  ENA sale. TRUALT's own retail fuel outlets (7 operational, B04 input_gaps)
  are immaterial to this TAM and excluded.
- **Price segment.** Administered/negotiated procurement price, set per
  feedstock type per Ethanol Supply Year by GoI/OMCs (prospectus p.228-229,
  235). This is a price-taker market by design (B04 pricing_power:
  "price-taker").
- **Explicit inclusions:** 1G ethanol (sugarcane/syrup/molasses/maize/FCI
  rice routes), ENA, industrial ethanol.
- **Explicit exclusions:** 2G/cellulosic ethanol (a distinct, largely
  pre-commercial ~2,500-3,000 cr L national potential per AR p.53-54;
  TRUALT's own 2G plan is a ~6 cr L pre-revenue "going forward" vertical,
  prospectus p.284), CBG, SAF, retail fuel marketing.

### 1B Management's own TAM claim

Two separate management claims exist, for two different segments, and
they should not be merged:

1. **Ethanol (prospectus, filed 01-Oct-2025, p.230):** "By ESY 2025-26, at
   20% blending level, ethanol demand will increase to 1,016 crore litres.
   Therefore, the worth of the ethanol industry will jump by over 500% from
   around INR 9,000 crore to over INR 50,000 crore." Source cited: Ministry
   of Petroleum and Natural Gas / CRISIL. Date: 01-Oct-2025, 11 months old
   at run date, not stale under the 2-year rule. **Credibility read:
   reasonable** — the Rs9,000cr base-year figure is undated and unsourced
   within the quoted sentence, but the Rs50,000cr forward figure holds up
   against this stage's own bottom-up cross-check (Section 2).
2. **CBG (Q1FY27 Investor Presentation, filed 28-Jul-2026, p.31):**
   "Dedicated CBG policy framework that could unlock a ~USD 30 billion
   investment opportunity, with domestic CBG production projected to grow
   nearly sevenfold by 2030." Date: 28-Jul-2026, 2 months old, fresh.
   **Credibility read: broad.** This conflates a national *investment*
   (capex) opportunity with a *revenue* TAM, the two are not the same
   number, and the figure is not tied to a stated methodology or year. Set
   against an independent CBG market-size report found this stage
   (marketsandata.com, via WebSearch, accessed 2026-09-18): India CBG
   market valued at USD 15.64 million in FY2024, forecast USD 40.14 million
   by FY2032 (12.5% CAGR) — a revenue-market figure roughly 750-1,900x
   smaller than management's USD 30bn framing. Even allowing that "capex
   opportunity" and "revenue market" are different units, a gap this large
   is a promotional claim, not a sizing input. **FLAG-TAM-MGMT-CBG-CLAIM.**

## SECTION 2: TAM ESTIMATION, MULTIPLE METHODS

All core-TAM methods below size the **ethanol/ENA/industrial market**
(Section 1A core scope). CBG and SAF are sized separately at the end of
this section as adjacencies, consistent with 1A.

### Method 1 — Top-down (national ethanol demand x realisation)

- National ethanol demand for blending, ESY 2025-26F: **1,016 cr L**
  (prospectus p.230, source Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas).
  Total ethanol demand (blending + other uses: potable, industrial,
  chemical): **1,350 cr L** (prospectus p.23/231, "Ethanol demand in India
  is expected to grow at a CAGR of 17.7% from 828 crore litres in Fiscal
  2023 to 1,350 crore litres by 2026"). Independently corroborated by
  WebSearch (accessed 2026-09-18, PIB press release, Jul-2026): "ethanol
  procurement grew from approximately 38 crore litres in ESY 2013-14 to
  over 1,200 crore litres in 2025-26" and confirms 20% blending achieved,
  a fresh (2-month-old) government-sourced figure in the same range.
- Weighted-average realisation, using the ESY 24-25 feedstock realisation
  table (prospectus p.235: sugarcane/syrup Rs65.61/L, B-heavy Rs60.73/L,
  C-heavy Rs57.97/L, damaged foodgrains Rs64.00/L, FCI rice Rs58.50/L,
  maize Rs71.86/L) weighted by the FY23 national feedstock-mix shares
  (prospectus p.232: C-heavy 2%, B-heavy 33%, sugar/syrup 53%, DFG/maize
  6%, FCI rice 6%): **~Rs63.8/litre**.
- **Total-demand TAM (realistic):** 1,350 cr L x Rs63.8/L = **~Rs86,100 cr**.
- **Blending-only TAM at feedstock price floor (conservative):** 1,016 cr L
  x Rs58/L (C-heavy floor) = **~Rs58,900 cr**.
- Confidence: M (single primary source family — CRISIL/MoPNG via the
  prospectus — cross-checked by one independent, fresher PIB figure).
  Not stale.

### Method 2 — Bottom-up (installed capacity x realisation, full-penetration ceiling)

- India's installed ethanol production capacity, FY26: **~1,995 cr L/year**
  (AR p.73, "the country's installed ethanol production capacity has
  reached approximately 1,995 crore litres per annum, of which around
  1,002 crore litres is grain-based"). WebSearch cross-check (accessed
  2026-09-18): "ethanol production capacity in India has increased ...
  to approximately 2,000 crore litres in 2026", consistent.
- **Full-penetration TAM ceiling** = 1,995 cr L x Rs63.8/L = **~Rs1,27,300
  cr**. This is a physical ceiling, not a demand estimate: Stage 6 (peer
  concall verification) found TRIVENI and BALRAMCHIN independently and
  repeatedly describing this as *structural overcapacity*, with national
  offtake running only ~1,100-1,300 cr L against ~2,000-2,300 cr L of
  capacity (TRIVENI-Concall_Aug_2026_Transcript.pdf p.10-11). Confidence:
  **L** — the ceiling is real, but peer evidence says it will not clear
  without a blending mandate well above 20% or a wave of distillery exits.

### Method 3 — Peer/market-structure cross-check (no true "unorganised" segment)

Ethanol is a licensed, government-administered industry (OMC tender
allocation, administered pricing); the standard India "unorganised sector
adds 30-60%" instruction does not transpose cleanly here, since supply is
gated by licence and tender participation, not informal trade. The
relevant structure check instead is concentration: prospectus p.232
states the market is "characterized by a high degree of fragmentation",
with TRUALT the single largest player at **3.6% of national installed
capacity, FY25** — the leader, at under 4% share, confirms fragmentation
and cross-checks Method 1's scale (a market this fragmented cannot be a
small number if TRUALT alone is already a multi-hundred-crore revenue
business at under 4% share). Exact top-3 aggregate concentration is
**NOT FOUND** in this corpus. Confidence: M, qualitative.

### Method 4 — Import substitution

This lever is largely **already spent**, not a fresh source of TAM growth.
Blending reached 19.93-20.0% by ESY 2025-26 (AR p.73 table; prospectus
p.230), five years ahead of the original 2030 target, with cumulative
forex savings of "more than INR 1.10 lakh crore" (prospectus p.239) — a
completed substitution, not forward headroom. WebSearch (accessed
2026-09-18, PIB, Jul-2026) confirms and updates this to "more than INR
1.90 lakh crore" saved since 2014-15, corroborating that the 20% milestone
is achieved and stable. Forward TAM growth from here rides two separate
levers instead: (a) national petrol-consumption volume growth (base
~5,500 cr L/year, concall Aug-2026 p.?, each 1pp of blending = 55-56 cr L,
Stage 5 report), and (b) blending intensity beyond E20 (BIS has notified
E22/E25/E27/E30 standards, AR p.31, but **no committed government volume
or date target for blending above 20% is found in this corpus** — NOT
FOUND, aspirational only at run date). This method is used as a Section
4A growth-driver input, not a fresh ₹ TAM figure.

### Method 5 — Global benchmark

The prospectus's own claim: India is "potentially making it the third
largest market for ethanol by 2026" (prospectus p.[page marker near line
14345], source CRISIL), implicitly behind the US and Brazil. No explicit
per-capita or absolute comparative sizing table is present in this corpus
to quantify an implied India TAM from the benchmark. **Directional only,
confidence L.**

### Adjacent TAM 1 — CBG (not in core TAM, per 1A)

- SATAT's aspirational ceiling: 15 MMTPA CBG by 2025 (missed; target date
  already passed at run date), at the IOCL procurement price table's top
  slab, Rs78.01/kg with GST (prospectus p.253) = 15,000,000 tonnes x
  Rs78,010/tonne = **~Rs1,17,015 cr**, a distant policy ceiling, not a
  current or near-term realistic figure — current installed capacity is
  under 1% of this target (prospectus p.251).
- Current realistic CBG market: WebSearch (accessed 2026-09-18,
  pelletrates.com/industry trackers) cites 133 operational plants, 926 TPD
  combined output as of Jan-2026. At 330 operating days/year: 926 TPD x
  330 = 305,580 tonnes/year x Rs78,010/tonne = **~Rs2,384 cr** (annualised
  installed-capacity basis). A second cross-check on *actually sold*
  volume: 94 plants sold 31,423 tonnes (prospectus p.251, likely FY24) x
  Rs78,010/tonne = **~Rs245 cr** (actual, not capacity-annualised). The
  ~10x spread between these two readings shows how far current utilisation
  sits below even the *current* installed base, let alone the SATAT ceiling.
- **STALE/inconsistency flag:** the IOCL price table in the prospectus
  (undated "IOCL Notice", cited inside the Oct-2025 prospectus, p.253,
  top slab Rs78.01/kg with GST) is contradicted by TRUALT's own Aug-2026
  concall, which cites CBG priced at **Rs84-85/kg** under the current
  SATAT scheme (Stage 5/6 reports). Unreconciled in this corpus.

### Adjacent TAM 2 — SAF (not in core TAM, per 1A; pre-revenue)

- National SAF blending requirement, by government-mandated blending
  target: **14 cr L at 1% (2027), 28 cr L at 2% (2028), 70 cr L at 5%
  (2030)** (AR p.75 table, sourced CRISIL Report via AR text). TRUALT's
  planned facility is 10 cr L/annum (100 million litres, prospectus p.284
  and Investor Presentation Q1FY27 p.30 both confirm), which, if the
  national 2030 target is met and fully delivered as planned, would be
  **~14.3% of the entire national SAF requirement in 2030** from one
  plant — a genuinely large single-plant share of a still-tiny national
  market, and a measure of how immature this market is today, not a
  realistic near-term SOM claim.
- **Rs/litre price for SAF in the Indian market is NOT FOUND in this
  corpus.** AR p.76 states SAF production costs "estimated to be two to
  five times higher than conventional aviation turbine fuel." WebSearch
  (accessed 2026-09-18) found current ATF at ~Rs121.28/litre (Sep-2026,
  univest.in) against a government price cap near Rs115/litre. Applying
  the AR's own 2-5x multiplier to this ATF anchor gives a **derived, low-
  confidence** SAF price range of **~Rs230-605/litre**, implying a
  national 2030 SAF TAM of **~Rs16,100-42,400 cr** (70 cr L x range).
  This is a derived estimate built on a web-sourced ATF price, not a
  filed SAF price; flagged PENDING LIVE VERIFICATION and excluded from
  the headline TAM/SAM/SOM funnel.

## SECTION 3: SAM & SOM

### 3A SAM (core ethanol/ENA market)

Applying the five filters to the realistic TAM (Rs86,100 cr):

1. **Product fit:** no subtraction — TRUALT's 1G ethanol/ENA output is
   already the entire scope of the 1,350 cr L national demand figure (2G
   is a separate, still-near-zero-volume category, AR p.53-54).
2. **Geography:** OMC allocation is distance-linked to the distillery
   (prospectus p.229). No anchored top-down South-India demand split
   exists; this filter is folded into the capability/capacity filter
   below via TRUALT's own realised allocation history rather than a
   separate top-down percentage cut.
3. **Channel/customer:** no material subtraction — TRUALT already sells
   to both public and private OMCs and to the ENA/potable market.
4. **Capability/capacity — the binding filter.** TRUALT's own installed
   ethanol capacity is 2,000 KLPD (1,800 KLPD operational per AR/
   prospectus). Converting: 2,000 KLPD x 365 days = 730,000 KL/year =
   **~73 cr L/year nameplate**; 1,800 KLPD operational = **~65.7 cr L/year**.
   This brackets almost exactly against management's own stated monthly
   capability of "5.5 to 6 crore litres of ethanol every month" (Investor
   Presentation Q1FY27 p.8) = 66-72 cr L/year. **Take the midpoint, ~70 cr
   L/year, as the capacity ceiling.**
5. **Price/realisation:** TRUALT's own blended realisation, not the
   national average, is the right price to apply to its own capacity
   ceiling: ~Rs67-74/litre (concall Aug-2026 p.8; blended average across
   feedstocks and ENA/DDGS uplift). **Midpoint ~Rs70/litre.**

**SAM = 70 cr L x Rs70/litre = ~Rs4,900 cr.**
**SAM as % of realistic TAM (Rs86,100 cr): ~5.7%.**

Cross-check: TRUALT's own disclosed FY25 national capacity share is 3.6%
(prospectus p.232); the SAM/TAM ratio here (5.7%) is modestly higher
because it applies TRUALT's own better-than-national-average realisation
(grain/FCI-rice/ENA mix) rather than the national blended price — a
reasonable, evidenced gap, not an unexplained one.

### 3B SOM at 3 and 5 years

**Current base (FY26):** ethanol-segment revenue = 97.6% of FY26 total
revenue (B04-bizmodel.yaml, AR p.89/109) x Rs1,727.51 cr (REVENUE_ANCHOR,
screener-Data_Sheet.csv) = **~Rs1,686 cr**.

**Current SAM share:** Rs1,686 cr / Rs4,900 cr = **34.4%.** This is
consistent with (not identical to, given the differing denominators used
across disclosures — Stage 5 separately flags that TRUALT's own reported
utilisation percentage shifts between "95% on operating days", "~60%
gross-quarter" and "<35% run-rate" without reconciliation) the FY25
prospectus-disclosed capacity utilisation of 45.08% (prospectus p.164)
and the general 35-60% range management itself has cited.

Share-gain trajectory, applying the standard rules with a skeptical
overlay from Stage 5 (management credibility grade **C**, "Poor" on
quantified-guidance delivery) and Stage 6 (peer-confirmed **structural**
national overcapacity, not TRUALT-specific):

- **3-year (~FY29):** TRUALT's own FY27 order book already implies ~60%
  utilisation of the ~70 cr L nameplate (44 cr L base case, concall
  Aug-2026, Stage 5 1B/3D). Given peer evidence that *national* offtake
  runs only ~50-57% of *national* installed capacity (TRIVENI Q1FY27
  concall, Stage 6 Q4), hold utilisation flat at **60%** through FY29
  rather than assume further gains — the aggressive share-gain rule
  (3-5pp/yr) is not invoked given the credibility grade and the sector-
  wide, not company-specific, nature of the constraint.
  SOM 3yr = 60% x 70 cr L x Rs70/L = **~Rs2,940 cr**.
- **5-year (~FY31):** modest improvement to **70% utilisation** as
  blending intensity gradually escalates beyond E20 (Section 4A), still
  well short of management's own 80-85% target (which this stage does not
  adopt uncritically, given the C-grade delivery history).
  SOM 5yr = 70% x 70 cr L x Rs70/L = **~Rs3,430 cr**.

**Implied revenue CAGR (arithmetic):**
- 3yr: (2,940 / 1,686)^(1/3) - 1 = **~20.4%**
- 5yr: (3,430 / 1,686)^(1/5) - 1 = **~15.3%**

The deceleration from 20.4% to 15.3% is intentional and evidenced: it is
front-loaded on ramping *already-installed, already-delivered* multi-feed
capacity (B04/B07: multi-feed conversion is a **delivered** capability),
then decelerates as the national overcapacity ceiling binds.

### 3C Capacity cross-check

B07's capex_embedded_growth_pct is **7.7%** (Note 56 contractually-
committed capex only; CBG ~Rs760cr and SAF ~Rs2,000-2,250cr are guided but
uncommitted, excluded by design). This figure and the SOM CAGRs above
(15.3-20.4%) are **not in tension**: the SOM growth modelled here comes
entirely from raising *utilisation* of capacity TRUALT has *already
built* (the 2,000 KLPD multi-feed platform), which requires **no further
capex**. The nameplate ceiling (~Rs4,900 cr at 100% utilisation and
today's realisation) sits well above even the 5-year SOM ask (Rs3,430 cr,
only ~70% utilisation).

**Capacity check: SUFFICIENT.** Installed capacity can physically produce
the SOM revenue without new capex. The optimistic assumption in this
funnel is **not** the capex plan; it is the **demand/allocation**
assumption — specifically, that utilisation holds at 60-70% against a
peer-confirmed backdrop of national industry offtake running at only
~50-57% of installed capacity (Stage 6). If OMC allocation does not clear
that bar, the SOM figures above are the optimistic side, not the capacity
build.

### 5B Runway assessment

- **Revenue headroom** = SAM / current (ethanol-segment) revenue =
  Rs4,900cr / Rs1,686cr = **~2.91x**.
- **TAM growth rate (historical):** 17.7% CAGR, FY23-FY26F (prospectus
  p.23). This reflects the now-largely-completed 12%-to-20% blending ramp;
  **forward TAM growth is likely materially slower** — the primary lever
  (import substitution via blending escalation) is mostly spent (Method
  4). No anchored forward industry CAGR beyond FY26 exists in this corpus
  — NOT FOUND.
- **Company CAGR vs TAM:** TRUALT's own restated ethanol-sale revenue grew
  from Rs700.8cr (FY23) to Rs956.4cr (FY24) to Rs1,433.9cr (FY25)
  (prospectus p.165) — roughly 43% CAGR over that window, well ahead of
  the market's 17.7%, i.e. TRUALT was gaining share via capacity build,
  not merely riding the market. FY26 standalone revenue then declined
  9.33% (B04-bizmodel.yaml), which B04 attributes to a planned three-plant
  shutdown for multi-feed conversion, not demand loss — the two-period
  comparison should not be read as a trend reversal without that context.
- **Years to saturate SAM** at the modelled 5yr trajectory (~15.3%
  terminal CAGR): from SOM5yr (Rs3,430cr) to full SAM (Rs4,900cr) is
  ln(4,900/3,430)/ln(1.153) = **~2.5 more years**, i.e. **~7.5 years from
  FY26** to exhaust the current-capacity SAM ceiling absent fresh capex —
  consistent with 3C's finding that capacity is not the constraint.

### 5C Runway classification

**MODERATE.** Revenue headroom (~2.91x) sits at the boundary between the
GOOD and MODERATE bands on a standard working matrix (MASSIVE >10x,
STRONG 5-10x, GOOD 3-5x, MODERATE 1.5-3x, LIMITED <1.5x; exact cut-points
are not specified in the injected stage instructions and are stated here
for transparency). Given the peer-confirmed structural overcapacity
(Section 4B) actively working against the growth side of this ratio, this
stage classifies conservatively at **MODERATE** rather than GOOD.

### 5D SAM expansion levers actually being pursued

- **CBG (Sumitomo TGPL + GAIL Leafiniti JVs, committed capex per B07).**
  Combined nameplate across the 10 identified plants: 132-152 TPD
  (unreconciled across disclosures, B07 flag). At the 140 TPD midpoint x
  330 operating days x Rs78,010/tonne (prospectus slab price) =
  **~Rs360 cr** of potential CBG revenue at full commissioning. **Revised
  SAM with CBG converted: Rs4,900cr + Rs360cr = ~Rs5,260cr (~7% uplift).**
  Small next to the core ethanol number at current disclosed plant counts,
  despite management's promotional framing (Section 1B).
- **SAF (pre-revenue until FY28 per B07).** If TRUALT's planned 10 cr L
  facility is built and the national 2030 target (70 cr L) is met, TRUALT
  would hold ~14.3% of the entire national SAF market from one plant — a
  genuinely large potential addition given SAF's derived Rs230-605/litre
  price range (Section 2), materially higher than ethanol's ~Rs70/litre.
  This is the single most interesting **unquantified** upside in this
  analysis, and it is excluded from the core SOM by design (pre-revenue,
  no committed capex per B07) to avoid crediting the same optionality
  through two mechanisms (CLAUDE.md).

## Analyst note

The single most consequential finding this stage adds to the run is that
**capacity is not TRUALT's TAM constraint; OMC allocation is.** The
already-built 2,000 KLPD multi-feed platform can physically produce the
full 5-year SOM at only ~70% utilisation, with no further capex (Section
3C) — yet Stage 6's peer evidence shows the *national* market clearing
allocation at only ~50-57% of installed capacity, a structural, not
TRUALT-specific, ceiling. That reframes the transition thesis: the
delivered capex (B04/B07: multi-feed conversion, a real, completed
capability) is not the open question; the open question is whether OMC
allocation ever lets TRUALT sell what it can already make. Management's
two headline market-opportunity claims diverge sharply in credibility:
the ethanol claim (Rs50,000cr, prospectus) is reasonable and holds up
against independent cross-checks; the CBG claim ("~USD 30 billion
opportunity") is off by roughly three orders of magnitude against the one
independent market-size estimate found and should be read as promotional
framing, not a sizing input, when this run reaches Stage 11 and Stage 13.
