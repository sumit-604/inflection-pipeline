PATTERN READ — IS THIS ONE PATTERN OR SEVERAL?
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

The task brief asks directly whether the six cross-stage items (freight gross-up,
associate reconciliation, RPT concentration, MD&A/cash-flow gap, the Rs 243.87 lakh
reclassification, and the ROE/ROCE restatement) are separate findings or one pattern.
Reading them together against the full notes a third time surfaces THREE distinct
patterns, not one, plus one item (the associate reconciliation) that remains genuinely
resolved and should not be folded into any pattern as a negative.

**PATTERN A — the audited Notes are consistently the LAST place a material number
appears, not the first, and the AR's other sections do not tie to them.**
Three independent instances, found across three different sections of the same
Annual Report:
1. The freight gross-up is disclosed ONLY in the Reg 33 exchange results filing
   (2026-05-30_FY26_audited_results.txt, line 385) — absent from Note 23 Revenue,
   Note 1.2.2(f) Revenue Recognition policy, and Note 30 Other Expenses, all of which
   are the natural homes for it (Pass 1, LBF-1).
2. The MD&A states the FY26 investing outflow as Rs 3,441.49 lakh (p.104); the audited
   Cash Flow Statement, in the SAME Annual Report, states Rs 3,254.59 lakh (p.117
   standalone, p.162 consolidated) — a Rs 186.90 lakh gap between two sections of one
   document (Pass 1, LBF-4).
3. The 1:4 bonus issue recommended 03-Sep-2026 — a significant corporate action —
   receives full treatment in the Directors' Report and the AGM Notice (extracted text
   lines 808-866, 1538-1595) but NO subsequent-events note in the audited financial
   statements. Note 49 (consolidated, the only "events after balance sheet date" note
   in the AR) contains only the procedural board-approval-date statement, nothing on
   the bonus issue (Pass 1 category sweep). Confirmed in this pass: no note titled or
   labelled "subsequent events" beyond Note 49's one line exists in either the
   standalone or consolidated note sets.
Taken together this is a genuine pattern, not three coincidences: the Company's other
filings (Reg 33 results, MD&A, Directors' Report/AGM Notice) consistently carry
information that the audited Notes either omit entirely or state at a different figure.
None of the three, examined individually, rises to fraud — the bonus issue is
correctly outside the FY26 financial-statement period (it is a post year-end Board
recommendation, so its absence from Note 49 is defensible under Ind AS 10 unless it
was approved before the financial statements were authorised for issue, which the AR
timeline confirms it was not: board approval 30-May-2026, bonus recommended
03-Sep-2026). But the freight and cash-flow instances ARE both squarely within the
audited FY26 period and both matter to a reader relying on the Notes alone. This
pattern is carried into the consolidated top findings as a disclosure-transparency
dimension issue, not as three separate accusations.

**PATTERN B — the SAME historical year's ratios move between two Annual Reports, with
no reconciliation note in either, and the mechanism differs each time.**
Two instances, both isolated to FY25 as reported in AR_FY2025 versus FY25 as shown in
AR_FY2026's comparative column:
1. The Rs 243.87 lakh reclassification (Other Financial Liabilities -> Trade
   Payables, with a new Employee Payables line carved out) — fully traced by Pass 2,
   balance-sheet grand total unaffected, moves Trade Payables Turnover 18.06x -> 25.52x
   and Net Capital Turnover 1.08x -> 2.73x for the identical year.
2. FY25 ROE and ROCE, on BOTH standalone and consolidated bases, differ across the two
   ARs even though FY25 PAT, Total Equity, and Total Debt are IDENTICAL in both —
   meaning the ratio DENOMINATOR build (Average Shareholder's Equity / Capital
   Employed) changed, not any reported line item. Pass 2 confirmed this is a distinct
   mechanism from the trade-payables reclassification (that reclassification cannot
   move a numerator built from PAT and cannot move Total Equity, since both are
   unchanged), so this is a SECOND, separate undisclosed restatement, not a downstream
   consequence of the first.
This pass's incremental contribution: both instances share a structural feature worth
naming as the pattern — Note 45's own "Reasons for Variance" commentary in the FY26 AR
explains YEAR-ON-YEAR movements (FY26 vs FY25) using a FY25 base that has silently
shifted from what the company's own FY25 Annual Report reported. A reader who trusts
the FY26 AR's trend commentary at face value, without independently pulling the FY25
AR, cannot detect either shift. This is the single most important pattern-level finding
of this pass: it means Note 45's narrative interpretation of its own numbers (not just
the numbers) is unreliable as a standalone read, for TWO different ratios families
(liquidity/turnover and returns), via two different and unexplained mechanisms. That
breadth, more than either instance alone, is what elevates this from a one-off
oddity to a disclosure-quality pattern worth a dedicated red flag.

**PATTERN C — related-party dependency is growing on the COST side, shrinking on the
REVENUE side, and the notes give no pricing benchmark for either direction.**
Cost of materials consumed from related parties rose from 31.0% to 37.1% of the total
(Note 41), led by Phoenix Bio Sciences at 26.0% and +73.6% YoY, while revenue FROM
related parties fell 18.9% and its share of total revenue fell from 3.8% to 2.3%
(Pass 1). Read together as one line rather than two: the related-party relationship is
becoming more important to the Company as a SUPPLIER dependency and less important as
a customer relationship — the opposite of what a "related-party diversion of profit to
promoter entities via sales" story would look like (that story would need revenue TO
related parties rising, not falling). This pass's contribution is to state plainly what
this pattern rules out and what it does not: it rules out profit-shifting via
related-party SALES; it does NOT address whether Phoenix Bio Sciences' PURCHASE prices
to the Company are at arm's length, because no pricing benchmark, cost-plus
disclosure, or third-party comparable is given anywhere in the notes for the Rs
2,574.32 lakh (Rs 25.74 cr) of FY26 purchases from that one entity. The concentration
and growth rate are the fact; arm's-length-ness is unverifiable from this document and
remains the single largest open question for live verification.

**Associate reconciliation (LBF-2) — confirmed a non-pattern, kept out of the above.**
Re-checked a third time in this pass for any residual gap: the AOC-1 (Rs 651.12 lakh),
Note 5 (Rs 239.91 lakh addition), and consolidated P&L (Rs 243.80 lakh total
associate-profit line, both associates) tie out to the rupee once the AOC-1 column is
correctly read as the associate's OWN full profit rather than TBL's proportionate
share. This is a labelling clarity issue in the AOC-1 disclosure, not a numerical
inconsistency, and it does not belong in Pattern A (nothing is omitted; the AOC-1 IS in
the AR, Directors' Report Annexure) or Pattern B (no cross-year ratio consequence).
It stays a standalone 🟡 WATCH item for the label-clarity point, carried at a lower rank
than the three patterns above.

## E. NOTES-BASED RED FLAGS

- **Aggressive accounting presentation, not fabrication:** the freight gross-up
  inflates reported revenue growth optics (31.8% reported vs ~28.0% like-for-like) via
  a presentation choice disclosed only outside the audited notes. No evidence this
  changes profit (freight is added to both revenue and expenses, net-zero to PBT), but
  it is a choice that flatters a headline growth number the market watches.
- **Undisclosed comparator instability across Annual Reports (earnings-management-
  adjacent, not confirmed as earnings management):** two independent, unreconciled
  changes to the SAME FY25 year's figures across two Annual Reports (the Rs 243.87
  lakh reclassification and the ROE/ROCE restatement), both of which happen to make
  FY26's reported trend commentary look more favourable against the restated FY25 base
  than it would against the originally-reported FY25 base (trade payables turnover
  decline reads as -32.4% against the restated 25.52x base, versus a much smaller
  decline against the original 18.06x base). No evidence of intent is available from
  the documents; the pattern is worth flagging as a risk indicator, not asserting as
  proven manipulation.
- **Undisclosed risk indicator: related-party raw-material dependency growing without
  a pricing benchmark** (Rank 1), the single highest-priority item for live
  verification.
- **No evidence found of:** revenue fabrication, channel stuffing beyond the
  observable finished-goods/customer-advance build (itself plausibly explained by
  genuine order momentum per Q1FY27's 27% growth), asset diversion, off-balance-sheet
  liabilities, or going-concern risk.
