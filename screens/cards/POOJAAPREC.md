## Business Understanding Narrative

**What does it do, and how does it make money?** Poojaa melts aluminium alloy, die-casts it, machines it, and ships finished parts to OEMs and Tier-1s (Deck, p.9). 600 plus SKUs, from cylinder heads to switchgear conductors (Deck, pp.13-22). FY26 revenue Rs 293.86 crore, profit Rs 33.45 crore (AR26, p.56). 99% domestic (Deck, p.24). Metal cost is passed through (Call, p.6). The margin comes from casting and machining content, in-house dies, and critical machining kept inside (Call, p.17).

**What is changing?** Mix and size. Automotive fell to 63% of H1 FY27 revenue from 72.6% in FY26; non-automotive rose to 25% from 17.6%, and six months of energy revenue (Rs 48.2 crore) beat all of FY26 (Rs 31.0 crore) (Deck, pp.11-12, 41). Non-auto earned 60% of H1 margin (Call, p.9). Bigger parts are replacing 1 kg to 9 kg parts (Call, p.10). Unit III doubles capacity (RHP, p.97).

**Why now?** The IPO brought Rs 159.83 crore at 259 times subscription (Deck, p.18). Unit III began partial production on 30-Aug-2026 at 20% of plan (U3, pp.1-2). Management guided 50% revenue growth for FY27, 30% to 35% a year after, and Rs 1,000 crore by FY30 on a Rs 1,180 crore order book (Call, pp.5, 16-17; Deck, p.32).

**What must be true?** H2 FY27 must deliver about Rs 231 crore of revenue at a 12% PAT margin. Unit III must run fully by September 2027 (Deck, p.5). Energy tenders must repeat. Customer 1, 31.75% of FY26 sales, must stay (RHP, p.25). The promoter-group foundries supplying about Rs 35 crore of alloy a year must not become a leak (RHP, p.62).

**What breaks it?** A commercial-vehicle downturn: CVs were 53% of H1 FY27 revenue (Deck, p.40; derived). A miss on the first guidance the company ever gave. A multiple that has already paid for FY28.

## 2. Business model and archetype

Casting capacity rose from 3,900 MT in FY24 to 6,000 MT in FY26 at 84% use, and to 7,320 MT in H1 FY27 at 71% (Deck, p.31).

H1 FY27 revenue, Rs million (Deck, pp.40-41): automotive 1,334, of which CV 1,127; EV 250; non-auto 532, of which energy 482. Customer 1 was 31.75% of FY26 sales, down from 41.94% in FY24; the top five 74.72% (RHP, pp.25-26). 58 customers, up from 39 (Deck, p.4).

Gross margin fell to 52.6% in H1 FY27 from 61.0% while EBITDA margin held near 19%: bigger parts carry more pass-through metal and less offloaded machining (H1 res, p.5; derived; Call, p.10). The one engine is casting and machining content per part on OEM-approved dies.

**Archetype: Build-to-spec component maker.** Customer capex cycle, design wins from Rs 2,000 crore of RFQs a year at an 8% to 10% strike rate (Call, p.15), content per unit rising with part size, full input-cost pass-through. Amendment 17 does not bind; the metal spread is not the engine.

## 3. Competitive advantages

Qualification is present: IATF 16949, three ISO marks, and Tata Motors, Siemens, Toshiba and Mahindra supplier awards (Deck, p.29). In energy the company sits on every Fortune 500 panel, so it earns a share whoever wins the tender (Call, p.11). Integration is present: dies and gauges in-house (Deck, p.27). Pricing power is partial: metal is passed through, so the spread is protected, not expanded (Call, p.6). **Verdict: a qualification and tooling moat, a supplier moat, not a franchise.** Customer 1 at 31.75% holds the pricing pen.

## 6. The transition and the quality ladder

**FROM: R2, cost-advantaged converter with a spec'd layer.** A GDC foundry selling small auto parts, 42% to one customer, metal passed through.

**TO: R3, value-added spec'd supplier.** Larger import-substituting parts, seven end markets, in-house dies and critical machining, ROCE in the high twenties (AR26, p.49).

Management's claim, once: "we are moving up the engineering value chain through advanced components such as cylinder heads, motor housings and large precision-engineered parts that are currently imported" (Deck, p.5).

Ladder check. One rung, within the base rate. Aerospace and magnesium (Call, pp.7, 12) are stated, not climbed.

## 8. Proof check

The company reports half-yearly, so this is a two-half test on H2 FY26 and H1 FY27.

**The proof point is the mix shift with margin held. It is present.** Revenue ran 124.89, 168.97, 210.09 across three halves; PAT 13.29, 20.17, 24.93 (H1 res, p.5). Non-auto grew 194% year on year, EV 80% (Deck, p.12). EBITDA margin stayed near 19% while gross margin fell 4 points, so the mix explanation holds on the filed lines. Tax paid at 26.4%, no one-off (H1 res, p.5).

**What the halves do not prove.** Unit III contributed one month. The energy jump is tender-driven (Call, p.11). CV revenue was 53% of the half (Deck, p.40), a cyclical base.

## 11. Posture: the Transition Decision Matrix

- **Proof gate: FIRED.** Two halves of delivered mix shift with margin held and tax paid (step 8), on the existing plants, not on Unit III.
- **Ugliness: ARTIFACT-OF-CLIMB** on the optics the market reads: gross-margin compression from bigger parts, utilisation at 71% on new capacity, an SME listing under Indian GAAP. Two items are STRUCTURAL and stay as tripwires: promoter-group alloy supply and customer 1. The separating observation is whether promoter-group purchases fall as Unit III's own melting replaces bought-in alloy.
- **Recognition gap: CLOSED.** Market cap Rs 2,013 crore at Rs 1,009 (MD) is 44.6 times trailing profit. At Rs 301 the company was worth Rs 600 crore, 18 times FY26 profit (derived from AR26 pp.36, 56). The IPO opened the gap; the market closed it in twelve weeks. The R3 neighbourhood is about 19 times.

**Posture: EARNINGS-ONLY.** The climb is real and already priced. Returns ride profit growth alone against a multiple two times the destination rung. If the alloy-supply dependence is later classed STRUCTURAL, the overlay becomes AVOID.

## 12. Verdict card

**WATCH.** A real transition at a price that has paid for FY28, with the group's foundries inside the cost line.

**Load-bearing facts to verify first**
1. The FY27 guide: 50% growth needs about Rs 231 crore in H2 against a September 2026 month of Rs 42 crore (Call, pp.7, 11, 16).
2. Unit III: Rs 40.6 crore deployed, Rs 65.7 crore left, full installation by September 2027 (Deck, pp.5, 42; U3, pp.1-2).
3. Promoter-group supply of about Rs 35 crore in FY26 and the Rs 50 crore limits (RHP, p.62; AR26, pp.28-29).
4. Customer 1 at 31.75% and CV at 53% of H1 FY27 revenue (RHP, p.25; Deck, p.40).

**What would change the view**
- Up to PROCEED: the price re-opens the gap toward the R3 neighbourhood on a delivered H2 FY27, or full Unit III commissioning confirms the FY28 phasing.
- Down to PASS: H2 FY27 revenue below H1; promoter or pre-IPO selling at lock-in expiry; related-party purchases rising faster than revenue.

## 13. Forward view

**Basis tier: A, guided.** Revenue growth "approximately 50%" for FY27 (Call, pp.5, 16); EBITDA about 19% and PAT about 12% expected to hold into H2 (Deck, p.38).

- **FY27 bottom line: Rs 52.6 crore.** Rs 293.86 crore times 1.5 = Rs 440.8 crore. H1 delivered Rs 210.09 crore and Rs 24.93 crore of PAT (H1 res, p.5). H2 needs Rs 230.7 crore, up 9.8% on H1, at 12% = Rs 27.7 crore. 24.93 + 27.7 = Rs 52.6 crore, fully taxed at 26.4%. No seasonality warning (Call, p.11).
- **Growth:** +57% on FY26 PAT of Rs 33.45 crore (AR26, p.56).
- **Forward PE: about 38.3x.** Rs 2,013 crore (MD, 28-Oct-2026 close, a service record) divided by Rs 52.6 crore.
- **The two readings.** If H2 only repeats H2 FY26 profit of Rs 20.17 crore, FY27 PAT is Rs 45.1 crore, PE 44.6x. If the Rs 42 crore month holds, FY27 PAT is about Rs 55 crore, PE 36.6x. The separating observation is H2 FY27 revenue beside the CV line.
