**Six diagnostic answers:**
1. **Did revenue grow YoY?** Yes, interest income +181.2% (26.60 -> 74.79, L288) and total revenue from ops +88.3% (39.88 -> 75.10, L291). This is real book and leverage growth (D/E 0.73 -> 2.88; AUM 541.8 -> 1,270.4).

3. **Did core operating profit (PPOP) grow YoY?** NO. PPOP was 11.61 (Q1FY26) vs 11.41 (Q1FY27) — down 1.7%. The single cleanest test of operational health says core pre-provision profit is FLAT while the book more than doubled. Headline PBT/PAT growth is NOT core-driven.
4. **What drove the gap between flat PPOP and +64% PBT?** The provision line fell 4.58 Cr (4.78 -> 0.20). PBT bridge: PPOP delta −0.20 + provision-release +4.58 = +4.38 Cr = the entire reported PBT increase. All of the growth is a provision write-back, not operations.
5. **Are finance/employee costs scaling faster than income?** Yes. Finance cost +277% and employee cost +240% both outran interest income +181%. Cost-to-income worsened from 64.8% to 76.8%. This is early-warning operating deleverage, not operating leverage.
6. **Is income concentration changing?** Yes, adversely for quality: fee income collapsed (−99.5%) into a Note-8 regroup, and the earnings mix now leans entirely on interest income while the provision line is doing the profit work. Strip the provision release and Q1 FY27 PBT is ~5.87 Cr, below Q1 FY26's 6.83.

## 6. PLAIN-LANGUAGE BRIEF

### Summary narrative

Finkurve (brand: Arvog) reported Q1 FY27 PAT of Rs 8.44 Cr, up 65.8% from Rs 5.09 Cr a year ago, on interest income of Rs 74.79 Cr, up 181%. On the surface this is a fast-growing gold-loan NBFC. Underneath, the growth in reported profit is almost entirely a provisioning holiday: the impairment charge fell to Rs 0.20 Cr from a run-rate near Rs 5.5 Cr a quarter, and provision cover on bad loans is only 10.45%. Strip that out and pre-provision profit was flat year on year at about Rs 11.4 Cr, even though the loan book more than doubled. Bad loans are still tiny in absolute terms (GNPA 0.54%) but rose roughly sixfold off a 0.09% base, and the loan-to-value on the gold book climbed to 77.3%, the highest in its own history and well above peers at 57-61%. Borrowing costs rose faster than lending income (finance costs up 277% vs interest income up 181%), so the spread is thinning as the company levers up (debt-to-equity 0.73 to 2.88). Capital is ample (CRAR 26.63%) and collections are healthy (98%). The board has cleared enabling resolutions for up to Rs 5,000 Cr of borrowing and fresh NBCs, and put a set of Augmont related-party payments to shareholders, pointing to a much larger, more levered balance sheet ahead. The deck omits GNPA from its headline, never quantifies off-book AUM, gives zero forward guidance, and describes the equity raise as "111.5 cr" when the filing shows Rs 141.50 Cr with Rs 30 Cr of warrant money still uncalled. There is also a visible EPS error (diluted 0.58 above basic 0.50) and a monitoring-agency inconsistency across two fund raises. None of this breaks the thesis, but it confirms why the Notion status is WATCHLIST / AVOID: the quality of the reported quarter is lower than the headline, two tripwires (asset quality and the path to a 14% ROE) moved toward firing, and the numbers you would need to get comfortable (Stage-wise ECL, warrant price, RPT quantum, forward guidance) are simply not disclosed. Decision stays human and unchanged; the flags are raised.

### SECTOR INTELLIGENCE

The company operates in Indian gold-loan NBFC lending, a structurally large and under-penetrated market: the deck cites household gold wealth of Rs 394 lakh Cr and a current gold-loan market of ~Rs 17 lakh Cr with ~60% still informal (PROVENANCE: THIS quarter's deck, macro/HDFC-Securities source, S16/L486-496; MACRO_NOT_COMPANY — not a Finkurve KPI). The tailwind is real (formalisation, gold-price support), but the same gold-price dependence is the sector's cyclical risk: roughly half of Finkurve's AUM growth is price/LTV-driven, not volume (THIS quarter: AUM +134.5% vs gold tonnage +46.6%, S7/L176,178). Regulation is tightening around gold-loan LTV discipline and co-lending economics; Finkurve's LTV at 77.3% (THIS quarter, S28/L884) is the metric most exposed to a regulatory or price shock. Peer LTV band 57-61% is PRIOR Notion/peer work. Payer mix is not applicable (lender). The sector read: strong secular demand, but Finkurve is running the price-sensitive, high-LTV end of it.

### BUSINESS-MODEL INTELLIGENCE

Finkurve makes money by borrowing wholesale (term loans 48.6%, NCDs 37.6%, OD/WCDL 13.8%; THIS quarter S24/L727-735) at ~10-11% and lending against gold at a ~20-22% yield (THIS quarter S28/L862-863), earning a spread that it is now amplifying with leverage (D/E 2.88, L360). The book is 96% gold loans (THIS quarter S17/L543), having pivoted from personal loans since FY23. Two model-drift signals this quarter: first, a Rs 69.73 Cr FY26 fee-and-commission EXPENSE (S31/L962) that is an Augmont Goldtech related-party pass-through (Service Fees/Commission/Brand/Tech, L135) — roughly a third of revenue is paid to a related party for sourcing/branding/tech, a genuine single-point-of-failure dependency (Augmont RPT flag is PRIOR Notion; the 69.73 Cr quantum and its RPT nature are THIS quarter). Second, an off-book/co-lending vehicle (Godrej Finance, THIS quarter S23/L705) that is blended into every AUM chart but never quantified (~3% today via Rs 37.86 Cr co-lending outstanding, L337). Unit economics are deteriorating at the margin: cost-to-income rose to ~76.8% from ~64.8% (THIS quarter, calc) on a tripling of employee cost, and provisions are running below any credible through-cycle level. The model works only if gold prices hold, the RPT sourcing stays intact, and provisioning eventually normalises without denting the ROE the thesis needs.

### COMPETITION INTELLIGENCE

Finkurve is a sub-scale challenger (AUM Rs 1,270.4 Cr, 118 branches; THIS quarter S7/L176,180) against national gold-loan majors and banks. Where it wins: fast disbursal (~25 minutes TAT, THIS quarter S9/L243), a promoter bullion legacy (Augmont), and a rapid branch-rollout claim (30-45 days, THIS quarter S21/L675). Where it is structurally weaker: LTV at 77.3% is materially above the peer band of 57-61% (peer band PRIOR Notion; Finkurve LTV THIS quarter S28/L884), meaning less collateral buffer than competitors on the same gold; ROE at ~10% (THIS quarter deck RoAE 9.4%, S29/L901) trails the mid-teens the larger players earn; and provisioning at 10.45% PCR (THIS quarter L384) is thin versus peers who carry heavier cover. Notably a Muthoot-affiliated holder (Thomas John Muthoot / Muthoot Bankers, 13.2%, THIS quarter S36/L1072) sits on the register — a competitor-adjacent ownership fact worth watching. The competitive risk to watch: in a gold-price drawdown, the highest-LTV, thinnest-provisioned lender is the one whose asset quality and capital move first, and Finkurve is currently that lender in this peer set.
