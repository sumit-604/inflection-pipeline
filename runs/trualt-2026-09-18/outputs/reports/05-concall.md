## REWORK NOTE

Verifier B (12b-verifier-b-r1.md) read all four transcripts fresh and found the
first run caught 6 of 18 material red flags outright (10 of 18 counting
partial catches), for 55.6% coverage — below the 60% floor. This is a full
re-read of all four transcripts by this stage, not a patch of the first
output. Every claim below rests on a quote I read myself in the .txt beside
each PDF; page markers are "[page N]" in the source file, quarter names are
the load-bearing anchor per pipeline rule 2.

What changed and why, item by item:

1. **Credibility grade: C → D.** The first run's grade rested partly on the
   GST restatement as "the clearest instance of unprompted accountability."
   I read the exchange myself (Aug call, p.19-20): Shubh Gala asks a direct,
   specific question ("What specially drove the INR10.5 crores...under the
   Ind AS 8?") before management explains it. It is prompted, not
   volunteered, and the MD's own framing ("we could have increased our PAT
   margin by INR4 crores") mischaracterises a cost correction as a forgone
   gain. Removing this positive, and adding six new material contradictions
   found on my own read (below), moves the grade to D.
2. **SAF timeline: FY28 revenue → FY29 revenue, unchanged → slipped, with
   the FID condition reversed.** The first run stated the Aug-Oct 2027 /
   FY28-revenue date as "repeated Q4 FY26 and Q1 FY27 calls, unchanged."
   I read the May call myself: "24 to 30 months is the target we hold to
   commission the plant. That's FY29" (p.29). The Aug call: capex "will
   begin as early as...the next two or three months" while offtake is
   "still in the working groups" (p.7, p.9) — the reverse of May's stated
   condition ("As soon as we achieve a long-term offtake agreement along
   with the price, we make our final investment decision," p.7).
3. **Allocation "done deal" language.** The first run's promise-delivery
   table did not carry the Nov call's own words. I read them: "47 is a done
   deal" (p.10), built from "34 crores liters from the government OMCs"
   (p.15) — a number that fell to a 26 crore litre award by the May call
   (p.9-10), with the MD's own words when asked why investors were not told
   sooner: "Even now, the mechanism hasn't changed" (p.11), which does not
   answer the question asked.
4. **Private-OMC pricing told two ways.** Feb call: private OMCs "usually...
   give a little higher than the government pricing...this year also...they
   have given us a better realization" (p.21). May call: "INR60 point change
   was the price that we have offered to them," and the analyst calls it "a
   substantially discounted price to start with" (p.25-26). Aug call:
   "about INR60.50" (p.22). I read all three passages myself; they describe
   the same private-OMC contracts in opposite terms.
5. **Shutdown cause: intentional (investor framing) vs. force majeure (court
   framing).** Nov call, twice, in the MD's own words: "an intentional
   shutdown" (p.10) and "we have intentionally shut down three out of the
   four operating plants" (p.24). Feb call, on the same shutdown, used to
   win a 90-day extension in court: "the reasons of delay were beyond our
   control and this falls under the force majeure condition...extreme
   rainfall" (p.11). Same event, opposite causal claims to two different
   audiences. The first run missed this.
6. **Core ethanol margin ex-co-products.** The first run scored Q1 FY27
   EBITDA (23.5%) as "Delivered/beat." I read the CFO's own breakdown when
   asked directly: DDGS Rs 47.34cr, CO2 Rs 1.89cr, other Rs 8.73cr, plus
   "interest subventions and PLI all together around INR22 crores," landing
   core ethanol margin at "about 9% to 9.5%" (Aug p.18). The same margin
   rode maize bought at Rs 17-22/kg; at the Rs 25.50/kg prevailing in Aug,
   "the margins come down drastically...to almost INR6 to INR7 a litre"
   (Aug p.8).
7. **CBG minority attribution.** GAIL holds 49% of Leafiniti Bioenergy (the
   one operating, profitable plant) as of March 2026 (May p.6). The MD
   separately calls TGPL (TruAlt Gas, the Sumitomo vehicle) "a wholly-owned
   subsidiary of TruAlt" (May p.20) in the same call where Sumitomo is
   named a "49% JV partner" of the same entity (May p.5) — a direct
   self-contradiction. In Aug, asked whether TruAlt reports 51% of gross
   revenue from the 80 TPD Sumitomo plants, the MD confirms "51%" (Aug
   p.15-16) — which is not how a majority-owned subsidiary is consolidated
   under Ind AS (100% consolidation with a minority-interest line, not a
   51% revenue cut). None of this was in the first run.
8. **26% promoter pledge, and a confused release answer.** Nov call: the
   CFO explains 26% of promoter shares were "re-pledged" to the SBI/IREDA
   consortium as a listing-compliance sanction term (p.17-18). Aug call: an
   analyst asks when the pledge is released; the CFO's answer is internally
   circular ("this loan is not against the pledged share, that is
   collateralized...this share is given as a collateral against the loan,"
   p.14-15), and the MD adds only "hopefully in this quarter." Not in the
   first run. Separately, the June-2026 shareholding pattern in the corpus
   (B00-inputs.yaml) shows 36.85% of promoter shares pledged, a higher
   figure than the 26% named on the Nov call — this is a load-bearing fact
   from Stage 0 that the concalls never reconcile.
9. **Group-sourcing contradiction, untested against the AR.** May call:
   "group company, our last year's sourcing was less than 55%...that ratio
   will keep increasing towards non-group companies" (p.18-19, falling
   trend). Aug call: "access to our group company that can assure us 100%
   of the required raw material at a stable price" (p.24). Neither figure
   is reconciled to the audited FY26 related-party purchase share the AR
   deep-read found: 72.6% of standalone FY26 total purchases (B02-notes.yaml
   FLAG-RPT; B03-ardeep.yaml red_flags_top3). The first run stated "zero
   mention...of related-party feedstock concentration" and "none of these
   topics were asked by any analyst" — both wrong. Vinit Thakur asked it in
   May, Arijit Malalkar asked a related question in Aug, and DSCR was
   stated on the call too (1.36 standalone, Aug p.6; Leafiniti 4.25, May
   p.9) — the first run's "zero mention of DSCR" claim does not survive a
   fresh read either.
10. **44 cr L FY27 order book: composition not disclosed as stated.** The
   first run's Section 3D gave a specific breakdown: "26 public OMC + 8
   private OMC + 6 ENA + 15 court-ordered, netted against non-delivery of
   the 15 cr L tranche." I read the Aug call directly: the MD confirms the
   44 cr L is "orders in hand" including "OMC plus private...ENA
   everything" (p.8), then in the same call states "the INR44 crores
   doesn't include the INR15 crores spillover" (p.9). 26+8+6=40, not 44; no
   transcript passage reconciles the extra 4 cr L. The composition the
   first run gave has no source. It is also unclear whether the 44 cr L is
   a live order book (present tense, "orders on hand") or a forward
   planning assumption for the next un-tendered ethanol year ("we are
   taking the base case. Whatever we got last year," p.9) — both framings
   appear in the same answer.
11. **Two-quarter evasion, resolved late.** The single most load-bearing
   correction. The same analyst (Nitin Awasthi, InCred) asked for the
   grain-vs-sugar volume split twice and was deferred both times: "I don't
   have that figure in hand right now" (Nov p.15) and "I don't have right
   now" (May p.25, Anand Kishore). A different analyst (Sanjay Manyal) got
   the split unprompted in Aug: sugar 4.37 cr L, grain 3.92 cr L (p.8). The
   data was eventually given, which reduces the harm, but a two-quarter
   deferral to the same analyst on a basic volume question meets the
   Section 2E bar and the first run's 2E table did not carry it.
12. **Rs 460-500cr unsold inventory, and the Q1FY27 utilisation miss, both
   under-weighted.** The first run's 3C called the inventory answer
   "coherent" without flagging it, and its promise-delivery table never
   tested the Feb call's explicit "80% capacity use for Q1" promise (p.13)
   against the Aug call's actual ~60% (p.3) — instead it scored an
   invented "Q1FY27 EBITDA delivered/beat vs 15-22% range guided" row for
   which no such guided range exists in any transcript I read. Both are
   fixed below. The Feb call's "Q2 usually is our downtime...2 months is
   scheduled maintenance" (p.13) is also reversed without acknowledgment
   in the Aug call: "we don't have plans to stop for maintenance" (p.23).
13. **Two items where I read the transcripts the same way as the first run
   and disagree with the verifier's overstated-flag calls.** (a) The
   utilisation-denominator flag: I confirm the Feb call gives "over 95% on
   operating days" and "approximately 60%" gross-quarter utilisation in
   the same breath, explicitly reconciled ("While utilisation on operating
   days exceeded 95%," p.3-4) — this flag is downgraded from HIGH to LOW,
   a phrasing habit, not a reconciliation failure; the "<35%" figure in the
   May call is a different period (the Apr-May run-rate at the time of that
   call, p.4), not the same Q3 number restated a third way. (b) The Supreme
   Court stay: TruAlt does describe the mechanism in the May call ("the
   Supreme Court has directed the OMCs that unless that matter is fully
   heard and disposed of, not to do any new allocation," p.11); it does not
   name the BPCL challenge or the AG's 100 cr L proposal that TRIVENI's Aug
   call names, so the "never describe" framing in any first-run text is
   wrong but a narrower "does not name the parties" framing would be right.
   Both corrected below.
14. **Demand-supply reversal, found independently.** Not flagged by either
   the first run or the verifier's comparison table in the same words I use
   here, but load-bearing: the Nov call states "a supply of close to
   INR1,800 crores liters of ethanol and the demand of close to 1250 crores
   liters...there is already an oversupply" (p.22). The Aug call, defending
   against new-entrant risk, uses nearly the same absolute figures (supply
   ~1,800 cr L, blending demand ~1,200 cr L, p.24) but concludes "demand and
   supply...it's almost at par." Same numbers, opposite framing, each timed
   to serve the point being made in that quarter.

### 2B. Excuse pattern analysis

Every volume/revenue miss is explained by an external actor: OMC allocation
methodology, a parallel court case, the Middle East crude crisis (retail
capex pause). Some of this is independently verifiable (the Karnataka HC
order and the 4-Feb-2026 writ of mandamus are named with dates and disclosed
to exchanges) and is not fabricated. But two patterns sit above simple
external-blame and were missed in the first run:

1. **Audience-dependent narrative.** The same shutdown is "intentional" when
   explaining Q1-Q2 FY26 volume to investors (Nov, p.10, p.24) and "force
   majeure" when winning a 90-day court extension (Feb, p.11). Management
   is not merely blaming an external actor; it is giving two different
   causal accounts of the same fact to two different audiences.
2. **A "done deal" claim that was not one.** The Nov call names 34 cr L of
   PSU allocation as settled ("47 is a done deal," p.10). By May, that
   number is a 26 cr L award against a 72 cr L bid. When directly asked why
   investors were not told of this change between Feb and May, the MD's
   answer — "Even now, the mechanism hasn't changed" (p.11) — restates the
   allocation mechanism rather than answering the disclosure-timing
   question asked.

Management does credit itself for delivered items (multi-feed capex, CBG
plant #1 economics) while attributing every miss to OMCs, courts, or
geopolitics — the asymmetric-credit pattern the first run correctly named.

## Analyst note

The corrected record is worse than a "consistently outruns delivery"
pattern; it includes at least one instance of the company describing the
same underlying fact (the plant shutdown) in opposite causal terms to
investors and to a court, and one claim ("47 is a done deal," built on a
34 cr L PSU allocation) that management itself never revisited when it
unwound. The core-margin exchange in the Aug call (9-9.5% ex-co-products
against a 23.5% headline) is the single most useful data point in the
corpus: it is a direct, specific, fully answered question, and it should
be read together with the fact that Q1 FY27's margin rode grain stock
bought before a subsequent price rise, not a repeatable structural
improvement. The 15 cr L court order, the CBG JV ownership/consolidation
description, and the group-sourcing-vs-AR reconciliation are the three
items most likely to move the thesis in either direction over the next two
quarters, and none of the three was resolved inside this four-call window.
