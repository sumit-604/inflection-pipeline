## Business Understanding Narrative

**What does it do, and how does it make money?** Marushika buys IT and telecom hardware from OEMs and installs it on government-linked projects. Data centre racks and cooling, IP telephone exchanges, CCTV and video walls are the core. In FY26, Rs 66.82 crore of the Rs 116.43 crore revenue was product sales and Rs 49.61 crore was services (screener/Annual_Report_2026.pdf, p.99). It runs on 24 permanent employees (AR p.46). Sub-contractors do most of the field work: sub-contract cost was Rs 38.34 crore (AR p.82).

The end customer is the state; the paying customer usually is not. In FY26, 93.46% of revenue came from non-government entities, mostly system integrators and PSU prime contractors such as BEL (AR p.56). The flagship job shows the chain: DMRC awarded Delhi Metro Phase IV DS-12 to BEL, and BEL engaged Marushika at about Rs 31.19 crore (filings/…09032026…Press_Release, p.2).

**What is changing?** Management says the mix. Three claims: services rose from Rs 17.44 crore to Rs 49.61 crore; a defence vehicle refurbishment vertical started; an own SaaS learning platform (LMS) is being built (AR p.55 to 56). FY26 EBITDA margin rose 105 bps to 13.7% (AR p.57).

**Why now?** The IPO raised Rs 26.97 crore in February 2026. Short-term debt fell from Rs 18.82 crore to Rs 4.37 crore (AR p.81). H2 FY26 EBITDA margin was 15.1% against 11.7% in H1 (AR p.57).

**What must be true?** The margin gain must come from a better business, not from a better half-year. Receivables must start turning into cash. Neither has happened yet.

**What breaks it?** Working capital and governance. Receivables are 180 days of revenue. The company lends promoter companies money with no agreement and no interest.

## 2. Business model and archetype

Two engines inside one segment (AR p.106 note 33):

- **Value-added distribution (VAD).** Resell OEM kit (Rittal, Matrix, Delta, Barco, LG) into projects. Product purchases were Rs 56.73 crore against Rs 66.82 crore of product sales (AR p.82, p.99).
- **System integration (SI).** Supply, install, test and commission, often as a sub-contractor to BEL, L&T or HFCL. The prospectus says many non-government clients win the state contract and then engage Marushika (offer p.37).

Gross spread (revenue less purchases and sub-contract cost) was 18.3% in FY26 and 18.4% in FY25 (AR p.82). The services shift did not raise it. [INFERENCE] If the sub-contract cost maps to service lines, services earn a spread of about 23% and are largely bought in.

Concentration is high. The top customer was 28.35% and the top ten 82.96% of H1 FY26 revenue (offer p.38). Delhi and UP were 61.82% (offer p.40). The top ten suppliers were 90.71% of purchases (offer p.44).

**Archetype: order-book business** (EPC sub-contractor with a VAD trading base). The variables are order inflow, book-to-bill, execution pace, working capital and margin on backlog.

## 3. Competitive advantages

**Absent, with one partial exception.** The prospectus lists diversified offerings, OEM relationships, an experienced team and long customer relationships (offer p.189 to 190). These are issuer claims. Its own numbers undercut two: OEM relationships average 1 to 6 years and top-ten customer relationships 1 to 4 years (offer p.189 to 190). The MD&A names the threat: "larger, better-capitalised system integrators and OEM-direct channels" (AR p.55).

The partial exception is empanelment. Registrations with PSUs and DMRC sub-contractor status are a real entry ticket for small tenders. They give no pricing power. Management itself puts SI margin at 15 to 18% and non-direct work at 6 to 8% (transcript p.11).

## 4. The promoters

Monicca Agarwaal (MD) holds 38.61%. Jai Prakash Pandey (WTD) holds 16.02% (AR p.90). Sonika Aggarwal (CFO, ED) is the MD's sister (AR p.44). The promoter group total is 58.19% at Sep 2026 (market-data-2026-10-18.md, service record). The promoters declared no encumbrance at 31-Mar-2026 (filings/team_bbodade…, p.1, read visually).

The three executive directors drew Rs 1.64 crore. That is 55% of the Rs 3.00 crore employee cost (AR p.82, p.106).

**Related-party dependence, from audited notes:**
- Loan of Rs 1.98 crore to Marushika Infrastructure Services Pvt Ltd (MISPL), unchanged since FY25. Note 35 states the loans have "no formal written agreement and no interest" (AR p.107).
- MISPL is 99.92% owned by the MD. It is a real-estate company. It lost Rs 2.42 crore in FY25 and its net worth was negative Rs 1.68 crore (offer p.256 to 257).
- MISPL also mortgaged three properties to secure Marushika's bank limit (offer p.284). [INFERENCE] The loan may be the price of that collateral. It is still an unsecured, unpriced exposure to an insolvent-looking promoter vehicle.
- Rs 50 lakh is due from Volo Bharat, a group company that "has not undertaken any business activities to date" (AR p.106; offer p.53).
- Naivedyam Info Solutions, a promoter-group software firm, sold Rs 70.52 lakh to Marushika and bought Rs 75.00 lakh from it in FY26. Rs 75.91 lakh is outstanding (AR p.105 to 106). A non-compete with Naivedyam was signed in July 2025 (offer p.53).
- CSR runs through Women on Making Notion Foundation (AR p.38), a promoter-group entity (filings/team_bbodade…, p.2).

Related-party recoverables total Rs 3.24 crore. That is 36% of FY26 standalone PAT.

FY26 revenue grew 36.6% and PAT 42.5% (AR p.57). The margin gain came below gross spread: employee and other costs fell from 5.7% to 4.7% of revenue (AR p.57). That is operating leverage, not mix.

Cash is the problem. Trade receivables rose from Rs 39.85 crore to Rs 57.59 crore. That is 180 days of revenue (AR p.81). Rs 7.45 crore is more than one year past due, with no provision (AR p.98 to 99). Short-term advances rose from Rs 5.90 crore to Rs 14.84 crore (AR p.81). Operating cash flow was Rs 1.90 crore against PAT of Rs 9.03 crore (AR p.83). From FY23 to FY26, cumulative operating cash flow was about negative Rs 6.4 crore against cumulative PAT near Rs 18.9 crore (offer p.45; AR p.83; offer p.190).

Leverage is now low: debt-equity 0.15 and ROCE 28.5% (AR p.108). ROE on closing post-IPO equity is 19.1% (AR p.81 to 82).

**Classification: earnings-led growth on IPO-funded working capital.** The IPO paid for the receivables. It did not change the cash cycle.

## 6. The transition and the quality ladder

**FROM R1, commodity price-taker.** A reseller and sub-contractor of OEM products, with an 18% gross spread, no pricing power and returns that depend on how slowly the state chain pays.

**Claimed TO: R3, value-added specialist.** Defence refurbishment, an own LMS product, managed services and AI analytics. Management's claim, once: the company "transitions into a diversified, pan-India technology execution partner" (AR p.56).

The evidence does not show the climb:
- Defence auto-tech was 0.63% and smart solutions 0.11% of FY26 revenue; IT and telecom was 99.27% (AR p.55; deck p.17, read visually).
- The LMS is an "e-learning app" in CWIP at Rs 3.29 crore. Project cost and timelines are "yet to be finalised" (AR p.104). It has no revenue.
- Rs 4.13 crore of bought software was capitalised as an intangible and made a key audit matter (AR p.72, p.96).
- New ventures keep arriving: Graymatics reselling (Feb 2026), a Nashik parking PPP stake (Aug 2026), exoskeleton distribution (Sep 2026). Each is a distribution or consortium role, not owned capability.

Reading: **R1, no climb evidenced.** Two readings remain. Either the H2 margin is the start of a richer mix, or it is Q4 billing seasonality (offer p.112). The H1 FY27 gross spread separates them.

## 8. Proof check

SME reporting is half-yearly. **The two-quarter proof test degrades to a two-half-year test.** The halves held are H1 and H2 FY26. They are halves of the same year, not a trend. No H2 FY25 was published (Board outcome 22-May-2026, p.16, note 4). The prospectus says revenue is booked heavily in Q4 (offer p.112).

H2 beat H1: revenue up 39.4%, gross spread 19.7% against 16.5% (AR p.57). That is consistent with seasonality and does not prove a mix shift.

Forward order cover is thin. The auditor-certified unexecuted book was Rs 35.45 crore at 31-Dec-2025 (offer p.111). That is 0.3 times FY26 revenue. New orders in FY25 were Rs 36.22 crore against Rs 85.25 crore executed (offer p.111). The CFO put the book at "more than Rs 35 crores" in May 2026 (transcript p.8). The AR and deck instead cite Rs 108.56 crore "under execution" (AR p.54; deck p.19). The two do not reconcile.

**Proof point: absent.** The H1 FY27 result is the first post-IPO half and is not in the corpus.

## 9. Flags from the documents

- **Section 186 non-compliance.** CARO reports no interest charged on loans to related parties and Rs 2.48 crore outstanding "without specifying any terms or period of repayment" (AR p.76). Integrity-grade flag.
- **Receivables.** Rs 57.59 crore, a key audit matter, 180 days, Rs 7.45 crore over one year old and unprovided (AR p.71, p.98 to 99).
- **Misstated summary tables.** The deck and MD&A label receivables as "Inventories" and show receivables of Rs 2.65 crore (deck p.7; AR p.58). The audited figure is Rs 57.59 crore (AR p.81). ROE is claimed at 52.77% (AR p.17); the audited note says 28.91% (AR p.108).
- **HFCL order.** A Rs 45.06 crore order was announced on 29-Apr-2026 and cancelled two days later "due to some unavoidable reasons" (filings 29-Apr and 01-May-2026). The prospectus had said the company had never faced a cancellation (offer p.39).
- **Controls.** No internal audit in FY26 (AR p.77). Audit trail not enabled (AR p.110). Daily back-up rule not met (AR p.73). Bank stock statements differ from books by Rs 1.56 crore (AR p.75).
- **ROC penalty.** Rs 53,400 on the company and Rs 50,000 each on the MD and CFO for a missed AOC-4 CFS filing (filing 08-May-2026, p.2). The directors' report says no material regulator orders (AR p.41).
- **IPO cost.** Issue expenses were Rs 4.00 crore, 14.8% of Rs 26.97 crore raised (AR p.33).
- **Disputes.** GST claim Rs 1.42 crore and income-tax demand Rs 0.11 crore under appeal (AR p.101).
- Positive: a former GM of Northern Railway joined as independent director from 01-Sep-2026 (filing 11-Aug-2026, p.5). The audit opinion is unmodified (AR p.71).

## 10. Independent check: the credit rating

**NOT FOUND.** None is held, and screener.in lists none. The audited CARO annexure is the only independent voice, and it is the source of the Section 186 flag.

## 11. Posture: the Transition Decision Matrix

- **Proof gate: NOT FIRED.** Diversification revenue is 0.74%. Gross spread is flat year on year. No post-IPO half exists.
- **Ugliness: STRUCTURAL-FEATURE.** The prospectus says receivables have historically run at 6 to 8 months of revenue (offer p.112). The related-party loans predate the IPO. Neither is a cost of climbing.
- **Recognition gap: OPEN.** Market cap Rs 101 crore (screener.in service record, 18-Oct-2026) is 11.3 times FY26 PAT. That sits at the R1 neighbourhood of about 12x and far below R3.

**Posture: VALUE-TRAP RISK.** The multiple is cheap because the earnings do not turn into cash. Classification would flip only if receivable days fall and the promoter loans are repaid.

## 12. Verdict card

**PASS.**

The bull case is real on its face: revenue compounded 38.5% over FY24 to FY26 (deck p.24), debt is low, and a forward multiple near 8 times screens cheap. The bear case carries the same evidence bar and wins on three filed facts. The auditor reports undocumented, interest-free loans to a promoter company with negative net worth. Receivables absorb the profit. The ladder claim rests on verticals that are under 1% of revenue. The strategy hunts a climb; this name shows growth without one.

**Load-bearing facts, if the name is reopened**
1. H1 FY27 gross spread and receivable days against H1 FY26 (16.5%, and Rs 45.79 crore receivables at Sep 2025, offer p.110).
2. Repayment, with interest, of the Rs 1.98 crore MISPL loan.
3. A reconciled rupee order book: unexecuted value against the Rs 108.56 crore "under execution" claim.
4. Revenue from defence, smart solutions and LMS above 10% of the half.

**What would change the view**
- Receivable days below 150 with positive operating cash flow above half of PAT, plus the related-party balances cleared. That moves the name to WATCH.
- A fresh cancellation, a receivable write-off, or a new related-party advance confirms PASS.

No Role 1 valuation was run and no target price is set. This verdict decides only whether the name earns a `/step1`. It is not a position.
