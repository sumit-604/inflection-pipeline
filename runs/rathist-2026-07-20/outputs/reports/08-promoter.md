### 2B. Criminal cases — the decisive finding of this report

**2016 CBI Special Court conviction (Kesla/Kesala North coal block allocation
scam).** 📰 MEDIA REPORTED, corroborated across multiple independent outlets (The
Tribune, India.com, Business Standard, and a 2026 ScanX article citing RSPL's own
exchange disclosure of subsequent court relief — the last of these amounts to the
company confirming the underlying case exists via its own regulatory filing).

- **Facts alleged (CBI):** RSPL, as part of a conspiracy, misrepresented ownership of a
  land parcel in a feedback form submitted to the (now-defunct) Screening Committee for
  coal block allocation. On the strength of that misrepresentation, the Ministry of Coal
  allocated the Kesla North Coal Block in Chhattisgarh to RSPL in August 2008.
- **Conviction:** A Special CBI Court convicted RSPL (the company) together with
  **Pradeep Rathi** and **Udit Rathi** (both then in MD/CEO-type roles at RSPL) and
  **Kushal Aggarwal** (AGM) of **cheating (IPC 420)** and **criminal conspiracy (IPC
  120B)** on 26-27 July 2016.
- **Sentences:** Udit Rathi — 3 years; Pradeep Rathi — 3 years; Kushal Aggarwal — 2
  years. **Fines:** RSPL (the company) Rs 50 lakh; individual fines reported in the
  Rs 5-50 lakh range across the three individuals (sources vary slightly on which figure
  attaches to which named person — reported consistently is that the company and both
  Rathis were fined Rs 25-50 lakh each and Aggarwal a smaller amount).
- **Bail:** granted 28-07-2016 on personal bonds of Rs 2 lakh each; sentence **suspended
  for appeal**.
- **Appeal:** Delhi High Court admitted the appeal and sought CBI's response (reported
  2016-2017 window). **Status of the appeal as of this run's search (July 2026) could
  not be confirmed as resolved** — no acquittal, no dismissal, no "conviction upheld"
  outcome was found in web search. This should be treated as **live, unresolved
  litigation**, not a closed historical matter.
- **Follow-on PMLA action:** the Enforcement Directorate filed a money-laundering
  prosecution complaint in **August 2024** against RSPL, Udit Rathi, Pradeep Rathi and
  Kushal Aggarwal, built on the 2016 CBI conviction as the predicate offence. On
  **6 March 2026**, the Special Judge (PC Act) (CBI) (Coal Block Cases)-01, New Delhi
  **declined to take cognizance** of the ED's complaint — real, near-term relief on the
  money-laundering front, reported by RSPL itself via exchange disclosure (per ScanX).
  This does **not** overturn or resolve the underlying 2016 criminal conviction.

**This is a genuine deal-breaker-class finding** under the framework's checklist
("economic offence conviction") — see Section 6C.

Separately, RSPL received court relief (per the same search results) when a Special
Judge declined to take cognizance of a related ED complaint — this is the same coal-block
matter, not a second, independent case; not double-counted here.

Despite this, **CARO (Auditor's Annexure-A, para 13, AR p.72)** states: *"transactions
with related parties are in compliance with section 177 and 188 of the Act, where
applicable and the details have been disclosed in the financial statements as required
under applicable Accounting Standard."* The Board's Report (AR p.23) separately states
*"There were no contracts or arrangements entered into by the Company in accordance with
provisions of Section 188... there were no material related party transactions... which
are not at arm's length."*

**This is the FLAG-RPT-INTEGRITY item from stages 2-3, now directly confirmed and
sharpened by primary-source cross-reference between Note 11 and Note 12 in the FY25 AR
itself** — a fresh Rs 7.15 Cr promoter-directed preferential allotment (13,00,001 shares
x Rs 55) plus continuing personal guarantees by promoter individuals are not treated as
RPTs, and the auditor's clean sign-off does not reconcile against what Note 11 itself
discloses about who received preferential shares. Whether this is a disclosure-scope
technicality (e.g., preferential allotments to promoter-group holders on conversion of
already-approved instruments are treated as capital transactions rather than "RPTs" under
the company's internal reading of Ind AS-24) or a genuine compliance gap could not be
resolved from the documents available — but the gap itself, and the clean CARO sign-off
sitting on top of it, is the finding.

### 3B. Capital allocation behaviour

The dilution/capital-raise pattern is more a **rescue-financing story than an extractive
one**:

- Net worth (Other Equity, Note 12, AR p.81) went from **-Rs 10,041.14 lakh (FY23)** to
