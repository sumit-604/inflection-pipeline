## 1. MY RULINGS

Every call below is made. Nothing here is a question. Where a call is genuinely uncertain I give both readings and say which one the numbers in this draft used, so you can overturn it in one line.

### Setup calls

**Forward window: 3 months primary, 6 months secondary, 12 months for ROCE.** Entero reports quarterly, so the default stands with no adjustment.
Confidence: `sure`. Proved wrong by: the company moving to semi annual reporting.

**Business type: standard operating business. Running the four standard transitions.** B04 labels it hybrid, but that describes a revenue mix of distribution, agency, private label and MedTech. It is not a bank, NBFC, MFI or HFC, so the lender transition set does not apply.
Confidence: `sure`. Proved wrong by: nothing in the evidence; this is not a lending business.

**Workup intent: first workup.** No prior company memory file exists and no prior FTTCP entry exists. There is no buy or hold thesis to test forward, no prior destination PE, and no prior devil's advocate.
Confidence: `sure`. Proved wrong by: an existing companies/ENTERO.md that I failed to find.

**Sector cap row: CORRECTED from Pharma / CDMO 38x to Cybersecurity / VAD 25x.** The manifest row is a collector mis pick. Entero manufactures nothing and does no contract development. It is a value added distributor, and VAD is the only distribution row in the Section 1B table. The run's own peer set makes the point: Redington and RPTech are both value added distributors. Entero is deliberately moving the same way, taking a full commercial role (demand generation, installation, after sales) on 10 to 15% of sales at roughly twice normal distribution margin.
Confidence: `fairly sure`. The honest alternative is Logistics (WC heavy / project cargo) 20x, on the grounds that Entero carries 67 debtor days and is genuinely working capital heavy. I did not take it because Entero earns a distribution margin on goods it owns and sells, not a freight fee.
Proved wrong by: nothing in this run. Note this ruling is close to costless here, because the four pillar output lands at 13.4x, far below either 25x or 20x. The cap does not bind. It would only matter if a later run lifted the pillars above 20x.

**Source availability: two required inputs are missing or unusable, out of seven.** No exchange announcements were collected, so the documented action ledger (Step 2E) runs on concall and annual report evidence only and cannot grade recent Reg 30 actions. The credit rating report is present but is an image only PDF with no text layer and no OCR available, so the rating agency working capital opinion could not be read. That second gap is the direct reason the cash determination stays open, because the rating agency view is the evidence the framework leans on to separate structural from growth induced. Two gaps is below the three that would force an inconclusive call, so this runs at reduced confidence, not inconclusive.
Confidence: `sure`.

### The two accounting basis calls that drive everything downstream

**PAT basis: use owners' PAT of Rs115.04 Cr, not the Rs146 Cr headline.** The FY26 audited results split profit as owners of the company Rs115.04 Cr and non controlling interests Rs30.80 Cr (FY26 results, statement of profit and loss, profit attributable to). The widely quoted Rs146 Cr is the total before minorities. Minorities took 21.1% of FY26 profit, up from 11.7% in FY25, and 38.2% of the fourth quarter alone. Management guides 25 to 27% for FY27. Earnings per share and therefore the exit multiple must sit on the owners' number: Rs115.04 Cr on 4.35 Cr shares is Rs26.45 per share, which puts the stock on 48.1 times trailing earnings at Rs1,273.
Confidence: `sure`. This is read straight off the audited filing. Proved wrong by: nothing. It is arithmetic.
Why it matters: on the Rs146 Cr headline the stock looks like 37.9 times. On what shareholders actually own it is 48.1 times. Phase 3 must use 48.1.

**ROCE basis: use statutory ROCE of 9.41%, not the 14.6% management quotes.** The gap is goodwill. Management's figure works out to earnings before interest and tax over capital employed excluding goodwill, which for a company that has bought 51 businesses means excluding most of what shareholders paid to build the earnings. The acquisitions are the growth engine and the goodwill is the cost of that engine, so it belongs in the denominator.
Confidence: `fairly sure`. The opposite reading, which you may prefer, is that goodwill is a sunk accounting artefact and the operating business genuinely earns 14.5% on the capital it actually works with. If you rule that way the Pillar 1 base rises from 13.0x to about 15.4x. This draft used 9.41%.
Proved wrong by: evidence that acquired businesses are earning materially above the group return on their own invested capital, which would mean the goodwill is not impaired economics but simply price paid for real returns.

### Transition calls

**Revenue forward: FIRING.** Growth is 26% compounded over three years, every one of the last four quarters grew, and FY27 guidance of 23% excludes new acquisitions.
Confidence: `sure`. Proved wrong by: two consecutive quarters below 15% growth.

**Margin forward: STARTING, rounded down from FIRING.** Margin has expanded every year, 2.85% to 3.37% to 4.04%, which is real but roughly 60 to 70 basis points a year, well short of the 300 basis points over two years that a FIRING verdict requires. The FY27 guide asks for a full 100 basis point step to 5.0%, larger than anything delivered so far, and the 4.5% fourth quarter that makes it look easy was helped by a government project sale booked once in a partly owned subsidiary.
Confidence: `fairly sure`. Proved wrong by: two quarters at or above 4.8% with no one time help.

**Cash conversion determination: INDETERMINATE, leaning growth induced. Forward verdict STARTING.** This is the swing call in the whole draft and I am flagging it hard. The case for growth induced: if growth stopped tomorrow a distributor's receivables and inventory convert to cash inside roughly one 68 day cycle, and FY26 proved the point by generating Rs96.2 Cr of operating cash while still growing 29%. The case against, and the reason it does not resolve: receivables grew 46.0% against revenue growth of 29.4%, so debtor days went the wrong way, from 59.5 to 67.1. Management refused a direct analyst request for the ageing beyond six months. The rating agency opinion is unreadable. The improvement in the headline working capital number came substantially from stretching payables, not from collecting faster.
Confidence: `genuinely uncertain`. The two readings: STARTING, because operating cash actually turned positive and the disclosed working capital day target was actually hit in reported numbers, which is documented action rather than a promise; or STAGNANT, because one positive year against six negative ones and expanding debtor days is not yet a transition. This draft used STARTING, worth +1.
If you rule STAGNANT the composite falls from +5 to +4 and the position drops one band from BUY candidate to deep watch leaning buy on dips. That is the single largest lever in this document.
Proved wrong by: first half FY27 debtor days above 70 with operating cash flow to EBITDA below 50%.

**ROCE backward: STRUCTURALLY LOW, not temporarily depressed.** ROCE has been below 15% in all seven years on record and has never had a high return cycle to recover to. It is rising, not recovering: 4.39%, then 6.68%, then 9.41%.
Confidence: `sure`. Proved wrong by: audited evidence of a pre 2020 period at 15% or better, which the absent prospectus might have shown.
This ruling matters mechanically: because the backward verdict is not temporarily depressed and the capital base carries no meaningful construction in progress or idle raised capital, neither normalization route is available. Statutory ROCE feeds Pillar 1 unnormalized.

**ROCE forward: RECOVERING.** Four consecutive years of increase with identifiable drivers, margin expansion on a capital base that stops inflating while the stated acquisition pause holds.
Confidence: `fairly sure`. Proved wrong by: FY27 ROCE flat or lower, which is what happens if the acquisition pause ends early and fresh goodwill lands on the denominator.

**Composite: +5 out of 8. Position: BUY candidate, standard conviction, small to medium sizing if the other gates clear.** Neither cap engaged: no transition is declining with no catalyst, so the Kernex cap does not apply, and the transitions were not all firing backward, so the trim rule does not apply.
Confidence: `fairly sure`, and it rests on the cash call above. At STAGNANT cash it is +4.
Important: this scores the business transition only. It is not a buy recommendation. Governance sits at CONCERN and the price has not been tested yet. Both are separate gates.

**Disposition cap: PROCEED WITH CAVEATS.** Cash conversion is INDETERMINATE, and that never resolves to a clean pass. The missing evidence is named: the receivables ageing beyond six months, and the rating agency working capital rationale.
Confidence: `sure`.

## 6. THE CATALYST STORY IN PLAIN WORDS

**What could make revenue keep firing.** Seven acquisitions closed in FY26 and only part of their revenue landed in the FY26 year, so roughly 11 of the guided 23% for FY27 arrives simply from owning them for twelve months instead of four or five. That part is close to arithmetic rather than forecast. The rest depends on organic growth holding, and organic growth is the bit that has softened to 1.4 times the wider market. Confirmed by: two quarters above 20% growth with organic disclosed at or above 1.5 times the market. Killed by: organic growth converging on the market rate, which would mean Entero is simply a bigger version of the industry rather than a share gainer.

**What could make margins keep expanding.** MedTech is now above 15% of revenue heading toward an aspiration of 20%, and management says it carries higher gross and EBITDA margin because Entero does the full commercial job rather than just warehousing. The agency model does the same thing on the pharma side at roughly double the usual margin. Both are genuine mix shifts and both showed up in the FY26 numbers. Confirmed by: gross margin above 10.8% for two consecutive quarters. Killed by: management continuing to refuse a segment margin split while blended margin stalls near 4%, which would suggest the premium is being competed away or was never as large as claimed.

**What could make cash conversion take hold.** The working capital task force delivered, the disclosed day target was met, and operating cash turned positive. That is documented action, not a promise, and it is the reason this transition scores anything at all. Confirmed by: debtor days back below 60 with operating cash flow at or above half of EBITDA. Killed by: debtor days above 70 while payables stop extending, which would expose the FY26 turn as a one year balance sheet manoeuvre.

**What could make returns on capital keep rising.** The acquisition pause is the catalyst, and it is unusual because it is a catalyst of restraint rather than action. For two to three quarters management says it will integrate rather than buy. If it holds, EBIT grows while the capital base sits still, and that is the fastest route from 9% to the low teens. Confirmed by: no large acquisition announced through Q2 FY27 alongside a rising quarterly return. Killed by: a large deal inside the pause window, which would add goodwill to the denominator and reset the climb.

**What is missing, and it is worth naming.** There is no documented action anywhere on the receivables side specifically. No factoring facility, no discounting line, no disclosed customer payment term renegotiation, no stated debtor day target separate from the netted working capital number. Management has vision on cash and has delivered on the netted metric, but on the single line that is actually deteriorating, the ageing of what customers owe, there is commentary and a refusal rather than a programme. Under the intent and action ledger that absence confirms the conservative reading rather than loosening it.

## 7. THE VERDICT

Entero scores **+5 out of 8**, which puts it in the **BUY candidate** band at standard conviction and small to medium sizing if the other gates clear. Revenue is firing outright, margins and cash are starting, and returns on capital are recovering from a low base, which is the classic shape of a business two years into a transition rather than one that has completed it. Neither the Kernex cap nor the trim rule engaged, because no transition is going backwards without a catalyst and the backward record was never uniformly strong.

The whole call turns on one print: first half FY27 debtor days read against operating cash flow. If days come back below 60 while cash conversion holds above half of EBITDA, the cash transition is real, the score is right, and the argument moves entirely to price. If days push past 70 while payables stop stretching, the FY26 cash turn was a balance sheet manoeuvre, cash drops to stagnant, the composite falls to +4, and this becomes a growth story still funding itself from suppliers.

Two things this score does not say. It does not say buy, because governance sits at CONCERN on auditor confirmed fraud findings at three subsidiaries that carry no explanation anywhere in the annual report. And it does not say the price is right, because that exercise has not run yet. On the evidence alone the disposition is capped at PROCEED WITH CAVEATS by the unresolved cash question.

## 11. THE P/E BASE CARD (for your approval)

This is a preview of the exit multiple the valuation will inherit, computed from Section 1B on what is known now. Role 1 still runs the full dual track exercise in Phase 3. Nothing here is a fair value.

| Step | Calculation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| A. ROCE Base | ROCE 11.05% → 0.5 × 11.05 + 7.5 | 13.0x |
| B. Cash Multiplier | Band: CFO negative or below 30% cumulative → 0.80x. Growth offset WITHHELD because the determination is INDETERMINATE | 0.80x |
| C. Quality Adjusted Base | A × B = 13.0 × 0.80 | 10.4x |
| D. Growth Visibility Premium | 3a +2x (SOM implied CAGR 33.8% qualifies, delivery grade B qualifies, two of four tests) plus 3b +1x (EM 25 band) plus 3c +0x (no documented order book) | +3x |
| E. Strategic Premium | Pricing power is weak per B04, no licence monopoly, ROCE route barred by single credit | +0x |
| F. Raw Destination PE | C + D + E | 13.4x |
| F2. UA Adjusted | UA not applied, all three qualifiers not met | 13.4x |
| G. Sector Cap | Cybersecurity / VAD | 25x |
| **H. Final Destination PE, additive track** | **min(F2, G)** | **13.4x** |
| Destination range | H ±7.5% | 12.5x to 14.5x |

**Track 1, RRM.** Base r for a small cap is 14%, adjusted up 1.5 points for governance (promoter CONCERN, auditor confirmed fraud findings at three subsidiaries, audit trail exception) to 15.5%. RRM = 1 + (13.5 − 15.5) × 0.12 = 0.76. Destination PE = 13.4 × 0.76 = **10.2x**.

**The two tracks diverge 24%, which is above the 15% threshold, so a governing choice is required. RRM governs, at 10.2x**, because the divergence is driven by governance risk that the additive track does not price and because the more conservative track sets the entry zone.

**Sensitivities you may want to change:**
- If you rule ROCE on the ex goodwill basis of 14.5%, Pillar 1 rises to about 15.4x and the additive destination to about 15.3x.
- If you rule cash GROWTH INDUCED at this gate, the multiplier goes 0.80x to 0.90x and the additive destination to about 14.7x.
- Both together put the additive destination near 17x and the RRM track near 13x.

**What this implies, stated plainly and not priced.** The stock trades at 48.1 times owners' earnings. The destination is 10.2x to 13.4x. That is a large de rating over the holding period, and it means the return has to come from earnings growth outrunning a falling multiple. Role 1 will settle it in Phase 3 with the Hurdle Ratio. I am not calling it here.

### THE EARNINGS BASIS QUESTION, for you to decide

Do you want the exit multiple applied to **one year forward earnings** or to **trailing earnings**?

Forward fits a company mid integration, because seven acquisitions closed part way through FY26 and trailing earnings understate the base that already exists. Trailing is safer here for two reasons: management guidance carries a B grade rather than an A, and the minority interest share of profit is rising fast enough that a forward owners' earnings estimate carries real error. The choice materially moves the entry zone and it is yours, not mine.
