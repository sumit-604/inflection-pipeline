Wine brand story the audited numbers contradict

# BUSINESS UNDERSTANDING NARRATIVE

Fratelli sells two physical things. The first is bottled wine, made from grapes grown on about 400 owned acres and roughly 1,000 contract farmed acres near Akluj in Maharashtra, crushed and aged at the company's own winery of 5.4 million litres, and bottled under more than 50 labels across luxury, super premium, premium and value tiers; bottled wine is about 90% of revenue. The second is Shotgun, a canned ready to drink alcoholic beverage launched in February 2025, sold alongside TiLT wine in a can, together about 10% of FY26 revenue. No customer needs either product. Wine is under 1% of India's alcohol market by volume, so every wine rupee competes against beer and Indian made foreign liquor.

The customers are distributors and retailers across 29 states and union territories, HoReCa venues, state government beverage corporations in corporation model states, the Canteen Stores Department that supplies defence canteens, and export buyers in 15 to 17 countries. The buying behaviour that matters here is payment, not qualification. Government corporations set their own payment cadence, and a substantial share of receivables sits with them. The largest single external customer fell to Rs 21.04 cr, 11.6% of FY26 group revenue, from 17.3% a year earlier. Switching costs are not evidenced anywhere in the record: no reorder rate, no active outlet count, no distributor contract lock in is disclosed.

Demand today is set by the state, not by the consumer. State excise and licensing rules decide where the product can be sold and at what shelf price, and the run names state excise duty and licensing notifications as the first downstream signal to track. CSD wine empanelment and procurement is the second, because that channel was 8% of the Q1FY27 topline. On the cost side the Nashik and Akluj table grape price index drives the input bill. The India EU FTA wine tariff schedule and the USD INR rate together set the landed price of imported wine, and Sula Vineyards and Grover Zampa disclosures are the only independent read on who holds what share.

Management says demand grows through premiumisation and through the canned format. The externally checkable parts of that are thin. A peer buying grapes from the same belt discloses that the wine category grew low single digit at best over two years, and that its own revenue has stayed near flat around Rs 600 cr for three to four years. Fratelli's own audited wine segment result moved from a profit of Rs 18.92 cr in FY24 to losses of Rs 10.49 cr in FY25 and Rs 10.61 cr in FY26 on broadly flat revenue.

The competitive advantage sits in the bottled wine line only, and it is narrow. 15 years of grape clone development, multi year bearer plant gestation and state by state excise licensing are real barriers a new entrant must clear. The forward moat scan scored 13 and graded three categories Moderate: product platform cadence, backward integration into grape supply, and Canteen Stores Department qualification. The two newest scan categories both scored zero against their own tests. The backward looking scorecard confirmed zero moats. The canned RTD line has no moat at all. Shotgun needs no vineyard, and any beverage company with a canning line and a distribution network can enter. That matters because the growth case rests on that line.

Fratelli Vineyards in eight paragraphs

FY26 revenue was Rs 181 cr, all of it wine, and the net loss was Rs 25 cr. Gross margin was 78.1% on the audited notes. Selling, distribution and marketing cost Rs 42 cr, or 23.3% of wine revenue, down from 25.7% the year before. Earnings per share was minus Rs 11. The shares trade at Rs 135 with a market value of Rs 589 cr.

The transition is real as an event and unproven as a result. The listed shell was Tinna Trade, an agri commodity trading business. Wine came in through a share swap, not an IPO. FY24 was 47% wine, FY25 was 59% wine, and FY26 is the first wine only year. Shotgun launched in February 2025 and did about Rs 18 cr through roughly 9,000 outlets in its first year. Management guides to 30% revenue growth and net breakeven in FY27, and to Rs 500 cr of revenue by FY30.

The mechanical scorecard says avoid. Fratelli scores 10 out of 160, with 10 of 100 on the core blocks and zero on moats. Seven of nine deal breakers fire. Growth, cash and returns all sit at the scoring floor, and the history check downgrades the name for a short comparable record. Two cautions belong next to that number. The scoring window is only three years long, and its FY24 anchor is a pooling restated blend of wine and the agri trading book that has since been exited. The scorecard cannot separate the legacy cleanup from the wine business underneath it.

Cash is the hardest fact in the file. Group cash fell 84.5% to Rs 7.78 lakh against Rs 119.74 cr of borrowings. The holding company holds Rs 0.88 lakh and has given a corporate guarantee of Rs 114.50 cr to the wine subsidiary. Receivables are Rs 105 cr on Rs 181 cr of sales, a collection cycle of 212 days. Working capital days went from 149 in FY24 to 349 in FY26. FY26 produced positive operating cash flow of Rs 5.0 cr, but operating profit before working capital changes was minus Rs 3.65 cr, and the cash came from rebate and payables accruals nearly doubling to Rs 15.98 cr. Free cash flow was minus Rs 7.31 cr in FY26 and minus Rs 66.7 cr across FY24 to FY26, a bigger drain than the Rs 39.0 cr of cumulative reported loss.

Management's own numbers do not survive a check against its own filings. FY26 revenue guidance was cut in four straight calls, from 20% to 25% growth and about Rs 250 cr, to 15% to 20%, then 12% to 15%, then about 7%. The year printed Rs 181 cr. On the Q3 FY26 call management said profit before tax turned positive at about Rs 0.1 cr and that profit after tax also turned positive; the results filed four days earlier showed a loss before tax of Rs 4.95 cr and a net loss of Rs 8.72 cr. On the Q4 FY26 call management described FY26 as about 1% growth, using a wine only prior year figure against a blended prior year base, without saying so. The audited consolidated comparison is a decline of 40%. The credibility grade is D on 12 tracked promises: one delivered, three partial, eight missed.

Some things hold up. The 78.1% gross margin is correct and reconciles to the audited notes; an earlier reading that suggested a cost reclassification turned out to be a data vendor artifact, not a company action. There is no promoter share pledge and no promoter selling across both reported years, and the managing director lends money to the company at about 10%. Three independent directors carry checkable outside credentials, including a former managing director of Bunge India. The second largest shareholder, at 9.48%, is the vice chairman and managing director of PI Industries, who also sits on the wine subsidiary board. The distribution network of 31,000 touch points and the Canteen Stores Department listing are documented, not asserted. Against that, the promoter warrant issue of August 2024 lapsed 65.1%, and the entire lapse belongs to unnamed non family allottees.

Peers complicate the story rather than confirm it. Twelve peer transcripts were read. Sula, which buys grapes from the same belt, reported grape and input cost pressure across three straight quarters and quantified a gross margin hit of 350 to 400 basis points, against Fratelli's claim of no major changes in input costs for the same harvest. Sula also describes slow traction in canned wine, the opposite of Fratelli's claimed 30% quarter on quarter growth in the RTD wine category. Across all 12 peer transcripts and roughly 60 analyst questions, the words Fratelli, duopoly, Shotgun and RTD appear zero times, which sits awkwardly against Fratelli's framing of an 85% to 90% two player market with Sula.

The market is large enough and the share claim is not. Total Indian branded wine plus wine based RTD is Rs 2,417 cr to Rs 2,920 cr, and the addressable slice is Rs 1,919 cr. Fratelli's own audited revenue against management's own cited market size gives a 9.4% share, not the one third claimed on two investor deck slides. Revenue headroom is 10.6 times, which is a good runway on the framework's own scale. The gap is pace, not size: the run's realistic three to five year path supports about 9% compound growth, while the Rs 500 cr Vision 2030 target needs 28.9%, with no new winery capacity committed anywhere in the filings. The India EU FTA lands duty cuts near the Rs 2,000 shelf price that Fratelli calls its insulated boundary and its fastest growing tier.
