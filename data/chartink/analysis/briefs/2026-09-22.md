## The picture in one paragraph

The market climbed back from last Tuesday's washout without a thrust day. A
thrust day is a session where 300 or more stocks jump four and a half percent.
The best day this week had 166, on Friday, and each day since has had fewer.
The short-term count repaired fast. About 850 stocks now sit above their
twenty-day line, against about 470 a week ago. The long-term floor did not
repair. Slightly fewer stocks sit above their two-hundred-day line than below
it. Yearly highs doubled, and small companies lead them. Fear fell back to
cheap. Gold's lead over stocks shrank to a third of its size a week ago. The
Nifty sits below its long trend line, while the smallcap and microcap indices
sit above theirs. It is a quiet, thin repair of the short end, with the long
end still undecided.

Tuesday had 102. Each day since Friday brought fewer big risers. The bounce is

thrust can hold. It has less proof behind it.

## What would change this read

The constructive read fails if the fifty-day count falls back under about 700,
its washout-day reading. It fails if a second washout
arrives, 300 or more stocks falling four and a half percent in one session,
before any thrust. That is how March played out. It fails if the two-hundred-day
count drops back to about 1,030, the washout-day reading. It also fails if big
risers keep shrinking under about 50 a day while volume stays below a year ago.
That would show a bounce that ran out of buyers.

The cautious read fails if a thrust day arrives, 300 or more stocks jumping four
and a half percent in one session. It fails if the two-hundred-day count closes
above about 1,150 and holds for a week. That would put more stocks in long
uptrends than out of them. It fails if the large-cap list above the day's
average price climbs above 50 while bank four-week breadth stays above one in
four. It also fails if heavy-volume stocks start to outnumber light-volume ones
on up days.
