## Where the market stands

Each stock in the record has three trend lines: 20 days, 50 days and 200 days. A stock above a line is in an uptrend on that clock. On Wednesday 1,681 stocks stood above the short line, about seven in ten. That is the highest count since 22 June. 1,255 stood above the middle line, a little more than half. 1,124 stood above the long line, a little under half. The short trend belongs to the buyers. The middle trend has now tipped to the buyers too. The long trend is close to even, with the sellers ahead by about 90 stocks.

The year gives these counts their scale. On 23 March the long count hit a low of 286, on a day when 958 stocks fell hard. On 1 April came a great thrust day, when 1,127 stocks rose hard. The long count then climbed through the summer and peaked at 1,364 in late August. It slid through September and fell to 879 on 8 October. A short repair followed, and then the floor broke. On 21 October 411 stocks fell hard in one day. On 23 October only 605 stocks held the long line. Wednesday's 1,124 sits more than 500 stocks above that low. The count last stood higher on 25 September, at 1,133.

The bounce is now fourteen sessions old. On 23 October only 188 stocks held the 20 day line. By Thursday 5 November the count had reached 1,492. Friday 6 November brought the first test, with 151 hard fallers and a short count down to 1,275. Monday was quiet and thin. Wednesday then pushed the short count to a new high for the bounce.

The middle line tells the most new thing. Last night's brief called the 50 day count the swing vote. It named a move past about 1,165 as the point where more than half the market stands above it. Wednesday's 1,255 clears that point with room. The middle count last stood this high on 28 August, at 1,289. A market with more than half its stocks above the 50 day line is in a medium term uptrend by this record's own test.

Wednesday itself was broad and busy. The hard risers outnumbered the hard fallers by almost ten to one. About one stock in three traded four percent above its prior close at some point. Only about one in fifteen traded four percent below it. About three in ten stayed inside a narrow three percent range, fewer than Monday.

The volume backed the move. Turnover came to about 570 crore, the highest since 5 November. A year ago the same session saw about 450. The ratio of heavy volume stocks to light volume stocks reached about one to one. On Monday it was about three to ten. This was not a great thrust day. The 1 April thrust had six times as many hard risers. Still, Wednesday was the first wide up day of the bounce that came with ordinary or better volume behind it.

The top of the market widened while the bottom emptied. New yearly highs came to 66, matching the best of the bounce. New yearly lows came to seven. The record last showed so few on 18 June. About one stock in six sits within fifteen percent of its yearly high. About one in five sits within fifteen percent of its yearly low. Those two groups are now close to equal, the narrowest gap of the past month.

Fear has not eased with the price. The India VIX measures what traders pay for protection against big Nifty swings. It rose a little on Wednesday and closed near 17.6. It is down a little on the week. It is still up more than half over three months and sits about a fifth above its thirty week line. Gold funds fell about five percent over the month. The Nifty rose about eleven percent over the same month. Money has left the safe asset for stocks, and traders still pay up for insurance.

## Thirty points

### For the trader

**1. Wednesday was a real wide day.** 184 stocks rose hard and 19 fell hard, close to ten to one. About one stock in three traded four percent up at some point in the session. This was the widest day since last Thursday. One wide day does not make a trend, but it ends the debate about Monday's rest.

**2. The volume came back, though not to thrust levels.** Turnover rose to about 570 crore from about 400 on Monday. Heavy volume stocks roughly equalled light volume stocks. The 1 April thrust had about six times as many hard risers. Wednesday was a good day, and it was not a great one.

**3. The first test from last night's brief nearly passed.** It asked for 200 or more hard risers on turnover above about 610 crore. Wednesday brought 184 on about 570. Both numbers fell just short. A trader should count this as a near miss and not as a pass.

**4. The swing vote has tipped.** 1,255 stocks hold the 50 day line, against 1,085 below it. That is more than half the market for the first time since 28 August. Last night's brief named this the count to watch first. It has moved before the long count, as the swing vote should.

## What would change this read

The read is an early uptrend that widened on Wednesday, led by banks, autos and healthcare, with the middle trend now in the buyers' hands. It turns worse if a day of 300 or more hard fallers returns and goes unanswered. A 50 day count back under half the market, about 1,170, would undo Wednesday's main gain. A short count back under about 1,270, the 6 November level, would say the bounce is losing its grip. A long count back under about 1,050, Monday's level, would erase this week's progress. A long count under about 605, the 23 October low, would erase the whole bounce. New yearly lows above about 100 would say sellers have returned. A VIX close above about 18 would say fear has taken charge. A sharp fall in banks on heavy volume would remove the group now carrying the market.

The cautious read fails if the long clock clears half and the fear gauge falls. The first test is the long count above about 1,170, half the market. Wednesday reached 1,124. The second test is a wide up day, with 200 or more hard risers, on turnover above about 610 crore. Wednesday reached 184 on about 570. The third test is a VIX close under 15. Wednesday closed near 17.6. The fourth test is gains held into the close. About half of the stocks that trade four percent up should keep it. Wednesday held fewer than four in ten. Two of these in the same week, with the 50 day count still above half, would confirm the uptrend on every clock.
