## PLAIN-LANGUAGE SUMMARY

Satin held its June-quarter earnings call and, unusually for a small company, the
chairman himself answered every question, which is a good sign for candour. The numbers
were strong on the surface: profit of about Rs 123 crore, the best June quarter in eight
years, bad loans down to 2.2%, and loan book up 27%. Management was refreshingly open
about one thing: it deliberately set aside an extra Rs 36 crore cushion this quarter and
reported lower profit than it could have, because it wants steady returns through good
and bad years rather than a flashy number today. On the two questions we most wanted
answered, they delivered: their foreign loan of about Rs 1,573 crore is fully hedged
(net effect just minus Rs 3 crore), and the lumpy loan-sale income sits at a steady
20-22% of the book. They also finally gave the write-off number: Rs 127 crore was
written off, and slippages (fresh bad loans) actually halved from Rs 90 to Rs 49 crore,
which is genuinely encouraging. But here is the catch: that same Rs 127 crore write-off
is what pushed the bad-loan ratio down, so part of the "improvement" is loans being
removed from the books, not fully recovered. And they still would not say how the write-
off splits between real charge-offs and sales to asset-reconstruction companies, would
not commit to how big the cushion will get, and once again did not give a group-level
return-on-equity number, which is the single figure our thesis watches most. No analyst
even asked the most important sector risk, which is whether the same borrowers are being
lent larger and larger amounts. Verdict: PROCEED WITH CAVEATS, unchanged, credibility
Grade B, decision status held at Watchlist/Buy; the call reassured but did not clear the
core asset-quality question.

## SECTOR KNOWLEDGE (append to sectors/NBFC-MFI.md)

NBFC-MFI lessons from the Satin Q1 FY27 concall (2026-07-31; weigh, never anchor):
- **Buffer / overlay cycle-proofing is now an explicit management philosophy, and a
  discretionary earnings lever.** Satin runs an on-book provision (Rs 250 Cr) well above
  the RBI-required minimum (Rs 152 Cr) and a management overlay it builds "through the
  good quarters" with no committed ceiling. For MFI analysis this means reported credit
  cost and ROA are partly discretionary: the same book can show 1.97% or 3.06% credit
  cost depending on the overlay. Always demand both reported and ex-overlay, and a buffer
  policy tied to Stage-2 coverage; absent that, treat the overlay as a smoothing lever.
- **Guardrails 2.0 sector share shift: banks are ceding micro-loan share to NBFC-MFIs.**
  Bank share of micro-loans fell to 26.4% from 32.6% YoY while NBFC-MFI share rose to
  43.7% from 38.9%; industry GLP stabilised (~Rs 3.31 lakh Cr) after 8 quarters of
  decline, and 95% of exposure now sits with borrowers having <=3 lenders. Specialist
  lenders carry more of the cycle than before, so their underwriting behaviour now sets
  sector quality. Post-guardrails, the sector cycle position is "repair -> early
  expansion."
- **NatCat / natural-catastrophe insurance is a live MFI risk-mitigation tool.** Satin's
  Assam flood exposure (~44,000 borrowers, Rs 149 Cr) was ~Rs 97 Cr NatCat-covered,
  leaving net residual ~1%. For flood/drought-prone MFI geographies, NatCat coverage +
  a management overlay can contain a state-level shock to a low single-digit net hit —
  worth checking peer MFIs for equivalent coverage before pricing weather risk.
- **DA (direct assignment) run-rate norm ~20-22% of AUM.** Satin frames DA income as
  quarterly-lumpy (Q4 seasonally heavy) but the assigned book as a steady 20-22% of
  consolidated AUM. Use that band to judge whether an MFI's gain-on-sale income is
  structural or timing; a spike above it flags earnings pulled forward.
- **Management-named stable-state bands (Satin, weigh as guidance not fact):** steady-
  state NIM ~14.35-14.50%; stable-state credit cost aspiration 2.5-3.0% for unsecured MFI;
  reported credit cost guide 3.0-3.5% inclusive of buffer; standalone ROA 3.5-4% reported.
  New-branch breakeven ~1,000 customers. These are useful sector reference points for
  unit economics and for the credit-cost-vs-asset-quality divergence (write-offs can pull
  GNPA down even as credit cost rises when the buffer is being built).
- **Over-indebtedness watch, unprompted:** the most important MFI risk — whether AUM
  growth comes from new borrowers or from larger tickets on the same near-static borrower
  base — can go entirely unasked on a congratulatory boutique-analyst call. Build it into
  the question list every quarter regardless of whether the Street raises it.
