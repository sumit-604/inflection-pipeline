## 2. Sector Intelligence (Indian NBFC / MSME lending cycle)

Paisalo is a non-bank lender to small and micro businesses — one of the fastest-growing but most cyclically-sensitive corners of Indian finance. The structural tailwind is real and is a Notion thesis premise: tens of millions of MSME and micro-enterprises remain underserved by banks, and NBFCs with deep last-mile distribution (Paisalo cites 5,995 touchpoints across 22 states) plug that gap. Co-lending with banks (Paisalo's SBI MSME tie-up) is the policy-blessed model that lets an NBFC originate on a bank's balance sheet and earn fees, growing assets without consuming its own capital. [Sector framing: Notion/prior work. This quarter's filing confirms the distribution scale and the "new verticals / partnerships" direction (lines 248-249) but discloses no co-lending economics.]

The headwind is the cost of money and the credit cycle. NBFCs borrow wholesale and lend retail; when funding costs rise or asset quality turns, the model is squeezed from both ends. Paisalo's cost of borrowing improving 64bps to 10.1% is a genuine positive in the current rate environment. The regulatory backdrop matters too: the RBI has been tightening scrutiny of NBFC growth, co-lending disclosures, and micro-lending conduct, which raises the value of clean asset quality (Paisalo's 0.70% GNPA is a genuine strength) and the cost of opacity (its non-disclosure of cost-to-income and fee income runs against the disclosure-transparency direction the sector is being pushed toward). The classic sector risk to watch — disbursement growth sprinting far ahead of the seasoned book (here +128% vs +28%) — is exactly the pattern that precedes asset-quality surprises in this space if collection infrastructure lags; collection efficiency was not disclosed. [Sector risk model: prior/protocol work; the divergence itself is this quarter's filing, FN4.]

## 3. Business-Model Intelligence (how Paisalo makes money; co-lending unit economics; model drift this quarter)

Paisalo makes money two ways. First, net interest income — it borrows at ~10.1% and lends higher, keeping a spread; net interest margin was 6.6% and NII was Rs144.7 crore this quarter. Second, fee/commission income — principally from co-lending, where it originates loans that sit (partly) on a partner bank's book and earns an origination/servicing fee rather than carrying the full asset and its funding cost. In a healthy co-lending model, fee income rises as a share of total income and returns on equity improve because assets grow off the NBFC's own capital. [Business model: Notion/prior work; NII and NIM are this quarter's filing.]

This quarter shows possible model drift, though we cannot confirm it because the disclosure is thin. Three clues point the same way: (a) disbursement grew 4.6x faster than on-book AUM (+128% vs +28%) — a lot was lent that is not fully showing up on the balance sheet, consistent with off-book co-lending origination; (b) net interest margin was flat despite a 64bps fall in borrowing cost — the spread did not widen, consistent with a growing share of lower-yield or fee-based (rather than interest-earning) business; and (c) the company withheld both the fee-income line and the cost-to-income ratio. If co-lending is scaling, fee income should be up and disclosed — its absence (FN7) is conspicuous. The bear reading is that off-book origination is diluting spreads (the UGRO analogue in our Notion notes) while the headline AUM and PAT still look fine. The bull reading is simply short-tenor churn in a growing book. We cannot adjudicate from these documents; hence the questions and the wait. [Drift clues: this quarter's filing, FN4/FN7; UGRO analogue: Notion.]

## 4. Competition Intelligence (vs Muthoot, IIFL, UGRO, Satin, Aye, Northern Arc, etc.)

Paisalo competes in a crowded MSME/micro-lending field. Where it looks structurally strong: **asset quality** — GNPA 0.70% / NNPA 0.49% is better than most microfinance-adjacent lenders (Satin and other MFI-heavy names have run materially higher stress through recent cycles), and **capital** — CAR 33.1% is far above regulatory minimums and above most leveraged NBFC peers, giving it room to grow and a buffer if the cycle turns. Its **cost of borrowing** at 10.1% and improving is competitive for a mid-size NBFC, though large diversified players (Muthoot, IIFL) still fund cheaper on stronger ratings. [Peer positioning: Notion/prior peer work; Paisalo's own metrics: this quarter's filing.]

Where it is structurally weaker or unproven: **return on equity** at 13.4% is modest — well below the best-in-class NBFCs and below what would earn a premium price-to-book, which is why our framework caps the sector and keeps us disciplined on entry. Its **co-lending economics are unproven and undisclosed**, exactly the area where a focused competitor like UGRO has shown that co-lending fee spreads can be thinner than hoped (our Notion "margin-dilutive / UGRO analogue" flag). And on **efficiency**, the withheld cost-to-income ratio is the competitive tell: peers that run genuinely lean (helped by scale or digital origination) disclose improving cost ratios; Paisalo touts "AI-led efficiencies" and a 2% headcount cut but hides the ratio that would prove it — against peers like Aye Finance and Northern Arc that lean on tech-driven cost advantage, an undisclosed, historically-above-35% cost-to-income is a competitive question mark, not a proven edge. **The competitive risk to watch:** that Paisalo is buying growth (disbursement +128%) through lower-yield off-book co-lending while its cost base stays high — strong on the balance sheet, unproven on the P&L efficiency where the best peers win. [Competitive read: Notion/prior peer work reconciled with this quarter's disclosed and withheld metrics.]
