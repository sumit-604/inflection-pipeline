## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

**2A. Demand environment.** The peer set tells a two-phase demand story. 2023 through mid-2024 was weak on both sides ("last year and a half were slow... not due to structural changes... due to the post-COVID relaxation" pipeline hangover) (BODALCHEM, Q2 FY25 call, Ankit Patel, p.7), normalizing by "more than 6 months back" as of the Feb-2025 call (BODALCHEM, Q3 FY25 call, Ankit Patel, p.18-19) — an organic, volume-led recovery. A second, unrelated disruption begins around Feb-Mar 2026: a geopolitical energy/input shock (West Asia conflict, described as closing the Strait of Hormuz) that spikes ammonia and sulphur costs and, per SHREEPUSHK, causes buyers to "pause" purchases at the new higher prices before absorbing them (SHREEPUSHK, Q4 FY26 call, Punit Makharia, p.5-6; Q1 FY27 call, Punit Makharia, p.7-8). This second phase is a cost shock, not a demand-pull event, and it complicates any reading of SHHARICH's FY26/Q1 FY27 growth as organic demand strength.

**2B. Pricing and input costs.** See Q1/Q4/Q9 above: prices for H-Acid, Vinyl Sulphone and K-Acid all rose sharply (roughly 40-45%) between Feb and Aug 2026, tracking a 2x-4x spike in sulphur and ammonia costs. This is a cost-push price rise with a documented lag between input cost rise and finished-good price catch-up (both peers describe this lag independently), matching the shape, though not necessarily the full-year timing, of SHHARICH's own stated "increase in Raw Material Price" margin narrative.

**2C. Capex cycle.** The two peers split cleanly on capital allocation, which is itself informative: this is not an industry-wide capacity race. SHREEPUSHK is the aggressive, self-funded expander, running three capex programmes (~Rs 175cr completed, ~Rs 155cr Unit 5/6 near-complete, ~Rs 350-512cr Unit 8/Meghnagar underway), funded through internal accruals plus promoter preferential allotments and explicitly avoiding external debt (net debt/equity -0.01x as of Mar-2026) (SHREEPUSHK, Q4 FY26 call, opening remarks, and Deepak Beriwala throughout). BODALCHEM, by contrast, explicitly rules out major capex for 4-6 quarters across three consecutive calls, prioritising debt paydown toward a debt/EBITDA target near 2.5x after its own leveraged benzene-derivatives greenfield project underdelivered (BODALCHEM, Q4 FY25 call, Ankit Patel, p.14; Q1 FY26 call, Mayur Padhya, p.9-10). SHHARICH's promoter-warrant-funded expansion sits structurally closer to the SHREEPUSHK pattern (promoter capital funding growth, low external leverage) than to BODALCHEM's post-capex deleveraging posture.

**2D. Competitive mentions.** BODALCHEM's management, asked directly to name dye-intermediate competitors, lists "Kiri Industries is one of them, AksharChem is one of them, Shree Pushkar is another player" (BODALCHEM, Q3 FY25 call, Ankit Patel, p.15) — three names, and SHHARICH (Shree Hari Chemicals) is not among them, nor mentioned anywhere else across all eight substantive transcripts. This is an informative silence: despite SHHARICH describing itself as "one of the largest H-Acid producers in India" (step1-business-brief.md), the largest branded peer's management does not name it when specifically asked, consistent with SHHARICH's much smaller scale (~Rs 180cr revenue) placing it below the radar of listed-peer competitive commentary.

**2E. Risks peers discuss that SHHARICH's AR does not.**
- A named, dated, quantified raw-material shock (sulphur/ammonia, tied to a specific geopolitical trigger) versus SHHARICH's unquantified "increase in Raw Material Price" (SHREEPUSHK, Q4 FY26 and Q1 FY27 calls).
- The advance-payment-input/credit-sale-output working-capital mismatch that becomes acute exactly when input prices spike (SHREEPUSHK, Q1 FY27 call, Punit Makharia, p.12) — a mechanism SHHARICH's AR does not name despite its own sub-1.0 current ratio.
- China's export competitiveness eroding (VAT refund withdrawal) and a reversed trade flow (India net-exporting H-Acid to China) — a structural tailwind SHHARICH's AR frames instead as a generic Chinese-competition threat, the opposite direction (SHREEPUSHK, Q3 FY26 call).
- Bangladesh political instability and its knock-on effect on textile-linked export demand, actively tracked and quantified (~7-8% of SHREEPUSHK's revenue) (SHREEPUSHK, Q3 FY26 and Q4 FY26 calls) — a geography-specific customer-concentration risk SHHARICH's AR does not surface.
- Electricity/grid-connection delay as a concrete capacity-expansion execution risk, repeatedly pushing SHREEPUSHK's Unit 5/6 start date across three consecutive calls (SHREEPUSHK, Q2 FY26 through Q4 FY26 calls) — a risk category entirely absent from SHHARICH's AR, which discloses no capacity or timeline detail at all for its SDPL expansion.
- Overseas-subsidiary currency/hyperinflation accounting drag, quantified every quarter (BODALCHEM's Turkey unit, AS 29 losses of Rs 1.45-2.44cr per quarter across all four calls) — illustrates a disclosure standard for subsidiary risk that SHHARICH's own SDPL-related disclosure does not meet.

## PART 5: CROSS-PEER HYPOTHESIS

Hypothesis: the sector is bifurcating along balance-sheet flexibility, not product mix, in how it responds to the same FY26 raw-material shock. Neither peer states this axis explicitly — each narrates only its own situation — but placing the two balance sheets and the two managements' stated behaviour side by side during the identical industry shock makes the pattern visible: low-leverage converters can arbitrage a spread cycle by throttling volume, while levered converters cannot and must keep running. This is testable against SHHARICH's own low-leverage, promoter-warrant-funded structure: if SHHARICH's FY26/Q1 FY27 behaviour shows deliberate volume restraint alongside rising realization (the SHREEPUSHK pattern), that would support the low-leverage-arbitrage read of its own inflection; if it shows rising volume at flat or falling realization typical of a levered converter forced to keep running, that would argue against it. SHHARICH's AR does not disclose enough (no volume/realization split) to resolve this here, but it is a concrete, checkable question for FTTCP.
