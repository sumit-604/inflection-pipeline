## 1. SIGNED MENTAL MODEL (v2)

**Status: SIGNED by operator, 29 August 2026.**

### Part A — The FROM state

**A1. Archetype.** Outsourcing partner (IT services), single reportable segment. Dominant variables per the archetype library: client concentration, wallet share, capacity fill, contract stickiness, price per unit.

**A2. The analogy.** An Indore workshop renting engineers by the month to London and New York. It pays its craftsmen in rupees and bills clients in pounds and dollars, so every rupee of local wage buys more foreign revenue than it costs. About ten clients supply two-thirds of the orders. Three supply about 40 per cent.

**A3. Tension flagged, not resolved.** A cost-arbitrage archetype should not print ROCE of 27 to 33 per cent. Either the company is further along the transition than its disclosures prove, or the ROCE is flattered by the accounting and concentration issues in Part E. FTTCP should test this directly rather than accept the ROCE at face value.

### Part B — The transition

**FROM:** R2 cost-advantaged converter. Margin from cost position, India delivery billed in GBP and USD, with a narrow and replicable moat.

**TO (claimed):** R3 value-added / spec'd supplier. Partial pricing power from spec-in and embedded-team switching costs, via a stated shift toward AI, ML and GenAI work and early solution licensing.

**The engine.** Two things must physically change. Revenue mix shifts toward AI/ML/Data/Cloud work at higher realisation per employee. Delivery moves from pure staff augmentation toward embedded product-team pods and owned licensable IP.

**The proof gate.** Revenue per employee keeps rising from its FY26 base of about Rs 29.4 lakh, AND the AI/ML/Data/Cloud share of technology-line revenue holds at or above its 46 per cent FY26 level, for two consecutive disclosed periods, with revenue growth outpacing headcount growth.

**Operator amendment to the proof gate (signed).** The mix-share leg counts ONLY if the figure appears in a filed document, meaning the annual report or a results filing. The 46 per cent figure currently comes from a pie chart in an unaudited investor deck with no prior-year comparison. A gate measurable only from a slide the company can redraw is not a gate.

**Transition falsifier.** The AI/ML/Data/Cloud share falls back toward or below its pre-FY26 level, and/or revenue per employee flattens or reverses, over two consecutive disclosed periods.

**Current proof-gate status: NOT PROVING.** See Part C.

### Part C — The engine has stalled, and one year of it was not real

| Year | Revenue (Rs cr) | Staff | Revenue per head (Rs L) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| FY23 | 52.3 | 312 | 16.8 |
| FY24 | 56.6 | 275 | 20.6 |
| FY25 | 67.1 | 276 | 24.3 |
| FY26 | 90.4 | ~308 | ~29.4 |

**FY24 is an artifact.** Revenue grew 8 per cent while headcount fell 37. Most of that year's per-head gain is shrinkage, not improvement. FY25 and FY26 are genuine: revenue outgrew headcount in both.

**Within FY26 the climb stopped.** Annualised, H1 FY26 ran at about Rs 30.7 lakh per head, H2 FY26 at about Rs 29.2 lakh. The full-year figure rises only because the strong half is blended into the weak one.

**Revenue by half (Rs cr):** 28.7, 27.9, 28.8, 38.4, 45.5, 44.9.

Flat for eighteen months, a surge for twelve months, then flat. H2 FY26 revenue sits marginally below H1 FY26; PAT fell 23 per cent half on half, from 18.3 to 14.0; EBITDA margin fell from 42.2 per cent to about 32 per cent. Against the prior-year half, H2 FY26 was still up about 17 per cent, so the business is not shrinking. It has stopped climbing.

**The co-movement that matters.** In the same half the surge peaked, top-three client concentration rose to 46-48 per cent. Surge and concentration arrived together. That is the signature of a small number of large contracts running and finishing, and FTTCP should treat the one-off hypothesis as the base case until H1 FY27 disproves it.
