## Business Understanding Narrative

Omnitech machines metal to five microns and ships it, mostly abroad. It makes high-precision
engineered components and assemblies at three facilities totalling 80,803 square metres, sells to over
256 customers in 24 countries, and takes about 78% of revenue from outside India (IP p.12). North
America alone is 52.5% of the June quarter (IP p.9). The customer list is the kind an engineering
company spends two decades earning: SLB, Weatherford, Halliburton, Suzlon and Lufkin in energy;
Oshkosh, Donaldson and Aerotech in industrial equipment (IP p.13).

The unit of production is machining hours. Installed capacity went from 26,49,504 hours to 31,98,624
annualised, up 20.7% in a quarter (IP p.12). The economics are unusually good for contract machining:
EBITDA margin was 30.4% and profit after tax margin 17.8% in the June quarter (IP p.6). About 5.3% of
reported revenue is scrap sales and government export incentives rather than product, Rs 64 million
and Rs 24 million of Rs 1,666.6 million (IP p.9).

What is changing is scale, and the number is startling. The order book went Rs 575 million in FY23, to
Rs 839 million in FY24, to Rs 2,837 million in FY25, to Rs 30,550 million at 31 July 2026 (IP p.10).
That is close to eleven times in about sixteen months, and it includes "a multi-year order received
from Weatherford with total order value of >US$100mn" (IP p.10). Against FY26 revenue the book is
several years of work.

Why now: the company listed in February 2026, repaid debt, and is building two new facilities. Net
debt to equity fell from 2.87 times in FY24 to 0.34 in FY26 (IP p.8). The prospectus objects include up
to Rs 500 million of debt repayment and Rs 2,335.58 million for "Setting up of New Projects at
Proposed Facility 1 and Proposed Facility 2" (Monitoring p.10). A heavily levered private company
became an unlevered listed one with an order book it could not previously have financed.

What must be true: the order book must convert into revenue at something like current margins, and the
working capital to carry it must not swallow the returns.

What breaks it: two things. Working capital already runs at 294 days of revenue at FY26 and 233
annualised in the June quarter (IP p.8), which is eight to ten months of sales tied up before the book
even starts converting. And the deck cannot decide what FY26 revenue was. Page 7 says Rs 3,429.1
million; page 12 says Rs 5,113.0 million (IP p.7, p.12). A 49% disagreement on the top line, inside one
document, is where this card stops being able to underwrite anything.

## 2. Business model and archetype
Sell machining capacity against a customer's drawing, to a tolerance most shops cannot hold. Revenue
is hours times a rate, and the rate is set by how few suppliers can do the work. Five-micron precision,
AS9100:2016 aerospace approval at both Metoda and Chhapara, and NADCAP certification in progress for
surface treatment, non-destructive testing and welding (IP p.9, p.12) are the barriers that let a
contract machinist earn 30% EBITDA.

Archetype: **Outsourcing partner**, the CDMO and EMS shape from the library. The dials fit exactly:
client concentration, wallet share, capacity fill and contract stickiness. Capacity fill is disclosed
in machining hours, contract stickiness in the order book and the certifications, and wallet share in
a customer list of 256. Client concentration is the one dial the company does not disclose, and it is
the one that matters most here.

## 3. Competitive advantages
Better evidenced than most in this run, and the evidence is external.
- **Qualification barriers.** Aerospace approval under AS9100:2016 at both named plants, with NADCAP
  in progress (IP p.9). Aerospace and oilfield qualification takes years and does not transfer.
- **Precision as a spec.** Up to 5 microns, 0.005 millimetres (IP p.12). That is a narrow field of
  competitors.
- **The customer list is the moat, restated.** SLB, Weatherford, Halliburton, Suzlon, Lufkin, Oshkosh,
  Donaldson (IP p.13). These companies do not re-source safety-critical parts casually.
- **Margin as evidence.** A 30.4% EBITDA margin in contract machining is not achievable without
  pricing power of some kind (IP p.6). This is the strongest single piece of evidence on the card.
- **Location economics.** Rajkot labour and overheads serving North American customers at 52.5% of
  revenue (IP p.9).

Against: the same customer list is a concentration risk, energy is 49.4% of revenue and 77.5% of the
order book (IP p.9, p.10), and oilfield services capex is one of the most cyclical end markets there
is. **Verdict: a real moat, narrowly based.** The barrier is qualification, and qualification is
customer-specific.

**The revenue row has to be written twice, and that is the finding.** Page 7 of the Q1 FY27 deck puts
FY26 revenue at Rs 3,429.1 million. Page 12 of the same deck puts FY25 at Rs 3,429.1 million and FY26
at Rs 5,113.0 million. Both cannot be right. The quarterly figures favour page 12: Q1 FY26 of 1,032.1
plus Q4 FY26 of 1,487.2 is already 2,519.3, which would leave only 909.8 for the two middle quarters
combined if the year were 3,429.1, less than Q1 alone in a year of rising revenue. Under page 12's
FY26 of 5,113.0, the middle two quarters average about 1,297, which sits neatly between 1,032 and
1,487. **The card's working assumption is that FY26 revenue is Rs 5,113.0 million and page 7's FY26
column is FY25 data mislabelled.** It is an assumption, not a fact, and it is stated as such. Note the
consequence: on Rs 5,113.0 million of revenue, FY26 EBITDA of Rs 1,176.5 million is a 23.0% margin, not
the 34.3% the deck prints.

**The June quarter's profit growth is not what the headline says.** Profit before tax rose 425.7% and
profit after tax 468.7% year on year (IP p.5). The deck attaches a note: "PBT for Q1 FY27 includes an
impact of change in depreciation method (WDV to SLM) effective this quarter" (IP p.5, p.6, p.7).
Depreciation fell from Rs 105.0 million in Q1 FY26 and Rs 134.7 million in Q4 FY26 to Rs 62.7 million
(IP p.7), on a capital base that grew. A swing of roughly Rs 40 to Rs 70 million against profit before
tax of Rs 396.8 million is somewhere between a tenth and a fifth of the quarter's pre-tax profit, and
the company does not size it. The honest reading is revenue up 61.5% and EBITDA up 90.8%, which are
clean, and profit growth that cannot be compared year on year.

Sequentially the quarter is flat, not explosive: revenue up 12.1%, EBITDA up 1.7%, profit after tax up
1.4%, and the EBITDA margin down from 33.5% to 30.4% (IP p.6).

**Working capital is the discipline problem.** 197 days, 283, 294, then 233 annualised (IP p.8). Even
233 days is nearly eight months of revenue. The Infomerics schedule shows a Rs 195.00 crore cash credit
line within Rs 400 crore of rated facilities (Rating p.5), which is what carrying that cycle costs.
Return on capital employed fell to 11.7% in FY26 from 23.8% in FY24 (IP p.8), part of which is the
IPO's fresh equity sitting in the denominator before it has been deployed.

**Inflection classification: EARNINGS-LED on revenue and EBITDA, UNVERIFIABLE on profit.** Cash
conversion is **INDETERMINATE**: no cash flow statement is held, and with a 294-day working capital
cycle this is the gap that matters most. The framework caps it at PROCEED WITH CAVEATS with the
missing evidence named.

## 6. The transition and the quality ladder
FROM **R3 VALUE-ADDED / SPEC'D SUPPLIER.** This company already sits there. Five-micron precision,
spec-in with SLB and Weatherford, switching costs from qualification, and a 30% EBITDA margin that no
price-taker earns. The FY24 return on capital of 23.8% is R3 territory.
TO **R4 FRANCHISE / SHARE-OF-WALLET LEADER.** The claim is not a change of business, it is a change of
position: become the supplier a global oilfield and aerospace customer defaults to, with capacity and
certifications nobody else in the region carries.
Mechanism: convert an eleven-fold order book using IPO-funded capacity, and add aerospace and defence
on top of energy. "Omnitech's existing plants at Metoda and Chhapra have been approved for Aerospace
under AS9100:2016. NADCAP certification is currently in progress... From Defense & Aerospace segment,
FA Development Orders are in progress" (IP p.9).
**Management's claim, quoted once:** "Order Book as on 31st July 2026 of >INR 30,550 million
indicating strong order inflows; opportunity pipeline continues to be robust" (IP p.9).
**Ladder ruling: one rung, and the company starts from a genuinely high rung.** That is a good setup.
The caution is that R4 requires the wallet share to be durable, and 77.5% of the order book sitting in
energy, with one order above US$100 million, is concentration rather than franchise.

## 8. Proof check
Split, and the split follows exactly the line between numbers that are clean and numbers that are not.

**Clean, and moving the right way.** Revenue rose 61.5% year on year to Rs 1,666.6 million (IP p.6).
EBITDA rose 90.8% to Rs 506.2 million, so the margin widened from 25.7% to 30.4% and operating leverage
is real in rupees. Installed capacity rose 20.7% in one quarter. Net debt to equity fell from 1.60 to
0.34. An outside agency upgraded the credit two notches on the long-term scale. The monitoring agency
confirms the IPO money went where the prospectus said. Every one of those is checkable and none
depends on the contested figures.

**Not clean.** Profit before tax growth of 425.7% is inflated by an unquantified change in
depreciation method (IP p.5). Return on capital employed fell from 21.6% to 11.7% in FY26 (IP p.8).
Working capital days rose for three consecutive years to 294. And the FY26 revenue base, against which
all of this is measured, is stated two ways in one document.

**The proof point, named: order book conversion.** The book went from Rs 2,837 million to Rs 30,550
million. The test is not whether orders arrive, it is whether they turn into revenue at these margins
without the working capital cycle lengthening again. The June quarter converted Rs 1,666.6 million
against a book of Rs 30,550 million, which is a run rate of about eighteen quarters of backlog.

**The proof gate has FIRED on order book and revenue, and is NOT VERIFIABLE on profit or returns.**
That is an unusual verdict and it is the honest one.

## 9. Flags from the documents
The first two are the ones that decide this card.
- **FY26 revenue is stated two ways in the same deck.** Rs 3,429.1 million on page 7, Rs 5,113.0
  million on page 12 (IP p.7, p.12). A 49% gap on the primary top line. Every multiple, growth rate and
  order-book cover on this company depends on which is right.
- **Profit before tax growth is inflated by an unsized accounting change.** The move from written-down
  value to straight-line depreciation took the quarterly charge from Rs 105.0 million and Rs 134.7
  million to Rs 62.7 million (IP p.7). The deck discloses that the change affects profit before tax but
  never says by how much, while printing "425.7%" as the growth headline.
- **The order book components exceed the order book.** Energy 23,739 plus Motion Control 3,841 plus
  Industrial Equipment 4,070 is 31,650 against a stated 30,550, and the percentages total 103.17%
  (IP p.10).
- **The Q3 FY26 deck exists in two uploads with Energy and Industrial Equipment transposed.** One reads
  Energy 21,568 at 74.1%, the other Energy 3,800 at 13.1%.
- **ROCE and ROE labels are reversed between pages 8 and 12**, and the geography split differs between
  pages 9 and 13 of the same deck.
- **Working capital of 294 days at FY26** (IP p.8), against a Rs 195 crore cash credit line
  (Rating p.5).
- **Return on capital employed halved**, 23.8% to 11.7% across FY24 to FY26 (IP p.8).
- **Concentration.** Energy is 49.4% of quarterly revenue and 77.5% of the order book, with one
  Weatherford order above US$100 million (IP p.9, p.10). Oilfield services capex is deeply cyclical and
  no customer-level revenue share is disclosed.
- **5.3% of reported revenue is scrap and export incentives**, Rs 88 million of Rs 1,666.6 million
  (IP p.9). Disclosed properly, and it should be stripped before comparing margins to peers.
- **The listed entity's CIN dates to 2021** while the deck claims operation since FY2007 (Rating p.1;
  IP p.12). A corporate-history question, not an error, but it means any long-run history of this
  ticker needs care.
- **Promoters carry personal guarantees** on Union Bank facilities (RHP p.311).
- **Six months listed, no annual report, no audited year as a listed company.**

Items three through five may be artefacts of the chunk reader's table parsing rather than errors in
the filed PDF, and should be checked against the source before being held against the company. Items
one and two are in plain tables with explanatory notes and need resolving either way.

## 10. Independent check: the credit rating
**PARTIAL, and favourable as far as it goes.** Infomerics Valuation and Rating Ltd upgraded Omnitech
on 18 May 2026: long-term from IVR BBB+/Stable to IVR A/Stable, short-term from IVR A3+ to IVR A1, on
total bank facilities enhanced from Rs 314.18 crore to Rs 400.00 crore (Rating p.1, p.2). A two-notch
long-term upgrade is a real signal, and the facility schedule is fully disclosed: six term loans
totalling Rs 109.81 crore maturing between January 2029 and July 2032, a Rs 195.00 crore cash credit
line, Rs 15.00 crore of export packing credit, and Rs 70.08 crore proposed long-term (Rating p.5).

Three caveats the operator should weigh. The held document is the **rating letter, not the press
release**: it carries the conclusion and the numbers but none of the agency's reasoning. The letter
says so itself, "The press release for the rating(s) will be communicated to you shortly" (Rating p.2),
and that press release is not in the inventory. The upgrade rests on "FY25 (A) and 9MFY26 (UA)"
(Rating p.2), so it predates both the completed IPO and the FY26 full year. And Infomerics is a smaller
agency than CRISIL, ICRA, CARE or India Ratings; its disclaimer notes it "does not conduct any audit,
due diligence or independent verification of the rated issuer" (Rating p.4), which is standard language
but worth reading here.

A second independent voice does appear. CRISIL Ratings acts as monitoring agency for the IPO proceeds
and reports: "Whether all utilization is as per the disclosures in the Offer Document? Yes", with no
change to the means of finance and "no major deviation observed over the earlier monitoring agency
reports" (Monitoring p.6). That is narrow assurance, covering only the use of issue proceeds, and it is
clean.

## 11. Posture: the Transition Decision Matrix
- **Proof gate: FIRED on order book and revenue, NOT VERIFIABLE on profit.** Step 8.
- **Ugliness: ARTIFACT-OF-CLIMB, provisionally.** The falling return on capital and the 294-day working
  capital cycle are what an order book growing eleven times does to a balance sheet before it converts,
  and the IPO equity sits in the ROCE denominator undeployed. That reading is plausible and it is not
  yet evidenced, because the cash flow statement is not held.
- **Recognition gap: CLOSED.** Rs 6,672 crore of market value against FY26 profit after tax of Rs 43.9
  crore is about 152 times. Against the June quarter annualised at Rs 118.9 crore it is about 56 times,
  and that quarter's profit is the one inflated by the depreciation change. On any reading the market
  has already priced the order book converting.

**Posture: EARNINGS-ONLY, shading toward PRICED NARRATIVE.** The climb is real and the market has paid
for it in advance. Returns from here ride earnings growth alone, and at 56 times a flattered quarter
the earnings growth required is very large.

## 12. Verdict card

**WATCH.** Not a PASS, and not yet a PROCEED.

The business deserves a deep run. An order book of Rs 30,550 million against Rs 2,837 million fifteen
months earlier, held by SLB, Weatherford, Halliburton and Oshkosh, machined to five microns, at a 30%
EBITDA margin, with aerospace certification landed and a two-notch credit upgrade behind it, is not a
story stock. Seven of twelve triggers delivered inside a year. Whatever else is true, this company
executes.

It does not get a PROCEED today for one reason. **Its own investor presentation states FY26 revenue two
different ways, forty-nine per cent apart, and the headline profit growth is inflated by an accounting
change the company declines to size.** A `/step1` run on these inputs would be building a valuation on
a top line the company has not settled and a profit trend that cannot be compared year on year. The
framework's own rule applies: never estimate a missing number, and NOT FOUND is the only valid fill.
Here the number is not missing, it is doubled, which is worse.

The good news is that the block is cheap to clear. The audited FY26 results and the three earnings call
transcripts Bull AI already holds will resolve both questions in one session. This is a WATCH with a
named unblocking action, not a WATCH on a judgement call.

State the bear case at the same bar as the bull. Working capital at 294 days and a Rs 195 crore cash
credit line. Return on capital employed halved in two years. Energy at 77.5% of the order book with one
US$100 million-plus customer, in the most cyclical capital cycle there is. Six months listed, no annual
report, no audited year as a listed company, promoter holding unknown, and about 56 times a flattered
quarter's annualised earnings.

**Load-bearing facts to verify first, and the first two unblock the rest:**
1. **What was FY26 revenue?** Rs 3,429.1 million or Rs 5,113.0 million. Read the audited FY26 results.
2. **How large is the depreciation-method benefit?** Quantify the WDV to SLM change in Q1 FY27 profit
   before tax, and restate the year-on-year profit growth without it.
3. Customer concentration: revenue share of the top one, three and five customers, and Weatherford's
   share specifically.
4. The order book's delivery schedule by year, and whether the margins in the book match the margins in
   the profit and loss account.
5. The FY26 cash flow statement. With a 294-day cycle, whether operating cash flow is positive at all
   is the question, and the corpus cannot answer it.
6. Promoter holding, pledge, and how much promoters sold in the offer for sale.
7. The three earnings call transcripts held by Bull AI (FY26 Q3, FY26 Q4, FY27 Q1), which should carry
   management's own account of all of the above.
8. The Infomerics press release, for the reasoning behind the upgrade, and whether any larger agency
   rates this company.

**What would change the view.** To PROCEED: FY26 revenue confirmed, the depreciation benefit sized and
the underlying profit growth still strong, plus positive operating cash flow and working capital days
holding at or below 233. To PASS: the higher revenue figure proving wrong, meaning the company is
smaller and dearer than it looks; or operating cash flow negative through a year of this order book
converting; or energy concentration above 75% of revenue with no visible diversification.
