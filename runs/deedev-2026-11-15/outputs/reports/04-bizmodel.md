## 0. Answer first: what DEE sells and how it earns margin (for the sector cap row check)

- DEE sells made-to-order, build-to-print steel piping: prefabricated pipe spools, induction bends, fittings, pressure vessels, skids, and since March 2026 heavy-wall seamless pipe. Customers are boiler and gas-turbine makers (BHEL, L&T, GE, Siemens, Doosan, Thermax), EPC firms and refiners (BPCL, Numaligarh). (AR p.99 "build-to-print, specification-driven basis"; AR p.16; Inv. Pres. slide 12, 21; CARE p.2.)
- It is product supply from DEE's own factories, not site EPC. Goods revenue is booked at one point in time, on delivery. Only INR 127.15 lacs of FY26 standalone revenue (0.14% of 91,855.73 lacs, derived) is over-time erection and design service. Contract assets are nil. (AR p.209 Note 19 table; AR p.166 revenue policy.)
- Margin comes from four things: procurement of steel (about 50% of order value bought up front against a fixed price), welding and conversion productivity at automated plants, the mix of material-supplied versus job-work orders, and fixed-cost leverage from the new Anjar and seamless plants. It does not come from price escalation. CARE reports no price escalation clauses in contracts. (CARE p.2; AR p.100 "back-to-back pricing strategies"; Q1 FY27 call, page 10 of 17.)
- Power-generation segment (biomass, Abohar and Muktsar, plus a pellet plant from Q1 FY27): FY26 consolidated revenue 5,546.64 lacs = 4.9% of 114,199.92 lacs (derived), segment profit before tax (1,083.14) lacs. It is a loss-making non-core appendage, not the earnings engine. (AR p.333 Note 31.) BRSR gives the electricity share of standalone turnover as 4% (AR p.104-105). CARE gives 4.47% of TOI (CARE p.2).
- Do not confuse two uses of "power". The "power sector" (about 60% of FY27 order inflow guidance, about 65% of the order book) is a CUSTOMER end-market: boilers, HRSG and turbines. It buys DEE's piping. The "power segment" in the accounts is DEE's own biomass generation. (AR p.101; CARE p.3; AR p.333.)
- For the orchestrator's sector cap check: the closest honest description is capital-goods / process-equipment component supply with an order book. "Cables / Industrial products" is a loose fit, and I read it as the nearest industrial-products bucket, not an exact one. The archetype is Order-book business (EPC/defence/capital goods) with Build-to-spec component maker traits. It is not a commodity converter and not a lender. The cap row is the orchestrator's call; the facts above are what it should test against.

### 1A One-line description
DEE welds and bends steel pipe to a customer's drawing, in its own factories, and ships finished pipe spools to power-plant and refinery builders; it also runs a small, loss-making biomass power business. (AR p.99; Inv. Pres. slide 12, 15.)

### 1E The chai-stall-uncle version
Think of a tailor who stitches suits for big shops. The shop sends a drawing and cloth, or asks the tailor to buy the cloth. The tailor must buy half the cloth the day the order comes, because cloth prices move and the price he quoted does not. So his money sits in cloth for months. The tailor just built a bigger workshop with new machines. Now the machines must stay busy, because rent and loan interest run whether the machines work or not. He also runs a small side shop that burns farm waste for power, and that side shop loses money. The question is whether the new workshop fills up and whether customers pay on time.

### 2D Industry lifecycle stage
Cyclical-growth. The ordering cycle is up: India thermal power 220 GW to 300 GW, refining 258 to 310 MMTPA by 2030, global gas-turbine and HRSG backlogs (Inv. Pres. slide 30; AR p.98). DEE sits at the capacity-fill stage of its own cycle: the capex is done (INR 20,903.09 lacs FY26 capex consolidated, AR p.333), and FY27 is the first year the plants are meant to fill. The customer cycle is capex-led, so a down-leg in thermal or refining orders would hit DEE with a lag of 12-15 months (CARE p.2 execution time).
