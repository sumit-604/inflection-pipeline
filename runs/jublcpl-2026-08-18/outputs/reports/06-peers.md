### Q1. Synthetic/VP latex market growth (3-4% CAGR) vs "subdued" FY26 global latex demand
**Claim (JACPL, verbatim from B05 handoff):** Management says synthetic/VP latex markets
grow 3-4% CAGR yet also says global latex demand was "subdued" in FY26, resulting in
lower volumes.
**check_peers:** APCOTEXIND, NOCIL

**Peer evidence:**
- APCOTEXIND (Apcotex Industries) is a direct India-based synthetic/VP latex, NBR and
  nitrile-latex producer — the closest true latex comparator available. Across all three
  FY26 quarters reviewed:
  - Q2 FY26 (APCOTEXIND, Nov-2025 call): volumes up 11% QoQ and 18% YoY for H1 FY26;
    EBITDA up 48% YoY; management stated "highest ever" export volumes for the half.
  - Q3 FY26 (APCOTEXIND, Feb-2026 call): 9M volumes up 10% YoY; 9M EBITDA up 61% YoY;
    "highest ever" 9M sales and export volumes. Management separately flagged a "slight
    degrowth" specifically in carpet/textile/tyre-linked export volumes attributed to US
    tariffs — not to general demand softness.
  - Q4 FY26 (APCOTEXIND, ~May-2026 call): continued volume growth reported; West Asia
    conflict flagged as a new, late-arriving raw-material risk (Butadiene, Strait of
    Hormuz), not a demand-side one.
  Nowhere across three quarters does APCOTEXIND describe latex demand as "subdued";
  every quarter reports record or near-record volumes and export sales for the same
  FY26 period JACPL characterises as demand-subdued.
- NOCIL (rubber process chemicals, not latex itself — an adjacent tyre/rubber value-
  chain peer rather than a direct latex comparator): Q2 FY26 call (Nov-2025) attributes
  softness in its rubber-chemicals realisations to competitive/Chinese-dumping pressure
  and tariff uncertainty in end markets, not to a demand contraction per se. This is
  adjacent-market colour, not a direct latex read (Rule 4).

**Peers silent:** Neither peer used JACPL's specific phrase "subdued global latex
demand"; the verdict below is inferred from reported volume/EBITDA trajectories, not a
verbatim peer statement contradicting the word "subdued."

**Verdict: CONTRADICTED.** APCOTEXIND — the only true latex peer in the set — reports
record or near-record volumes and export sales through every FY26 quarter, the opposite
empirical direction of JACPL's "subdued... lower volumes" framing for the same period
and same product category. NOCIL's adjacent evidence does not independently support
"subdued" either (it points to competitive pressure, a different mechanism). The
triangulation suggests JACPL's latex volume softness, if real, is more likely
company-specific (product-mix, customer concentration, or share loss) than an
industry-wide demand phenomenon, though the possibility of a narrower JACPL latex
sub-segment not captured by APCOTEXIND's broader latex mix cannot be ruled out.

### Q2. Sulphur/rock-phosphate cost spikes (Agri) and Butadiene supply risk (Latex)
**Claim:** JACPL cites sharp sulphur/rock-phosphate cost rises (Agri) and Butadiene
supply risk (Latex) — do peers report the same input-cost spikes?
**check_peers:** KRISHANA, APCOTEXIND, NOCIL

**Peer evidence:**
- KRISHANA (direct SSP/NPK/DAP fertiliser peer, same sulphur/sulphuric-acid input as
  JACPL's Agri business) gives a specific, quantified, multi-quarter sulphur price
  trajectory:
  - Q2 FY26 (KRISHANA, Oct-2025 call): sulphur ~₹27,500/tonne (Apr-2025) rising to
    ~₹33,000/tonne by the quarter's end; "sulfur prices... now almost at their highest
    levels of all time" (management, Praveen Oswal).
  - Q3 FY26 (KRISHANA, Jan-2026 call): sulphur ₹28,000-29,000/tonne (Apr) → ₹35,000/tonne
    (Oct-Nov) → ₹45,000/tonne (Dec-2025); sulphuric acid ₹8,000/tonne (Apr) → ₹9,500/tonne
    (Oct) → ₹12,000/tonne (Dec).
  - Q4 FY26 (KRISHANA, Apr-2026 call): "Sulphur prices also firmed up towards March...
    creating a timing mismatch" versus slowing seasonal demand.
  - Q1 FY27 (KRISHANA, Jul-2026 call — same quarter as JACPL's Q1 FY27): sulphur rose
    from ₹65,000-70,000/tonne (April-2026) to "almost ₹1 lakh per tonne in June and
    July," explicitly attributed to the "West Asia conflict" and Strait of Hormuz
    disruption. Ammonia, phosphoric acid and rock phosphate also cited as elevated.
  This is a direct, magnitude-matched, multi-quarter corroboration of JACPL's
  sulphur/rock-phosphate claim from the closest available fertiliser peer.
- APCOTEXIND (~May-2026 Q4 FY26 call) and NOCIL (May-2026 and Aug-2026 calls) both flag
  the same West Asia/Strait of Hormuz shock as driving crude-linked and petrochemical
  raw-material volatility into their cost base during the same window; APCOTEXIND's
  Aug-2026 (Q1 FY27) call references Butadiene availability/sourcing specifically in the
  context of that disruption.
- BALAMINES (not on the check_peers list for Q2 but independently corroborative):
  Q4 FY26 (May-2026 call) states raw materials were running at "double even 3 times also
  2.5 times of its regular prices" because of "the current geopolitical situation," with
  monoethanolamine up "almost 3 times."

**Peers silent:** None of the three named peers is silent on this topic.

**Verdict: VERIFIED.** Two or more independent peers (KRISHANA with matched-magnitude
sulphur/sulphuric-acid pricing; APCOTEXIND and NOCIL with the same West Asia/Strait of
Hormuz causal chain affecting Butadiene-adjacent and crude-linked inputs; BALAMINES as
unsolicited third-party confirmation) corroborate both the direction and the rough
magnitude of the cost spike JACPL describes.

### Q3. "Weak and uneven monsoon" blamed for Q1 FY27 Agri weakness
**Claim:** Management blames Q1 FY27 Agri weakness on a "weak and uneven monsoon" — do
UP/Rajasthan/MP-exposed peers report similar softness?
**check_peers:** KRISHANA

**Peer evidence:**
- KRISHANA is headquartered in Bhilwara, Rajasthan with its Meghnagar plant in Jhabua,
  Madhya Pradesh — squarely the geography the question asks about. Its Q1 FY27 call
  (Jul-2026, the exact same quarter as JACPL's Q1 FY27) states: "The quarter began amid
  heightened uncertainty after the IMD forecast the southwest monsoon at 92% of the Long
  Period Average (LPA)... By the end of June, cumulative rainfall was nearly 40% below
  normal, resulting in national Kharif sowing declining by 22.7% year-on-year to 182.7
  lakh hectares, compared to 236.5 lakh hectares during the corresponding period last
  year. However, rainfall improved materially during early July, resulting in a rapid
  recovery in sowing progress, improved farmer sentiment, and accelerated fertilizer
  offtake."
- The same call separately notes industry-wide fertiliser production fell 28% YoY
  (April-May 2026) while underlying NPK sales grew 33% YoY and SSP sales grew 28% YoY —
  KRISHANA's own read is that the shortfall was a supply/logistics-side production issue
  layered on top of (not instead of) the monsoon-driven sowing dip, with demand actually
  recovering strongly once rain resumed.
- KRISHANA's own Q4 FY26 call (Apr-2026, three months earlier) had explicitly
  downplayed the incoming monsoon risk: management cited a Skymet forecast of "94% of
  the long period average" and called it "not a major variation," expecting "no stress
  on the agriculture sector." That forecast proved too sanguine relative to what
  actually unfolded by end-June (40% rainfall deficit) per KRISHANA's own July
  disclosure — a useful data point on how monsoon risk was underestimated across the
  sector heading into the quarter, not manufactured after the fact by JACPL.

**Peers silent:** No other check_peer was designated for this question.

**Verdict: VERIFIED.** KRISHANA, an independent, geographically matched (Rajasthan/MP)
fertiliser peer reporting on the identical Q1 FY27 quarter, corroborates both the
direction (real, IMD-confirmed monsoon deficit through June) and the timing (recovery
only from early July) of JACPL's claim, using independent meteorological data (IMD) and
its own Kharif sowing statistics. This is one of the strongest-anchored verifications in
this stage.

## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

**2A. Demand environment.** KRISHANA and APCOTEXIND both show strong, often record,
volume and revenue growth through FY26 and into Q1 FY27, despite the Q1 FY27
monsoon/logistics shocks (demand recovers quickly once rain and freight normalise).
NOCIL's picture is more mixed: rubber-chemical realisations pressured by Chinese
competition/dumping rather than end-demand weakness, and a domestic cooking-gas
shortage separately hit its non-tyre segment in Q1 FY27. BALAMINES describes "moderated
demand in select pharma and agrichem segments" in Q2 FY26 and a generally "mixed
operating environment" — the softest demand language in the whole peer set, though this
is a chemicals-intermediate business only loosely comparable to JACPL's SSP/Agri
segment. Net: the peer set does not corroborate a broad, sector-wide demand slump in the
periods JACPL's Agri/Latex commentary might imply; where softness appears, peers
attribute it to competition (China) or specific logistics/production disruptions, not
underlying demand destruction.

**2B. Pricing and input costs.** This is the most consistent theme across all four
peers: sulphur, sulphuric acid, ammonia, methanol, monoethanolamine and other
petrochemical-linked inputs all spiked sharply from roughly April through July 2026,
uniformly attributed to the West Asia conflict and Strait of Hormuz disruption. KRISHANA
gives the most granular, quarter-by-quarter numeric trail (sulphur ~₹27,500/tonne in
April 2025 to ~₹1,00,000/tonne by June-July 2026). This matches JACPL's own cost
narrative closely in direction and broad timing, even though the specific molecules
differ across peers.

**2C. Capex cycle.** At least three of the four peers (APCOTEXIND, BALAMINES, KRISHANA)
are mid-expansion in the same window, generally at larger absolute scale than JACPL's
project. This reads as an industry-wide capacity build, not a lone-expander situation —
JACPL's Rs 50 crore Samlaya project is a small, consistent participant in a broader
sector up-cycle rather than an outlier bet.

**2D. Competitive mentions.** No peer transcript reviewed mentions Jubilant Agri &
Consumer Products, JACPL, or an unmistakable description of it by name, product, or
plant location, in either management remarks or the Q&A. peer_mentions_of_company is
empty.

**2E. Risks peers discuss that JACPL's B05 record does not.** (i) Chinese
dumping/import competition is a recurring, named risk for NOCIL (rubber chemicals) and
BALAMINES (EDA, DMF — both subject to live or completed antidumping investigations) —
JACPL's disclosed risk set (per B05) does not surface an equivalent import-competition
risk for Latex or Adhesives. (ii) US tariff exposure on carpet/textile/tyre-linked
export volumes is specifically named by APCOTEXIND as a driver of export softness in
Q3 FY26, distinct from the West Asia/logistics disruption JACPL cites — JACPL's Q1 deck
attributes its own export miss only to "geopolitical/logistics disruption," without
separately flagging a tariff-specific channel. (iii) KRISHANA's credit-rating agency
(CRISIL, upgrade with a sector-level negative outlook) explicitly names ammonia-supply
and gas-availability risk tied to the Iran-Israel/West Asia conflict as a structural,
ongoing (not one-quarter) industry risk — a framing more forward-looking than JACPL's
apparent treatment of the West Asia shock as a one-quarter headwind.

**Overall assessment.** The peer set is symmetric in its effect on JACPL's Q1 FY27

## PART 5: CROSS-PEER HYPOTHESIS

Across all four peers, the same West Asia/Strait of Hormuz raw-material shock
(April-July 2026) is affecting everyone's cost base roughly equally, yet the peer set's
margin and demand outcomes diverge sharply along a specific axis: KRISHANA and
APCOTEXIND — both backward-integrated or product-mix-flexible, and both able to pass
cost increases through via MRP/pricing revisions or premium product-mix shifts (KRISHANA
explicitly attributes its ~17% EBITDA margin, versus ~8-9% for named competitors
Paradeep and Coromandel, to its own sulphuric/phosphoric acid backward integration; new
higher-margin NPK grades introduced mid-shock) — are converting the shock into
record-or-near-record volumes and margins. NOCIL and BALAMINES, by contrast, are
absorbing margin compression and demand softness in specific product lines (NOCIL's
rubber chemicals, BALAMINES's EDA/DMF) that face direct, sub-cost Chinese import
competition, where the same input-cost spike cannot be passed through against an
underpriced import benchmark.

**Testable hypothesis:** the West Asia shock is functioning as a stress test that
separates peers by backward-integration/pricing-power rather than by which raw material
they buy — peers that can flex product mix or control upstream inputs (KRISHANA,
APCOTEXIND) are gaining margin and share through the disruption, while peers whose
commoditised product lines compete directly against underpriced Chinese imports (NOCIL,
BALAMINES) are losing margin through the identical disruption. If this bifurcation axis
is real, JACPL's own resilience through this same window should track not which inputs
it buys (sulphur, Butadiene) but whether its specific product lines — SSP/Agri versus
Adhesives/Latex — face direct Chinese/import competition or hold differentiated pricing
power; this is a testable question for the next quarter's disclosures (Q2 FY27) and is
not stated explicitly by any single peer in this set.
