## SECTION 1: THE BUSINESS MODEL IN PLAIN ENGLISH

### 1A. One-line description
Fratelli grows grapes on its own vineyards and a contract-farming network,
turns them into premium bottled wine sold through a 31,000-point national
distribution network, and since February 2025 also sells a canned
ready-to-drink alcoholic beverage (Shotgun) through roughly 9,000 of those
outlets, using the same winery, sales force and licences (Chairman's message,
Annual_Report_FY2026.pdf p.9-10; Investor_Presentation_Q1FY27_Aug2026.txt
slide 14/p.13).

### 1B. Money flow chain, by revenue stream

**Still wine (bottles, all price tiers):**
Grapes (own 400 acres + ~1,000 contract-farmed acres, Akluj and Jambhali,
Maharashtra) → crushed, fermented and aged at the Akluj winery (5.4 million
litre capacity) → bottled under 50+ labels across five price segments
(luxury, super-premium, premium, value) → sold to (a) private
distributors/retailers across 29 states/UTs and (b) government beverage
corporations in corporation-model states → consumer or HoReCa venue pays MRP,
minus state excise duty and distributor/retailer margin, at point of sale
(Annual_Report_FY2026.pdf p.2-5, p.15, p.26; Note 37 consolidated,
Annual_Report_FY2026.pdf p.190-191).

**Shotgun RTD (canned, launched Feb-2025) + TiLT wine-in-a-can:**
Pre-mixed/blended beverage produced using existing winery
infrastructure → canned/packaged centrally → distributed through the
existing field-sales network into ~9,000 outlets across ~18-22 states,
skewed to Tier 2/3 cities → sold at a materially lower price point than
bottled wine, targeting a younger, more casual occasion
(Annual_Report_FY2026.pdf p.9-10, p.13-14; Investor_Presentation_Q1FY27
slide 14/p.13, slide 36/p.35).

**Exports:** Bottled wine, TiLT and (from Q1FY27) Shotgun sold into 15-17
countries plus two Indian duty-free zones; export revenue Rs 313.95 lakh
FY26 vs Rs 268.74 lakh FY25, i.e. it roughly doubled YoY but from a small
base (Note 37 consolidated, Annual_Report_FY2026.pdf p.191).

**CSD (Canteen Stores Department, defence canteens):** A distinct
institutional government channel; 29 canteens listed, ~50% share of that
channel, contributing 8% of topline in Q1FY27 (Investor_Presentation_Q1FY27
slide 17/p.16). This is a channel cutting across both wine and RTD, not a
separate product.

### 1E. The chai-stall-uncle version
Think of Fratelli as a family that has spent 19 years learning to grow good
grapes in one corner of Maharashtra and turn them into wine people are
willing to pay a premium for. That is the slow, patient business: plant a
vine, wait years for it to mature, make wine, age it, sell it through
thousands of shops and restaurants across the country, one state licence at
a time. Eighteen months ago they added a second, much faster business next
to it: Shotgun, a canned drink sold cheap and fast, using the same trucks
and salespeople. It is like a bakery that spent decades perfecting a
wedding-cake business suddenly also selling packaged cookies through the
same delivery vans — the cookies move fast and cheap, the cakes move slow
and dear, and mixing their numbers together tells you very little about
either one.

### 2D. Industry lifecycle stage
Indian wine is an emerging/early-growth category: under 1% of India's
alcohol market, valued at USD 229-266 million (2024/2025 depending on
source cited) and projected to reach USD 803-986 million by 2032-2034, a
~16% implied CAGR (Annual_Report_FY2026.pdf p.21; Investor_Presentation_Q1FY27
slide 44-45/p.43-44 — the AR and the deck cite slightly different base-year
figures from different named sources, both anchored, neither reconciled to
each other in the corpus). Fratelli positions itself as a category-definer
within this early-stage industry ("a category that is still being created in
India," Chairman's letter, Annual_Report_FY2026.pdf p.9). However, the
company's own wine-segment revenue has NOT participated in that category
growth for two straight years (Rs 212.88cr FY24 to Rs 178.44cr FY25 to Rs
181.20cr FY26, Note 37) — a company-specific execution gap sitting inside a
genuinely early-stage, high-growth category.

## SECTION 3: FINANCIAL METRICS THAT MATTER FOR THIS BUSINESS MODEL

### 3A. Ignore-these-track-these

| Commonly tracked ratio | Why misleading/irrelevant here |
|---|---|
| Net profit margin, taken alone | Distorted by large, one-off working-capital swings; FY26's positive CFO (Rs 4.996cr, per Stage 3) was manufactured from a near-doubling of rebate/payables accruals, not profitability. Use recomputed EBITDA instead. |
| Consolidated/blended ROE or ROCE as a single number | Meaningless while the wine segment alone just swung from a Rs 18.92cr profit (FY24) to two years of loss (Note 37); a blended number averages a legacy engine with a new one that has one year of data. |
| Blended inventory days (DIO) | Still wine legitimately carries multi-year vintage-dated stock by design (a structural feature, not inefficiency); Shotgun should turn in weeks. A single DIO number conflates the two. |
| DSO level in isolation | 212 days is genuinely high, but the corporation-vs-private-party receivables mix (Note 36(C)) matters more than the headline number — a corporation-heavy state mix can structurally run longer than private retail. Track the ageing-mix trend, not just the day count. |
| EV/EBITDA | EBITDA is near-zero/negative (recomputed -2.5% margin FY26 per Stage 3); a small denominator makes the multiple swing wildly on trivial changes. |
| Gross margin vs generic FMCG benchmarks | Alcobev structurally carries excise-duty pass-through and runs 70-80% gross margin by category convention; comparing the level alone to packaged-food FMCG peers (typically 35-40%) overstates apparent quality without also comparing the much heavier distribution/A&P cost below the gross-margin line. |

### 4C. Quarterly monitoring checklist

1. Recomputed consolidated EBITDA (PBT + finance cost + depreciation) — good: 2+ consecutive positive quarters; trouble: any negative quarter after a "breakeven" claim.
2. Wine-segment Ind AS 108 result (annual only) — good: positive, approaching FY24's +Rs 18.92cr; trouble: a third straight negative year.
3. Selling/distribution/marketing % of wine revenue — good: continues below 23.3%; trouble: rises back toward 25.7%+.
4. DSO and receivables ageing mix — good: DSO <180 days, >6-month bucket keeps shrinking below 24.3%; trouble: DSO exceeds 212 days again or the bucket reverses.
5. Consolidated cash & bank balance vs the Rs 114.50cr guarantee — good: cash rebuilt materially; trouble: guarantee called or cash falls further.
6. Gearing ratio — good: below 64.6%; trouble: above 88.0%.
7. Shotgun outlet count and case volumes, quarter on quarter — good: sustained net adds and sequential case growth from the 50,000-case Q1FY27 base; trouble: outlet count plateaus or volumes decline.
8. Directors' Report vs audited-note reconciliation (FY27 AR) — good: headline figures match the audited P&L; trouble: another mismatch repeats the FY26 pattern.
9. Bad-debt write-offs and ECL provisioning discipline — good: write-offs return to a normally-provisioned pattern; trouble: another large unprovisioned write-off like FY26's Rs 483.09 lakh.
10. State-level regulatory disruption incidents named in quarterly commentary — good: fewer/none; trouble: recurrence in a large state.
11. Luxury segment growth rate (J'NOON, Sette) — good: sustained double-digit growth; trouble: deceleration below high single digits.
12. Promoter holding / Bhupinder Kumar Sekhri board status — good: clarified via appointment or public rationale; trouble: continued opacity.
13. MSME payable ageing — good: reverts toward the FY25 level; trouble: continues rising past FY26's +235% YoY.
14. Bearer-plant pre-operative pool and capitalisation — good: capitalisation resumes, signalling the capex cycle genuinely ending; trouble: pool keeps growing with nothing capitalised.

### 4D. Highest-value questions for management

1. **Per-engine economics.** What is the standalone revenue, gross margin and contribution margin for Shotgun RTD versus still wine, given the audited segment note reports them as a single "wine manufacturing and sales" line?

2. **The segment-profit reversal.** Why did the wine segment (Note 37) swing from a Rs 18.92cr operating profit in FY24 to losses of Rs 10.49cr (FY25) and Rs 10.61cr (FY26) on broadly flat revenue?
   Reassuring: a clearly identified, bounded driver with a stated glide path back to profit. Worrying: a vague answer, or an admission that unit economics deteriorated with scale.
3. **Receivables structure.** Which states route sales through a government beverage corporation, and what is the average payment cycle for corporation customers versus private-party customers?
   Reassuring: a named, bounded list with disclosed DSO by channel, showing 212 days is a structural feature, not a worsening one. Worrying: management cannot name the states or the corporation share of receivables.
4. **The guarantee.** Is the Rs 114.50cr corporate guarantee from the (near-zero-cash) holding company likely to be called, and what is the contingency if it is?
   Reassuring: a covenant-light facility with no near-term trigger, backed by a credible contingency. Worrying: no contingency plan beyond "we do not expect it to be called."
5. **Margin structure.** How much of the 78% gross margin is structural (brand-priced) versus a function of current winery-capacity utilisation, and what is the utilisation rate today?
   Reassuring: gross margin shown to be stable across utilisation levels. Worrying: gross margin shown to depend heavily on utilisation, implying compression risk if volumes disappoint.
6. **Distribution quality.** What proportion of the 31,000 touch points and 9,000 Shotgun outlets are active/reordering versus one-time stocking, and what is same-outlet revenue growth?
   Reassuring: a disclosed active-outlet or reorder-velocity metric. Worrying: touch-point counts are gross/cumulative and no velocity metric exists.
