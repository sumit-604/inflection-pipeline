## PART A: THE FROM STATE

**A1. Archetype.** The archetype library has no hospital entry. Declared: **fits no known archetype**. Nearest neighbour: licence and scarcity business, because owned land and local approvals set capacity (B04 moats). It fails as a fit because tariffs follow insurers and inflation, not a regulated tariff (B04, B07). The unit is one occupied census bed-day: occupancy x revenue per occupied bed (ARPOB) x margin (B04). Revenue lines are in-patient 78.3%, out-patient 17.5%, pharmacy 2.6%, cafeteria 0.9%, hotel 0.7% (B04). The lines do not differ in business model, so one archetype line stands. The AR reports one segment, healthcare services (Section 6, question 2). Business type is services, asset-heavy, secular growth (B04).

**A2. The simple analogy.** Picture a group of large hotels where the guests are patients. The group owns the buildings. Each night an occupied room earns about Rs 71,800 on average (B04, H1 FY27). Visiting doctors are paid by the case, and that cost runs near a quarter of income (B04). Three hotels in Thane, Pune and Indore run mostly full. Thane and Pune sit near 70% (B04). A fourth hotel in Dombivli opened in February 2026. About a third of its rooms are filled (B04, B05). Its staff and power bills started on day one. The group is now building three more hotels, in Pune South, Mira Road and BKC, and each opens on a dated schedule (B05). The profit line is flat while the new rooms fill and the building loans start (B03, B04).

# SECTION 3: BUSINESS UNDERSTANDING NARRATIVE (draft; Stage 13's copy is final)

JLHL runs four multi-specialty hospitals in Thane, Pune, Indore and Dombivli (B04, B05). In-patient care is 78.3% of revenue and out-patient care is 17.5% (B04). In-patient care is a stay in a bed under a medical or surgical team, with an average stay of 3.79 days, and the transplant, robotic and cath lab programmes rest on a few key doctors (B05, B04).

The customers are patients, and insurers pay for most of them. Insurance and others pay 55.4% of revenue, self-paying patients 43.6% and government schemes 1.0% (B04 report). The run did not establish payer concentration by insurer, or any switching cost (B04, B01).

Demand exists because these micro-markets are dense and the good beds are full, with Thane and Pune near 70% occupancy (B04). Insurance cover sets hospitalisation rates and payer mix, so the health insurer and TPA cashless empanelment and claim settlement signal matters (B09). Peers report inpatient volume up 27% to 30% in Q2 FY27 and no softness, though MMR, Pune and Indore are not short of beds by count (B06, B09).

Demand should grow as new beds open and fill. Dombivli has about 200 operating beds at mid-30% occupancy, and the signals are NABH accreditation of Dombivli and insurer empanelment (B05, B09). Pune South opens in CY28, Mira Road in CY29 and BKC in CY31, and the signals are the MMRDA BKC lease registration and MBMC Mira Road approvals, and Pune private bed supply from peers (B05, B09). A 40-50 bed expansion at Thane has a municipal LOI (B07). The run did not establish any tariff gain or market-share gain, because management says mature-unit ARPOB tracks inflation (B07).

The competitive advantage sits in two places and not in a third. Owned land and approvals in dense Mumbai and Pune markets is the strongest, rated moderate to high, and a local brand with NABH accreditation and doctor teams is moderate (B04). The Emerging Moat scan finds a geographic first-mover land bank and an execution moat, with a total of 13.6 against a floor of 12, and the scoring basis is an open operator ruling (B07, B12c C-04). The run found no tariff or retention advantage, and the pharmacy and hotel lines carry no moat (B07).
