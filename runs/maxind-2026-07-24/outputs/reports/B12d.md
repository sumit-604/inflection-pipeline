## 4. Findings

**D-1 (MAJOR) — GPTHEALTH guidance-slippage quote misattributed to the wrong quarter.**
Location: B06 Part 1, Q2 (Care Home maturity curve), and Part 4 ("single most consequential caution").
B06 states: "By Q3 FY26 (Feb-2026 call) this guidance had lengthened materially: 'A hospital generally takes almost 24 to 36 months to break even...'" This exact quote is **not** in the Feb-2026 transcript. It is found verbatim only in the **May-2026** (Q4 FY26) transcript. The actual Feb-2026 transcript shows management reaffirming the original timeline ("on track to achieve EBITDA breakeven in the next 6 to 8 months, well within the targeted time frame" — i.e., consistent with the original 12-15-month guide from a May-2025 commissioning). This means the guidance slippage B06 treats as the report's single most useful cautionary cross-check actually occurred one quarter later than stated (between Feb-2026 and May-2026, not between Nov-2025 and Feb-2026), and Part 4's framing ("moving... within three quarters of disclosure") should read four quarters. The qualitative caution (guidance did eventually slip) survives correction, but the stated chronology anchoring it does not.

**D-2 (MAJOR) — KRSNAA credited with a risk it never raises.**
Location: B06 Part 2E ("Risks peers raise that MAXIND does not") and the corresponding `risks_peers_raise` YAML line.
B06 attributes "insurance-empanelment lag (12-15+ months)" jointly to "GPTHEALTH/KRSNAA." A full-text search of all four KRSNAA transcripts (Aug-2025, Nov-2025, Feb-2026, May-2026) returns zero matches for "empanelment" or the concept. The term and the risk appear only in GPTHEALTH's May-2026 transcript. This over-attributes a specific, named risk category to a peer whose transcripts do not support it — B06 itself correctly notes elsewhere (Part 1, Q2) that KRSNAA's PPP-tender ramp is a structurally different mechanism, making this cross-read inconsistent with the report's own stated peer-fit caveats.

**D-3 (MINOR) — MAXESTATES Q4 FY26 call consistently mislabeled "May-2026" instead of "Jun-2026."**
Location: B06 Part 1 Q4, and Part 1 Q2 cross-reference to MAXESTATES realizations.
The injected file list gives Max Estates' four calls as Aug-2025/Nov-2025/Feb-2026/**Jun-2026** (a different quarterly cadence than GPTHEALTH/KRSNAA, whose Q4 call is May-2026). B06 labels the Sector 105 GDV-doubling quote and the ~29%-realization-increase figure both "(Q4 FY26 call, May-2026)" when the actual transcript file is Jun-2026. Content is accurate and correctly located within the right call; only the calendar month attached to it is wrong. This recurs at least twice in the report.

**D-4 (MINOR) — "West Asia conflict" cross-corroboration overstates quote-level precision.**
Location: B06 Part 2A (Demand environment).
B06 states real-estate peers "both" flag moderation "explicitly tied to the escalation of the 'West Asia conflict'... independently uses nearly identical language." A full-text search finds the exact phrase "West Asia conflict" only in the MAXESTATES Jun-2026 transcript. Ashiana's Jun-2026 transcript discusses "geopolitical situations" and "the war" in the same period but never uses the phrase "West Asia conflict" itself. The underlying cross-corroboration point (independent geopolitical-caution language across two real-estate peers) is still directionally supported, but the claim of "nearly identical language" between the two peers overstates the actual textual match.

**D-5 (MINOR) — one industry-context data point left unused for KRSNAA.**
Location: B06 Part 1 Q5 / KRSNAA coverage-map entry.
Krsnaa's own "20%, 25% minimum CAGR" company-growth framing (May-2026 call) was not mentioned in B06's Q5 discussion of quantified growth figures, alongside Ashiana's CAGR quotes. Directional-only, would not change the verdict; scored MINOR per the rubric's "industry-context miss" category.

## 5. Overall read

B06 substantively used three of the four assigned peers (ASHIANA,
MAXESTATES, GPTHEALTH) and correctly, conservatively classified the
fourth (KRSNAA) as cited-only. Every claim in B05's peer_questions
handoff received an explicit verdict; no claim was skipped, and no
verdict was upgraded from silence. The report's overall shape (net
effect "complicates," 0 of 6 claims fully verified, 2 unverifiable due
to genuine peer-fit gaps) is well-supported by the underlying
transcripts on independent spot-check.

However, two MAJOR citation-accuracy defects were found on direct
verification against the source transcripts: the report's own
headline cautionary finding (GPT Healthcare's Raipur guidance slippage)
is anchored to the wrong quarter, and a specific risk claim is
attributed to KRSNAA when its transcripts contain no support for it at
all. Both defects sit in the analytical connective tissue (Parts 2 and
4) rather than in the core Part 1 claim-by-claim verdicts, so the six
headline verdicts (Q1-Q6) likely survive correction — but the citation
discipline underpinning two of the report's most-quoted supporting
facts does not hold up to a fresh read of the primary sources, which is
exactly the failure mode this audit exists to catch.
