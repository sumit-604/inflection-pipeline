Precision turbine parts maker scaling through capex

Azad Engineering forges and machines precision components for turbines: airfoils, blades, and 3D rotating parts that sit in the hot, high stress core of energy, aerospace, defence, and oil and gas equipment. Rakesh Chopdar founded it in Hyderabad in 1983. The customer list is Tier 1 OEMs: GE, Mitsubishi Heavy Industries, Siemens Energy, Arabelle and EDF, BHEL, Honeywell, GE Aviation, Baker Hughes, Rolls Royce, and Pratt & Whitney. FY26 revenue was Rs 590 Cr, PAT Rs 132 Cr, and EBITDA margin 37%. The order book stands at Rs 6,500 Cr, roughly 13 times trailing revenue, split Rs 3,966 Cr energy, Rs 1,834 Cr aerospace, and the rest oil and gas. Revenue has compounded 30% over three years and PAT 36%.

The transition is a move from one Hyderabad site to eight dedicated factories, each built for a single OEM, four already commissioned and four more guided by around November 2026. Azad raised about Rs 700 Cr in a FY25 QIP on top of its December 2023 IPO to fund that build. The product mix is climbing into higher value hot section work, headlined by an 8 year single source Nozzle Vane contract with Mitsubishi, plus qualification batches queued for Rolls Royce and a signed Pratt & Whitney contract. Governance moved in parallel: a non family professional Managing Director from Bharat Forge America, a genuine outside independent director, and institutional ownership built to 22% within fifteen months of listing.

The numbers split cleanly into strong operations and weak cash. On the operating side, growth scored 18 of 20 and the balance sheet 16 of 20 on the Gate 0 screen, on a FORTRESS backward moat with 6 of 12 moat tests confirmed. On the cash side, the same screen returned a core score of 38 of 100, an AVOID band, because cumulative operating cash flow over seven years is just 0.04 times cumulative profit, and FY26 operating cash flow was negative Rs 123 Cr against positive PAT of Rs 132 Cr. The working capital cycle stretched to 344 days in FY26 from 287 the year before, driven by inventory for parts under development and the three units commissioned during the year.

Cash reads as growth induced rather than structural. The rating agency attributes the elongated cycle to customised alloys bought in bulk and three dedicated units mid commissioning, and states it is expected to normalise once serial production begins. Management delivers on the parts it can measure and dodges the parts it cannot. Revenue came in near 30% against 25% to 30% guided, and margin at 37% against 33% to 35% guided, across all three FY26 calls. The credibility grade is B. Against that, the FY26 working capital normalization promised for the second half was missed and the target quietly loosened from 140 to 150 days out to 160 to 170 days, then pushed into FY27. Operating cash flow questions were deflected every quarter, and an indigenous defence engine promised as a couple of months away slipped across three straight calls without delivery.

Peers make the cash story more believable and the exclusivity story less so. MTAR, Unimech, and PTC Industries all run elongated working capital cycles and build capacity well ahead of utilization during ramps, so Azad is not an outlier. MTAR is the clearest precedent: its working capital days fell from 260 to 172 and operating cash flow swung to positive Rs 197 Cr once its ramp stabilized. No peer contradicts Azad on any claim. But no peer could verify the three claims that carry the pricing thesis: that Azad is the only qualified Indian supplier of gas and nuclear turbine airfoils, that its margin gains come from domestic alloy sourcing rather than execution, and that it has taken share from Howmet and Precision Castparts in hot section parts. The rating agency does state there are no other qualified domestic suppliers, which cuts toward Azad, but that sits inside a company informed rating rationale, not independent peer data.

The market runway is wide and the concentration risk is real at the same time. The addressable slice of the global precision component market runs to about Rs 15,200 Cr, against which Azad holds roughly 3.9%, leaving headroom of about 26 times current revenue and a MASSIVE runway class. The three year serviceable opportunity of about Rs 1,325 Cr fits inside committed capex, but the five year figure of about Rs 2,232 Cr needs capex phases beyond what is committed today. On the other side of that runway, the top five customers are 51% of revenue and rising, energy and oil and gas together are 81% of revenue, and management has never quantified single customer concentration despite being asked.

Promoter conduct sits at CAUTION, not clean and not disqualifying. Against it: an unresolved SEBI insider trading code of conduct violation still open at the August 2025 secretarial audit, a family linked related party ecosystem of six plus entities, and a proxy advisor flag on MD remuneration and on choosing a QIP over a rights issue. For it: zero promoter pledge maintained since listing, the two genuine outside professional board additions, and the institutional ownership build. Two accounting items warrant tracking regardless: a Rs 22.56 Cr put option charge on the Azad Prime minority stake routed straight to equity and bypassing profit, equal to 26% of consolidated PAT, and plant depreciated over 15 years against the 7.5 year statutory default, which flatters the adjusted ROCE that management headlines at 20.7% versus a statutory 9%.
