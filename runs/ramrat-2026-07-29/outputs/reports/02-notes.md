## PASS 3: PATTERN RE-READ FINDINGS

The pattern pass targeted contradictions, notes-vs-statements mismatches, deliberately vague disclosures, restatements, post-balance-sheet events, and going-concern language, cross-checking Pass 1 and Pass 2 anchors directly against the primary financial statements. This yielded the following material items, in addition to confirming several Pass 1/2 anchors verbatim:

1. **Standalone operating cash flow also swung sharply negative**, confirming the deterioration is a parent-company-level phenomenon and not merely an artefact of Tefabo subsidiary consolidation. Standalone Net Cash Flow from Operating Activities: ₹(9,584.84) Lakhs = ₹(95.85) Cr (FY26) vs +₹22,507.12 Lakhs = +₹225.07 Cr (FY25) (Standalone Cash Flow Statement, p.180), essentially mirroring the consolidated swing of ₹(9,299.42) Lakhs = ₹(92.99) Cr vs +₹22,733.31 Lakhs = +₹227.33 Cr (Consolidated Cash Flow Statement, p.264). Standalone working-capital drag: Trade receivables ₹(24,212.26) Lakhs, Inventories ₹(24,766.10) Lakhs, partially offset by Trade payables +₹20,528.18 Lakhs. This is the single most important pattern-pass confirmation: it rules out "it's just the subsidiary" as an explanation and anchors FLAG-CASH at the parent-entity level. 🔴

2. **Scope mismatch identified between Pass 1's receivables/inventory figures (standalone) and the cash-flow-driven FLAG-CASH signal (consolidated)** — both point the same direction but at different magnitudes and must not be conflated. Consolidated Trade Receivables (Note 9, p.329): ₹64,060.62 Lakhs = ₹640.61 Cr (FY26) vs ₹39,014.83 Lakhs = ₹390.15 Cr (FY25), +64.2% YoY. Consolidated Inventories (Note 8, p.292): ₹48,609.06 Lakhs = ₹486.09 Cr (FY26) vs ₹23,368.41 Lakhs = ₹233.68 Cr (FY25), +108.1% YoY, with cost of inventories written down disclosed as ₹NIL both years (Note 8.3, p.292) — confirming Pass 1's standalone-level zero-write-down finding also holds at Group level. Standalone-only figures (Pass 1: receivables ₹615.55 Cr vs ₹370.17 Cr; inventory ₹469.38 Cr vs ₹221.72 Cr) are lower because they exclude Tefabo subsidiary balances. Both scopes confirm the same qualitative deterioration; the consolidated figures are the correct anchor for FLAG-CASH since they tie directly to the consolidated cash flow statement.

3. **Auditor's Emphasis of Matter cross-references Note 30.4 and Note 30.1, not Note 30.3 as anchored in Pass 1** — confirmed directly from the Independent Auditor's Report (Consolidated, p.251, para 3): "We draw attention to note 30.4 which refers to tax demand of ₹6,790.77 Lakhs (excluding interest of ₹3,149.05 Lakhs)... Further, reassessment notices u/s 148... have been served on Global Copper Private Limited... Assessment Years 2019-20 and 2020-21. The management has not made any adjustments in the Standalone Financial Statements in respect of the aforesaid matters as explained in note 30.1. Our opinion is not modified with respect to the above matter." This is a note-numbering clarification, not a contradiction: the underlying contingent-liability table sits at Note 30.3 (as Pass 1 found) while the narrative explanation and management's non-adjustment rationale sit at Notes 30.1 and 30.4. All three sub-notes describe the same Section 132 search/Section 148 reassessment exposure; the total contingent income-tax quantum (₹10,361.69 Lakhs demand + ₹3,149.05 Lakhs interest not provided) is unchanged from Pass 1.

4. **HUF partition correction (from Pass 2) confirmed verbatim against the source document**: the related-party list explicitly reads "Mahendra Kumar Kabra (HUF) (total partition w.e.f. 19th March, 2025)" (Note 35(c), p.320) — confirming Pass 2's correction of Pass 1's error ("Kabra Shreegopal Rameshwarlal HUF," partitioned "19 March 2026"). Kabra Shreegopal Rameshwarlal (HUF) is confirmed as a separate, continuing related party in the same list. This restatement/correction is carried into this consolidation.

5. **Going concern language confirmed as routine boilerplate only, with no doubt or emphasis anywhere.** Standalone Note 1(a)(ii), Basis of Preparation (p.183): "The Financial Statements have been prepared on an accrual and going concern basis." The consolidated equivalent (p.267) uses identical routine language. The consolidated Independent Auditor's Report's standard going-concern procedures paragraph (p.254) is boilerplate SA-570 methodology language, not a qualification. No management commentary, audit qualification, or material uncertainty disclosure on going concern exists anywhere in the notes reviewed across either pass. This confirms Pass 1's "NONE" finding with direct document verification.

6. No further contradictions between notes and the primary statements, no additional restatements, and no additional post-balance-sheet events beyond those already catalogued in Pass 1/2 (proposed final dividend; WC consortium amendment and personal-guarantee release dated 8 May 2026; final Labour Code rules notified 8 May 2026) were found on this re-read.

## D. FIVE QUESTIONS FOR MANAGEMENT

1. What specifically drove the combined ~₹497 Cr (₹250 Cr receivables + ₹247 Cr inventory, consolidated) working-capital build in FY26, and what is management's plan and timeline to normalise operating cash conversion in FY27?
2. What is the realistic, probability-weighted exposure from the Section 132 search (CIT(A) appeals) and the Section 148 reassessment on the merged Global Copper Pvt Ltd entity, and what is the legal basis for contesting each?
3. When will legal title to the ~₹23 Cr of GCPL-titled land/factory-building assets be formally transferred into Ram Ratna Wires Ltd's name, and what, if anything, is impeding that transfer?
4. Is the ₹333.01 Lakhs (standalone) / ₹356.43 Lakhs (consolidated) Labour Code past-service cost genuinely a one-time item, or should investors expect a further top-up once the final rules (notified 8 May 2026) are fully assessed?
5. Given zero inventory write-downs despite a 108-112% inventory build, what is management's basis for concluding there is no obsolescence or slow-moving stock risk at the new Bhiwadi facility, and why did the CFO appointed in June 2025 resign at the same fiscal year-end?

## E. NOTES-BASED RED FLAGS

- **Earnings management**: No direct evidence of revenue manipulation (point-in-time recognition, no unusual DTA recognition, no aggressive channel-stuffing proof beyond inference). However, Other Income is inflated by a ₹1,000.48 Lakhs net FX gain (~52% of Other Income, ~6.6% of standalone PBT) that is not netted against the ₹198.20 Lakhs FX borrowing/translation loss sitting in Finance Costs (Note 23/27) — an earnings-quality gross-up, not manipulation, but relevant to underlying PBT quality.
- **Aggressive accounting**: Zero inventory write-down against a 108-112% inventory build is the clearest soft-provisioning red flag in the notes; the Labour Code cost's "non-recurring exceptional item" classification is a judgment call that carries open recurrence risk given the pending final-rules assessment.
- **Undisclosed risk indicators**: EPCG export-obligation contingency (Note 18.1) sits outside the formal Contingent Liabilities table (Note 30) despite carrying a repayment-plus-interest downside if export obligations are not met; the ₹691.23 Lakhs "Others" tax-rate reconciliation credit is unexplained; the lease liability fair-value/carrying-value gap (~71% FY26 vs ~5% FY25) is unexplained.
- **Overall**: Moderate-to-concerning, driven primarily by cash conversion and litigation exposure rather than by classic revenue-recognition or DTA-based earnings manipulation.

---

## F. ONE-LINE NOTES VERDICT

The notes reveal moderate accounting practices bordering on concerning given cash conversion. Key concern: operating cash flow swung sharply negative at both standalone (₹95.85 Cr) and consolidated (₹92.99 Cr) levels against record PAT, driven by a 108% consolidated inventory build and 64% consolidated receivables build with zero write-downs, layered on an auditor-flagged ₹103.62 Cr income-tax search exposure inherited from an unresolved merger. Key strength: no going-concern language, no covenant breaches, and materially improved FX-hedging discipline. Overall accounting quality: 5/10.
