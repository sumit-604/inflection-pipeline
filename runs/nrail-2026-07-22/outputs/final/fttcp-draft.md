## THE CATALYST STORY IN PLAIN WORDS

The engine of any upside here is one thing: a paperboard price recovery. Everything the company controls is already done. It built the plant, it runs it at 95%, it sells everything it makes. So the four transitions do not have four independent catalysts; they largely share one, and it is a cyclical price recovery the company cannot manufacture.

Revenue. The documented catalyst is the Rs 293.5 Cr of FY26 capex and Rs 227 Cr of capital work in progress sitting on the audited balance sheet with no explanation in the filed results. This is real spending (📄), and it hints at more capacity, possibly the second unit at Dahej the operator relayed. What confirms it: a board resolution or exchange filing naming a site, a capacity, and a cost. What kills it: the capex turning out to be routine maintenance with no new-capacity revenue.

Margin and ROCE. The shared catalyst is paperboard realization turning up off the roughly Rs 37 a kg trough. What confirms it: two quarters of EBITDA margin holding above roughly 11% on price, not just volume. What kills it: realizations breaking below Rs 35 a kg, or a fresh capex round adding fixed cost before prices recover.

Cash conversion. The honest finding here is NONE FOUND. There is no factoring facility, no stated debtor-day target, no working-capital program in any filing, and with no concall there is no management commentary to weigh. In a business where receivables jumped 114% in a year, the absence of any cash-discipline action is itself the signal. What would change it: a sanctioned, operational receivables facility or a hit debtor-day target in reported numbers.

The peer read complicates two comforting stories. JK Paper management says on the record that coated board stays 50% to 60% imported and will keep being imported, which undercuts the idea that NRAIL's value-added board is capturing an import-substitution gap. JK Paper also says smaller mills are closing on their own inefficiencies, not on import dumping, which undercuts the idea that consolidation is a tailwind flowing to NRAIL. Peers do confirm one thing: the whole sector is in a capex race through the downturn, so a second unit would be normal industry behaviour, not a lone bet.
