## 8.15 Narrative (the story behind the numbers)

IndiaMART's FY26 Annual Report tells two stories at once. The audited, decomposed story is different. Reported PAT fell 13.8%. EPS fell 13.9%. Operating EBITDA on the standalone book grew just 1.32% on revenue growth of 9.3%, because Other Expenses jumped 30.9% (Advertisement spend went 7x to ₹26.7 Cr) and Employee Costs grew 15.3%. That is genuine negative operating leverage. The company is investing harder to defend ground that isn't quite holding — active buyers fell 3% YoY for the first time ever, unique inquiries went mildly negative, and Top 10% ARPU growth decelerated to 9% (the floor of management's prior guided range). On the strength of the cash quality and Busy execution alone, this stock is not broken.

But the governance signals deserve attention. The Rule 11(g) audit trail issue — which last year was a 4.5-month lapse in the holding company — is now a roughly 12-month lapse in a subsidiary, almost certainly Busy Infotech (the entity that IS an accounting software business, which makes the irony pointed). The auditor has, for the first time, elevated Goodwill Impairment (₹454 Cr against Busy and Livekeeping) to a Key Audit Matter on the consolidated statements. The Livekeeping ₹42 Cr piece is most at risk because TallyPrime now ships its own native mobile app, and that's what Livekeeping was built to be. IB MonotaRO's investment impairment has built from ₹23 Cr to ₹39 Cr cumulative — 33% written down. The AR's silence on active buyers, ARPU, treasury MTM, and the audit trail issue is narrative management, not transparency. The Q1 FY27 result in mid-July 2026 is still the decision event. If net adds turn positive and active buyers stabilise, the war-disruption story holds and the position can be restored to 3%. If they don't, the position trims to 1.5% — and then this becomes a slow walk to the exit rather than a thesis to defend.
