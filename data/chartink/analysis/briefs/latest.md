## Where the market stands

Picture three clocks on every stock. The 20 day line is the short clock. The 50 day line is the middle clock. The 200 day line is the long clock. A stock above a line is in an uptrend on that clock. On Friday 1,060 stocks stood above the short line, 989 above the middle line and 1,026 above the long line. All three counts sit below half the market. All three fell on Friday.

The autumn gives these counts their meaning. 23 September was the last day when more stocks stood above the long line than below it. October brought two waves of selling. On 21 October, 413 stocks fell hard in a single session. Two sessions later, on 23 October, 390 more fell hard, and only 607 stocks held the long line. That is still the low of the year on this count.

The climb out came in two bursts. On 4 November, 320 stocks rose hard, and 243 more did the next day. Those were real thrust days. By 12 November the long count reached 1,153 and the short count reached 1,719. That was the high water mark of the bounce, and the market has given ground on the surface clocks ever since.

This week told the story in five sessions. Monday brought 217 hard fallers, the widest down day since 30 October. Tuesday was quiet. Wednesday was narrow but wide in loss, with about seven stocks in ten closing lower. Thursday answered with about six in ten closing higher. Friday then undid most of Thursday. Only 48 stocks rose hard, and 81 fell hard. About four stocks in ten closed higher and six in ten closed lower. Over the full week the short count fell from about 1,600 to 1,060. The middle count fell from about 1,270 to 989. The long count fell from about 1,140 to 1,026. The Nifty rose through all of it.

Friday's turnover is the detail that matters most. Money came in at about 568 crore, against about 464 crore on Thursday. That is in line with the same session a year ago. So Friday was no quiet drift. Real money traded, and most of it went into a few large names while the average stock fell. About one stock in seven traded four percent below the prior close at some point in the day. Only about three in ten of the stocks that traded four percent up kept that gain into the close. That share has stayed near a third all week. Sellers own the close.

The quiet that built through the week eased a little. About four stocks in ten still moved less than three percent from low to high on Friday. The share of stocks stuck in a tight five day range slipped from Thursday's twenty session high.

Slow damage kept piling up in the tail. The share of stocks down ten percent or more over five sessions rose for a fifth straight day. It started the stretch at less than one in a hundred and now stands at about one in sixteen. On the other side, fewer than one stock in a hundred is up fifteen percent over five sessions. That is low for this history.

Fear moved the wrong way for a rising index. The India VIX measures what traders pay for protection against large swings in the Nifty. It rose about seven and a half percent on Friday, to about 17.3, while the Nifty rose about one percent. It is up about half again over three months. Protection got dearer on two up days in a row. ## What would change this read

The read is a bounce off the October low that lost its width for a second week and kept its height through the banks. The index rose while the average stock slid under its short, middle and long lines. The constructive view fails if the slide under the surface continues. A long count under about 1,000 would say a new leg down has begun, from 1,026 on Friday. A day of 300 or more hard fallers would repeat the October pattern. New yearly lows above about 100 would say sellers have returned in force, from 68. A VIX close above about 18, from 17.3, would say fear has taken charge. A wide fall in banks on heavy volume would remove the group that carries the index. A long count under about 607, the October low, would erase the whole bounce.

The cautious view fails if the members catch up with the index. The first sign is the short count back above half the market, about 1,170, from 1,060. The second is the long count back above 1,050 and then turning toward half, from 1,026. The third is a wide up day with 200 or more hard risers on turnover above about 600 crore. The fourth is new yearly highs pulling clear of new lows, from level at 67 and 68. The fifth is the share of stocks down ten percent in a week turning lower, from about one in sixteen. The first two signs together, with any one of the others in the same week, would put the repair back on track.
