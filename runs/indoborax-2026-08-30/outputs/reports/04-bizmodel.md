## SECTION 1: THE BUSINESS MODEL IN PLAIN ENGLISH

### 1A. One-line description
Indo Borax makes boron chemicals, mainly boric acid, at one plant in Madhya
Pradesh. It buys almost all its raw ore from abroad and sells almost all its
output inside India (AR p.6, Note 27 p.104; AR p.33 forex table).

### 1B. Money flow chain, by revenue stream

**Stream 1: Manufactured boron products (boric acid technical, boric acid
IP grade, DOT, boron oxide)**
[Imported boron ore, mainly from overseas mines, no Indian ore reserve
exists (AR MD&A p.69)] → [Company processes ore into boric acid, DOT,
boron oxide, and gypsum by-product at the Pithampur, MP plant (AR p.6)] →
[Delivers packaged boric acid powder, DOT, and boron oxide to industrial
buyers in steel, ceramics, glass, pharma, personal care, and agriculture
(Inv. Pres. slide 13)] → [Domestic industrial buyers across states, West
Bengal, Rajasthan and Maharashtra named as key markets (AR MD&A p.70)] →
[Buyers pay on credit; the Company collects fast, receivable days fell to
about 18 in FY26 from about 33 in FY25 (computed from AR Note 10 p.99 and
Note 25 p.103)].

**Stream 2: Traded goods (bought-in chemicals resold without processing)**
[Company buys chemicals it does not manufacture, sulphuric acid in FY25,
"chemicals and others" in FY26 (AR Note 25 p.103, Note 28 p.104)] →
[Company resells the same goods, no manufacturing step] → [Delivered to
the same or adjacent industrial buyer base] → [Small, non-recurring line;
each year names a different traded product, no strategic continuity
disclosed (AR Note 25, p.103; Note 28, p.104)].

### 1D. Simplified business model canvas

| Element | This company |
|---|---|
| What they sell | Boric acid (technical and IP/pharma grade), DOT, boron oxide, gypsum by-product (AR p.1) |
| Who buys | Domestic industrial buyers: steel/refractory, glass, ceramics/tiles, flame retardants, pharma, personal care, agriculture, plywood, insecticides (Inv. Pres. slide 13-14). No named individual customer disclosed |
| Why them | ~50% India market share in boric acid; sole IP-grade boric acid manufacturer with an FDA licence; BIS-certified; decades of customer and distributor relationships (Inv. Pres. slide 3, slide 10) |

| WC intensity | High. Inventory alone ran 99 days of cost of goods in FY26 and 210 days in FY25 (computed from AR Note 8 p.97 and Note 27 p.104), reflecting import lead times for ore |

### 1E. The chai-stall-uncle version
Think of Indo Borax like a spice trader who buys one rare spice from
overseas, because India does not grow it, and grinds it into a form that
factories across India need for glass, steel, and even some medicines.
Almost nobody else in India can sell the pharma-grade version, so the
company has a near-monopoly at home. But it has to pay for the raw spice
in dollars, so when the dollar price of the spice moves, its profit
margin moves too, even though its own selling price barely changes for a
while. It sells only inside India, none of this spice leaves the country
yet. And a big chunk of its money currently sits in mutual funds, not in
the spice business itself, because a new owner has just taken charge and
is still deciding what to do with the cash pile.

### 2A. Five forces, plainly

| Force | Finding | Helps / Hurts / Neutral |

| Competitive rivalry | Company states "leading player of Boric Acid in India, with market share of ~50%" (Inv. Pres. slide 3). No named domestic competitor disclosed in either document | Helps |
| Threat of new entrants | FDA licence for pharma-grade boric acid and BIS certification are named barriers; company was "the only company to do so as per the Indian Pharmacopoeia" when it received the licence in 1999-2000 (Inv. Pres. slide 10) | Helps |
| Supplier power | 82.51% of raw material consumed is imported (AR Note 27, p.104); MD&A states "commercially viable boron ore reserves are not available in India" so the industry as a whole depends on imports (AR MD&A p.69). | Threat of substitutes | Not directly addressed in either filed document. Boron's role in each end use (flame retardant chemistry, glass strengthening, fertiliser micronutrient) is fairly specific, which argues for low substitution risk, but this is an inference, not a filed finding | Neutral (inference only) |

### 2D. Industry lifecycle stage
The India boric acid market is forecast to grow at 7-8% CAGR from FY26 to
FY30, rising from 38,000-40,000 tonnes to 50,000-53,000 tonnes (Inv. Pres.
slide 14). That is steady, mature-market growth, not an early-stage or
declining category. Indo Borax sits inside it as the entrenched
incumbent leader by volume share, currently mid-way through an
ownership-driven strategic pivot: from a single-product domestic
converter toward a stated ambition of a "diversified Boron chemistry
company of global relevance" (AR MD letter, p.2-3). That ambition is
management's stated intent; it is not yet evidenced by an export revenue
line (still nil, AR p.33).

## SECTION 4: RISKS, VALUATION APPROACH & MONITORING

### 4A. Business-model-specific risks

| Category | Risk | First financial line item to watch |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue model | Single plant, single reportable segment (AR Note (t), p.92); any disruption stops nearly all output | Quarterly revenue from operations, and any disclosed production-shutdown note (MD&A already flags "temporary operational disruptions due to plant maintenance shutdowns", AR MD&A p.70) |
| Margin | Imported ore price and currency exposure, 82.51% of raw material imported (AR Note 27) | Raw material cost as % of revenue, quarter over quarter |
| Balance sheet | Legacy Inter-Corporate Deposit of Rs 509.04 lakh under IBC with no interest accrued (AR Note 47 reference; Auditor's Emphasis of Matter, p.72) | Provision for doubtful advances / write-off line in future notes |
| Execution | Boron Oxide expansion (~Rs 20 crore, 4,000 MTPA) and a newly reconstituted board/management team (6 of 6 non-founder directors appointed 23-Jan-2026, AR p.28) running the transition | Capital work-in-progress note and commissioning-date disclosure |
| Structural | Zero export revenue and total reliance on a single country-agnostic import channel for the core raw material, with no disclosed alternate domestic source (AR p.33; AR MD&A p.69) | Foreign exchange inflow line (currently nil both years) |

### 4B. Valuation method applicability (handoff to Role 1)

| Method | Applicable? | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| EV/EBITDA on normalised, clean operating EBITDA | Yes — PRIMARY | Strips out the Other Income/treasury distortion (22.4% of PBT, FY26) and exceptional items (Rs 1,014.75 lakh gain), leaving a comparable operating-earnings base for a business whose margin already swings with the input-cost cycle |
| Sum-of-the-parts (core operations + treasury/investment property, valued separately) | Yes — SECONDARY | Roughly 46-58% of total assets sit in mutual funds, an investment property (Rs 3,851.48 lakh, AR Note 4, p.95), and a wholly-owned subsidiary investment (Rs 1,040.93 lakh, AR Note 5, p.96); blending this into a single operating multiple misprices the stock either way |
| P/E on normalised (ex-exceptional) earnings | Yes — TERTIARY | Useful cross-check once the Rs 1,014.75 lakh exceptional gain and the Other Income share are stripped out |
| DCF | Not applicable currently | Multi-year cash flow visibility is weak amid an active ownership transition, an unproven export/diversification plan, and unresolved capital-allocation direction (Section 186 acquisition envelope referenced in company memory, not yet disclosed in the AR read for this stage) |
| Dividend Discount Model | Not applicable currently | FY26 dividend of Rs 40/share (4000% of face value, AR p.5) is explicitly funded partly by "monetisation of non-core assets" (AR MD letter, p.2), a one-off, not a stable payout base |
| Replacement cost / asset-based | Not applicable as primary | Understates the licence and relationship intangibles (FDA/BIS, decades of customer relationships) that are the real moat, and understates nothing of the treasury (which is already at fair/mark-to-market value) |
| Platform/user-metric based methods | Not applicable | Not a platform business |

**Cycle stage that matters for valuation:** mid-cycle. The Company is
inside a period of unusually high margin-driver volatility, EBITDA
margin moved from 26.2% to 20.5% year on year on the same underlying
capacity (Inv. Pres. slide 6), so any multiple should be applied to a
normalised, not a single-year, earnings base.

3. Raw material cost as % of revenue, and imported vs indigenous split if disclosed

10. Status of the Rs 509.04 lakh Inter-Corporate Deposit under IBC
11. Board/management continuity (any further resignations after the January 2026 reconstitution)
12. Dividend policy versus the FY26 one-off Rs 30/share special dividend
13. Any disclosure of capacity utilisation percentage for Boric Acid or DOT
14. Related-party transaction disclosures tied to the new promoter group
15. Any named competitor or market-share data appearing in a future filing or concall

### 4D. Highest-value questions for management

1. What is current capacity utilisation for Boric Acid (20,000 MTPA) and DOT (6,000 MTPA), and how much of FY26's 22.9% revenue growth was volume versus price/ore-cost pass-through? Reassures if utilisation and volume both rose; worries if growth is entirely price-driven.
2. Given 82.5% of raw material is imported (AR Note 27), what multi-sourcing or hedging strategy exists beyond "developing alternative sourcing channels" (AR MD&A p.69)? Reassures with named alternate origins or forward contracts in place; worries if the single-channel dependency continues unhedged.
3. What is the recovery plan and timeline for the Rs 509.04 lakh Inter-Corporate Deposit under IBC (AR p.67, p.72)? Reassures with a disclosed NCLT resolution timeline; worries with continued silence or a further write-off.
4. Why did the Board fund a Rs 30/share special dividend through "monetisation of non-core assets" (AR MD letter, p.2) rather than retain the amount for the disclosed Boron Oxide capacity expansion? Reassures if expansion capex is already ring-fenced separately; worries if treasury depletion precedes a capital-intensive phase.
5. What concrete export markets and customer commitments exist behind the stated ambition to "expand our international footprint" in South America and South-East Asia (AR MD letter, p.2)? Reassures with named registrations or letters of intent; worries if this remains aspirational language only.
6. Does the plan to add Boron Oxide capacity change the raw-material import mix or exposure (AR MD letter, p.2)? Reassures if it reduces net import dependency; worries if it simply adds another imported-input product line.
7. With 6 of 6 non-founder board seats filled on 23-Jan-2026 and 11 board meetings held in FY26 (AR p.26, p.28), what specific transition milestones is the new board tracking for FY27? Reassures with named, dated milestones; worries with generic language only.
