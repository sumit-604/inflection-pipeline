## SECTION 1: MARKET DEFINITION

### 1A Precise boundaries

- **Product scope (IN):** IVD reagents and consumables for clinical
  chemistry, haematology and basic immunoassay; rapid/lateral-flow test
  kits (pregnancy, COVID-19, H1N1, HIV, HBsAg, malaria, dengue, VTM);
  biochemistry and haematology analysers (traded high-end + manufactured
  entry-level); AMC/CMC service on placed instruments. (Source: B04-bizmodel
  revenue-stream list; H2 FY26 call p6-7, Concall_Jul_2026_Transcript.txt.)
- **Product scope (OUT):** molecular diagnostics/PCR platforms at scale,
  high-end immunoassay/chemiluminescence platforms, imaging (radiology),
  oxygen concentrators (non-IVD medical device, kept as a manufactured line
  but excluded from the IVD TAM math below), managed-lab/testing-services
  model (evaluated, not yet funded, call p5).
- **Geographic scope:** current — Delhi NCR and eastern Uttar Pradesh
  (Mindray channel-partner territory) plus Uttarakhand (new territory via
  the ~Rs 47 Cr order). In discussion — exclusive North India rights with
  Mindray for selected products (call p4, p7: "currently discussing North
  India with Mindray... expect to explore expansion into eastern, southern
  and western India subsequently"). Excluded — rest of India, exports (Rs
  5-7 Cr FY27 target, call p4, immaterial to the India SAM).
- **Customer scope:** government hospitals, private labs, diagnostic
  centres, healthcare institutions (deck p5, "Business Model" slide);
  "several state governments" for tender business (call p4), named states
  today: Uttar Pradesh, Uttarakhand.
- **Channel scope:** Mindray non-exclusive regional distribution (72% of
  FY26 revenue, closed-loop reagent-rental placement, B04); own CDSCO-
  licensed manufacturing (88 approved products, ~175 targeted by FY27-end,
  call p6); government tender route (nascent, no Reg 30 order-win filing
  exists since listing per B00).
- **Price segment:** mid-market Indian institutional pricing (government
  tender rates, hospital procurement), not premium imported-platform
  pricing and not retail/home-testing.

### 1B Management's own claim

Avience management gives no single "our TAM is Rs X Cr" figure anywhere in
the corpus (RHP, AR, deck, call). What exists instead:
- **Revenue guidance, not a market-size claim:** FY27 >=60% growth to
  >Rs 100 Cr; FY28 aspiration Rs 160-165 Cr (call p5-6, Dharam Deo
  Choudhary).
- **A capacity ceiling, the closest thing to a stated opportunity size:**
  "the new facility can support peak revenue of approximately Rs 250 crore
  ... [an] indicated capacity of around Rs 250-265 crore, subject to

  MANUFACTURING capacity ceiling, not a market-size claim, and management
  is explicit about the conditionality.
- **Industry-level claims are third-party, filed in the RHP "Industry
  Overview" chapter (pdf pp 138-156), not company-specific:** India IVD
  market USD 1.94 Bn (2026) to USD 2.66 Bn (2031), 6.49% CAGR, sourced to
  Mordor Intelligence (RHP p151/[page 156]); Indian diagnostics market ~USD
  13 Bn (2023), expected to double by 2028 (RHP p150/[page 155], sourced to
  Statista, dated 2023 data — STALE at run date, direction only); Indian
  medical devices market USD 15.35 Bn (2023) to USD 20.51 Bn (2029) at
  5.35% CAGR (RHP p148/[page 153]) — this conflicts internally with a
  separate RHP citation two paragraphs later giving "USD 14 billion...
  expected to grow to USD 30 billion by 2030" (RHP p149/[page 154], sourced
  to a PIB press release) — a ~2x divergence in the SAME chapter for the
  SAME market. **Credibility read: BROAD.** These are boilerplate national
  industry-chapter statistics common to Indian SME IPO prospectuses, not
  claims tailored to Avience's actual regional, product-narrow footprint,
  and they conflict with each other before any comparison to Avience is
  even made. Management has NOT committed the cardinal sin of claiming
  TAM=SAM=runway — it never states a TAM figure for itself at all — but
  the RHP's own industry-chapter numbers are internally inconsistent and
  need discounting accordingly (held for Section 2's triangulation table).

## SECTION 2: TAM ESTIMATION, MULTIPLE METHODS

### Method 1 — Top-down (national IVD market, filtered)

Base: India IVD market, Mordor Intelligence via RHP p151, USD 1.94 Bn
(2026) -> USD 2.66 Bn (2031), 6.49% CAGR 2026-2031. This is the single
most on-point figure in the corpus (product-scope match: "instruments/
analysers, reagents & kits, software & services... clinical chemistry,
immuno-diagnostics, hematology, molecular diagnostics, coagulation,
microbiology" — RHP p151/[page 156]).

- 2026 TAM (national, all-India, all IVD product types) = USD 1.94 Bn x
  Rs 96/USD = **Rs 18,624 Cr**.
- 2027 TAM (one year forward, +6.49%) = **Rs 19,833 Cr**.
- 2031 TAM = USD 2.66 Bn x Rs 96 = Rs 25,536 Cr.

Subtraction 1 — product-type filter: Avience does not compete in
molecular diagnostics/PCR platforms at scale (though it manufactured
COVID-19 RT-qPCR kits during the pandemic, deck p10) nor in high-end
immunoassay/chemiluminescence platforms (Roche/Abbott/Siemens/Beckman
territory). The RHP/Mordor text states "by test type, clinical chemistry
holds the largest market share, followed by molecular diagnostics and
hematology" (WebSearch, Mordor Intelligence, 2026-09-26) but gives NO
percentage split. **NOT FOUND: numeric test-type segmentation for the
India IVD market.** The only sourced product-type split is "reagents and
kits held 64.78% of revenue in 2025" (WebSearch, Mordor Intelligence,
2026-09-26) — a product-form cut (reagents/kits vs instruments/software),
not a test-type cut, but it is the only sourced filter available and it
roughly matches Avience's own revenue shape (consumables-heavy: 59.83% +
14.62% + 9.15% = 83.6% of FY26 revenue is reagents/consumables/rapid
cards, B04). Applying it: Rs 18,624 Cr x 64.78% = **Rs 12,065 Cr**
(2026, reagents/kits-equivalent national IVD demand).
- No further product subtraction is made for lack of a sourced test-type
  split; this is flagged as a real gap, not smoothed over with an
  estimate.

This Rs 12,065 Cr figure is carried into Section 3 as the product-filtered
national base before the geography filter is applied.

### Method 2 — Bottom-up (addressable unit)

**DATA-LIMITED.** The defined addressable unit per B04 is "one placed
diagnostic instrument under a 5-year reagent-rental contract." Neither the
number of instruments currently placed nor the average annual reagent
revenue per placed instrument is disclosed anywhere in the corpus (B04
unit_economics: both NOT FOUND). A web search for hospital/lab counts in
Delhi NCR, eastern UP and Uttarakhand returned only a single-city directory
count (149 pathology labs listed for New Delhi on a consumer aggregator
site, WebSearch 2026-09-26) — not a market-report-grade statistic, and no
count exists at all for eastern UP or Uttarakhand specifically. **A
rigorous bottom-up TAM cannot be built from sourced numbers.** The one
adjacent bottom-up-style data point found is a Uttar Pradesh state budget
line of Rs 12 Cr for an "In-house Reagent Facility" (IRF) programme to let
state medical colleges/hospitals run diagnostics cheaper (WebSearch,
2026-09-26) — this is a single-state, single-year government capex line,
not a market size, and is carried instead as a downstream signal candidate
(Section 6), not forced into the TAM math. Method 2 does not produce an
independent TAM estimate this run; it is a named gap.

### Method 3 — Peer revenue aggregation

Named organised IVD/diagnostics-adjacent players with disclosed FY25/FY26
revenue (screening/*.csv, WebSearch):
- Transasia Bio-Medicals (part of the Erba Mannheim group; India's largest
  indigenous IVD player): **Rs 1,450 Cr**, FY25 (WebSearch, 2026-09-26).
- Molbio Diagnostics (molecular diagnostics leader, recently listed):
  **Rs 1,313.4 Cr**, FY26 (screening/MOLBIO-Data_Sheet.csv).
- Q-Line Biotech (QLINE, closest structural peer per B04): **Rs 341.74
  Cr**, FY26 (screening/QLINE-Data_Sheet.csv).
- Tarsons Products (labware/consumables, adjacent not core IVD reagents):
  **Rs 422.51 Cr**, FY26 (screening/TARSONS-Data_Sheet.csv).
- Avience itself: Rs 52.51 Cr, FY26.

Named-peer sum (excluding Avience) = Rs 3,527.65 Cr. **This materially
understates the organised market**, because the largest India IVD revenue
pools sit inside MNC subsidiaries whose India-specific IVD segment revenue
is NOT FOUND anywhere in this corpus or in public search this run: Roche
Diagnostics India, Abbott Diagnostics India, Siemens Healthineers India,
Beckman Coulter India, and Mindray's own India revenue (Mindray is
Avience's principal, not a competitor, but its scale illustrates the gap:
Mindray Bio-Medical Electronics is a multi-billion-dollar global group;
its standalone India P&L is not disclosed). Add an unorganised-sector
estimate per pipeline methodology (stated explicitly as an estimate, 30-
60% of the total market is typically unorganised in Indian healthcare
distribution): if the named-peer-plus-MNC organised tier is even loosely
consistent with the Rs 12,065 Cr Method-1 product-filtered figure, the
unorganised/regional-distributor tail (companies exactly like Avience) is
plausibly Rs 3,600-7,200 Cr of it. **Confidence: LOW.** This method is
retained as a sanity floor (the named organised peers alone are ~29% of
the Method-1 product-filtered TAM), not as an independent TAM estimate,
given the unquantified MNC gap.

### Method 4 — Import substitution

RHP: "India still imports 70-80% of its medical devices" (RHP p148/[page
153]); PLI Scheme for Pharmaceuticals explicitly "covers Invitro
diagnostic devices... five (5) industry applicants have been selected...
for Invitro diagnostic medical devices," incentivised on incremental sales
FY21-FY29 (RHP p148-149/[page 153-154]). No IVD-specific import ratio is
sourced (the 70-80% figure is for medical devices broadly, not IVD
narrowly) — **NOT FOUND at the IVD-specific level**, flagged rather than
assumed equal. Applying the broad medical-device import ratio as a
directional proxy only: 70-80% of the Rs 12,065 Cr product-filtered 2026
TAM (~Rs 8,446-9,652 Cr) is plausibly import-substitutable over time. This
is the single most direct link to Avience's own strategy — the YEIDA plant,
WHO-prequalification ambition (call p8) and manufacturing-mix-shift target
(27:72 to 50:50, B04) are explicitly aimed at this substitution pool. It is
a directional cross-check on the manufacturing thesis, not a SAM/SOM input
in its own right, and is carried into Section 4 as a growth driver.

### Method 5 — Global benchmark

Global IVD market: estimates range widely by scope/methodology (USD 88.88
Bn to USD 152.42 Bn for 2026 across different providers, WebSearch
2026-09-26) — **too methodologically inconsistent across sources to use as
a single global anchor this run**; named but not relied upon.
China vs India, same-vintage sources: China IVD market USD 19.57 Bn (2026)
-> USD 24.92 Bn (2031) (WebSearch, OpenPR/GrandView, 2026-09-26) vs India
USD 1.94 Bn (2026), i.e. **India is ~10% of China's IVD market size**
despite comparable population (~1.44 Bn vs ~1.41 Bn). Per-capita: China
~USD 13.9/head vs India ~USD 1.35/head — roughly a 10x per-capita gap.
This is consistent with, and corroborates, the RHP's own healthcare-spend-
as-%-of-GDP framing (India 3.3% of GDP in 2022, targeted to reach 5% by
2030, RHP p145/[page 150] — a 2022 data point, borderline stale, direction
only). **One divergent source gives India IVD at USD 6.2 Bn by 2030**
(WebSearch, 2026-09-26), roughly 2.3x the Mordor/RHP 2031 figure of USD
2.66 Bn for a comparable date. Per the CONSERVATIVE BIAS rule, the lower
(Mordor/RHP-sourced) figure is used for the headline TAM; the higher figure
is named as an upside case, not averaged in.

TAM with its own achievable revenue. The economically meaningful
comparison is against the regionally- and channel-filtered SAM in Section

## SECTION 3: SAM & SOM

### 3A SAM

Starting base: Rs 12,065 Cr (2026, product-filtered national TAM, Method
1).

**Filter 1 — Geography.** Avience's live and near-term territory is Delhi
NCR + eastern Uttar Pradesh (current Mindray channel-partner allocation,
call p4) plus Uttarakhand (new, via the ~Rs 47 Cr order, call p5).
"North India" (typically Delhi, Punjab, Haryana, UP, Uttarakhand,
Rajasthan, Himachal, J&K in market-research usage) is reported to hold
**32% of the India diagnostics-services market by value in 2025**
(WebSearch, 2026-09-26) — this is a diagnostics-SERVICES share (labs
running tests), used here as a proxy for the IVD-PRODUCT demand a region
generates, which is an approximation, not a like-for-like figure, and is
flagged as such. Applying it: Rs 12,065 Cr x 32% = **Rs 3,861 Cr** (full
North India, product-filtered). Avience's ACTUAL current footprint
(Delhi NCR + one part of UP + Uttarakhand) is a sub-set of this 32%
region, not the whole of it — Punjab, Haryana, Rajasthan and the rest of
UP are excluded from what Avience touches today. No sourced state-by-state
split of the 32% exists (NOT FOUND) to cut this further with evidence, so
Rs 3,861 Cr is carried as an outer-bound geography filter, with the
channel filter below doing the remaining, necessarily judgment-based, work.

**Filter 2 — Channel/capability.** Avience's actual reach inside that
geography is constrained by: (a) a NON-exclusive Mindray distribution
relationship that could be curtailed or reallocated (B04 FLAG-
CONCENTRATION; Mindray purchases were 63-73% of Avience's own purchases
FY24-FY26, RHP p38); (b) a single manufacturing facility until YEIDA ramps
(commissioning guided Oct-2026, FY27 utilisation only 15-20%, call p6-7);
(c) an unrated balance sheet (rating/: EMPTY, B00) that limits the size of
government tenders it can bid and bond for; (d) no pan-India sales
network (deck's "nationwide customer reach across India" slide claim,
p9, sits in tension with the company's own admission that it operates a
regional Mindray allocation and is only "discussing" North India
exclusivity — a marketing-vs-filing inconsistency worth naming). A 50%
channel/capability discount is applied to the geography-filtered figure.
**This 50% figure is an analyst judgment, not a sourced percentage** — no
filing states a channel-penetration rate — and is named as such rather
than dressed up as evidence.

SAM = Rs 3,861 Cr x 50% = **Rs 1,930 Cr, rounded to Rs 1,900 Cr.**

**SAM as % of TAM (national, 2026): Rs 1,900 / Rs 18,624 = 10.2%.**

### 3B SOM at 3 and 5 years

Current SAM share: FY26 revenue Rs 52.51 Cr / SAM Rs 1,900 Cr = **2.76%**.

Share-gain trajectory used: the AGGRESSIVE band (3-5pp over 3 years, per
pipeline share-gain rules), justified because Avience's capacity build
(YEIDA plant, substantially complete, call p3-4) and territory expansion
(confirmed Uttarakhand order, call p5) are both already underway, not
merely aspirational — the normal-case 1-2pp band would understate a
company mid-execution on both levers, but the aggressive band is the
ceiling justified by evidence, not a base case.

- **3-year (FY29), midpoint +4pp:** share -> 6.76%. SAM grown at 6.49%
  CAGR over 3 years: Rs 1,900 x 1.0649^3 = Rs 2,296 Cr. SOM3yr = 6.76% x
  Rs 2,296 Cr = **Rs 155 Cr**.
- **5-year (FY31), +6-8pp (only credible if North India exclusivity
  converts and YEIDA runs at higher utilisation), midpoint +8pp:** share
  -> 10.76%. SAM grown 5 years: Rs 1,900 x 1.0649^5 = Rs 2,608 Cr.
  SOM5yr = 10.76% x Rs 2,608 Cr = **Rs 281 Cr** (rounded to Rs 280 Cr
  for the headline figure below the arithmetic is shown at Rs 261 Cr
  under a more conservative +6pp assumption; the range is Rs 261-281
  Cr — the block below carries the midpoint, Rs 270 Cr, flagged as a
  range not a point estimate).

**SOM-implied revenue CAGR (FY26 base Rs 52.51 Cr) — the FORMAL handoff
to Stage 11:**
- Yr3: (155/52.51)^(1/3) - 1 = **43.4%**
- Yr5: (270/52.51)^(1/5) - 1 = **38.7%**

**Cross-check against management's own guidance:** FY26 (Rs 52.51 Cr) to
FY28 (Rs 160-165 Cr midpoint Rs 162.5 Cr) implies a 2-year CAGR of
**75.9%** — well above even the aggressive-band SOM3yr CAGR of 43.4% over
a LONGER (3-year) window. **This is the single most important finding of
this stage: management's near-term guidance runs materially ahead of what
an evidence-based, aggressive-case share-gain model supports.** The most
likely explanation is not that the SAM is under-sized but that the ~Rs 47
Cr Uttarakhand order is a lumpy, largely one-off government contract with
a hardware-heavy component (call p7: "large equipment component"), not a
recurring market-share gain — a single order win, once delivered, does not
repeat automatically. Stage 11 should treat the FY27/FY28 guidance
growth as ORDER-DRIVEN, not SHARE-DRIVEN, until a second comparable order
is confirmed (B00 LBF1 already flags the absence of a Reg 30 order-win
filing since listing).

### 3C Capacity cross-check

Capex-embedded-growth figure from Stage 7 (B07) was not available at this
stage's run time (parallel execution); per task instructions, the filed
capex figures are used directly:
- YEIDA total project cost: **Rs 30.18 Cr** (Rs 3,018.46 Lakh — RHP
  p96/[page 101]: land Rs 191.00 Lakh + building Rs 1,195.81 Lakh + plant
  and machinery Rs 1,631.66 Lakh), of which **Rs 15.95 Cr** (Rs 1,595.53
  Lakh) is financed from IPO net proceeds (RHP "Objects of the Issue,"
  cross-checked at RHP line 1369 and 5881-5897).
- FY26 AR: Capital Work-in-Progress "Projects in progress — Biochemistry
  Analyser Plant at YEIDA" = **Rs 12.85 Cr** (Rs 1,285.28 Lakh),
  cumulative additions Rs 823.37 Lakh in FY26 on an opening Rs 461.91 Lakh
  (Annual_Report_FY2026_Avience.txt, note 13, lines 3711-3725).
- Management's own peak-revenue capacity claim for the new facility:
  **Rs 250-265 Cr** (call p5-6), explicitly conditioned on "product mix,
  approvals, demand and execution."

**Can capacity produce the SOM revenue? YES, with an important caveat
on WHICH revenue.** The Rs 250-265 Cr figure is a MANUFACTURING-only
ceiling (own reagents, rapid cards, own-brand instruments) — it does not
constrain the traded/Mindray-distribution engine (72% of FY26 revenue),
which is capacity-free from Avience's own balance sheet (Mindray
manufactures the traded goods). Under the current 27:72 manufacturing:
trading mix (call p7), the manufacturing SLICE of SOM5yr (Rs 270 Cr) is
only ~Rs 73-135 Cr depending on how far the guided 50:50 mix-shift
actually lands — comfortably inside the Rs 250-265 Cr ceiling even before
the plant reaches full utilisation. **Capacity check: SUFFICIENT.** The
binding constraint on hitting either the SOM figures above or
management's own guidance is NOT physical manufacturing capacity; it is
(a) whether the manufacturing mix-shift from ~27:72 toward ~50:50 actually
executes (B04 must_track_metrics), and (b) working-capital funding for
the trading/government-tender engine, where management itself flagged an
unfunded Rs 35-40 Cr requirement at Rs 100 Cr revenue with financing "not
available today" (call p6). The optimistic side of any gap, if one opens,
is therefore the WORKING-CAPITAL PLAN, not the capex plan.

## SECTION 4: GROWTH DRIVERS, RISKS & STRUCTURE

### 4A TAM growth drivers

- **Penetration / import substitution:** medical devices sector imports
  70-80% of India's needs (RHP p148); PLI Scheme for Pharmaceuticals names
  IVD explicitly with 5 selected applicants, FY21-FY29 tenure (RHP
  p148-149) — direct tailwind for Avience's own-manufacturing ambition.
  Evidence: filed, national-level, not company-specific. Impact: HIGH,
  multi-year.
- **Regulatory tailwind — GST 2.0:** GST on medical devices/consumables
  (diagnostic kits, reagents, glucometers) cut from 12-18% to 5% (RHP
  p146/[page 151]). Impact: MEDIUM, immediate, lowers end-price and can
  lift volumes; also compresses distributor gross margin unless pass-
  through terms are favourable (not tested in this corpus).
  **STALE FLAG: none — this is a current-year policy citation.**
- **Formalisation:** government push toward state-run "In-house Reagent
  Facility" programmes (e.g., UP's Rs 12 Cr IRF budget line, WebSearch
  2026-09-26) and medical device parks (YEIDA, Ujjain, Visakhapatnam,
  Nalagarh, call p8) is shifting government diagnostics procurement toward
  organised, licensed suppliers — directly relevant to Avience's tender
  ambitions. Impact: MEDIUM, multi-year, evidence: mixed (filed +
  web-sourced).
- **Demographics / disease burden:** national diabetes prevalence 101
  million, anaemia 57% (women) / 67% (children under 5) per NFHS
  (WebSearch, Mordor Intelligence citation, 2026-09-26) — direct volume
  driver for clinical chemistry (diabetes) and haematology (anaemia)
  reagents, Avience's actual product core. Impact: HIGH, structural,
  slow-moving.
- **Technology enablement / POC growth:** rising point-of-care and rapid-
  test adoption nationally (RHP p150-151) matches Avience's rapid-card
  line (9.15% of FY26 revenue). Impact: MEDIUM.
- **New applications:** OEM/private-label discussion with Mindray for a
  closed-loop, barcoded reagent system (call p4) would, if signed, expand
  Avience's addressable reagent volume on Mindray-placed instruments
  beyond its own manufactured SKUs. Impact: potentially HIGH but
  UNCONFIRMED — "discussions," no signed term sheet (B04 mgmt_questions).

### 4B TAM risks

- **Import competition / MNC dominance:** 70-80% import reliance (RHP
  p148) means the highest-value IVD segments (advanced immunoassay,
  molecular platforms) remain MNC-controlled (Roche, Abbott, Siemens,
  Beckman) — Avience's addressable slice stays structurally in the lower-
  value reagent/consumable tier. Monitoring signal: Avience's own gross
  margin mix shift (B04 must-track: manufacturing:trading mix).
- **Regulatory complexity:** "Complex and Evolving Regulations... Frequent
  updates to Medical Devices Rules 2017" and "Fragmented Oversight" cited
  as a sector-wide risk (RHP p149/[page 154]). Monitoring signal: CDSCO
  approval timeline slippage against the 175-product FY27 target (call
  p5).
- **Price regulation:** NPPA price caps exist for some device categories
  (RHP p149) — a monitoring risk if extended to reagents/IVD kits.
  Monitoring signal: any NPPA notification naming IVD product categories.
- **Cyclical/one-off order concentration:** the Uttarakhand order is the
  single largest identified near-term growth driver and is NOT a repeat/
  recurring contract by nature (Section 3B finding). Monitoring signal:
  FY27 half-yearly revenue split ex-Uttarakhand.
- **Saturation is not a near-term risk** given SAM/current-revenue
  headroom (Section 5), but channel saturation (Mindray relationship
  ceiling) is a real, nearer-term constraint (B04 FLAG-CONCENTRATION).

### 4C Market structure

- **Competitor count / concentration:** fragmented at the regional-
  distributor tier (companies like Avience) beneath a concentrated top
  tier of MNC platform makers (Roche, Abbott, Siemens, Beckman, Mindray)
  and a handful of scaled domestic players (Transasia/Erba Rs 1,450 Cr,
  Molbio Rs 1,313 Cr FY26). Top-3 concentration at the MNC-platform level
  is high (qualitative, no sourced %); at the regional-distributor level
  it is low/fragmented.
- **Organised vs unorganised:** India's broader diagnostics-distribution
  tail carries a meaningful unorganised component; no IVD-specific
  organised/unorganised % split is sourced for India (NOT FOUND); the
  30-60% range used in Method 3 is stated explicitly as a rule-of-thumb
  estimate per pipeline methodology, not a filed number.
- **Reagent-rental (razor-and-blade) adoption:** "almost 80 percent of
  floor [large analyser] instruments are on rentals... much smaller for
  bench-top. Semi-automated instruments are all procured, with almost none
  on rentals" (WebSearch, 2026-09-26) — this closely corroborates
  Avience's own disclosed mix ("approximately 80-90% of the reagent and
  consumables business is from closed systems," call p5; "only around
  10-15% of instruments are sold outright... approximately 80-85% are
  placed under reagent-rental arrangements," call p7). Avience's business
  model is a mainstream, not an outlier, instance of the sector norm.
- **Consolidating or fragmenting:** directionally consolidating at the
  top (MNC + scaled-domestic tier growing via own capex — Transasia
  molecular diagnostics push, Molbio's national/international scale) while
  the regional-distributor tier (Avience's tier) remains fragmented and is
  the layer government formalisation programmes (medical device parks,
  IRF schemes) are trying to professionalise.
- **Import share trend:** structurally high (70-80%) and only slowly
  declining under PLI/Make-in-India incentives (RHP), consistent with
  Avience's own manufacturing-mix-shift thesis being a multi-year, not
  multi-quarter, project.

### 5B Runway assessment

- **Revenue headroom (SAM / current revenue):** Rs 1,900 Cr / Rs 52.51 Cr
  = **36.2x**.
- **TAM growth rate:** 6.49% CAGR (2026-2031, Mordor/RHP).
- **Company CAGR vs TAM:** management's own FY26-FY28 guided CAGR (75.9%)
  and even the SOM-implied CAGR (38.7-43.4%) are far above the 6.49% TAM
  growth rate — Avience is unambiguously a SHARE-GAIN story, not a
  ride-the-market story, consistent with its small base.
- **Years to saturate SAM at SOM-implied growth (~38.7% CAGR):**
  ln(36.2)/ln(1.387) = 3.589/0.328 = **~10.9 years**. At the more
  aggressive management-guided rate (if it persisted, which Section 3B
  argues it likely will not without repeat large orders), saturation would
  arrive in ~4.5 years — the gap between these two horizons IS the
  order-driven-vs-share-driven question flagged above, restated as a
  runway question.
