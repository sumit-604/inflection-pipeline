### R1. Inventory-gain contribution never given on one basis; direct challenges deflected in four straight calls. CRITICAL

The question "how much of the margin is gold-price inventory gain" comes up on every call. The answer changes its basis every time, and the sharpest versions of the question get no answer.
- Q2 FY26. Vijay Chauhan asks how much inventory gain sits in gross margin. Vikas Kataria: "we've seen an inventory gain of approximately 20-25%", no base stated (Nov, p8).
- Q3 FY26. Vikas Kataria: inventory gains "account for roughly 25% to 28% of the margin increase" (Jan, p6); then "approximately INR20 crores" of INR 73 Cr PAT (Jan, p8). Two bases in one call.
- Q4 FY26. Chetan asks what drove the Q4 gross-margin drop and whether lower inventory gains caused it. Manish Laddha answers with full-year gross profit, 7.72% to 10%, and does not address Q4 (May, p6). Shreeya Bhatia asks the FY26 inventory-gain benefit. Vikas Kataria: "Approximately 28% to 30% of the inventory gains have been reflected in our books" (May, p9). The sentence contains no quantity.
- Q1 FY27. Manish Laddha: "only around 10% to 15% of the growth can be attributed to inventory gains" (Jul, p14). Lokesh does the arithmetic live: book cost ~Rs 1.20 lakh vs sale ~Rs 1.50 lakh on ~Rs 780 Cr of gold, so ~Rs 150 Cr of gross profit from price alone. Manish Laddha converts pure-gold to 22-karat rates and restates "normal gross margin 10-11%" without a number for the gain (Jul, p18-p19).

Pattern: 4 quarters, 4 bases (unstated %, % of margin increase, % "reflected in books", % of revenue growth), one rupee figure (Q3 only). This is the load-bearing LBF1 question (margin source). It meets the rule 5 definition of a repeated evasion.

### R2. Q1 FY27 margin arithmetic contradicts the stated margin source. MAJOR

- Reported Q1 FY27 EBITDA margin 11.01% (Jul, Manish Laddha, p5).
- Same call, same speaker: "our normal gross margin is in the range of 10% to 11%" (Jul, p19).
- EBITDA sits below gross margin by definition. So Q1 FY27 gross margin sat above the CFO's own stated normal. The CFO attributes "the majority of the improvement" to "higher making charges" and gives inventory gains "only around 10% to 15%" (Jul, p14).
- The "normal gross margin" drifts upward call by call: 8-9% (Nov, Vikas Kataria, p8), 10% FY26 actual (May, Manish Laddha, p6), 10-11% (May, p12; Jul, p19).

Management's stated split cannot produce the reported number without an above-normal gain. The transcripts never name that gain.
