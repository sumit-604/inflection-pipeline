## PASS 3 — PATTERN CHECK

Approached the notes one final time hunting specifically for: notes that contradict each other;
numbers in the notes that don't match the main financial statements; deliberately thin disclosure
next to detailed disclosure elsewhere; prior-year restatements/reclassifications; post
balance-sheet-date events; going-concern language anywhere in the document (not just the
accounting-policy note).

**Contradicting notes / numbers not tying — already surfaced across Pass 1/2, re-verified here as a
set, not three isolated incidents:**
1. Standalone Note 9(c)/Note 10A: "fully impaired" narrative vs a static ₹313.09mn allowance
   against a ₹562.74mn cost / ₹249.65mn net carrying value for the MENA investment (Pass 1 Rank 1;
   sharpened by Pass 2 Finding 4 — the "reduction" in the matching loan was 99.86% a non-cash
   reclassification into that same nominally "fully impaired" equity line).
2. Consolidated Note 25(b): FY24 comparative revenue ties at ₹12,494.99mn in the headline/
   disaggregation sub-tables but at ₹12,240.21mn in the discount-reconciliation sub-table — a
   ₹254.78mn (₹25.48cr) gap within the same note, comparative year only (Pass 1 Rank 2). Re-checked
   against the standalone equivalent (Note 25(b), p.148) which ties correctly — confirms this is
   specific to the consolidated note's FY24 column, not a document-wide pattern.
3. Consolidated Note 46 (p.248) vs Standalone Note 43 (p.169): "the Group has recorded any
   impairment" vs "the Company has not recorded any impairment" — the consolidated sentence, read
   literally, states the opposite of the parallel standalone sentence and of what the surrounding
   context implies (Pass 2 Finding 9).
4. Standalone Statement of Cash Flows (p.114) vs Standalone Note 26 (p.149): FY24 comparative for
   "Gain from termination of leases and outlet closure, net" shown as ₹(120.00)mn in the cash flow
   statement vs ₹111.41mn in the note — ₹8.59mn face-statement-to-note gap (Pass 1, Section 12).

Taken together this is now a **four-item pattern of numbers/narratives that do not tie within the
same annual report** (two note-internal, one note-vs-note, one note-vs-face-statement), spanning
both the standalone and consolidated note sets and both the revenue and investment lines. Re-reading
with this pattern lens specifically, no fifth instance was found on this pass; the ratio-analysis
table (Note 44) variance narratives, the segment note, and the financial-instruments/fair-value
hierarchy notes were checked for internal tie-out and found consistent.

**Deliberately thin disclosure next to detailed disclosure elsewhere:** confirmed pattern, not new —
the ₹744.41mn direct tax contingent liability (18.98% of net worth) carries no case-level breakdown
where the borrowings note (Note 19) discloses individual term-loan instalment schedules to the
month; the ₹77.17mn "Others" deferred-tax reconciling line (85.7% of the theoretical tax credit) is
undecomposed where the DTA components table is granular to seven line items; the "Adjustments
arising out of consolidation" Schedule III residual (₹650.55mn net-asset impact, 3.5x YoY) carries
no decomposition where the rest of Note 45 itemises every subsidiary individually.

**Restatements/reclassifications:** targeted re-read for "restated"/"reclassified" language found
none in the notes (confirmed via full-text check of the extract — no hits for "restat*" anywhere in
the document). The MENA loan-to-equity movement (Pass 2 Finding 4) is a reclassification between
balance sheet line items within the year, not a prior-period restatement, and is disclosed (if
imprecisely) rather than silently reclassified.

**Events after balance sheet date:** confirmed again, no "Events after the Reporting Period" note
exists in either note set (Board approval May 22, 2025; AGM notice August 13, 2025 — no subsequent
event note bridges the ~3 month gap).

**Going concern language:** present in three places — the accounting policy note (Standalone Note
3(e)/Consolidated equivalent), the Board's/management's explicit assessment, and the Independent
Auditor's Report (both standalone and consolidated), all stating no material uncertainty exists.
Full-text search confirms no additional going-concern caveat appears elsewhere in the document (e.g.
Board's Report, MD&A) that would contradict this. Boilerplate in form, but repeated here as required
by the Pass 3 protocol; the substance concern is the trend it sits against (losses widening for a
third-plus consecutive year), already captured in Section D below, not the language itself.

**PASS 3 verdict:** no new standalone findings beyond the pattern consolidation above; the value of
this pass is confirming the four tie-out failures form a genuine cluster (not analyst
over-interpretation of isolated typos) and confirming no restatement or subsequent-event disclosure
exists that would change the picture. Proceeding to consolidation.

### D. FIVE QUESTIONS FOR MANAGEMENT

1. Regarding Note 47/48 (Maintenance of Books of Account): has the Company since obtained, or does
   it plan to obtain, a Service Organisation Controls (SOC) report from its third-party accounting
   software provider so the audit-trail integrity limitation carried in both the notes and the
   Independent Auditor's Report can be resolved, and by when?
2. Regarding Note 9(c)/Note 10A: the narrative states the Barbeque Nation MENA equity investment was
   "fully impaired," but the impairment allowance (₹313.09mn) is static against a ₹562.74mn cost and
   ₹249.65mn net carrying value — is the equity investment considered fully impaired or not, and why
   did the combined MENA provision (investment + loan + interest, ₹777.05mn) not move when
   ₹249.65mn shifted from loan to equity?
3. What is the composition of the ₹77.17mn "Others" reconciling item that reduces the standalone
   deferred tax credit from a theoretical ₹90.00mn (at the statutory 25.17% rate) to the actual
   ₹4.78mn recognised (Note 32)?
4. How is Barbeque Nation Restaurant LLC (Dubai) — the Group's largest single subsidiary profit
   contributor — capitalised or funded given persistent deeply negative net assets (₹(641.23)mn
   FY25, ₹(690.00)mn FY24), and is any intercompany support arrangement specific to this entity in
   place (Note 45)?
5. What drove the introduction of the 12x EBITDA terminal-value floor and the simultaneous
   discount-rate reductions across every CGU (BNHL/Red Apple, MENA, Blue Planet) this year, and what
   quantitative headroom exists before any CGU would fail its impairment test (Note 7/10A)?

### E. NOTES-BASED RED FLAGS

- Auditor-confirmed inability to verify audit-trail integrity, tamper-freedom, and statutory record
  retention for books held on third-party-hosted accounting software (Rule 11(g) limitation,
  Standalone Note 47/Consolidated Note 48, repeated in the Independent Auditor's Report; overall
  opinion unmodified).
- MENA "fully impaired" impairment narrative does not reconcile with the static allowance/carrying
  value in the same note set, and the year's ₹250.00mn loan "reduction" was 99.86% a non-cash
  loan-to-equity reclassification rather than a real repayment (Standalone Note 9(c), Note 10A,
  Note 45).
- Consolidated revenue reconciliation (Note 25b) FY24 comparative fails to tie across sub-tables
  within the same note by ₹254.78mn.
- Goodwill/CGU impairment-test discount rates fell and a new 12x EBITDA terminal-value floor was
  introduced across every CGU in the same year the largest domestic subsidiary swung from profit to
  loss, with no quantified sensitivity disclosed for any CGU.
- ₹260.90mn cumulative two-year premium for increasing the Red Apple stake charged directly to
  retained earnings, bypassing P&L and any impairment test, in the period Red Apple's own result
  collapsed.
- Three separate note-drafting inconsistencies within the same reporting cycle (MENA narrative vs
  table; Note 25b tie-out; Note 46 apparent missing "not"), plus a fourth face-statement-vs-note
  gap (Note 26 vs cash flow statement), indicating a broader note quality-control gap this year.

### F. ONE-LINE NOTES VERDICT

The notes reveal concerning accounting practices. Key concern: an auditor-confirmed inability to
verify audit-trail integrity on third-party-hosted books, compounded by a cluster of four unrelated
note tie-out failures and impairment-methodology changes timed with subsidiary deterioration. Key
strength: leases, depreciation, and RPT policies are disclosed with clear rationale and the overall
audit opinion remains unmodified with no direct evidence of intentional misstatement. Overall
accounting quality: 4/10.
