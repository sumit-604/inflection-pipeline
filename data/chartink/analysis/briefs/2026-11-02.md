## The picture in one paragraph

The market swung hard both ways across two sessions. On Friday 285 stocks fell four and a half percent or more, and only 47 rose that hard. On Monday 242 rose that hard and 61 fell. The short trend count dropped from 614 to 491 on Friday, then jumped to 712 on Monday. That is the best reading since the October repair. The long count held well above the 23 October low and closed at 678, about three stocks in ten. The Nifty rose about 1.7 percent, led by oil and gas, metals and infrastructure. Reliance alone rose more than eleven percent. The money tells the same story it told last week. Friday's selling carried about 609 crore of turnover. Monday's buying carried about 494. The heavy selling days of 23 and 30 October each carried more money than any buying day since mid October. The large names hold their long trends far better than the broad market. The bounce survived its first real test, but buyers have still not shown up in size.

## Where the market stands

Picture the market as about 2,230 stocks. Each stock has three trend lines. The short line covers 20 days, the middle line 50 days and the long line 200 days. A stock above a line is in an uptrend on that clock. On Monday 712 stocks sat above the short line, a little under one in three. 528 sat above the middle line, about one in four. 678 sat above the long line, about three in ten. So seven stocks in ten still sit in long downtrends.

The year gives these counts their scale. The spring bottom came on 23 March, when the long count fell to 287. The climb out began on 1 April and ran for four months. The long count peaked at 1,363 on 7 August. Autumn has been a staircase down from that peak. In late September the long count sat near 1,000. A repair in mid October lifted it to 938. Then the floor broke, and on Friday 23 October the long count fell to 579. Since then the market has risen in five sessions out of six. Monday's 678 is about 100 stocks above that low. It is about half the August peak.

Friday was the test the last brief named. It asked what a day of 300 hard fallers would look like. Friday came close, with 285. The short count fell by about 120 stocks in one session. New yearly lows jumped from 66 to 156. Turnover rose to about 609 crore, almost exactly the 611 of the 23 October selling day. That is how a failed bounce starts. The October repair broke the same way, with a heavy selling day after a quiet climb. This time the next session reversed it. Monday erased all of Friday's damage on the short clock and more. The long count dipped only to 628 on Friday and never came near the October low.

Monday was one of the widest up days of the autumn. 242 stocks rose four and a half percent or more. Only 27 October had more, with 272. About one stock in six closed up four percent or more. About one stock in three traded four percent above Friday's close at some point in the day. A little over four in ten of those held the gain into the close. Only about one stock in five stayed inside a three percent range all day. Most of the market moved, and most of it moved up.

Volume is still the weak part of the story. The sheet compares the number of stocks trading on heavy volume with the number on light volume. On Monday there was about one heavy stock for every two light ones. Turnover came to about 494 crore. That is more than any of the four up days last week. It is still well under the 609 of Friday's selling. The pattern now has two clear examples. The two heavy selling days, 23 October and 30 October, each carried about 610 crore. No buying day since mid October has carried that much. Sellers come in size when they come. Buyers come wide but light.

The large names hold up far better than the broad market. The daily record tracks the share of Nifty 500 stocks above their 200 day line. On Monday it stood at about six in ten. Across the whole market it is three in ten. The 500 biggest companies are mostly in long uptrends. The smaller ones mostly are not. The index move matched this. The Nifty rose about 1.7 percent. The damage at the far end has healed further. ## What would change this read

The read is a wide bounce on top of a falling staircase. It passed one hard test on Friday, but it still runs on light money while selling runs on heavy money. It turns worse if a heavy selling day repeats and the next session fails to answer it. A day of 300 or more hard fallers would be the first sign. A long count back under about 580, the 23 October low, would erase the bounce. New yearly lows back above about 300 would say the same. A VIX close above about 18 would say fear has returned. A short count back under about 450 on rising turnover would repeat the way the October repair broke.

The cautious read fails if buying turns heavy and keeps going. The first test is a wide up day on turnover above about 610 crore, the level of both recent selling days. Monday reached about 494. The second test is the short count above about 800, where the October repair stalled. Monday reached 712. The third test is the long count above about 840. Monday reached 678. The fourth test is new highs past about 50 while new lows stay under 100. Monday had 39 highs and 91 lows. A VIX close under 15 would support the turn. Two or more of these passing in the same week would change the read from bounce to repair.
