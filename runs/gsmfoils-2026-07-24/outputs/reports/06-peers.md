## PART 1: CLAIM-BY-CLAIM VERIFICATION

### Claim 1 — Indian pharma packaging / blister-foil market genuinely tracking toward doubling over five years
**Suggested peers:** pharma-packaging/foil converters, Uflex, Huhtamaki, Ester
**Verdict: PARTIALLY VERIFIED**

Peer evidence:
- CGRAPHICS/Wahren (the only true segment peer) confirms a large and structurally growing Alu-Alu/blister TAM: "Alu-Alu market... is close to 5000 tons a month... also another Blister market, which is maybe the double of this, and also PVC, PVDC market is 3000 to 4000 tons per month" (CGRAPHICS, May 2026 call, Deepanshu Goel). Wahren's own trajectory — group sales from ~₹40cr a few years ago to ₹175cr in H1 alone (CGRAPHICS Nov 2025 call, Pulkit Agrawal), guiding to ₹1,000cr — is consistent with a market expanding fast enough to absorb aggressive capacity adds from at least two converters (Wahren and GSM) simultaneously, without either claiming to be starving for orders on the demand side.
- Wahren repeatedly frames demand as never the constraint: "there has actually not been an open order, only open inquiries... continuous demand" (CGRAPHICS Nov 2025) and, even during the March 2026 supply crisis, "demand is not the issue, supply is the issue" (CGRAPHICS May 2026, Pulkit Agrawal) — directionally matching GSM's own demand narrative.
- ESTER and HUHTAMAKI do not address pharma packaging; their commentary (BOPET films growing "9% to 13%" over decades per ESTER, flexible packaging industry "11%, 12%" per a HUHTAMAKI analyst) is about adjacent FMCG/flexible packaging, not blister-foil TAM, and cannot be used to size the pharma segment.

Peers silent: Uflex (transcript not supplied — gap, not silence); TCPLPACK does not discuss pharma packaging TAM.

Net read: The one true segment peer corroborates a large, fast-growing pharma-packaging/foil TAM able to support simultaneous aggressive expansion by multiple converters, which is directionally consistent with GSM's doubling thesis, but no peer independently quantifies a market-wide five-year doubling CAGR — the growth evidence available is company-level (Wahren's own revenue trajectory), not TAM-level.

### Claim 2b — March 2026 aluminium/petrochemical price spike tied to the Iran-Israel-US conflict
**Verdict: VERIFIED**

Peer evidence:
- CGRAPHICS/Wahren dates and quantifies the same spike GSM describes: "H2 was a very volatile period for all of us. There were tantrums from America and there were also war-related supply chain issues... Aluminium is 50% of the raw material for our pharma packaging business, and we were significantly impacted... around 5 crores of impact in our gross margins... March month was especially impacted... and that has continued in April and May also" (CGRAPHICS, May 2026 call, Pulkit Agrawal). This is an independent, dated, magnitude-quantified confirmation of the same event GSM's management describes in its own Apr 2026 call ("the entire commodity has gotten a very bad hit," "aluminium LME and even the MCX is almost on an all-time high... other products like all the chemicals and all the petrochemicals... have become extremely expensive," GSMFOILS Apr 2026 call, Sagar Bhanushali).
- TCPLPACK independently corroborates the timing and channel of the same conflict on its own business: "FY26 was a challenging year... marked by... geopolitical disruptions in the Middle East during Q4" and "shipments were affected by disruptions in the Middle East, particularly in Q4 when shipments were affected" (TCPLPACK, Jun 2026 call, Akshay Kanoria), and later: "we are still going through the Middle East crisis. But, of course, post the ceasefire, things have improved with some more vessels sailing" (same call) — confirming the same Q4 FY26 (Jan-Mar 2026) window.
- ESTER corroborates the broader input-cost/currency channel in the same window: "The ongoing conflict in West Asia has kept crude oil prices elevated and volatile, contributing to a firmer raw material cost environment during the quarter" (ESTER, May 2026 call, Vaibhav Jha), and separately confirms the currency-depreciation leg (₹/USD and ₹/EUR both moved sharply against Ester in the same period).
- HUHTAMAKI confirms the same window's raw-material spike from the buyer side: "we see prices going up significantly... started like end of March... probably low to medium double-digit raw material impact... but... we've been very successful... where we passed on most of these costs to our customers" (HUHTAMAKI, May 2026 call, Kamal Taneja).

Net read: Three independent peers (CGRAPHICS, TCPLPACK, ESTER, with HUHTAMAKI as a fourth) place a raw-material and logistics cost spike in the same March-April 2026 window and tie it to the same West Asia conflict. This is the strongest, most broadly corroborated claim in this stage.

### Claim 4 — 90-150 day payment terms from large pharma as an industry constraint
**Suggested peers:** packaging suppliers to large-cap pharma
**Verdict: PARTIALLY VERIFIED**

Peer evidence:
- CGRAPHICS/Wahren, which supplies the same category of large pharma names GSM aspires to (Aurobindo, Torrent, Cadila, Zydus, Intas), confirms the 90-day floor independently in two separate quarters: "The debtor days in most of the pharma space is 90 days" (CGRAPHICS, Nov 2025 call, Pulkit Agrawal) and "in the pharma industry, it seems 90 days is the norm" (CGRAPHICS, Mar 2026 call, Pulkit Agrawal). This matches the lower bound of GSM's own claimed 90-120-150 day range (GSMFOILS Feb 2026 call: "the payment that they give now these days is easily between 90 to 120 days and even 150 days... certain few companies like Sun Pharma, Cipla and all. They are very sharp with their payment terms," Sagar Bhanushali).
- CGRAPHICS' own working-capital response mirrors GSM's: rising debtor days as it moves up-market ("as we continue to grow... you will continue to see a larger increase in working capital... The debtor days in most of the pharma space is 90 days," Nov 2025 call), pursuit of bill discounting and debtor insurance as mitigants, and negative operating cash flow tolerated as a cost of chasing large-pharma customers ("we expect the cash flows from operations to be negative for a while," Nov 2025 call).

Peers silent: ESTER, HUHTAMAKI, and TCPLPACK do not supply primary packaging to large-cap pharma manufacturers and do not discuss pharma-specific payment terms.

Net read: One independent peer, repeated across two separate quarterly calls and drawn from the same large-pharma customer base GSM is targeting, corroborates the 90-day floor of GSM's claim. The specific 120-150 day tail (attributed by GSM specifically to Sun Pharma/Cipla) is not independently named by CGRAPHICS, so the claim is corroborated in direction and at the lower end of magnitude, not fully in range — hence PARTIALLY VERIFIED rather than VERIFIED under the two-peer rule.

### Cross-reading claim A — EBITDA margin ~11.5% sustainable amid RM inflation
**Verdict: PARTIALLY VERIFIED**

Peer evidence:
- CGRAPHICS/Wahren discloses a directly comparable long-term margin target for the same product category: "In the long term, we expect our EBITDA margins in the ALU-ALU value business to be low teens 11, 12%, maybe 13%" (CGRAPHICS, Mar 2026 Arihant call, Pulkit Agrawal), later refined to "mid-teens 14% kind of EBITDA" for the broader Wahren business at scale (CGRAPHICS, May 2026 call, Pulkit Agrawal) — a band that brackets GSM's own 11.5% figure and lends it plausibility as a realistic segment margin.
- However, CGRAPHICS/Wahren's own realised results in the identical March 2026 window undercut the "sustainable" half of the claim: consolidated EBITDA fell to roughly 10.5% with PAT margin near 5.2% in the same quarter GSM reported its 120bp EBITDA compression (CGRAPHICS, May 2026 call, caller Lokesh citing "350 crore business and 10.5 EBITDA... just 5.2 PAT margin"), and management itself would not commit to a margin floor beyond "should be, should be, yes" (CGRAPHICS, Nov 2025 call) and later "we are still not out of the woods yet" on margin normalisation (CGRAPHICS, May 2026 call).

Net read: One peer (the only true segment comparable) confirms 11-13% is a realistic long-run target band for Alu-Alu/pharma packaging, which supports GSM's 11.5% as plausible, but that same peer's actual results compressed below its own target in the identical RM-inflation window GSM is describing — meaning the "sustainable" claim is not confirmed, only the magnitude is. This is a genuine complication, not a clean pass.

### Cross-reading claim B — Receivables/WC stretch being industry-normal for pharma packaging
**Verdict: PARTIALLY VERIFIED** (see Claim 4 for full peer evidence)

CGRAPHICS/Wahren, across three separate quarterly calls, independently and consistently confirms that receivables stretch, negative operating cash flow, and reliance on bill discounting/debtor insurance/new banking lines are structural features of chasing large-pharma customers, not GSM-specific mismanagement: 90-day debtor norm (Nov 2025, Mar 2026 calls), negative CFO tolerated as a growth cost (Nov 2025 call), LC ticket sizes and debtor balances rising mechanically with per-kg raw material inflation (May 2026 call: "The normal LC that we used to open for 60 lakhs earlier is now a 90 lakh rupees LC automatically. ## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

### 2A Demand environment
Domestic demand across the peer set is broadly described as resilient-to-strong through the period, with export demand as the swing factor hit by tariffs and, later, the West Asia conflict. CGRAPHICS/Wahren never once cites weak pharma-packaging demand as a constraint — every quarter frames the problem as supply/logistics, not orders. TCPLPACK confirms improving domestic demand (low-double-digit domestic volume growth by Q3 FY26) with export weakness concentrated in the same Q4 FY26 window as the conflict. ESTER's BOPET domestic demand strengthens through the year on PWMR/GST-driven substrate switching, while its US-tariff-hit export/value-added volumes recover only in Q4 FY26 alongside the trade-deal resolution. HUHTAMAKI is the one outlier — flat-to-declining volumes throughout, but management insists this is a deliberate mix/customer-selectivity choice ("where to play"), not a demand problem, and repeatedly declines to confirm the ~11-12% industry growth rate an analyst cites for the broader flexible packaging market. Net: the peer set's demand narrative is broadly consistent with GSM's own framing (demand intact, disruption on the supply/logistics side), with Huhtamaki as a partial, self-declared exception.

### 2B Pricing and input costs
Every peer describes some form of raw-material pass-through as core to its business model (ESTER: "raw material is a pass-through... margins depend on demand and supply," CGRAPHICS/Wahren: monthly Hindalco-letter pass-through, HUHTAMAKI: "we passed on most of these costs to our customers," TCPLPACK: "whatever we can pass through immediately, we are passing through"). But nearly every peer also flags that sharp, sudden spikes (as opposed to gradual moves) cause temporary absorption/lag even in nominally full-pass-through models: CGRAPHICS cites a one-month carryover lag on orders already booked; TCPLPACK notes "when there is a steady drip-by-drip increase... it is harder to pass through" versus a single large jump; ESTER's reported EBITDA repeatedly diverges from its "adjusted" (ex-MTM, ex-one-off) EBITDA by several hundred basis points in volatile quarters. This nuance directly supports GSM's own Q4 FY26 EBITDA miss (11.5% vs 12.7% prior quarter) as a pattern consistent with what peers describe as normal short-term friction during a sharp spike, rather than a company-specific failure of pass-through.

### 2C Capex cycle — a segment-specific answer, not a single sector-wide pattern
The four peers split cleanly by sub-segment. CGRAPHICS/Wahren (pharma-packaging/Alu-Alu, the true GSM analog) is an aggressive expander racing capacity: Bangalore and Oman flexo plants, NCLT-acquired PVDC/tandem lines, and an 8,000→20,000 ton Alu-Alu expansion, all layered within roughly 18 months. TCPLPACK is a steady, calibrated expander (~₹100-150cr/year, Chennai greenfield, gravure backward integration, a fourth flexible-packaging line) but explicitly not chasing a capacity race ("we are being a little calibrated in our capex this year considering all the headwinds," Jun 2026 call). ESTER's film segment capex is largely sustaining (~₹40-70cr/year); its one large-ticket item (the ~₹1,600cr ELITe chemical-recycling JV) is a different technology bet, not commodity-film capacity. HUHTAMAKI is explicitly not expanding — "no significant capex," deliberately shrinking its addressable volume by exiting low-margin business. This is a lone-expander pattern concentrated specifically in pharma-packaging/Alu-Alu (GSM and CGRAPHICS/Wahren both expanding fast simultaneously), not an industry-wide capacity race across flexible/film/carton packaging generally — which sharpens, rather than dilutes, GSM's own low-cost-entrant-wave concern (Claim 5b): the capacity race is real, but it is concentrated in GSM's own sub-segment, where at least one other well-funded new entrant (Wahren) is scaling in parallel.

### 2D Competitive mentions
No peer among the 12 transcripts mentions GSM Foils by name or by an unmistakable description (no reference to a Vasai- or Ahmedabad-based Tier 2 Alu-Alu/blister foil converter). CGRAPHICS/Wahren names its own competitors (Svam Toyal, ACG, ACG Bilcare, "I Get" Packaging, Pac Time) — none of which match GSM's stated peer set. This is a genuine absence, not evidence either way.

### 2E Risks peers discuss that GSM does not
- **Statutory-auditor qualification / MSME and gratuity provisioning gaps**: CGRAPHICS disclosed unresolved limited-review observations on gratuity actuarial provisioning, MSME dues, and a machine pending capitalisation where "our statutory auditor has taken a different view" from the internal auditor (CGRAPHICS, Nov 2025 call) — a governance/accounting-control risk category GSM's own concalls do not raise.
- **Explicit negative operating cash flow as a standing, multi-quarter condition**: CGRAPHICS repeatedly and explicitly flags negative CFO as an ongoing state ("we expect the cash flows from operations to be negative for a while," Nov 2025 call) requiring debtor insurance, a second banker (Citibank), and active bill-discounting scale-up — a more forceful and sustained framing of the same working-capital risk GSM alludes to but has not framed with comparable explicitness or financing-line detail.
- **Foreign-currency debt / MTM volatility**: ESTER's results are repeatedly and materially distorted by mark-to-market losses on EUR/USD-denominated term loans (tens of crores per quarter) — a risk category not present in GSM's disclosures (no FX-denominated debt currently), but relevant if GSM ever raises foreign-currency capex debt for its Ahmedabad or future expansions.
- **Trade-remedy/import-dumping dependency**: Both ESTER (Chinese BOPET dumping, antidumping duty petition) and TCPLPACK (minimum import price on paperboard) flag structural dependence on government trade-remedy action to protect domestic pricing — a risk class (import competition into Indian pharma-packaging foils) that none of GSM's own concalls address, despite aluminium being a globally traded, tariff-sensitive commodity.

## PART 5: CROSS-PEER HYPOTHESIS

**Hypothesis:** The four peers are not experiencing one shared "packaging sector" cycle — they are bifurcating along a capex-aggression axis that maps almost exactly onto sub-segment structural growth rates, and GSM sits on the aggressive side of that split alongside its only true segment peer (CGRAPHICS/Wahren), which is testable: if pharma-packaging/Alu-Alu TAM growth is genuinely structural (PWMR-adjacent regulatory tailwinds, large-pharma outsourcing of primary packaging), the two aggressive expanders (GSM and Wahren) should both show utilisation catching up to new capacity within 4-6 quarters of commissioning; if the growth is instead being pulled forward by price-based new-entrant competition for a roughly fixed pool of large-pharma orders (as Wahren's own "lower price to enter" strategy suggests), utilisation ramp will lag guidance for both, and industry-wide pricing (the Hindalco-letter pass-through spread, not just the headline LME price) should compress across both GSM and Wahren simultaneously over the same window — a pattern no single peer's disclosures state outright, since each frames its own capex only in terms of its own demand pipeline, not the combined capacity being added by named and unnamed Tier 2 entrants system-wide. Huhtamaki's and TCPLPACK's capital discipline in slower-growth adjacent segments, by contrast, is the control group: their utilisation and margin outcomes should NOT show the same compression pattern, because they are not part of the same capacity race. This is a specific, testable near-term marker (utilisation ramp-through-rate at GSM's Ahmedabad plant and Wahren's Alu-Alu/PVDC lines over the next 2-3 quarters) that the pipeline should watch for at the next FTTCP or quarterly-review pass.
