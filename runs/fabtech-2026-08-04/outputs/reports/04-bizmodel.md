**Sector-tag correction (flagged upfront):** the manifest provisionally tagged FABTECH "Pharma / CDMO — verify." That tag is wrong. FABTECH does not manufacture or contract-manufacture drugs. It designs, procures, installs and commissions the cleanroom, HVAC, water-purification and process-equipment infrastructure that pharma/biotech/healthcare clients use to manufacture drugs. It is a milestone-billed turnkey EPC / process-engineering contractor whose end-customers happen to be pharma companies — the correct comparison set is process-equipment and EPC/engineering peers (Anup Engineering, HLE Glascoat, Praj Industries, Ion Exchange), not CDMO/pharma manufacturers. ## SECTION 1: THE BUSINESS MODEL IN PLAIN ENGLISH

### 1A. One-line description

FABTECH is a turnkey engineering contractor: it designs, procures equipment for, installs and commissions the cleanroom, air-handling (HVAC), water-purification and process-machinery systems that pharma, biotech and healthcare facilities need — mostly for clients in the Middle East, Africa and other emerging markets — and gets paid in milestone instalments as the project (typically 12-36 months) moves from design to commissioning (Inv. Pres., "The business at a glance"; RHP p.217).

### 1B. Money flow chain, by revenue stream

**Stream 1 — Turnkey (start-to-finish) services, 75.51% of FY25 revenue from operations (RHP p.217):**
[Client capex budget for a new/expanded pharma or healthcare facility] → [FABTECH does feasibility study, design/engineering, then sources equipment for bio-clean-air, clean-water and process systems from Related Entities/third-party vendors, manages quality checks, ships, installs via third-party contractors, and commissions the facility] → [client receives a validated, GMP-compliant, ready-to-operate facility] → [pharma/biotech/healthcare company or government health authority pays] → [milestone-based payments tied to the project cycle: proforma invoice at order finalisation, progress payments through design/procurement/installation, final payment on completion certificate] (RHP p.228-230, p.377).

**Stream 2 — Standalone/equipment-supply services, 24.49% of FY25 revenue (RHP p.217):**
[Client or a third-party EPC contractor needs specific equipment only — feasibility, design and other execution done by someone else] → [FABTECH supplies/procures the equipment and logistics management for that piece of scope] → [equipment installed at site, typically by others] → [client pays] → [invoiced against a standalone purchase order, shorter cycle than full turnkey] (RHP p.217).

### 1E. The chai-stall-uncle version

Imagine a contractor who builds hospital operation theatres, except instead of one hospital he does it for pharmaceutical factories, all over the Middle East and Africa. He doesn't own a factory that makes the air-conditioners, the water-purifying machines, or the pill-making equipment — he has a network of "family shops" (group companies and trusted vendors) that make these things, and he buys from them, adds his design and project-management know-how, hires local labour to install everything, and hands over a working, government-inspection-ready factory. He gets paid in instalments as he finishes each stage — some money up front, some when the equipment arrives, the rest when the client signs off that everything works. Because each project takes one to three years and the payment comes in dribs and drabs, his cash can look tight even in a year when he's actually making good money on paper — a lot of that profit is sitting as "the client owes me" rather than as cash in hand.

### 2D. Industry lifecycle stage and FABTECH's position

Growth stage, fragmented and competitive. Post-COVID localisation of pharmaceutical manufacturing in the GCC and Africa, biologics/specialty-pharma capex acceleration, healthcare-infrastructure investment and "China+1" supply-chain diversification are cited industry tailwinds (Inv. Pres., "Strategic direction"). Within this growing-but-crowded field, FABTECH is a small, still-scaling regional player (Rs 410.77 Cr FY26 consolidated revenue, AR p.10) — not an industry leader like Exyte or Azbil-Telstar, but growing faster than some peers and recently IPO'd (October 2025, raising ~Rs 230 Cr, AR p.5) to fund working capital and inorganic growth.

### 3D. Unit economics — the physics of the business

**Unit definition:** one executed project/order (blended average across turnkey and standalone scopes). FABTECH executed 136 orders in FY25 for Rs 32,666.85 lakh of revenue from operations (RHP p.219, 380) — an average of roughly Rs 240 lakh (~Rs 2.4 Cr) of revenue per order, though individual project sizes vary enormously (the top-5 projects alone averaged ~Rs 3,663 lakh each, RHP p.374).

| Element | FABTECH-specific answer |
|---|---|
| Revenue per unit | ~Rs 2.4 Cr average per order (FY25 blended, RHP p.219/380); wide dispersion — top-20 projects alone were 87.69% of FY25 revenue |
| Cost per unit | Dominated by procured-equipment cost (63.11% of total income industry-wide, RHP p.381), plus employee/design-engineering cost (10.22%), other execution costs (project erection & commissioning, freight, travel — 21.67%, RHP p.381-384) |
| Volume drivers | New order wins (bid conversion rate, currently ~10%), order-book replenishment, geographic expansion (Africa strategy) |
| Price drivers | Scope complexity/design content (higher-engineering-content scope carries structurally higher margin per Inv. Pres., "Why the margin matters"), competitive bidding intensity, project geography |
| Cost drivers | Equipment/vendor procurement cost (import- and forex-sensitive), labour/installation-contractor costs, logistics/freight (freight & forwarding expense rose from Rs 549.85 lakh in FY24 to Rs 1,314.61 lakh in FY25, RHP p.384), rework/variation-order incidence |
| Incremental margin / operating leverage | Emerging: Q1 FY27 employee cost fell 2.8% YoY even as revenue rose 10.3% YoY, and finance costs fell 35.9% YoY post-IPO deleveraging (Inv. Pres., "Read it year on year" / "Financial highlights") — company explicitly attributes this to "operating leverage emerging" |

## SECTION 4: RISKS, VALUATION APPROACH & MONITORING

### 4A. Business-model-specific risks

| Category | Risk | First financial line item that would deteriorate |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue model | Continuous need to re-win new orders in a low-conversion (~10%) competitive bidding market; a substantial part of business is "project-based and generally non-recurring" (RHP p.373) | Proposal-to-order conversion ratio and quarterly new-order-booking value (both disclosed in KPI tables) |
