## Identity note

The screen list describes this as an "infrastructure developer, classified under
Infrastructure Developers & Operators". The filings are narrower and more
useful. Technocraft Ventures builds **water and wastewater infrastructure for
government clients**: water supply schemes, sewerage networks, sewage and
wastewater treatment plants, transmission mains, reservoirs, trenchless and
micro-tunnelling works, plus roads, electrical transmission and urban
infrastructure, and operation and maintenance of public utilities (PROSPECTUS,
FY2027, Q2, page 525). It is not the same company as Technocraft Industries
(India) Ltd, NSE TIIL, BSE 532804.

## Business Understanding Narrative

**What does it do, and how does it make money?** It bids for government water
and sewerage contracts, builds them, and often operates them afterwards.
Revenue is recognised as work is executed. Its own words: "We derive almost all
of our revenue from contracts awarded by government entities" (PROSPECTUS,
FY2027, Q2, page 525).

**What is changing?** Margin, sharply and recently. Cost of revenue from
operations fell from 87.82% of total revenue in FY25 to 67.44% in FY26, while
changes in work-in-progress inventory swung from minus 10.58% to plus 6.89%
(PROSPECTUS, FY2027, Q2, page 512). Those two lines move together in a
percentage-of-completion business, so the apparent margin jump is partly an
accounting swing in how work in progress was carried. Read together, total
expenses fell from 86.46% to 83.18% of revenue. That is a genuine 328 basis
point improvement, not the 20 points the cost line alone suggests.

**Why now?** Profit grew 53.6% in FY26. Restated profit for the year went from
Rs 28.20 crore in FY25 to Rs 43.32 crore in FY26, on revenue up 23.4% from Rs
279.56 crore to Rs 345.00 crore (PROSPECTUS, FY2027, Q2, page 96). The company
listed in August 2026 on the back of that year.

**What must be true for the thesis to work?** Government must keep awarding, and
must keep paying. Both are outside the company's control and both are named in
its own risk factors.

**What breaks it?** The receivable. The company warns that "in projects
involving government or public sector undertakings, even timely execution may
not ensure prompt payments" and that such delays hit "our billing cycle, working
capital requirements, and liquidity" (DRHP, FY2027, Q2, page 53). For a
contractor, that is the whole risk in one sentence.

## 2. Business model and archetype

One engine: convert an awarded contract into billed work at a spread. Revenue is
the order book drawn down; margin is bid price less execution cost; cash is
whatever the government department releases.

Restated consolidated, Rs million (PROSPECTUS, FY2027, Q2, pages 96 and 512):

| | FY24 | FY25 | FY26 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue from operations | 2,261.02 | 2,795.64 | 3,449.96 |
| Total revenue | 2,272.98 | 2,810.04 | 3,469.98 |
| Cost of revenue | 1,907.99 | 2,467.84 | 2,340.16 |
| Change in WIP inventory | (68.04) | (297.34) | 239.20 |
| Finance costs | 79.29 | 92.39 | 115.02 |
| Total expenses | 2,015.20 | 2,429.59 | 2,886.42 |
| Restated PBT | 260.60 | 385.65 | 586.69 |
| **Restated profit for the year** | **190.54** | **282.04** | **433.15** |

Finance cost at 3.31% of revenue in FY26, up from 3.29% and 3.49%, is the
working-capital tell. A contractor paying over 3% of revenue in interest is
funding the government's payment cycle.

**Archetype: order-book business (EPC).** Order inflow, book-to-bill, execution
pace, working capital and margin on backlog are the binding variables. The
2026-09-21 run recorded MARINE as a name where the order book, the forward
variable for this archetype, was simply missing. **The same defect applies
here, for a different reason: the book exists but is fifteen months old.**

## 3. Competitive advantages

Absent, and the company effectively concedes it. There is no brand, no
technology claim, no exclusivity. Competitive position in government water EPC
is decided at the bid, by price and by pre-qualification.

The one durable asset is pre-qualification itself. A contractor who has
completed sewage treatment plants can bid for larger ones; a new entrant cannot.
That is a real but common barrier, shared with every established competitor.

Banking relationships are disclosed and reasonable: no-objection certificates
from Punjab National Bank, Kotak Mahindra, ICICI and HDFC, all dated June 2025
(DRHP, FY2027, Q2, page 68). Four lenders is adequate support for an EPC working
capital line and says the banks are comfortable.

**Moat absent.** Pre-qualification is a licence to compete, not a moat.

## 5. Financial trajectory

Growth is strong and consistent: revenue up 23.6% then 23.4%; profit up 48.0%
then 53.6%. Profit has compounded faster than revenue for three straight years,
which is the operating-leverage signature.

Cash conversion is the problem, and it is disclosed for the older years
(DRHP, FY2027, Q2, page 472), Rs million:

| | FY23 | FY24 | FY25 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Net cash from operating activities | 100.35 | **13.99** | 216.84 |
| Net cash from investing | 62.03 | 2.63 | (126.38) |
| Net cash from financing | (140.65) | (32.56) | (96.55) |
| Net change in cash | 21.72 | (15.94) | (6.09) |
| Closing cash | 27.16 | 11.22 | **5.13** |

FY24 converted Rs 190.54 million of profit into Rs 13.99 million of operating
cash, 7.3%. FY25 converted Rs 282.04 million into Rs 216.84 million, 76.9%. The
swing is enormous and it is the working-capital cycle of a government
contractor. Closing cash of Rs 5.13 million, Rs 51 lakh, at the end of FY25 on
revenue of Rs 280 crore is startlingly thin.

**FY26 cash flow statement: NOT FOUND** in what was read. The DRHP table stops
at FY25 because the DRHP was drafted on FY25 accounts. The Prospectus carries
FY26 profit but the FY26 cash flow was not located.

**Cash conversion is INDETERMINATE.** Under the framework's rule this caps the
verdict at PROCEED WITH CAVEATS, and the missing evidence is named: the FY26
cash flow statement and the receivable ageing.

**Inflection classification: earnings-led, with unverified cash.**

## 6. The transition and the quality ladder

FROM R1 COMMODITY PRICE-TAKER. TO R2 COST-ADVANTAGED CONVERTER.

A government EPC contractor wins on price at the bid, which is R1 by
definition: no pricing power, returns cyclical with the award cycle. The
observed improvement, total expenses falling 328 basis points as a share of
revenue across two years while revenue grew 47%, is a cost-position gain, which
is R2. Execution scale on repeat project types is the mechanism.

Management makes **no transition claim at all** in this corpus. There is no
quoted forward statement, no margin target and no growth band anywhere in the
three offer documents that were read. That absence is recorded rather than
filled.

Half a rung at most, inferred from the cost line, asserted by nobody.

## 8. Proof check

The historical proof is good. Three consecutive years of profit growing faster
than revenue, culminating in 53.6% profit growth in FY26, is exactly what the
framework's proof gate looks for, and the numbers are restated with no audit
qualification requiring effect (PROSPECTUS, FY2027, Q2, page 525).

**But the gate cannot be said to have fired**, for two reasons. First, there is
no post-listing period: the company listed in August 2026 and the index holds no
result. Second, the FY26 margin improvement runs partly through the
work-in-progress inventory line, which swung Rs 53.65 crore between FY25 and
FY26. In percentage-of-completion accounting a favourable WIP swing can flatter
a year, and without the FY26 cash flow statement there is no way to tell
execution margin from timing.

The named absence: **the FY26 cash flow statement**, and any order book dated
after June 2025.

## 9. Flags from the documents

- **Near-total government dependence.** "We derive almost all of our revenue
  from contracts awarded by government entities", with budgetary allocation the
  named swing factor.
- **Payment risk explicitly disclosed.** Timely execution does not ensure prompt
  payment.
- **Operating cash collapsed to 7.3% of profit in FY24** and closing cash was Rs
  51 lakh at FY25 year-end on Rs 280 crore of revenue.
- **FY26 cash flow NOT FOUND.** Cash conversion is INDETERMINATE.
- **Order book fifteen months stale.** The forward variable for this archetype
  is unobservable today.
- **Margin improvement partly a WIP inventory swing**, not clearly execution.
- **Lender covenants restrict growth.** The banks' terms bar "undertaking any
  new business, operations or projects or substantial expansion of any current
  business" and any pledge of shares without consent (DRHP, FY2027, Q2, page
  68). A contractor that must ask permission to expand has a real constraint.
- **Controlling promoter is a partnership firm** holding 57.10% post-issue.
- **Joint-venture execution** of Rs 383.86 crore sits outside the wholly owned
  book and its accounting treatment was not read.

No audit qualification and no litigation against the company appears in what was
read.

## 11. Posture: the Transition Decision Matrix

- **Proof gate: NOT FIRED.** No post-listing period, and the FY26 margin gain is
  not cleanly separable from a WIP timing swing.
- **Ugliness: STRUCTURAL, provisionally.** This is the judgement call on the
  card. Thin cash and a volatile operating-cash line are not a build-phase
  artifact for a government EPC contractor; they are the permanent condition of
  the business model. FY24's 7.3% cash conversion was not a commissioning year,
  it was an ordinary year. The classification is provisional only because the
  FY26 cash flow might show a durable improvement.
- **Recognition gap: partly CLOSED.** Market capitalisation of Rs 1,733.96 crore
  against FY26 restated profit of Rs 43.32 crore is 40.0 times trailing. The
  ladder puts R1 near 12x and R2 near 15-17x. At 40x the market is already
  paying well above the destination rung for a government contractor.

**Posture: VALUE-TRAP RISK, shading to AVOID.** Proof not fired plus structural
ugliness maps to DEEP WATCH or AVOID unless the structural classification is
disproven. The closed gap reinforces it.

## 12. Verdict card

**PASS for `/step1` now. Re-screen on the first two post-listing results.**

The growth record is genuinely good and the promoter facts are clean. The
objection is the combination that the framework is built to catch: a
government-dependent contractor, with cash conversion that has already gone to
7% in one recent year and is unverified in the most recent, at 40 times trailing
earnings, with its order book unobservable and its lenders contractually
restricting expansion.

Any one of those is workable. Together they are the profile the matrix calls a
value trap: clean optics, a narrated improvement, and a price that has already
paid for it.

Load-bearing facts a deep run must verify first, when it runs:
1. **The FY26 cash flow statement.** Whether FY25's 76.9% conversion or FY24's
   7.3% is the normal year decides this company.
2. **The current order book**, with inflow since June 2025 and book-to-bill.
3. **Receivable ageing by government client**, and retention money outstanding.
4. **Whether the FY26 margin gain survives** into the first two post-listing
   quarters, or reverses with the WIP swing.
5. **The joint-venture accounting** for the Rs 383.86 crore of work.

What would change the view to PROCEED. Two post-listing quarters holding the
FY26 expense ratio, with operating cash above 60% of profit and a refreshed
order book above Rs 700 crore. Then the R2 claim is real and only the multiple
is the argument.

No price. No position.

Derivation. Bull AI holds no management guidance records for this company, and
the three offer documents state no forward revenue, margin or profit number.
Tier A is unavailable. There is no post-listing quarter, so tier B is unavailable
too.

The tier C basis is the delivered rate with a mechanism. Profit compounded from
Rs 19.05 crore in FY24 to Rs 43.32 crore in FY26, a two-year compound rate of
50.8%. That rate is not carried forward. The estimate uses 25% to 35%, well
below the delivered rate, because the mechanism behind it is the order book, and
the order book is fifteen months stale. The June 2025 book of Rs 685.83 crore
plus Rs 383.86 crore of joint-venture work supports two to three years of
revenue at FY26 levels, which is what justifies any positive growth number at
all here.

Market capitalisation is the Bull AI company record at 22 September 2026, a
service record, not a filed document. As with METALIC, it reflects the
post-issue share count against a pre-issue earnings base, so the per-share
multiple is understated.

**The warning this step exists to carry.** This estimate is a trend extrapolated
from a stale order book, which is the weakest construction on any card in this
run. A government contractor's forward year is decided by award flow and payment
release, neither of which is observable in this corpus at any date after June
2025. Note also the framework's own caution on earnings that carry no cash: FY24
converted 7.3% of profit into operating cash. A forward PE built on accrual
profit that may not convert is precisely the screen result that misleads. Read
this step with steps 5, 8, 9 and 11, never instead of them.
