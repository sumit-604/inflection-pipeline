identified by Deloitte Haskins & Sells for the CONSOLIDATED audit only:

   2(iv)(i), 3.10, 20) — auditor's own procedures (design/operating

   judgment in the P&L: three separate recognition patterns coexist
   (straight-line, percentage-of-completion, right-to-invoice) and the
   choice between them is a management judgment call (p.168, line ~20971).
   🟡 This corroborates and elevates Pass 1's Note-2(iv)(i) "watch" flag
   (gross/net + judgment disclosure) from an analyst inference to an
   externally-validated audit risk area — worth a specific management
   question on the revenue mix across the three recognition methods, not

2. **Business combinations — BioPharm** (cross-refs Note 2(iv)(iv), 3.4,
   Note 7) — critically, the KAM text states the BioPharm **purchase price
   allocation "has been finalised during the year"** (p.168, line ~21024).
   This RESOLVES an ambiguity Pass 1 left open: only the Cake Kommunikations
   PPA is provisional (Note 7d); BioPharm's ₹6,429mn (₹642.9cr) goodwill and
   ₹2,158mn (₹215.8cr) intangible allocation are final, not subject to

   flagged uncertainty.
   No goodwill-impairment KAM was raised despite the tripling of the

## Auditor identity / appointment — CORRECTION to a Pass 1 statement
Pass 1 stated (Note 21A standalone) "Auditor: Deloitte Haskins & Sells
(unchanged)." **This is not fully accurate and is corrected here.** The
Consolidated Auditor's Report states: "The consolidated financial statements
of the Company for the year ended March 31, 2025, were audited by
**predecessor auditor** who expressed an unmodified opinion... on April 28,
2025" (p.169, line ~21093–21100). Cross-referenced against the Board's
Report (p.63, line ~6306–6316): "The Members at the 27th AGM held on 26 June
2025, appointed Deloitte Haskins & Sells... as the Statutory Auditors of the
Company, for a consecutive term of five years." 🟡 Watch — **FY26 is
Deloitte's FIRST year as the company's statutory auditor**, replacing a
predecessor firm (not named in the notes/report text extracted). CARO clause
(xviii) confirms "there has been no resignation of the statutory auditors of
the Company during the year" (p.135, line ~16443) — i.e., this was an
orderly non-reappointment/rotation at AGM, not a forced resignation — so not
itself a red flag, but it means: (a) the FY26 audit is a first-year
engagement occurring in the same year as three acquisitions, a new hedge
program, a TCPA litigation provision, and an escalated TP dispute — a
first-year auditor has less institutional history with the group's
judgment calls than a continuing auditor would; (b) Pass 1's "unchanged"
characterization should be read as incorrect and is superseded by this
finding.

## Board's Report / Secretarial Audit Annexure — TWO compliance-lapse items
(outside the Notes to Financial Statements proper, but material to
governance quality and directly touch items the notes reference — ESOP
share issuance, Note 15/15a — flagged per the task brief's instruction to
check auditor/governance disclosures Pass 1 under-weighted.)

1. **Form ADT-1 filing gap for the incoming auditor** (p.69–70, line
   ~7386–7405, repeated verbatim at ~7581–7601): the company "has not filed
   Form ADT-1 for the incumbent Auditor, Deloitte Haskins & Sells," because
   the ADT-1 filed for the ERSTWHILE auditor in January 2022 contained a
   wrong "period of accounts" end date (22 December 2025 instead of 31 March
   2025). The Board passed a resolution (21 Aug 2025) to seek cancellation
   of the erroneous filing via the MCA; company applied via Form GNL-1 (7
   Jan 2026); "as on the reporting date, the said Registrar is yet to take
   the necessary action." 🟡 Watch — an unresolved statutory-filing defect
   around the auditor appointment, self-reported and being actively
   remediated; low standalone severity but adds to a picture (see next item)
   of avoidable compliance friction this year.
2. **RSU share allotment before receipt of funds — Companies Act/FEMA/SEBI
   deviation** (same pages, line ~7360–7382): on 20 Feb 2026 the company
   allotted 1,966 equity shares to a non-resident employee under the RSU
   2020 plan, but due to a documentation delay the USD 43.42 exercise amount
   was credited to the company's account only on 11 March 2026 — AFTER the
   allotment date. The company itself characterises this as "an unintended
   deviation from the spirit of" Section 39 of the Companies Act, FEMA 1999,
   and applicable SEBI regulations (which require shares to be allotted only
   after receipt of funds), and has voluntarily sought adjudication/
   compounding/settlement with the relevant authorities (Board resolution 29
   April 2026). 🟡 Watch — trivial in quantum (1,966 shares) and clearly
   inadvertent/self-reported, but it is a genuine securities-law procedural
   lapse in the same ESOP/RSU mechanism Note 15/15a describes as the
   company's sole source of share issuance this year (893,392 shares from
   ESOP/RSU exercise) — a data point for governance-process quality, not a
   quantum concern.

## ★ MAJOR NEW FINDING — Standalone vs Consolidated profit trajectories
DIVERGE sharply, not discussed in Pass 1
Pulling the two P&L extracts together (Consolidated p.172; Standalone
p.137), a finding Pass 1 did not surface because it analysed each statement
set separately without a side-by-side profit comparison:

| Metric | Standalone FY26 | Standalone FY25 | YoY | Consolidated FY26 | Consolidated FY25 | YoY |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Profit for the year | ₹2,119mn (₹211.9cr) | ₹1,813mn (₹181.3cr) | **+16.9%** | ₹4,011mn (₹401.1cr) | ₹4,067mn (₹406.7cr) | **−1.4%** |

| Diluted EPS | ₹8.78 | ₹7.59 | +15.7% | ₹16.62 | ₹17.02 | −2.4% |

🔴/🟡 The India parent (standalone), whose revenue is 86.8% intercompany
service fees from Indegene Inc (Note 27 standalone, already flagged in Pass
1), grew profit nearly 17% and EPS nearly 16% — while the CONSOLIDATED
Group's profit and EPS both DECLINED. This means the entire earnings drag
Pass 1 catalogued (TCPA provision ₹203mn, D&A +57.6%, sub-contracting
+78.7%, contingent-consideration/hedge volatility) is arithmetically
concentrated in the international/acquired subsidiaries, NOT in the core
India delivery engine, whose underlying unit economics (as visible through
the intercompany pricing lens) actually improved this year. Investor
importance: this reframes Pass 1's finding #10 (EPS decline) — it is not a
broad-based earnings deterioration but an acquisition-year/US-litigation-
year phenomenon layered on top of a standalone base that grew profit
double-digits; stage 11 should model consolidated normalised earnings power
explicitly separating the two, and should treat the intercompany transfer
price (subject to the TP dispute) as the single largest lever connecting the
two profit trajectories — if the TP dispute outcome forces a repricing of
the intercompany fee, BOTH the standalone growth story and the consolidated
drag would need to be reassessed together, not independently.

## ★ Dividend history — RESOLVES a Pass 1 "NOT FOUND"
Pass 1 (finding under Note 42) stated: "NOT FOUND: whether this is the
company's first-ever dividend since listing (May 2024)." Both cash flow
statements answer this: **"Payment of dividend (480)" appears in the FY26
column of BOTH the Standalone (p.138, line ~16851) and Consolidated (p.173,
line ~21792) cash flow statements, with Nil in the FY25 column.** ₹480mn
(₹48cr) / ~239.6mn shares implies roughly ₹2.00/share was already paid
DURING FY26 (i.e., the FY25 final dividend, approved at the 27th AGM held 26
June 2025, per the Board's Report auditor-appointment paragraph on the same
page). This is corroborated by the Board's Report language on continuing
"reminders to shareholders to claim their outstanding dividend amounts" and
IEPF transfer procedures for unclaimed dividends (p.66, line ~6870–6878),
which presupposes an existing dividend payment history. 🟢 CONCLUSION: the
₹2.25/share final dividend proposed for FY26 (Note 42/Note 37 subsequent
event, already flagged in Pass 1) is **NOT the company's first dividend** —
it is at minimum the second annual payout, and the per-share amount appears
to be growing (~₹2.00 → ₹2.25). This is a positive data point for a capital-
discipline/shareholder-return narrative that Pass 1 left as an open
question.

## Note 26 — Segment reporting (Consolidated, p.195) — disclosure gap Pass 1
did not name explicitly
Management states directly: **"Assets and liabilities used in the Group's
business are not identified to any of the operating segments, as these are
used interchangeably between segments. Management believes that it is
currently not practicable to provide segment disclosures relating to total
assets and liabilities since a meaningful segregation of the available data
is onerous."** (p.195, line ~25309–25312). 🟡 Watch — this is a NAMED
disclosure limitation (Pass 1 flagged the "Others" segment's persistent
losses as a finding but did not flag that investors CANNOT independently
verify how much capital/goodwill is tied up in that loss-making segment
because segment balance sheets are not disclosed at all — including the
₹330mn/₹33cr of goodwill Note 6 allocates to the "Others" CGU). This
materially limits any segment-level ROIC or capital-efficiency calculation
for stage 4/11 — flag as a genuine disclosure constraint, not merely an
observation.
