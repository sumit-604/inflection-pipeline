## SECTION 3: COMPETITIVE INTELLIGENCE FROM WRITTEN SOURCES (adapted)

### 3A. What management says about competitors

Management never names a specific competitor. The entire competitive
narrative is a generic policy story: STQC/BIS-ER certification requirements
have (per the company) "permanently barred non-certified foreign brands"
and "eliminated the cost arbitrage long held by Chinese imports" (Inv. Pres.
slide 34). **Credibility check:** this is a real and externally verifiable
regulatory mechanism (BIS-ER/STQC rules are public policy, not a Prizor
invention), but the claim that it has *already* "permanently" and
"irreversibly" reshaped the competitive landscape in Prizor's favour is
asserted, not evidenced with market-share data, and is exactly the kind of
claim peer concalls should be used to check (see Section 4B).

### 3B. Industry and market intelligence

- Global video-surveillance market: "$35.9 billion in FY25... projected to
  grow at a 10.4% CAGR through FY30", volumes "1.11 billion to 1.60 billion
  units" over the period — no primary source cited (Inv. Pres. slide 20).
- Domestic surveillance systems market size chart: volume (Mn units) and
  revenue (INR Bn) series 2022-2029, revenue growing from ~28 to ~200 INR Bn
  — sourced generically, no named research house on this specific slide
  (Inv. Pres. slide 20).
- City-level CCTV density table (Hyderabad highest at 79.4 cameras/1,000
  people, Ahmedabad lowest listed at 2.3) — sourced to "Comparitech"
  (Inv. Pres. slide 21).
- Demand drivers cited: urbanization/smart cities, regulatory mandates
  (schools, banking, public safety), infrastructure build-out (25mn new
  homes by 2030, 80 new airports by 2025) (Inv. Pres. slides 19-21).
- No pricing-environment commentary, no raw-material/import-tariff
  commentary, no capacity-addition-by-industry commentary anywhere.
