# Market read, 28 September 2026

This brief comes from the 34 Chartink table files for the 28 September trade
date. All 34 of 34 were present, so every section below has its data. The full
figures sit in the data appendix, `2026-09-28-appendix.html`. Claude Code has no
web access. This brief checks no news and gives no reason for any move. It
describes what the market is doing. It is not advice to buy or sell anything.
The operator decides.

This brief reads Monday against the 25 September brief. It was written on
Tuesday, a day late, because the Monday run stopped before it wrote anything.
Monday is the first day of a new week. So the weekly index figures in the
appendix cover only Monday, and this brief does not use them as a week. The
last brief named four ways the constructive view would fail. One came true in
full on Monday. The count of stocks above the two-hundred-day line fell below
its washout-day level. A second test came true in half. The VIX rose above its
thirty-week average, but the count of stocks down twenty percent in five days
did not climb. None of the four ways the cautious view would fail came true.

## The picture in one paragraph

Monday undid the repair on the long line. Big fallers beat big risers 110 to 45,
the worst day since the 15 September washout, and about eight stocks in ten
closed down. The Nifty lost about one and a half percent. Only the fear gauge
rose. The count of stocks above the two-hundred-day line fell to 991, below the
washout-day level of about 1,035 and the lowest since June. The shorter lines
held above their washout lows, so this is a lower low on the slow measure and
not yet on the fast ones. It was not a second washout. Fewer than a third as
many stocks fell hard as on washout day. Small and micro indices still sit above
their trend lines, and healthcare still holds its long breadth. Fear moved from
cheap to normal in one session. The repair from the washout lost its best proof
on Monday.

## Where the market stands

Size still orders the market. The small end stays on top and the large end sits
at the bottom. The Nifty closed at about 22,780, down about one and a half
percent. It sits about five percent under its thirty-week trend line, one point
further than on Friday. The midcap 150 index fell by about the same amount. It
sits about two percent under its own line, the third close under it. The
smallcap 250 fell too, and it still sits about two percent above its line. The
smallcap 50 sits nearly four percent above. The microcap index sits about five
percent above. All the size indices lost ground. The small ones had more ground
to lose before they break.

The story of the last three weeks runs like this. On 7 September, 1,254 stocks
held their two-hundred-day line. Then the count slipped for a week. On 15
September, 345 stocks fell four and a half percent in one session. That was the
washout. The two-hundred-day count fell to 1,036. Buyers came back for five
sessions and lifted the count to 1,186 on 23 September. Thursday gave some of it
back. Friday gave a little back to buyers. Monday took the count to 991. The
whole repair is gone on that line, and 45 more names went with it.

Monday is the lowest two-hundred-day count since 11 June. In the spring the
count sat near this level for weeks, from late May into mid June. That was the
last time fewer than 1,000 stocks held the line. Today about 44 stocks in 100
sit above it and about 56 sit below. The long floor had held a little under half
of the market all month. It now holds well under half.

The short lines fell hard but held above their washout lows. About 575 stocks sit
above their twenty-day line, down from about 815 on Friday. On washout day that
count was 477. About 730 stocks sit above their fifty-day line, down from about
890. On washout day it was 705. So the fast lines made a lower high and then a
higher low. The slow line made a lower low. The two measures now disagree. The
fast lines say the washout low still stands. The slow line says it has broken.

Monday was a heavy day, and it was not a washout. In this record a washout means
300 or more stocks falling four and a half percent in one session. Monday had
110. That is the most since washout day. Before that, the last day with more was
8 July. Only 45 stocks rose that hard. About one stock in five traded four
percent under its prior close during the day. More than four in ten of those
closed near the low. On Friday only a quarter did. The selling stuck on Monday.

Volume did not rise to meet the selling. Total turnover stayed well below its
level of a year ago. Light-volume stocks outnumbered heavy-volume stocks by more
than two and a half to one. So Monday was a broad drop on ordinary volume. Sellers
did not rush for the door. Buyers stepped away. The coil that the last brief
described broke to the downside. About a third of the market traded in a band
under three percent, down from almost half on Friday. The share of stocks stuck
in a tight five-day range fell by about a third. The range ended, and it ended
down.

Fear woke up. The India VIX, the market's fear gauge, rose about twelve percent
to about 13.6. It was the only index that rose on Monday. On Friday it sat about
ten percent under its thirty-week average. On Monday it closed just above that
average. Fear is now normal, not cheap. Gold fell about three percent over the
month. The Nifty fell about six percent over the same month. Both fell, and gold
still fell less. The gap between them narrowed a little, to under three points.

## What is leading

Only four are large. The typical one still rose about two percent over the week.

**1. Sellers took the lead back in force.** Big fallers beat big risers 110 to

## What would change this read

The read tonight is cautious. The long-line repair failed and the short lines
held. The cautious read fails if the two-hundred-day count climbs back above its
washout level of about 1,035 within a few sessions and stays there. It fails if
the large-cap list above the day's average price climbs back above about 60 and
holds. It fails if the VIX falls back under its thirty-week average, which sits just under 13.6.
It also fails if a thrust day arrives, 300 or more stocks jumping four and a half
percent in one session, on volume above its year-ago level. That would turn
Monday into a shakeout inside a base.

The cautious read turns worse, and the last of the constructive case fails, if
the fifty-day count falls under about 705, its washout-day low. It fails if the
twenty-day count falls under about 475, its washout-day low. It fails if a second
washout arrives, 300 or more stocks falling four and a half percent in one
session. It also fails if the count of stocks down twenty percent in five days
climbs past about ten while the VIX stays above its thirty-week average. That
would show a real down tail forming, and the first half of that test fired on
Monday.
