### 2. Sector intelligence
The company turns vegetable-oil refining leftovers (acid oil and deodoriser distillate) into fatty acids, dimer acid, isostearic acid and tocopherols (MGMT, deck p5-p6).
These leftovers are about 1.25% and 0.25% of oil processed (MGMT, deck fulltext L98-104). So feedstock supply follows India's edible-oil refining volume. It cannot grow faster than refining.
India cut the import duty on crude soya oil from 16.5% to 11.0% (MGMT, deck R035 R036). More crude oil refined in India should mean more acid oil for Fairchem (MGMT claim; INFERENCE on size).
Selling prices track the cost of imported finished products (MGMT, deck R430). Management says lower imports helped prices this half (MGMT). A trade deal with the EU or UK could cut both ways. It may help exports, but it may also let cheaper imports in (INFERENCE, A3P-05).
End markets are paints and inks, soaps, animal feed, cosmetics, pharma and FMCG (MGMT, deck R347). Demand-cycle data for these markets was not in the run (not disclosed).

### 3. Business-model intelligence
Fairchem earns a spread: the price of its acids minus the cost of oil leftovers. Gross margin was 23.07% this quarter against 20.32% a year ago (FILED, derived).
Operating costs (staff, power, other) rose only 13.5% while sales rose 49.7% (FILED, derived). That operating leverage added about 4 points of margin (INFERENCE from FILED lines). It reverses if prices fall.
The company pays its suppliers at the factory gate on short terms (MGMT, deck fulltext L233-235). Supplier credit is about 8 days (FILED, derived). So when prices rise, the extra working capital lands on bank borrowings.
The business has one plant, one segment, and almost all sales are domestic (FILED, Note 3). It runs with almost no cash and funds itself on short-term bank lines (FILED: cash Rs 0.57 L; borrowings 68.65 Cr, all current).
Model drift this quarter: none in the product mix that the filing can show. The capital pattern is the change to watch. A debt-funded buyback (INFERENCE, A3-05) is now followed by a promoter land purchase (FILED). The deck says a capacity expansion is "completed with minimal capex" but gives no size (MGMT).
The deck promises "low volatility in the cash flow generation" (MGMT). Its own cash-flow history swings from minus 16.8 Cr to plus 41.6 Cr in two years (MGMT, deck p20).

### 4. Competition intelligence

A global cost leader should hold margin better than that. By management's own account it is a price-taker against imports.
