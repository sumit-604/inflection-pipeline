## BUSINESS UNDERSTANDING NARRATIVE

Balaji Telefilms makes video content. It has done so since 1994. The business
splits three ways and each way earns money differently.

The largest line is Commissioned Programs. Balaji produces television serials
to order for a broadcaster. The broadcaster commissions a show, Balaji
produces the episodes, and Balaji bills per hour of delivered content. FY26
revenue per hour was Rs 34 lakh. This is contract production. Balaji does not
own the show economics, it owns the production capability. Three shows ran in
FY26, Nagin 7, Kyunki Saas Bhi Kabhi Bahu Thi 2, and a capsule show. All three
were commissioned by one customer, Jiostar India Private Limited.

The second line is Films. Balaji Motion Pictures produces theatrical films,
then earns from box office, and from selling digital and satellite rights. The
economics are lumpy by construction. Film cost is capitalised into inventory
while the film is made, then released to the profit and loss statement when
the film releases. One release moves a whole year. Bhooth Bangla, starring
Akshay Kumar and directed by Priyadarshan, released on 18 April 2026 and took
Rs 21.60 cr on day one in India. That single film carried the Films segment
from Rs 1.37 cr of revenue in Q1 FY26 to Rs 185.16 cr in Q1 FY27.

The third line is Digital, business to consumer, run as the ALTT
subscription platform plus YouTube channels. It sells subscriptions and
advertising against original shows.

Demand for the television line comes from broadcasters filling prime time.
Management is explicit that this pool does not grow. Prime time runs 7pm to
11pm, there are four main broadcasters, and no channel gives one producer more
than one or two shows, so the ceiling is six to eight shows a year. Management
has said the television business operates in a Rs 250 to Rs 350 cr range and is
"getting stagnated". Demand for films comes from a record theatrical market,
Rs 13,395 cr of Indian box office in 2025 on the company's own industry slide.

Where does the competitive advantage sit. On the television line it sits on
production capability and a three-decade relationship, and it does not sit on
price. Management states realisation per hour is still 25% below pre-COVID
levels and says it will not return to those levels, so television will be
volume-led, not price-led. That is an explicit statement that the line has no
pricing power. On the films line there is no durable advantage, because each
film is a separate commercial bet. On the digital line the advantage was a
subscriber base, and that base was disrupted by a regulatory block. This
shallow pass did not run a moat scan, so no moat category is scored.

## TWO ITEMS THAT EXPLAIN THE SHAPE OF FY26

First, the regulatory block. On 25 July 2025 the Ministry of Information and
Broadcasting ordered 25 streaming apps blocked for obscene content. ALTT was
named. Reported at the time: ALTT had over 1 million subscriptions and about
Rs 20.3 cr of revenue in the year to March. The company told the exchange the
order would have no significant impact. Source: public reporting, 25-Jul-2025,
PENDING LIVE VERIFICATION against the company's own Reg 30 filing.

What the filings then show. Digital revenue fell from Rs 53.31 cr in FY25 to
Rs 38.58 cr in FY26, and from Rs 22.04 cr in Q1 FY26 to Rs 9.67 cr in Q1 FY27, a
fall of 56%. The segment lost Rs 16.10 cr in FY26 and was still losing in
Q1 FY27. The stated "no significant impact" is not what the segment numbers
show. This is a guidance-versus-delivery pair and it is the single most
important management-credibility item on this name.

Second, the idle money. Balaji raised Rs 130.68 cr by preferential issue in
FY25, on a shareholder notice dated 19-Dec-2024. The monitoring agency report
for the quarter ended 30-Jun-2026 shows Rs 32.30 cr of the Rs 65.00 cr movie
production object unutilised, and Rs 32.46 cr of the Rs 33.18 cr second object
unutilised. It records that NO proceeds were utilised during the quarter. So
about Rs 64.8 cr of a Rs 130.7 cr raise was still idle roughly eighteen months
after approval, in a year when operations burned Rs 72.8 cr. Sources: Outcome
of Board Meeting, FY2026, Q4, page 14, and Monitoring Agency Report, FY2027,
Q2, pages 6 and 8.

## UGLINESS VERDICT

Reading one, ARTIFACT-OF-CLIMB. FY26 was a trough caused by dated external
events. The ALTT block hit digital mid-year. The film slate was empty, so
Films booked Rs 15.33 cr while its costs stayed on the balance sheet as
inventory. Q1 FY27 then delivered Rs 240 cr of revenue and Rs 22.4 cr of profit,
which shows the machine works when a film releases.

Reading two, STRUCTURAL-FEATURE. Management itself says the television line is
stagnant, capped at six to eight shows, and that realisation will not recover
to pre-COVID levels. That is a permanent statement about pricing power, made by
the people running it. Revenue fell from Rs 625 cr in FY24 to Rs 211 cr in FY26.
The single customer is the buyer of all three shows. The digital platform's
distribution was removed by a regulator.

The one observation that separates them: whether Balaji prints positive
consolidated EBITDA in a quarter with NO theatrical release. Q2 FY27 and
Q3 FY27 will answer it. Every quarter without a release since FY24 has been a
loss.

Verdict: STRUCTURAL-FEATURE. The pricing-power statement is management's own,
it is about the largest revenue line, and it is not framed as temporary.

## THE BULL CASE, STATED AT THE SAME BAR

This is not a dead company and the bull case deserves its evidence tier.

- The film machine demonstrably works. One release produced Rs 185.16 cr of
  segment revenue and Rs 23.11 cr of segment profit before tax in a single
  quarter. The slate ahead carries Vvan with TVF and Sidharth Malhotra, and
  Hero ki Heroine directed by Sajid Khan.
- The balance sheet is not stressed. Borrowings are Rs 13.63 cr against
  Rs 127.63 cr of cash and current investments. The company can fund several
  more loss years without refinancing risk.
- Rs 64.8 cr of raised money is unspent and earmarked for film production and
  digital IP. That is dry powder, not just idle cash.
- The Rs 116.58 cr deferred tax asset shields a large slice of future taxable
  profit if profit arrives.
- Reliance holds about 21% and Jiostar is the sole television customer. The
  same group sits on both sides. Consolidation or a strategic transaction is a
  live possibility.
- The theatrical market set a record in 2025 at Rs 13,395 cr, with 37 films
  crossing Rs 100 cr against 22 in 2024.

Why this does not change the posture. Every item above is an option, not an
engine. The strategy buys businesses climbing a quality ladder before the
market reprices the climb. An option on hits has no rung. Framework rule
v3.8 Amendment 18 bars carrying an unresolved probability slice into an exit as
a probability, and the entire bull case here is one unresolved probability.
Strategic-transaction hope is not a thesis either.

## SECOND-ORDER CHAINS

1. Films cost sits in inventory until release. Inventories rose from
   Rs 134.78 cr to Rs 207.72 cr in FY26 while Films revenue was Rs 15.33 cr.
   [INFERENCE] A large slice of the balance sheet is unreleased film cost whose
   recoverable value is decided by future box office. If a slate film
   underperforms, the loss arrives as an inventory write-down against a book
   value of Rs 644 cr, and there is no earnings stream to absorb it.

2. The deferred tax asset rose from Rs 102.30 cr to Rs 116.58 cr during a year
   with a Rs 64.02 cr pre-tax loss. [INFERENCE] The company recognised more
   future tax benefit while its loss deepened. Recognising a deferred tax asset
   requires convincing evidence that taxable profit will be available. That
   judgement now supports 18% of net worth. Verify the recognition note and the
   auditor's treatment in the FY26 annual report.

3. All three television shows are commissioned by Jiostar, and Reliance holds
   about 21% of the equity. [INFERENCE] The largest customer and the largest
   non-promoter shareholder sit in the same group. That concentration cuts both
   ways. It supports order flow and it makes pricing a related conversation
   rather than a market one, which is consistent with realisation stuck 25%
   below pre-COVID.

4. Management stated the ALTT block would have no significant impact, and
   digital revenue then fell 56% year on year in the following first quarter.
   [INFERENCE] Forward statements from this management carry a demonstrated
   optimism bias on the digital line. Any later run must discount digital
   guidance specifically, and should grade management delivery on the Role 5
   trailing-four-quarter scale before any projection is built.

5. The company burned Rs 72.78 cr of operating cash in FY26 while Rs 64.8 cr of
   issue proceeds earmarked for film production sat unutilised. [INFERENCE]
   Either the film pipeline is slower than the raise assumed, or the company is
   preserving the earmarked cash to avoid touching general liquidity. Both
   readings mean the deployment story the raise was sold on has not happened.
   The separating observation is the next monitoring agency report.

## VERDICT

AVOID for the transition-alpha strategy. Do not run the pipeline on this name.

The reasoning is short. The strategy buys a business climbing from a lower
quality tier to a higher one, before the market pays for the climb. Balaji has
no named destination tier, no engine that moves it there, and management's own
words describe the largest line's pricing power as permanently lower. The one
profitable quarter in the last two years came from a single film. The trailing
twelve months, including that film, still lost Rs 21.3 cr.

This is a different instrument from what the strategy buys. It is a leveraged
option on Indian box office, wrapped around a stagnant contract-production
business and a regulatorily impaired streaming app, priced at 1.8 times a book
value that is 18% deferred tax and 32% unreleased film cost. Someone may make
money on it. It will not be made by the mechanism this framework hunts.

Reconsider only if the structural classification is disproven, and the test for
that is public and specific: two consecutive quarters of positive consolidated
EBITDA with no theatrical release in either.
