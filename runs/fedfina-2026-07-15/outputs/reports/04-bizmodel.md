## SECTION 1: THE BUSINESS MODEL IN PLAIN ENGLISH

### 1A. One-line description
Fedfina borrows money wholesale (from banks, bond markets and its own equity) and re-lends it, in small pieces, to self-employed Indians and small businesses against two kinds of collateral — gold jewellery and property — pocketing the difference between what it pays to borrow and what it charges to lend (AR p.3, "Measuring Our Progress"; Inv. Pres. slide 7, "About Fedfina").

### 1B. Money flow chain, by revenue stream

**Stream 1 — Gold loans (net interest income)**
[Input: wholesale borrowings — bank term loans, NCDs, CPs, ECBs, at ~7.8% cost] → [company appraises pledged household gold and disburses cash in 10-30 minutes at up to 75-85% of gold value depending on ticket size] → [delivers short-tenure (bullet/monthly-interest) credit against gold held in branch vaults] → [borrower (self-employed individual/housewife/trader needing quick working capital) pays] → [pays interest monthly or as a bullet at loan maturity, redeems gold on repayment; unredeemed gold is auctioned] (AR p.24-25; Inv. Pres. slide 14, slide 20-21).

**Stream 2 — Mortgage loans / LAP (net interest income)**
[Input: same wholesale borrowings] → [company underwrites income/cash-flow of an MSME or self-employed borrower and lends against a mortgage of self-occupied residential or commercial property] → [delivers a multi-year secured term loan, Small Ticket (avg ₹13.2 lakh) or Medium Ticket (avg ₹63.1 lakh)] → [borrower (trader, distributor, small manufacturer) pays] → [pays monthly EMIs over the loan tenure; property is the fallback if the borrower defaults] (AR p.22-23; Inv. Pres. slide 14).

**Stream 3 — Direct assignment / co-lending income (fee + gain on DA)**
[Input: originated loan assets sitting on Fedfina's book] → [company sells down a portion of the pool to banks/co-lenders under DA or Co-Lending Model (CLM) arrangements, retaining minimum mandated exposure and continuing to service the loan] → [delivers off-balance-sheet funding capacity and a servicing relationship] → [the buying bank pays] → [pays an upfront gain on assignment plus ongoing servicing/spread income] (AR "Income on direct assignment" note 26, p.207; Inv. Pres. slide 24, "Off-book AUF").

**Stream 4 — Fee and commission income (minor)**
[Input: loan origination and third-party product distribution] → [company cross-sells insurance/other financial products and charges processing/servicing fees] → [delivers distribution service] → [customer or partner institution pays] → [pays a one-time fee at origination or servicing] (AR note 27, p.207).

**Stream 5 (being wound down) — Unsecured business loans**
New disbursals discontinued from December 2024; portfolio is being run down / assigned off (100% of the Q1 FY26 Business Loan book of ₹770 Cr was assigned and derecognised). Not a going-forward revenue stream (AR p.2, "This version is old..."; Inv. Pres. slide 24-25).

### 1E. The chai-stall-uncle version
Imagine a corner shop that borrows money cheaply from a big trusted uncle (Federal Bank) and from banks, then lends it out in small amounts to the tailor, the small trader, and the housewife down the street — either against their gold jewellery locked in a safe, or against the paperwork of their little shop or house. The shop makes its money on the gap between what it pays the big lenders and what it charges the tailor. If gold prices go up, the shop can lend more against the same gold. If the tailor can't pay, the shop can sell the gold or the property to get its money back — that's why this business rarely loses its shirt, but it also means growth is capped by how much capital and cheap borrowing the shop can line up, not by demand (which is huge and largely untapped).

**Overall moat read: weak-to-moderate.** The strongest durable edge is parentage-linked funding access and licensing/compliance infrastructure; there is no pricing power, no network effect, and a demonstrated cost-of-funds/cost-to-income disadvantage versus larger peers.

### 2D. Industry lifecycle stage

Gold loans: secular growth phase — industry AUM roughly doubled from ~₹1 lakh Cr (Mar-24) to ~₹2.1 lakh Cr (Mar-25), driven by rising gold prices, RBI-driven LTV liberalisation for small tickets, and formal-vs-informal channel shift (AR p.51). Mortgage/LAP: mature-but-growing, MSME credit gap of ₹92-104 Trillion cited as the addressable opportunity (AR p.30, p.52; Inv. Pres. slide 13). Fedfina itself is mid-cycle in its own turnaround — FY25 was explicitly framed as a "year of rebuilding" after unsecured business-loan stress, with FY26 as "transition and rebuild" and gold-loan scale-up as the near-term growth engine (AR p.14-15).

| Driver | Direction | Impact on Fedfina |

## SECTION 3: FINANCIAL METRICS THAT MATTER FOR THIS BUSINESS MODEL

### 3A. Ignore-these-track-these

| Commonly tracked ratio | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Inventory turnover / days inventory | IRRELEVANT | No inventory — Fedfina's "product" is a loan, not a physical good |
| Gross block turnover / fixed asset turnover | IRRELEVANT | Fixed assets (branches, IT) are trivial relative to the loan book; capital intensity is regulatory (CRAR), not PP&E-based |
| Working capital days / cash conversion cycle | IRRELEVANT (in the manufacturing sense) | Replace with ALM gap analysis — the lending equivalent of WC management |
| Gross margin % | MISLEADING | An NBFC has no "cost of goods sold" line; the equivalent concept is Net Interest Margin / Spread |
| EV/EBITDA | IRRELEVANT | Interest expense is Fedfina's core operating cost, not a financing add-back item — EBITDA is meaningless for a lender |
| Revenue growth alone (without asset quality context) | MISLEADING | AUM growth funded by deteriorating underwriting (as happened in unsecured business loans) inflates revenue short-term while destroying value; must always be read alongside GNPA/credit cost |
| Debt/Equity as a leverage "red flag" | MISLEADING taken alone | High leverage is structural and necessary for an NBFC (it is the business model); the correct lens is CRAR relative to regulatory minimum and ALM matching, not D/E in isolation |

### 3D. Unit economics — the physics of the business

**Define one unit:** ₹1 lakh of average loan AUM outstanding for one year (blended across gold and mortgage, since the two products have materially different unit economics — shown separately below per the company's own disclosure).

| | Gold Loan | Medium Ticket LAP | Small Ticket LAP & HL |
|---|---|---|---|
| Average ticket size | ~₹2.3 lakh (Q1 FY27) | ~₹75.2 lakh (Q1 FY27) | ~₹15.2 lakh (Q1 FY27) |
| Origination/portfolio yield | ~17.8% (Q1 FY27, origination) | ~12.0% (Q1 FY27, origination) | ~15.1% (Q1 FY27, origination) |
| Origination LTV | ~69.1% | ~52.9% | ~53.4% |
| Underwriting basis | In-house gold valuation | Assessed/verified income | Assessed income |
(Inv. Pres. slide 14)

**Revenue per unit:** Interest income at the product yield above, minus cost of funds (blended ~7.8% Q1 FY27) = the spread. Company-wide blended yield 16.4%, cost of borrowings 7.7%, spread 8.6% (Q1 FY27) (Inv. Pres. slide 24).

**Cost per unit:** Cost of funds (largest component) + operating cost to originate/service/collect (branch, employee, tech) + credit cost (provisioning for expected loss). Operating expense ran at ~4.8-5.5% of average total assets (Inv. Pres. slide 35, RoA tree); credit cost 0.7-0.8% of average total assets in recent quarters, versus a 1.8% full-year spike in FY25 during the business-loan cleanup (AR p.3).

**Volume drivers:** branch/DSGL network expansion, gold price appreciation (mechanically increases eligible loan size per gram), MSME credit formalisation, co-lending/DA capacity from partner banks.

**Price drivers:** RBI LTV/regulatory caps (a ceiling on gold-loan pricing power), competitive intensity from banks and larger NBFC peers, cost of funds pass-through.

**Cost drivers:** cost of borrowings (rating-and-parentage-dependent), branch/employee cost (5,376 employees, 757 branches), credit losses (collateral quality and macro cycle dependent).

**Incremental margin / operating leverage:** Because branches are largely fixed-cost and gold-loan/DSGL AUM per branch has been rising steadily (₹13.0 Cr to ₹17.7 Cr per branch across five quarters, Inv. Pres. slide 20), incremental AUM on an existing branch network carries high incremental margin — this is the core operating-leverage story management is pursuing via co-located gold+MSME branches (AR p.7; Inv. Pres. slide 16). Cost-to-income falling from 58.2% to 52.8% over the trailing year (Inv. Pres. slide 31) is direct evidence of this leverage playing out.

### 4B. Valuation method applicability (formal handoff to Role 1 valuation stage)

| Method | Applicability | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| P/B (Price to Book), ROE-adjusted | **PRIMARY** | Standard for NBFC/lenders — book value is the tangible capital base against which the regulatory (CRAR) and earnings (RoE) engine operates; P/B should be benchmarked against sustainable RoE, not book value alone |
| P/E | **SECONDARY cross-check** | Useful once earnings normalise post the FY25 BL-driven PAT dip and ST-LAP rebuild stabilises; less reliable while credit cost/provisioning is still in a transition/rebuild phase (per company's own framing, AR p.15) |
| Sum-of-the-parts / AUM-multiple by segment | Tertiary, situational | Could be relevant if gold-loan AUM (now the dominant, faster-growing, higher-yield engine) and mortgage AUM warrant materially different multiples given diverging growth/risk profiles — worth testing as a cross-check once segment-level profitability (not just AUM) is disclosed |
| EV/EBITDA | NOT APPLICABLE | Interest expense is core operating cost for a lender, not a financing item to be added back |
| DCF on FCFF/FCFE (standard) | NOT APPLICABLE in standard form | Standard FCFF/FCFE framework built for operating companies does not map cleanly onto a balance-sheet-based lender where "capex" is loan growth funded by leverage; an excess-return/residual-income model (RoE vs. cost of equity, applied to book) is the appropriate DCF-family substitute if used at all |
| EV/Sales | NOT APPLICABLE | "Sales" for a lender is interest income, which is not comparable to a revenue multiple framework used for operating companies |
| Cycle stage that matters for valuation | The credit cycle (GNPA/credit-cost trajectory) and the rate cycle (spread trajectory) both matter more than the general economic cycle; Fedfina is currently mid-repair on the credit side (post-BL exit, ST-LAP rebuild) and benefiting on the rate side (falling cost of borrowings) — valuation should explicitly weight where in this dual cycle the current print sits |

### 4D. Highest-value questions for management

1. **Question:** With gold loans now over half of AUM and rising, what is the company's explicit ceiling or target mix between Gold and Mortgage, and why?
   - *Reassures:* A stated, disciplined target range with rationale tied to risk-adjusted RoA/RoE by segment.
   - *Worries:* No stated ceiling — implies growth is being chased opportunistically wherever origination is easiest (gold), with LAP/ST-LAP genuinely deprioritized rather than "rebuilt."

2. **Question:** What specifically went wrong in the Small Ticket LAP book that drove the FY25 PAT dip and provisioning build, and what concrete underwriting/collections changes (not just leadership changes) have been made?
   - *Reassures:* Specific, quantified underwriting-rule changes plus early-vintage performance data showing the new cohorts are performing better.
   - *Worries:* Vague answers pointing only to "new leadership" and "Salesforce migration" without segment-level Stage III trend evidence.

3. **Question:** How is the RBI co-lending framework (≤5% DLG cap, 10% minimum retention, 15-day transfer window) expected to affect off-book capacity and capital consumption over the next 4-6 quarters?
   - *Reassures:* A clear roadmap showing how the company will fund AUM growth if off-book capacity structurally shrinks (e.g., planned capital raise, retained-earnings buildup).
   - *Worries:* No clear plan, with CRAR already down 169bps YoY and growth accelerating.

4. **Question:** What is the sustainable, through-cycle credit cost assumption embedded in current pricing for each product, and how does it compare to the FY25 actual (1.8% of average assets, versus ~0.7-0.8% in the most recent quarters)?
   - *Reassures:* A credible, evidenced through-cycle number with clear attribution of the FY25 spike to a one-off (BL exit), not a persistent risk feature of the secured book.
   - *Worries:* Management cannot articulate a through-cycle number, or the recent low credit cost is itself unusually benign (masking building risk).

5. **Question:** Given Fedfina's cost-to-income and RoA still trail several listed peers (per the company's own 2023 RHP-disclosed CRISIL benchmarking), what specific operating-leverage milestones (cost-to-income target, AUM/branch target) is management targeting over the next 2-3 years?
   - *Reassures:* Specific, time-bound targets with visible quarterly progress already evident (cost-to-income has in fact improved from 58.2% to 52.8% over the last year).
   - *Worries:* No specific targets, or targets that assume continued gold-price tailwinds rather than genuine volume/productivity gains.

6. **Question:** How dependent is the current AUM/earnings growth on gold price appreciation versus tonnage and borrower-count growth, and what happens to AUM and NIM if gold prices are flat or fall for 2-3 quarters?
   - *Reassures:* Management can decompose AUM growth into price vs. volume and shows tonnage/branch productivity growing independent of price.
   - *Worries:* Growth narrative collapses into "AUM grew because gold price rose," with no independent volume evidence.

7. **Question:** What is management's plan to reduce the ~75% Top-5-state AUM concentration, and over what timeframe?
   - *Reassures:* A specific multi-year geographic diversification plan with early evidence of new-state AUM contribution.
   - *Worries:* No plan, or diversification framed purely in terms of branch count rather than AUM contribution from new geographies.
