# PLAIN-LANGUAGE BRIEF (mandatory standing deliverable)

## 1. SUMMARY NARRATIVE

Gem Aromatics is a first-time coverage name and this is an initiation read, not a position change. In the June 2026 quarter the two sets of accounts tell opposite stories. The parent company alone made a small profit of Rs 7.3 crore, up 11% on last year, on revenue of Rs 83.0 crore, up 8.6%. But the whole group, including its two subsidiaries, lost Rs 7.9 crore, against a Rs 8.0 crore profit a year ago. Two things went wrong, and they are different. First, the gross margin, the money left after raw materials, fell sharply: the parent's dropped from 25.0% to 17.7% and the group's from 29.5% to 16.7%, with group raw-material cost actually exceeding sales for the quarter. This matters because the parent carries none of the new Dahej plant's depreciation, yet still lost 7.3 percentage points of gross margin, so a big part of the problem is product mix, a Madagascar clove raw-material price spike, and a switch in inventory accounting to FIFO, none of which is fixed by simply running the new plant harder. Second, the new Dahej plant, commissioned in February 2026, now carries about Rs 9.1 crore of depreciation a quarter while its new products (cooling agents, Safranal, phenol derivatives) are still pre-revenue; this part is a genuine transitional cost that higher utilisation would absorb. Even the parent's headline profit growth is not real operating strength: strip out higher other income and lower interest cost and its core pre-tax profit fell about 9%. Management's deck and press release talk only about revenue growth and never mention the loss or the gross-margin collapse, a transparency concern. There is no cash-flow statement this quarter, so cash quality is INDETERMINATE. The verdict is PROCEED WITH CAVEATS. The maiden recommendation is WATCHLIST, not buy: the plant-absorption story is a real transition-alpha setup, but the gross-margin break is a separate, unresolved question, and the turn is entirely unquantified. What would change the call is a gross margin recovering toward the historical 24-30% band plus a specific Dahej utilisation number. The next hard checkpoint is the Q2 FY27 half-yearly cash flow and the first revenue from Safranal and cooling agents.

## 2. SECTOR INTELLIGENCE

Provenance note: no prior Notion sector work exists (first coverage); all figures below are from THIS quarter's filings, market sizes sourced by the company to a Frost & Sullivan 2025 study (deck pp14-17), which is a company-commissioned source, not independent. Gem operates in specialty ingredients, essential oils and aroma chemicals, selling into oral care, cosmetics, pharma, nutraceuticals and personal care. It is a natural-plus-synthetic play with raw-material cyclicality: this quarter the Clove business was hit by floods in Madagascar (a sourcing origin) that raised raw-material prices and cut sales, now said to be normalised (PR L114-116) — this is the leading candidate cause of the gross-margin hit and, if genuinely transitory, the most reversible part of it. Demand is described as seasonally softer in Q1. The structural tailwind the company is pursuing is forward integration from base oils (mint, clove, eucalyptus) into higher-value molecules (menthol, eugenol derivatives, cooling agents, phenol derivatives such as MEHQ, guaiacol, 4-MAP, and citral-chemistry molecules like Safranal). Stated India positioning (Frost & Sullivan, company-sourced): mint ~53-55% of global supply, eucalyptus oil ~58% India share, clove ~13%, phenol ~20-25%. A 15% concessional corporate tax rate "till perpetuity" under a government incentive is claimed for the new capacity (deck p22) but is uncited and needs verification. Regulation/payer mix is not a driver here; raw-material availability and pricing, plus customer qualification cycles, are the swing factors — and the gross-margin evidence this quarter shows raw-material cost is currently the dominant one.

## 3. BUSINESS-MODEL INTELLIGENCE

Provenance: all from this quarter's filings; no prior model work. Gem makes money by processing botanical and petrochemical feedstocks into essential oils, aroma chemicals and value-added derivatives across three facilities (Silvassa 1997, Budaun 2016, Dahej 2024) with a stated installed capacity of 16,171 MTPA (PR L212 — the only capacity figure disclosed; per-facility tonnage is absent, a silence flag). Revenue mix FY26 (deck): Mint ~65% (Rs 237 Cr), Clove ~23% (Rs 85 Cr), Citral/Other ~10% (Rs 36 Cr), Phenol ~1% (Rs 5 Cr). Geography FY26: India 56%, USA 28%, Brazil 6%, China 3%, RoW 7%. Named customers include Colgate-Palmolive, Dabur, Patanjali, SH Kelkar, Symrise (PR L208-209); dOTERRA is a strategic investor. Unit economics historically were sound and, importantly, STABLE at the gross line: group gross margin held 24.6-25.3% across FY23-FY26 (deck p27), so the Q1FY27 drop to 16.7% is a fresh break, not a long decline. Below the gross line, FY26 broke on profitability, EBITDA margin 11.1%, PAT Rs 1.4 Cr, and estimated ROCE ~3%, because the Rs 265 Cr Dahej plant was capitalised (PPE jumped Rs 43 Cr to Rs 243 Cr) with no incremental revenue yet. What this quarter says about model drift: the company has front-loaded a large fixed-cost, capital-intensive bet on advanced chemistry, so the model is now hostage to BOTH utilisation (fixed-cost absorption) AND gross-margin recovery (mix / raw material / FIFO) — two levers, not one. Metrics NOT disclosed that the model turns on: Dahej utilisation %, order book, segment profitability, per-facility capacity, gross margin by product line, and the debt/accruals funding split.

## 4. COMPETITION INTELLIGENCE

Provenance: this quarter's filings only; no prior peer work loaded, and no peer concall reported in the ±4-week window (stated in Section B). Gem's claimed edge is scale and backward-to-forward integration in niche naturals: "one of the largest processors of Clove Oil and Eugenol in India by volume (FY25)," largest eucalyptus-oil processor at ~58% India share, and entry into "limited-competition, high-margin molecules" via vapor-phase phenol chemistry (deck pp15-16). Where it wins: established mint and clove positions with real branded customers, three-decade operating history, and location advantages (Budaun mint belt, Dahej near phenol supply and JN Port). Where it is structurally weaker: it is a small-cap (Rs 914 Cr market cap) competing in molecule categories (cooling agents, Safranal, phenol derivatives) where global specialty players and larger Indian aroma-chemical companies (the deck names none as competitors) already operate; its new lines are unproven at commercial scale and pre-revenue, and this quarter shows it does not yet control its input costs (clove RM spike passed straight through to gross margin). The competitive risk to watch is execution AND margin quality, not positioning: if the Q3-Q4 FY27 Krystal ramp slips OR the higher-value molecules do not earn the "superior margins" the deck claims, the Dahej capacity is stranded while competitors with existing scale absorb the same demand, and Gem carries a ~Rs 15 Cr/quarter drag with an unrecovered gross margin in the meantime. Institutional ownership is thin (FII 0.8%, DII 4.8%), which per house rules is NOT treated as a risk, but does mean the ramp and the margin recovery will be scrutinised on delivery, not narrative.
