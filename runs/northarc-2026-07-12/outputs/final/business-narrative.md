Diversified retail NBFC strengthening after its IPO

Northern Arc Capital is a 16 year old diversified retail lender, RBI registered NBFC Middle Layer, rated ICRA AA- (Stable), that trades at Rs 325 for a market value of Rs 5,257 Cr. It lends two ways. It originates directly to borrowers through 432 branches and digital channels (D2C), and it partners with 368 originators and 57 digital platforms to place and co lend loans (Intermediate Retail). Alongside the loan book it runs NAIM, a capital light fund management arm with Rs 3,092 Cr of fund AUM, and Pragati Finserv, a rural subsidiary. Interest income drives 97% of segment revenue; fund management fees are the small, higher predictability remainder. The core exposures are MSME, non gold consumer finance, and rural microfinance.

The transition is a post listing rebuild. The company IPO'd in September 2024 and used the equity to strengthen the balance sheet: CRAR rose from 18.26% to 24.72%, managed gearing fell from 4.02x to 2.87x, and the liquidity coverage ratio sits at 154% against the 100% RBI minimum. AUM grew 22% in FY26. The D2C book, the more profitable channel, moved from 51.8% of AUM to 59% and management targets 65% to 70%. RoA improved from 2.4% to 2.8% and RoE from 10.0% to 11.1% on the pipeline's reconciled basis. Founder generation CEO Kshama Fernandes completed her board exit effective March 2026, external CEO Ashish Mehrotra has held the seat since March 2022, and IFC (Rs 382 Cr) and Eight Roads both entered as fresh institutional holders in FY25.

The backward looking score reads AVERAGE. Gate 0 lands at 42 of 160, with a core score of 32 of 100 and 3 confirmed moats. Four deal breakers fire: Block A below 8, Block B below 8, median ROCE below 10% at 9.62%, and cumulative CFO to PAT below 0.50. All four trace to NBFC structure rather than earnings deterioration. The ROCE proxy is distorted because Ind AS NBFC balance sheets carry no current liabilities line, and the negative operating cash flow is the mechanical result of loan disbursements being booked as an operating outflow while the book grows. Roughly 20 of 80 nominal points cover ratios that have no analogue for a balance sheet lender and score zero by convention. On the operator authorised adjusted basis that excludes those points, the score is exactly 40%, the AVERAGE threshold. The forward Emerging Moat scan reads MODEST at 22 of a possible score, three points short of the STRENGTHENING band, so the combined read stays AVERAGE.

Cash carries two separate signals that must not be blended. The first is benign. Operating cash flow has been negative in all eight years with comparable data and cumulative CFO to PAT is -5.02x, but that is the normal Ind AS artifact of a lender whose disbursements count as operating outflows as AUM compounds 22% a year; the ratio is improving, from -6.92x in FY24 to -3.79x in FY26. The second is genuine. Loan book asset quality deteriorated: GNPA doubled from 0.47% to 0.99% in the FY25 accounts and reached 1.2% by FY26, the P&L impairment charge rose 207% to Rs 378.53 Cr, Stage 3 coverage fell from roughly 82 to 84% down to about 68%, and the RBI forced the exclusion of FLDG credit enhancements from the ECL model, absorbing Rs 68.35 Cr of Q4 FY25 PBT under an Auditor's Emphasis of Matter. Consolidated PAT fell 5.2% as Pragati Finserv swung to a Rs 29.39 Cr loss with 85.7% net worth erosion, even as the standalone headline rose.

Management credibility grades B. In year FY26 guidance delivered cleanly across all three calls: AUM growth of 22%, RoA of 2.8%, opex ratio of 3.6%, and cost of funds of 8.5% all landed at or inside the guided bands. What softened was the forward path. The FY27 credit cost guide moved from 2.3% to 2.5% down to 2.7% to 3.0% and then 2.7% to 2.8% without ever reconciling to the original number. The RoA target compressed from an implied 3.7% to 3.8% down to "3 plus," and the late teens RoE goal slid from "2 to 3 years" to "8 to 10 quarters." A promised HFC NCLT resolution update, due within a stated 4 to 6 weeks, went unmentioned on the Q4 call. CEO remuneration rose 43% in a year when consolidated PAT fell, a 1:173 CEO to median ratio, and the company carries a live RBI KYC penalty from January 2026 plus recurring BSE listing fines.

Peers contradict four of the company's claims. Northern Arc says its cost of funds bottomed at 8.5% to 8.6% with no further reduction expected; UGROCAP reported a fifth consecutive quarterly improvement and guided to continue improving, and cost of funds fell across all four peers with no one else calling a floor. It framed the Q3 MSME credit cost jump as "industry wide"; FEDFINA, the closest comparator, called its own comparable stress "our issue, not necessarily an environment issue." It says AUM growth is "outpacing the industry"; CGCL grew 40% to 60% year on year against Northern Arc's 20% to 25%. It framed its placement and co lending fee franchise as a differentiated moat; UGROCAP called the same income structurally thin at a 1% margin and is actively steering away from it.

The market runway is genuinely MASSIVE and the management framing is inflated. The addressable Indian retail credit market across MSME, non gold consumer finance, and rural microfinance runs to Rs 795,600 Cr at the conservative cut and Rs 2,851,200 Cr at the realistic cut, growing about 17% a year, against a current SAM share of 0.39% and revenue headroom of roughly 254x. Balance sheet capacity is sufficient at 22.6% CRAR; funding mix access, not capital or market size, is the real constraint. But the company's own headline TAM claim reads 23.5x the served opportunity, and its rural growth case rests on a CRISIL projection dated June 2025 that independent MFIN and CareEdge data overtook within months, showing the microfinance portfolio contracting rather than growing toward the projected Rs 5 tn.
