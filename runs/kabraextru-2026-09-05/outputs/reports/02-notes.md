### 1. Contradictions between notes, and between notes and the primary statements

**NEW — Note 15 (Statement of Changes in Equity, "Other Equity"): the
dividend-paid per-share LABEL is stale/wrong in BOTH statements, and
resolves a gap Pass 1 flagged as unreconciled.** The SOCE dividend-paid row
reads, standalone: "Dividend paid - ₹3.50 per share (2024: ₹3.50 per share)
(874.32) (1,224.05)" (Note 15, PDF p.91); consolidated: "Dividend paid -
₹0.00 per share (2025: ₹3.50 per share) (874.32) (1,224.05)" (Note 15, PDF
p.145). Dividing the actual cash amounts by the 3,49,72,836 shares
outstanding: ₹1,224.05 Lakh ÷ shares = ₹3.50/share exactly (the FY25 row,
correctly labelled) but ₹874.32 Lakh ÷ shares = ₹2.50/share exactly (the
FY26 row) — NOT ₹3.50/share as the standalone label states. ₹2.50/share is
also exactly what Note 44 separately and correctly discloses as "recommended
for FY25" (Note 44, PDF p.112 standalone / p.167 consolidated: "₹0.00 (31
March 2025: ₹2.50)"). So Note 44's figure is right; the standalone Note 15
label is wrong (a stale "₹3.50" copied forward from the template's prior
year); the consolidated Note 15 label fixed the CURRENT-year framing (₹0.00,
correctly reflecting FY26's own recommendation) but left the COMPARATIVE
label at "₹3.50" when the actual FY25-comparative cash paid was ₹2.50/share.
This is a genuine, PDF-verifiable numeric/label inconsistency present in
BOTH statements, and it is the pattern-pass's best explanation for Pass 1's
finding #9 ("the two dividend figures... do not reconcile"): they DO
reconcile once the stale "3.50" label is set aside; the underlying cash
figure (₹874.32 Lakh) is consistent with ₹2.50/share throughout. (Note 15,
standalone PDF p.91 / consolidated PDF p.145; cross-checked against Note 44,
PDF p.112/167). 🔴 Red Flag — a fifth independent instance of the
"stale/unrevised disclosure" pattern (see Section 3 below), and unlike the
first four instances, this one appears in BOTH the standalone AND
consolidated statements simultaneously, which rules out "consolidated
copied from standalone" as the sole mechanism — the underlying defect is
template labels not being re-checked against the numbers they describe,
company-wide.

**Confirmed, not a contradiction: the "2026 preferential issue" referenced

### 3. Deliberately vague disclosures

The ₹16.68 Cr "Other" income (Note 23, Pass 2 finding #1) remains the
starkest example: every other line in the same note (Government Incentive,
Dividend received, Interest income, Profit on sale of investments) carries
either a footnote or is self-explanatory by label; "Other"/"Others" alone,
at 73x its prior-year size and larger in magnitude than the entire reported
standalone pre-tax loss, carries none. Re-reading the note a third time
confirms: no footnote reference mark, no cross-reference, no sub-schedule
exists anywhere in either statement. This is the single most consequential
disclosure gap found across all three passes, given its P&L materiality in
a loss-making year. 🔴 Red Flag, unchanged from Pass 2, reconfirmed here as
the top vagueness pattern.

The ₹31.77 Cr capital commitment (Note 41(b), Pass 1) and the FY26 lease
addition (Note 2E/40, Pass 2) share the same vagueness pattern: both are
large, quantified, and entirely un-narrated by segment or purpose. Read
together with the ₹16.68 Cr "Other" income, THREE separate large FY26
line items (one P&L, two balance-sheet/commitment) carry zero qualitative
disclosure despite each being individually large enough, in a small-cap
context, to warrant a sentence of explanation. 🟡 Watch, pattern-level.
