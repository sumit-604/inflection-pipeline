## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

**2A. Demand environment.** All three peers describe a demand environment consistent with GNG's framing: a Windows-10-end-of-support-driven refresh super-cycle (Redington, RPTECH — both cite it explicitly and repeatedly across Q2-Q4 FY26 calls) compounded by anticipatory/pre-buying behaviour as channel partners and consumers front-load purchases ahead of further price increases (RPTECH, Feb 2026: "channel partners stocked inventory ahead of expected price increases"; May 2026: "when the price hike cycle started... a lot of large corporate ISVs extended their refresh cycle... now everyone is realizing that if I delay the purchase by 1 year, I may have to pay another 30% or 40% more"). No peer frames the current cycle as comparable to COVID-era demand (both Redington and RPTECH explicitly say the current cycle is a supply/price-driven phenomenon without a matching demand spike, unlike COVID) — this nuance is consistent with, and slightly more cautious than, a reading of GNG's demand narrative as "boom-like." Both RPTECH and Redington flag a genuine near-term affordability risk to consumer volumes in H2 (RPTECH, May 2026: "there could be some softness on the consumer demand" in H2).

**2B. Pricing and input costs.** Strong, consistent peer corroboration (see Q1/Q2 above) of a component-cost-driven, industry-wide margin/pricing story: distributors uniformly report percentage-based margins remaining structurally fixed while absolute per-unit profitability rises with ASP (RPTECH, Feb 2026: "margins are normally fixed... with the economies of scale plus our efficiency, we are able to get some better margin"). This matches a "pass-through with modest operating-leverage upside" margin narrative rather than GNG-specific outsized margin capture — useful context for GNG's own margin narrative in synthesis: peers see revenue/ASP tailwind but not gross-margin-percentage expansion from the shortage per se.

**2C. Capex cycle.** This is an industry-wide expansion, not a lone-expander situation. Redington is investing heavily in SSG platforms, AI CoEs, and data-center strategy (₹60cr increase in net fixed assets YoY, Q3 FY26); RPTECH is opening new branches (55 by Q4 FY26, up from low-50s), launching a semiconductor subsidiary, and building AI Bootcamp/demand-generation infrastructure; CNL is expanding into surveillance/AI/data-center categories, opened a new Noida distribution center, and upgraded ERP. All three peers are expanding capacity and category breadth concurrently with GNG's own expansion narrative — this is a **sector-wide capacity/category race**, not an isolated bet, which somewhat lowers (but does not eliminate) the differentiation value of GNG's own expansion moves; synthesis should note that GNG's investments track a broader industry pattern rather than a contrarian or first-mover position.

**2D. Competitive mentions.** No peer transcript mentions GNG, Electronics Bazaar, or an unmistakable description of GNG's business by name or description, in any of the 12 transcripts reviewed. This is expected given GNG operates in the refurbished-hardware niche, which is adjacent to but distinct from all three peers' core new-hardware/licensing businesses — no peer would have occasion to discuss a small refurbisher competitively.

**2E. Risks peers discuss that GNG's own materials may not have flagged (feeds missing-risks analysis):**
- **Vendor/supply discontinuation risk**: RPTECH (Feb 2026, Gaurav Bhanshali Q&A) flags that Micron is discontinuing its consumer "Crucial" memory brand, creating a near-term supply gap ("next financial year, crucial definitely will be a dent") — a component-supply concentration risk relevant to any hardware-dependent distributor/refurbisher.
- **Currency risk compounding component inflation**: RPTECH (Aug 2025, Kapal Pansari) explicitly notes rupee depreciation is a secondary contributor to price increases on top of underlying component cost inflation, since "lot of our components or the equipments are imported."
- **Extended-credit/receivables risk from entering new high-growth categories**: CNL's explicit management commentary that receivable days are rising specifically because of credit extended to win business in new categories (AI/surveillance/data center) is a directly transferable risk pattern for any distributor (including GNG) chasing new higher-growth segments.
- **Possibility of Windows-10-refresh delay/extension**: Redington (Nov 2025) notes "there may be extended support provided beyond the timelines given," which would blunt the refresh-cycle tailwind that both GNG and peers are counting on.
- **Inventory/slow-moving stock provisioning risk**: RPTECH (May 2026) disclosed a gross-margin dip in Q4 FY26 attributed to "some slow-moving inventory... additional charge," a live example of inventory-obsolescence risk materializing in the current pricing environment despite the general tailwind.
- **Geopolitical/logistics disruption to component and finished-goods supply chains**: CNL (May 2026) quantifies Red Sea freight cost inflation from $1,500-1,700 to $7,500-8,000 per container due to the Middle East conflict, and Redington's Q4 FY26 results show a direct one-month revenue/margin hit from the same conflict — a supply-chain risk vector not obviously present in GNG's own materials as reviewed at this stage.

## PART 4: TRIANGULATION SUMMARY

- **Claims verified**: 0 of 5 fully "VERIFIED" in the strictest sense; **2 of 5 VERIFIED on direction/persistence with magnitude only partially pinned down** (Q1), and **3 of 5 PARTIALLY VERIFIED** (Q2, Q3, Q5). 1 of 5 UNVERIFIABLE (Q4).
- **Claims contradicted**: 0 outright contradictions. The closest thing to a contradiction is Q5, where RPTECH's own debtor-days trend (improving, 61→46-47) runs counter to GNG's stated worsening trend (30-35→40-45), though this is a divergence in trend direction between two distinct companies rather than a peer directly disputing GNG's own numbers.
- **Single most consequential tension**: On Q5 (receivables), RPTECH — GNG's closest peer in business model — demonstrates that debtor days can be *held flat or improved* even through the same component-shortage/price-inflation cycle that GNG cites as the reason its own days lengthened. This suggests GNG's receivables drift may reflect company-specific credit-policy choices (e.g., extending terms to protect volume/channel relationships) rather than an unavoidable industry-wide mechanical effect, and synthesis should treat this as a point requiring explanation rather than an externally-forced outcome.
- **Single strongest independent confirmation**: The AI-datacenter-driven memory/component shortage and its persistence through the Oct-2025 to Apr-2026 window is corroborated unprompted, in the same time windows, by two independent core-IT-distribution peers (Redington and RPTECH) using their own language and their own customers' experience — this is the highest-value triangulation in this stage, since neither peer had any incentive to support GNG's narrative and both were answering their own analysts' questions, not GNG-related ones.
- **Overall assessment**: The peer set **supports** the macro backdrop GNG's management describes — component shortage, price surge, refresh-cycle tailwind, industry-wide capacity investment — with two of three peers (Redington, RPTECH) providing strong, independently-sourced, same-window corroboration of the direction, approximate timing and rough magnitude of the pricing/shortage story. The peer set **complicates** two specific elements: the precise multiples GNG cites for DRAM (peers' own figures run somewhat lower), and the GNG-specific receivables-drift narrative (RPTECH's own experience shows the drift is not universal or mechanically forced). The peer set can **neither confirm nor undercut** the GNG-specific claims that require primary-source evidence outside any peer's natural disclosure scope — formal distributor channel access via Ingram/Supertron, and the specific international ($) retail price data points. No peer evidence undercuts GNG's core narrative; the complications identified are matters of degree and company-specific execution, not directional challenges.
