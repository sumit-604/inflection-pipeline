Refurbished ICT trader grows fast, cash negative

GNG Electronics refurbishes used laptops and other ICT devices and resells them under the Electronics Bazaar brand. Laptops are 68% of revenue, and desktops, tablets, servers, smartphones and accessories make up the other 32%. It is an authorised refurbisher for HP, Lenovo and Microsoft, selling across 44 to 46 countries and more than 4,700 touchpoints from a Sharjah hub and Indian facilities. A UAE subsidiary, Electronics Bazaar FZC, produced about 78% of FY24 consolidated profit and roughly half of revenue. The company listed in July 2025, trades near Rs 634, and carries a market value around Rs 7,200 Cr at about 55 times trailing earnings.

Revenue grew 34% in FY26 to Rs 1,891 Cr and has compounded 40.6% a year from FY20 to FY26, with profit compounding 107.8% over the same stretch to Rs 132 Cr. Gross margin around 20% in FY26 trails all three named peers. The IPO raised about Rs 400 Cr gross and cut gearing from roughly 2.0x to 0.53x.

The transition on offer is thin. GNG wants to move from a working capital heavy refurbishment trader into a distributor supplied, multi geography scale player. Tie-ups with Ingram Micro and Supertron are signed but contribute a share of revenue management itself calls minuscule as of Q4 FY26. US and Europe distributor talks are described as advanced and remain unsigned. The forward moat read lands at MODEST and the combined posture at AVERAGE.

Gate 0 scores 64 of 160, core 48 of 100, classified AVERAGE, held there by two deal breakers rather than by weak growth. Growth scores a clean 20 of 20. Cash generation quality scores 0 of 20. Operating cash flow was negative Rs 215 Cr in FY26 and negative Rs 134 Cr in FY25 while profit kept rising, cumulative CFO to PAT sits at minus 0.93, and working capital days rose from 136 to 178. Debt service coverage never exceeded 1.0x across the four disclosed periods, falling 0.43 to 0.40 to 0.25 to 0.12. Reported operating cash flow leans on working capital loan draws the cash flow statement classifies as operating rather than financing, and in FY24 that inflow of Rs 2,071 M exceeded cash generated from operations itself. CARE states the operating cycle rose to 122 days in FY25 and is expected to stay above 150 days over the medium term, and flags negative cash flow from operations since FY20. Whether the leak is structural or the cost of fast growth cannot be settled until the first post-listing statutory cash flow statement, so the determination is INDETERMINATE and it holds.

Management earns a B on delivery. It beat both its original 20 to 25% and revised 28 to 30% FY26 revenue guidance, delivering 34% growth and 166 to 209 bps of margin expansion. It missed two specific promises: Rs 10 to 12 Cr of second half interest savings that never came, with finance cost instead rising to about Rs 14.5 Cr a quarter, and debtor days it said would hold at 30 to 35, which drifted to 40 to 45. Both misses were disclosed with exact figures rather than buried. FY27 guidance was reset conservatively to 25% revenue and 50 bps margin. One assurance broke quietly: after promising no repeat of prior year Q4 margin compression, Q4 EBITDA margin fell from 11.2% to 9.8% sequentially.

Governance sits at CAUTION. Promoter firm Kay Kay Overseas Corporation is landlord, trading counterparty and loan security provider at once, with no arm's length benchmark disclosed, and carries a Rs 305 M aggregate pending tax demand still unresolved. Against that, eight institutions including Goldman Sachs, Motilal Oswal and Mirae Asset took a roughly 3.94% block worth about Rs 175 Cr in June 2026, CARE upgraded the rating from BBB minus positive to BBB stable and short term A3 to A3 plus in April 2026, and promoter pledge stands at zero.

Peers back the tailwind, not the company specific story. Redington and RPTECH independently confirm the memory and component price surge that anchors the whole FY26 to FY27 narrative, its direction and its persistence from October 2025 through April 2026, plus India entry laptop price inflation and the IDC forecast of falling PC shipments. No peer contradicts a GNG claim. The one claim no peer can confirm or deny is formal distributor channel access through Ingram and Supertron. RPTECH's own debtor days improved over the same window GNG's worsened, so the working capital drift looks company specific. The market runway itself is large: SAM is about Rs 1,49,900 Cr, GNG holds roughly 1.26%, leaving revenue headroom near 79x, rated MASSIVE, though the current lift is a macro price cycle and the certifications that qualify GNG are attainable by any compliant competitor.

Valuation ends the story at AVOID. Both mechanical destination PE tracks land near 16 times, and the operator set a 20x forward exit on FY30 earnings, crediting the long consumption runway as growth duration above the mechanical stack; that 20x governs and still sits below the 25x Recycling and Manufacturing cap. On projected FY30 EPS of about Rs 28.65 it gives a base fair value of Rs 573, below the Rs 634 price, so the three year hurdle fails with a STOP and the probability weighted CAGR is negative 5.6%. The stock becomes actionable only in an entry zone of Rs 261 to 293, with a margin of safety price of Rs 235. The devil's advocate reinforced the AVOID: the supercycle is a single point of failure coupled across revenue, margin, ROCE and a Rs 743 Cr inventory bet, the cash drag may be structural, and the 20x entry zone is perhaps 25% generous against the mechanical Rs 234 read. Reaching Rs 261 to 293 would take a 54% fall from here, below the lowest price traded since listing, which most likely means a thesis-breaking trigger has already fired, so the zone is a deep watch level and a value-trap risk rather than a live buy order.
