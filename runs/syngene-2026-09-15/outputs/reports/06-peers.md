## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

**2A Demand environment.** The peer set describes a broadly positive-to-recovering demand backdrop through mid-2026: Piramal's CDMO grew 19% YoY in the same Q1 FY27 quarter, citing "the continued recovery in US Bio-Pharma funding over the last few quarters" and "a meaningful increase in RFP activity" (Nandini Piramal / opening remarks, PPLPHARMA-Jul-2026, p.3); Sai Life grew 12% overall with CRO up 26% (Siva Chittor, SAILIFE-Aug-2026, p.6); Anthem's FY26 (ended March 2026) delivered a record Q4 on an 18% CRDMO growth base (ANTHEM-May-2026, p.3). The one point of genuine sector-wide softness is quarter-level lumpiness: Anthem's own Q1 FY27 was soft enough that an analyst characterized it as "down almost like a 25% on a Y-o-Y basis" without management disputing the figure (Mehul Sheth, ANTHEM-Jul-2026, p.8), attributed by management to customer delivery-timing shifts, not demand loss or client attrition. This is a useful qualifier: some of Syngene's Q1 FY27 weakness could plausibly sit inside a genuinely lumpy CDMO delivery cycle that also hit Anthem the same quarter — but Anthem's own destocking issue was already declared "mostly it's behind us" by May-2026 (Ajay Bhardwaj, ANTHEM-May-2026, p.4), materially earlier in the cycle than Syngene's continuing softness, and Anthem discloses no second, newly emerging demand problem analogous to Syngene's research-services attrition.

**2B Pricing and input costs.** No peer describes eroding pricing power or discovery-services commoditization. Anthem instead reports margin expansion from backward integration — discontinuing China-sourced intermediate procurement and manufacturing in-house — with the story running from initial disclosure ("backward integration, material margins have improved," Gawir Baig, ANTHEM-Feb-2026, p.4) through detail ("we have completely discontinued China supplies... completely backward integrated," Gawir Baig, ANTHEM-Feb-2026, p.5) to confirmation ("outsourcing is nil right now and the full impact is baked in," Gawir Baig, ANTHEM-Feb-2026, p.7); it also calls its employee-cost ratio "amongst the lowest" in the industry (Gawir Baig, ANTHEM-Nov-2025, p.7). Piramal explicitly cites "pricing discipline" as a positive EBITDA driver (Nandini Piramal, PPLPHARMA-Jul-2026, p.3), including "different pricing measures" to manage rising input costs in CDMO (Nandini Piramal, PPLPHARMA-Jul-2026, p.12). A genuine strategic divergence surfaced in the transcripts, though not from the identical quarter: Anthem runs an explicit no-hedge FX policy and recognized net forex gains as a margin tailwind in its Q3 FY26 call (Gawir Baig, ANTHEM-Feb-2026, p.14; that call covers the quarter ended December 2025), while Syngene's Q1 FY27 (quarter ended June 2026) operating EBITDA of Rs91cr/12% margin already includes a Rs50cr FX hedge LOSS (Syngene Jul-2026, p.5). The two data points are not from the same quarter, but they show a durable policy difference — Anthem unhedged and currently benefiting, Syngene hedged and currently absorbing a loss — worth carrying as an analyst observation rather than a same-period comparison.

**2C Capex cycle.** This is an industry-wide capacity race, not a lone expander. Anthem: Rs1,000-1,200cr Unit-IV (doubling custom synthesis, adding fermentation/peptide capacity, ANTHEM-May-2026, p.7) plus completed Unit-II/III expansions. Sai Life: Rs1,100-1,300cr FY27 capex guidance (Siva Chittor, SAILIFE-Aug-2026, p.10), covering Bidar (450 KL), a new peptide greenfield site (operational 2028), an XDC Centre of Excellence, and a formulation facility. Piramal Pharma: $120-135mn annual capex, inside a $90mn ADC/sterile-injectable expansion program spanning Riverview (just commissioned) and Lexington (on track for calendar 2027, PPLPHARMA-Jul-2026, p.12). Every peer justifies its build with a version of Anthem's own framing: "in our business we have to build it and then they will come" (Ajay Bhardwaj, ANTHEM-Nov-2025, p.6; repeated, ANTHEM-Feb-2026, p.11). Syngene's own Unit3/Bayview build-ahead-of-demand posture is directionally consistent with peer behaviour, not an outlier strategy — the open question the peer set raises is what happens when several large, similarly-timed capacity additions targeting the same advanced-modality customer base go live in the same FY28-29 window (see Part 5).

**2D Competitive mentions.** No peer transcript names Syngene, directly or by unmistakable description, across all six calls read. Two unnamed references surfaced that could plausibly, but not confirmably, point elsewhere: an analyst's question to Anthem about "a listed peer [that] claims to have also developed the capability of making the full peptide chain... and started expanding capacity" (Vivek Rakholiya, ANTHEM-May-2026, p.9), and a question about "one of your peers... facing scaling issue... on the API manufacturing front" for semaglutide (Bansi Desai, ANTHEM-Jul-2026, p.10). Both are unnamed and describe activities (peptide-chain synthesis scale-up, generic semaglutide API) that do not match Syngene's disclosed business lines closely enough to attribute with confidence; they are noted but not counted as Syngene mentions.

**2E Risks peers discuss that Syngene's calls do not.** After the correction pass, one of run 1's three items turned out to be a false gap and is removed; two stand.
- CHECKED AND REMOVED: New Indian Labour Code compliance cost. Anthem took a one-time Rs25.4cr exceptional charge tied to the four Labour Codes notified 21-Nov-2025 (Ajay Bhardwaj, ANTHEM-Feb-2026, p.3). Run 1 stated Syngene's calls carried no equivalent disclosure; this is incorrect. Syngene recognized a comparable Rs58cr net exceptional item under the same Labour Codes in Q3FY26 (Syngene Jan-2026, p.5, with analyst follow-up p.7) and a Rs20cr net gratuity remeasurement credit under the same codes in Q4FY26 (Syngene Apr-2026, p.5). This is a parallel disclosure, not a missing one.
- (i) Overseas-subsidiary loss and deferred-tax-asset drag during a new site's ramp: Piramal discloses "significant carry-forward tax losses" across its North American, UK and European sites and an elevated group tax rate until those sites reach profitable scale, plus an accumulated overseas loss referenced at about Rs146cr (Vivek Valsaraj / Devang Shah exchange, PPLPHARMA-Jul-2026, p.18-19) — a close structural parallel to Bayview (a new, not-yet-profitable US site) that Syngene's calls have not framed in tax-rate terms per B05.
- (ii) AI/automation as a use-case-by-use-case, not yet quantifiable, disruption to discovery and manufacturing economics: both Anthem and Sai Life describe live pilots (document review automation, chemistry-route prediction, high-throughput experimentation) with explicit caveats that "a lot of AI talk is a moment hype" (Ajay Bhardwaj, ANTHEM-Jul-2026, p.15) — this mirrors, rather than exceeds, Syngene's own "aspirational, never quantified" AI story per B05, so it is a parallel rather than a gap, noted for completeness only.
