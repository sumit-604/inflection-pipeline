Contract surfactant maker with unresolved cash question

Aarti Surfactants makes surfactants and specialty home and personal care ingredients and sells them to large FMCG accounts. HUL, P&G, Colgate-Palmolive and Dabur sit among the named customers. It is a single segment manufacturer with about 113,000 tonnes of nameplate capacity, carved out of Aarti Industries in the 2019 demerger, and it carries a market value near Rs 443 Cr. Two customers alone are 71% of product sales, up from 64% a year earlier, and the top five are about 92%. That concentration is the whole business in one number, both its qualification moat and its single largest risk.

Revenue grew 30% in FY26 to Rs 859 Cr, after 12% growth to Rs 659 Cr the year before. Reported profit did not follow. FY26 PAT was Rs 13 Cr against Rs 15 Cr in FY25, and diluted EPS fell to 14.96 from 17.71. The optical drop is a mirror of FY25 one time gains rather than a fresh decline, so the clean earnings line held roughly flat. Cost of materials rose 33% while revenue rose 30%, so the growth is at least partly raw material price passing through at a thinner margin, not evidenced volume.

Gate 0 lands AVERAGE. ROCE fell from 17.43% in FY24 to 9.72% in FY26, ROE from 11.50% to 5.27%, and EBITDA margin compressed from about 11% to 5.5% over the same window. CARE downgraded the company twice through FY26, long term bank facilities from A minus to BBB plus and instruments from BBB plus to BBB in December 2025, citing margin that stayed subdued into H1FY26. All three peer relative moat tests scored zero, Aarti being the smallest and lowest margin name in the surfactants peer set. The forward moat scan reads MODEST and the combined posture is AVERAGE, not a turnaround or high potential setup.

Cash is where the story is genuinely unresolved. Operating cash flow collapsed 78% in FY25, from Rs 52 Cr to Rs 11 Cr, CFO to EBITDA fell from 82% to 25%, and free cash flow turned negative. FY26 operating cash flow then recovered to about Rs 77 Cr, but that recovery came substantially from a one time working capital release, inventory down Rs 16 Cr and payables up Rs 33 Cr, not from margin. Receivables ageing actually improved over this period, with amounts overdue more than six months falling from 7.94% to 6.03%, so the strain sits in inventory, payables and other current assets, not in collections. Whether the cash weakness is a cyclical raw material trough that reverses or a structural margin problem cannot be settled from the record as it stands.

Management says more than it has delivered. The credibility grade is C. The Chairman's Letter reports EBITDA of Rs 49.82 Cr, which reconciles to the correct audited Rs 45.61 Cr plus a one time insurance gain of Rs 4.20 Cr, a 9.2% overstatement in the most read section of the report. The management discussion prints Return on Net Worth of 12.28% as an increase, while the audited ratio note in the same document shows Return on Equity of 6.61%, a 42.5% fall off the same base. The stated priority to protect margins was missed, and the explanations lean external, on raw material, GST and tariffs. Set against that, revenue growth and the dividend were both delivered, and Q1FY27 operating margin recovered to 7.49% from 5.22% the prior quarter, one real data point.

Peers do not corroborate the good parts. Rossari and Galaxy show margins flat to down in the same June 2026 quarter and both describe a fresh West Asia war raw material and freight spike moving into it, the opposite of a sector tailwind behind Aarti's Q1FY27 margin bounce. Aarti's debt funded Pithampur and Silvassa expansion runs against the peer trend, with Rossari targeting debt free status in 18 months and Galaxy having paused growth capex since August 2025, leaving Aarti the lone debt funded expander. Rossari also reports no single customer above 2% of sales and top ten customers at 12% to 13%, which places Aarti's 71% top two and 92% top five well outside disclosed peer practice.

The market runway is real but oversold by management. The addressable Indian surfactant ingredient market sits near Rs 19,000 Cr to Rs 21,000 Cr, the serviceable slice near Rs 5,280 Cr, and Aarti holds about 13% of that slice, so revenue headroom is roughly 6 times before saturation. The runway class reads STRONG. Management's own cited market figure is 5.38 times the independently sourced base, drawn from a longer horizon and a higher source, so the size claim reads inflated even where the underlying runway is genuine.

Promoter conduct reads CAUTION, below the concern threshold, so no promoter flag fires, but the caution items carry forward. The independent Chairman holds parallel board seats at Aarti group adjacent Valiant entities, a Rs 17.4 lakh environmental compensation order landed in June 2026 inside a wider group level compliance pattern, and non executive and KMP pay rose while PAT fell 33%. Pledge percentage is NOT FOUND in any source. On the other side, a genuinely outside independent director joined in October 2025, the first dividend since the 2019 demerger was paid, and the promoter stake rose through a disclosed 2020 open offer rather than any exit.
