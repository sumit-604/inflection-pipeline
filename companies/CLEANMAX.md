## 7. MENTAL MODEL SEED (DRAFT — for Halt 1 stress-test)
**Archetype:** capital recycler wearing an annuity costume.
The annuity half is real. Contracts run 15 to 25 years, working capital is structurally negative
because customers pay advances, collections are at 35 days, and the niche is close to a
monopoly among listed names.
The recycler half determines shareholder returns. Each new gigawatt is funded with roughly 75%
debt plus minority equity sold down at the project company level. That is why subsidiaries sit at
51%, 74% and 80% ownership. The parent shareholder's slice of each incremental megawatt shrinks
as the fleet grows. Fleet growth and per-share value growth are therefore not the same thing.
**Dominant variables:**
1. Blended cost of debt, and whether interest capitalisation is masking the true charge
2. Minority sell-down economics — what the parent gives away per MW to fund the build
3. Per-MW EBITDA at steady state
**Rejected as noise:** headline consolidated revenue growth. Q1 FY27 revenue doubled, but power
sales grew only 47% (₹358 Cr to ₹528 Cr) while lumpy third-party services grew 7.3x (₹41 Cr to
₹300 Cr). Margins fell from 66% to 51% purely on mix. Also rejected: standalone accounts, where
₹5,291 Cr of the ₹6,003 Cr revenue is the parent selling projects to its own subsidiaries.
**Model's own falsifier:** if per-MW EBITDA at steady state comes in materially below ₹0.55 Cr/MW,
or if the parent's economic share of new capacity falls below 60%, the annuity half is not large
enough to carry the recycler half and the model is wrong.
