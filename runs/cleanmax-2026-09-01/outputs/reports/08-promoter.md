## SECTION 2: LEGAL & REGULATORY RECORD

### 2A. SEBI actions
**SEBI settlement order, 16-Oct-2024** (✅ VERIFIED, RHP p.878, "Outstanding actions by statutory and regulatory authorities against our Promoters"): a thematic SEBI inspection of "Clean Max Renewable Trust" (an AIF managed by Clean Max Energy Ventures Pvt Ltd) for FY23 found the AIF had **exceeded the 25% single-investee investment limit** under Regulation 15(1)(c) of SEBI AIF Regulations across three portfolio entities (Clean Max Pluto Solar Power LLP, Clean Max Power 3 LLP, KAS Onsite Power Solutions LLP). Kuldeep Jain, Nikunj Ghodawat and Nitai Vijay were named as AIF KMPs in violation of Regulation 20(5)/20(1) and the AIF Code of Conduct. Show-cause notice issued 29-May-2024; the noticees settled without admitting or denying guilt, paying ₹1.93 million on 10-Oct-2024. Matter closed. A settled-without-admission SEBI order is itself a signal per the pipeline taxonomy — recorded, not waved off, though the underlying breach (AIF concentration limit, not investor-facing fraud) is a technical compliance failure rather than market manipulation or insider trading.

No SAT appeals, insider trading, market manipulation, or LODR non-compliance action found beyond the above.

### 2B/2C. Criminal cases; tax and revenue
- **Green Earth criminal complaint** (✅ VERIFIED, RHP p.871-872, p.879): M/s Green Earth filed a criminal complaint dated 5-Aug-2020 before the Court of Duty Magistrate, Sirsa, against the Company, **Kuldeep Jain personally**, and other representatives, under IPC Sections 406 (criminal breach of trust), 420 (cheating), 467/468/471 (forgery), 120B (criminal conspiracy) and 506 (criminal intimidation) — alleging unpaid land-acquisition commission under an October 2018 MoU that was later terminated for commercial reasons. The Judicial Magistrate took cognizance and issued summons; the Company's quashing petition under CrPC s.482 is pending before Punjab & Haryana HC, which stayed trial-court proceedings (Feb-2021) and referred the matter to mediation (Jan-2026). A second complaint by Green Earth is also pending on revision before the District & Sessions Court, Sirsa. **This is the most serious single legal item found**: a commercial dispute has escalated to a personal criminal complaint against the MD naming forgery/conspiracy sections, six years unresolved. No conviction, no SFIO, matter now in mediation.
- **CGST/Central Excise summons, Jaipur** (✅ VERIFIED, RHP p.877-878): Superintendent, CGST & Central Excise Commissionerate, Jaipur, issued a summons dated 19-Sep-2025 to **Kuldeep Jain and Pratap Jain personally** (plus an employee), for evidence, statements and financial records for Apr-2019 to FY25. Replies filed 23-Sep-2025; matter pending, no allegation of tax evasion established in the filed record.
- **CGST/MGST summons, Mumbai** (✅ VERIFIED, RHP p.875-876): a separate summons dated 3-Oct-2025 to Company signatories under CGST/MGST s.70, seeking audited financials and corporate-guarantee details; responses filed Oct/Nov 2025; pending.

## SECTION 3: BUSINESS CONDUCT & ETHICAL TRACK RECORD

### 3A. Related-party transactions
The AGM notice carries **56 material-RPT resolutions** — almost entirely EPC/O&M/tariff arrangements between the parent and its own wholly-owned or majority subsidiaries (the project SPVs), reflecting the structure of the business rather than promoter self-dealing. The Audit Committee engaged a **Big-4 firm** to (i) validate arm's-length pricing workings across all transaction segments, (ii) confirm SEBI-compliance, (iii) build a written arm's-length-principles framework, and (iv) certify each transaction type as arm's length. VERIFIED (AR pp.7327-7333). No promoter personal loans from the company, no non-arm's-length asset sales to promoter entities, no competing-business RPT vehicle identified. KEMPINC LLP is described as a "consultancy and advisory" entity in the renewable sector but no consultancy fee flow from CleanMax to KEMPINC was found in the disclosures reviewed — its role in the group is as a holding/investment vehicle, not a fee-extraction vehicle. Corporate guarantees exist for subsidiary SPV project debt (normal parent-guarantee structure for an IPP) — not invoked in FY23-H1FY26. VERIFIED (RHP p.68).

### 3B. Capital allocation behaviour
- Dilution: Pre-IPO placement to Jongsong Investments Pte Ltd (Temasek affiliate) at ₹1,053/share, 6-Feb-2026, ₹296.90 Cr (VERIFIED, AR p.343; RHP). Fresh IPO issue ₹1,202.98 Cr + OFS ₹1,876.91 Cr at the same price. No promoter warrants issued/lapsed found.
- No dividend paid (typical for a capital-intensive, growth-stage IPP reinvesting in capacity) — not itself adverse for this business model.
- Kuldeep Jain's remuneration showed **0% increase** in FY26 even as overall managerial remuneration rose 15% (vs 11.54% for the broader employee base) — the MD's own pay was held flat while some other managerial pay rose faster than staff pay; mildly worth noting but not a red flag on its own (AR p.325).
- Envision Energy India paid ₹74.94 Cr to secure/block future wind-turbine supply (VERIFIED, AR p.343) — capex commitment, not a related-party payment (Envision Energy is an independent Chinese-origin turbine OEM, not a promoter entity).
- No buybacks, no value-destructive related-party acquisitions found.

### 3C. Promoter share transactions — pledge, trend
This is the most material finding in Section 3. Full pledge chronology, all VERIFIED from filed documents:
- **FY25 (31-Mar-2025)**: 2,05,404 shares pledged by Kuldeep Jain + KEMPINC LLP — against NCD issuance, immaterial in scale (AR Note 19(g)).
- **22-Jul-2025**: KEMPINC LLP, Kuldeep Jain and Nidhi Jain pledged shares to **360 One Prime Limited** under a master facility agreement securing a loan of **up to ₹6,700 million (₹670 Cr)** availed by KEMPINC LLP. Stated purpose in the filed pledge schedule: **"Acquisition of Equity Shares of our Company"** — i.e., a personally-leveraged purchase used to buy incremental CleanMax stake, consistent with KEMPINC LLP's holding rising from 3.30% to 11.10-12.86% over the same period, funded in part by secondary purchase from Brookfield's BGTF (whose holding fell from 49.92% to 21.31%). (RHP p.50-55, "Capital Structure").
- **As on RHP date (25-Feb-2026)**: "all the Pledged Shares had been released" — but the RHP itself flagged, in the same paragraph, that "all or a portion of the remaining Pledged Shares will be re-pledged post creation of statutory lock-in" — i.e., the release-then-re-pledge sequence around listing was **pre-disclosed as planned**, not a concealment.
- **31-Mar-2026 (AR, post-listing)**: 1,15,97,866 shares pledged in aggregate by Kuldeep Jain (2,76,898) and KEMPINC LLP (1,13,20,968) to 360 One Prime Ltd, under the **same** 22-Jul-2025 pledge agreement, "in relation to certain borrowings availed by KEMPINC LLP" (AR Note 19(g), p.420).
- **Pledge as % of promoter group holding**: 1,15,97,866 ÷ 5,79,30,860 (total promoter-group shares, AR p.351) = **20.02%** — matches the priority-verification figure independently.

**Assessment of what the borrowing is for**: the loan financed KEMPINC LLP's (i.e., Kuldeep Jain's personal LLP) purchase of additional CleanMax equity from Brookfield in a secondary transaction ahead of listing — a leveraged insider stake-increase, not consumption or an unrelated personal need. The structure creates a real risk: promoter shares are collateral for a large (₹670 Cr) personal-entity loan, so a sustained share-price decline could trigger a margin call / forced sale, which would be a market-moving event. It is disclosed, it is below the 40% deal-breaker threshold, and the underlying purpose (buying more of the company, not extracting cash from it) is directionally aligned with minority interests — but the leverage-on-own-stock structure itself is a tripwire worth tracking (pledge trend, LTV, any top-up covenants).

Pledge trend: **rising** — from immaterial (FY25) to 20.02% of promoter-group holding (FY26 year-end), via a disclosed release-and-planned-re-pledge sequence around the IPO.

10. **Ajay Kaul** (ex-CEO Jubilant FoodWorks/Domino's India; IIT Delhi, XLRI) appointed independent director, 14-Aug-2025.
11. **Arijit Basu** (ex-MD, State Bank of India; ex-MD/CEO SBI Life; 37-year SBI career) appointed independent director 14-Aug-2025, then **resigned 17-Mar-2026** — *not* a conduct issue: Basu was concurrently appointed **Chairman of IndusInd Bank** (effective 31-Jan-2026, as part of IndusInd's RBI-driven post-scandal leadership overhaul), and since CleanMax borrows from IndusInd Bank, RBI rules bar a bank director from simultaneously sitting on a borrower's board. MEDIA REPORTED + VERIFIED (AR narrative matches).
12. **Dinesh Khara** (former Chairman, State Bank of India) appointed independent director, 17-Mar-2026 — a strong credential upgrade replacing Basu.

Net read: the raw churn count (12) looks alarming in isolation, but on inspection it decomposes into (a) one self-corrected Companies Act compliance failure, (b) a coordinated, expected Brookfield board-seat wind-down as its control stake fell, and (c) one regulation-forced exit of a highly credible independent director, replaced by an equally credible one (ex-SBI Chairman). No director departure cited "personal reasons" as a euphemism, and no departure coincided with an unresolved adverse finding against that director.

**Murzash Manekshana** (Non-Executive Nominee Director, Managing Partner at Brookfield Advisors India) is named as a respondent in a Srei Equipment Finance complaint under the Payment and Settlement Systems Act, solely as a former director of MEP Infrastructure Developers Ltd (currently in insolvency resolution) — he is no longer associated with MEP; unrelated to CleanMax.
