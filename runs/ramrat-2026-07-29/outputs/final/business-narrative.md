Record profit hides a cash conversion break

Ram Ratna Wires makes enamelled copper and aluminium winding wires and strips, about 76% of sales, and copper tubes and pipes, about 22%, with a small contract fabrication remainder. It sells roughly 70% to 75% of output to large OEMs in power, auto, consumer durables, transformers and construction, and the balance through dealers. The company sits inside the Mumbai based RR Global group, is run by the Kabra family, listed on the BSE in 1995, and operates three plants at Dadra Nagar Haveli plus a new copper tube plant at Bhiwadi. FY26 revenue was about Rs 5,077 Cr on a standalone basis and about Rs 5,177 Cr consolidated, PAT about Rs 108 Cr standalone and Rs 107 Cr consolidated, against a market value of Rs 4,249 Cr at Rs 454 a share. The pipeline reported the consolidated figure without declaring basis and did not reconcile it to the audited standalone results, so treat the exact latest year figures as provisional.

The transition is a push into copper tubes. Bhiwadi added 24,000 MTPA of copper tube capacity and started production in June 2025, copper tubes now run about 22% of revenue and grew about 115% year on year, and management frames this as import substitution against a claimed 70% import dependence. A further Rs 86 Cr Silvassa expansion was board approved in March 2026, CTC and HVDC entry is targeted for the second calendar quarter of 2026, the merger of Global Copper Pvt Ltd completed under NCLT order in June 2025, and the Tefabo wind tower stake rose to 64%.

Gate 0 came out AVERAGE, with a core score of 40 out of 80. Two things pull it down. Several blocks scored zero because no shareholding, pledge or contingent liability disclosure was in the sources read. And a genuine FY19 to FY21 return trough of 8% to 11% ROCE, over the COVID window, drags the ten year medians even though FY22 to FY26 recovered to 17% to 19%. The emerging moat scan came out MODEST at 21, and the combined read is AVERAGE, which is not a confirmed transition setup.

Cash is the problem. FY26 operating cash flow swung to about negative Rs 96 Cr standalone and negative Rs 93 Cr consolidated, from positive Rs 225 Cr and Rs 227 Cr the year before, in the same year PAT hit a record. Receivables rose 64% to about Rs 640 Cr and inventory rose 108% to about Rs 486 Cr against roughly 40% revenue growth, with zero inventory write downs recognised, and cash was negative even before tax was paid. Off balance sheet receivables factoring more than doubled to about Rs 187 Cr, which understates the reported debtor position. The cash determination is INDETERMINATE: the copper price spike mechanically inflates working capital across this industry, and Bhiwadi was stocking a new plant, but no concall exists to explain the build and inventory outran revenue nearly threefold with no provisioning. The CARE rating from September 2025 still describes a working capital cycle that shortened to 23 days on FY25 data, a picture the FY26 numbers contradict.

Management credibility grades B. Three specific commitments were delivered as narrated, Bhiwadi commissioned, the Global Copper merger completed, and Tefabo taken to 64%, with no identified misses. It holds at B rather than rising higher because the evidence window is a single fiscal year, the FY26 cash reversal is unexplained in the MD&A, and the dividend was held flat at Rs 2.50 despite PAT rising more than 50%. There are no earnings call transcripts.

Governance and promoter posture is a CONCERN. A Section 132 income tax search from November 2023, plus a Section 148 reassessment on the merged Global Copper, leaves an unresolved contingent tax exposure that the pipeline itself records at three different figures, from about Rs 67 Cr on a single demand line to about Rs 104 Cr aggregate, still to be reconciled against the annual report note. Independent director Ankit Kedia attended 2 of 6 board meetings, about 33%, though that figure needs re anchoring in the report. The board is 50% independent but the family holds 5 of 10 seats, and sister listed R R Kabel holds 6.05% of the company and shares an independent director. Against that, a professional CFO, Iqbal Singh Saggu, joined on 1 April 2026, promoter personal guarantees were released under an 8 May 2026 agreement, and pledge is zero.

Two listed peers, Bhagyanagar and Vidya Wires, confirm the parts of the story that touch winding wire but not the parts that matter most here. Both are running the same capex cycle, both confirm copper price pass through, and both confirm that rising copper prices inflate working capital even under full pass through, which supports the cash reading. Neither operates in copper tubes, so the Bhiwadi import substitution claim and the copper tube demand claim cannot be verified by this set. Vidya Wires named Ram Ratna as a peer with 50% to 60% product overlap, and Bhagyanagar's own analyst flagged industry overcapacity risk from new entrants including Adani and Hindalco.

The market runway is real but the sizing is stretched. The realistic domestic TAM is about Rs 37,862 Cr, the addressable slice about Rs 28,260 Cr, current share about 18%, revenue headroom about 5.5x, and the runway class is STRONG. Management's copper tube market growth claim of 16% to 17% CAGR projects to about 2.9x an independent conservative estimate, which reads inflated, and the capex embedded growth of 14% sits about 3 points above the share implied 11%, so the plan is the more optimistic side of the story, not the physical capacity.
