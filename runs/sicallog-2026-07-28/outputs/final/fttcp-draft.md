## THE CATALYST STORY IN PLAIN WORDS

**What could make revenue keep firing.** In the March 2026 quarter Sical won a
coal overburden removal contract from South Eastern Coalfields worth Rs 3,422 Cr
running about 140 months, and reports a mining order book of Rs 4,600 Cr as at 1
April 2026. Against revenue of Rs 385.68 Cr that is 11.9 years of work. The
Chennai rail linked park started commercial operations in December 2025, so FY26
carried roughly four months of it and FY27 carries twelve. Container volumes grew
at two of three yards, Chennai up 14% and Visakhapatnam up 31%. Confirmation
would be FY27 quarterly revenue holding above Rs 130 Cr. What kills it is
execution slippage on the coal contract or a tender dispute, and the concentration
risk is severe: one contract is roughly 74% of the order book.

**What could make margin hold.** The mix is doing the work. Coal logistics is the
segment the company describes as capital light and it went from a small base to
43% of revenue. The terminals business carries high fixed costs, so once the
Chennai park fills, incremental volume drops through. Confirmation would be two
FY27 quarters above 20%. What kills it is the thing the company's own annual
report warns about, aggressive bidding on PSU tenders, and the thing the peer
evidence warns about: Gateway Distriparks found diesel escalation clauses did not
protect its container yard margin in practice.

**What could fix cash, and why I am not counting on it.** Everything documented
points at debt, not at working capital. The rights issue closed at Rs 93.03 Cr,
Rs 274.36 Cr of long term debt was repaid, borrowings fell by Rs 122.75 Cr, and
Rs 111.30 Cr of assets held for sale are earmarked for repayment inside twelve
months. That lowers the interest bill, which is what stands between Rs 52 Cr of
operating cash and Rs 5 Cr of cash after interest. But there is no factoring
line, no debtor day target and no working capital programme in any document, and
the coal contract will absorb working capital as it ramps. In a company with no
earnings calls, the absence of any stated plan on the weakest transition is not
neutral, it is the loudest thing in the file. Confirmation would be a sanctioned
receivables facility with an amount and a date. What kills it is debtor days going
back above 110 as the contract ramps.

**What could keep capital efficiency recovering.** The capex is already spent and
already working: work in progress fell from Rs 52.14 Cr to Rs 4.20 Cr as the
Chennai park moved into service, which is the textbook case of capital deployed
yesterday earning today. Asset sales shrink the base. The coal contract model
adds profit without much new capital. Confirmation would be FY27 return on
capital above 12% before exceptional items. What kills it is the Bengaluru park,
which would start a fresh capital cycle, though it currently has no disclosed cost
and no disclosed date, so it is not yet a threat inside the twelve month window.

## THE VERDICT

**Composite +3 out of 8, which is DEEP WATCH: wait for confirmation.** Three of
four transitions are pointed the right way and one, the cash transition, is not
moving and has no plan attached to it. The Kernex cap did not engage, because the
cash transition is stagnant rather than declining and the deleveraging action is
documented; the TRIM rule does not apply, because this is not a business coming
off a peak. The whole call turns on one print: whether the first two quarters of
FY27 show the standalone parent making an operating profit before exceptional
items and generating its own cash, without another asset sale to carry it. If
that prints, this moves quickly toward the buy candidate band. If it does not,
the FY26 profit was a land sale and the recovery is a mix shift that the interest
bill eats.
