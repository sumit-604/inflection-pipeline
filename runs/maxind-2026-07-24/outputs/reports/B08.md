### 2E. Civil litigation — the most material item found this pass

**Neelu Analjit Singh v. Analjit Singh and others, NCLT (Sections 241/242, Companies Act — oppression & mismanagement), filed March 2022, settled January 2025.**
Neelu Analjit Singh (24.1% holder in Max Ventures Investment Holdings Pvt Ltd, the promoter holding entity that also carries MAXIND's promoter stake) filed a petition alleging fund diversion and siphoning of assets through "sham transactions," sought an independent audit, sought to have Analjit Singh declared "unfit" to be a director/shareholder of the holding company, and separately alleged that fund diversion was connected to an extramarital relationship, naming board seats allegedly held by the other party (📰 MEDIA REPORTED — Free Press Journal, Business Standard, Editorji, BusinessWorld, Asia Samachar; allegations corroborated across five independent outlets as to their existence and substance, though none confirm the underlying facts as proven). Analjit Singh publicly called the allegations "downright untruthful" (Business Standard, 28-Mar-2022 — 📰 MEDIA REPORTED). No relief was granted at the first hearing; the parties reached a "global settlement" in January 2025, all Delhi High Court and NCLT suits/petitions were withdrawn and disposed of, and Neelu Analjit Singh's marriage was dissolved (Patiala House Court, 19-Feb-2025) (📰 MEDIA REPORTED, multi-sourced). She subsequently filed to re-classify from Promoter to Public category at both MAXIND (16-Apr-2025, ✅ VERIFIED via AR footnote) and Max Financial Services (announced 15-Apr-2025, 📰 MEDIA REPORTED).

**Assessment:** this is a serious, multi-year, publicly litigated family-cum-corporate-governance dispute involving allegations of fund diversion at the direct promoter holding entity for MAXIND. It was settled, not adjudicated — no court or tribunal made a finding of fact on the fund-diversion or affair allegations, and Analjit Singh disputed them from the outset. It is recorded here as a civil-litigation red flag under the evidence-taxonomy rule (👉 an un-adjudicated allegation is never treated as fact) while noting the resolution is itself a data point for the Section 6D transition scan (the overhang is now closed, and the disputing family member has exited the promoter category at both listed entities where she held shares).

No other family-partition suits, JV disputes, or defamation suits were found for MAXIND specifically.

## SECTION 3: BUSINESS CONDUCT & ETHICAL TRACK RECORD

### 3A. Related-party transaction history

Per B03 (Consol Note 38, AR p.279-286, ✅ VERIFIED, cross-checked independently in this pass): Senior Living Management Fee from Max Estates Gurugram Rs19.3 Cr (nil prior year); Icare Health Projects development-rights deposit Rs40.5 Cr; Max Estates Noida stake Rs7.0-7.1 Cr (two figures, internal AR inconsistency independently flagged by B03); and the **Max Towers floor sale**: MAXIND sold three floors at Max Towers, Noida to Max Estates Ltd. (promoter-group entity, same Analjit Singh/Vachani-family control) for Rs105.08 Cr, against an independent valuation of Rs65.50 Cr obtained approximately seven months earlier (~60% premium gap) (✅ VERIFIED, B03 Phase 2B, AR Consol Note 38 and Chairman's Letter p.23). The auditor's own Key Audit Matter at both standalone and consolidated levels is RPT evaluation, explicitly because RPTs "constitute significant part of the Company's/Group's operating revenue" (✅ VERIFIED, Auditor's Report p.132-133, p.211). The transaction was put to shareholder postal ballot and passed with 99.45% approval among eligible (non-interested) voters (✅ VERIFIED, CG Report p.90-91) — a governance process was followed, but the pricing-fairness question (why Rs105.08 Cr against a Rs65.50 Cr valuation just seven months old) remains unanswered in any document read this pass, including the Chairman's Letter, which mentions the sale's completion without ever referencing the valuation gap.

Loan history: a legacy, fully-impaired Rs14.16 Cr loan to Max Skill First Ltd. (negative-net-worth shell, Section 1B) sits on the books — an unresolved historical RPT write-off. Guarantee: the parent has disclosed it "may provide financial support" to subsidiaries ASLL/AACSL (Note 32A(ii)) and has approved a contingent corporate guarantee up to Rs75 Cr for Antara SLL/AACSL (operator context, non-anchored).

### 3B. Capital allocation behaviour

- Dilution: Rights issue (₹124.23 Cr, May-2025) and warrant conversion (₹80.35 Cr, 23-Jul-2026, including promoter entity Max Ventures Investment Holdings subscribing 9,91,162 shares) — the promoter **participated in and funded** these raises rather than allowing them to lapse or diluting away passively (✅ VERIFIED, B03/operator context) — a genuine positive signal on capital-allocation alignment.
- Dividend/hoarding: not applicable — the company is loss-making at the consolidated level; no dividend paid, no cash-hoarding-with-high-salary pattern found (Chairman's own comp is a flat Rs3 Cr/year, not escalating — Section 3C below).

The merger collapsed before the fee was paid (no cash confirmed to have changed hands), but the structuring choice itself — routing promoter compensation through a mechanism that dilutes minority economics — is a directly relevant data point on promoter capital-allocation instinct, roughly a decade old.
