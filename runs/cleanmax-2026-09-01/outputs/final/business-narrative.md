# CLEANMAX business narrative

Renewable IPP building capacity faster than earnings

CleanMax sells renewable power to companies under long contracts, and it also builds and services renewable assets for them. FY26 consolidated revenue was Rs 1,913 Cr. Power Sales under 23 year PPAs made up 73% of that, at an 83% to 84% EBITDA margin. The lumpier EPC and services engine made up 26%. The operating fleet grew from 1.7 GW to 3.5 GW. Customers include named hyperscalers, Apple, and large industrials, and about 74% of new volume comes from repeat buyers. The legal structure forces a minimum 26% customer equity in each group captive SPV, so CleanMax owns only part of what it consolidates.

The change under way is a shift from building to earning. The fleet is young, so reported profit lags the capacity already on the ground. Operating profit before other income moved from minus Rs 497 Cr in FY24 toward a small positive in Q1 FY27. Management guides to Rs 3,000 Cr or more of EBITDA in FY28 off a 4.6 GW base by April 2027. That guidance comes from the concall, not the annual report.

Gate 0 scored the company AVOID, and the score is mechanical, not a judgement on demand. Net debt to EBITDA is 8.03x, interest coverage is 1.17x, and median ROCE over the window is 5.83%. The driver is capex timing on an IPO year build-out, not eroding unit economics. Revenue never declined, it compounded 25% a year over five years, and operating margin widened every year since FY23. Low ROCE reflects capital sitting in projects not yet earning, not a broken business.

Cash tells the harder half of the story. Free cash flow was minus Rs 4,023 Cr in FY26 and it is widening. Borrowings rose from Rs 5,570 Cr to Rs 12,684 Cr in three years. Operating cash flow scaled 20 times to Rs 1,731 Cr, but capex scaled faster, 3.1 times to Rs 5,754 Cr. Operating cash flow was 20.2 times PAT in FY26, a ratio distorted by a near breakeven profit base. Consolidated current liabilities exceeded current assets by Rs 1,724 Cr. About Rs 320 Cr of interest was capitalised, so true group interest was near Rs 1,100 Cr against Rs 786 Cr shown in the P&L. The useful life of solar and wind assets was extended from 25 to 30 years in the same year the operating profit crossover appears. The crossover is not yet proven.

Management credibility grades B. It delivered on cost of debt, which fell from 8.7% to 8.4% over three quarters, on the credit rating, which moved from A+ to AA, on within budget builds, and on the 74% repeat rate. It missed on Bikaner curtailment, where its resolution forecast slipped across three calls while the curtailment worsened from 30% to 70%. One call used three EBITDA labels against two figures, Rs 494 Cr and Rs 462 Cr, with no bridge. Accounting quality scored 4 out of 10, with 103 group entities carrying auditor CARO qualifications and about 90 posting cash losses in both years.

Peers confirm the demand and complicate the margin story. Adani Green, ACME Solar, and JSW Energy all report live grid curtailment this year, so Bikaner is not company specific mis-forecasting. But those peers protect curtailment revenue once a project reaches GNA permanent connectivity. Bikaner sits on TGNA temporary connectivity, so its power is not paid for when curtailed, and the roughly Rs 170 Cr full year hit stands. The claimed 12% to 14% C&I market share could not be corroborated by any peer or third party source. New PPA tariffs fell across the sector, from Rs 4.12 per unit in FY24 to Rs 3.57 in FY26.

The market runway is genuine. The addressable C&I renewable segment is near Rs 44,460 Cr, the served slice near Rs 25,650 Cr, and the three year obtainable share near Rs 5,003 Cr, which leaves about 13 times revenue headroom and a STRONG runway class. Management's own Rs 3 lakh Cr TAM claim is about 9.6 times the obtainable share and reads inflated. Data and AI customers are now 42% of contracted capacity, up from 14% two years ago, so concentration is rising, not falling. Adani, Reliance, and NTPC are named by management as new entrants to the same C&I space.

Governance carries live tripwires. The promoter pledged 20.02% of the promoter holding to fund a leveraged purchase of more CleanMax stock from Brookfield, which is a margin call risk on the controlling shareholder. A Green Earth criminal complaint naming the promoter under forgery and conspiracy sections has been unresolved for six years and sits in mediation. A SEBI settlement and a personal RoC penalty are on the record. Against that, Temasek and Bain entered before the IPO, the ex SBI chairman Dinesh Khara joined the board, the CFO has held the seat 11 years, and the Big 4 auditor was retained and separately engaged to certify the related party framework.
