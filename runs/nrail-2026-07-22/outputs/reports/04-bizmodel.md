## SECTION 1: THE BUSINESS MODEL IN PLAIN ENGLISH

### 1A. One-line description

NRAIL turns imported and domestically-collected waste paper into two things — packaging board (duplex/FBB/SBS board used to make cartons and boxes) and writing/printing paper — at two Gujarat sites, selling mostly to Indian FMCG packagers and publishers/printers, with roughly a sixth of revenue exported (AR p.3, p.6).

### 1B. Money flow chain, by revenue stream

**Packaging Board (duplex/FBB/SBS)**
[Waste paper — imported via nearby ports + domestically collected — plus chemicals and power] → [Company pulps the waste paper and runs it through paper machines, including the newly-commissioned 900 TPD / 240,000 TPA Unit V PM-2 at Sarigam] → [Delivers coated duplex board, Folding Box Board and Solid Bleached Sulphate board, CFTRI-certified for food contact, suited to offset printing/foil stamping] → [FMCG packaging converters and printers buy it] → [They pay on standard trade credit, 30–120 days depending on customer] (AR p.3, p.7, p.13, p.128 note a).

**Writing, Printing & Copier Paper**
[Waste paper + chemicals] → [Pulped and run on the legacy machines — Units I/III/IV at Vapi and Unit V PM-1 at Sarigam — into paper across a range of GSM specifications] → [Delivers writing/printing/copier paper used in textbooks, notebooks, print media] → [Publishers, printers, stationery makers and distributors buy it] → [Standard trade credit, 30–120 days] (AR p.3, p.20, p.128 note a).

### 1E. The chai-stall-uncle version

Picture the neighbourhood recycling guy who collects everyone's old newspapers and cardboard — except NRAIL does this at giant industrial scale, in Gujarat, and turns that waste paper into two things: the cardboard-like board that becomes cereal boxes and shoe boxes, and the plain paper that becomes notebooks and textbooks. It just built its biggest-ever machine to make more of the box board, betting that the price paper mills get for board — which has been falling because everyone in the world is making too much of it — will eventually recover. Right now it's a bit like a shopkeeper who just took a big loan to double the size of his shop, right as the market got quiet — sales are up because the new shop floor is bigger, but the loan payments are eating the profit. It doesn't have a brand people ask for by name; it wins by being cheaper to build and closer to the port than most rivals.

### 2D. Industry lifecycle stage and NRAIL's position

The Indian paperboard packaging market is a **growth industry** on volume: $13.72 bn (2025) projected to $18.92 bn by 2030, a 6.63% CAGR, and global paperboard packaging growing from $422.32 bn (2024) to $447.11 bn (2025), 5.9% CAGR (AR p.15). But the sector is simultaneously at a **cyclical pricing trough**: management describes "an extended downtrend" in global paperboard driven by oversupply and cheap imports, with realizations down from ₹41/kg peak to ₹37/kg (AR p.5, p.7). NRAIL's position: it just completed its largest-ever capex cycle (₹1,000 cr, 900 TPD) *during* this downturn, explicitly betting that scale plus low capital cost per ton will let it outlast smaller, weaker rivals and capture share when pricing normalizes (AR p.7-9). The writing & printing paper sub-segment (37.51% of revenue) sits in a more mature-to-declining phase given the digitalization headwind the company itself names as a risk (AR p.16).

## SECTION 3: FINANCIAL METRICS THAT MATTER FOR THIS BUSINESS MODEL

### 3A. Ignore-these-track-these

| Commonly-tracked ratio | Why misleading/irrelevant here |
|---|---|
| Trailing P/E | EPS collapsed from ₹73.72 to ₹10.37 (AR p.9) purely on a step-up in depreciation and finance cost from the new plant, not a comparable operating deterioration — trailing P/E at this point in the capex cycle will look extreme and is not decision-useful without normalization |
| Net profit margin (standalone) | Fell from 9.78% to 1.08% (AR p.17) mostly because interest expense jumped ~5.3x (₹6.47 cr → ₹53.66 cr net of subsidy, Note 35) on new project debt — a financing-structure effect layered on top of, and larger than, the operating (EBITDA-margin) decline |
| Return on Equity (point-in-time) | 1.04% FY25 vs 7.37% FY24 (AR p.17) — a capital-intensive business that just deployed ₹1,000 cr is mechanically going to show a depressed ROE until utilization and pricing normalize; must be read through-cycle |
| Current ratio (standalone) | 1.33x FY25 vs 1.60x FY24 (AR p.17) — a meaningful share of trade payables (₹106.77 cr) is settled through bill-discounting/vendor-financing arrangements (Note 25), so a lower current ratio does not automatically signal liquidity stress the way it would in a business without negotiated trade finance |
| Debt-Equity ratio in isolation | 0.70x (AR p.9) looks modest, but Interest Coverage Ratio collapsing to 2.34x from 17.60x (AR p.5, p.9) is the more decision-relevant leverage-risk signal for a capital-intensive cyclical business — leverage should be read jointly with coverage, not on its own |

## SECTION 4: RISKS, VALUATION APPROACH & MONITORING

### 4A. Business-model-specific risks and first deterioration signals

| Category | Risk | First financial line item to watch |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue model | Continued global paperboard oversupply/import dumping suppresses realizations further (AR p.7, p.16) | Quarterly paperboard realization (₹/kg) falling further below the ₹37/kg FY25 trough |
| Margin | Input cost inflation (waste paper, coal, chemicals) not passed through given oversupply-driven pricing pressure | EBITDA/Turnover falling below 8% in a subsequent quarter (vs 8.59% FY25, AR p.17) |
| Balance sheet | High leverage post-expansion combined with a debt-service cushion already thin | Interest Coverage Ratio below 2.0x (vs 2.34x FY25, AR p.9) or Net Debt/EBITDA above 4.5x (derived, currently ≈4.3x) |
| Execution | Company is "exploring" a second large greenfield paperboard unit while the first unit's returns have not yet normalized (AR p.7-8) | A firm capex/board-approval announcement for the second unit before realizations recover above ₹40/kg or before the ₹150 cr balancing programme (due Sept 2025, AR p.44) is delivering |
| Structural | Digitalization erodes writing & printing paper demand — company's own risk section names this (AR p.16) — for a segment that is 37.51% of revenue (AR p.6) | Writing & Printing segment revenue share declining further alongside absolute volume decline; granular segment-level P&L is NOT FOUND (Ind AS 108 single-segment reporting, Note 42, AR p.152), so this must be tracked via revenue-mix disclosure in future quarters/ARs |

### 4B. Valuation method applicability — handoff to Role 1 valuation stage

| Method | Applicable? | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| **EV/EBITDA (normalized, through-cycle) — PRIMARY** | Yes | Cyclical capital-intensive manufacturer sitting at a margin trough (8.59% FY25 vs a 12–16% historical range, AR p.17/p.39); EBITDA is less distorted by the recent, financing-driven swing in depreciation and finance cost than P/E is. Must be normalized to mid-cycle margins, not applied to the trough year as-is |
| **EV/tonne of installed capacity — SECONDARY** | Yes | The company's own strategic narrative is built around capital cost per ton (₹40,000/ton actual vs ₹1,00,000/ton greenfield, AR p.9); for an asset-heavy commodity manufacturer, anchoring valuation partly to replacement-cost/capacity economics is a sound cross-check |
| **P/B — TERTIARY** | Yes, limited use | Large, recently-deployed fixed-asset base and a currently-depressed but positive ROE make P/B a useful floor/sanity check, though not a primary driver given the cyclicality |
| **Trailing P/E** | Not applicable | Distorted by the trough-year earnings collapse (EPS ₹73.72 → ₹10.37, AR p.9); would produce a meaningless multiple at this point in the cycle |
| **DCF** | Not applicable | High cyclicality and pricing volatility, combined with limited multi-year explicit management guidance (only FY26 revenue guidance of ₹2,200 cr is given, AR p.8, with no multi-year margin trajectory disclosed), make a long-horizon DCF unreliable without heroic assumptions |
| **Sum-of-the-parts** | Not applicable | Single reportable segment under Ind AS 108 (Note 42, AR p.152); no subsidiaries or joint ventures (Directors' Report, AR p.47) |

**Cycle stage that matters for valuation**: the paperboard sub-segment is explicitly at the bottom of a global pricing downtrend per management (AR p.7) even as underlying Indian volume demand is structurally growing (AR p.14-15) — any multiple applied should be understood as either (a) a trough multiple that should compress as the cycle recovers, or (b) a normalized/mid-cycle multiple applied to normalized (not trough) EBITDA. The two must not be mixed.

### 4C. Quarterly monitoring checklist (10–15 items)

1. Paperboard realization (₹/kg) — good: stable-to-rising off ₹37/kg trough; trouble: further decline
2. EBITDA/Turnover margin — good: trending back toward 12%+; trouble: sub-8% for two consecutive quarters
3. Interest Coverage Ratio — good: rebuilding above 3x; trouble: sub-2x
4. Net Debt/EBITDA (derived) — good: declining toward <3.5x; trouble: rising past 4.5x
5. Capacity utilization at the new 900 TPD/240,000 TPA plant — good: sustained >85%; trouble: sub-75%
6. Balancing programme (Pulp Mill capacity + converting upgrade) completion status vs the September 2025 target (AR p.44) — good: on/ahead of schedule; trouble: slippage
7. Trade receivables growth vs revenue growth — good: converging; trouble: receivables continuing to outrun revenue growth
8. Any firm capex commitment toward the "second large paperboard unit" (AR p.7-8) — good: deferred until deleveraging; trouble: sanctioned while ICR is still depressed
9. Export mix % and realized export pricing — good: stable/growing; trouble: sharp decline signalling demand loss
10. Writing & Printing segment revenue share trend — good: stable; trouble: accelerating decline
11. Waste paper input cost trend — good: stable/declining; trouble: sharp inflation without pass-through
12. Working capital/trade financing reliance (acceptances as % of trade payables, Note 25) — good: stable; trouble: rising sharply (could mask real payables stress)
13. Dividend continuity (₹2/share declared for FY25, AR p.45) — good: maintained; trouble: cut, signalling cash stress
14. Credit rating (ICRA A / A1, AR p.44/p.83) — good: affirmed/upgraded; trouble: downgrade or outlook change
15. Anti-dumping duty petition status (referenced generically as a risk mitigant, AR p.16) — good: petition filed/progressing; trouble: no action, oversupply persists unchecked

### 4D. Highest-value questions for management

1. **Given paperboard realizations fell from ₹41/kg peak to ₹37/kg (AR p.7) on stated global oversupply, what concrete evidence — forward order book, competitor capacity exits, anti-dumping filings — supports an expected recovery, and on what timeline?**
   - Reassures: specific, verifiable data points (named competitor closures, filed petitions, firming forward pricing)
   - Worries: a vague "we expect recovery" with no underlying evidence, or an admission oversupply could persist multiple years

2. **The Company is "exploring" a second large greenfield paperboard unit (AR p.7-8) while Interest Coverage Ratio has fallen to 2.34x and Net Debt/EBITDA is already elevated — what capital-structure/free-cash-flow thresholds must be met before it is sanctioned?**
   - Reassures: explicit guardrails (e.g., ICR back above 4x, deleveraging first) and a funding structure designed to minimize balance-sheet strain, as with the first unit (₹665 cr debt of ₹1,000 cr total, AR p.9)
   - Worries: no explicit thresholds, an intent to "order equipment during the downturn" regardless of current leverage

3. **FY26 revenue guidance of ₹2,200 cr (AR p.8) implies ~33% growth — how much is assumed volume (from the balancing programme completing September 2025) versus assumed price recovery, and what is the sensitivity if paperboard stays flat at ₹37/kg?**
   - Reassures: guidance is volume-led with price held conservative/flat
   - Worries: guidance embeds meaningful price-recovery assumptions that may not materialize

4. **Interest expense rose from ₹6.47 cr to ₹53.66 cr net of subsidy (Note 35) and drove the PAT collapse — with ₹43.75 cr of term-loan current maturities due in FY26 (Note 18) plus working-capital borrowings, what is the FY26 repayment schedule and covenant headroom under the ICRA A rating?**
   - Reassures: comfortable headroom, repayment matched to expected cash flows
   - Worries: tight covenant headroom or reliance on a price recovery to service debt comfortably

5. **Related-party arrangements show promoter-family members holding "places of profit" (Manager–Product Development, Manager–HR, AR p.60-61) alongside ₹3.535 cr of unsecured director loans to the Company (Note 40) — how does the Nomination & Remuneration Committee benchmark these appointments and loan terms against arm's-length alternatives, particularly given the NRC met only twice in FY25 (AR p.71-72)?**
   - Reassures: an active, evidenced independent benchmarking process
   - Worries: a rubber-stamp approval process or thin independent-director engagement

6. **Anti-dumping duty is cited only generically as a risk mitigant (AR p.16) — has NRAIL or an industry body filed a formal anti-dumping petition against specific countries of origin, and what is its status?**
   - Reassures: an active petition with a government timeline
   - Worries: no petition filed, or an industry too fragmented to coordinate one
