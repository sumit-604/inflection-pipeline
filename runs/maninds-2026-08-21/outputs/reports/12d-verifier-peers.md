## FINDINGS

**Finding 1 (MAJOR) — misattributed quote, wrong transcript/quarter.**
Location: B06 Part 1, Q4 verdict ("All input costs fully hedged"), second bullet.
B06 writes: *"Jindal SAW COO, Q2 FY26 call: 'we don't accumulate or hold steel in anticipation of
getting the order... we book the steel the moment we get the order for the pipe...'
(JINDALSAW Oct 2025, p.16)."*
This exact quote does not appear anywhere in the Oct 2025 (Q2 FY26) transcript. It appears in the
May 2026 (Q4 FY26) transcript, PDF page 8 ("Page 7 of 17" printed), spoken by Vinay Kumar Gupta
in answer to an analyst question about steel-price inflation. The speaker attribution (Vinay Kumar,
COO) is correct; the call/quarter and page anchor are wrong. This means Q4's "two peers, Jindal SAW
+ Welspun" corroboration is real (the statement genuinely exists and genuinely belongs to Jindal
SAW), but as cited it fails Verifier D Rule 2's "locate the actual citation... confirm it exists in
that peer's transcript" test — a reader checking JINDALSAW Oct 2025 p.16 will not find it. It also
means the Q4 FY26 (May 2026) Jindal SAW transcript is doing more evidentiary work in Part 1 than
the Part 3 coverage map credits it for (its listed "Key contribution" for that row does not mention
the steel-hedging quote at all).

**Finding 2 (MAJOR) — relevant material left unused where it mattered most: Part 5 cross-peer
hypothesis.**
Location: B06 Part 5 (Cross-Peer Hypothesis on Saudi ownership structures); source left unused:
RATNAMANI Q4 FY25 (May 2025 transcript, PDF p.4).
Part 5 argues: *"Across all three peers, Saudi/GCC market entry is structured as a minority or
joint local-partner arrangement, never a wholly-owned acquisition... Ratnamani frames its Saudi
stainless-steel entry around being present locally ahead of a named local competitor rather than
acquiring outright (RATNAMANI Nov 2025, p.6-7)."*
The RATNAMANI May 2025 transcript (graded CITED-ONLY, not cited in Part 5) states explicitly:
*"Saudi joint venture is 75-25 JV wherein Ratnamani holds 75% and we have a local partner who
holds 25%."* This is directly on-point for the hypothesis and was available in the supplied
transcript set, but was not surfaced. It matters because it complicates rather than supports the
"minority/joint local-partner" framing: Ratnamani's own Saudi JV is majority-Ratnamani-owned
(75%), structurally closer to a controlled foreign subsidiary than to Jindal SAW's 51%/49% JV or
Welspun's associate-company arrangement. Citing this fact would not have overturned the hypothesis
outright, but it would have required either a caveat (Ratnamani's structure is majority not
minority) or a sharper distinction between "has a local partner" and "local partner holds a
controlling or blocking stake." As written, Part 5's grouping of all three peers under one
ownership-structure umbrella is less precise than the underlying evidence supports, and the
precision gap traces directly to leaving a CITED-ONLY transcript's most relevant line unused.

**Finding 3 (MINOR) — anchor off by one page.**
Location: B06 Part 1, Q4 verdict, "Peers silent" paragraph: *"its commentary (RATNAMANI May 2025,
p.9) attributes margin variance partly to raw-material price moves."*
The raw-material/margin discussion (Dhiraj Dave: "we see that raw material price also declined,
metal price... despite that we achieve phenomenal revenue. But when we look at bottom line, we do
not find that corresponding operating leverage") is on PDF page 10 of that transcript, not page 9.
Content exists and the characterization is accurate; only the page anchor is imprecise.

**Finding 4 (MINOR) — thematically relevant line in an UNUSED transcript.**
Location: RATNAMANI H2 FY25 (Nov 2024 transcript), graded UNUSED; relevant to Part 5's
protectionism thread (which cites only JINDALSAW Oct 2025 on anti-dumping).
Manoj Sanghvi, on why the Middle East cold-finishing plant was sited abroad: *"these days with the
countries being protective and the local content required, that is another reason for us to set up
this plant in Middle East"* (PDF p.7). This is an earlier and independent peer statement of the
same in-country/local-content protectionism dynamic that Part 5 and Section 2E build around Jindal
SAW's anti-dumping disclosure. The overall UNUSED grading for this transcript is reasonable (it
predates the FY26 order-book window and does not touch LSAW/HSAW), but this one line was
available, on-theme, and would have strengthened (via a second independent peer) a finding B06
currently rests on a single peer (Jindal SAW) alone.
