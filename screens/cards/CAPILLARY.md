## Business Understanding Narrative

Capillary sells software that keeps large brands' customers loyal. Clients license Loyalty+, Engage+
(messaging), Insights+ (analytics), and Rewards+ (redemption network) (RHP p.273, p.275). More than 90% of revenue is long-term subscription
contracts, 3 to 7 years (Concall Q1FY27 p.3). It serves 465 brands in 53 countries, 25 of them
Fortune 500, and the US is 60% of revenue (IP Q1FY27 p.5).

What is changing is how growth is built. Capillary buys older loyalty platforms cheaply and
migrates customers onto its stack. Acquired revenue starts near 30% gross margin, reaching
65% to 75% after migration, a 4 to 5 year cash payback engine (IP Q1FY27 p.9). SessionM,
bought from Mastercard for $169.1 million net cash on 1 May 2026, is the newest of five such
deals; Kognitiv, Brierley, Persuade and Rewards came earlier. An AI layer, aiRA, prices
analytics and campaign actions on usage, under 3% of revenue (Concall Q1FY27 p.6, p.10).

Why now: the company listed late 2025 and is on its first full public-company quarters.
Q1FY27 revenue grew 43% year on year and adjusted EBITDA margin rose from 11% to 17% (IP
Q1FY27 p.15). The same quarter carried a Rs 333.9 million cyber fraud loss at a Czech
subsidiary and an ongoing forensic audit (IP Q1FY27 p.13, p.21). Governance is being tested
under pressure in only its second reported quarter.

What must be true: NRR near 111% to 114% must hold, the AI-assisted Kognitiv/SessionM
migration must land inside its 18 to 24 month claim rather than the 3 to 5 years earlier
deals took, and aiRA revenue must scale past its $2-2.5 million run rate (Concall Q1FY27 p.6,
p.10, p.11).

What breaks it: a second control failure, a migration delay repeating Brierley and Persuade,
or a forensic finding deeper than one incident. The founder holds only 2.36% directly; the
controlling vehicle is a Singapore company with no single controller, and PE funds hold 81%
of its economic interest (RHP p.388, p.391). Alignment is a real question.

## 2. Business model and archetype
A platform business. Customers pay per transaction, per member, or on outcome-based pricing
for the AI layer, on multi-year contracts (Concall p.3, p.6). Archetype: platform/network.
Two engines: an organic core at 75%-plus gross margin growing through NRR (usage growth,
upsells, new brands/geographies), and an acquired layer that buys
under-invested platforms cheaply and re-prices the margin gap as customers migrate, from about
30% to 65-75% gross margin (Concall p.4, IP p.9). Revenue is 94.5% subscription in Q1FY27, up
from 89.4% prior quarter (IP p.23). Retail is the largest vertical, 28-30% of revenue, then
healthcare and BFSI/telecom (RHP p.276).

## 3. Competitive advantages
Real, not yet a hard moat. Forrester's Q4 2025 Wave rates Capillary highest of eleven vendors
on offering and strategy, ahead of Salesforce, Oracle and Merkle, 5-of-5 on 22 of 27 criteria
(IP p.6). Win rates run above 35% where aiRA is part of the pitch. Switching costs exist: 3 to
7 year contracts and a loyalty ledger not trivial to replace. But the RHP's own risk section
names Salesforce, Epsilon, Merkle and "newer startups offering modular or AI-native
solutions" as active competitors in a market it calls "highly competitive, rapidly evolving
and fragmented" (RHP p.270). Top-5 concentration fell 40% to 34% year on year, a widening
base (IP p.24). Moat: forming, on product leadership, not scarcity.

## 4. The promoters
Two promoters: Aneesh Reddy Boddu, founder, and Capillary Technologies International Pte.
Ltd. ("CTIPL"), a Singapore holding company. At RHP date CTIPL held 65.47% and Boddu 2.36%
directly, 67.83% combined (RHP p.388). CTIPL has no promoter and no natural person controls
15%+ of its voting rights; its constitution caps every shareholder's voting rights at 8% (RHP
p.390, p.391). Boddu's economic interest in CTIPL is 7.53%, alongside Qualcomm, Peak XV
Partners, and holding vehicles (Pandora, Neyiri, AVP I Fund) near 20-23% each. Rolled through,
PE and institutional investors hold 81.38% of the "Promoters of CTIL" slice the RHP itself
displays (RHP p.391). No related-party property interest, pledge, willful-defaulter or
fugitive-offender flag (RHP p.392). Post-listing shareholding: NOT FOUND.

## 5. Financial trajectory
FY26: revenue Rs 7,346 million, up from Rs 2,071 million in FY23, a 53% three-year CAGR
(guidance p.13; a separate citation gives Rs 734 crore, 23% YoY, consistent within rounding).
Adjusted EBITDA Rs 1,069.2 million, 15% margin, up 43% (IP p.18). OCF Rs 1,499.1 million, 140%
of adjusted EBITDA (guidance p.16). ARR Rs 7,654 million, up 26% (guidance p.14). FY26 organic
NRR is stated as both 114% and 110% in two guidance citations; unresolved, flagged not
averaged.
Q1FY27: revenue Rs 2,566.4 million, up 43%, organic growth 17% (IP p.15). Adjusted EBITDA
Rs 440.2 million, 17% margin, up 132%. Reported PAT negative Rs 95.5 million (-4%), carrying
the Rs 333.9 million fraud provision and a Rs 16.1 million deferred tax charge; normalised
PAT Rs 254.4 million, 10% margin (IP p.13, p.17). ARR Rs 10,266 million, up 43% (IP p.16).
Total debt, ROCE, balance sheet: NOT FOUND.
Inflection classification: margin-led and mix-led, resting on the M&A-migration mechanic more
than organic pricing power alone.

## 6. The transition and the quality ladder
Management's claim, quoted once: "AI does not replace the platform. It multiplies what the
platform can do. The customer never leaves Capillary, and the unit of marketing shifts from
the segment to the person" (IP Q1FY27 p.10).
FROM R3, value-added spec'd supplier: 75%-plus organic gross margin and NRR above 110%
evidence real stickiness.
TO R4, franchise-and-share-of-wallet leader. Rests on two unproven engines: aiRA (under 3% of
revenue) and the M&A-migration engine. Brierley, Persuade and Rewards are fully migrated, but
Persuade alone took five years (Concall Q1FY27 p.11), against an 18 to 24 month AI-assisted
target now claimed for Kognitiv and SessionM. No ROCE is held here to test the rung directly.

## 8. Proof check
What fired: revenue up 43%, EBITDA margin up from 11% to 17%, organic gross margin above 75%
for several quarters, customer concentration genuinely falling (IP p.15, p.24). What has not
fired: only one Kognitiv customer was live as of the call, full cohort not due until
September 2027, fifteen months out (Concall p.11). SessionM was two months into a claimed
one-year break-even path, too early to judge. aiRA revenue is real but small. Named proof
point: whether the next Kognitiv migrations land inside 18-24 months, against the five-year
prior track record.

## 9. Flags from the documents
- **Cyber fraud, Rs 333.9 million, about 13% of quarter revenue.** Social engineering
  impersonating the Group CEO induced a Czech subsidiary executive to wire funds to Hong Kong
  and Singapore, 24 June to 2 July 2026 (IP Q1FY27 p.21). Complaints filed in three
  jurisdictions; about Rs 47 million frozen; recovery expected 6-8 months.
- **Forensic audit ongoing, self-initiated, not regulator-ordered.** KPMG engaged, scope
  covers the transaction, banking processes and internal controls (Forensic Audit Letter
  p.2). Outcome not yet disclosed. Insurance coverage is claimed but unsettled, 5-6 month
  process; the concall's coverage answer is cut off in the held transcript (Concall Q1FY27
  p.12).
- **Founder's direct stake is thin, 2.36%**, control via a Singapore vehicle with no single
  controlling shareholder and an 8% voting cap per shareholder (RHP p.388, p.390).
- **Two NRR figures for FY26**, 114% and 110%, both guidance-sourced (step 5).
- **Non-GAAP metrics and self-commissioned data**: Adjusted EBITDA, ARR and NRR are flagged
  by the company itself as undefined under Ind AS/IFRS/US GAAP (IP p.4); the Zinnov industry
  report cited through the RHP was "exclusively commissioned and paid for by our Company"
  (RHP p.272).
Not seen: annual report, balance sheet, ROCE, post-listing shareholding, auditor's opinion,
related-party schedule, audit outcome.

## 11. Posture: Transition Decision Matrix
- Proof gate: PARTIALLY FIRED. NRR and margin expansion are proven; AI-migration speed and
  aiRA monetisation, the rung-climb claims, are not.
- Ugliness: the fraud reads as ARTIFACT-OF-CLIMB, a one-off external attack, not structural.
  The audit is not yet closed; a weak-controls finding would move this to STRUCTURAL.
- Recognition gap: INDETERMINATE. No CMP, multiple or Section 1B work here.
This maps closest to RESEARCH/WATCH: narrative until the migration and aiRA proof points
fire, and the audit closes. A control-weakness finding moves this to CONTRADICTION.

## 12. Verdict card
**WATCH.** Capillary is a fast-growing loyalty-SaaS platform with genuine product leadership
(Forrester's top rating) and a working margin-arbitrage M&A engine. But this card rests on a
corpus with no annual report, no credit rating and no post-listing shareholding, for a
company two quarters into listed life, one of which carried a Rs 333.9 million cyber fraud
loss under an unresolved forensic audit. That argues for watching, not proceeding.
Load-bearing facts a deep run must verify first:
1. KPMG audit outcome, especially internal-control adequacy, not just the fraud story.
2. Post-listing shareholding: has CTIPL's 65.47% stake and its PE ownership changed since the
   RHP, and any near-term lock-in expiry.
3. Whether Kognitiv migration lands inside 18-24 months, against the five-year Persuade
   precedent.
4. FY26 NRR discrepancy, 114% versus 110%, resolved against the annual report.
5. Total debt, ROCE, and a full balance sheet, none of which are in this corpus.
What would change the view: a clean audit finding plus on-time migrations support a PROCEED.
A control-weakness or second failure makes this a PASS regardless of product metrics. No
price here.
