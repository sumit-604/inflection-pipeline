## Business Understanding Narrative

Awfis rents desks. It takes space from a landlord, fits it out, and sells seats to
companies by the month. Revenue is seats times price times occupancy. Cost is the rent it
pays the landlord. The profit is the spread between the two, after fit out capital is
recovered. A second business, Transform, does the fit out work for outside clients as a
contractor (IP p.23). A third layer sells food, IT and transport services into the same
centres.

Two things make this more than a sublease. The first is Managed Aggregation, where the
space owner puts in part of the fit out capital and takes part of the revenue (IP p.7). MA
is 57% of supply. That structure is why reported ROCE reads 55% (IP p.8). The second is
scale: 242 operational centres, 159,000 seats, 18 cities, 3,600 clients (Concall p.7).

What is changing is the mix, not the model. Management is moving the portfolio upmarket
into Grade A and A+ buildings, where it says new inventory prices 30% to 50% above the
current book (IP p.5). Premium centres are 15% of the portfolio today and are guided to
20% by March 2027 (Concall p.16). GCCs now pay 24% of rental revenue (IP p.5).

Why now: growth is fast and self funded. Revenue rose 27% to Rs 425 crore in the June
quarter (IP p.20). Since the May 2024 IPO the company put Rs 400 crore of capex to work
while raising only Rs 128 crore, and it still holds net cash (Concall p.7).

What must be true: the seat spread must widen as the portfolio premiumises. Cash EBITDA
margin has to climb off 10%.

What breaks it: it does not climb. Awfis signs nine year leases and its customers sign for
26 months (IP p.7, Concall p.12). That mismatch is the whole risk.

## 2. Business model and archetype
Seat aggregator and operator. Two reported segments: co-working and allied services
Rs 352 crore in Q1 FY27, construction and fit out Rs 73 crore (IP p.19). Supply comes three
ways: Managed Aggregation at 57% of the portfolio, selective straight lease for ultra
premium assets, and partial managed office where half the seats are pre-committed before
signing (Concall p.5).

The archetype library does not carry this shape. The closest entry is the outsourcing
partner, which keys on capacity fill and client stickiness, and both apply. But the one
economic engine is a spread between rent paid and rent charged, carried on a nine year
lease and refilled every 26 months. That is closer to a levered annuity than to an
outsourcing partner. This needs an operator ruling before any Section 1B work, on the
CLEANMAX precedent where a new archetype was declared rather than forced.

## 3. Competitive advantages
Present but narrow. Switching costs are real once a GCC has fitted out and staffed a floor,
and lock in runs 26 months with total tenure 38 months (IP p.7). Scale is real: management
claims the largest network in India, and 80% of outside Transform revenue comes from
clients who first arrived through flex (Concall p.6). The distribution advantage with GCCs
looks genuine, with 100 plus clients and "long-standing GCC consultant relationships, first
right to pitch" (IP p.16).

Against that, the supply side has no barrier. Anyone can sign a lease. Pricing power shows
up in contractual escalations of 4% to 7% (Concall p.13), which is inflation, not
franchise. Moat verdict: forming, on the demand side only.

## 4. The promoters
Amit Ramani is Chairman and Managing Director, with 20 years in real estate and workspace
design and a prior business, Nelson Planning & Designs (IP p.26). The company is
institution backed rather than family held: Peak XV invested in 2017 and ChrysCapital in
2019 (IP p.24). Peak XV then sold 53.5 lakh shares at Rs 705.64 in December 2024, about
Rs 378 crore (market/insider file). The CEO sold Rs 5 crore of stock in June 2024, weeks
after listing.

Promoter holding percentage and pledge status are NOT FOUND. They are in the FY26 annual
report and the shareholding pattern, neither of which this session could reach. Verify both
before any deeper work.

## 5. Financial trajectory
Six years (IP p.33): revenue 178, 257, 545, 849, 1,208, 1,493 crore FY21 to FY26. Operating
EBITDA 53, 68, 156, 245, 402, 550 crore. Reported ROCE 60% in FY26.

The last two quarters (IP p.20): revenue 410 then 425 crore; reported EBITDA 152 then 162
crore; PAT 23 then 24 crore.

Now the number that matters. Reported EBITDA is struck before rent, because Ind AS 116
pushes lease payments below the line. On the company's own reconciliation, validated by
MSKA under an agreed upon procedures report, Rs 162 crore of reported EBITDA becomes Rs 44
crore of cash EBITDA after Rs 132 crore of rent paid to landlords, and operating profit
before tax is Rs 11 crore (IP p.21). Cash EBITDA margin is 10.1%, against 10.2% a year ago.
Reported profit before tax of Rs 24 crore is exactly equal to other income of Rs 24 crore
(IP p.20).

Classify the inflection: revenue led, and margin flat. Not margin led, whatever the 38.2%
headline says.

## 6. The transition and the quality ladder
FROM R1, a commodity price taker selling space in a market with no entry barrier. TO an R3
claim, a value added supplier whose premium product and GCC franchise give partial pricing
power. The mechanism is premiumisation: Grade A+ inventory priced 30% to 50% above the
book, at 15% of portfolio going to 20% (Concall p.16).

Management's claim, quoted once: "Premiumization is translating into structurally better
realization, stronger pricing power, longer client relationships, and a high-quality
revenue mix. And importantly, the full financial benefit is still ahead of us as these
centers continue to mature" (Concall p.5).

The rung climb is claimed, not shown. The ladder is read in cash margin, and cash margin
did not move.

## 8. Proof check
The triggers claim rising realisation and rising margin. The last two quarters show revenue
and reported EBITDA rising, and cash margin flat at 10.1% (IP p.21). Blended occupancy has
sat at 76% for three quarters. An analyst put it directly: "is your strategy of going for
straight lease benefiting you, because clearly the margins aren't showing up" (Concall
p.10). Managed Aggregation, the capital light structure the ROCE claim rests on, fell from
62% to 57% of supply in one quarter (Concall p.11).

Management gives two reasons for flat H1 margin, and both are checkable. One enterprise
client with 3,000 seats across five centres left in May 2026 after being acquired, and the
fixed cost is being carried while the space refills (Concall p.17). Separately, leases
signed in 2021 are hitting five year rent resets, and there is a three to four quarter lag
before the increase can be passed to customers (Concall p.17).

Proof of scale: delivered. Proof of a rung climb: not delivered. The named proof point is
Q4 FY27, which management volunteered twice.

One more number belongs here. Guided FY27 cash EBITDA is Rs 190 crore to Rs 200 crore and
guided FY27 capex is Rs 200 crore to Rs 210 crore (Concall p.7, p.12). Growth is self
funded, and it consumes the whole cash margin. Free cash is about nil by design.

## 9. Flags from the documents
- Cash EBITDA was introduced this quarter, after investor requests, and the reconciliation
  needed an agreed upon procedures report to be credible (Concall p.10, IP p.21). The
  metric is welcome. Its arrival in the eighth quarter as a listed company is the flag.
- Reported PBT equals other income exactly (IP p.20). The operating business covered its
  own costs and no more.
- Nine year landlord commitments against 26 month customer lock in (Concall p.12, IP p.7).
  Duration mismatch is structural to the model.
- 1,800 seats across three centres exited in one quarter (IP p.12). Portfolio churn is
  described as discipline. It also means seats built with capital were abandoned.
- MA share fell 62% to 57% in one quarter (Concall p.11). Straight lease puts the rent risk
  on Awfis.
- Peak XV sold Rs 378 crore of stock in December 2024 (market file).
Not seen in corpus: promoter holding, pledge, auditor commentary, related party
transactions, litigation.

## 10. Independent check: the credit rating
PARTIAL. The company states a Fitch India rating of A+ (Stable), upgraded from A in May
2025 (IP p.8, p.24), and management adds that borrowing costs 9.05% with incremental
borrowing at 8.5% (Concall p.7). The rationale is not in the corpus and the agency sites
are blocked. A grade without a rationale tells you lenders are comfortable. It does not
tell you what the agency thinks of the lease liability.

## 11. Posture: Transition Decision Matrix
- Proof gate: NOT FIRED. Cash margin flat, occupancy flat, MA share down.
- Ugliness: ARTIFACT OF CLIMB, provisionally. Thin cash margin while premium centres fill
  and 2021 rents reset is a timing story, and the reasons given are testable in two
  quarters. It becomes STRUCTURAL if Q4 FY27 passes without movement.
- Recognition gap: OPEN. Rs 1,788 crore of market value against Rs 96 crore of annualised
  reported PAT is about 19 times, and against Rs 44 crore of annualised operating profit
  before tax it is far more. Peak XV sold at Rs 705 in December 2024; the implied price
  today is roughly Rs 250 (derived from Rs 13.4 annualised EPS at IP p.9, verify live).
  The market has repriced the model downward, not upward.
Posture: RESEARCH / WATCH. Proof not fired, ugliness plausibly temporary, gap open.

## 12. Verdict card
**PROCEED to a spear pass. /step1 only if the spear holds.** This is the best shaped of the
four names read today: real scale, real GCC demand, net cash, self funded growth, and a
price that has already fallen a long way.

Load-bearing fact to verify first: does cash EBITDA margin move above 10.1% in Q2 and Q3
FY27, and is the Q4 improvement management promised twice actually delivered. Everything
else is secondary.

Verify next: (2) rent per seat against revenue per seat, by cohort, so the spread is
visible without Ind AS; (3) promoter holding and pledge from the FY26 annual report;
(4) the MA versus straight lease split trend over four quarters, since the ROCE claim rests
on MA; (5) the Fitch rationale, for the agency's treatment of lease liabilities.

What would change the view: cash EBITDA margin at 12% or better with occupancy above 80%
would make this a live transition candidate. A second flat quarter with MA share still
falling would move it to AVOID, because then the premium mix is buying revenue and not
margin. No price here. The spear sets it.
