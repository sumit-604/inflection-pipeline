## SECTION 1: THE BUSINESS MODEL IN PLAIN ENGLISH

### 1A. One-line description
KCPSUGIND crushes sugarcane into sugar at one Andhra Pradesh factory and squeezes every by-product of
that process (molasses into alcohol, bagasse into power, press mud into bio-fertiliser) into separate
saleable products, while a fast-growing but currently loss-making Urad Dal (black gram) processing line
and a small in-house fabrication shop sit alongside; the real profit engine of the listed group is a
wholly-owned capital-goods subsidiary, The Eimco-K.C.P. Limited, that makes filtration/materials-handling
machinery mostly for export (AR p.91, p.132-133; Note 76, AR p.128).

### 1E. The chai-stall-uncle version
Think of KCPSUGIND like a sugarcane juice stall that decided not to waste anything: the cane goes into
sugar, the leftover fibre (bagasse) burns to make electricity, the leftover syrup (molasses) gets
distilled into alcohol, and even the mud left over from filtering becomes fertiliser. That's clever, but
the core sugar business itself lost money in FY25 and FY26 because what the stall pays the farmer for
cane keeps rising faster than what it can charge for sugar — sugar is a commodity, so it can't just raise
prices. The stall also started a side business buying and processing black gram (Urad Dal) that grew
very fast but hasn't yet made money. The part of the group that actually pays the bills is a separate
machine-making workshop (Eimco) the family also owns, which sells filtration equipment to mines and power
plants, including abroad — the profitable child supporting a struggling parent's core trade.

### 2D. Industry lifecycle stage
Indian sugar: a mature, policy-managed, cyclical commodity industry undergoing a structural shift toward
ethanol diversion (AR p.35, "Government of India's thrust for ethanol production will enable the sugar
millers to divert surplus sugar towards ethanol manufacturing, thus reducing the cyclicality in sugar
business"). KCP's own position within that shift is currently regressive, not progressive: its Distillery
unit produced only 11.61 lakh litres in FY25 versus 65.41 lakh litres in FY24 (AR p.37) — output fell
sharply even as the broader industry narrative is diversion toward ethanol/alcohol. Urad Dal processing is
an early-stage diversification (4x revenue growth FY24→FY25) that has not yet reached profitability at the
segment level (Note 76, AR p.128). Eimco/Engineering (capital goods for mining/power) sits in a more
mature, order-book-driven equipment industry with a rising order book (Rs.9,185.96 lakh FY25 vs
Rs.7,044.29 lakh FY24, AR p.132).

### 3D. Unit economics — the physics of the business

**Unit chosen: 1 Quintal (100 kg) of bagged sugar**, the company's largest single revenue line (56.7% of
FY25 standalone revenue).

- Revenue per unit (FY25): Sugar revenue Rs.12,881.33 lakh ÷ 332,986 Quintals sold ≈ **Rs.3,868/Quintal**
  (Note 40, AR p.114).
- Cost per unit of cane input (FY25): Sugarcane consumed Rs.9,836.90 lakh (Note 41, AR p.114) ÷ sugar
  bagged 214,236 Quintals (21,423.6 MT) (AR p.36) ≈ **Rs.4,592/Quintal of sugar produced** — i.e. the raw
  cane cost alone, before any conversion cost, already exceeds the realised sale price per Quintal.
  (Caveat: quantity *sold* 332,986 Qtls and quantity *produced/bagged* 214,236 Qtls differ because the
  company also sold from opening inventory of 308,325 Qtls (AR p.114-115); this is a directional, not
  exact, per-unit bridge, but it is consistent with the Sugar segment's reported pre-depreciation/
  finance/tax loss of Rs.(821.03) lakh in FY25, Note 76, AR p.128.)
- Volume drivers: cane crushed (MT), crushing days, sugar recovery % — all three moved against the
  company in FY25 (AR p.36).
- Price drivers: sugar realisation is a national/regional commodity price the company does not set;
  cane cost is government/State-Advised-Price-anchored and rose even as volumes fell (AR p.36).
- Cost drivers: cane cost (~88.5% of standalone FY26 revenue when measured as total cost of materials
  consumed against revenue from operations, FY26 Results p.3), employee costs, finance costs on
  season-linked working-capital borrowing.
- Incremental margin / operating leverage: because cane cost per unit of output already exceeds sale
  price, incremental crushing volume does not clearly improve unit economics unless recovery % improves
  or by-product credits (power, alcohol, bio-fertiliser, bagasse sales) rise faster than cane cost — the
  by-product stack is the only lever management explicitly cites for insulating margin (AR p.36).
- **For Eimco (consolidated only)**: unit economics are order-book/margin-based, not disclosed at a
  per-unit level; FY25 PBT margin on turnover was ~22.1% (Rs.2,262.64 lakh PBT ÷ Rs.10,228.07 lakh
  turnover and other income, AR p.132) — materially higher-margin than the standalone core, but not
  itemised by product.

| Method | Applicable? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Sum-of-the-parts (SOTP) | **PRIMARY** | The group is structurally two different businesses under one listed entity: a loss-making, capital-intensive, cyclical commodity core (Sugar/Chemicals/Power/Bio-Fert/Urad Dal, standalone) and a profitable, order-book-driven capital-goods subsidiary (Eimco, consolidated-only).
