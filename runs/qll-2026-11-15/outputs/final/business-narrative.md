## BUSINESS UNDERSTANDING NARRATIVE

Qualitek Labs runs 16 accredited testing labs, ten of its own and six government labs it operates under PPP contracts. The largest line tests transport, defence and mechanical parts: vibration, durability, corrosion, emission and EV battery tests, some to the defence standards JSS 55555 and MIL 810. Management puts that line at 40 to 45% of revenue, food and feed testing at 25 to 30%, and pharma, cosmetics and life sciences testing at 15 to 20%. These are management ranges for the FY27 growth drivers; the company files one segment, so the actual FY26 split is not disclosed. Food labs check residues, allergens, shelf life and nutrition labels, and pharma labs test impurities, nitrosamines, stability and sterility. A customer cannot sell a regulated product without an accredited test report, and that report is what QLL sells, billed per test when the report is issued.

The buyers are auto and component makers, defence suppliers, food and feed makers, exporters, drug and device makers, miners and traders, and public bodies such as Indian Oil and the Quality Council of India. Management claims more than 5,000 customers; the top ten were 32.34% of revenue at H1 FY24, and no later concentration figure is filed. Switching costs are plausible because a customer must qualify a lab's methods, but the run found no retention or repeat revenue data to prove them.

Present demand is mandated testing, which the tracker names as "BIS Quality Control Orders and FSSAI NABL-lab mandates", "India vehicle and EV production and registrations" and "Mineral output and Paradip port cargo volumes". Demand should grow with "USFDA inspection outcomes and CDSCO approvals for Indian pharma", "Ministry of Defence indigenisation lists and procurement orders" and "India food and pharma export volumes and export-rejection notices", each visible in public monthly or event data. The three core verticals form a Rs 24,354 to 27,871 Cr market growing about 8.9% a year, and QLL holds 0.78% of the part it can serve, so market size does not limit it.

No testing line has a proven moat. The emerging moat scan scored 11.0 and found no emerging moat; only qualification lock-in and regulatory and government work reached moderate strength. Accreditation is real, but any lab, global inspection majors included, can earn the same scope, so each line stands or falls with "NABL accreditation scope grants and suspensions for QLL labs". Transport, defence and pharma testing hold partial pricing power in niche accredited scope, while food and minerals testing are weak on price. The six PPP labs, one on a Bhubaneswar contract that runs 27 years, give local scale in clusters and are the nearest thing to a moat, but their terms are not filed and their cash depends on "Odisha and other state PPP payers: payment cadence and contract renewals". The talent asymmetry and cannibalisation barrier tests both scored zero: a better funded rival would have to destroy nothing to copy QLL.

Fast growing testing labs, cash still trailing

QLL listed in January 2024 and has since bought labs and built new ones to become a testing group across five verticals. FY26 revenue rose 77% to Rs 12,452 Lakh, but most of the jump came from a full year of ITCPL, a lab group first consolidated from 11 Sep 2024. Standalone revenue grew 47%. Management calls that organic. The Mumbai lab bought by slump sale sits inside standalone from 1 Sep 2025, so true organic growth is lower by an amount the filings do not give. The climb the company claims is from a single lab business to a multi vertical testing house. The proof is margin, and it has not shown yet: FY26 incremental EBITDA margin was 24.0%, the same as the average, so scale added no operating leverage.

The backward record scores 60 of 160 at Gate 0, an AVERAGE classification, with a core score of 45 of 100 and four moats confirmed for a STRONG moat class. Growth earned full marks, but acquisitions and a tiny FY21 base inflate it. ROCE fell from 22.4% in FY23 to 10.8% in FY26 as capital employed grew about ten times on goodwill and capital work in progress. Free cash flow was negative in all six years. Recomputation moves the core score between 44 and 45 and the moat score between 14 and 17; the class stays AVERAGE in every case. The depressors all date from the build after listing. That is the pattern a rebase case needs, and it is also the pattern a failed build would leave.

Cash is the weakest part of the record. FY26 cash from operations covered 75.7% of pretax profit, down from 100.8% in FY25. In H1 FY27 it covered 42.0%, and 39.1% at the standalone company. Receivables older than six months were Rs 944 Lakh, 22.4% of a Rs 4,220 Lakh gross book, against a provision of Rs 6 Lakh. Rs 220 Lakh is older than three years. Standalone receivable days rose from 93.8 to 106.4, and standalone receivables grew 67% on revenue growth of 47%. The aged share barely moved from 22.2% in FY25, which fits a book that grows with new labs and acquisitions. The rising days, the thin provision and the six government PPP labs fit a payer problem. No credit rating rationale was found, and the filings do not split receivables by customer type, so the run cannot tell the two readings apart.

Goodwill of Rs 7,812 Lakh is 51.2% of equity, and most of it traces to one related party deal. QLL bought the last 50.01% of ITCPL from its promoter holding company, TIC Services, and one other seller, and paid in Rs 5,200 Lakh of new shares at Rs 310.89 each. The implied ITCPL price rose 57.5% in twelve months, within one point of the rise in QLL's own issue price. ITCPL earned Rs 711 Lakh in FY26, and QLL's cost of Rs 8,499 Lakh is 4.46 times ITCPL's own net worth of Rs 1,906 Lakh. No valuation report and no impairment test inputs are filed. The results notes call the sellers "external equity shareholders" though ITCPL is a promoter group company. One reading is a fair price for a profitable lab group. The other is insider priced paper, and the missing valuation report is what separates them. Borrowings rose from Rs 9,298 Lakh at March 2026 to Rs 11,467 Lakh at September 2026, and promoter side guarantees cover about 84% of bank debt.

Management kept two of nine filed promises, kept three in part and missed four. It used the IPO money as stated and grew revenue 4.3 times in two years, mostly by buying. It missed the 30% EBITDA margin goal, cut the FY27 PAT margin guide from 15 to 17% down to 14 to 15% with no stated reason, and dropped three long dated targets from the annual report. H1 FY27 printed a 21.6% EBITDA margin and a 9.1% PAT margin against a FY27 guide of 25 to 27% and 14 to 15%. The verifier found the H1 gap sits in the acquired subsidiaries, whose derived EBITDA margin fell to 14.6% from 18.6%, while the standalone company held 25.9%. The filed record grades credibility C. The red flag verifier reads D, citing the promoter group deal called external, deck return ratios (ROE 22%, ROCE 17%) that the annual report (RoNW 6.9%, ROCE 7.4%) does not support, and a managerial pay cap raise proposed and then withdrawn without reason. The same verifier warns that H1 is the weak half: the same half margin rose from 20.7% to 21.6%, so the full year guide is behind, not settled as missed. A full year margin of 25% needs H2 FY27 at 27.4 to 27.6%, against 25.7% in H2 FY26.

Three listed peers give base rates, but none names QLL and none is a like for like testing business. Dr Lal PathLabs grew 19 to 20% organically and lifted margin about four points on existing capacity, with receivable days of 17. Krsnaa grew 22 to 32% on new footprint, lost two to three margin points, and carries receivable days of 139 to 155 with government payers. Vimta, the closest lab model, says new capacity takes two to three years to reach its return targets; QLL ties its margin recovery to H2 FY27. No peer reports organic growth near 47%, and none shows margin rising within four quarters of fast new footprint growth. The peers contradicted no QLL claim. They confirm the mechanism, that new labs dilute margin first and recover as they fill, but not QLL's pace.

The market is not the constraint; capacity, margin and cash are. Market reports disagree by 1.6 times on the size of India testing, so the market figure is a range, not a point. The obtainable market estimate is Rs 263 Cr in three years and Rs 392 Cr in five, a revenue path of 28% and 26% a year. Committed capacity supports about Rs 214 Cr, so the three year figure needs Rs 49 Cr of capacity that is not yet funded. Capital work in progress already stood at Rs 6,977 Lakh at 30 Sep 2026, but only 11.5% of it was capitalised in FY26 against 31.5% in FY25, and three of nine capex completion milestones were delivered. Management's Rs 500 Cr FY31 ambition depends on acquisitions, and Rs 2,200 Lakh of the new share issue is set aside for acquisitions not yet named, cybersecurity among them. The market sizing stage ran partial: it skipped peer revenue aggregation and a global benchmark.

The promoter review graded CAUTION, below the CONCERN line, and ran partial because the SEBI, court and company registry portals were out of reach. It found no pledge, no promoter selling and no regulator action. Ashish Kacholia holds 4.35% and rises to 5.65% after the September 2026 issue. TIC Services is to receive 5,20,000 warrants at Rs 518.55, Rs 26.96 Cr in all, with Rs 6.74 Cr paid up front and the source of TIC's money not found. Against that, three company secretaries left in 16 months, and the seat has been empty since 30 Jul 2026. An independent director resigned on 29 Oct 2026. The FY24 annual report reached the exchange 342 days late. Rent paid to a promoter group lessor rose 7.1 times to Rs 153.60 Lakh, and an advance to a related party reached Rs 151.06 Lakh with interest waived. An ITC Ltd trade mark suit against the subsidiary claims Rs 50 Cr and does not appear in the annual report's contingent liabilities.

Scope note: phase 1 lite. Valuation has not run, so this file carries no entry zone, no decision and no destination multiple. Filings report in INR Lakh and presentations in INR Crore; each figure keeps the unit of its source.
