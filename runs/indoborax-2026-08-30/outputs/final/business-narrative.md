# Business understanding narrative

Indo Borax makes boron chemicals at one plant in Pithampur, Madhya Pradesh. The main products are boric acid in technical grade and pharma grade, disodium octaborate tetrahydrate known as DOT, and a new boron oxide line. Boric acid goes into steel and refractory linings, ceramics and glass, pharma, and personal care. Manufactured boron products are about 99 percent of sales. The company is the sole India maker of pharma grade boric acid and holds FDA and BIS certification, so a pharma or personal care buyer cannot change supplier without requalifying the source. The customers are industrial buyers, and steel and refractory demand drives 55 to 60 percent of boric acid use. One named customer is Procter and Gamble, which buys IP grade boric acid. The company names West Bengal, Rajasthan, and Maharashtra as key markets, gives no customer concentration figure, and collects fast at 18 to 33 receivable days. Present demand tracks India steel and refractory output, which the run flags as the monitor signal through Joint Plant Committee production data. The raw material is boron ore imported mainly from Turkey and Eti Maden, so the cost sits outside India while the sales sit inside it. Forward growth rests on four claims: a DOT ramp from 980 to 1,500 tonnes, a 4,000 MTPA boron oxide plant priced at 2.5 to 3 times boric acid, first export revenue, and Procter and Gamble cross selling. Each claim has an outside check: DGCIS import and export volumes, Eti Maden supply disclosures, and the company's own capex filings. None of the four has fired yet. The competitive advantage sits in the boric acid line, where the sole India pharma grade licence and 45 years of incumbency give partial pricing power. DOT carries no moat and has ramped slowly for years off idle capacity. Boron oxide is not yet a business, with zero capital work in progress in the FY26 accounts. The emerging moat scan scored NONE, because the cash war chest is real but undeployed and two forward claims contradict the same annual report.

# Boron chemicals maker under new control

Indo Borax booked about Rs 215 Cr of revenue in FY26, up 23 percent, and reported PAT of Rs 50.27 Cr. Rs 10.15 Cr of that PAT is a one off gain on assets sold to the exiting family, so clean earnings are lower. Clean operating EBITDA margin was 20.5 percent, down from 26.1 percent the year before as imported ore cost rose. The balance sheet carries zero debt and a current ratio near 20 times. About 46 percent of total assets sit in mutual funds, so half the company is a treasury book, not an operating business.

Control changed hands in December 2025. The Jain family, owners for 40 years, sold 50.80 percent at Rs 256.30 a share to Zenrock Chemicals and the EAAA Alternatives funds from Edelweiss. Zenrock is beneficially owned by Sunil Malhotra, a non executive director, and his son Harsh Malhotra is executive director. A professional chief executive from outside the family, Suresh Kalra, runs the company. The three Edelweiss funds hold about 20 percent as co investors. The whole board was replaced in January 2026 and a new CFO joined in March 2026.

The cash flow reads worse than it is. FY25 operating cash flow was minus Rs 104 Cr on a consolidated basis, then swung to plus Rs 87 Cr in FY26. The swing came from a treasury and investment portfolio reclassification, not from operating distress. Receivables quality improved over the same window: the book more than 90 days past due fell from Rs 95.50 lakh to zero, and receivables turnover rose from 11.92 times to 16.47 times. The one genuine structural cash drag is inventory. Imported ore lead time keeps inventory near 99 to 101 days.

What management says and what the filings show do not line up. The annual report says boron oxide was introduced in July 2025, while the chief executive told the June 2026 call it is still a project going on. The chairman's letter states an intent to increase R and D spending, while the same report's technology disclosure says the company has no R and D department. The letter calls margins robust, while the report's own table shows pre tax margins compressing and pre exceptional PBT falling 3.75 percent. A roughly Rs 90 Cr FY27 to FY28 capex figure appears in no filed document. The reported 18 percent profit growth omits the one off gain that produced it.

Governance carries the sharpest items. The company sold Rs 62.02 Cr of property and vehicles to the departing Jain family and leased one office back to them at an escalating rent, all approved four months before the family sold control. The office went for about three times book value with no independent valuation disclosed. The incoming Zenrock block is 100 percent pledged to secure the acquisition debt, a Rs 255 Cr NCD and Rs 75 Cr of preference capital with a single FY31 repayment. A Rs 3.30 Cr commission was still owed to the departed managing director at year end. A Rs 5.09 Cr inter corporate deposit to an insolvent counterparty, Radius Estates, sits unprovided.

Peers confirm the risk but not the opportunity. DMCC, the closest raw material peer, confirms that boron ore concentration in Turkey is an industry wide risk, and three peers confirm that margin compression from Middle East cost inflation is sector wide, not company specific. No true boric acid peer exists in the set, so six of eight company claims, including the 50 percent market share and the boron oxide pricing, stay unverifiable. DMCC is holding back capex, citing the same ore uncertainty that Indo Borax is spending through.

The market gives moderate runway. The India addressable market for boric acid and DOT is Rs 565 Cr to Rs 608 Cr, and the company's share leaves about 2.62 times revenue headroom. The addressable market math implies about 7 percent revenue growth over three to five years, well below the 20 to 35 percent management guides to. Management itself ties three quarters of that guide to price and new products, not to share capture.
