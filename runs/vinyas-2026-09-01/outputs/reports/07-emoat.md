### 2C Growth embedded in capex — arithmetic shown

**Method 1 (framework-instructed): total capex under execution × historical fixed asset turnover.**
- Net PP&E, FY25 close = Rs 3,912.97 lakh = Rs 39.13 Cr (AR Note 2, p.81).
- Revenue from operations, FY25 = Rs 39,663.56 lakh = Rs 396.64 Cr (AR Note 18, p.91).
- Historical FAT (FY25) = 396.64 / 39.13 = **10.14x**.
- Capex committed/spent in the current round: gross PP&E additions FY26 Rs 3,089.06 lakh + CWIP
  addition Rs 450.34 lakh = Rs 3,539.40 lakh = **Rs 35.39 Cr** (AR Note 2/Note 4, p.81, p.83).
- Implied incremental revenue = Rs 35.39 Cr × 10.14x = **~Rs 359 Cr**.
- As % of FY26 actual revenue (Rs 518 Cr): **+69%**.

**Method 2 (management's own capacity-ceiling math, cross-check only, not the primary field value).**
- Pre-expansion ceiling (100% utilisation of existing base): Rs 1,100-1,200 Cr (midpoint Rs 1,150 Cr).
- Post-expansion ceiling: Rs 2,000-2,100 Cr (midpoint Rs 2,050 Cr).
- Incremental ceiling over FY26 actual revenue (Rs 518 Cr): **+296%**.

**The two methods diverge sharply (+69% vs +296%).** This divergence is itself informative: management's
stated capacity-ceiling figures assume a materially higher asset-turn efficiency than the company's own
trailing fixed-asset-turnover ratio would produce, OR the capacity ceiling is a theoretical 100%-utilisation
maximum that is not a realistic near-term revenue run-rate (management itself conceded this gap when a
participant noted "peak revenue is almost 4x of current revenue," Jun-2026 call, p.6). The
`capex_embedded_growth_pct` field below uses **Method 1 (+69%)** as the more conservative, arithmetic-derived
figure; Method 2 is carried as a cross-check, not the anchor.
