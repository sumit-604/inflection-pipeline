## Business Understanding Narrative

TAAL Tech sells engineering hours. It is an engineering design services company,
the kind that takes a piece of a Western manufacturer's product development and
does it in India for less. The industry name for this is engineering research
and development outsourcing. The customer is a machinery, automotive or
industrial company that wants a design team without hiring one. Revenue is
billable engineers times utilisation times rate per hour.

It reaches those customers through two overseas arms. TAAL Tech GmbH is a
subsidiary (doc MrfVUD p.9) and TAAL Technologies Inc., USA is a step down
subsidiary (doc hG9xTv p.7). Front offices sit near the client and delivery sits
in India. That is the standard shape of the trade.

What is changing is the company itself, and this is the whole reason the name is
here. The listed entity used to be TAAL Enterprises, a holding company. A
document records TAAL Enterprises Limited as the "Parent company that amalgamated
with TAAL Tech India Private Limited" (doc 9OHZZw p.31). Then, in November 2025,
the listed company filed a change of name and became TAAL Tech Ltd. A holding
structure was collapsed into its operating subsidiary and the parent took the
subsidiary's name.

Why now: because that is a dateable event and it finished recently. A holding
company trades at a discount because nobody can see through it. A pure play
operating company does not. The re-rating engine here is structural, not
operational, and the switch has just been thrown.

What must be true is that the engineering services business is good on its own.
Nothing in the reachable record shows its revenue, its margin, its growth or its
client list. The FY2026 annual report exists, filed 2026-08-07, and could not be
read this session.

What breaks it is that nobody can check. This company has never filed an
investor presentation and has never filed an earnings call transcript, in any
year Bull AI indexes. It also lost two chief financial officers, in July 2022
and May 2023. A company that does not talk to the market and changes its finance
head twice is a company where the numbers must be taken from the annual report
or not at all.

## 2. Business model and archetype

TAAL Tech provides engineering design services. Delivery is in India, client
facing entities are in Europe and the United States.

Archetype: **outsourcing partner**, the CDMO, EMS and IT services family. The
library's five markers are the right questions here and all five are unanswered:
client concentration, wallet share, capacity fill, contract stickiness and price
per unit. None is held.

Revenue, segment split, headcount, utilisation and billing rate: all NOT FOUND.

The one economic engine is engineer utilisation. In this trade the cost is
salary and the revenue is billed hours. Margin is the gap between the two,
widened by moving work offshore and by climbing from staffing toward owned
project scope. That climb is what separates a good ER&D firm from a body shop,
and where TAAL Tech sits on it is NOT FOUND.

## 3. Competitive advantages

Cannot be assessed. This is an honest cannot, not a weak yes.

Nothing in the reachable record describes a client, a contract, a domain
specialisation, a patent or a rate card. An engineering services firm's moat, if
it has one, lives in domain depth and in multi year framework agreements with a
handful of large clients. Neither is visible.

What can be said from structure alone. Owning delivery arms in Germany or
Switzerland and the United States (doc MrfVUD p.9, doc hG9xTv p.7) is a real
asset in this trade, because clients buy from a local entity and a local
contract. Setting those up takes years. That is a modest barrier and it is the
only one evidenced.

Sector context, offered as context and not as evidence for this company: Indian
engineering research and development services has structural tailwinds, because
Western manufacturers keep moving development work offshore. A rising sector
lifts weak firms too, so it argues for reading the name rather than for buying it.

Verdict: **moat unknown.** Step 3 is effectively NOT FOUND.

## 6. The transition and the quality ladder

FROM R0, non operating and holding relic. TO R3, value added and specified
supplier, with an R4 case if client relationships prove sticky.

This is the cleanest ladder story of the eight names screened today, and it is
structural rather than operational.

The mechanism is legal, not commercial. A holding company that owns an operating
subsidiary is valued at a discount, because the market cannot see the operating
business cleanly and cannot be sure the cash will ever reach it. Collapsing the
holding company into the subsidiary removes that discount. Renaming the listed
entity after the operating business tells every screen and every index what the
company now is.

Management's claim, quoted once, and note that it is not a claim so much as a
recorded fact: TAAL Enterprises Limited is the "Parent company that amalgamated
with TAAL Tech India Private Limited" (doc 9OHZZw p.31). The Change of Name
filing of 2025-11-04 completes it.

Against the base rate this is unusual and it does not break the rule. The rung
jump base rate of one rung per two to three years applies to operating climbs.
An R0 to R3 move by amalgamation is not an operating climb at all. The business
underneath did not change. What changed is that it became visible.

That is a real and legitimate transition under this framework, and it comes with
its own trap. If the operating business was always mediocre, making it visible
does not make it good.

## 12. Verdict card

**PROCEED to `/step1`.** This is the best use of a deep run among the eight.

The argument is about what a deep run buys. Everything this card lacks sits in
one document that exists and is free: the FY2026 annual report filed 2026-08-07.
It carries the post amalgamation profit and loss, the segment note, the client
concentration disclosure, the share capital movement and the related party
schedule. Unlike India Nippon, where the deciding fact has not happened yet, and
unlike Molbio, where the corpus is a single unread offer document, this name has
a knowable answer waiting in a filed report.

The setup is the shape the framework hunts. A small cap at Rs 1,410 cr. A
dateable structural transition, completed in November 2025, that converts a
holding relic into a pure play. A sector with genuine tailwinds. A company so
quiet that no market mechanism has repriced it. That combination is where a
recognition gap lives.

The risks are equally clear and must not be softened. Two CFO exits inside about
eleven months. No investor communication of any kind, ever. Unknown client
concentration in a business where one lost client can remove a quarter of
revenue. And the real possibility that the operating business is ordinary, in
which case making it visible achieves nothing.

Load-bearing facts to verify first:
1. FY2026 revenue and EBIT margin for the merged entity, against FY2025 for the
   engineering business alone. This decides the thesis.
2. Client concentration. Revenue share of the top one, three and five clients.
3. The amalgamation terms. Swap ratio, shares issued, dilution and effective
   date.
4. Why two CFOs left, between July 2022 and May 2023.
5. Promoter identity, current holding and any pledge.

What would change the view:
- An EBIT margin in the mid teens or better with growth would turn RESEARCH into
  RE-RATING LIVE and make this a live position candidate.
- A top client above about 30% of revenue would cap the position hard whatever
  the margin says.
- The company starting to publish a presentation or hold a call would begin
  closing the recognition gap on its own, which raises urgency.
- An unexplained third CFO exit would end it.

No valuation, no target price and no position is set here.
