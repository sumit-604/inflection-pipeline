[Opening — Krishna Raj Sharma, co-founder & director]
Statutory disclaimer: intent is knowledge-sharing, not forward-looking statements or influencing decisions.
Introduces self as co-founder/director. Organization is 18 years old; founders came out of a Wipro division 18 years back; started in cyber security.
Business framing: everything revolves around applications (travel example — MakeMyTrip, DigiYatra, Uber/Ola). Digitization depends on four pillars: Performance, Availability, Scalability, Security ("PASS"). Cold Play / BookMyShow scalability example; year-end tax-filing load example. Offerings make applications perform, be available, scalable, secure.
Value chain (balloon slide): (1) Enterprise = end customer (banks, large enterprises, retail); (2) System Integration partner (IBM, HP, Infosys, TCS, regional/specialized players) — gets approved by the bank, issues/receives RFP, integrates every component; (3) OEM = original equipment manufacturer who owns the IP/product; iValue = VAD (value-added distributor). "We hate the word distributor because we do not do anything a classic distributor does."
Four technology pillars/buckets:
 - Cyber security = largest vertical, ~50% of business, ~60-70 OEMs.
 - Information Life Cycle Management (ILM) = ~26% of business (large storage, data protection, backup/recovery/archive).
 - Data Centre Infrastructure = ~12% of business; two silos: private/capex data centres (owned by orgs, e.g., Bank of Baroda) and public data centres (hyperscalers Azure/AWS/GCP; Indian players Yotta/Adani/Reliance). Strong in private + local national DCs; "very least and small play" in hyperscaler market. Away from power/cooling/cabling/racks; supply security, storage, switches, routing, backup.
 - Application Life Cycle Management (ALM) / Cloud / Others = new bucket, ~3 years old, ~12%. First VAD of Google Cloud in India. ~110 OEMs across buckets.
 "ILM and cyber security together ~70% of business." DC ~12%, ILM ~26%.
Differentiation vs classic distributors and VADs: classic distributors manage supply chain at scale (~50,000 partners; laptops/printers/servers/basic networking). iValue builds ~800 SI partners/year within the four tech pillars. Multi-OEM stack game, not a product game: a cyber-resilient org needs ~25 products / ~40 solution stack across levels; each solution customized per client (Bank A != Bank B). 50% of manpower technical (450 people; 200+ pre-sales/post-sales with 1000+ certifications). Built a multi-brand Centre of Excellence (CoE) demo environment for customers. Offers L3 managed-services / ticket model to SIs (sustenance business, item #5). In-house "iValue Academy" (7th batch) — homegrown talent pool, some supplied to industry.

Q2 — Vibhau Khandelwal (new to company): why can't AI tools deflate iValue's value proposition, or the end customer / SI / OEM get smarter and disintermediate you, or at least negotiate harder (halve your billing rate)?
A2 — CFO Swaroop: iValue does not deal in commodities; offerings are technology-understanding/implementation-relevant and sized per requirement (SOC for a Kotak-type bank cannot be reused for an Axis-type bank; done from scratch). Not iValue-exclusive IP — SIs (Infosys/TCS) can and do it; iValue is not the whole market. Will the SI become a threat? In a $100 end-customer order, application development/maintenance/security ~$80-85; what iValue supplies ~$10-15. SIs operate at EBITDA north of 20-25%; iValue at ~5.5-6%; an SI will not come down for a 10% wallet-share item at much lower EBITDA. OEMs (globally + India) sell only through distributors (reach) — e.g., GCP appointed distributors incl. iValue. iValue complements, not competes with, the SI; does not bid direct even though it has capability. Return ratios good though EBITDA lower.
