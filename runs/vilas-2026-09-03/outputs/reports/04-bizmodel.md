## SECTION 1: THE BUSINESS MODEL IN PLAIN ENGLISH

### 1A. One-line description

Vilas Transcore buys imported electrical steel coil (and soon copper), cuts and shapes it to exact specification, and sells the finished core components to companies that build transformers — it is a precision job-shop sitting between a global commodity market and India's transformer manufacturers.

### 1E. The chai-stall-uncle version

Think of a tailor who does not weave cloth — he buys bolts of imported fabric and cuts them into perfectly-fitted suits for a handful of trusted showrooms he has served for thirty years. He cannot control the price of the fabric; when cloth gets cheaper, his revenue per suit falls even if he sells more suits. Right now he has just tripled the size of his workshop and is trying three new lines of stitching (fancier linings, a cooling-panel side business, and copper thread work) that have barely started earning. The workshop expansion and the new lines cost money before they earn money, which is why his profit margin dipped even as his output jumped. The core suit business is steady and known; the three new lines are still unproven.

## SECTION 2: INDUSTRY DYNAMICS & COMPETITIVE POSITION

### 2A. Five forces, plainly

| Force | Assessment | Helps/Hurts/Neutral |
|---|---|---|
| Competition count/intensity | CRGO lamination processing in India is described as "moderately fragmented," but the ability to deliver "transformer-grade, high-precision output at scale" is limited to a few specialised players (AR p.35, MD&A). No named peer processors are disclosed anywhere in the corpus — NOT FOUND | Helps (few scale-capable rivals), but unverifiable extent |
| Entry barriers | Precision slitting/shearing/stacking technology, OEM qualification and testing cycles, and a multi-decade quality track record (AR p.35) | Helps, moderately — capital and qualification barriers, not IP or licence barriers |
| Supplier power | India has zero domestic CRGO steel production; VTL imports from Japan, South Korea, Europe and other mills, or buys through Indian intermediaries (AR p.34, MD&A; Note 25 FY25: 53.5% imported / 46.5% "indigenous" by value, but "indigenous" almost certainly means Indian-traded re-sale of already-imported coil, not domestic smelting) | Hurts — concentrated global supplier base, VTL has no leverage over CRGO price |
| Customer power/concentration | FY25 AR MD&A names "a concentrated set of transformer OEMs" as a demand-cyclicality and concentration risk (AR p.38, Risk #3) without disclosing a percentage. | Substitutes | Amorphous and nanocrystalline alloys are emerging substitutes for CRGO in niche high-efficiency applications (AR p.35, MD&A) — VTL is itself entering nanocrystalline, which is a hedge rather than a threat if the ramp succeeds | Neutral to Helps, contingent on execution |

### 2D. Industry lifecycle stage and VTL's position

The Indian transformer and T&D component industry is in a demand-supply-gap growth phase: transmission capacity is projected to grow from ~1,451 GVA (FY26) to ~2,412 GVA by FY32 (Inv. Pres. p.25); industry transformer capacity is projected to grow from ~375 GVA (FY25) to ~650 GVA (FY27E), a ~300 GVA addition over two years (Inv. Pres. p.25); an HVDC pipeline of ~32 GW is under implementation (Inv. Pres. p.25). VTL's own position within that cycle is a capacity-constrained incumbent now tripling scale (12,000 → 36,000 MTPA CRGO, Inv. Pres. p.6, p.20) precisely to capture this demand, while simultaneously trying to move up the value chain into nanocrystalline cores, radiators, copper conductors and (via the 25%-owned associate) HV bushings.

## SECTION 3: FINANCIAL METRICS THAT MATTER FOR THIS BUSINESS MODEL

### 3A. Ignore-these / track-these

| Commonly tracked ratio | Why it misleads for THIS converter |
|---|---|
| Rupee revenue growth (YoY) | Conflates CRGO price movement with real volume growth. FY26 is the textbook case: revenue grew 30% while volume grew far faster (see flag below) purely because CRGO price fell (Inv. Pres. p.5, p.7) |
| Rupee working-capital growth (inventories, receivables) | Amendment 17: rupee WC swings mix real volume ramp with CRGO price swings. Inventories nearly tripled Mar-24 to Mar-25 (Rs 258 Mn → Rs 691 Mn, Inv. Pres. p.8) mostly on price and ramp, not on a change in WC discipline |

Pres. p.7) — this reads as "margin compression" but is a blend of CRGO price fall (helps cost, but selling prices also fell) and Unit-3 fixed-cost drag (Chairman's Message, Inv. | ROE/ROCE at spot year | Amendment 17 forbids feeding spot-year ROCE into Section 1B/FTTCP for a CONVERTER. FY26 ROE 13.1%, ROCE 14.5% (Inv. Pres. p.14) sit at a cyclical trough (CRGO price low, new capacity under-utilised) and are not representative of mid-cycle economics |

## SECTION 4: RISKS, VALUATION APPROACH & MONITORING

### 4A. Business-model-specific risks

| Category | Risk | First financial line item that would deteriorate |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue model | Customer concentration among a small set of OEMs, including a promoter-related counterparty (Atlas Transformers, ~12.5% of FY25 revenue combined sales+purchases, AR p.13-14) (AR p.38, Risk #3) | Revenue growth decoupling sharply from disclosed volume growth without a price explanation, or a step-change in the Atlas Transformers related-party transaction line (AOC-2 annexure) |
| Margin | CRGO price volatility plus new-unit fixed-cost drag (AR p.38, Risk #1 and #6; Inv. Pres. p.5) | Sequential decline in gross margin % (already seen: 21.31% FY25 → 17.82% FY26, Inv. Pres. p.7) |

| Execution | Delayed validation/ramp of nanocrystalline, radiator, copper-conductor and HV-bushings lines (AR p.37, Opportunities & Challenges #2-3) | New-line capacity utilisation staying near zero more than two quarters after a "commercial production" announcement |
| Structural | India has zero domestic CRGO steel manufacturing; VTL is entirely import-dependent for this input (AR p.34, MD&A) | Import cost/duty rising in the CIF-value-of-imports note while realised CRGO selling price stays flat or falls (AR p.70, Note 32) |

### 4B. Valuation method applicability

| Method | Applicable? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| EV/EBITDA on normalised spread and utilisation | **PRIMARY** | Strips out the FY26 CRGO price trough and Unit-3 ramp fixed-cost drag; matches the Section 1B converter destination-multiple framework, which this report does not itself compute (that is Stage 11's job) |
| Segment/SOTP cross-check (CRGO core priced separately from nano/radiator/copper/bushings optionality) | **SECONDARY** | The four new lines have different proof status; blending them into one multiple hides the optionality (Amendment 18 caution against carrying an unresolved probability into a single number) |
| Replacement-cost / asset check on installed capacity (Rs per MTPA) | **TERTIARY** | Useful floor check given the scale of ongoing capex (3x CRGO capacity plus Rs 25-30 Cr copper line, Inv. Pres. p.19), but not a driver of fair value for an operating business |
| DCF as primary tool | NOT APPLICABLE now | Too early in the multi-line ramp; spot margins and utilisation are not representative of steady state, and three of the four new lines have no earnings history to project |
| Dividend Discount Model | NOT APPLICABLE | Company has never paid a dividend and has explicitly stated intent to retain funds for growth (AR p.17, Board's Report Item 4) |

**Cycle stage that matters for valuation:** VTL sits at a simultaneous capacity-ramp trough and CRGO-price trough (Rs 180-185/kg, Chairman's Message, Inv. Pres. p.5). Valuation must not extrapolate the FY26 depressed margin, nor the FY26 low CRGO price, into a steady-state assumption in either direction.

### 4D. Highest-value management questions

1. What is the realised blended spread (selling price minus landed CRGO cost) per kg in the latest quarter, and how has it moved versus the Rs 180-185/kg trough cited for FY26? — *Reassures:* spread stable or expanding as utilisation rises. *Worries:* spread still compressed, fixed-cost drag persisting.
2. What is Unit 3's actual utilisation rate today versus the ~100% utilisation the old 12,000 MTPA base achieved? — *Reassures:* rapid convergence toward full utilisation. *Worries:* utilisation stuck well below capacity months after "commercial production."
3. Beyond nanocrystalline core's Rs 3.48 Cr H2 FY26 revenue, what is the realistic FY27 quarterly run-rate and gross margin for this line? — *Reassures:* margin materially above the CRGO converter margin, validating the higher-value-add thesis. *Worries:* margin converging to CRGO-like levels.
4. What is the single largest customer's share of FY26 revenue, and how has that changed as capacity tripled? — *Reassures:* concentration falling as new customers and PGCIL access add breadth. *Worries:* concentration unchanged or rising, especially via the Atlas Transformers related-party relationship.
5. What is the expected timeline and capital commitment for the HV bushings associate entity to reach commercial revenue, and what is VTL's economic exposure beyond the 25% equity stake? — *Reassures:* capped, disciplined capital commitment. *Worries:* open-ended follow-on funding obligations.
6. Now that PGCIL approval is "in process," what is the expected approval date and the addressable order value it unlocks? — *Reassures:* near-term and sizeable. *Worries:* indefinite timeline, unquantified upside.
7. With short-term borrowings rising to Rs 390 Mn in FY26 to fund working capital, how will FY27's guided 45-50% volume growth be funded without further leverage or dilution? — *Reassures:* internal accrual and residual IPO funds are sufficient. *Worries:* further borrowing or an equity raise is needed.
