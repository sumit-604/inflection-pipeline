## 1E Chai-stall-uncle version
Think of a village blacksmith who got very big and very old. He makes huge steel parts to the customer's drawing. For most of his life he made machines for sugar mills and cement plants. Now the biggest customers he wants are the government: rocket makers, the nuclear power board and the army labs. Those jobs pay better and come with fewer rivals, but they come in big lumps with long gaps. He pays his steel suppliers late and gets paid late, so he borrows to keep the shop running. The test is simple. Can he keep the shop full of government jobs while the old customers still pay, so the fixed cost of the big shop gets spread over more sales?
