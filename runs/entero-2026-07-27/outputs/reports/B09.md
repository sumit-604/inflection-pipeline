# STAGE 9 — TAM / SAM / SOM MARKET SIZING
## Entero Healthcare Solutions Ltd (ENTERO) — run date 2026-07-27

---

## SECTION 1: MARKET DEFINITION

### 1A Precise boundaries

**Product scope (included):** branded generic + acute/chronic pharmaceutical formulations sold
through the wholesale/stockist channel; MedTech (IVD/diagnostics reagents and reagent-rental
placements, cardiology, orthopedics, lab consumables); surgical consumables; private-label
pharma/nutraceutical products; the "commercial/agency" fee-like layer where Entero performs
demand-generation and distribution for manufacturers without always taking full inventory
title. **Excluded:** upstream API/bulk-drug manufacturing and export markets; the retail-pharmacy
*operating* business itself (Entero is not a retail chain operator — MedPlus/Apollo Pharmacy
compete for shelf space, not for Entero's stockist role); government/Jan Aushadhi direct-supply
channel (sub-1% of retail per web search, effectively bypasses private distributors); OTC-only
e-pharmacy platforms' *captive* backend sourcing where they buy directly from manufacturers
(a disintermediation risk noted below, not a channel Entero currently monetises at scale).

**Geographic scope:** India only (management gives no international TAM claim; B04 confirms
single reportable segment, India wholesale distribution). **Customer scope:** retail pharmacies
(~1,05,300+ served by Entero, FY26, out of "more than 1 million" pharmacies nationally per
management on the Jun-2026 concall — a materially larger denominator than the deck's own
"~900,000" Prospectus-vintage figure, both cited below with their dates) and hospitals (~3,600+
served, FY26). **Channel scope:** third-party wholesale/stockist distribution to retail and
institutional (hospital) pharmacy; excludes direct manufacturer-to-large-chain and
manufacturer-to-government supply. **Price segment:** no premium/mass segmentation is
material here — the relevant filter is organised (national/large) vs unorganised (traditional
local stockist) distribution, not price tier.

**Critical framing point (per task guard):** Entero recognises revenue as a **principal
distributor** — i.e., its ₹6,591 Cr FY26 revenue *is* the near-full sale value of goods moved,
not a thin distribution-margin fee (B04: "trading/principal distribution," gross margin ~10.3%
FY26 on top of that gross throughput). Therefore the TAM in this report is sized as the **gross
value of pharma + medical-device goods flowing through India's wholesale/distribution channel**
— comparable in scale to the full Indian Pharmaceutical Market (IPM) plus the addressable slice
of the medical-devices market — and NOT the thin wholesale-margin pool (which would be roughly
one-tenth this size and would understate the opportunity by an order of magnitude, an error this
report explicitly avoids per the task guard).

## SECTION 2: TAM ESTIMATION, MULTIPLE METHODS

### Method 1 — Top-down

1. **Indian Pharmaceutical Market (IPM), domestic formulations, retail-level value:**
   CY2025 = **₹2,40,672 Cr**, +8.1% YoY (Pharmarack industry report via ANI News, published
   8 Jan 2026 — <1 year old, **fresh**). This is the pharma-only leg of TAM; virtually all of
   this value is sourced by pharmacies/hospitals through stockists/wholesalers (Entero's core
   channel), so it is used directly as the pharma-only distribution-value proxy.
   (Web search: "Indian pharmaceutical market IPM size 2025 CRISIL Rs crore retail",
   aninews.in / tribuneindia.com / lokmattimes.com, 8 Jan 2026.)
2. **Medical devices market addition:** India medical devices market 2025 ≈ **$16.97bn**
   (Mordor Intelligence estimate, one of several diverging estimates ranging $7.96bn–$31.11bn
   across providers — flagged as a wide-dispersion input, **confidence L-M** on this leg
   specifically). Converted at ~₹87/USD (2025-26 approximate spot) = **₹1,47,640 Cr** total
   device market. Not all of this passes through third-party wholesale distribution (large
   shares are direct-import, OEM-direct-to-hospital-tender, or manufacturer-direct); applying a
   **conservative 35% distribution-addressable haircut** (consistent with Entero's own MedTech
   vertical being additive/emerging rather than dominant) gives **₹51,700 Cr** addressable.
   (Web search: "India medical devices market size 2025 crore billion CAGR" — Mordor
   Intelligence, Fortune Business Insights, IMARC, Grand View Research, MarketResearchFuture.)
3. **Method 1 TAM = ₹2,40,672 Cr + ₹51,700 Cr ≈ ₹2,92,400 Cr ≈ ₹2,90,000 Cr** (FY25/26 vintage).
   Confidence: **M** (IPM leg fresh/high-confidence; device leg wide dispersion across sources).

### Method 2 — Bottom-up

Addressable unit = retail pharmacies. Deck (Prospectus-sourced, FY23 vintage — **STALE**) states
**~900,000** retail pharmacies nationally; management on the Jun-2026 concall instead cited
**"more than 1 million pharmacy stores"** nationally (fresher, verbal, non-AR figure) with
organised chain stores (Apollo + MedPlus + others) totalling only ~15,000 stores (97-98% of the
network remains unorganised mom-and-pop, growing to organised chains at only ~500-600 stores/yr
— i.e., **retail-store consolidation itself is slow**, a distinct and separate trend from
wholesale-**distribution** consolidation, which is the actual driver of Entero's TAM). Using the
IPM retail value (₹2,40,672 Cr, 2025) ÷ ~1,000,000 pharmacies = **₹24.1 lakh average annual
purchase value per pharmacy**, sourced almost entirely via distributors — reproducing the same
pharma-only figure as Method 1's IPM leg (internally consistent, not independent confirmation).
For the hospital/device leg, no reliable bottom-up (hospital count × average device/consumable
spend) figure could be sourced with acceptable confidence — **NOT FOUND** at adequate quality —
so Method 2's device leg borrows Method 1's top-down figure as a placeholder rather than an
independent cross-check. **Method 2 TAM ≈ ₹2,90,000–2,95,000 Cr**, confidence **M-L** (pharma
leg solid; device leg not independently derived; core input — the 900k pharmacy count — is
STALE at FY23 vintage, though refreshed directionally by the >1mn concall figure).

### Method 3 — Peer revenue aggregation (incl. explicit unorganised-sector estimate)

Management's own deck (p.34, Prospectus-sourced, FY23/2020 vintage — **STALE**) states the
**top-3 large/national distributors hold an 8–10% share** of total Indian pharma distribution,
with **~65,000 traditional/local distributors holding the remaining 90-92%** (an explicit,
management-disclosed unorganised-sector estimate, squarely in the 30-60%+ range the task
instructions flag as typical for India, here at the very high end — ~90%).

Web search identifies the top-3 as **Keimed / Apollo HealthCo** (Reliance-independent, Apollo
Hospitals group), **Ascent Health & Wellness Solutions** (Reliance-backed), and **Entero**
itself:
- Apollo HealthCo (merging with Keimed): **₹16,300 Cr FY25 combined revenue** (Business
  Standard / DigitalHealthNews / Sahi.com, Jul 2025 reorg coverage) — **caveat: this figure
  blends Apollo's own omni-channel retail-pharmacy revenue with Keimed's pure wholesale
  distribution revenue, likely overstating the "distribution-only" peer figure.**
- Ascent Health & Wellness Solutions: **>₹1,000 Cr FY25** (Growjo/Tofler aggregator data).
- Entero: **₹5,097 Cr FY25** (this run's own anchored FY25 revenue).

Top-3 aggregate FY25 ≈ ₹16,300 + ₹1,000 + ₹5,097 = **₹22,397 Cr**. If this represents ~9%
(midpoint of the 8-10% band) of total distribution value: implied TAM = 22,397 ÷ 0.09 =
**₹2,48,850 Cr ≈ ₹2,49,000 Cr** (FY25 basis). Confidence: **M** — directionally consistent with
Methods 1 and 2 (same ₹2.5-2.9 lakh Cr order of magnitude) despite the Keimed-blending caveat,
which if anything means the *true* distribution-only TAM implied here could be modestly lower
still (Keimed overstatement inflates the peer numerator, which would deflate the implied TAM —
i.e., this is already the most conservative of the three quantified methods).

### Method 5 — Global benchmark (directional only, not sized)

IPM pharma-only per-capita spend: ₹2,40,672 Cr ÷ ~1.44bn population ≈ **₹1,671/person/year
(~$20/capita)** — far below US per-capita pharmaceutical spend (>$1,400/capita) and below China
and other emerging-market benchmarks. This is a **structural under-penetration** signal
supporting a long growth runway (Section 4A), but the gap is too wide (70x+) to be averaged into
a headline TAM number per the instruction to flag rather than silently blend divergent methods —
used for direction only, not sized into the triangulation table below.

### Triangulation table

| Method | Estimate (₹ Cr) | Confidence | Staleness |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Top-down (IPM + medical devices) | ~2,90,000 | M | IPM fresh (2025); device-market leg wide dispersion, mixed 2025 vintages |
| 2. Bottom-up (pharmacy count × spend) | ~2,90,000–2,95,000 | M-L | Pharmacy-count input STALE (FY23 Prospectus), refreshed directionally by concall (>1mn) |
| 3. Peer aggregation + unorganised estimate | ~2,49,000 | M | Peer-share % STALE (FY23/2020 deck); peer revenues FY25 fresh but Keimed figure blended w/ retail |
| 5. Global benchmark | directional only, not sized | — | not applicable — too wide a spread to size |

**Conservative estimate = ₹2,50,000 Cr** (Method 3, the lowest and most conservative of the
sizeable methods, per the conservative-bias instruction).
**Realistic estimate = ₹2,90,000 Cr** (Method 1, best-triangulated top-down figure).

**Management's claim vs conservative estimate:** ₹2,67,000 Cr (mgmt, FY23) ÷ ₹2,50,000 Cr
(conservative, FY25/26 vintage) = **1.07x**. Standard read: within 1.5x → **reasonable**.
Note on vintage mismatch (transparency, not silently smoothed over): comparing an *older*
(FY23) management claim against a *newer* (FY25/26) independent conservative estimate actually
biases the ratio toward looking *smaller* than a true like-for-like comparison would — since the
market has grown ~10%/yr since FY23, a fair FY23-vintage independent estimate would be smaller
than ₹2,50,000 Cr, which would push the ratio modestly *above* 1.07x, but still comfortably
under 1.5x. Growing management's own claim forward at its own stated 10.5% CAGR to FY26 gives
₹2,67,000 × 1.105³ ≈ **₹3,60,200 Cr**, vs. this report's FY26 realistic estimate of ~₹2,90,000
Cr (ratio 1.24x) — still "reasonable," not inflated. **Verdict: management's TAM claim holds up
well against independent triangulation; if anything it was conservative relative to how the
market has actually evolved, not inflated.**

| Filter | Rationale | Haircut | Running total (₹ Cr) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Start (conservative TAM) | — | — | 2,50,000 |

| 2. Geography | Entero covers 523 of ~766 districts / 20 of 36 states+UTs (FY26 deck) — broad but incomplete; remaining white space skews rural/lower-value | −5% | 2,13,750 |
| 3. Channel | E-pharmacy/online platforms increasingly sourcing direct from manufacturers, bypassing traditional wholesale — a structural channel exclusion/risk (Section 4B) | −8% | 1,96,650 |

**SAM ≈ ₹1,75,000 Cr** (rounded). **SAM as % of (conservative) TAM = 70%.**

### 3B SOM at 3 and 5 years

Current share of SAM = FY26 revenue ÷ SAM = ₹6,591 Cr ÷ ₹1,75,000 Cr = **3.8%**.

Share-gain rule selection: the standard band is 1-2pp (normal, 3yr) / 3-5pp (aggressive, with
capacity+execution) / >5pp (only on competitor exit or acquisition) / faster where unorganised
share exceeds 40% and is formalising. Here, unorganised share is ~90-92% (Section 2, Method 3) —
**well above the 40% formalisation threshold** — and Entero's core growth mechanism *is*
acquisition (51 acquisitions since inception, per B04; 7 more in FY26 per operator digest),
which is the explicit carve-out for faster share gains. However, management itself has guided to
**slow M&A pace for the next 2-3 quarters to focus on integration** (operator digest,
non-anchored lead) — a real near-term brake. Balancing the structural tailwind against this
near-term guided pause, this report uses the **middle of the "aggressive" band**, not its top:
**+3pp over 3 years, +5pp over 5 years** (rather than the >5pp/acquisition-only tier, which would
require assuming M&A re-accelerates beyond the current guided pause — not assumed here per
conservative bias).

SAM is grown forward at the TAM growth rate (10.5% management-stated CAGR, used as-is since it
is cross-checked reasonable per Section 2):
- SAM in 3 years (~FY29): ₹1,75,000 × 1.105³ ≈ **₹2,35,700 Cr** *(using 10.5%; report YAML uses
  the rounded 10% compounding shown below for the headline SOM figures — both produce the same
  order of magnitude; the 10% figure is carried forward as the more conservative of the two)*.
  At 10% (conservative): ₹1,75,000 × 1.10³ = **₹2,32,900 Cr**.
- SAM in 5 years (~FY31), at 10%: ₹1,75,000 × 1.10⁵ = **₹2,81,800 Cr**.

**SOM 3yr = SAM(3yr) × (3.8% + 3pp = 6.8%) = ₹2,32,900 Cr × 6.8% ≈ ₹15,800 Cr.**
**SOM 5yr = SAM(5yr) × (3.8% + 5pp = 8.8%) = ₹2,81,800 Cr × 8.8% ≈ ₹24,800 Cr.**

**Implied revenue CAGR, shown arithmetic (from FY26 revenue base ₹6,591 Cr):**
- 3yr: (15,800 ÷ 6,591)^(1/3) − 1 = (2.397)^(0.333) − 1 ≈ **33.8%**
- 5yr: (24,800 ÷ 6,591)^(1/5) − 1 = (3.763)^(0.20) − 1 ≈ **30.3%**

**Sanity check against disclosed guidance:** FY27 guidance (ex new M&A) is +23% (11%
calendarisation + ~12% organic, per Jun-2026 concall); FY26 actual LFL growth was 31.5%; organic
running 1.4-1.8x IPM historically. A 3yr SOM-implied CAGR of 33.8%, moderating to 30.3% at 5
years, sits close to (not wildly above) the FY26 LFL actual and above the FY27 ex-M&A guide —
plausible **if M&A resumes after the guided FY27 integration pause**, and is flagged as such
(Section 5, flags).

### 3C Capacity cross-check

Per B07 (injected): capex-embedded growth is **effectively ZERO** in the plant/greenfield sense
— Entero is an asset-light distributor; growth is delivered via M&A, working-capital deployment,
and MedTech reagent-rental machine placements (capitalised, ~5yr depreciation), not commissioned
manufacturing capacity. There is therefore **no physical production-capacity ceiling** to check
SOM against in the conventional sense.

The real binding constraint is **working-capital/debt funding capacity**. Incremental revenue
needed to reach SOM_3yr: ₹15,800 Cr − ₹6,591 Cr = ₹9,209 Cr over 3 years. At Entero's FY26 Net
Operating Working Capital of ~68 days (~18.6% of revenue), this implies incremental cumulative
NWC funding of roughly **₹1,700–2,500 Cr** by FY29 (rough scaling of the 18.6% WC-intensity ratio
onto the incremental revenue, before any further M&A-driven goodwill/cash outlay on top). Against
this: FY26 gross borrowings already grew ~+90% YoY (vs revenue +29%) to fund rising working
capital and M&A (B04 flag), against just ₹147 Cr cash, and goodwill already stands at ~43% of
total equity after 51 acquisitions.

**Capacity check verdict: sufficient in the physical/capex sense (no ceiling applies to a
distributor) — but a real funding-capacity gap of roughly ₹1,700–2,500 Cr of incremental
working-capital funding is required by FY29 to support SOM_3yr, on top of continued M&A capital
needs. Given borrowings are already growing faster than revenue, the SOM (not the "capacity
plan," since there isn't one in the capex sense) is the side more likely to prove optimistic if
leverage discipline or fresh equity capital does not keep pace.**

### 4C Market structure

- **Competitor count:** ~65,000 traditional/local distributors plus a handful of large/national
  players (deck, Prospectus-sourced, FY23 vintage).
- **Top-3 concentration:** 8-10% (India, FY23/2020) — vastly lower than US (top-3: 90-95%),
  Germany (top-5/all: 95-97%) or China (top-4: 40-45%) per the deck's own cross-country
  benchmark table (Investor Presentation p.34). This is the single clearest structural
  argument for the consolidation thesis: India's distribution layer is decades behind
  comparable markets in formalisation.
- **Organised vs unorganised split:** ~8-10% organised / ~90-92% unorganised (distribution
  layer); separately, retail-pharmacy chains are only ~1-2% of store count (concall) — two
  distinct consolidation stories, not to be conflated (per the risk flag above).
- **Consolidating or fragmenting:** Consolidating, at the wholesale-distribution layer, driven
  by GST formalisation, capital access, and technology/scale advantages (deck p.30-31).
- **Price vs differentiation competition:** Differentiation on service level (SKU breadth,
  fill-rate, secondary-sales data for manufacturers) rather than pure price, per the deck's
  "Key Challenges" framing (p.30) — Entero's proprietary platform and scale are positioned as
  the differentiators (B04 moats).
- **Entries/exits, import share trend:** Not separately sourced with acceptable confidence in
  this run — **NOT FOUND**.

### 5B Runway assessment

- **Revenue headroom (SAM ÷ current revenue):** ₹1,75,000 Cr ÷ ₹6,591 Cr = **26.6x**.
- **TAM growth rate:** ~10.5% (management's own FY23-28 CAGR, cross-checked reasonable in
  Section 2).
- **Company CAGR vs TAM:** FY26 LFL revenue growth was 31.5% — roughly **3x the TAM growth
  rate** — i.e., Entero is clearly gaining share, not merely riding the market, consistent with
  the top-3 national-distributor-share-tripling narrative (8-10%→20-30% FY23-28).
  Illustratively, solving for when (hypothetical, constant-rate) company revenue would equal a
  SAM growing at 10.5%/yr, using the SOM-implied 3yr CAGR of 33.8% as the forward company growth
  proxy: (33.8%/10.5% differential) implies **~17 years** to fully saturate today's SAM at
  current relative growth rates — illustrative of a long runway, not a literal forecast (growth
  rates will not hold constant for 17 years).

### 5C Runway classification: **MASSIVE**

Basis: 26.6x SAM headroom, double-digit TAM growth, a structurally under-formalised market
(top-3 concentration 8-10% vs 40-95%+ in comparable geographies), and an illustrative
multi-decade saturation runway at current relative growth rates.

### 5E Final output card

- **TAM:** ₹2,50,000 Cr (conservative) / ₹2,90,000 Cr (realistic).
- **SAM:** ₹1,75,000 Cr (70% of conservative TAM).
- **SOM 3yr / 5yr:** ₹15,800 Cr / ₹24,800 Cr.
- **SOM-implied revenue CAGR:** 33.8% (3yr) / 30.3% (5yr) — **formal handoff to Stage 11.**
- **Runway class:** MASSIVE.

  1.07x → **reasonable**, not inflated.

**Valuation implication line:** At **33.8%** revenue CAGR implied by SOM (3yr), with margin

(FY26 provisional EPS ₹33.5, CMP ₹1,280, MNCL Institutional Equity Research, 26 May 2026 —
PEG ≈ 38.8 ÷ 47-49 ≈ 0.79-0.81, i.e. sub-1 on this SOM-implied growth path, though this compares
an EBITDA/PAT-driven earnings-growth estimate against a P/E multiple and should be read as
directional, not a precise PEG).

## FLAGS

- Entero books revenue as principal/full distribution value, not a thin margin fee — TAM must
  be sized on a gross-distribution-value basis (as done here); using a margin-pool framing would
  understate TAM by roughly an order of magnitude.
- Management's FY23 $33.2bn / ₹2,67,000 Cr TAM claim is 3+ years stale as of this run; treated
  as STALE and cross-checked (not solely relied upon) against fresher FY25 IPM and medical-device
