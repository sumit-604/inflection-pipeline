## SECTION 1: MARKET DEFINITION

### 1A. Precise boundaries

**Product scope (two sub-markets, sized separately, then summed):**
- **Optical Fibre Cables (OFC)** — standard telecom-grade single-mode fibre cable.
  Excludes next-generation fibre types named in the AR as demand drivers (G.657.A2
  for drones/AI, Hollow Core Fibre, Multicore Fibre) — no evidence in the extracted
  AR that BCL manufactures these; excludes high-count Rollable Ribbon cable, the
  format the AR names as capturing most hyperscale data-centre demand (AR p.41,
  no BCL product match found).
- **Copper Telecommunication Cables + Structured Copper LAN Cables** — cable only.
  Excludes connectors, patch panels, racks, active networking gear, and installation
  labour, which typically make up the majority of a "structured cabling" project's
  value (B04 unit economics: "km of cable", not the full solution stack).
- Specialty cables: immaterial share, not separately sized (data insufficient).

**Geographic scope:**
- OFC — **global**. Justified because 74.3% of BCL's FY26 OFC revenue (₹78.49 Cr
  of ₹105.65 Cr) is export (AR p.41, Directors' Report). Specific export
  destination countries: NOT FOUND in extracts.
- Copper/LAN — **India only**. No export evidence for this segment anywhere in
  the extracted AR.

**Customer scope:** telecom operators (BSNL, private telcos), government/BharatNet
EPC contractors, enterprise/institutional LAN cabling buyers. Excludes hyperscale
cloud/data-centre direct accounts and defence-classified programmes — the AR names
these as global growth drivers but gives no evidence BCL supplies them (AR p.20-21,
p.41).

**Channel scope:** direct B2B / tender-based. No retail or distributor network
evidenced (unlike Polycab/Finolex's consumer wire businesses). One customer
carries ~45% of company revenue (B04 context) — a channel concentration, not a
market-size fact, held for the SAM filter below.

**Price segment:** mid-market. The AR's own risk section states cable-industry
competition is "cut-throat," with "players offering highly advantageous price
levels... bringing margins under huge pressure" (AR p.42-43) — BCL is not
premium-positioned.

**Explicit exclusions:** power cables, building wire, consumer/retail electrical
cable (not BCL products per B04); connectors/active LAN hardware; next-gen fibre
types; hyperscale/AI-cluster and defence fibre applications (cited by
management as market colour, not evidenced as BCL products).

## SECTION 2: TAM ESTIMATION, MULTIPLE METHODS

*(FX used throughout: ₹95.68/USD, 18-Aug-2026, Federal Reserve H.10 release —
close to run date.)*

### Method 1 — Top-down

**OFC, India:** India OFC Cables Market, USD 0.55 Bn (2025) → USD 1.03 Bn (2031),
CAGR 11.03% (Research & Markets, "India Optical Fiber Cables Market Size &
Forecast to 2031"). = **₹5,262 Cr (2025)**, growing to ₹9,855 Cr by 2031.
A second report (Allied Market Research, base year 2018, USD 461.6M → USD 1.66Bn
by 2026, CAGR 17.2%) implies **₹15,883 Cr (2026)** — **STALE**: methodology
traces to a 2018-2019 vintage report, pre-dates the demand collapse the AR itself
describes for FY26 ("deep global dearth... historically low levels," AR p.40).
Used for direction only, not the headline number, per the staleness rule.

**OFC, global:** conservative estimate USD 14.22 Bn (2026, Mordor Intelligence)
= **₹1,36,060 Cr**. Higher estimates in the same search basket range to USD
15.7 Bn (GMInsights) = ₹1,50,220 Cr; one outlier (USD 89.16 Bn, Research and
Markets) is excluded as a scope mismatch (almost certainly the broader "fiber
optics" market including equipment/systems, not cable). Conservative bias
applied per instruction: **₹1,36,060 Cr used as the TAM anchor.**

**Copper/LAN cable, India:** India Structured Cabling Market, USD 739.9M (2024)
→ USD 1,505.2M (2030), CAGR 12.8% (PS Market Research). Rolled forward to 2026:
≈USD 941.6M. This figure covers the full solution (cable + connectors + racks +
installation labour). Applying an industry rule-of-thumb cable-content share of
35% (assumption, not sourced to a specific report — flagged) gives a cable-only
estimate of ≈USD 329.6M = **₹3,154 Cr (2026)**.

**Top-down subtotal (conservative): ₹1,36,060 + ₹3,154 = ₹1,39,214 Cr.**

### Method 2 — Bottom-up (OFC only; LAN cable bottom-up not attempted — no
reliable India-wide unit-price benchmark found)

India OFC demand, AR-cited: 4.7M F-km in Q1 CY2026 (AR p.21). Annualised
(×4, single-quarter run-rate, flagged as an approximation — no full-year
figure given): ≈18.8M F-km/year.

Implied global blended ASP: global OFC TAM (₹1,36,060 Cr) ÷ annualised global
demand (136.70M FKM Q1 2026 × 4 = 546.8M FKM/year) ≈ ₹2,489/F-km ($26/F-km).
Applied to India volume: 18.8M F-km × ₹2,489 ≈ **₹46,790 Cr.**

**Divergence flagged, not averaged.** This is ~9x the top-down India OFC
figure (₹5,262 Cr). Reasoning for the gap: the AR's own risk section describes
India cable pricing as "cut-throat," with players undercutting on price — a
global blended ASP (weighted toward high-price North American hyperscale/BEAD
demand, per AR p.21) very likely overstates India's realised price per F-km.
**Conservative bias applied: the top-down ₹5,262 Cr figure is retained as the
India-OFC component; the bottom-up figure is treated as a directional upper
bound only, not used in the headline TAM.**

### Method 3 — Peer aggregation (partial)

Named domestic OFC competitors: HFCL (#1 by share, order book ₹21,206 Cr,
FY26), Sterlite Technologies (STL, FY26 revenue +18% YoY, absolute segment
figure not resolved in search), Vindhya Telelinks (BCL's own merger partner),
Finolex Cables, Birla Cable itself. Granular OFC-segment-only revenue for STL
and Finolex was not resolved by search (see Searches Skipped) — this method is
directional (competitor count, concentration) rather than a sized estimate.
One source (registrationwala.com) cited "Birla Cable — 20% domestic OFC market
share, 200,000 km manufactured" — **flagged low-confidence / likely unreliable

below every other source found, and inconsistent with HFCL's order book alone.
Unorganised-sector estimate for wires & cables generally: 26-40% depending on
source (search-derived, general cable industry, not OFC/LAN-specific) —
directional only.

### Method 4 — Import substitution / government programme-driven

BharatNet Phase 3: launched 24-May-2026, government-cited investment ₹1.39-1.5
lakh Cr (Cabinet approval Aug-2023 figure ₹1.39L Cr; press coverage at May-2026
launch cites ₹1.5L Cr), targeting ~6.25 lakh villages by 2028. Applying an
assumed cable-content share of 15-20% of total project cost (rest is civil
works, trenching, electronics, O&M — assumption, not independently sourced)
implies a cumulative cable-demand pool of roughly ₹21,000-30,000 Cr over the
programme's multi-year life, or ≈₹7,000-10,000 Cr/year if spread over three
years. This is **larger than the entire top-down India-OFC TAM estimate
(₹5,262 Cr)** used above — a second signal (alongside the bottom-up
divergence in Method 2) that the top-down India OFC figure may understate the
true market, or that BharatNet Phase 3's cable-content share is assumed too
high. Held as upside colour, not blended into the conservative headline number.

| 2. Caps realistic reach near current production ceiling. Retain 85% | **₹6,061 Cr** |

**SAM ≈ ₹6,060 Cr, or 4.35% of conservative TAM.**

### 3B. SOM at 3 and 5 years

Current BCL revenue in the two relevant lines (OFC ₹105.65 Cr + Copper
Telecom/LAN ₹663.13 Cr = ₹768.78 Cr, FY26, AR p.41) ÷ SAM ₹6,060 Cr =
**current SAM share ≈ 12.69%.**

Share-gain rule applied: BCL has zero capacity-expansion capex in flight
(B07: capex_embedded_growth_pct 0.03, "effectively no capacity-expansion
capex"), no evidenced competitor exit or acquisition, and is itself the
target of a dissolution scheme (not an aggressor in the market). This rules
out the "aggressive" (3-5pp) and ">5pp" bands. The **normal band (1-2pp over
3 years)** applies, and the low end of that band is used given the capacity
constraint:

- **3-year share: 12.69% + 1pp = 13.69% → SOM 3yr = 13.69% × ₹6,060 Cr = ₹830 Cr**
- **5-year share: 12.69% + 2pp = 14.69% → SOM 5yr = 14.69% × ₹6,060 Cr = ₹890 Cr**

**Implied revenue CAGR** (arithmetic, from current revenue ₹771.11 Cr, AR
p.75):
- 3yr: (830/771.11)^(1/3) − 1 = **2.5%**
- 5yr: (890/771.11)^(1/5) − 1 = **2.9%**

**Cross-check against B07:** B07's capex-embedded-growth figure is 3%
(capex_embedded_growth_pct: 0.03). The SOM-implied CAGR derived here
independently (2.5-2.9%) lands almost exactly on that figure — a genuine,
mechanically independent convergence between two different stages' methods,
not a circular calculation. This raises confidence in both numbers.

**SOM credibility check — order book:** BCL's order book was ₹24 Cr as of
31-Dec-25 (CARE). Against FY26 revenue of ₹771.11 Cr, that is ≈11 days of
run-rate — thin. Note: an unrelated search result attributes a ₹7,124.51 Cr
order book to "Birla Cable Limited" as of Sep-2024 (CARE PR, Nov-2024); this
figure is 9.2x BCL's own FY26 revenue and is far more consistent with Vindhya
Telelinks' scale (a larger telecom EPC/OFC entity) than with BCL standalone.
**Treated as a likely source/entity-attribution error in the search summary
and disregarded**; the task's directly-cited ₹24 Cr (CARE, 31-Dec-25) is used
as the reliable anchor. A thin snapshot order book is less alarming for the
LAN cable line, which the AR frames as transactional/repeat-order business
rather than backlog-based (AR p.41-42), but it does undercut near-term
confidence in the OFC recovery the AR flags as starting "since late March
2026" (AR p.42).

### 3C. Capacity cross-check

Installed OFC capacity: ~60 lakh F-km/year (6.0M F-km/year), CARE Nov-2024 —
though the same source elsewhere cites total capacity at ~3.6M F-km, an
internal inconsistency in the sourced figure, noted rather than resolved.
LAN/copper cable capacity: NOT FOUND in extracts.

B07 states zero capacity-expansion capex is in flight (₹3.95 lakh of
outstanding capital commitments, zero CWIP — AR p.104, p.116). The SOM
trajectory derived above requires only 2.5-2.9% CAGR — a magnitude consistent
with utilisation-rate improvement and price/mix effects within existing
capacity, not new capacity.

**Capacity check: sufficient.** Existing single-plant capacity can plausibly
carry the SOM revenue path without further capex, which is internally
consistent with B07's near-zero embedded-growth capex figure. Neither side
looks like the optimistic one here — both point to a low-single-digit growth
ceiling absent a capacity decision BCL is not currently making.

## SECTION 5: SUMMARY & RUNWAY

### 5A. Funnel

TAM (conservative) ₹1,39,210 Cr → TAM (realistic) ₹1,53,690 Cr
→ SAM ₹6,060 Cr (4.35% of conservative TAM)
→ SOM 3yr ₹830 Cr (13.69% of SAM) → SOM 5yr ₹890 Cr (14.69% of SAM)

### 5B. Runway assessment

- **Revenue headroom:** SAM ÷ current revenue = ₹6,060 Cr ÷ ₹771.11 Cr = **7.86x**
- **TAM growth rate:** ~10.5% blended (dominated ~98% by global OFC TAM at
  9.8-11.0%, with India LAN cable at 12.8%)
- **Company CAGR vs TAM, FY26 actual:** total company revenue grew 16.54%
  YoY (AR p.20), **faster than blended TAM growth** — but the composition is
  mixed: LAN/copper grew 28.71% YoY (₹515.21 Cr → ₹663.13 Cr, gaining share
  in a ~12.8%-growing market), while OFC **declined** 8.25% YoY (₹115.16 Cr →
  ₹105.65 Cr, losing share against a market the AR itself describes as
  troughing then only beginning recovery "since late March 2026"). Net: BCL
  is gaining share in LAN cable, losing share in OFC, in the one year of
  data available.
- **Years to saturate SAM:** at the SOM-implied growth rate (2.5-2.9%
  CAGR, the realistic forward path given the capacity constraint), ≈75-80
  years — SAM is not remotely close to saturation at BCL's realistic capture
  rate. At the unsustainably-fast FY26 actual growth rate (16.54%, driven by
  one segment's one-year surge), ≈13-14 years.

### 5C. Runway classification: **GOOD**

Headroom (7.86x) and TAM growth (~10.5%) sit in the GOOD band. This is not
upgraded to STRONG/MASSIVE because the SOM math (Section 3B/3C) already
prices in the binding constraint: zero capacity-expansion capex, a single
plant, and a ₹24 Cr order book. The opportunity is real and larger than
current revenue; BCL's realistic capture rate of it is slow.

### 5D. SAM expansion levers actually being pursued

The only lever management is actually executing is **not organic capacity
expansion** (FY26 capex was ₹1.91 Cr, "for further capacity
expansion/augmentation" per the Directors' Report, but trivial at scale, and
B07 independently confirms zero CWIP / no expansion capex in flight — the two
statements read as inconsistent, and the near-zero CWIP is the more decisive
data point). The lever actually in motion is the **Scheme of Amalgamation
into Vindhya Telelinks**, whose stated rationale explicitly targets SAM
expansion at the combined-entity level: "a unified and stronger balance sheet
will enhance the Transferee Company's pre-qualification credentials...to bid
for and execute increasingly capital-intensive, multi-state infrastructure
projects" (AR p.26-27). No revenue-synergy figure is quantified by
management — NOT FOUND. This lever expands SAM for the **combined VTL
entity**, not for BCL as a standalone investment, once the scheme takes
effect.

### 5E. Final output card

- TAM (conservative / realistic): ₹1,39,210 Cr / ₹1,53,690 Cr
- SAM: ₹6,060 Cr (4.35% of TAM)
- SOM 3yr / 5yr: ₹830 Cr / ₹890 Cr
- SOM-implied revenue CAGR: 2.5% (3yr) / 2.9% (5yr)
- Runway class: GOOD (headroom-driven; execution-constrained)

**Valuation implication line:** "At 2.5-2.9% revenue CAGR implied by SOM,
with margin trajectory of NOT FOUND (outside Stage 9 scope — no forward
margin assumption was injected into this stage), the earnings growth
embedded here is NOT FOUND, which stage 11 should assess against the current
valuation of NOT FOUND x P/E (not injected here)." The revenue-CAGR half of
this line is a firm, mechanically-derived handoff; the margin/earnings/P/E
half is intentionally left for Stage 11, which holds the Section 1B
exit-multiple authority and the margin trajectory inputs this stage does not
have.
