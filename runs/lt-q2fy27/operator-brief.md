### 1. NARRATIVE

1. L&T builds large projects under fixed-price contracts: plants, metros, refineries, power lines. It also owns two listed IT firms and a listed lender.
2. Q2 orders hit a record Rs 1,47,665 Cr, 28% above a year ago. One segment did it. Energy Conventional orders doubled. All other segments together fell 10.6%.
3. The Middle East gave 51% of Q2 orders. It now holds 40% of the Rs 8,53,514 Cr order book. The book covers 2.84 years of sales.
4. Revenue rose 16% to Rs 78,750 Cr. Operating profit did not follow. Operating EBITDA rose 0.6%, and the margin fell from 10.0% to 8.7%.
5. Big new jobs book no margin until they pass a set stage of work. So a burst of new orders holds margin down first. Indian water projects also carry cost overruns.
6. Owners' profit rose 33% to Rs 5,202 Cr. Other income gave 71% of the pre-tax gain. A Rs 337 Cr deferred tax credit cut the tax rate to 22.6%.
7. Lower interest cost gave 22% of the gain. Most of it came from the Hyderabad Metro leaving the accounts after 30 April. The parent's own debt is also lower than a year ago (A5 item N1).
8. The segment margins include some other income. Management said so on the call. So the 7.0% core margin and the 7.8% FY27 target do not show pure contract profit.
9. Working capital fell to a record 1.7% of sales. Customer advances on the new Gulf orders likely explain much of it. Management expects the ratio to rise again.
10. Cash before working capital fell 3.6% in H1, excluding the lender. Advances and sale proceeds built about 77% of the rise in net cash.
11. Management held its FY27 guidance: orders and revenue up 10-12%, core margin 7.8%. Held guidance after a 22% rise in H1 orders implies flat H2 orders.
12. Watch the Q3 FY27 operating margin against a year ago. A gap of 30 bps or less supports the higher reading below. A gap of 100 bps or more supports the lower one.
