## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

### 2A. Demand environment

The peer set shows a genuinely mixed, segment-dependent demand picture, not
a single narrative. Commodity legacy lines are soft: Tata Chemicals'
global soda ash market is oversupplied, with Chinese export prices down
54-58% since Q2 FY23 and "no clear evidence of demand erosion" language
repeated across all four calls despite persistent oversupply; DMCC's
European specialty chemicals demand has structurally shrunk (management,
Nov-2025 call: "I do not think the European chemical industry will
revive... under current policy scenario"), being slowly replaced by
Latin America and China. Newer, technology-linked lines show strength:
Tanfac's solar-grade DHF is 80-85% pre-contracted for years out, and its
R-32 refrigerant capacity is 65-70% booked before commissioning, both
riding India's solar and refrigerant-quota buildout. Indo Borax's own
demand narrative (steel-sector pull, ~8% CAGR boric acid TAM, DOT
micronutrient growth) sits in neither bucket — no peer discusses boric
acid demand at all, so this cannot be tested directly. The one relevant
observation: DMCC's boron business is described as comparatively
demand-stable ("boric acid is sidelined normally," i.e. relatively
insulated) while its non-boric borax/glass-frit demand saw a temporary
glut from an unrelated gas-price shock to Morbi ceramics customers
(May-2026 call, p.4). This is weak, indirect support for the idea that
boric-acid-linked demand (steel/refractory, per Indo Borax) is more
stable than adjacent borate/glass demand, but it is DMCC's own framing,
not independent confirmation of Indo Borax's specific TAM claim.

### 2B. Pricing and input costs

This is the strongest cross-read finding of the stage. Three peers
(DMCC, Tanfac, Tata Chemicals), each exposed to a different raw material
(sulfur, sulfur again, and limestone/HFO/coal respectively), all report
Middle-East-geopolitical-driven cost inflation compressing margins in the
same FY26-FY27 window, each describing a similar mechanic: dollar input
cost spikes, a stated pass-through lag (30-45 days for DMCC and Tanfac),
and management assurance that the increase is "being passed on" over time
rather than permanently eaten. This directly and materially supports
Indo Borax's own "geopolitical conditions" framing for its margin
compression (Claim 4 above) as a real, external, sector-wide phenomenon
rather than a company-specific excuse — a meaningfully different read
than Stage 5 could support on its own (Stage 5 classified it
EXTERNAL-BLAME, unverified).

### 2C. Capex cycle

Mixed, and the split matters. Tata Chemicals and Tanfac are both
aggressively expanding into higher-value, technology-linked downstream
products (Tanfac: Rs 495 cr R-32 project plus a further Rs 1,500-1,700 cr
over four years, funded via a Rs 250 cr QIP plus promoter preferential
allotment; Tata Chemicals: Rs 1,000 cr+ India capacity program across
salt/silica/bicarb/dense-ash, Kenya electric calciner, the Novabay
Singapore bicarb acquisition, funded via Rs 1,500 cr NCDs). DMCC — the
peer with the closest raw-material overlap to Indo Borax (Turkish boron
ore) — is the one outlier: management stated explicitly, twice, "we are
not currently looking at any large CAPEX... it is better to be cautious
under such an uncertain scenario" (Nov-2025 call, p.7) and confirmed only
~Rs 10 cr of approved capex for the year (May-2026 call). Indo Borax's own
boron oxide expansion (4,000 MTPA) puts it in the aggressive-expander
camp alongside Tanfac and Tata Chemicals, not the cautious camp with its
closest boron-supply-chain comparator. This divergence is a genuine,
testable signal, developed further in Part 5.

### 2D. Competitive mentions

No peer names Indo Borax, or gives an unmistakable description of it,
anywhere across the 12 transcripts. DMCC is the one name-adjacent
competitor by product category (boron chemicals) but never references
Indo Borax directly. This is itself informative given Indo Borax's own
call names zero competitors against a 50% market-share claim (Stage 5,
Section 3A) — a peer set this size, drawn precisely for triangulation,
turned up exactly one other domestic boron-chemicals producer (DMCC) and
zero direct confirmation or denial of Indo Borax's specific standing.

### 2E. Risks peers discuss that Indo Borax's own call does not

- **Undisclosed comparable ore-supply disruption window.** DMCC's H1 FY26
  boron-ore disruption (distributor change, 90-100 day lead times, full
  stock-out until November 2025, industry-wide impact per DMCC's own
  framing) covers the same period Indo Borax's Q4 & FY26 call (02-Jun-2026)
  discusses — yet that call never mentions a comparable ore-supply event,
  despite raw-material risk being the single most-repeated topic on the
  call (5+ separate analyst questions per Stage 5). Either Indo Borax's
  supplier/distributor relationship differs materially from DMCC's and
  avoided the disruption, or a disclosure gap exists. This is a concrete,
  checkable item for the operator's verification list: did Indo Borax's
  own ore deliveries slow in H1 FY26/CY2025, and if not, why not, given a
  peer sourcing from the same country reports an industry-wide event?
- **Working-capital mechanics tied to the ore-supply shift.** DMCC
  disclosed a specific mechanism — moving from India-stock credit terms to
  pay-advance-then-wait-90-120-days import terms — that materially
  expanded its working capital and short-term borrowing (May-2026 call,
  p.3). Indo Borax's own call, per Stage 5, does not discuss its ore
  inventory or payment terms at all ("ore inventory of 4-5 months"
  claimed in the injected CONTEXT was explicitly NOT FOUND in the
  transcript). If Indo Borax imports on similar terms, a comparable
  working-capital exposure exists and is undisclosed.
- **FX/hedging discipline.** Tata Chemicals and Tanfac both describe
  active, quantified hedging practices on dollar-denominated inputs (gas,
  HFO cover through October, coal contracts). Indo Borax's own call
  discusses de-risking qualitatively only ("successful in the past few
  months," unquantified) with no stated hedging policy on its
  dollar-denominated ore purchases — a lower level of disclosed financial
  risk management than two of its peers show on comparable exposures.

**The single most consequential finding (not quite a contradiction, but
close):** DMCC's own H1 FY26 boron-ore supply crisis from the same
Turkish-import structure Indo Borax depends on is real, dated, and
described by DMCC itself as affecting "supply to India in general" — yet
Indo Borax's own concall for the overlapping period never mentions a
comparable disruption. This is not a stated contradiction of anything
Indo Borax said (Indo Borax made no claim of smooth ore supply), but it is
a live, checkable gap: either Indo Borax's supply chain genuinely differs
from DMCC's, or a material event went undisclosed on the one call this
run holds.

**The single strongest independent confirmation:** three peers exposed to
three different commodities (sulfur x2, limestone/HFO) all report the
same Middle-East-geopolitical margin compression mechanic in the same
window Indo Borax reports its own margin compression. This rehabilitates
management's external-blame framing as evidence-based rather than
unverified excuse-making — a materially different read than Stage 5 could
reach in isolation.

**Overall: the peer set COMPLICATES the main company's narrative, in both
directions.** It supplies real, independent corroboration for the two
claims that matter most to the mental model's dominant risk variable
(ore concentration and its effect on margins), rehabilitating management's
otherwise unverified excuse-making on margin compression. At the same
time, it surfaces an undisclosed comparable risk (the ore-supply
disruption gap) and a disclosure-quality gap (FX/hedging practice, thinner
than two peers show) that the main company's own call does not address.
On the product-specific claims that matter most to the growth thesis
(TAM, market share, boron oxide economics, DOT market, the P&G
relationship), the peer set is silent by construction — no true boric-acid
peer exists in this transcript set — and those six claims remain exactly
as unverified as before this stage, resting entirely on management's own
word.

## PART 5: CROSS-PEER HYPOTHESIS

Every commodity-adjacent Indian chemical peer in this set — regardless of
underlying chemistry (soda ash, sulfuric acid, boron chemicals,
fluorochemicals) — is running the same two-part playbook simultaneously
in FY26-FY27: (1) attribute near-term margin compression to Middle-East-
linked geopolitical raw-material and freight inflation, with an explicit
pass-through lag, and (2) use that same disrupted window to justify large
forward capex into higher-value, technology-linked downstream products
(Tanfac's R-32/solar-grade DHF, Tata Chemicals' premium bicarb/prebiotics/
electric-calciner soda ash, Indo Borax's own boron oxide), funded through
external capital raises (QIPs, NCDs, preferential allotments) rather than
internal accruals. DMCC is the one clear outlier: it is the single peer
whose raw-material exposure most closely overlaps Indo Borax's (Turkish
boron ore), and it is the one peer explicitly declining large capex,
citing the very raw-material uncertainty its commodity peers are treating
as a reason to accelerate spending elsewhere. This is a specific,
testable divergence: if DMCC's caution reflects real information about
boron-ore-supply durability rather than a company-specific balance-sheet
constraint, then Indo Borax's own boron oxide capex commitment is a
higher-risk bet, made into a supply environment its closest comparator
judges too uncertain to fund, than Indo Borax's own transcript
acknowledges. Testable signal: watch whether DMCC resumes large capex on
its boron line inside the same window Indo Borax commits construction
capital to Dahej/boron oxide — a DMCC capex freeze continuing past that
point would sharpen the divergence into a real red flag.
