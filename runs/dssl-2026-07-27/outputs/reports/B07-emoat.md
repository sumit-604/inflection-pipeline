### 2C. Growth embedded in capex — arithmetic shown, then flagged as not decision-useful for this business model

Mechanical application of the standard formula (capex under execution × historical fixed-asset turnover = implied incremental revenue):

- Capex under execution (FY26 incremental PPE+ROU build): Rs148cr (Rs158cr FY26 − Rs10cr FY25, per 2A, 📄).
- Historical fixed-asset turnover, using the last "normal" pre-buildout year (FY24): Revenue Rs1,024cr ÷ (PPE Rs3cr + ROU Rs2cr + CWIP Rs0.3cr = Rs5.3cr) ≈ **193x** (Inv. Pres. p.41-42, 📄).
- Implied incremental revenue: Rs148cr × 193x ≈ Rs28,564cr, i.e. ~2,006% above FY26 revenue (Rs1,424cr).

**This number is not credible and is discarded.** DSSL's pre-FY25 fixed-asset base was structurally tiny because the legacy EPC/reseller model required almost no owned capital (hardware was bought and resold, not held) — so its historical FAT ratio is an artefact of a capex-light model, not a base rate applicable to the new capex-backed annuity model. Applying it here manufactures an absurd result; the standard 2C multiplier is **methodologically mismatched** for this business and is not used to populate the scorecard.

**Decision-relevant cross-check instead**: the disclosed order book of Rs2,964cr (as at 30-May-2026, Jun 2026 call) is 2.08x FY26 revenue of Rs1,424cr — i.e. order-book-implied growth of roughly +108% above current revenue, executable over an average 18-24 months per management (Jun 2026 call, Gunit Singh Q&A), though individual annuity contracts run out to 5 years. This is the more meaningful "embedded growth" figure and is what is carried in the YAML `capex_embedded_growth_pct` field is set to 0 with this note, per the instruction not to force a misleading number — see `flags`.

### FAMILY B — SUPPLY CHAIN

**B1 (backward integration and RM security) — Weak**. **B2 (qualification lock-in) — Strong**. Evidence: CMMI Maturity Level 5, ISO 9001:2015, ISO 20000-1:2018, ISO 27001:2022 certifications held (📄, AR PDF p.4). Management explicitly frames this as the moat: "these pre-qualifications enable us to bid against companies which are significantly larger than us" (🎙️, Feb 2026 call, Neeraj Dalal Q&A) and reiterated in Q4 FY26 call ("Dynacons' capabilities across the projects undertaken, with the kind of qualifications that we have, in terms of the certifications that we carry… gives us really a good confidence and higher chance in success" — Jun 2026 call, Akash Jain Q&A). Backed by signed 5-year contracts requiring these qualifications: RBI enterprise application platform (~Rs249cr), RBI private cloud (Rs750.82cr), Punjab & Sind Bank (Rs108.88cr), J&K Bank DaaS (Rs74.99cr), NABARD CBaaS for 38 banks live (📄, Inv. Pres. p.7). Strength: Strong. Evidence type: 📄 (dominant).

**B3 (supply chain network effect) — NO EVIDENCE FOUND**. The presentation's "Dynacons sits at the centre of the ecosystem" slide (Inv. Pres. p.19-20) is a value-proposition/marketing framing, not a measured multi-sided network effect — no participant count, no growth metric, no hub-and-spoke transaction data disclosed. Deliberately not force-fit per the completionist guard.

## SECTION 4: REGULATORY & POLICY TAILWINDS (R1)

**4A. Regulatory approvals in pipeline**: NOT FOUND — no DSSL-specific regulatory approval, license application, or registration is pending in either concall or the AR. DSSL operates as a contracted vendor to regulated entities (RBI, NABARD, PSU banks) rather than itself holding a sector license.

**4B. Government policy tailwinds**: (i) NABARD's Core Banking as a Service initiative for state/district cooperative banks — a genuine government-programme-linked revenue stream, with DSSL as an *awarded vendor*, not exclusive nationwide (📄, 38 banks live, Feb 2026 call; more banks — Haryana ~20, Telangana — joining, Jun 2026 call). (ii) Data-localisation and AI-workload-driven public-sector/BFSI IT spending, cited across both calls and the presentation as a broad tailwind (🎙️, e.g. "AI workloads, automation and data localization requirements" — Feb 2026 call). (iii) Government directive pushing BFSI to reassess cybersecurity posture post recent AI-tooling developments, generating pipeline (🎙️, Jun 2026 call, Dev Shah Q&A: "the government has already put its directives to the entire BFSI industry"). None of these are exclusive to DSSL — competitors (large IT majors, the newly listed "Unified Data," and others) can and do bid for the same government-linked tenders; DSSL's edge is the pre-qualification/certification moat (B2), not the policy itself.

**4C. Regulatory moat assessment**: this is an *active, currently monetising* tailwind (not merely emerging) — RBI and NABARD contracts are signed and executing today. But because the benefit is sector-wide and shared with every qualified competitor, it is rated Moderate rather than Strong: sustainability depends on DSSL continuing to win its historical ~30% share of the bidding pipeline (🎙️, Jun 2026 call, Akash Jain Q&A) rather than on any DSSL-exclusive regulatory position.

- **12-24 months**: Execution of the Rs2,964cr order book (average 18-24 month cycle per management) into FY27/FY28 revenue, including go-live progress on the RBI Rs750.82cr private-cloud win. Managed-services share likely continuing to climb past 23-25% of revenue.
- **24-36 months**: Possible first APAC entry if the "phased" geographic plan moves from concept to signed local contracts. ROCE steady-state validation of the opex/annuity capex model at scale (management's own stated aim, unresolved as of run date — Feb 2026 call, Ankush Agrawal Q&A).
- **3-5 years**: Data Centre & Cloud potentially crossing a majority share of revenue mix if the 52% historical CAGR persists (🔍 inference, not guided); possible first acquisition if the inorganic framework (Inv. Pres. p.28) is acted upon.

### 6B. Risks to each top-scoring emerging moat

- **B2 (qualification lock-in)**: newer/larger entrants (e.g. the newly listed "Unified Data," cited by an analyst as running 15-20% margins vs DSSL's ~10%) could match certification requirements and out-price DSSL on qualified tenders. Early warning: DSSL's historical ~30% win rate on its bidding pipeline declining, or order-book growth decelerating.
- **C1 (customer ecosystem)**: the "stickiness" narrative is not yet reflected in an improving concentration metric (C2 found no evidence of improvement) and the underlying annuity capex is working-capital/ balance-sheet intensive. Early warning: ROCE compression, or a reversal of the cross-sell anecdote (net customer losses in the top-10 cohort).
- **F2 (execution moat)**: the Q4 FY26 margin miss is the immediate live risk — if "short-term cost pressures" persist into Q1/Q2 FY27 rather than normalising as management claims, the execution-moat thesis weakens materially. Early warning: further sequential margin compression, or revenue growth staying near FY26's 12.4% rather than reverting to the prior ~20-27% CAGR.
- **G2 (WC improvement)**: the net-17-day working-capital story depends entirely on continued OEM/distributor credit generosity; FY26 FCF was already negative (B01 FLAG-CASH) on the capex build. Early warning: net WC days rising back toward the FY21-23 range (38-50 days), or CFO/PAT ratio deteriorating further from FY26's already-weak trend (CFO down 30.1% YoY per B01).
- **H2 (strategic partnerships)**: Cygeniq and the OEM partner-tier network are non-exclusive; competitors can and do hold equivalent OEM partnerships (Platinum/Gold tiers are available to any qualifying integrator). Early warning: 12-18 months passing with no named Cygeniq contract disclosed.
- **R1 (regulatory/policy tailwind)**: shared by every qualified competitor bidding into the same RBI/NABARD/PSU tender pool; a larger, better-capitalised player could out-invest DSSL specifically on AI-infrastructure tenders. Early warning: declining win rate on AI-ready/large-ticket tenders specifically (as distinct from the aggregate ~30% pipeline win rate).
