# FTTCP Draft: Aurum Proptech Ltd (AURUM)

Company: Aurum Proptech Ltd. Ticker: AURUM. CMP ₹240. Run date: 2026-07-14.
First workup, not a refresh (no prior companies/AURUM.md, manifest run_type
full). Ran with actual concall transcripts, so NOT no-concall mode: Q2, Q3 and
Q4 FY26 calls read directly, with the Q1 FY26 call as the earliest promise
anchor.

One paragraph on what this is. Aurum is the old Majesco listing rebuilt into a
proptech platform. Selling the Majesco US software business dropped sales from
₹988 Cr in FY19 to ₹10 Cr in FY20, and everything since is a new company grown
from that shell to ₹381 Cr of revenue by FY26. It runs three parts: Rental
(NestAway managed lettings and HelloWorld co living beds), Distribution (Aurum
Analytica developer leads, the Sell.do sales CRM, and PropTiger primary
brokerage), and Capital (a nascent SM-REIT and asset management arm). FY26 was
the first full year profit, ₹1.9 Cr, with two profitable quarters in a row in
Q3 and Q4. The whole forward call is whether that profit is the start of a
durable inflection or a thin crossing of zero helped by one time items.

## THE CATALYST STORY IN PLAIN WORDS

**Revenue.** What fires it: the PropTiger brokerage now consolidated and posting
record ₹42.8 Cr quarterly gross commission, Sell.do at 10,378 active licenses,
Analytica lead volumes up 93% YoY, and the SM-REIT license giving a new product
line. What confirms it: organic revenue, stripped of the PropTiger
consolidation, still growing 20%+ in the next two quarters. What kills it: the
H2 FY26 jump proving to be mostly the consolidation with flat organic growth
underneath.

**Margin.** What fires it: operating leverage as fixed platform and technology
cost spreads over a bigger revenue base, the mix shift toward high margin SaaS
and lead generation, HelloWorld already at adjusted EBITDA breakeven by March,
and Rental guided to profitability in FY27. What confirms it: two quarters of
adjusted EBITDA margin above 10% with no one time other income doing the work.
What kills it: the profit sliding back below zero once the lease reversals and
asset sale gains stop.

**Cash conversion.** What fires it: nothing documented. There is no factoring
facility, no stated debtor day target, and no management commentary on
collections across four calls. The debt free move helps the debt line but not
the collection cycle. This is a NONE FOUND on the collection specific catalyst,
and the absence is itself the signal. What would change it: a factoring or
receivables discounting facility announced with an amount and a date, or an
FY26 annual report showing the over one year buckets falling.

**ROCE.** What fires it: margin expansion on a capital base that does not grow
proportionally, plus the debt free balance sheet lowering capital employed at
the margin. What holds it down: the ₹174 Cr goodwill denominator and the two
negative net worth subsidiaries. What would change it: a clean FY27 operating
ROCE in the low to mid teens excluding surplus cash and goodwill, or a goodwill
impairment that right sizes the base.

Composite score +3 out of 8, which is DEEP WATCH: revenue firing, margin
starting, cash and ROCE both stagnant. The Kernex cap is a genuine close call on
cash, because collections are the one thing going the wrong way with no
documented catalyst, but it does not change the answer, since the cap holds at
DEEP WATCH and the score already sits there. The disposition caps at PROCEED
WITH CAVEATS because the cash conversion determination is INDETERMINATE with the
credit rating rationale named as the missing evidence. The whole call turns on
one print: whether the next quarter shows an operating profit once one time
other income and asset sale gains are stripped out. If it does, this is an early
inflection worth sizing on strict entry discipline; if it does not, the first
profit year was a thin crossing of zero.
