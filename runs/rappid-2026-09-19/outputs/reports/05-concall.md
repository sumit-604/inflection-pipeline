### 1C Trigger evolution and timeline slippages

**Timeline slippages:**
- **US export customer (data-center valves).** Feb-2025: "prototype valve
  developed for a U.S. client." Jul-2025: "successful development and
  qualification of a prototype... potential high-volume export orders in
  the pipeline." Oct-2025: UL approval achieved, valves said to have
  "undergone extensive testing" and to be exported "exclusively... from

  and has "not scouted for any, in India, for data centers" (p.28) even
  though the company's own Oct-2025 update announced a dedicated
  6,000 sq ft export unit (16-Oct-2025 GBU p.4) and the FY26 AR (signed
  29-Aug-2026, three months later) states the company "intend[s] to
  pursue emerging opportunities in data-centre cooling" (AR p.24-ish,
  Outlook section). The export/data-centre narrative moves between "not
  focusing on it," "not scouted for it," and "intend to pursue it" inside a
  three-month window, without any filing naming the shift.
- **Backward integration / foundry acquisition.** Feb-2025: "advanced talks
  to acquire a Pune-based foundry." 21-Mar-2026 board meeting: the Rs 400
  Lakh acquisition object formally proposed for redirection to working
  capital. The postal ballot's own Explanatory Statement gives the reason:
  the company "was evaluating various options... however, the Company
  unable to tap the right opportunity or find suitable companies which
  will complement its growth" (24-Mar-2026 notice p.18). This is a stated
  reason, not silence, but it appears only in the postal ballot notice, not
  in any interim Reg 30 update between Feb-2025 and Mar-2026. On the
  Jun-2026 call the CMD separately deflects: "we are unable to have any
  inorganic... synergy or joint venture with a foundry" (p.22-23), a
  softer, less specific framing than the notice's own admission.
- **Mechanism correction: this was a postal ballot, not a classic EGM.**
  The 27-May-2026 results PDF's Annexure A note says the IPO-object
  variation was approved "pursuant to the resolution passed at the EGM
  held on April 17, 2026." The primary process documents show this is
  imprecise on two counts. First, the approval mechanism was a Postal
  Ballot conducted entirely by remote e-voting (Section 110, Companies
  Act, 2013; no physical or VC meeting vote), per the 24-Mar-2026 Postal
  Ballot Notice and its 30-Mar-2026 Corrigendum, which itself records that
  "certain details relating to an Extraordinary General Meeting (EGM)
  were incorrectly mentioned" in the original notice. Second, the actual
  e-voting window was revised by that corrigendum from 13-15 April to
  31-March-29-April 2026, and the Scrutinizer's Report and voting results
  were dated 30-Apr-2026, not 17-Apr-2026 (30-Apr-2026 scrutinizer
  filing). The company's own later results filing therefore carries a
  compounded date-and-mechanism error, on top of an already-corrected
  notice. Round 1 transcribed the "17-Apr-2026 EGM" language without
  flagging this; corrected here (see CORRECTIONS section).
- **Capex completion.** Feb-2025: "capex completion by June 2025 likely to
  boost production scalability." 09-Mar-2026 deck: a VMC (2 units) and
  4 automated test benches described as ordered/being installed in two
  phases (10-Mar and 25-Mar 2026). Jun-2026 concall: describes acquiring
  "two VMC machines" and "5 new test benches" as if a new, separate
  purchase, "likely to be delivered within the next 25 days" (p.4, p.6),
  also referenced once as "7 new machines" (p.8) worth Rs 1.25 Cr (p.13).
  The 09/10-Jul-2026 Q1 FY27 update again announces fresh purchase
  orders for 2 VMCs and 6 hydraulic testing benches. The FY26 AR (signed
  29-Aug-2026), the most recent and most formal disclosure, describes
  only "planned deployment of four automated test benches and a new VMC
  machine" (singular) (AR text, Outlook/Strengthening Manufacturing
  Capabilities section) - a lower count than the 5-6 benches and 2 VMCs
  disclosed as already ordered in the Jun/Jul-2026 filings, with no filing
  reconciling the counts or the repeated "within 25 days" delivery
  language across three separate disclosure dates (Mar, Jun, Jul 2026).
  Separately, Rs 364.5 Lakh of the original plant-and-machinery IPO object
  remained unutilized through FY26 (audited capex per the cash flow
  statement was Rs 179.4 Lakh for the full year, 27-May-2026 results p.10)

### 2B Excuse pattern analysis

Classification: **external-blame-heavy**. Every miss traces to the same
external factor (non-ferrous commodity price volatility, once compounded by
a geopolitical LPG-supply disruption), stated consistently and with
specific, plausible numbers (NAB price ~1,250 to ~1,650 Rs/kg). Management
credits itself for the response ("conscious management decision," "we
firmly believe protecting shareholders' capital... is more important than
pursuing growth at any cost") while attributing the shortfall entirely to
the outside event. The one filed exception is the acquisition abandonment,
where the postal ballot notice itself states plainly that the company
"unable to tap the right opportunity" - closer to an honest admission than
external blame, though it appears only in a regulatory notice, not in an
investor-facing update. No instance across the sources read this stage
where management, on the call itself, says a forecast was wrong; the
closest is "if I overcommit and underperform, that's a problem" (p.14), a
hedge offered defensively under analyst pressure, not a retrospective
admission about the Feb-2025 target.

Also newly noted this round: the FY26 results note attributes the
working-capital build to "business expansion and execution of higher order
volumes during the year" (27-May-2026 results p.9), a form of self-credit
that sits awkwardly against the same document's own +2.1% revenue growth
and the CMD's on-call admission that Rs 10 Cr of finished goods sat
undispatched in March pending a price revision (Concall p.5-6). The
written explanation and the spoken explanation for the same balance-sheet
movement are not the same story.

Pattern check: hard topics (the capacity/utilization math, the abandoned
acquisition, the missed guidance, the capex-figure confusion) surfaced only
under analyst pressure at the one available call, not proactively in the
Reg 30 updates between the miss (H2 FY26, visible by the 16-Oct-2025
order-book print) and the eventual explanation (01-Jun-2026 concall,
roughly seven months later).

## SECTION 3: COMPETITIVE INTELLIGENCE

### 3A Competitor commentary and credibility check

Management names China as the direct international competitor for export
orders ("directly challenged with China... a very big challenge for the
Indian manufacturers," p.5) and claims domestic defence-valve competition is
self-limiting: "people who have entered last year, whatever orders we have
lost, they're not bidding for any more orders," citing the working-capital
and type-approval burden as the deterrent (p.28). This is plausible given
the certification list (API, DNV, IBR, ClassNK, UL) but is an unverifiable,
self-serving claim with no named competitor or evidence. Two further
unverifiable, self-promotional claims from the same call, not previously
flagged: "we are supplying all the valves through both the shipyards for
FSS" (p.15, HSL and L&T; the filed FSS orders are via BHEL, Shree
Refrigerations, and Muller-BBM, not a claim of supplying "all" valves), and
"90% capacities [of our non-ferrous foundries] are dedicated to Rappid
valves" (p.21), neither independently verifiable from any filing read this
stage. All flagged to peer_questions for a check against KSB (the scaled
incumbent) and Atam Valves (a size-matched comp).

### 3B Industry and market intelligence

- NAB (nickel-aluminium-bronze) price: ~Rs 1,250/kg to ~Rs 1,650/kg, a ~32%
  rise, cited as the proximate cause of the H2 FY26 slowdown (p.6); a
  further 25-30% broad metals rise (including stainless/carbon steel) cited
  as extending into the new financial year (p.8).
- No raw-material hedging is in place. Asked directly how the company could
  be better protected against the raw-material swings that drove the FY26
  miss, the CMD states hedging "requires huge working capital... blocking
  15-20 crores, that's not something I'm looking at" (p.26). The mitigation
  used instead is paying suppliers 50% advances to lock prices (p.16, p.21).
- PSU tenders carry no price-escalation clause; private-sector orders can
  carry one, negotiated bilaterally, though only on later shipsets within
  a multi-ship order (the first three shipsets confirm existing rates, the
  remaining shipsets due 6-12 months later get the clause) (p.5-7, p.16-17).
  An analyst (Deepanshu Bhatia) flagged an apparent within-call conflict on
  this point ("you already mentioned that we don't have a price escalation
  clause," p.16), which the CMD resolved by clarifying the PSU/private
  distinction rather than contradicting himself; a minor case of an analyst
  needing the nuance repeated, not management reversing position.
- India "building more than 20 warships at a time" (p.21), an unverified
  scale claim; the FSS programme specifically involves 5 ships, with 2
  offloaded from Hindustan Shipyard to L&T Shipbuilding (p.19).
- Valve content per vessel: management estimates 2-3% of a ~Rs 4,000 Cr
  vessel cost but states this "keeps changing" and that shipyards
  themselves (L&T, HSL, Cochin) could not give a consistent answer (p.20).
- Repeat-customer rate stated at 75% (p.17), with all PSU relationships
  characterized as effectively repeat by default absent blacklisting.
