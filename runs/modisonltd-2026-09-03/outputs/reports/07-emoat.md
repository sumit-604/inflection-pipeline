**Test against the R&D decline.** Every FY26-dated "new product" item above
comes from the Annexure F Technology Absorption disclosure, the same
disclosure that says: "During the year under the review no such new or
significant efforts were made towards technology absorption" (AR p.67) and
records R&D spend falling from Rs.210.56 lakh (FY25) to Rs.159.11 lakh
(FY26), a 24.4% decline (AR p.68, Directors' Report). Read together, the
bullet list of "developments" is best read as routine, low-capital
product-engineering work, not a step-up in innovation capacity — the
company's own filing explicitly disclaims a "significant" technology
effort in the same year it lists these items. This directly contradicts
the investor-presentation claim that Modison holds "specialized technology
developed by way of inhouse research and development resulting in high
entry barriers" (Inv. Pres. slide 27, Mar-2024) — a claim never
refreshed with a matching FY26 figure and now running against a falling
R&D trend.

One nuance: within the Rs.159.11 lakh FY26 total, the *employee-cost*
component of R&D rose (Rs.37.50 lakh → Rs.42.90 lakh, +14.4%) even as the
*capital-expenditure* component fell sharply (Rs.126.13 lakh → Rs.71.47
lakh, -43.3%) (AR Note 46, p.179/243). Modison is spending marginally more
on R&D people and much less on R&D equipment. This is consistent with a
company doing incremental alloy/process tweaks rather than funding new
capability — it does not support an accelerating IP moat.

### 2C. Growth embedded in capex — arithmetic shown

Using FY26 (📄 AR):
- Revenue from Operations FY26 = Rs.71,032.89 lakh (AR Directors' Report, p.37)
- Net carrying value of Property, Plant & Equipment, Mar-2026 = Rs.9,148.93 lakh (AR Note 3, p.157)
- Implied fixed-asset turnover = 71,032.89 / 9,148.93 = **7.77x**
- Capex currently under execution (CWIP not yet capitalised, Mar-2026) = Rs.349.35 lakh (AR Note 4)
- Implied incremental revenue = 349.35 × 7.77 = **Rs.2,714 lakh (~Rs.27.1 cr)**
- As % of current revenue (71,032.89 lakh): **3.82%**

This is a modest number. It confirms the picture from 2A: FY26 capex is
maintenance/modernisation-scale, not a step-change capacity programme. No
large named capex programme is currently "under execution" per the filed
FY26 balance sheet.

## SECTION 3: THE 22-CATEGORY SCAN

### FAMILY A — Product & Technology

**A1. Rare manufacturing capability.** Repeated management claim across
sources: "Only company globally manufacturing electrical contact
materials and finished contacts for Low, Medium, High & Extra High

company in India across all three [LV/MV/HV]" (AGM Jul-2026). This is a
real, decades-old positioning (technology transfer from Doduco, Germany,
1983-97; HV "100% market share" claimed as a 1990s-era milestone, Inv.
Pres. slide 9) — i.e. it is the **existing** moat Gate 0 already scored,
not something newly forming. The only FY26-incremental evidence is
continued SF6/HV-related Plant & Machinery capex (Rs.3.65 cr addition, AR
Note 3) sustaining rather than expanding the capability. Strength: WEAK
(for emerging purposes). Evidence: 🎙️ (uniqueness claim never
independently verified against a competitor list; B05's peer_questions
flag this exact gap). Time to materialise: N/A, already established.

**A2. Patent and IP pipeline.** NO EVIDENCE FOUND. No patent filing,
patent grant, or licensing-revenue line appears anywhere in the AR or
transcripts. The AR affirmatively states technology absorption saw "no
new or significant efforts" in FY26 (AR p.67) against a 24.4% YoY decline
in total R&D spend (Rs.210.56 lakh → Rs.159.11 lakh, AR p.68). This is a
documented negative, not an absence of data — the filed disclosure itself
undercuts the IP-moat narrative implied elsewhere in investor materials.

**A3. Process innovation.** Kaizen activity "up ~50% this year, 150

claim of "3x in 2019-20 to >5x" (🎙️ Concall Oct-2022, p.11) — but this
directly contradicts B01's documented finding that working-capital days
*rose* ~50 days over FY24-26 and that inventory turnover computed from
filed financials shows deterioration in the same window (B01 flags). A
process-innovation claim contradicted by the company's own filed
cash-flow and balance-sheet trend cannot be scored above Weak. Strength:
WEAK. Evidence: 🎙️, contradicted by 📄.

**A4. Product platform / modular architecture.** "~260 new products
developed last year" (🎙️ AGM) with no SKU-level detail, no revenue
linkage, and no cross-check possible (single-segment disclosure). Weak.

### FAMILY B — Supply Chain

**B1. Backward integration and RM security.** This category needs the
most rigorous test in the whole scan. Investor materials present
"Modison Copper Pvt Ltd, Silvassa" as one of two "Modison facilities" and
describe the group as "fully backward integrated set up from refining to
end product" (Inv. Pres. slide 18). The filed AR tells a different story:
**MCPL is not owned by Modison Limited at all.** "The Company does not
hold any equity share capital, directly or indirectly, in Modison Copper
Private Limited. MCPL is, however, controlled by certain promoters of the
Company and their immediate relatives, who collectively hold more than
98% of its equity share capital" (AR p.26-27, RPT Explanatory Statement).
MCPL in turn holds 1.26% of Modison Limited's own equity (AR p.27) — the
ownership runs the *opposite* direction from what "backward integration"
implies. The FY26 RPT filing further discloses MCPL depends on Modison for
**48.76% of its own FY26 turnover** (Rs.16,406.88 lakh MCPL turnover; the
proposed Rs.8,000 lakh RPT ceiling = 48.76% of that) (AR p.27-28) — a
figure that directly conflicts with the AGM's claim that Modison is "only
~25%" of MCPL's sales (B05 red flag, HIGH severity, unresolved). Genuine
captive backward integration exists only for Modison's own 1975-vintage
silver/copper refining (in-house, no RM security *contract* disclosed for
the MCPL relationship — supply runs on ordinary related-party purchase
terms, renewed annually via shareholder RPT approval, not a multi-year
supply agreement). Net finding: **B1 scores 0 for emerging purposes.**
The MCPL relationship is a governance/RPT-dependency item, not a
documented moat, and the investor-presentation framing of it as "Modison
facilities" is optics that the filed AR does not support.

**B2. Qualification lock-in.** Existing, not emerging: ISO 9001/14001,
OHSAS 18001/ISO 45001, DNV certification (Inv. Pres. slide 20) all
predate this cycle. No new certification (e.g. AS9100, NADCAP — relevant
given the Aerospace/Defence customer claims) is disclosed in FY26. NO NEW
EVIDENCE FOUND this year.
