Sical logistics turnaround leans on land sale

Sical is a seventy year old Chennai logistics operator that came out of NCLT insolvency under new owners, the professional Pristine Malwa sponsor group, whose principals carry an ex-CONCOR and IRTS background and are distinct from the extinguished Coffee Day / Siddhartha chain that ran the company into default. The business runs three lines by the investor deck: mining logistics, mostly overburden removal and coal transport for PSU miners, at roughly 43% of revenue; terminals, a container freight station and rail-linked multimodal parks, at roughly 36%; and warehousing and 3PL at roughly 21%. That split is deck-only and unaudited. The audited accounts still report a single business segment, so the mix the thesis leans on is not auditor assured. Market cap is about Rs 850 Cr at a share price of Rs 107.

FY26 was the first reported profit, Rs 45 Cr, after five straight years of insolvency-era losses. Control changed hands, three new independent directors joined, a first FII position of 3.09% appeared from zero, and consolidated debt to equity fell from 4.1x to 1.6x. A single mining contract was secured, SECL Porda-Chimtapani, worth Rs 3,422 Cr over about 140 months, and the Chennai rail-linked terminal commenced commercial operations in December 2025 on its stated timeline. The ultimate holding company, Pristine Logistics, has filed a confidential DRHP for an IPO of about Rs 1,200 Cr, which would put the group under IPO-grade governance.

The reported profit is substantially one item. Rs 55.59 Cr of it is a one-time land-sale gain. Strip the exceptional line and consolidated pre-exceptional PBT was Rs 1.50 Cr, and the standalone parent ran a pre-exceptional loss of Rs 16.03 Cr. The mechanical Gate 0 screen scores AVOID at 14 out of 160, driven by the FY20 to FY25 loss years, a median ROCE near 2.8%, and cumulative operating cash flow that fell short of reported profit across the window. Read the FY26 print as a first data point, not a proven operating recovery.

The cash picture points two ways and resolves to neither. At the listed parent, CARO cash losses widened 45.6% year on year to Rs 30.37 Cr even as the P&L loss narrated an improvement, net trade receivables grew 63.7% against 0.3% revenue growth, and the unbilled-receivables loss allowance sat frozen at Rs 28.58 Cr for two years while the gross book grew 45% to 49%. Against that, consolidated operating cash flow reported Rs 52.29 Cr, but it is struck before interest and lands near zero after about Rs 47 Cr of finance cost, and the deleveraging was funded by one-time asset sales plus the rights issue rather than by operations. The ICRA rating is withdrawn at [ICRA]D, a default grade with no live working-capital rationale to read. The cash-conversion call is INDETERMINATE, with the cash-loss reconciliation and the unbilled-allowance rationale named as the missing evidence.

Management credibility grades C. One AR-guided commitment, the Chennai terminal, was delivered on time. Against it sit an unexplained pivot from a Rs 250 Cr QIP to a smaller Rs 93 Cr rights issue, an unconfirmed status on the 25% public-float requirement, a profit narrative that did not foreground the pre-exceptional loss, and a CFO who resigned in July 2026 to a subsidiary role with no parent-level succession named. About 51% of consolidated equity is non-controlling interest, because the entity holding the profitable terminals business is only 53.6% owned, so the best asset sits one level down and the listed parent carries thin to negative net worth.

Promoter risk trips a hard line. The promoter-entity share pledge is 56.75% as of June 2026 on media-tier sourcing, up from 50.34% at creation, past the 40% deal-breaker. The counterweight is real: professional non-family sponsors, the fresh FII entry, the board refresh, a promoter that funded MPS compliance by forgoing its own rights entitlement, and the parent DRHP. One overlap to watch is a sitting audit-committee member, Amit Kumar, who also holds a board seat at Kanpur Logistics Park, the related-party lender behind the year's largest new loan of Rs 130 Cr at 12.5%.

The peers complicate the story rather than confirm it. Six transcripts were used, ALLCARGO current and GDL five years stale, and none covers the mining and coal-logistics segment that is 43% of revenue, so the most central growth claim cannot be triangulated. ALLCARGO's own macro citations show FY26 demand decelerating, e-way bill growth easing from the low twenties to 13%, against Sical's secular-boom framing. GDL's CFS experience shows diesel escalation clauses failing to protect margin in practice, against Sical's implicit pass-through assumption on a segment that is 36% of revenue.

The runway is large but shallow at the point Sical touches it. Conservative TAM is about Rs 94,200 Cr and SAM about Rs 64,000 Cr, classed MASSIVE, but the classification is dominated by warehousing, where Sical holds roughly 0.6% of SAM. The SOM-implied revenue CAGRs of 34% to 38% are a low-base artifact and should be read as a ceiling, not a forecast. Management's own combined CFS and warehousing market claim of about Rs 1,46,500 Cr runs about 3.8x the conservative estimate for those same segments, which reads as inflated. The one contracted number that holds up is the Rs 4,600 Cr mining order book, of which about Rs 3,422 Cr sits in the single SECL contract, roughly 74% concentration in one counterparty.
