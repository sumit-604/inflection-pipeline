## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

### 2A. Demand environment
Sugar: uniform peer view that FY26 closed with the tightest domestic sugar inventory in years (Balrampur: "lowest stock level which I remember seeing in my recent living memory," May 2026 call; Triveni: closing stock estimates repeatedly revised down toward 4-4.6 million tonnes, "the lowest level since September 2017," Jul 2026 call), driving a genuine step-change in sugar prices (~₹41/kg in Feb 2026 to ~₹45-49/kg by Aug 2026 per both Balrampur and Triveni). Ethanol/distillery demand: uniformly described as capacity-constrained by policy and OMC offtake, not by end demand (see Claim 4/5). Spirits/IMFL: both Globus and Piccadily report the premium end of the market (P&A, luxury single malt) growing far faster than mass-market/country liquor (Globus P&A +55% H1 FY26, +58% ex-Delhi FY26; Piccadily branded Alco-Bev +42% FY26, guiding 60-70% FY27) — a structural premiumization trend, consistent with (not contradicting) IGL's stated IMFL growth ambitions in direction, even though the specific UP figures could not be triangulated (Claims 6-7).

### 2B. Pricing and input costs
The dominant, repeated grievance across both sugar/distillery peers is a **three-year freeze in B-heavy and juice-route ethanol prices** despite rising cane costs (Balrampur, every one of its three calls; Triveni, references the same freeze and calls for "a rational policy... to match cane price"). This is a direct, structural margin headwind for cane/molasses-route ethanol producers, distinct from (but related to) the OMC-offtake pressure in Claim 5. On the input side: maize prices fell through H2 FY26 (Triveni: ₹22.50/kg April-May 2025 → ₹20/kg by Feb 2026; Globus: 15% YoY, 4% QoQ reduction reported Jan 2026) before firming again into FY27 on lower Bihar/UP crop yields (Triveni, Jul 2026 call). Both spirits peers (Globus, Piccadily) report new packaging/glass cost inflation of 10-17% attributed to the Iran geopolitical crisis (Globus: "cost-push of glass... in mid-teens," Jul 2026; Piccadily: "price of glass, packaging materials have gone up by 40% to 50%" per one analyst framing, management response confirming pressure but citing hedges, Apr 2026 call) — a live, cross-sector cost risk not raised by the sugar/ethanol peers (who do not buy glass at comparable scale).

### 2C. Capex cycle
This is **not** an industry-wide capacity race in ethanol/ENA — quite the opposite. Every peer that discusses forward capex explicitly rules out further ethanol/ENA capacity: Globus (Jul 2026): "we don't have any plans for capex in increasing ENA/ethanol capacity... I do not see any significant change in capacity as part of our FY29 strategy at least." Triveni describes distillery growth for FY27 as coming from de-bottlenecking and reactivating an idle acquired unit (Shamli), explicitly "without incurring any new CapExes for greenfield or brownfield expansion" (Jun 2026 call). Instead, capital is visibly rotating toward differentiated, higher-margin verticals adjacent to (but outside) core ethanol: Balrampur into PLA bioplastics (₹3,080 crore capex, a "parallel Balrampur" per management, Aug 2026 call); Triveni into defence/gearbox manufacturing (now demerged into Triveni Power Transmission); Globus and Piccadily into premium/luxury IMFL brand-building (malt whiskey inventory, new states, new SKUs) funded by manufacturing/bulk cash flow. This is a **lone-capacity-holder, no-industry-race** situation for ethanol specifically, with the broader capital story being a sector-wide pivot toward differentiated products.

### 2D. Competitive mentions
No peer transcript mentions India Glycols Ltd, IGL, or any unmistakable description of the company by name across all 13 transcripts. This absence is itself notable given IGL's overlapping ethanol and IMFL/spirits exposure — peers discuss competitors in gearboxes (RENK), PLA (TotalEnergies Corbion, Zhejiang), and IMFL (implicitly, via UP market-share framing) but never IGL specifically.

### 2E. Risks peers discuss that IGL's stage 5 handoff does not surface
- **Standalone-distillery interest-moratorium cliff**: Triveni (Jul 2026 call) flags that PSU-bank interest subventions for many standalone ethanol distilleries expire in FY27, which "comes under huge question" for their survival — a sector solvency risk that could reshape competitive supply, not mentioned in the B05 handoff.
- **Judicial/regulatory disruption to OMC tenders**: the BPCL vs. Karnataka HC Supreme Court stay froze ethanol allocation cycles mid-year (Triveni, Jun/Jul 2026 calls) — a live legal-process risk to near-term ethanol offtake, distinct from the general overcapacity narrative.
- **Rice-vs-maize tender mandates**: MoPNG's ESY 2025-26 condition requiring a minimum 40% FCI-rice share in grain ethanol tenders (Triveni, Nov 2025 call) directly compresses maize-route margins and is a policy lever not referenced in the handoff.
- **Iran-crisis packaging/glass inflation**: a live, quantified (10-17%) cross-sector cost risk for any IMFL-exposed name (Globus, Piccadily, Jul-Aug 2026 calls), not raised in the B05 handoff's silence list.
- **El Niño / monsoon risk to next season's cane crop**: repeatedly flagged by both Balrampur and Triveni (Aug 2026 calls) as the single largest swing factor for FY27 sugar/cane economics — general sector risk, not IGL-specific but relevant context.

## PART 4: TRIANGULATION SUMMARY

- Claims verified: 2 of 9 (Claim 4 — industry ethanol overcapacity; Claim 2 — current grain feedstock mix magnitude, with historical baseline unverifiable)
- Claims partially verified: 4 of 9 (Claim 1 — 20% blending ahead of schedule; Claim 3 — DDGS margin support; Claim 5 — OMC allocation decline direction; Claim 9 — molasses-reservation policy)
- Claims contradicted: 1 of 9 (Claim 7 — UP market volume range, pending scope clarification) — **this is a priority item for synthesis**

**Single most consequential contradiction**: Globus states the UP "Regular and Others" market size as ~95 lakh to 1 crore cases/month, consistently across four consecutive quarters (Nov 2025-Jul 2026) — roughly 4x the top end of the 15-25 lakh cases/month range implied by IGL's stage-5 claim. Either the two figures describe different market scopes (total category vs. a narrower IMFL-only or brand-specific slice), or one of the two companies' framing of "the UP market" needs correction. This should go to synthesis as a flag requiring the operator to pin down exactly which segment IGL's cited growth figure describes before it is used in any valuation or thesis narrative.

**Single strongest independent confirmation**: Three independent peers (Balrampur, Triveni, Globus), spanning both the sugar-distillery and grain-distillery sub-sectors, each state — unprompted by each other and across six separate quarterly calls — that national ethanol production capacity materially exceeds blending demand, with Triveni supplying the sharpest numbers (~2,000-2,300 crore litres capacity vs. ~1,100-1,300 crore litres of OMC/private demand). This is the best-triangulated finding in the entire peer set and directly corroborates the core margin/demand headwind implicit in IGL's Bio-Fuels segment.

**Overall assessment**: the peer set **complicates** the main company's narrative rather than cleanly supporting or undercutting it. On the ethanol/Bio-Fuels side, peers strongly corroborate the two most important structural facts IGL's management stayed largely silent on per the B05 flag — industry-wide overcapacity and OMC-offtake pressure — which sharpens rather than resolves the concern about IGL's EBP allocation decline. On the IMFL/spirits side, peers corroborate the general direction of a premiumization-led growth story (which supports IGL's stated ambitions) but cannot triangulate the specific UP growth-rate figures IGL cited, and one peer's consistently stated market-size figure sits in outright tension with the range implied by IGL's claim. Symmetrically: nothing in the peer set contradicts IGL's ethanol/feedstock-mix framing on direction, and the DDGS margin-support claim is directionally corroborated by the one peer that discusses it. The peer evidence is therefore mixed rather than one-sided — it neither vindicates nor demolishes IGL's framing, but it does surface two priority items (Claim 5 OMC-decline magnitude, Claim 7 UP market-size discrepancy) that the operator should treat as open questions rather than settled facts.

## PART 5: CROSS-PEER HYPOTHESIS

Across all four peers, every one that discusses forward capital allocation is simultaneously (a) explicitly declining to add further ethanol/ENA distillation capacity despite currently running well above the reported industry-average utilisation, and (b) redirecting free cash flow generated by that same bulk/commodity ethanol business into a differentiated, higher-margin, branded vertical outside core ethanol economics — Balrampur into PLA bioplastics, Triveni into defence/gearbox manufacturing (now a separately listed entity) plus branded country liquor, Globus and Piccadily into premium/luxury IMFL brand-building funded by their bulk-alcohol cash generation. None of the four peers frames this as a sector-wide pattern; each describes it purely as its own company-specific diversification story.

**Hypothesis**: the Indian ethanol-and-allied-distillery sector is functioning as a **cash-generation engine for capital reallocation away from itself** — commodity ethanol/ENA capacity is treated by every well-run operator in this peer set as a maxed-out, policy-capped utility business whose main strategic value in FY26-27 is throwing off cash to fund unrelated, differentiated, pricing-power businesses (bioplastics, defence gearboxes, or premium spirits) rather than being reinvested in more ethanol capacity itself. This is testable: track FY27-28 capex disclosures across these four peers (and IGL) and confirm that ethanol/ENA capacity additions stay flat or near-zero industry-wide while non-ethanol/differentiated segment capex continues rising as a share of total capex. If IGL's own capital allocation (Bio-Fuels vs. Spirits vs. bio-glycols/specialty chemicals) diverges from this pattern — for example, if IGL is still adding ethanol capacity while peers are not — that divergence itself would be a distinct, material flag for the thesis.
