## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

### 2A Demand environment
- Order books are at records and pipelines are large: DP Rs 2,654 Cr including negotiated, plus about Rs 2,000 Cr of expected FY27 inflow (DP Q1 FY27, Rangarajan and CFO, p.3-4); Apollo Rs 1,765 Cr and "an all-time high" (Apollo Q2 FY27, Sai Kumar, p.4).
- The gate is timing. DP says negotiated orders took "additional 6 months" and the programme stretched over 1.5 years (DP Q1 FY27, p.6). Apollo says QRSAM, MIGM and Pinaka orders are still expected (Apollo Q2 FY27, p.7-8). Apollo's book grew by about Rs 35 Cr in the latest quarter (Apollo Q2 FY27, p.17).
- Guidance drift is a sector pattern. Apollo cut its growth guide 45-50% to 40-45% to 35-45% (see P3). DP held 20-25% but printed +17% in Q1 FY27 (DP Q1 FY27, p.4).
- Astra sits at the slow end of the three on FY26 growth: Astra +10.67% standalone (B05) against DP +31% (DP Q4 FY26, p.5) and Apollo standalone about +36% (analyst figure, Apollo Q4 FY26, p.17). Caveat: DP FY26 includes the one-off Rs 180 Cr strategic project (DP Q2 FY26, p.3), and Apollo's consolidated growth includes acquisitions.
- Read: consensus on demand strength, consensus on timing delay. This matches Astra's framing. It does not explain why Astra grew slower.

### 2B Pricing and input costs
- No peer discusses realisation inflation or raw material cost pass-through.
- Margin story is mix, not price: DP takes some contracts "at lower cost and lower margin ... to build capability" (DP Q4 FY26, Rangarajan, p.7); Apollo's COGS improves only with production volume (Apollo Q3 FY26, p.10); DP says the Rs 180 Cr project cut Q2 EBITDA to 22% and that without it the quarter would have been inside the 35-40% guide (DP Q2 FY26, Rangarajan reply to Hardik Rawat, p.11).
- Apollo names semiconductors as the primary raw material (Apollo Q4 FY26, p.14). This matches Astra's foreign-content theme but gives no price or forex data.
- Read: peers support the idea that margin follows mix. They give no support for or against Astra's gross margin level as a price effect.

### 2C Capex cycle
- Both peers are expanding, ahead of orders: DP at least Rs 150-200 Cr over two years (DP Q1 FY27, p.16); Apollo to about 12 times capacity, with 2,000 more acres planned (Apollo Q2 FY27, p.7 and p.16). Both are building in-house test and qualification capacity (Apollo Q2 FY27, p.18).
- Situation: a capacity race among the private defence electronics peers, with Astra the lightest planned spender (Rs 40-50 Cr a year, B05). This is not a cautious peer set with a lone expander. It is a lone light spender in an expanding set. Peers add a caution: DP is "not overspending" before contracts land (DP Q4 FY26, p.17); Apollo's expansion runs ahead of an order book that grew Rs 35 Cr last quarter.

### 2D Competitive mentions
- DP Q4 FY26: an analyst names Astra as "providing more on complete system similar to us"; Rangarajan declines: "I wouldn't like to comment on specific competition on open line" (DP Q4 FY26, Aditya and Rangarajan, p.19).
- DP Q1 FY27 (quote in full, Rangarajan, p.18): "It's not a DcPP partner here. Astra is a DcPP partner for MCA variant, not the Su30 variant as I understand ... They have an independent development team from DRDO. We are doing it in parallel ... it depends on where we can qualify our products and get a contract or we don't qualify it, and they qualify it and they get a contract." DP's Hawk-based radar is a parallel path to a radar in which Astra claims a role. This is a direct competitive overlap for Astra trigger 6.
- DP Q3 FY26 on Uttam (programme, Astra supplies AAAU): "we don't have a role to play in that ... we're looking at partnership with DRDO or LRDE to see whether we work with our radar as against working only Uttam as a second alternative" (DP Q3 FY26, Rangarajan, p.20).
- Apollo Q1 FY27: an analyst states that HAL "have given orders for Uttam AESA components of Uttam radar to Astra Microwave" (Apollo Q1 FY27, Amit Dixit, p.5). This is an analyst remark, not Apollo management. Apollo's own HAL content is small: "some INR100 crores, INR150 crores order" with another Rs 150 Cr expected (Apollo Q1 FY27, Reddy, p.6).

### 2E Risks peers discuss that Astra does not
- Order allocation can be restructured. MoD plans to split QRSAM first-lot missiles 50% BDL, 50% private (Apollo Q2 FY27, Reddy, p.7). Astra's QRSAM subsystem share and timing depend on this. Not in B05.
- Test and qualification capacity as a bottleneck: waits of "weeks ... months" for DRDO test facilities (Apollo Q2 FY27, p.18). Astra attributes slippage to inspection and supply chain, not to test queues.
- Customer inspection staff availability (DP Q1 FY27, p.5).
- Programme-level stretch: a whole programme stretched over 1.5 years, delaying signed-off negotiated orders (DP Q1 FY27, p.6).
- Operating cash flow stays weak until production orders dominate: Apollo "from FY29 onwards" (Apollo Q2 FY27, p.14); DP says development contracts delay collection (DP Q3 FY26, p.10). Astra said it expected to maintain its CFO trend (B05).
- Foreign OEM tie-ups compete for Indian orders (DP Q2 FY26, Rangarajan, p.13).
- Private-sector entry and state ToT programmes: MoD offers ToT on "eight, nine programs" for private firms (Apollo Q2 FY27, Reddy, p.15); Apollo puts the entry barrier at "four, five years", while DP warns that "competition is nimble and capable and they will catch up" (DP Q3 FY26, p.17).
- Governance flags at Apollo (shareholders defeated a corporate guarantee resolution, Apollo Q3 FY26, p.13-14; promoter pledge near 39%, Apollo Q4 FY26, p.11). Not an Astra risk; listed only because it shows what institutions test at peers.
