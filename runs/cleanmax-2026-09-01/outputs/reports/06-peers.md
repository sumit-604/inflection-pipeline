## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

**2A Demand environment.** The peer set is broadly constructive on demand this period, consistent with CleanMax's framing, but for reasons that complicate rather than simply confirm it. KPI Green explicitly undershot its own 20-30% revenue-growth expectation in Q1 FY27 (16% actual), attributed to institutional-client billing delays and "geopolitical conditions," while reaffirming a 40-50% CAGR target with visible hedging language. Oriana had a "mixed" FY26 with acknowledged shortfalls against its own PAT-doubling guidance, blamed on commodity/currency swings, an EPC-tender deferral (Actis), and state-election-related administrative delays, while insisting the underlying demand pipeline (order book ~Rs6,800-7,000 Cr) remains healthy. ACME and Adani Green both report unambiguously strong execution and demand, with ACME noting increased central-government urgency to clear pending state PPAs. JSW cites data centres, EVs, and induction heating as structural long-term demand drivers. No peer describes a demand contraction that would undercut CleanMax's Data & AI narrative, but equally, no peer independently confirms a hyperscaler-driven demand surge at CleanMax's scale - the two smaller-cap peers with data-centre commentary (KPI Green, Oriana) both describe it as early-stage/inbound rather than a delivered growth driver.

**2B Pricing and input costs.** This is the most consistent cross-peer signal in the corpus and one CleanMax's own calls do not surface (per B05, no call discusses commodity/currency cost inflation as a margin driver). Both KPI Green and Oriana independently cite the same input-cost basket moving sharply against them in the trailing year: Oriana quantifies silver +130-180%, copper +30-40%, steel +25-30%, aluminium +30-40%, polysilicon and glass +30-35%, crude oil +88% (Apr'25-Mar'26), and INR depreciation from ~84.5 to ~95/USD - a genuinely severe, broad-based cost shock. KPI Green independently corroborates the same theme with less precision ("geopolitical conditions" cited repeatedly as the reason for margin/execution misses across KPI Green and its EPC subsidiary KP Energy, whose EBITDA margin fell from 22% to 12%). This convergent, quantified peer signal that FY26/early-FY27 was a genuinely difficult input-cost environment for the sector, and CleanMax's own EBITDA margin trend over the same period (83% -> 83.5% -> 84%, per B05) shows mild improvement rather than pressure, is worth flagging: either CleanMax's C&I contract structure (long-tenor, presumably with cost pass-through or fixed-price protections) is genuinely more insulated than peers' EPC/CPP models, or the topic has simply not come up on CleanMax's calls and should be asked directly.

**2C Capex cycle.** This is unambiguously an industry-wide capacity race, not a lone expander. KPI Green's total portfolio grew 71% YoY to 6.94 GW; Oriana carries a 2.5 GWp solar pipeline plus 1,500+ MWp of BESS under execution; ACME Solar's total portfolio stands at ~7.4 GW including 5.18 GW of signed PPA capacity; Adani Green is targeting 5 GW/year of greenfield additions with Khavda alone approaching 10 GW operational; JSW Energy has commissioned 1.1 GW YTD in the current year alone. CleanMax's >=1.5 GW FY27 guidance sits comfortably inside this sector-wide expansion pattern rather than standing out as unusually aggressive or unusually conservative.

**2D Competitive mentions.** None. A direct name search ("CleanMax," "Clean Max," "Cleanmax") across all 19 transcripts returns zero hits. No peer names CleanMax or gives an unmistakable description of it.

**2E Risks peers raise that CleanMax's own calls do not (per B05).**
- Commodity/currency cost-inflation shock (steel, cable, silver, copper, crude, INR) as an active, quantified margin risk this period - KPI Green, Oriana. Not discussed in any CleanMax call per B05.
- Battery/BESS input-cost volatility (lithium, cell pricing) as a live tender-economics and margin risk - Oriana. CleanMax's own BESS roll-out (trigger 5) carries no equivalent input-cost caveat in B05.
- The GNA-vs-TGNA revenue-protection distinction for curtailed capacity - ACME, JSW. Not addressed in any CleanMax call per B05, despite being directly relevant to interpreting the Bikaner EBITDA impact.
- Governance and promoter-pledge concerns weighing on institutional ownership and valuation multiple - KPI Green (investor questions on pledge levels, auditor change, absence of major domestic institutions). A sector-level governance theme, not evidenced as a CleanMax-specific issue in this corpus, but worth keeping in view given CleanMax's DRHP-stage disclosure regime.
- Rooftop/distributed-solar growth (8 GW added last year, ~12 GW annualised run-rate per one analyst estimate) as a potential demand-cannibalisation vector for utility-scale and, by extension, adjacent C&I supply - raised by an Adani Green analyst; management pushed back (sees no material cannibalisation), but the question itself is not visible anywhere in CleanMax's own calls per B05.

**Single most consequential finding.** Two independent peers (ACME Solar, JSW Energy) describe a specific regulatory mechanism under which CTU-connected renewable capacity that has reached GNA (permanent, granted) connectivity status is revenue-protected against curtailment - the developer is paid per schedule regardless of actual curtailment, because payment and physical curtailment are regulatorily decoupled for must-run renewable generation. If Bikaner's 525 MW sits on GNA rather than TGNA, this peer-described framework implies CleanMax should see minimal direct EBITDA impact from the curtailment itself, which sits in tension with CleanMax's own disclosed ~Rs170 Cr full-year EBITDA impact at 70% curtailment (per B05). This is not a proven contradiction - it depends on Bikaner's actual connectivity status, which this stage cannot establish from the peer corpus - but it is a specific, checkable question that should be carried into synthesis and verification with priority, because it bears directly on whether the Bikaner miss is genuinely external/mechanical (as CleanMax's proactive, consistent disclosure pattern suggests) or reflects a curtailment exposure CleanMax has not fully explained.

**Single strongest independent confirmation.** ACME Solar's utility-scale, ex-BESS EBITDA margin runs 88-92% across three consecutive quarters (91.5% Q3 FY26, 91% Q1 FY27, 88-89% seasonally softer quarters), landing squarely inside CleanMax's cited 89-91% peer band for utility-scale/SECI-tariff developers. This is a clean, multi-quarter, like-for-like corroboration of the utility-scale side of Claim 4, from the single closest capital-structure and business-model comparator in this peer set (recently listed, similar architecture, pure IPP).

**Overall assessment.** The peer set neither straightforwardly supports nor straightforwardly undercuts CleanMax's narrative; it complicates it in specific, useful ways. It corroborates the general shape of two claims (module-cost front-loading behaviour, and Rajasthan/CTU curtailment as a genuine live sector issue this period) while leaving every specific number CleanMax has attached to those claims unverified. It independently confirms the utility-scale margin band CleanMax cites as its comparator, which strengthens confidence in that half of the business-model argument. And it surfaces two genuinely new questions CleanMax's own calls do not address: the commodity/currency cost shock that squeezed peer margins this period but does not appear to have dented CleanMax's own margin trend, and the GNA/TGNA revenue-protection mechanism that bears directly on how seriously to weight the Bikaner curtailment miss. Symmetrically: nothing in this peer set suggests CleanMax's macro theses (Data & AI demand, C&I penetration headroom) are wrong; it simply confirms that none of them can be checked against independent sources from concall material alone, and two of CleanMax's most load-bearing numbers (the 12%->14% market-share trend, and the hyperscaler 1:6 GW conversion ratio) rest entirely on management's own say-so.

## PART 5: CROSS-PEER HYPOTHESIS

Across KPI Green, Oriana, ACME Solar, and Adani Green, a consistent pattern emerges that none of them states directly: **the sector is bifurcating along the GNA/TGNA connectivity axis, and that axis - not curtailment severity per se - is becoming the primary determinant of near-term earnings volatility for renewable IPPs and C&I developers with Rajasthan/CTU exposure.** ACME and JSW both independently describe the same regulatory fact (GNA-connected capacity is revenue-protected against curtailment; TGNA-connected capacity is not), and both are now visibly changing behaviour around it: ACME reports industry-wide reluctance to commission new capacity onto TGNA status specifically because of Rajasthan curtailment ("nobody in the industry would like to commission on TGNA"), and JSW is watching its own TGNA-status Rajasthan asset slip its connectivity timeline quarter over quarter, the same pattern of repeated slippage visible in CleanMax's own Bikaner disclosures. No single peer states this as a sector-wide strategic shift, but the combined pattern - two large IPPs independently citing the same specific regulatory distinction, both currently sitting with capacity stuck on the unprotected side of that line in the same region, in the same reporting window - suggests the real, testable near-term risk for the sector is not "how much curtailment," but "how much capacity is stuck on TGNA versus GNA when curtailment hits," which is a connectivity-administration and CTU-execution-pace variable, not a demand or pricing variable. This reframes the Bikaner question: the more decision-relevant fact for CleanMax's thesis may not be the 30%->70% curtailment percentage itself, but whether Bikaner's 525 MW has (or has not) achieved GNA status, since that single fact determines whether the curtailment translates into an earnings problem at all under the regulatory framework two peers describe.
