Pattern check: management credits itself for margin gains (sourcing team, R&D, product mix) while attributing
softness to external shocks (tariffs, Middle East logistics) — a normal, largely defensible split given the
shocks are independently verifiable (US tariff policy, Red Sea/Strait of Hormuz disruption is public record).
Management does say "we made a mistake" in substance on capex deferral and mix-driven margin compression
(self-attributed, not blamed externally). It does NOT proactively flag the WC-target miss or the MNC timeline
slip; both surface only by comparing across calls, not by management naming them. Hard topics (aggressive FY28
number, margin sustainability) are engaged mostly under analyst pressure (Manish Gupta, Parth Agrawal) rather
than pre-empted in opening remarks, which lean promotional.

## SECTION 3: COMPETITIVE INTELLIGENCE FROM CONCALLS

### 3A. Competitor commentary and credibility check

Management repeatedly acknowledges "strong," "tough" competition from Chinese and other Asian lube-additive/
rubber-chemical producers expanding capacity (Q3 FY26: capacity "expected to go live in FY27"; Q4 FY26: "Strong,
very strong... they do come hard"), but never quantifies impact on price, volume, or share. This sits in tension
with the reported margin trajectory (17.4% FY26 → 24.2% Q1 FY27), which moved sharply UP over the same window in
which the competitive threat was being flagged as intensifying. Credibility check: plausible but under-evidenced;
the qualitative warning and the quantitative outcome point in opposite directions without reconciliation, which
is a gap for peer verification (stage 6) rather than an internal contradiction management can resolve alone.

### 3B. Industry and market intelligence

- US tariffs: 50% at Q2 FY26 (context) → 25% notified reduction, other half "not yet back to 18%" at Q3 FY26 →
  largely resolved ("tariff is gone") by Q4 FY26/Q1 FY27 (FACT of trajectory; VIEW on "gone").
- India-EU FTA: chemicals to 0% tariff; timing inconsistent between calls — Q3 FY26 implies already helping
  engagement, Q4 FY26 says "will come in place only by early January, hopefully" (VIEW, date ambiguous relative
  to the May 2026 call date — likely January 2027, unconfirmed in transcript).
- Raw-material/petrochemical inflation: "10% to 15%" since "pre-war era" (Q4 FY26 call, Pujan Shah exchange) —
  a specific, checkable number for peer verification.
- Middle East supply-chain disruption (Strait of Hormuz referenced): import lead times 3-4 weeks → 8-12 weeks;
  export transit to the US 25 days → 60-70 days (Q4 FY26 and Q1 FY27 calls) — FACT of operational impact, cited
  consistently across two quarters.
- Domestic lube-packaging players setting up Indian plants, framed as a raw-material demand tailwind for Yasho
  (Q2 FY26 call, context) — never mentioned again in the three graded quarters; treat as a dropped narrative
  thread, not confirmed delivered.
- Sulfur availability "a challenge" though not a "problem" (Q4 FY26 call) — one of the cheapest inputs by
  Yasho's own description, so limited inventory buffer held for it.
- Addressable market framing introduced in Q1 FY27: "$12 billion to $15 billion" global lube-additive/adjacent
  market, Yasho "aspiring to become $200-300 million" (~INR1,700-2,500 cr) — a new number, unverified against any
  third-party market-size source in the transcripts.

- No customer losses or renegotiation-driven price cuts disclosed in any of the three calls; all pricing
  commentary is about raw-material pass-through formulas, not competitive discounting.
