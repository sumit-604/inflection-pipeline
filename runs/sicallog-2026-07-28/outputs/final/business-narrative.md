Sical logistics turnaround leans on land sale

Sical is a seventy year old Chennai logistics operator that came out of NCLT insolvency under new owners, the professional Pristine Malwa sponsor group, whose principals carry an ex-CONCOR and IRTS background and are distinct from the extinguished Coffee Day and Siddhartha chain that ran the company into default. The business runs three lines by the investor deck: mining logistics, mostly overburden removal and coal transport for PSU miners, at roughly 43% of revenue; terminals, a container freight station and rail-linked multimodal parks, at roughly 36%; and warehousing and 3PL at roughly 21%. That split is deck-only and unaudited. The audited accounts still report a single business segment, so the mix the thesis leans on is not auditor assured. Market cap is about Rs 850 Cr at a share price of Rs 107.

FY26 was the first reported profit, Rs 45 Cr, after five straight years of insolvency-era losses. Control changed hands, three new independent directors joined, a first FII position of 3.09% appeared from zero, and consolidated debt to equity fell from 4.1x to 1.6x. A single mining contract was secured, SECL Porda-Chimtapani, worth Rs 3,422 Cr over about 140 months, and the Chennai rail-linked terminal commenced commercial operations in December 2025 on its stated timeline. The ultimate holding company, Pristine Logistics, has filed a confidential DRHP for an IPO of about Rs 1,200 Cr, which would put the group under IPO-grade governance.

Rs 55.59 Cr of the reported profit is a one-time land-sale gain. Strip the exceptional line and consolidated pre-exceptional PBT was Rs 1.50 Cr, and the standalone parent ran a pre-exceptional loss of Rs 16.03 Cr. The mechanical Gate 0 screen scores AVOID at 14 out of 160, driven by the FY20 to FY25 loss years, a median ROCE near 2.8%, and cumulative operating cash flow that fell short of reported profit across the window. Read the FY26 print as a first data point, not a proven operating recovery.

The cash picture points two ways and resolves to neither. CARO cash losses at the parent widened 45.6% to Rs 30.37 Cr even as the P&L loss narrated an improvement, net trade receivables grew 63.7% against 0.3% revenue growth, and the unbilled-receivables loss allowance sat frozen at Rs 28.58 Cr for two years while the gross book grew 45% to 49%. Consolidated operating cash flow reported Rs 52.29 Cr but is struck before interest and lands near zero after about Rs 47 Cr of finance cost, and the deleveraging was funded by asset sales plus the Rs 93 Cr rights issue rather than operations. The ICRA rating is withdrawn at [ICRA]D, a default grade with no live working-capital rationale. The cash-conversion call stays INDETERMINATE. Management credibility grades C: the Chennai terminal was delivered on time, against an unexplained pivot from a Rs 250 Cr QIP to the smaller rights issue, an unconfirmed public-float status, and a CFO who resigned in July 2026 to a subsidiary role with no parent-level successor named.

Promoter risk trips a hard line. The promoter-entity share pledge is 56.75% as of June 2026 on media-tier sourcing, up from 50.34% at creation, past the 40% deal-breaker. The counterweight is real: professional non-family sponsors, the fresh FII entry, the board refresh, a promoter that funded public-float compliance by forgoing its own rights entitlement, and the parent DRHP. About 51% of consolidated equity is non-controlling interest, because the entity holding the profitable terminals business is only 53.6% owned, so the best asset sits one level down and the listed parent carries thin to negative net worth.

The peers complicate the story rather than confirm it. Six transcripts were used, ALLCARGO current and GDL five years stale, and none covers the mining and coal-logistics segment that is 43% of revenue, so the most central growth claim cannot be triangulated. ALLCARGO's own macro citations show FY26 demand decelerating, e-way bill growth easing from the low twenties to 13%, against Sical's secular-boom framing. The runway is large but shallow at the point Sical touches it. Conservative TAM is about Rs 94,200 Cr, classed MASSIVE, but the classification is dominated by warehousing, where Sical holds roughly 0.6% of SAM, and management's own combined market claim of about Rs 1,46,500 Cr runs about 3.8x the conservative estimate. The one contracted number that holds is the Rs 4,600 Cr mining order book, of which about Rs 3,422 Cr sits in the single SECL contract, roughly 74% in one counterparty.

Valuation lands on AVOID. On the operator-approved destination PE, RRM 8.6x governing and additive 12.4x, applied to an illustrative one-year-forward owners' clean EPS of about Rs 3.4 for FY30, the Year-3 fair value is Rs 29 on the governing track and Rs 42 on the additive track. The primary method, a sum-of-the-parts adjusted for the 51% non-controlling interest, puts current owners' NAV near Rs 22. Every cell sits below the Rs 107 price. Expected CAGR is negative 38%, the Hurdle Ratio is STOP, and the entry zone is Rs 12 to Rs 15 with a margin-of-safety price of Rs 11.9, roughly one seventh of the current price. This is not merely a price call: the AVOID is over-determined, with the Gate 0 AVOID, the promoter CONCERN, an upside-to-downside below 2x, and the Hurdle STOP each independently barring a buy.

The devil's advocate pass leaves the AVOID standing and dents only its magnitude. The one honest weakening is the land floor: the Chennai, Ponneri, Tuticorin and Vizag parcels are carried at book value, market value is NOT FOUND, and a credible mark could lift the SOTP floor from about Rs 13 to roughly Rs 30 to Rs 45 a share. Reaching the Rs 107 price would need about Rs 680 Cr to Rs 1,270 Cr of land uplift, before the non-controlling-interest and debt haircut, which the evidence does not support. A verified land revaluation is the single lever that would most change the picture, and it is the first thing to price if the stock ever enters the zone.
