# Real tower volumes, unproven cash and Kutch

Anawil Wire and Engineering Ltd (ANAWIL). Run 2026-11-12. Phase 1, evidence only. Valuation has not run. This file carries no entry zone, no decision and no exit multiple.

## Business understanding narrative

Anawil rolls and welds thick steel plate into tubular towers, up to 140 metres tall, that hold wind turbines up; it sells no wire. A turbine cannot stand without a tower, and oversized sections cost a lot to move, so a plant near the wind states matters. In integrated supply, 64% of first half FY27 revenue, Anawil buys the plate and sells a finished tower for Rs 3.0 to 3.5 Cr. In pure conversion, 21% of revenue, the customer sends plate, flanges and paint, and Anawil charges Rs 50 to 60 lakh a tower. Conversion plus internals, 11% of revenue, adds Anawil's own ladders, platforms and cable racks for Rs 1.0 to 1.1 Cr a tower. The buyers are wind turbine makers and developers: order lines name Sany and Envision towers, eight customers sit in the book, and the top ten took 97% of FY26 revenue. There are no long term agreements, one customer holds 37% of the book, and each plant needs customer approval before it ships, which the Kutch plant still awaits. Present demand comes from installations: India added a record 6.05 GW of wind in FY26, which the MNRE monthly installed wind capacity, state-wise (Gujarat and Karnataka) signal tracks. Envision towers are 49 of the 320 in the book, worth Rs 193 Cr, and Sany towers are 111, worth Rs 67 Cr; the Envision India wind installations and tower sourcing and the Sany Renewable Energy India installations and tower sourcing signals follow them. The industry roadmap rises from 8 GW in FY27 to 13.2 GW in FY31, about 17% a year, and the Wind and RTC/FDRE tender awards (SECI, NTPC, NHPC, state agencies) signal shows awards that turn into tower orders 12 to 18 months later. Heavier towers for 5 MW turbines also carry more steel per tower. Against that, Inox Wind is building its own tower plant in South India and KP Green is entering tubular towers, both tracked under Suzlon and Inox Wind quarterly deliveries, order books, tower make-or-buy and new tower plants. Peers point to cleared sites, not fabrication, as the limit on installations, an inference that would make tower supply loose and buyer priced. No business line has a moat. Pure conversion earns a high margin, but its fee stayed flat at about Rs 58.7 lakh a tower across two order books, and a new plant near the same states can copy it. Integrated supply earns about 3.4% on the extra steel it carries, and the run did not establish whether its contracts pass steel price moves to the customer. Location, customer approvals and weld certification give partial protection at company level, and certification is table stakes. The Emerging Moat scan scored 10.8, below the lowest band at 12, with only execution and the post IPO balance sheet at moderate; Categories 21 and 22, talent asymmetry and the cannibalisation barrier, scored zero.

## The company in brief

Anawil makes towers at Koppal in Karnataka and has a second plant at Kutch in Gujarat. It moved into towers in 2023 and listed on NSE Emerge on 10 August 2026. Tower output went from 114 in FY24 to 135 in FY25 and 210 in FY26. Revenue rose from Rs 54 Cr to Rs 143 Cr over the same years. The first half of FY27 gave 107 towers, Rs 154 Cr of revenue and Rs 14.4 Cr of PAT. No annual report and no call transcript exist yet. The record rests on the Prospectus, the audited FY26 statements, the half year results, two presentations and the Crisil rating rationale.

## What is changing

Revenue per tower more than doubled, from Rs 68 lakh in FY26 to Rs 1.44 Cr in the first half of FY27, because integrated supply puts steel into the bill. EBITDA margin fell from 42.7% to 23.4%. That comparison sets a full year against a half. The first half of FY26 ran at 24.5% and the second half at 50.1%. EBITDA per tower was Rs 33.7 lakh in the first half of FY27, level with Rs 34.5 lakh in the second half of FY26. The record holds two readings of that 50.1% half. One is volume: other costs per tower fell from Rs 23.7 lakh to Rs 14.6 lakh as output rose. The other is year end timing: revenue per tower stayed flat across the two halves while profit per tower doubled, and stock and receivables were loaded in February and March 2026. Revenue and cost by contract form for each half year would separate them. The second change is Kutch, a plant rated at 192 towers a year. The Prospectus calls it commercial from March 2026, and the November release says trial runs are underway. The third change is still open. One steel price clause in the integrated supply contracts decides whether 64% of revenue is a steel spread business.

## What the numbers say

The backward scorecard reads 71 of 160: 66 of 100 on core financials and 5 of 60 on moats, with one moat confirmed. The class is AVERAGE after a one tier cut for only four years of history. Growth and returns score full marks. Revenue compounded 77% a year from FY23 to FY26, off a ramp year, and ROCE was about 27% in FY26 on the run's own computation (the Prospectus prints 23%). The cash block scored 3 of 20. FY26 capex was Rs 93 Cr, free cash flow was minus Rs 74.6 Cr, and working capital days rose from 68 to 109. The balance sheet scored 8 of 20 on the pre IPO year end. On the September 2026 balance sheet, after Rs 115 Cr of debt was repaid, it scores 15 and core rises to 73. Most depressors are historical: the ramp base, the pre IPO debt and the IPO dilution. The cash block is the one depressor not yet shown to be historical.

## Cash

FY26 operating cash flow was Rs 12.5 Cr in the audited statements and Rs 18.4 Cr in the restated ones, against PAT of about Rs 36 Cr. The gap is classification: capex advances and capital creditors sit inside operating cash on both bases. With those taken out, operating cash is Rs 28 to 31 Cr, 78% to 85% of PAT. Operating cash fell from 2.03 times PAT in FY24 to 0.79 in FY25 and 0.50 in FY26. Working capital absorbed Rs 52 Cr in FY26, most of it in February and March. Rs 30 Cr sits as advances with capital goods vendors that the filings do not name. Crisil puts gross current assets at 292 days and says the business needs long customer credit and large stock. The first half of FY27 turned the other way. Operating cash was Rs 28.3 Cr against PAT of Rs 14.4 Cr, with an Rs 18.4 Cr stock release, while payables fell Rs 20.9 Cr. Receivables ageing is clean: 98.6% was not yet due at March 2026. Debt fell from Rs 128 Cr to Rs 34 Cr after the IPO. The chairman and managing director then lent Rs 21.68 Cr at 6% to fund capex, on terms the company has not disclosed. A Rs 3.67 Cr demand on the concessional tax rate has no provision. If the rate is denied for FY24 to FY26, the exposure is about Rs 5.2 Cr, 14% of FY26 PAT.

## What management says and does

No transcript exists, so the credibility grade stays at the default C. Over three months the tracker shows 2 promises delivered, 3 partly delivered and 1 missed. A 38 tower letter of intent became an Rs 134 Cr order, 12% above the letter. EBITDA per tower rose as margin percent fell, as management said it would. Rs 115 Cr of IPO money repaid debt in 53 days. The monitoring agency still answered No to "all utilisation as per the offer document", while the results note says the money was used as raised. 182 of the 225 towers due by 31 December 2026 were still open on 31 October. Kutch is the miss: 16 towers in March 2026, then about Rs 3 Cr of Gujarat revenue in the whole first half. FY27 guidance of Rs 450 to 500 Cr revenue and Rs 55 to 60 Cr PAT needs Rs 296 to 346 Cr in the second half. That means 206 to 280 towers, against a best half of 148 and a Koppal half year nameplate of 210. The headline "EBITDA per tower" of Rs 32.82 lakh is a cash flow subtotal; reported EBITDA gives Rs 33.7 lakh. A 24 October filing says the company has no tax litigation while the tax demand stands. The promoter group company Darpan Infrastructure appears in four versions across the statements, and an Rs 97.6 lakh guarantee fee to it sits in no audited profit line. The promoter verdict is CAUTION, and the record says a heavier weighting would read CONCERN.

## What the peers say

The peer calls contradict no claim, but they complicate the picture. Inox Wind, Suzlon and KP Green confirm the October to March skew. Peer second halves run 60% to 75% of the year, so Anawil's 70% to 80% sits at the top, and its guidance needs only 66% to 69%. Steel is mostly a pass through cost in this chain. Working capital cycles of 86 to 210 days are normal, and Anawil's net cycle of about 178 days sits inside that range. Both turbine makers missed or reset plans loaded into the second half. The two tower facts in the peer set both add capacity near Anawil. Inox is building its own tower plant in South India, where Karnataka gives 98% of Anawil's first half revenue. KP Green is entering tubular towers while its plants run at 30% to 34%. Four claims have no peer test: scarce qualified suppliers, plant approval time, the share of towers turbine makers buy outside, and Envision and Sany sourcing.

## Where the market sits

The market is best counted in towers. At a flat 6.05 GW a year and 4.0 MW per tower, India needs about 1,512 towers a year. At the FY27 roadmap of 8 GW and 3.4 MW, it needs about 2,353. On the order book's average bill of Rs 1.24 Cr a tower, that is Rs 1,870 to 2,909 Cr a year. The Chinese turbine makers that Anawil serves take about a third, or Rs 960 Cr in FY27, on a second hand share estimate. The share rules give Anawil Rs 477 Cr by FY29 and Rs 646 Cr by FY31. The runway class is GOOD, with 6.7 times headroom over FY26 revenue and 2.0 times over the FY27 guidance midpoint. That midpoint, Rs 475 Cr, already matches the FY29 share figure, so guidance runs about two years ahead of it. Half the implied revenue growth is mix into integrated supply, which earns 14% to 16%, so profit will grow slower than revenue. The FY31 figure needs 522 towers. That is 85% of nameplate and above the 437 a year the plants can make if every tower weighs 474 tonnes. The market stage found no independent tower market source, so these figures rest on GW data and company arithmetic. Management's 12,500 tower claim is 1.88 times the flat case and 1.13 times the roadmap.
