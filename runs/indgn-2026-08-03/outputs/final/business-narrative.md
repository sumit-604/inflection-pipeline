Core grows while acquisitions distort the headline

Indegene sells outsourced commercialization, medical, regulatory and digital operations work to the world's largest drug makers. It is asset light, carries no debt, and books 71.5% of revenue in North America. FY26 revenue was Rs 3,510 Cr, up 23.6%, on a top 20 client roster that includes most of the global pharma majors. The shares trade near 33 times FY26 earnings at Rs 545 and a market value of Rs 13,142 Cr. This is a life sciences services firm, not a pharma manufacturer or a CDMO, and management says so in the annual report itself.

The transition here is quality of business, not a turnaround. Net revenue retention has stayed above 100% for five straight years. Top 20 client concentration fell from 76.2% to 66.6% even as absolute revenue from that cohort rose 85% since FY22. The company has moved GenAI platforms into production, Cortex, the Content Super App, and NEXT Medical Writing among them, and holds a proprietary healthcare professional data asset in Invisage and Tandem. On the backward Gate 0 scorecard it posts a core score of 90 out of 100 and lands GOOD+, and the forward moat scan reads STRENGTHENING, which combines to EXCELLENT+.

The Gate 0 grade sits on a boundary worth naming out loud. One internal-consistency question on the customer stickiness row, if resolved the conservative way, would move the moat class down a notch and relabel the company GOOD rather than GOOD+. Both grades are strong and neither depends on a missing number. It is a labelling call for the operator, not an evidence gap.

FY26 profit tells two stories at once. Consolidated PAT fell 1.4% to Rs 401 Cr while revenue grew 23.6%, but standalone PAT rose 16.9%. The entire drag sits in the international and freshly acquired subsidiaries. Three deals closed in the year, BioPharm in the US, Warn in the UK, and Cake in the DACH region, and they came with a Rs 20 Cr litigation provision, a roughly Rs 35 to 42 Cr fall in other income, and a step up in acquisition amortization. The core engine did not slow. The reported number did.

Cash backs the profit rather than flattering it. Consolidated operating cash flow ran 1.62 times PAT, up from 1.09 times, and standalone ran 1.63 times. Consolidated trade receivables grew 30.7% and unbilled revenue 77.5%, both faster than sales, but that swelling is concentrated in the BioPharm consolidation and in large deals whose revenue lands in FY27, while standalone receivables actually fell 31% and billed DSO improved from 72 to 62 days. The working capital build looks growth induced, not structural, though the credit rating rationale that would independently confirm this was not in the file.

The acquisition wave left a heavier balance sheet. Goodwill and intangibles tripled to Rs 16,176 mn and now sit at 51.5% of net worth, and this happened in the same year the impairment test discount rate for the enterprise commercial CGU was cut from 17.2% to 12.5% and terminal growth was raised to 5.0%. Contingent consideration climbed from Rs 152 mn to Rs 2,914 mn as earnouts stack up for FY27 and FY28. None of it is provisioned as a loss, but it concentrates future amortization and impairment risk in a smaller equity base.

Management earns a B on credibility, and the peers explain why it is not higher. Indegene delivered BioPharm's support function integration ahead of schedule and held its no-guidance policy without exception, but it softened the margin recovery target from an explicit near 20% to a vaguer return to earlier levels and pushed the timing to H2 FY27, and it never bridged the standalone versus consolidated profit gap for analysts. Its framing that a six to eight quarter margin recovery is routine, we have done this before, does not survive the peer read. The one comparable multi quarter recovery in the peer set, at IKS Health, beat its own timeline by three to four quarters, and Sagility absorbed its margin drag inside a single year. The precedent argues Indegene is slow, not proven.

Ownership is the cleanest part of the file. There is no identified promoter, and the promoter integrity flag does not apply. The Carlyle-linked CA Dawn stake of 10.2% exited in orderly, rising-price block trades into long only institutions, institutional holding climbed from 7.63% to 18.66%, two credentialed independent directors joined in January 2026, and the audit committee lost its sole executive seat. Set against that, one independent director has served roughly 18 years, committee attendance shows gaps, a Rs 2.43 mn employee fraud was caught and recovered, and two self reported filing defects are in remediation.

The runway is not the constraint. Independent triangulation puts management's stated market opportunity at 0.98 times a conservative third party estimate, so the claim reads modest rather than promotional. The three year serviceable opportunity of Rs 6,064 Cr implies roughly 20% revenue CAGR off a base the company currently serves at well under 1% share, a runway class of MASSIVE. The binding limit is domain trained talent and 15.7% attrition, not capital and not addressable demand.
