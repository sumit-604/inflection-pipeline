## SECTION 1: MARKET DEFINITION
---------------------------------------------------------------

### 1A Boundaries
- Product: LTL and part-load general parcel by road, 90.2% of revenue (B04). Excludes FTL (8.7%, a different market, truckload brokered), courier (0.6%), scrap (0.5%).
- Geography: India domestic. 1,293 branches, 5,932 owned vehicles, 81,700 tonnes carrying capacity at 31 Mar 2026 (AR PDF p.77). Regional mix South 42%, West 25%, North 21%, East/NE 10% of tonnage (Q1 FY27 call p.8, per B05).
- Customer: about 10 lakh GST-registered customers; largest 1% and top ten 3% of goods transport revenue (AR PDF p.79). Booking mix to-pay and paid 80-85%, account customers 15-16%, FTL 6-7% (Q4 FY26 call p.11, per B05).
- Channel: own branches plus franchisees and agents, hub-and-spoke (AR PDF p.77-78).
- Price segment: mid-market B2B and SME parcel. VRL runs no e-commerce business (Q1 FY27 call p.11, per B05).
- Inclusions: branded network parcel carriers (VRL, TCI Express, Delhivery PTL, Gati, Safexpress, V-Trans) and, in the wider TAM, unorganised small-fleet parcel movers.
- Exclusions: full truckload, rail, air, B2C express and e-commerce last mile, exports, warehousing.

### 1B Management's own claim
- AR MD&A: Indian logistics market "well over USD 240 billion" in 2026, 6.5-8% CAGR to USD 426 billion by 2034; road about 60% of modal share (AR PDF p.73, 'India's Logistics Industry'); 60-65% of freight traffic (PDF p.75); over 80% of fleet owners hold fewer than five trucks (PDF p.76); logistics cost 13-14% of GDP against an 8-9% target (PDF p.74). At Rs 94.40 per USD, USD 240 bn = Rs 22,65,600 Cr (DERIVED).
- Calls: industry "70% unorganised, 30% organised", share shifting to organised (Q1 FY27 call p.14; Q4 FY26 call p.8, per B05). No source given.
- VRL's own admission: "it is only the absence of validated industry data that prevents us from emphatically acclaiming" LTL leadership (AR PDF p.77).
- Credibility read: BROAD. The USD 240 bn number covers all logistics (warehousing, air, sea, rail), not VRL's market. Management gives no LTL TAM and says validated data is absent. The 70/30 split is unverified.

- Subtraction logic: the vendor number is already LTL only (FTL removed). Further subtractions (unorganised, B2C express, non-parcel) are not possible from filed data, so they move to SAM.
- Confidence: M for direction, L for definition. Staleness: base 2025, OK.
- Scale test (DERIVED): VRL Rs 3,221.11 Cr is 1.03% of Rs 3,11,610 Cr. At VRL's realisation of about Rs 8,145 per tonne (Rs 3,221.11 Cr / 3,955 thousand tonnes) this market would hold about 383 Mt of parcel tonnage. VRL realisation is a premium-network price, so informal flows at lower prices inflate that tonnage. The vendor number is a ceiling that includes every small-lot freight rupee, not the market a hub-and-spoke carrier fights for.

- SAM = Rs 6,945 Cr, 30.0% of conservative TAM, 2.2% of the vendor TAM. This is a floor: it equals the three named organised players' revenue.
- Two readings: (1) SAM is about Rs 6,945 Cr and VRL has 46% of it, so runway rests on the market growing, not on share. (2) SAM is larger because unlisted organised carriers and formalising unorganised volume are serviceable; the vendor ceiling at 30% organised gives Rs 93,483 Cr (3,11,610 x 0.30, DERIVED), where VRL holds 3.4%. Separating observation: the organised pool revenue of Safexpress, Gati, V-Trans. I carry reading (1) as central because only it rests on filings.

### 3B SOM
- Current share of SAM: 3,221.11 / 6,945 = 46.4%.
- SAM growth: 9.88% (Mordor CAGR 2026-31, M confidence). Cross-checks: management medium-term tonnage guide 9-10% (Q2 FY27 call p.6, p.8, per B05); TCI Express FY27 guide 10%+ volume and 15%+ revenue (Whalesbook, Q4 FY26); Delhivery PTL tonnage +17% FY26 (search summary). AR cites 6.5-8% for all logistics.
- SAM at year 3: 6,945 x 1.0988^3 = 6,945 x 1.3267 = Rs 9,213 Cr. Year 5: 6,945 x 1.6018 = Rs 11,124 Cr.
- Share-gain rule: 1-2pp in 3 years normal. Take +1.5pp at year 3 (47.9%) and +2.5pp at year 5 (48.9%, linear extension of the normal band, an assumption). Larger gains are not supported: SAM is a floor, and Delhivery and TCI Express grow faster than VRL, so VRL's share of the named pool can fall.
- SOM 3 years: 9,213 x 0.4788 = Rs 4,411 Cr. SOM 5 years: 11,124 x 0.4888 = Rs 5,438 Cr.
- Implied revenue CAGR on FY26 base of Rs 3,221.11 Cr: yr3 = (4,411 / 3,221.11)^(1/3) - 1 = 1.3694^0.3333 - 1 = 11.1%. yr5 = (5,438 / 3,221.11)^0.2 - 1 = 1.6883^0.2 - 1 = 11.0%.
- Cross-checks: management Feb 2026 FY27 revenue guide Rs 3,600 Cr (+11.8% on 3,221.11, DERIVED) and "10-12% revenue growth with no realisation change" (Q2 FY27 call, per B05). The Aug-Nov 2026 price step (+9% realisation YoY, tonnage +10% in H1 FY27, B05) suggests FY27 growth runs above 11%, so the FY26-based CAGR is conservative in year 1 and a normalised path after. Historical: FY26 revenue +1.9%. FY25 to FY26 tonnage -7.4%, so the FY26 base is a depressed-volume year; H1 FY27 tonnage 2,099 thousand t is still 3.0% below H1 FY25.
- Reading for stage 11: 11.0-11.1% is the SOM cross-check. It is not a cap: a faster share gain needs the 70% unorganised pool to move to branded carriers, which no document sizes.

### 3C Capacity cross-check
B07 is not on disk. Inputs used instead: fleet 5,932 owned vehicles, 81,700 t capacity at 31 Mar 2026, down from 85,261 t (B04 mgmt questions; AR PDF p.77); capex FY26 Rs 298.62 Cr, FY25 Rs 448.79 Cr (AR cash flow PDF p.173, per B01/B03); H1 FY27 Rs 158 Cr, H2 FY27 guide Rs 150-160 Cr, so Rs 308-318 Cr for FY27 (Q2 FY27 call p.4, p.7, per B05, DERIVED sum). Q1 FY27 vehicle capex was only Rs 18 Cr; net fleet rose 9 vehicles in six months (B05). The B07 capex-embedded growth percentage: NOT FOUND (stage not complete).
- Tonnes moved per tonne of capacity: FY25 = 4,271 thousand t (3,955 / 0.926, DERIVED from -7.4%) / 85.261 thousand t = 50.1. FY26 = 3,955 / 81.7 = 48.4. H1 FY27 annualised = 4,198 / 81.7 = 51.4; Q2 FY27 annualised = 4,316 / 81.7 = 52.8.
- SOM 3-year revenue Rs 4,411 Cr at Rs 8,840 per tonne (Q2 FY27 realisation, B04) needs 4,411 Cr / 8,840 = 4.99 Mt, +26% on FY26 tonnage, an 8.1% tonnage CAGR over 3 years, which sits on management's 9-10% guide.
- Capacity the existing fleet supports at FY25 turns: 81.7 thousand t x 50.1 = 4,093 thousand t x Rs 8,840 = Rs 3,618 Cr. Gap to SOM 3-year: 4,411 - 3,618 = Rs 793 Cr, about 18%, needing about 17.9 thousand t more capacity (+22%) (DERIVED). At Q2 FY27 turns (52.8): 4,314 thousand t x Rs 8,840 = Rs 3,814 Cr; gap Rs 597 Cr.
- Capex cost per tonne of capacity: NOT FOUND, so the gap in Rs Cr of capex is NOT FOUND. Which side is optimistic: the SOM tonnage CAGR matches the guide, so SOM is not the optimistic side. The capex plan is the thin side for owned fleet: FY27 spend of Rs 308-318 Cr is weighted to property (land and buildings, Rs 150-200 Cr, Q1/Q4 calls per B05), and vehicle adds just offset scrappage so far. Growth would come from higher turns and hired lorries (B05 notes hire cost already a margin item), which weighs on the own-fleet margin story. Flagged, not a verdict.

### 5B Runway
- Headroom: SAM 6,945 / revenue 3,221.11 = 2.16x.
- TAM growth 9.88% (Mordor 2026-31). VRL history: FY26 revenue +1.9% and tonnage -7.4%, so VRL lost share in FY26 against a growing market; H1 FY27 tonnage +10% is at market pace, not above it. VRL is riding the market, not gaining share.
- Years to saturate the floor SAM at 11.0% company CAGR with SAM growing 9.88%: the gap closes slowly; at share of 48.9% the 5-year SAM is not saturated but share gains beyond about 50% are not credible (DERIVED judgement). Saturation is not the binding limit; the binding limits are volume recovery and price.

### 5C Runway class: MODERATE
- The stage file names the five classes but not the thresholds. Thresholds applied here (stage-set, stated): headroom 10x+ MASSIVE, 5-10x STRONG, 3-5x GOOD, 1.5-3x MODERATE, under 1.5x LIMITED. 2.16x gives MODERATE. Sensitivity: on the vendor-ceiling reading (SAM Rs 93,483 Cr, 29x) the class would be MASSIVE; the evidence for that reading is absent, so MODERATE carries.
