## PASS 3 — PATTERN RE-READ

Re-reading with a "contradictions and patterns" lens across all three passes, rather than sequentially:

**Pattern 1 — a single connected capital-structure story, not three separate items.** The Rs(711.25) Cr accumulated
P&L deficit (corrected figure, see below) is deducted from CET1 at Pillar III DF-11 (Rs7,112.47 Million exactly ties,
PDF p.149); this deduction is a direct driver of why the Bank needed the dilutive rights issue (Note 1.1, PDF p.97) to
lift CRAR from 12.71% to 16.12%; and the same legacy-loss mechanism is what keeps Section 198 profits at nil and
therefore CSR spend at nil for a second consecutive year (Note 16i, PDF p.135), and, per the Auditor's Report (CARO
Rule 11(e)(v)/Section 123 Companies Act, PDF p.74), is consistent with no dividend having been declared. These four
disclosures (Schedule 2, Pillar III DF-11, Note 1.1/1.2, Note 16i, Auditor's Report) read separately in Pass 1 and
Pass 2 are one coherent capital-and-distributable-reserves constraint, not four independent findings. NOTE: the AR's
own text cites only Companies Act Section 123 for the no-dividend fact; no explicit citation to Banking Regulation Act
Section 15(1) (the usual statutory dividend-restriction mechanism for banks with accumulated losses) was found in the
extracted notes — flagged as NOT FOUND IN DOCUMENT rather than asserted, though the Section 15(1) constraint is the
standard regulatory reason a bank in this position cannot pay a dividend even after a profitable year.

**Pattern 2 — three separate notes tell one "improving headline, deteriorating underlying" asset-quality story.**
(a) Headline GNPA ratio improves 4.05%→2.98%, but Pass 2's full movement-table read shows fresh slippage is flat
(Rs152.26 Cr vs Rs152.24 Cr) and reductions were actually lower YoY — the ratio improvement is a denominator
(+18.4% advances growth) effect (Note 4a-i, PDF p.113-114). (b) The cumulative technical write-off pool sitting
outside the NPA schedule entirely has grown to Rs692.47 Cr, now nearly double the headline Gross NPA of Rs364.11 Cr,
with its annual recovery rate roughly halving (6.4%→3.2%) (Note 4a-i, PDF p.113). (c) Restructured advances grew 47%
YoY concentrated into fewer, larger MSME sub-standard accounts (Note 4d-ii, PDF p.116). Read together, these three
notes (asset quality movement table, technical write-off reconciliation, restructuring table) describe the same
underlying phenomenon from three angles: reported asset quality is being managed toward an improving headline via
denominator growth, write-off transfer, and restructuring, while the stock of impaired/stressed/written-off credit is
not actually shrinking. No single note alone would surface this; it only emerges by reading all three against each
other.

**Pattern 3 — earnings quality pattern across two note groups.** Note 14e (Provisions and Contingencies) shows both
FY25 (Rs3.74 Cr) and FY24 (Rs19.93 Cr) profit benefited from restructuring-provision write-backs, and FY24 additionally
had a Rs10.79 Cr reserve credit-back (fraud-provision unwind). Cross-read against Note 16a's Other Income line ("Recovery
from technical write-off accounts" falling 45% YoY) and the segment note (15c) showing Treasury and Retail carrying the
whole-bank result while Corporate/Wholesale segment losses widen — the pattern is that reported PAT improvement (Rs57.82
Cr FY24 → Rs66.64 Cr FY25) is a composite of a genuinely improving Retail/Treasury franchise partially offset by a
genuinely deteriorating Corporate/Wholesale book, with both years' comparability further clouded by non-recurring
provision releases. This is a quality-of-earnings pattern, not a single red flag.

**Restatements found**: Basic EPS restated Rs2.21→Rs2.37 (PY) for the bonus element of the rights issue, per AS20 (Note
15f, PDF p.129-130). No other prior-year figures were restated; Note 17's comparative-figures note is generic/
boilerplate with no quantified reclassification (PDF p.137).

**Events after balance sheet date**: none disclosed in the extracted notes (NOT FOUND IN DOCUMENT beyond the rights
issue, which was allotted 4 Feb 2025, i.e. within FY25, not a subsequent event).

**Going concern language**: NONE found anywhere in Schedule 17, Schedule 18, Pillar III, or the extracted Auditor's
Report text. The two Key Audit Matters identified (Classification of Advances/NPA/provisioning per Note 4; Valuation of
Investments/NPI per Note 3) are routine KAM categories for Indian banks, not going-concern qualifications or emphasis-
of-matter language.

No further material contradictions beyond those already surfaced in Pass 2 (the corrected accumulated-loss figure and
the Schedule 4 borrowings correction) were found on this pattern re-read. The three patterns above are synthesis-level
connections across notes already extracted, not new numeric findings.

### D. FIVE QUESTIONS FOR MANAGEMENT

1. The cumulative technical write-off pool (Rs692.47 Cr, Note 4a-i) is now nearly double the reported Gross NPA
   (Rs364.11 Cr) and its recovery rate has halved YoY (6.4%→3.2%). What is management's realistic multi-year recovery
   expectation for this pool, and should investors read the headline GNPA/NNPA ratios net of this shadow exposure?
2. Note 4a-i and Note 16a disclose two different figures for "recovery from technical write-off accounts" in both FY25
   (Rs21.42 Cr vs Rs16.70 Cr) and FY24 (Rs41.83 Cr vs Rs30.18 Cr). Please reconcile the gap between the asset-quality
   note and the Other Income note for what is nominally the same recovery event.
3. Note 15b's pension and gratuity funded-status tables show a sign inconsistency: the "(ii)-(i)" calculation implies a
   deficit (obligation exceeds plan assets) of Rs35.83 Cr (pension) and Rs7.01 Cr (gratuity), but the 5-year summary
   table in the same note labels these figures "Surplus/(Deficit)" with a positive sign. Are these plans in surplus or
   deficit, and which figure should investors rely on?
4. Restructured advances grew 47% YoY in Rupee terms (Rs48.44 Cr → Rs71.31 Cr) despite fewer borrowers, concentrated in
   MSME sub-standard accounts (Rs9.36 Cr → Rs56.99 Cr, Note 4d-ii). What is driving this concentration, and what
   proportion of the restructured book is expected to migrate to NPA versus cure over the next 12-24 months?
5. The Corporate/Wholesale Banking segment posted a widening loss (Rs(22.58) Cr FY25 vs Rs(13.48) Cr FY24, Note 15c)
   even as segment revenue grew. What is the turnaround plan for this segment, and at what point would continued
   losses there prompt a strategic reassessment of the segment's scale or scope?

### E. NOTES-BASED RED FLAGS

1. **Denominator-driven NPA optics**: the improving headline GNPA/NNPA ratios are not supported by the underlying
   slippage/recovery movement table, which shows flat fresh slippage and lower gross reductions YoY (Note 4a-i).
2. **Shadow bad-debt pool nearly 2x headline GNPA**: the Rs692.47 Cr cumulative technical write-off pool, with a
   halving recovery rate, sits entirely outside reported asset-quality ratios (Note 4a-i).
3. **Restructuring concentration in MSME sub-standard accounts**, +47% YoY despite fewer borrowers — a classic early-
   warning pattern (Note 4d-ii).
4. **Two years of profit flattered by non-recurring provision write-backs and a reserve credit-back**, rather than
   organic operating improvement alone (Note 14e, Schedule 2 draw-down note).
5. **Widening structural loss in the Corporate/Wholesale segment**, masked at the whole-bank level (Note 15c).
6. **Legacy accumulated loss of Rs(711.25) Cr** (corrected from Pass 1's erroneous Rs71.12 Cr), exceeding 50% of
   paid-up capital plus reserves, driving nil dividend/nil CSR capacity (Schedule 2; Pillar III DF-11; Note 16i;
   Auditor's Report).

### F. ONE-LINE NOTES VERDICT

The notes reveal moderate accounting practices with a genuine gap between headline optics and underlying asset
quality. Key concern: the improving GNPA ratio (4.05%→2.98%) is largely a denominator effect masking flat slippage, a
growing near-Rs700 Cr technical write-off shadow pool with a halving recovery rate, and 47% restructuring growth
concentrated in MSME sub-standard accounts. Key strength: clean related-party practices, conservative RBI-mandated
investment valuation, and a materially strengthened capital base (CRAR 16.12%, CET1 15.24%) following the rights
issue, against a Rs(711.25) Cr legacy accumulated loss that is now correctly quantified and understood as the true
scale of the Bank's distributable-reserves constraint. Overall accounting quality: 5/10.
