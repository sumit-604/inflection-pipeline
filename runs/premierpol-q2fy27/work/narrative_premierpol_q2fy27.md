# Premier Polyfilm Q2 FY27: the story in plain words

Source: committed review (work/review_premierpol_q2fy27.md), A5 audit (work/audit_premierpol_q2fy27.md) and work/notion-thesis.md. No agent was re-run.

1. **What the company does.** Premier Polyfilm makes PVC products at two plants in Uttar Pradesh, Sahibabad and Sikandarabad. A third plant is coming up at Chennai. (Corrected 2026-10-18 from the Crisil rating rationale of 7 August 2026; the filing letterhead names only Sahibabad. See work/crisil_rating_2026-08-07.md.) It makes vinyl flooring, PVC sheets, pond and canal liners, and artificial leather cloth. Its main raw material is PVC resin. It has no subsidiaries, so there is one set of accounts.

2. **Earlier analysis.** This is a first look, with no earlier analysis on record. There is no Notion page, no thesis, no Decision Status, no entry zone and no tripwires. So no tripwire moved, and there is no thesis to hold or break.

3. **What happened this quarter.** Sales grew fast, by about 40% over last year, to about 101 Cr after GST. Profit grew more slowly. Profit after tax rose about 22%, to about 10 Cr. A small part of that came from a lower tax rate.

4. **What improved.** The company sold much more. Its overheads, such as staff and other running costs, grew more slowly than sales. Return on capital stays high, near 30% or more. Customers pay on time, and the company holds little debt.

5. **What got worse.** Raw material now eats a much bigger part of each rupee of sales. Gross margin fell from about 37% to about 31% in one year. The company sells more, but it keeps less from each sale. The filing gives no sales volumes. So we cannot tell whether the company sold more units, or only charged more because resin cost more.

6. **Cash and balance sheet.** Profit did not fully turn into cash. Cash from operations was only 70% of profit in the half year, against 84% last year. The cash went into "other current assets", and the filing does not say what they are. In FY26, stock and dues from customers also built up a lot. The balance sheet looks strong, but how much cash it holds depends on how you read the debt and the investments.

7. **Management's plans.** The filing says nothing about the future. There is no outlook and no commentary, and the board met only to approve the results. But the numbers show the company is building something. Work in progress on new assets rose about eight times in six months. No note says what the project is, what it costs or when it starts.

8. **Red flags in plain words.** The balance sheet shows zero borrowings, but the same filing prints a debt ratio above zero and shows loan repayments and interest. So some debt sits somewhere we cannot see. Four balance sheet numbers do not add up to their totals; the scan was probably misread. The auditor's report is partly unreadable, and its identity code cannot be checked. The share count behind earnings per share is about 1% lower than the shares issued.

9. **Open items left after the review loops.** The final check found two gaps that were not fixed, because the loop limit was reached. First, in FY26 the company paid out 19.20 Cr in investing cash under an unnamed line. That is about 60% of FY26 profit. If it is a deposit or a fund, FY26 free cash flow was healthy. If not, it was slightly negative. Second, the review describes the FY26 build in stock and receivables wrongly in places, although the total is right. The FY26 audited annual results would settle the first gap. That filing was not in this run.

10. **What this means.** The verdict is PROCEED WITH FLAGS, as a first read. The business grows and earns high returns. But growth now comes with thinner margins, cash lags profit, and the filing hides the debt, the new project and the product mix. This is not yet a thesis. The next step is the Step 1 intake, if the operator wants to go further.

11. **Questions for management.** The review sends 16 questions to the company by email. The three that matter most are these. Why did the margin fall, and is the price catching up with resin? What is the new project, what does it cost, and when does it start? Where does the debt sit, and how much is it?

12. **What to watch next quarter.** Watch the Q3 FY27 gross margin, due by 14 February 2027. If it climbs back to about 33.5% or higher, the margin fall was likely a lag in passing on resin cost. If it stays below about 31%, the lower margin is likely here to stay. Also watch whether the project gets a name and a date, whether cash catches up with profit, and whether a clean copy of the auditor's report appears.
