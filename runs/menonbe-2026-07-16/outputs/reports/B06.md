## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

### 2A. Demand environment

All three peers describe a genuinely strong, resilient **Indian domestic market** across the period —
consistent with MENONBE's framing. Where the peer set diverges sharply from MENONBE's confident tone
is on **export/Europe demand**: HARSHA repeatedly uses "cautiously optimistic," "wait and see for 1 or 2
more quarters," across all four calls, only firming up confidence by Q3/Q4 FY26; NRB's own international
business grew just 4% in FY26 (Q4 FY26 call, May-2026), well below its automotive domestic growth, with
management explicitly flagging this as "not necessarily very satisfying"; PRECAM's European subsidiary
MFT went into formal insolvency in Q2 FY26 (Dec-2025 call) amid a "massive economic slowdown in the
European markets." None of the three peers describes the confident, accelerating structural export boom
that MENONBE's China+1/Europe+1 narrative implies — the peer consensus is closer to "genuine but slow,
uneven, and still fragile" than to a confirmed inflection.

### 2B. Pricing and input costs

Peer commentary broadly matches MENONBE's margin narrative on RM cost direction (copper and aluminium
inflation, lagged pass-through) — see Claim 2 above. The split in outcome is instructive: HARSHA and
PRECAM both report margin compression from this lag in the same period MENONBE reports its own RM-timing
misses, supporting an industry-wide read. NRB, however, *expanded* margins through the same window via
structural offsets (solar power, automation, vendor renegotiation) — showing the lag is not
insurmountable with sufficient self-help measures, which weakens any implicit excuse that frames RM
timing as an uncontrollable, purely external factor.

### 2C. Capex cycle

This is an **industry-wide capacity build cycle, not a lone-expander situation** — all three peers are
simultaneously investing meaningfully (HARSHA ~Rs300cr+ across Bhayla/Advantek plus a fresh China
brownfield project; NRB a Rs500cr five-year roadmap plus a new JV and an aerospace acquisition; PRECAM a
~Rs100-120cr Solapur facility plus further capacity enhancements). MENONBE's own capex (Rs20-35cr guided
for the next two years per B05) is far smaller in absolute scale than any of the three peers' programs,
even accounting for MENONBE's smaller base. The framing difference matters: peers uniformly describe
themselves as still building, while MENONBE describes itself as past the heavy-investment phase and now
sweating existing assets toward 2-2.5x turns — a different capex-cycle narrative than any peer currently
tells about itself (see Claim 7).

### 2D. Competitive mentions

No peer transcript mentions Menon Bearings, Menon Alkop, Menon Brakes, or any unmistakable description of
the company, in any of the 11 calls reviewed. This is unsurprising given the sub-segment differences
(bearing cages/bushings vs. needle/cylindrical bearings vs. camshafts, versus MENONBE's bi-metal
bushings/bearings, aluminium die-casting, and friction/brake components) — none of the three assigned
peers is a direct head-to-head competitor of MENONBE in any single product line.

### 2E. Risks peers discuss that MENONBE does not

- **Foreign-subsidiary financial distress escalating to formal insolvency**: PRECAM's German step-down
  subsidiary MFT GmbH went from "critical liquidity issues" (Q1 FY26 call, Aug-2025, p.3 of 6) to
  initiating liquidation/insolvency proceedings within one quarter (Q2 FY26 call, Dec-2025, p.2 of 5),
  with a Rs49.7cr impairment charge. MENONBE's own subsidiary governance concern — Menon Alkop's negative
  reserves against an unimpaired carrying cost (flagged independently in B02/B05) — is a structurally
  similar risk category (subsidiary financial distress within a promoted growth story) that MENONBE's
  management has never addressed on any of its four calls, while PRECAM's management proactively
  disclosed and quantified its subsidiary's failure in real time.
- **Promoter-family ownership disputes and settlement costs**: NRBBEARING's Q2 FY26 call (Nov-2025)
  opens with an extensive, voluntary explanation of a promoter-family split, a Rs55cr one-time settlement
  payment, and brand/logo ownership terms — a governance disclosure category MENONBE does not have an
  analogue for, but which illustrates that peers in this space do proactively surface complex governance
  matters when material, a contrast to MENONBE's silence on Alkop.
- **Greenfield/new-capacity loss-drag disclosed transparently**: HARSHA quantifies, quarter by quarter,
  the exact loss contribution of its new Advantek plant (e.g., "aggregate loss of about INR5.7 crores
  suffered by Harsha Advantek in first half," Q2 FY26 call, Nov-2025) due to depreciation/interest ahead
  of revenue scale-up — a level of granular, proactive capex-drag disclosure that MENONBE does not
  provide for its own newer capacity (Brakes/Alkop expansions).
- **EV-strategy reversal risk**: PRECAM notes (Q2/Q3 FY26 calls, Dec-2025/Mar-2026) that major global
  OEMs have executed "a complete U-turn" away from all-electric strategies back toward ICE/hybrid,
  materially slowing its own EV-linked business (Tata Ace conversion "nearly stopped"). MENONBE's own
  EV-linked growth bets (PTFE bushes, Alkop EV parts for Tesla/Concentric, Porsche/Eaton) are never
  stress-tested on any MENONBE call against this same technology-reversal risk that a directly comparable
  EV-exposed peer flags explicitly.
- **Aftermarket cost-structure economics explained**: NRB explains in detail why aftermarket business is
  structurally more expensive to serve (discount structures, higher headcount, distribution cost) than
  OEM business (Q3 FY26 call, Feb-2026) — independent context for MENONBE's own claim that Rane/Sundaram
  Brake Linings are low-margin aftermarket players, but MENONBE itself never explains the mechanism, only
  asserts the comparison.
- **Forex volatility as a standalone margin swing factor**: NRB's Q4 FY26 call (May-2026) attributes a
  gross-margin dip squarely to a last-day forex/COGS-conversion anomaly — a risk category MENONBE's own
  calls do not discuss as a distinct margin driver alongside RM cost and export-mix commentary.

now sweating existing assets toward 2-2.5x turns. Every peer in this set — HARSHA, NRBBEARING, and
PRECAM — is instead actively mid-expansion in the same period, and NRB's Vice Chairman explicitly states

their own new capex), but it means the specific "post-capex harvest" narrative MENONBE uses to describe
its stage in the cycle has no peer analogue in this set — either MENONBE genuinely is ahead of these three
peers because its capex program is much smaller in absolute terms, or the "sweating assets" framing is
doing more narrative work than the underlying capex numbers (Rs20-35cr next two years, small relative to
peers' Rs100-500cr programs) can support.

margin (~6.2%, computed independently from its published quarterly results) lands almost exactly on
