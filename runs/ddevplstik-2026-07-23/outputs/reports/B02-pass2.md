## PART A — INTERROGATION OF THE SIX FLAGGED ITEMS

### 1. RoE fell to 24.82% / RoCE 0.30 despite profit growth
**Verdict: CONFIRMED, and now mechanically explained.**
Note 16, Other Equity roll-forward (marker p.164 / footer p.176): Retained
Earnings moved from ₹55,773.44 Lacs to ₹73,288.37 Lacs — opening balance
+ PAT ₹18,549.70 Lacs − dividend paid ₹1,034.77 Lacs. **Dividend paid during
FY25 was only ₹1,034.77 Lacs against PAT of ₹18,549.70 Lacs — a payout ratio
of 5.6%.** Total Other Equity grew 26.9% (₹64,952.09 Lacs to ₹82,435.82 Lacs)
against PAT growth of only 2.1%. This — near-total profit retention into a
low-yield treasury (₹61.39 Cr parked in mutual funds this year, per Pass 1) —
is the direct, quantified mechanical driver of the RoE/RoCE decline. Not a
quality-of-earnings problem; it is a capital-allocation signal (cash
accumulating faster than it is being deployed into the core business or
returned to shareholders), which matters for GARP framing regardless.

### 2. Finished-goods inventory +60.3% vs revenue +7.08%
**Verdict: CONFIRMED and escalated — direct P&L quantification found.**
Note 29, Changes in Inventories of Finished Goods (footer p.180-181): the
FY25 build reduced Cost of Goods Sold (credited to P&L) by ₹1,407.66 Lacs,
versus only ₹80.14 Lacs in FY24 — a **₹1,327.52 Lacs favourable YoY swing**.
Total PAT grew by only ₹382.76 Lacs YoY. In other words, the finished-goods
inventory build contributed roughly **3.5x the entire year's profit growth**
to the P&L as an unsold-stock credit. This is the single largest unflagged
swing item found in this pass. It does not mean the accounting is wrong —
Ind AS requires this treatment — but it means FY25's reported profit growth
leans heavily on stock that has not yet been converted to cash, reinforcing
rather than merely echoing Pass 1's flag. Independent corroboration: the
Auditor's Report Key Audit Matters section (p.146 in this document's own
numbering) names "Inventory — existence and valuation (Note 10)" as a Key
Audit Matter in its own right, citing management judgment in provisioning
and NRV testing — the auditor independently treats this inventory line as
the highest-risk area of the FY25 audit.

### 3. Kkalpana royalty down 72.5% while purchases nearly doubled
**Verdict: CONFIRMED, still unexplained in the notes, no new mechanism found.**
Re-read of Note 41 in full (footer p.188-189) surfaces no additional detail —
no royalty agreement terms, rate basis, or renegotiation note anywhere in

Stands as flagged in Pass 1: unverifiable arm's-length basis from disclosure.

### 4. MSME payables +35.9% with recurring MSMED delayed-payment interest
**Verdict: CONFIRMED, scale context added.**
Note 21 (footer p.178-179): the ₹0.66 Lacs (FY25) / ₹0.86 Lacs (FY24)
delayed-payment interest is immaterial in isolation, but "Acceptances
secured" (bank-financed trade credit facilities, tracked separately from the
payables ageing table) grew 65.4% YoY (₹7,444.15 Lacs to ₹12,311.78 Lacs) —
this and the MSME growth both point the same direction: the company is
leaning more on supplier-side financing (both statutory-vendor and
bank-acceptance channels) even as it sits on ₹42.99 Cr of cash + ₹61.39 Cr of
mutual fund investments. Working-capital funding mix, not a liquidity
concern given the net-cash position, but worth watching as a discipline
signal.

### 5. Gratuity plan only ~16% funded
**Verdict: CONFIRMED and materially escalated.**
Note 39(b)(B), item (4) "Contribution made by the Employer" (footer
p.185-186): **₹0 in FY25 and ₹0 in FY24.** Separately, the actuary's own
"best estimate contribution for the company during the next year" is
disclosed as ₹91.73 Lacs (FY25 estimate) and ₹106.91 Lacs (FY24 estimate).
The company made zero contribution against either estimate in either year.
This reframes Pass 1's "funding gap is widening" into a firmer finding: **the
company has not funded the plan at all for at least two consecutive years,
against its own actuary's explicit recommendation.** The liability is fully
recognized on the balance sheet (not hidden), so this is a cash-discipline/
governance observation, not an accounting-quality one — but it is stronger
than Pass 1 characterized it.

### 6. Receivable days ~60 -> ~65
**Verdict: CONFIRMED direction; new nuance found that complicates the "improved ageing" read.**
Note 32, Other Expenses (footer p.182): "Provision for doubtful debts" fell
to **₹0 in FY25 from ₹499.32 Lacs in FY24**, while a new line, "Bad debts /
Advances Written off," appears at **₹79.44 Lacs in FY25 (₹0 in FY24)**. The
ECL allowance itself (Note 12) fell ₹295.46 Lacs (₹774.96 Lacs to ₹479.50
Lacs) YoY. The disclosed P&L captions only account for ₹79.44 Lacs of that
₹295.46 Lacs allowance reduction (if the write-off was drawn against the
allowance) — **roughly ₹216 Lacs of the allowance decline has no visible
matching P&L line in Note 32.** This does not necessarily indicate
misstatement (it could be a netted reversal/recovery not separately broken
out), but it means the "ageing quality improved" read from Pass 1 should be
held with more caution: the allowance fell partly because the company simply
stopped adding to the provision this year (zero new provisioning vs ₹499.32
Lacs last year) rather than solely because underlying receivable quality
improved. Receivable days trend itself (~60 to ~65 days) stands as Pass 1
reported it.

## PART B — NEW FINDINGS FROM THE RE-READ

1. 🔴 **Note 52 (Leases), footer p.197-198 — internal arithmetic inconsistency
   in the lease liability roll-forward.** The disclosed movement table reads:
   Opening balance ₹22.13 Lacs + Interest accrued ₹28.35 Lacs + Additions
   during the year "–" (blank/nil) − Payment of Lease Liabilities ₹113.04
   Lacs = a computed closing balance of **(₹62.56) Lacs**, printed as such in
   the table. The actual closing lease liability per the balance sheet /
   Note 17 is ₹429.46 Lacs (Current ₹108.25 Lacs + Non-current ₹321.21 Lacs).
   The gap is ₹492.02 Lacs — almost exactly the ₹504.78 Lacs of new ROU
   asset additions disclosed in the same note's asset roll-forward, which
   strongly implies the "Additions during the year" line in the liability
   table should show ~₹492 Lacs but is printed as nil. This is a genuine
   reconciliation failure within a single note (the two halves of the same
   Note 52 — ROU asset movement vs lease liability movement — do not tie to
   each other), not merely a rounding gap. Flagged red because the
   liabilities table on its face does not foot to the balance sheet without
   this missing line.

2. 🟡 **Note 27, Other Income, footer p.180 — 24.1% YoY decline, driven by
   volatile/non-recurring components, previously unexamined.** Total Other
   Income fell from ₹2,367.89 Lacs (FY24) to ₹1,796.16 Lacs (FY25), driven
   by: Exchange difference income ₹1,452.64 Lacs to ₹908.00 Lacs (−37.5%);
   Export Incentive ₹434.08 Lacs to ₹199.47 Lacs (−54.0%). Interest income
   rose (₹209.12 Lacs to ₹330.20 Lacs, +57.9%, consistent with the larger
   cash/investment book). Roughly 60% of FY25's Other Income is forex
   translation gain, a non-operating, currency-direction-dependent item —
   worth separating from "core" earnings quality when assessing the FY25
   PBT growth of +2.4% (₹24,465.62 Lacs to ₹25,064.30 Lacs).

3. 🟡 **Note 39(b), footer p.185-186 — zero employer contribution to the
   gratuity trust in both years, against actuary's own recommendation.**
   See Part A, item 5 above for the full figure. Recorded here as a discrete
   new finding since Pass 1 did not extract the "Contribution made by the
   Employer" or "best estimate contribution" lines.

4. 🟡 **Note 48, footer p.196 — imported raw material dependency fell
   materially, a positive de-risking not previously flagged.** Imported raw
   materials fell from 26% to 19% of total raw material consumption YoY in
   value terms (₹50,356.54 Lacs to ₹41,654.14 Lacs, a 17.3% absolute
   decline even as total consumption grew 9.4%). This sits in tension with
   Note 43's finding (Pass 1, item in Section 12) that unhedged FX earnings
   sensitivity grew 63.6% — i.e., import-cost FX exposure is falling while
   export-receivable FX exposure is rising; the two forex risk vectors are
   moving in opposite directions and should not be netted against each
   other in isolation.

5. 🟡 **Note 15, footer p.176 — a 20,000-share / 0.02pp discrepancy between
   two sub-tables of the same note for the same reporting date.** The
   ">5% shareholders" table shows Bbigplas Poly Pvt Ltd holding 7,67,50,853
   shares (74.17%) at 31-Mar-2025; the "Promoter shareholding" table in the
   same note shows the same entity holding 7,67,30,853 shares (74.15%) at
   the same date. Flagged with caution: the adjoining "% Change during the
   Year" column in the promoter table shows values (e.g. 10.18% for
   Bbigplas, 3072.40% for one individual promoter) that do not reconcile
   against static, unchanged share counts shown in the same row across both
   year-columns — this pattern is more consistent with a text-extraction/
   OCR column-alignment artifact in this pass's source cache than with a
   genuine error in the underlying PDF. Recommend a visual spot-check of the
   original PDF page before treating this as a hard finding; carried here as
   a flagged discrepancy, not a confirmed one.

6. 🟢 **Note 43, footer p.192-193 — the "hedged" vs "not designated as
   hedge" language across the FX tables is NOT a contradiction; explicitly
   reconciled in the note text.** Pass 1 did not address this; a literal
   read of the two adjacent tables (one labelled "I Hedged," the next
   "Derivatives not designated as hedges," both showing the identical
   forward contract notionals) looks contradictory at first pass. The note
   itself resolves it: "Although the company believes that these
   derivatives constitute hedges from an economic perspective these do not
   qualify for hedge accounting as per IND AS 109... such derivatives are
   categorised as financial asset or financial liability at fair value
   through profit & loss." Economic hedge, no formal hedge-accounting
   designation — standard and disclosed. Recorded here explicitly so Pass 3
   does not re-flag it as a contradiction.

7. ⚪ **Note 45, "Net Debt Reconciliation," footer p.194-195 — a labelling
   inconsistency, not a substantive one.** This note's table title says
   "Net Debt" but its build (opening/closing balances of ₹6,636.72 Lacs /
   ₹4,224.20 Lacs) reconciles only gross short-term borrowings movements
   (proceeds, repayments, interest); it does not net against cash or current
   investments. Note 44 (Capital Management, footer p.194) separately and
   correctly computes true net debt as negative (₹5,808.72 Lacs, i.e. net
   cash) using the proper definition. The two notes use the same phrase
   ("net debt") for two different calculations one page apart. Immaterial to
   the substance (Note 44's figure is the one that matters and is clearly
   disclosed) but a disclosure-hygiene point.

8. ⚪ **Note 48/49 export figures, footer p.196, vs Note 40-continuation

9. ⚪ **Note 32, footer p.182 — Loss on sale of Property, Plant & Equipment

10. ⚪ **Note 46, R&D expenses, footer p.195 — revenue R&D spend nearly
