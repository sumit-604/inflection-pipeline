### A1. Object clause widened into cranes, defence vehicles, infrastructure EPC and an investment business, with no rationale anywhere in the narrative
AGM Notice Item 15 (AR pp.17-19) inserts three new main-objects sub-clauses: (9) cranes,
lifting equipment, heavy machinery, earthmoving machinery, "vehicles including utility

and infrastructure-related projects "of all kinds", roads, bridges, tunnels, railways, metro
systems, airports, ports, EPC, turnkey, BOT, BOOT and DBFOT contracts. The explanatory
statement (AR p.43) says only: "In order to pursue the new business and activities in
addition to the existing object, the Board of Directors of the Company considers it

describe strategy exclusively as "dyes, dye intermediates and other organic and inorganic
chemicals". Both documents carry the same board date, 25-Aug-2026.
**Severity: MAJOR.** Archetype-relevant: an H-Acid converter proposing entry into EPC
infrastructure and defence-vehicle trading is a business-definition question, not a footnote.

### A2. The warrant terms cannot all hold at once, and the promoter stake goes to 73.75%
Item 16 (AR pp.44-45) grants 22,85,000 warrants at Rs 176.10, convertible "within a period
of eighteen months from the date of the allotment". The same page states: "The total
allotment of equity shares upon conversion of the convertible warrants in the present
preferential issue, in any financial year would be less than 5% of the post issue fully
diluted share capital of the issuer." 22,85,000 of a post-issue 85,97,880 shares (AR p.47)
is 26.58%. An 18-month window touches at most three financial years, so the stated 5% cap
permits under 15%. The conversion window and the compliance undertaking are arithmetically
incompatible. The same table shows promoter holding rising 64.25% to 73.75% and public
float falling 35.75% to 26.25%, against a 25% minimum public shareholding floor. Terms:
25% down (Rs 10.06 cr of Rs 40.24 cr), 75% payable any time in 18 months, forfeiture of
the deposit only if unexercised (AR p.45). Against a screener Current Price of Rs 247.65
the strike is about 41% in the money at grant.
**Severity: MAJOR.**

## PART 2: INDEPENDENT RED-FLAG LIST — LEG B (PEER TRANSCRIPTS)

### B1. BODALCHEM says the raw-material cost was passed through in tandem, not with a lag, and that its margin expanded through it
BODALCHEM Q4 FY25 call (29-May-2025), Mayur Padhya, internal p.9-10: "this increase in the
raw material prices, we are able to pass it on towards our finished good prices. So our
margins are coming quite decent from the sulfur-based products also." Asked directly, "are
we seeing any lag when it comes to pricing sulfuric acid per se or is it in the tandem...
with sulfur?", the answer is "It is usually in the tandem with sulfur." BODALCHEM's blended
EBITDA margin rose from about 8.6% to 11.6% across the same window. SHHARICH, facing the
same input chain, blames "increase in Raw Material Price" for a fall from 8.13% to 5.31%.
The peer evidence weakens the causal force of the excuse rather than supporting it.
**Severity: MAJOR.** It also contradicts B06's own 2B claim that "both peers describe this
lag independently".

### B2. The sulphur spike the peers document falls mostly outside SHHARICH's FY26
BODALCHEM Q4 FY25 (29-May-2025), Mayur Padhya, internal p.9-10: sulphur Rs 18,000-20,000
per ton in Q3 FY25 rising to Rs 25,000-27,000 in Q4 FY25; sulphuric acid Rs 8,000 to about
Rs 13,000. BODALCHEM Q1 FY26 (14-Aug-2025), Mayur Padhya, internal p.8: "It is still in the
same range. The sulfur is around 27,000 only and the sulfuric is also around INR12,000
only... No, it is the same as the first quarter only." SHREEPUSHK Q3 FY26 (12-Feb-2026),
Punit Makharia, internal p.4-5: sulphur "started going up from mid of the second quarter till
today travelling from $283-$284 as of now, to $560". SHREEPUSHK Q4 FY26 (19-May-2026),
internal p.5: the 3x ammonia and sulphur move is dated to 28-Feb-2026 onward. So for roughly
the first three quarters of SHHARICH's FY26 the sulphur chain was flat at an elevated level,
and the sharp leg lands in the final six weeks of FY26 and into Q1 FY27.
**Severity: MAJOR.** SHHARICH's full-year FY26 margin compression cannot be attributed to a
within-FY26 raw-material spike on this evidence.

### B3. SHREEPUSHK's Q3 FY26 was volume-led with realisations down 21%, the opposite of the regime flip B06 dates to that quarter
