### 2B. Excuse pattern analysis

Both misses in the table above (rows 3 and 7) share a pattern: **silence
or minimisation rather than substantive explanation.** The Malaysia JV
dissolution is disclosed only via the mandated Emphasis of Matter
paragraph in the auditor's report — a compliance-driven disclosure, not a
management-volunteered one — with zero qualitative discussion of why a
JV described as a strength one year earlier failed within roughly twelve
months. The repeat SEBI/BSE disclosure lapse is explained with
minimising language ("inadvertent," "purely... technical," "no
intention") that assigns the cause to process error without naming which
process or who owns it. Neither case shows the pattern "we made a
mistake, here is specifically what happened and what changes"; both are
closer to **deflection** than honest admission. There is no comparison
case here of blaming external factors while claiming credit internally
(no downturn or competitor-blame instance was found in the sources
read), so the excuse pattern is best classified as **external-blame/
technical-minimisation on compliance matters, silence on the JV
dissolution** — not a balanced or transparent pattern, but also not one
that invokes macro/competitor scapegoating.

### 3A. Competitor commentary
Not from management directly (no concalls); the rating agency's write-up
places HPBL's margins "relatively better than that of the commodity
segment of the HDPP sack industry, particularly manufacturers catering to
the cement and fertiliser segments, where EBITDA margins are typically

directional, not a verified competitive claim. HPBL is also described as
facing "competition from manufacturers in China, Vietnam, Bangladesh, and
other low-cost Asian countries" on export pricing (RATING p.7).

- Global FIBC market projected to reach US$12.6 billion by 2033, CAGR
  5.4% (MD&A, AR p.56).
- India cited as holding "75% share in the European FIBC imports and 72%
  share in the US import market" per an unnamed "industry expert" (MD&A,
  AR p.56) — source is not identified in the AR itself, weakening the
  claim's verifiability.
- India is described as the largest exporter of FIBC jumbo bags globally,
  ahead of Turkey and China, with July 2024 export shipment counts cited
  (MD&A, AR p.56).
- Global polypropylene woven sacks market expected to surpass US$6.1
  billion by 2034, CAGR 4.2% (MD&A, AR p.56-57).

  annual rate contracts and routine orders (RATING p.5).

  receivable days ~61, inventory days ~39 (RATING p.7).
