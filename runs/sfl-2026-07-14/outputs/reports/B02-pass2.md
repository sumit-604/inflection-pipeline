## NEW FINDINGS, BY CATEGORY

### 1. Accounting policies (Note 2.2, STA p.88 — partially covered in Pass 1)

- **Depreciation method is Written Down Value (WDV), not straight-line** (Note 2.2,
  STA p.88: "Depreciation ... is provided on a pro-rata basis on written down value
  basis"). Pass 1 flagged the extended useful lives themselves but did not record the
  method. WDV front-loads depreciation relative to SLM within a given useful life, but
  combined with materially longer lives than Schedule II (Pass 1 Finding #7), the *net*
  effect on the current year's charge is ambiguous from the notes alone — the method
  detail sharpens rather than resolves that flag. 🟡 Watch (context addition).

- **Depreciation & amortisation expense barely moved despite growth, a quantified
  corroboration of the useful-life flag**: [STA] Note 39 (p.115) total depreciation
  ₹3,238.96 lakh (FY22) vs ₹3,252.55 lakh (FY21) — **fell 0.4%** even though gross PPE
  additions were ₹1,607.21 lakh in the year (Note 3, p.98) and standalone revenue grew
  25.4%. [CON] Note 41 (p.184) total depreciation ₹8,077.73 lakh vs ₹7,286.72 lakh,
  **+10.9%**, materially slower than consolidated revenue growth of 22.4% and slower
  than Group borrowings growth (+64.3%, which funded new capex). This is the first
  *quantified* evidence in the notes that the extended-useful-life policy flagged in
  Pass 1 is suppressing the depreciation charge relative to the scale of the business,
  not merely a theoretical concern. 🟡 Watch — reinforces Pass 1 Finding #7 with actual
  reported numbers (no Schedule-II counterfactual is estimated here, only the reported
  trend itself).

### 2. Related party transactions (Note 44 STA pp.119–123; Note 3/58/59 CON — new items)

- **CWIP physical-asset transfer to ICT**: [STA] Note 3 note (p.98–99), CWIP
  "Disposals/transfer" footnote: *"Capital work in progress 'Disposal' includes
  ₹1,819 lakh (March 31, 2021: Nil) sold to its wholly owned subsidiary International
  Comfort Technologies Private Limited."* This is a new cross-reference Pass 1 did not
  connect: the parent transferred a specific **under-construction physical asset**
  (not just capital/loan/guarantee) to the newly incorporated ICT during the year, the
  first such transfer (prior year: nil). This item is most likely already embedded
  within the RPT Note 44 "Sale of material/capital goods (subsidiaries)" line of
  ₹3,286.20 lakh (Pass 1 Section 2) rather than additive to the ₹118 crore already
  flagged — flagged here for the *composition* detail (a plant-under-construction
  transfer, not routine trading stock), which changes the qualitative picture of what
  was moved to the new entity. 🟡 Watch.

- **Multi-site capex footprint funded by ICT's debt is broader than "Nandigram"
  alone**: [CON] Note 22(g) "Repayment schedule for secured loan taken during the
  year" (CON p.~183–184) states the JP Morgan INR loan and Kotak Mahindra USD loan
  fund capex "at their new plants in Nandigram and Jabalpur" and "at Maneri, Medhi
  Niwas, Jabalpur, Madhya Pradesh and Nandigram, Umbergaon, Valsad, Gujarat"
  respectively — five site names across two loans, versus Pass 1's single-site
  characterisation ("Nandigram capex"). Immaterial to the ₹ figures already reported,
  useful context only. 🟢 Clean (informational).

### 3. Investments / subsidiary performance (Note 58, CON pp.200–201 — largely new)

- **🔴 Consolidated PAT fell 8.9% YoY while consolidated revenue grew 22.4% —
  a Group-level profitability decline Pass 1 did not surface.** Note 58 "Statutory
  Group Information" totals row: Group "Share in profit and loss" fell from
  **₹24,015.17 lakh (FY21) to ₹21,872.80 lakh (FY22)**, and Group Total Comprehensive
  Income fell more sharply, from ₹25,896.27 lakh to ₹21,687.73 lakh (**-16.25%**)
  (Note 58, CON p.200–201). Cross-referenced against consolidated revenue of
  ₹2,98,180.84 lakh vs ₹2,43,719.22 lakh (+22.4%, Note 33 CON, already in Pass 1),
  Group net margin compressed from ~9.85% to ~7.34% of revenue.

- **Driver, quantified from the same note**: aggregate subsidiary-level profit
  contribution (all six subsidiaries plus NCI, ex-parent) collapsed from **₹6,140.19
  lakh in FY21 to ₹1,988.75 lakh in FY22, a ~68% fall** (computed from the individual
  entity rows in Note 58, CON p.200 — no estimation, all component figures are
  as-reported):
  - International Foam Technologies Spain SLU: profit ₹3,267.52 lakh → ₹1,977.80 lakh
    (**-39.5%**); its OCI line flipped from **+₹1,388.98 lakh (74% of Group OCI) in
    FY21 to -₹8.53 lakh in FY22** — almost the entire swing in Group OCI traces to
    this one entity, most likely FX-translation-reserve driven (not explained in the
    note itself — NOT FOUND as to cause).
  - Joyce Foam Pty Ltd, Australia: profit ₹2,694.96 lakh → ₹2,038.01 lakh (**-24.4%**),
    even as its net assets grew (₹9,281.71 lakh → ₹13,782.87 lakh, +48.5%).
  - Staqo World Pvt Ltd: swung from **+₹291.15 lakh profit (FY21) to -₹1,156.72 lakh
    loss (FY22)** — already flagged in Pass 1 as a standalone item, but not previously
    tied to the Group PAT decline as a contributing driver.
  - Sleepwell Enterprises Pvt Ltd (see below, new item).
  - International Comfort Technologies: first-period loss -₹591.13 lakh (already in
    Pass 1).
  - Divya Software Solutions: loss narrowed slightly, -₹421.95 lakh (FY21) to
    -₹390.48 lakh (FY22).
  - Parent (standalone Sheela Foam) profit still grew +11.2% (₹17,874.98 lakh →
    ₹19,884.05 lakh) — its *share* of consolidated PAT mechanically rose from 74% to
    91%, a presentation effect that could obscure the underlying subsidiary-level
    profit collapse if the table is read only in percentage terms rather than
    absolute ₹.
  🔴 Red Flag (new) — this is a materially important, previously unflagged Group-level
  earnings-quality signal: revenue growth at the Group level is being funded/achieved
  alongside a broad-based profit deterioration at every foreign and Indian subsidiary
  except the still-nascent Divya Software (whose loss merely narrowed, not turned
  positive).

- **Sleepwell Enterprises Pvt Ltd (pure trademark/royalty licensing entity — per Note
  59 CON p.201, its stated principal activity is solely "Providing of its Trademarks,
  Patents, Logos etc. and earning royalty thereon") flipped to a net loss of ₹28.56
  lakh in FY22 from a profit of ₹65.42 lakh in FY21**, with net assets eroding from
  ₹235.89 lakh to ₹207.32 lakh (Note 58, CON p.200). A pure IP-licensing shell turning
  loss-making is unusual for that business model and is not commented on anywhere in
  the notes (NOT FOUND — no explanation of the swing). 🟡 Watch.

- **Consolidation-scope cross-check (secondary, partly outside strict notes scope,
  flagged for completeness):** the CFS auditor's "Other Matters" paragraph (part of
  the audited financial statements package, not itself a numbered note) states the
  Group comprises **7 subsidiaries (including 2 step-down subsidiaries)** not audited
  by the principal auditor, matching Note 59 CON's (p.201) list of 6 direct
  subsidiaries plus 2 named step-downs (Interplasp SL Spain; Joyce WC NSW Pty Ltd).
  Separately, the Board's Report (MD&A, printed p.34–35, outside notes scope) states
  the Company "has six subsidiaries and **five** steps down subsidiaries" and names
  additional Staqo-World-linked step-down entities (Staqo World KFT Hungary, Staqo
  Inc USA, Staqo Technologies LLC, and a UAE entity) that do **not** appear in Note 59
  CON's ownership table or in the auditor's 2-step-down count. This is a scope
  reconciliation gap between the Board's Report subsidiary count and the audited
  consolidation scope disclosed in the notes — flagged as a question for management
  (are these entities dormant/immaterial/not controlled under Ind AS 110, or simply
  omitted from Note 59's table?). 🟡 Watch (secondary; not a pure notes-only item, use
  with appropriate caveat).

### 4. Ratio disclosure (Note 60, STA pp.135–136 — Pass 1 only used two rows of this table)

- **🔴 Broad-based decline across every profitability and return ratio in the
  company's own SEBI-mandated ratio note, not previously surfaced:** Note 60 (STA
  p.135–136) reports, alongside the two rows Pass 1 used (payable and receivable
  turnover):
  - Current Ratio: 1.56 (FY22) vs 1.67 (FY21), **-7%**.
  - Net Profit Ratio: 0.09 vs 0.11, **-17%** (no variance-reason given in the note).
  - Return on Equity Ratio: 0.16 vs 0.18, **-12%** (no reason given).
  - Return on Capital Employed: 0.21 vs 0.23, **-12%** (no reason given).
  - Return on Investment: 0.22 vs 0.25, **-11%** (no reason given).
  - Debt Service Coverage Ratio: 0.03 vs 0.04, **-16%** (no reason given).
  Cross-checked against the MD&A "Key Financial Ratios" table (printed p.30, PDF
  marker p.61 — outside strict notes scope but restating the same underlying figures
  in % form): Operating Profit Margin **12.08% → 9.48%**; Net Profit Margin
  **10.82% → 9.23%**; Return on Net Worth **18.08% → 16.22%**. All of this occurs
  despite standalone revenue growth of +25.4% (Note 31, already in Pass 1).
  **Disclosure pattern**: Note 60's own "Reasons for variances" column is populated
  only for the ratios that *improved* (Trade payable turnover +33%, Net capital
  turnover +54%, and Trade receivables turnover -13% — the last one's narrative was
  already flagged in Pass 1 as internally contradictory). None of the *declining*
  ratios above carry a variance explanation — none breaches the 25%-variance
  disclosure trigger under the SEBI/Schedule III format so this is not a compliance
  breach, but the net effect of the note as drafted is that only good news is
  narrated. 🔴 Red Flag (new) — a genuine, previously unflagged margin-compression
  signal at the standalone level, corroborating the consolidated PAT decline in
  Section 3 above.
  - Minor technical footnote: Note 60's own formula column defines "Net Profit" (for
    the Net Profit Ratio) as **"Total comprehensive income for the year"**, not
    standard PAT — a non-standard ratio definition, immaterial to the direction of
    the finding but worth noting for like-for-like peer comparison. 🟡 Watch.

### 5. Other Expenses (Note 40 STA p.115; Note 42 CON p.184 — new drill-down, quantifies the margin story)

- **Warranty expense (created) nearly doubled**: [STA] ₹983.10 lakh (FY22) vs
  ₹498.57 lakh (FY21), **+97.2%**; [CON] ₹970.42 lakh vs ₹498.57 lakh, **+94.7%** —
  both far outpacing revenue growth (+25.4% STA / +22.4% CON). Pass 1 discussed the
  warranty provision *movement* (Note 25) but did not flag that the current-year
  *charge* nearly doubled YoY. No explanation is given anywhere in the notes for the
  near-doubling (NOT FOUND — could indicate a product-quality issue, a change in
  provisioning estimate, or genuine claims increase; not distinguishable from the
  notes alone). 🟡 Watch — potential quality-of-earnings and product-quality signal.
- **Advertisement expense grew faster than revenue**: [STA] ₹6,379.10 lakh vs

  both outpacing revenue growth, a concrete driver of the margin compression flagged
  in Section 4. 🟡 Watch (informational, explains rather than adds new risk).
- **Legal and professional charges roughly doubled at Group level**: [CON] ₹1,323.07

  in Pass 1 (Section 2, 6, 7), but not explained in the notes (NOT FOUND). [STA]
  comparable line grew a smaller +34.3% (₹657.30 lakh vs ₹489.22 lakh). 🟡 Watch.
- **First-year FVTPL mark-to-market loss**: ₹382.97 lakh (FY22, both STA and CON;

  ₹5,006.80 lakh FVTPL-unquoted bond investment flagged in Pass 1 (Note 8, STA

  Pass 1 did not capture. 🟡 Watch.
