# SECTION 1: THE BUSINESS MODEL IN PLAIN ENGLISH

## 1A One-line description
JLHL rents out the use of its own hospital buildings, beds, machines and nurses to patients in Thane, Pune, Indore and Dombivli, earns a bill per patient stay and per clinic visit, and pays about a quarter of every rupee it earns to the doctors who treat those patients (AR26 p.5, p.43; C Note 24 p.218; C Note 29 p.219).

## 1E The chai-stall-uncle version
Think of JLHL as a landlord of very expensive hotel rooms with a medical team attached. The company owns the building and the machines. Doctors are like visiting chefs who get paid per customer they serve. Each night a room is filled, the hospital earns about Rs 68,000 on average (FY26 ARPOB, AR26 p.20). A new hotel costs a lot to build and sits half-empty for years while the staff is already on payroll. The old hotels in Thane and Pune are close to full at about 70% (CC Oct p.4), so they print cash. The new hotel in Dombivli is in its first year, and the cash from the old ones pays for it and for the next three.

## 2D Industry lifecycle and JLHL's position
Indian private hospital care is in growth. Hospital segment USD 193.42 Bn in CY2025 to USD 364.55 Bn by CY2034, CAGR 7.3% for CY2026 to CY2034 (AR26 p.39). Health insurance penetration rose from about 37% (CY21) to about 41% (CY25); out-of-pocket is still about 44% of spend; about 430 Mn people sit in a "missing middle" (AR26 p.41). JLHL is in a capacity-build phase: 1,248 operational beds at Mar-26 against 1,681 built or fitted capacity (Thane 364 + Pune 386 + Indore 431 + Dombivli 500, AR26 p.7-8, derived) and 2,881 planned (adds Mira 300, BKC 400, Pune South 500). So 433 beds (Indore 122, Dombivli 300, Baner 11) can open without any new land (derived). Revenue grew 15.2% in FY26 on the same-base re-cut (B03); the industry figure is in USD and a different base, so the two are not compared.

Cost drivers: doctor fees (growing faster than revenue) and the fixed block of other expenses, D&A and interest.

Operating leverage:
- FY26 incremental EBITDA margin = (343.3 - 300.1) / (1,499.8 - 1,302.4) = 43.2 / 197.4 = 21.9% (Rs Cr, derived; Pres Q4 p.5 and p.7; both years on the AR26 re-cut base). That is below the 22.9% average because of Dombivli's Q4 loss (Rs 9.4 Cr, Pres Q4 p.7) and new-hospital costs.
- Add back the Dombivli loss: (343.3 + 9.4 - 300.1) / 197.4 = 26.7% (derived, indicative; Dombivli revenue NOT FOUND so its revenue stays in the denominator, which makes this a loose read).
- H1 FY27: EBITDA +15.5 (186.2 vs 170.7) on income +120.9 (867.5 vs 746.6) = 12.8%; ex Dombivli loss of 9.7: +25.2 / 120.9 = 20.8% (derived, same caveat).
- FY26 profit bridge (Rs Cr, Pres Q4 p.7): EBITDA +43.2; D&A -30.5; finance cost -22.0; finance income +14.1; exceptional (Labour Code) -4.9; net PBT -0.3 (260.4 vs 260.7). H1 FY27: EBITDA +15.5; D&A -12.2; finance cost -10.6; finance income -3.0; PBT -10.3 (125.7 vs 136.0); PAT -7.4% (Pres Q2 p.6, p.8). So the squeeze deepened in H1 FY27, not eased.
- D&A step: FY26 D&A Rs Mn 876.40 vs 570.83, rise 305.57, of which right-of-use (leasehold land) depreciation +126.25 (41%) and PPE +170.53 (C Note 31 p.219, derived).
- Fixed-cost absorption: the Q1 to Q2 FY27 EBITDA margin jump from 19.3% to 23.4% (Pres Q2 p.8) shows how fast margin recovers when a ramp-up loss shrinks, helped by Q2 seasonality. It is not a steady-state read.

New-hospital unit: Dombivli. Cost Rs Cr 425 (AR26 p.11), but B03 shows capitalised Rs Mn 3,881.3 and "on budget" has no baseline. Capacity 500 beds, 200 operational, fit-out for 300+ (AR26 p.11); operating census beds about 144 (CC Oct p.8). Management guides EBITDA breakeven by end of year 2 (Feb 2028, AR26 p.12); Q2 FY27 loss Rs 0.2 Cr. Indore (bought Nov 2020) is the in-house comparison: FY25 occupancy 54.9% (AR25 p.5), Q2 FY27 revenue +18% with EBITDA flat, and management expects "bumps" as it adds beds and doctors (CC Oct p.6). So a mature hospital is a moving target: each bed add resets occupancy. Mature plateau for Thane and Pune is about 70% occupancy; Pune has no more expansion room, Thane's added beds need regulatory clearance not yet received (CC Oct p.4, oral). With ARPOB growth at those two tied to inflation, the next leg of growth is bed count, not price.

## 4B Valuation method applicability (formal handoff to Role 1 / stage 11)

The destination PE comes only from Section 1B at stage 11. This table picks the method, not the multiple.

| Method | Applicable? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Bottom-up EBITDA build per hospital (beds x occupancy x ARPOB x margin) then EV/EBITDA | PRIMARY | Earnings are depressed by D&A, interest and ramp losses, so P/E on current EPS misreads value. Capacity is dated (Pune CY28, Mira CY29, BKC CY31) and each hospital has its own occupancy path. |
| P/E on steady-state earnings, Section 1B destination PE | SECONDARY (exit and cross-check) | Entry and exit must sit on the same earnings basis (Amendment 18). Convert the EBITDA build to PAT after the higher D&A and interest. |
| EV per operational bed against replacement cost (Rs Cr 1.0 to 1.5 per bed ex land, decks) | TERTIARY | Floor cross-check only; land is owned and some beds are not yet earning. |
| DCF | Not primary | Terminal value dominates, ramp and capex timing carry the value; use only as a check on the build. |
| P/B or ROE-based | Not applicable | ROE 13% in a build cycle; book includes CWIP and land premium. |
| EV/Sales, P/S | Not applicable | Margin gap between mature and new hospitals makes revenue multiples misleading. |
| SOTP/NAV of land | Not applicable | Land is operating infrastructure, not surplus; real-estate value is a floor idea only. |
| DDM | Not applicable | Dividend payout NOT FOUND in the documents read; cash is being retained for capex. |

Primary: bottom-up EBITDA ramp by hospital. Secondary: P/E at the Section 1B destination on the same earnings basis. Tertiary: EV per bed. Cycle stage that matters: capacity-build stage (D&A and interest peak before Pune South CY28), not an economic cycle.
