### 1C Two readings for the material items the pipeline missed or only partly caught

M4, backlog pace (Fact 1).
- Reading A: the 85-90% applies to the core services order in hand only. The question asked about core. The CFO opened with "typically these are short cycle orders which gets complete within 12 months". This fits the MD's 2-year total-book visibility, the 3-4 year upgrade cycle and the plus or minus 5% revenue guide.
- Reading B: the 85-90% applies to the full INR 1,628 cr book. That is how the next analyst read it, and management let it stand. Then FY27 revenue is at least INR 1,384 cr from backlog alone (calc), and the plus or minus 5% guide was wrong.
- Separating observation: FY27 revenue against INR 1,384 cr. A management rupee figure for the core order in hand would also settle it. The CFO gave only "+40% YoY" (Q4 p.9).
- Effect: under Reading A, the 85-90% cannot be applied to the INR 1,218 cr deck "services backlog", which includes FGD O&M. B05 reached the same caution on labels. It did not use the contradiction, which is the stronger evidence.

M6, ECL reversal placement.
- Reading A: the CFO misspoke on Q4. The INR 44 cr sits in cost, as he said on Q3. The INR 316 cr is clean revenue, and the analyst left with a revenue base 13% too low (calc: 41 / 316).
- Reading B: Q4 revenue carries about INR 41 cr of one-off "other benefits". Underlying revenue was then about INR 275 cr (calc). That is below the INR 300-320 cr per-quarter band, and the "19%" Q4 growth "driven by upgrade volumes" is partly one-off.
- Separating observation: the line placement of the Q4 FY26 ECL reversal in the filed Q4 FY26 results, and any one-off inside revenue from operations. Verifier A or a corpus read can settle it. I cannot from the transcripts.
- Effect: B05 marks "INR 300-320 cr per quarter" Delivered using Q4 FY26 INR 316 cr. Under Reading B, the Q4 FY26 point fails. B05's Q1 and Q2 FY27 filed points would then carry the row.

M7, Q3 revenue attribution.
- Reading A: the MD's "4% upside" is core revenue for the Dec quarter. Then core did not drive the 22% growth. Settlement revenue and upgrades did.
- Reading B: the 4% refers to another base, such as nine-month core revenue. Then the CFO's attribution can stand for the quarter.
- Separating observation: quarterly core revenue in rupees. It was never given on a call.
- B05 has the core point that "revenue includes one-offs" (3C item 5). It does not have the 4% or the 68% share of the increase.

M10, three-party BHEL settlement.
- Reading A: arm's-length. GVPIL recovered 95% of its BHEL receivable (calc: 340 / 357). The promoter entities settled their own technology-gap exposure separately.
- Reading B: the listed company carried, for years, a receivable that BHEL withheld for defects in technology the promoter group supplied. It wrote off INR 17 cr (calc), keeps a PG-test support obligation, and the group-side terms are not disclosed.
- Separating observation: the related-party note or a Reg 30 disclosure of the settlement terms between BHEL and the Vernova entities, and of any GVPIL obligation performed on their behalf.
- Thesis link: related-party conduct is one of this run's load-bearing facts (B06 calls Facts 2 and 4 "related party, concentration and cash"). Neither report mentions this exchange.

M11, India-only perimeter.
- Reading A: the perimeter is a fixed group rule. Growth stays inside India plus GE-boiler spares abroad, and long-term TAM is capped by the parent's allocation.
- Reading B: the perimeter is moving. The Saudi Shoaiba order that B05 cites from Reg 30 (fuel conversion, supply plus service, about INR 550 cr) goes beyond "only for the supply of the spare parts".
- Separating observation: a management statement, or a group disclosure, that the export mandate now covers retrofit and fuel conversion.
- B05 records "India region only" in the 1C evolution table. It does not raise it as a flag or tie it to trigger 5.
