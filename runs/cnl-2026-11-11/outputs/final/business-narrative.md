## Business understanding

Creative Newtech sells technology products in two ways, and the two earn very different money. 86% of FY26 sales came from distribution: the company imports other companies' memory cards, monitors, imaging, networking and surveillance products, warehouses them, and sells them on credit to more than 10,000 channel partners in 300 cities. Principals use it because it puts their brand into Indian retail, online marketplaces, quick commerce and the government GeM portal without a sales force of their own, yet the company keeps less than ₹4 of gross margin on every ₹100 it sells. The other 14% is the brand business: Honeywell branded chargers, audio, laptop accessories, air purifiers and structured cabling sold under an exclusive licence across South Asia, Southeast Asia, the Middle East and Africa, plus CyberPowerPC gaming PCs and the new WOZOYO own brand. That line keeps about ₹33 of every ₹100 and produced 59% of FY26 segment profit.

The buyers are resellers, system integrators, large format retailers, online and quick commerce platforms, government departments, and, for the Honeywell range, distributors and retailers in 38 countries. They buy order by order on credit, and the run found no contract, qualification cycle or switching cost that ties them to the company. 25% of revenue now comes from the largest customer, down from a historical 50 to 60% according to the rating agency, while one related party, Click Retail, owed about ₹48 Cr at March 2026.

Today's demand rides on PC and component pricing, which the IDC India PC tracker and the INR/USD rate and memory prices will show first: peers report memory and CPU costs up 30 to 50% and passed through, with consumer units falling. Rashi Peripherals and Redington quarterly results give the channel read on that demand, and the STQC / MeitY CCTV testing rules decide which cameras can be sold in surveillance.

Three forward drivers can be checked from outside the company. India data centre capacity additions feed the cabling and server lines, and Redington reports its technology solutions group up 91% on AI and data centre deals. STQC rules are moving surveillance share to certified brands, which Aditya Infotech (CP Plus) quarterly results track, though CP Plus is a capex heavy manufacturer rather than a licensee. BSNL BharatNet Odisha billing and guarantees would add a project business of about ₹3,195 Cr that sits outside the distribution market, and the run found no exchange filing for that order.

The distribution line has no moat: principals can move their brands, pricing power is weak, and the spread is a price taker's spread, so its only edge is reach. The brand line has a narrow moat resting on one licence, and the Honeywell licence renewal decides it, because the term ends in March 2027 and nothing has been signed. The emerging moat scan scored 12.8, or 12.5 after the verifier regraded one evidence tier, the bottom of the MODEST band, with no category rated Strong, three Moderate (falling customer concentration, the Honeywell and CyberPowerPC partnerships, and the Zicom and Infinova bolt ons) and zero on both talent asymmetry and cannibalization barrier.

## A distributor growing faster than its cash

Creative Newtech booked ₹2,705 Cr of revenue in FY26, up 52%, and ₹70 Cr of profit. Most of that revenue is distribution of other companies' products at a gross margin under 4%. The money sits in the Honeywell licence, which carries 14% of sales and 59% of segment profit. 51% of group profit is earned by two Hong Kong subsidiaries that pay no tax and are not audited by the group auditor.

The company is trying to turn from a distributor into a brand owner. Brand share rose from 14% of sales in FY26 to 17% in the first half of FY27 and 19% in the September quarter. Market entry segment margin rose too, to 6.7% in the first half of FY27 from 3.4% a year earlier, on a Make-in-India supplier mix. Management now adds a BSNL BharatNet order of about ₹3,195 Cr, the Infinova camera acquisition, the ₹50.5 Cr Zicom SaaS deal and the WOZOYO brand, and targets ₹6,000 Cr of revenue with 40% from brands by FY29. None of those four lines had disclosed revenue by September 2026, and the Honeywell licence that carries the transition expires in March 2027.

The backward scorecard reads AVERAGE at 60 of 160, with a core score of 49 of 100. Growth carried the score: revenue compounded 33% a year and profit 40% from FY19 to FY26. Cash sank it. Operating cash flow was negative in seven of eight years, and cumulative operating cash flow of minus ₹329 Cr sits against ₹216 Cr of cumulative profit. That ratio, minus 1.5 times, is a deal breaker that caps the grade at AVERAGE. The verifier found the scorecard used a substitute return on capital formula; the corrected readings put the core score anywhere from 42 to 56 and leave the class unchanged.

Cash is the centre of this file. FY26 operating cash flow was minus ₹272 Cr in the audited accounts, and the Q4 results print minus ₹273 Cr. Receivable days went from 49 to 76, and short term bank borrowings went from ₹70 Cr to ₹324 Cr, above the ₹209 Cr of rated facilities. The first half of FY27 then showed plus ₹351 Cr. That turn came from ₹282 Cr of extra supplier credit and ₹193 Cr of other current liabilities, while receivables rose another ₹52 Cr to ₹617 Cr. The new CFO called the resulting 45 working capital days unsustainable and put normal at 52 to 55. On the other side, the rating agency reaffirmed BBB+ with a stable outlook in August 2026, says debtors are generally realised within the credit period, and puts the stretch down to year end billing and extended credit to a few customers. Domestic receivable days were about 112 in both years on the run's own derivation, which points to a shift toward Indian sales rather than slower collection. Until a half year shows collections, not supplier credit, carrying the cash, the turn is reversible.

Management grades C on promises against delivery: 6 delivered, 5 partial and 8 missed across 19 items over three calls. Profit beat, at ₹70 Cr against about ₹60 Cr guided, and Honeywell sales landed at ₹370 Cr inside the guide. Operations slipped. The own brand launch moved twice, revenue growth guidance fell from 25 to 30% to about 20%, brand EBITDA guidance fell from 15 to 16% to 13%, and receivables sat at 21% of turnover in September 2026 against a promised 13 to 14%. The verifier went further: on the May 2026 call the CFO told analysts bank debt rose about ₹14 Cr, while the audited balance sheet shows ₹84 Cr of bank debt growth on top of ₹171 Cr of vendor finance, and the verifier read the grade as C toward D. The promoter check came back CAUTION, not CONCERN. It found no regulatory or legal record and no pledge on aggregator data, but a related party web of 27 entities, a CFO resignation one month before the weak FY26 cash print, and company documents that disagree on whether the Honeywell subsidiary is wholly owned or 77.5% owned.

The three peers confirm the backdrop and complicate the story. Redington, Rashi and CP Plus all report memory, storage and CPU costs up 30 to 50% and passed on, and Redington and Rashi confirm AI data centre demand turning into revenue. Redington also reports consumer units falling, though the verifier noted CNL's own MD had already said consumers cannot absorb 30 to 40% hikes. Peer distributor margins sit in a narrow band, Redington EBITDA at 2.2 to 2.4%, so CNL's 6% market entry margin needs a company reason no peer can test. CP Plus, the closest brand analogue, runs a 31 to 32% gross margin and a 14 to 15% EBITDA margin and spends ₹200 to 300 Cr on capex, which complicates both the 40 to 42% brand gross margin claim and the asset light plan. Against that claim, the audited brand segment result is 33%. The ₹30,000 Cr surveillance market management quotes does not reconcile with CP Plus's own share arithmetic, which implies about ₹8,000 Cr.

The runway is GOOD. The run sized the addressable market at about ₹41,700 Cr on conservative inputs and ₹70,700 Cr on realistic ones, and the serviceable slice at about ₹20,100 Cr, of which CNL holds about 9%. That leaves 11 times revenue headroom. The obtainable share works out to about ₹3,240 Cr in three years and ₹4,040 Cr in five on India revenue, India growth of 22% then 18% a year, or 15% and 13% at company level with overseas sales held flat. Management's market figures run 9.3 times the conservative addressable market, and its ₹6,000 Cr FY29 target would need a fifth of the serviceable market, ₹1,860 Cr above the run's company level estimate. The serviceable cut rests on an unsourced 50% channel assumption, and the surveillance market sources diverge 4.3 times.
