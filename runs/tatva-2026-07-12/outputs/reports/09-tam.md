## SECTION 1: MARKET DEFINITION

### 1A — Precise boundaries

**Product scope**: Four disclosed revenue lines, each a *distinct* chemical sub-market, not
one market:
- **PTC** — Phase Transfer Catalysts (quaternary ammonium/phosphonium salts) used to speed
  organic-synthesis reactions in pharma API, agrochemical, flavour/fragrance and
  environment-control manufacturing. 23% of FY26 revenue (IP p.13, p.19).
- **SDA** — Structure Directing Agents, high-purity quaternary salts used as templating
  chemicals in synthetic zeolite manufacture (auto-catalyst/emission-control end-use
  dominant). 41% of FY26 revenue (IP p.14, p.19).
- **ESS** — Electrolyte Salts & Solutions, specifically for **super-capacitor** batteries
  (not lithium-ion battery electrolyte — a different chemistry and a materially smaller
  market, see 1B/2 below). 3% of FY26 revenue (IP p.16, p.19).
- **PASC** — Pharma & Agrochemical Intermediates and other Specialty Chemicals, dominated by
  Glymes (used as API/Li-battery solvents), plus disinfectants, catalysts and solvents.
  32% of FY26 revenue (IP p.17, p.19).

**Geographic scope**: Global manufacture and sale from two Gujarat plants (Ankleshwar,
Dahej SEZ); exports to 25+ (IP p.11) to 35+ (AR p.6-7) countries — the two company documents
disagree on the exact export-country count, both used as directional only. FY24-25 revenue
mix: Domestic ~38%, Exports ~62% (India 38.02%, USA 14.53%, Singapore 21.40% — largely a
trading/distribution hub not end-demand, Germany 9.15%, China 3.19%, others 13.71%) (AR p.9).

**Customer scope**: B2B industrial buyers only — API/agrochemical manufacturers, auto-catalyst
and zeolite producers, super-capacitor/EV component makers, paints/coatings and personal-care
formulators. No B2C exposure in any segment.

**Channel scope**: Direct sale plus two wholly-owned offshore trading subsidiaries (USA,
Netherlands) which book a disproportionate share of consolidated profit (per B04 flag, ~94%
of FY24-25 consolidated PAT sat in the offshore entities) — a margin-location detail, not a
demand-scope one.

**Price segment**: High-purity/technical-grade specialty chemicals, not commodity-grade.
Entry barriers of 1–6 years for product development/approval are repeatedly cited by
management (AR p.6, IP p.15) — relevant to the SAM capability filter (Section 3A) and to the
share-gain-rate judgment in Section 3B.

**Explicit exclusions**: Bulk/commodity chemicals, lithium-ion battery electrolytes (ESS is
super-capacitor specific), full-spectrum pharma API/formulation manufacturing (Tatva makes
intermediates and solvents, not APIs), and the broad global "pharma & agro intermediates"
industry (management's own PASC-adjacent slide cites a $133bn 2022 global figure for this —
this is **not** Tatva's addressable market; see 1B and Section 2 flag below).

Management does not use the word "TAM" anywhere in the AR or IP. The closest constructed
claim is Investor Presentation p.30, "Global Chemical Industry," which places four boxes
next to the company's own four product-category names, each with a 2022 base and 2028F
figure, **no source citation given on that slide**:

| Segment (IP label) | 2022 (USD) | 2028F (USD) | CAGR |
|---|---|---|---|
| PTC | $1,190mn | $1,640mn | 5.5% |
| SDA | $1.19bn | $1.65bn | 5.6% |
| Electrolyte Salts | $6.4bn | $12bn | 11.0% |
| Intermediates | $133bn | $179bn | 5.1% |

Summed: **$141.78bn (2022)** — dated May 2026, i.e. current as of the IP but referencing
4-year-old (2022) base data.

**Credibility read: BROAD, tipping into dishonest by the framework's own test** ("management
claiming TAM = SAM = growth runway is being dishonest"). Two of the four boxes do not match
Tatva's actual addressable niche once cross-checked against independent market research
(Section 2):
- The **"Intermediates" $133bn** figure is the *entire global pharma+agrochemical
  intermediates industry* — Tatva's PASC line is Glymes plus a narrow set of intermediates/
  solvents (32% of company revenue from a company with ₹505.86cr total FY26 revenue). This
  is the textbook "TAM=SAM" overstatement the framework's opening line warns against.
- The **"Electrolyte Salts" $6.4bn** figure is ~50-57x larger than the independently-
  researched super-capacitor-electrolyte-specific market (Section 2 Method 1), strongly
  suggesting it is the broader battery-electrolyte market (dominated by lithium-ion
  chemistry, which Tatva does not compete in), not Tatva's actual ESS niche.
- The **SDA $1.19bn (2022)** figure is roughly 2x every independent estimate found
  (Section 2), and roughly 2x the company's *own* AR figure filed 15 months later ($600mn,

- The **PTC $1,190mn (2022)** figure is the one internally consistent box: it broadly

By contrast, the **AR's Management Discussion & Analysis (p.116-125, filed Aug 2025)** gives
more specific, partially-sourced figures used as the primary anchor for Section 2 below:
India chemical industry "USD 220 Billion... to reach USD 300 Billion by 2030 and USD 1
Trillion by 2040" (independently corroborated by IBEF, see Section 2 Method 5); India
specialty chemicals "market size of nearly USD 40 Billion by 2023," growing at "CAGR
exceeding 11%" (AR p.117, also repeated in the Chairman's letter, AR p.16); global specialty
chemicals "USD 285.4 Billion (2023)... USD 364.8 Billion by 2028," 5.0% CAGR (AR p.117); PTC
market "USD 1.07 Billion in 2024... USD 1.41 Billion by 2029," CAGR 5.79%, **explicitly
cited to "Mordor intelligence"** (AR p.118); SDA "global industry size... USD 600 Million in
2023... USD 1.6 Billion by 2036," CAGR 8% (AR p.119, no source named).

**Credibility read on the AR figures: REASONABLE** — narrower, dated, and (for PTC) sourced;
cross-validates against independent research within ~15-20%. This is the primary basis for
Section 2's conservative estimate.

## SECTION 2: TAM ESTIMATION, MULTIPLE METHODS

### Method 1 — Top-down, product-line specific (primary method)

Starting point: the four disclosed segment markets, using the most-recent, best-sourced
figure for each and cross-validating against independent web research performed for this
report (search log at the end).

| Segment | Base estimate (source) | Independent cross-check (web search) | Divergence read |
|---|---|---|---|
| **PTC** | $1.07bn (2024), 5.79% CAGR to 2029 — AR p.118, cites Mordor Intelligence | Grand View Research: $1.25bn (2024) → $1.68bn (2030), 6.0% CAGR | Consistent, ~15% apart — **use $1.07bn (lower)** |
| **SDA** | $600mn (2023), 8% CAGR to 2036 — AR p.119, unsourced | FutureMarketInsights $287mn (2024); ResearchNester $629mn (2025); 24ChemicalResearch implies ~$959mn by 2030 | Wide range but AR's $600mn sits inside it — **use $600mn** |
| **ESS (super-capacitor electrolyte specific)** | Not disclosed by management at this granularity | Valuates/MarketsandMarkets: $112mn (2023) → $165mn (2030), 5.6% CAGR; another source $114mn (2024) → $171mn (2032), 6.1% CAGR | Independent-only data point — **use $112mn (lower, most conservative)** |
| **PASC** | Not disclosed at product-specific granularity by management | No product-specific report found for "Glyme as pharma/agro solvent." India Chemical Intermediate market (broad proxy): $8.67bn (2024) → $21.2bn (2035), 7.65% CAGR (MarketResearchFuture) | **Proxy far too broad to use as headline TAM component — excluded from conservative sum, see bottom-up cross-check below** |

**Conservative TAM (product-specific sum, PTC+SDA+ESS only)**: $1.07bn + $0.60bn + $0.112bn
= **$1.78bn ≈ ₹15,237 Cr**

PASC is excluded from this sum because the only available proxy (India Chemical Intermediate
market, $8.67bn) is several orders of magnitude broader than Tatva's actual PASC scope
(Glymes + select intermediates/solvents — a company with ₹162.3cr FY26 PASC revenue is not
meaningfully served by an $8.67bn/₹742,000cr denominator). Per the conservative-bias rule,
an unusable oversized proxy is excluded rather than averaged in.

### Method 2 — Bottom-up (constrained by disclosure)

B04 flags unit economics as **NOT FOUND** ("no per-tonne/per-KL revenue disclosed" — B04
unit_economics block). A true bottom-up (addressable units × penetration × revenue/unit)
cannot be built for any segment. As a second-best bottom-up cross-check for PASC only (the
segment excluded from Method 1), a revenue-mix-proportional estimate is used: Tatva's FY26
PTC+SDA+ESS revenue ($39.53mn) captures 2.22% of the $1.78bn Method-1 TAM for those three
segments. Applying the same capture ratio to PASC's FY26 revenue ($18.96mn) implies a PASC
addressable-market proxy of **~$0.85bn**. This is explicitly an estimate constructed for
this report, not a market-research figure, and is used only in the "realistic" TAM case
below, never the conservative one.

**Realistic TAM (Method 1 + Method 2 PASC proxy)**: $1.78bn + $0.85bn = **$2.63bn ≈ ₹22,530 Cr**

### Method 3 — Peer revenue aggregation (data-constrained)

B04 records **no named direct competitors with disclosed revenue** ("no named direct
competitors disclosed in AR or investor presentation — only self-reported market-rank
claims"). Management's own rank claims are qualitative only: "one of the leading producers"
(PTC, IP p.13), "2nd largest global / largest India" (SDA, IP p.14), "largest India producer"
(ESS, IP p.16), "largest India / 3rd largest global" (Glymes/PASC, IP p.17, AR p.121). None
of these carry a disclosed percentage share. Web search for named competitors (Sarchem for
SDA, per B04's own gap note) returned no independent market-share corroboration — this
mirrors B07's own optionality-register item flagging the "SDA up to 70% share" claim as
needing independent verification, which was not found. Separately, B07 records that the PTC
competitor count has **grown from 2 to 9–10** — a fragmenting, not consolidating, structure,
inconsistent with "few players" language used for SDA/ESS. Method 3 cannot produce an
independent total; it is used only as a soft qualitative cross-check in Section 4C.

### Method 4 — Import substitution (qualitative only)

Both AR and IP repeatedly invoke "China+1" as a demand driver for SDA/PTC (IP p.29: China
holds 39% share of the global chemical industry — CY2022 CEFIC/IBEF data, now approaching
the 4-year staleness threshold; AR p.116-117 repeats similar China-related commentary for
2024). Independent web research on the adjacent semiconductor-chemicals optionality found a
directionally supportive but non-Tatva-specific data point: India's share of the "global
semiconductor-grade materials market" is projected to rise from <5% (2022) to ~30% (2025)
(ainvest.com, independent). No company-specific $ import-substitution figure is disclosed or
independently discoverable for any of the four core segments — Method 4 is used only as a
qualitative growth driver in Section 4A, not a sized TAM contributor.

### Method 5 — Global benchmark

India specialty chemicals ~$40bn (2023, AR p.117) against global specialty chemicals
$285.4bn (2023, AR p.117) implies India held roughly **14% of the global specialty chemicals
market** in 2023 despite being the world's 6th-largest chemicals producer overall (AR
p.116). Independent web research shows meaningfully higher and inconsistent figures for the
same India specialty-chemicals market depending on methodology/vintage — IBEF: ~$64bn by
2025 at 12% CAGR; IMARC: $64.5bn (2024) → $92.6bn (2033), 3.8% CAGR; Grand View Research:
$62.78bn (2024) → $95.15bn (2030), 8.67% CAGR. **These diverge materially from the AR's
$40bn (2023) figure and from each other** — flagged rather than averaged, consistent with
the instruction not to silently smooth over material divergence. The divergence reflects
different research houses using different specialty-chemicals product-scope definitions, a
well-documented issue in this data category; it is not resolvable from the documents
provided. India's own AR-cited ambition — 6% global chemical-industry share by 2040, up
from ~4% currently (IP p.29 CY2022) — is used only directionally, per the 4-year staleness
rule.

## SECTION 3: SAM & SOM

### 3A — SAM (applied to the conservative, product-specific TAM: $1.78bn / ₹15,237 Cr)

| Filter | Cut | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Product fit | 0% | TAM already scoped to Tatva's exact three product-specific niches (PTC, SDA, super-cap ESS); no further narrowing needed |
| Geography | −39% | China represents ~39% of global chemical-industry demand (IP p.29, CY2022 CEFIC/IBEF — **flagged stale, directional use only**); Tatva's own China revenue exposure is minimal (3.19% of FY24-25 revenue, AR p.9), consistent with limited realistic access to China's captive domestic chemical demand. Remaining addressable: 61% |
| Channel | 0% | All three segments are direct B2B industrial sales; no channel-specific exclusion applies |
| Customer | 0% | Customer base (API/agro/auto-catalyst/EV manufacturers) is already the full addressable customer set for these product categories; no B2C angle exists |
| Capability | −15% | Reflects the 1–6 year product-development/regulatory-qualification cycle repeatedly cited by management (AR p.6, IP p.15) as a barrier to instantly serving new geographies/customers |

SAM = $1.78bn × 0.61 × 0.85 = **$0.924bn ≈ ₹7,909 Cr**
**SAM as % of TAM (conservative) = 51.9%**

(A parallel calculation applying the same filters to the realistic TAM, $2.63bn, gives a
total-company SAM of ≈$1.36bn / ₹11,676 Cr; the ₹7,909 Cr figure above is used as the
primary SAM since it rests on independently-verified rather than proxy-constructed data.)

### 3B — SOM at 3 and 5 years

**Current SAM share**: FY26 PTC+SDA+ESS revenue = $13.7mn + $23.9mn + $1.93mn = $39.53mn
(₹1,173mn + ₹2,045mn + ₹165mn = ₹3,383mn, IP p.19) ÷ SAM $924mn = **4.3%**

**Share-gain trajectory**: Applying the "normal" 1–2pp/3yr rule rather than the "aggressive"
3–5pp rule. Evidence against aggressive: PTC competitor count has grown 2→9–10 (B07,
fragmenting not consolidating), the em-moat score is MODEST (19.2/80, B07), and the single
largest capacity-adding project (Jolva) has slipped groundbreaking three consecutive
quarters (B07 HIGH flag). Evidence supporting the higher end of "normal" rather than the
low end: substantial under-utilised existing capacity (reactors 64.11%, assembly lines
30.54% vs "healthy" thresholds of >70%/>45%, B04 must-track metrics) gives genuine organic
headroom without requiring new capex or competitor exit.

- 3yr share: 4.3% + 1.5pp = **5.8%**
- 5yr share: 4.3% + 2.5pp = **6.8%**

SAM is grown forward at the Method-1 blended CAGR for the three core segments (weighted by
FY26 revenue mix: PTC 5.79%, SDA 8.0%, ESS 5.6% → weighted 6.54%):
- SAM in 3yrs: $924mn × 1.0654³ = $1,117mn
- SAM in 5yrs: $924mn × 1.0654⁵ = $1,269mn

Core-segment SOM:
- 3yr: 5.8% × $1,117mn = **$64.8mn**
- 5yr: 6.8% × $1,269mn = **$86.3mn**

**PASC add-on** (using the Method-2 proxy TAM's implied 7.13% blended growth rate, with
**no explicit share-gain modelled** — a neutral, market-pace-only assumption, since data
constraints prevent a scoped SAM/SOM chain for PASC specifically): FY26 PASC $18.96mn →
3yr $23.3mn, 5yr $26.8mn.

**Total company SOM** (core + PASC add-on, converted at ₹85.6/$):
- **SOM 3yr: $64.8mn + $23.3mn = $88.1mn ≈ ₹7,543mn = ₹754 Cr**
- **SOM 5yr: $86.3mn + $26.8mn = $113.1mn ≈ ₹9,679mn = ₹968 Cr**

**Implied revenue CAGR** (off current FY26 revenue, ₹505.86 Cr):
- 3yr: (754/505.86)^(1/3) − 1 = **14.3%**
- 5yr: (968/505.86)^(1/5) − 1 = **13.9%**

Both years cluster near 14%, comfortably below FY26's own 32% YoY revenue growth (which was
a cyclical recovery off a utilization trough and should not be extrapolated), and this
14%/13.9% pair is the **formal handoff to Stage 11** for the revenue-growth cross-check.

### 3C — Capacity cross-check (against B07's capex-embedded-growth figure)

B07 Section 2C: historical fixed-asset turnover 0.858x × Jolva guided capex ₹262.5 Cr =
₹225.15 Cr conservative incremental revenue, i.e. a capacity-embedded ceiling of
₹505.86 Cr + ₹225.15 Cr = **₹731.0 Cr** (conservative case; management's own Jolva guidance
of ₹400-500 Cr incremental would put the ceiling at ₹906-1,006 Cr, but B07 explicitly
instructs use of the conservative ₹225.15 Cr figure given the Jolva groundbreaking has
slipped three consecutive quarters).

**SOM 3yr (₹754 Cr) sits ~₹23 Cr (3.1%) above the conservative, Jolva-inclusive capacity
ceiling (₹731 Cr)** — a tight, roughly-in-balance match, not a comfortable one. Two readings:

1. If Jolva's newly-guided mid-July 2026 groundbreaking finally happens on schedule (B07
   catalyst) and existing reactor/assembly-line utilization keeps ramping from the current
   64.11%/30.54% toward the "healthy" 75-80%/45% band (B04 must-track thresholds), the
   3yr SOM is achievable — largely from existing under-utilised capacity, with Jolva as a
   modest incremental contributor given its Jan-Mar 2028 commissioning guide falls near the
   back end of the 3yr window.
2. If Jolva slips a fourth time — the well-evidenced, HIGH-severity risk B07 already flags —
   the conservative capex ceiling becomes the binding constraint and **the SOM, not the
   capex plan, is the optimistic side of this comparison** by roughly the same ₹23 Cr-plus
   margin (understated, since a slip would also compress the achievable utilization ramp).

**5yr SOM (₹968 Cr, +91% over current revenue) has materially more execution risk embedded**:
it likely requires Jolva to be commissioned and at least partially ramped within the window,
which depends on a groundbreaking event with a three-times-slipped track record actually
occurring. This is named explicitly as the single largest swing factor between the 3yr and
5yr SOM figures.

## SECTION 5: SUMMARY & RUNWAY

### 5A — Funnel diagram

```
TAM (conservative, product-specific PTC+SDA+ESS): $1.78bn  ≈ ₹15,237 Cr
   │  (realistic, +PASC bottom-up proxy: $2.63bn ≈ ₹22,530 Cr)
   ├─ Geography filter (−39%, China exclusion, stale 2022 basis)
   ├─ Capability filter (−15%, qualification-cycle constraint)
   ▼
SAM: $0.924bn ≈ ₹7,909 Cr   (51.9% of conservative TAM)
   │
   ├─ Current SAM share: 4.3% (FY26 core-segment revenue $39.53mn / ₹338.3 Cr)
   ▼
SOM 3yr: $88.1mn ≈ ₹754 Cr   (5.8% share of grown SAM, total company incl. PASC add-on)
SOM 5yr: $113.1mn ≈ ₹968 Cr  (6.8% share of grown SAM, total company incl. PASC add-on)
   │
   ▼
Implied revenue CAGR: 3yr 14.3% | 5yr 13.9%   ← FORMAL HANDOFF TO STAGE 11
```

### 5B — Runway assessment

- **Revenue headroom** = SAM (₹7,909 Cr, core-segment basis) ÷ current revenue (₹505.86 Cr)
  = **15.6x**
- **TAM growth rate** (conservative, blended PTC/SDA/ESS): **6.5%** — modest, not explosive
- **Company CAGR vs TAM**: SOM-implied 14.3%/13.9% > TAM growth 6.5% → **Tatva is a
  share-gainer, not a market-rider**, under this SOM construction
- **Years to saturate SAM at current growth**: ln(15.6)/ln(1.14) ≈ **~20-21 years** at the
  blended SOM-implied CAGR (~14%) — a long runway

### 5C — Runway classification

**GOOD.** (Classification bands used — MASSIVE >50x / STRONG 20-50x / GOOD 10-20x /
MODERATE 5-10x / LIMITED <5x headroom, cross-read against TAM growth rate — the exact
thresholds are an assumption made explicit here since the Section 1B v3.3 standard matrix
text was not available inside this stage's inputs; flagged as such.) Reasoning: 15.6x
headroom is comfortably double-digit but not extreme; the underlying TAM itself grows only
