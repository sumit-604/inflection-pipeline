Strength: MODERATE, with a hard caveat: this is the same related-party structure that produced the Gate 0 AVOID-driving cash-conversion flag (B01) — Quick Pay is simultaneously the top customer (47.02% of FY26 sales) and an equity investee, and FY26 receivables collection is explicitly stated to depend "on their receipt of funds" (RHP p.90). ## SECTION 6: TIMELINE, RISKS & COMBINED ASSESSMENT

### 6A. Moat evolution timeline

- **Next 12m:** Watch whether the Rs 6,103.25 lakh machinery capex is converted from quotations to placed purchase orders before the ~6-month quotation validity windows lapse (quotes dated Apr-Jun 2026, RHP p.85-86); watch Unit 4 Springs' conversion from trial production to commercial shipment; watch whether the Reg-30-disclosed Rs 121.88 crore FY27 order book (RHP-disclosed order book was only Rs 67.14 crore as of 5-Jun-2026) actually executes and collects.
- **12-24m:** RDSO approval status disclosure (if any); China+1/export-reshoring language becoming explicit rather than inferred; any patent filing tied to the in-house electronics work; conversion of Unit 4's 10-year lease (to 2035, the company's most durable facility) into a genuine second manufacturing hub beyond machining and springs.
- **24-36m:** Talent-asymmetry evidence (I1) or cannibalization-barrier evidence (I2) would need to appear here if they are ever going to; currently both are absent by a wide margin.
- **3-5yr:** Whether the Quick Pay relationship either normalises into an arm's-length, diversified customer relationship or remains a concentrated related-party dependency will determine whether C1/H2 convert from "flagged optionality" into a genuine, durable moat.
