No peer describes a services or R&M demand boom; the demand they describe is new-build and plant O&M for new capacity. That complicates, but does not contradict, a services-led thesis.

2B PRICING AND INPUT COSTS. Peers describe price-variation clauses that lag by a quarter (Power Mech, 10 Aug 2026, p.14) and fixed-price orders hedged by back-to-back supplier offers (ISGEC, 5 Nov 2026, p.13). ISGEC is holding margins; Power Mech standalone margin rose but consolidated fell on mining and royalty items. This is partial support for GVPIL's margin dip being input-linked and weak support for its size (see Q7). BHEL (dated 2022) said price-variation indices lag steel and copper, and caps limit recovery (May 2022, p.7).

2C CAPEX CYCLE. This is not a thermal-services capacity race. ISGEC is expanding about Rs 502 Cr in presses, castings, skids and modules (5 Nov 2026, p.4), not in boiler services. Power Mech's capex of about Rs 400 Cr at group and Rs 400 Cr in the Tasra SPV is mining and coal washery (22 May 2026, p.11). BHEL stated 10 GW a year of capacity (21 May 2024, p.14, dated). Peers are diversifying away from thermal projects into manufacturing, mining and non-power (Power Mech non-power 46% of Q1 FY27 revenue, p.4). No peer is adding thermal services capacity.

2D COMPETITIVE MENTIONS. None. No peer mentions GVPIL, GE Power or GE Vernova. ## PART 4: TRIANGULATION SUMMARY
- Claims verified: 0 of 8. Partially verified: 4 (Q1, Q4, Q6, Q7). Unverifiable: 4 (Q2, Q3, Q5, Q8).
- Claims contradicted: 0. No peer states the opposite of a GVPIL claim.
- Most consequential gap: the peer set cannot test the GVPIL-specific services claims. Third-party fleet share, R&M ordering, market size and the Saudi order have no current peer evidence. The closest contradiction-in-spirit is Q7: ISGEC's margins rose and Power Mech's standalone margin rose in the same period that GVPIL's fell 4.7 points, so sector input cost does not explain the full move.
- Strongest independent confirmation: FGD run-off. Power Mech (Rs 4,260 Cr written out, Rs 936 Cr left) and ISGEC (99% complete, Rs 165 Cr retention left) independently describe the FGD cycle as ending, with no new award named, which matches GVPIL's FGD position.
- Overall: the peer set complicates the GVPIL narrative. It supports the direction of a thermal build-out and of growing outsourced O&M, and supports run-off of FGD. It does not support, or refute, the services-specific numbers. Two points cut against the narrative: Power Mech's O&M earns 15-18% against GVPIL's 10-12% and R&M ordering appears nowhere in current peer pipelines. Two points support it: independent peers confirm the Middle East input-cost shock, and every peer pushes services as the higher-margin line, which agrees with GVPIL's mix move.

cross_peer_hypothesis: Across the combined set, the EPC and equipment players are all moving toward the same line. ISGEC created a services division for "retrofit and modernization, services, spares" and aims to double its O&M base in two years (12 Aug 2026, p.5, p.16). Power Mech lifts O&M to 30-35% of business and guides to margin gain from O&M mix (10 Aug 2026, p.16-17). BHEL (2021-2023, dated) pushed non-BHEL spares and services as high margin and short duration (p.3). None of them names the cause. [INFERENCE] The end of the FGD project cycle and a slow BHEL main-plant ordering rhythm are pushing project-led firms into thermal services, so the third-party servicing space GVPIL treats as its growth lane is becoming contested from the project side. Testable: within 12 months, ISGEC or Power Mech announce boiler spares, retrofit or R&M orders on non-OEM units, and the signal on GVPIL is a falling third-party share of core orders below the B05 kill signal of about 50%, or core margin below 11% ex other income with no input-cost reason.
