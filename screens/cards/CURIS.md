## Business Understanding Narrative

**What does it do, and how does it make money?** Curis makes generic tablets,
capsules, syrups, creams and eye ointments at one WHO-GMP plant in Sanand,
Gujarat (Deck, pages 6 and 10). It sells in three ways. It does job work for
other brands. It makes to contract for Indian pharma companies. It makes to
contract for merchant exporters, who sell under their own brands in Africa,
Yemen and Central America (Deck, pages 7 and 8; Investor meet, page 4). FY26
revenue was Rs 60.63 crore and profit after tax Rs 6.92 crore (Results FY26,
page 6).

**What is changing?** The company wants to sell its own brands in Africa. It
says own-brand sales are 1% of revenue today, and it wants 80% within three
to five years (Investor meet, pages 4 and 9). It is closing low-margin job work
(Investor meet, page 9).

**Why now?** The November 2025 IPO raised Rs 27.52 crore (Results FY26,
page 6, note 6). Management says working capital, not machines, capped
growth (Investor meet, page 6). On 12-Aug-2026 it took 49.95% of a Nigerian
distributor for Rs 5 lakh (Agreements filing, pages 2 and 3).

**What must be true?** Product registrations in Nigeria, Kenya and Ghana must
come through. Management expects the first Nigerian registrations around
March 2027 or later (Investor meet, page 7).

**What breaks it?** Cash. FY26 operating cash flow was negative Rs 16.42 crore
against profit of Rs 6.92 crore (Results FY26, page 7).

## 2. Business model and archetype

**Archetype: outsourcing partner.** Client concentration, wallet share,
capacity fill, contract stickiness and price per unit are the variables.

Named clients include Ipca, Lincoln, Troikaa, Cadila, JB and Makers (Deck,
page 9). Ghana, served through two or three clients, is about 20% of sales
(Deck, page 19).

Margins by model, per management: domestic contract work earns 10% to 15%
EBITDA, export contract work "a little bit higher", own brand "much, much,
much higher" (Investor meet, page 5).

Capacity is under-used. At 31-Mar-2025, tablets ran at 43.6%, capsules
16.8%, oral liquids 25.3% (Deck, page 10). Management says growth needs no
capex, only working capital (Investor meet, page 9).

## 3. Competitive advantages

Absent to forming.

- A WHO-GMP plant with in-house sterility and microbiology testing (Investor
  meet, page 5). Useful, not rare.
- 1,200-plus product formulations with stability data (Deck, page 19).
- Registrations held by partners, not by Curis. Kenya's 20 products were
  registered "via Centurion Pvt. Ltd." (Deck, page 19). Nigeria runs through
  Eurosun (Deck, page 19).

A merchant-export supplier owns neither the brand nor the market licence.
That is the switching-cost gap the own-brand plan tries to close.

## 6. The transition and the quality ladder

FROM R1 to R2, a contract manufacturer whose customer owns the brand and the
registration. TO a claimed R4 or R5, an own-brand pharma seller in African
markets.

Mechanism: register products in Curis's name, sell through local partners,
then build local offices (Investor meet, pages 4 and 7).

Management's claim, quoted once: "Within 3 or 4 or 5 years, we will be going
to do 80% of own brand marketing only" (Investor meet, page 9).

The rung-jump rule applies. One rung per two to three years is the base rate.
This claim spans two to three rungs in three to five years. **Ladder reading:
R1 to R2 today, claimed multi-rung leap, itself a red flag.**

## 8. Proof check

The triggers claim that IPO working capital lifts revenue and margin. H2 FY26
is the first half after the money arrived. Revenue fell 4.8% and profit fell
32.4% against H2 FY25.

**Named absence: no proof.** The working capital went into inventory and
advances, not sales. The observation that would fire the gate is H1 FY27
revenue above Rs 32 crore with positive operating cash.

## 12. Verdict card

**PASS.**

The own-brand story is years out and runs through partners. The first half
after the IPO shrank. Cash went out while profit came in. The results drew an
exchange query. The multiple already sits above the rung the business holds.

**Load-bearing facts, if the operator overrides**
1. Who received the Rs 20.64 crore of short-term loans and advances.
2. What the Rs 14.17 crore extraordinary cash line is.
3. H1 FY27 revenue, profit and operating cash.
4. Promoter holding from a filed shareholding pattern.
5. Registrations held in Curis's own name, by country.

**What would change the view**
- H1 FY27 with revenue growth, positive operating cash and a clean
  explanation of the advances moves it to WATCH.
- Nigerian registrations in Curis's name, filed, move the ladder claim from
  stated to underway.

No Role 1 valuation was run and no target price is set.
