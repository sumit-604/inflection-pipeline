# Supreme Power Equipment: H1 FY27 in plain words

Source: committed review, A5 audit and work/notion-thesis.md for runs/supremepwr-h1fy27. No agent was re-run.

1. **What the company does.** Supreme Power is a small company listed on the NSE SME board. The filing says it has one business segment and gives no more detail. Our background knowledge says it makes transformers in Tamil Nadu for utilities and renewable projects. That is not yet checked against a live source. It owns 90% of one partnership firm, Danya Electric Company.

2. **What happened this half.** The business grew fast. Group sales rose about 80% on the same half last year, to Rs 135 Cr. Profit after tax rose about 45%, to Rs 14 Cr. Profit grew, but slower than sales.

3. **What improved.** Growth is real at the profit level. It does not come from one-off income. The parent company's own margin also went up a little.

4. **What got worse.** The group margin went down, from about 18.8% to 17.3%. Depreciation and interest grew much faster than sales, because the company is building new assets with borrowed money. The parent margin rose only because of how profit is split between the parent and Danya. The group as a whole earned a thinner margin.

5. **Cash and balance sheet.** This is the weak spot. The business used cash in the half, about Rs 6 Cr, while it reported Rs 14 Cr of profit. Stock and customer dues both grew fast. Cash fell to almost nothing. Net debt rose about 42%, to Rs 57 Cr. Suppliers now fund the company more than banks do. A large part of the money owed is to small suppliers, and the payments are close to the legal 45-day limit.

6. **What management said or promised.** There was no earnings call and no presentation. The filing gives no guidance. It says one thing about the future: investors who bought warrants have paid 25%. The other 75%, about Rs 16 Cr, comes in only if they choose to convert, by about February 2027. Nobody has converted yet. The company also says it has used the warrant money as planned. It reports that money under one combined purpose, so we cannot test that claim.

7. **Red flags in plain words.** Danya's numbers are not audited, and the filing leaves a blank where it should say who checked them. The managing director owns part of Danya. The parent put more money into Danya this half while the other partners took money out. Goods move both ways between the parent and Danya, and no profit was removed for stock still sitting inside the group. All raw material is labelled as goods bought for resale, yet the company is building a factory. So we do not yet know if it is mainly a maker or a trader.

8. **The open items.** Our final check found two errors that we did not fix. First, the review says this half's profit is not boosted by goods held inside Danya. The check showed that claim cannot be proven. The boost could be anything from zero to a large share of profit. Second, the review says sales booked outside the parent were not collected. That is true only if the parent sold nothing to Danya in the half, which the filing does not show. Some extracted numbers were also hard to read in the scanned PDF and need a re-read.

9. **What this means for the thesis.** First look, no earlier analysis on record. There is no Notion page, no thesis, no entry price and no tripwires to compare against. Our verdict is "proceed with flags": the growth is real, but cash, the related subsidiary and the unclear business type all need answers first. Whether to take the name further, through the step 1 intake, is your decision.

10. **What would change our view.** The most important answer is how much of sales the company makes itself versus buys and resells. That decides what kind of business this is and what it is worth. The second is how much bank credit is still unused. The third is whether the auditor checked Danya.

11. **What to watch next.** About February 2027: do the warrant holders pay the Rs 16 Cr? About May 2027: the full-year results. The one number to watch is the cash cycle, meaning how many days money is tied up in stock and customer dues. If it falls back to about 104 days, the growth is healthy. If it stays above about 121 days, the strain is real. By September 2027: the annual report should show Danya's audit status and the trade between the parent and Danya.
