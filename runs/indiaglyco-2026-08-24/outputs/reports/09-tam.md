## SECTION 1: MARKET DEFINITION

### 1A — Precise boundaries, by entity

**(i) RESIDUAL India Glycols Ltd (Chemicals + Gases)**
- Product scope: bio-based Mono/Di/Tri-Ethylene Glycol (MEG/DEG/TEG), heavy
  glycols; ethylene-based glycol ethers and glycol ether esters (NOT
  propylene-based ethers — IGL does not make these, AR p.44); bio-polymers
  (guar-derivative hydrocolloids); performance/specialty chemicals (bio-amines,
  oilfield chemicals, plasticizers); Bio-based Ethylene Oxide (sold mainly to
  the Clariant JV); industrial gases (liquid O2, N2, Ar, CO2, ETO/"STERI GAS"
  sterilant mixtures).
- Geographic scope: India domestic + export to 40+ countries (Investor
  Presentation p.22); gases are logistics-bound to a shorter radius around the
  Kashipur plant — AR states the Company "developed a market for supply of EO
  within a limited distance in view of the safety aspects involved in
  transportation... over large distances" (AR p.45).
- Customer scope: B2B — PET/polyester/textile-fibre makers, paints &
  coatings, personal care/cosmetics, oil & gas, agrochemical formulators,
  industrial/medical gas users. AR describes the glycols client base as "a
  discerning portfolio of multinational corporations... with strong
  environmental sustainability mandates" (AR p.44).
- Channel: direct B2B sale; EO substantially routed through the Clariant IGL
  Specialty Chemicals JV.
- Price segment: mixed — commodity-adjacent bio-MEG/glycols competing against
  petrochemical MEG on a green premium, through to genuinely differentiated
  specialty/performance chemicals (bio-amines) with no direct substitute.
- Explicit exclusions: Potable Spirits, Bio-Fuel/ethanol, Ennature Bio Pharma
  (all demerging out); propylene-based glycol ethers (AR flags this as the
  growing sub-segment IGL does not participate in, AR p.44); non-bio
  petrochemical MEG production (IGL's flagship differentiator is the bio
  route, AR p.44).

**(ii) IGL Spirits Ltd (demerged) — Potable Spirits**
- Product scope: Country Liquor (branded, e.g. Bunty Babli) and IMFL (whisky,
  vodka, rum, brandy) plus Extra Neutral Alcohol (ENA) sold to third parties
  and used captively.
- Geography: core strength Uttar Pradesh and Uttarakhand (market leader),
  Delhi (government-controlled excise channel); expansion into Kerala, Andhra
  Pradesh, Haryana, Diu; institutional Defence/CSD and paramilitary channel
  (Investor Presentation p.9-10, 28-30).
- Channel: owned brands, contract manufacturing/bottling for Bacardi (15-year
  partnership), and the Amrut Distilleries tie-up for premium categories
  (Investor Presentation p.34-35).

**(ii) IGL Spirits Ltd (demerged) — Bio-Fuel**
- Product scope: fuel-grade ethanol supplied to Oil Marketing Companies
  (OMCs) under the Government of India's Ethanol Blended Petrol (EBP)
  Programme, from grain and molasses-based distilleries at Kashipur (600
  KLPD) and Gorakhpur (500 KLPD) (Investor Presentation p.37).
- Geography: India only, tender-allocated by OMC region.

**(iii) Ennature Bio Pharma Ltd (demerged)**
- Product scope: plant-derived APIs (Thiocolchicoside, Colchicine), branded
  nutraceutical ingredients (Maxicuma, Xanthogreen), nicotine and nicotine
  derivatives (AR p.47-48; Investor Presentation p.40).
- Geography: domestic + exports (US, Europe entry with new molecules;
  Southeast Asia presence, Investor Presentation p.40); global quality
  credentials (US FDA/EAR, REACH, WHO-GMP, EU-GMP, CEP-EDQM).

### 1B — Management's own TAM claim, credibility read

Management does not state a segment-specific TAM figure for BSPC/Chemicals,
Potable Spirits, Bio-Fuel, or Ennature anywhere in the injected AR or
Investor Presentation. What management does cite, in the AR's Management
Discussion & Analysis "global economy" preamble (AR p.42, filed with the
FY2025-26 Annual Report), is the size of the **entire Indian chemical
industry**: "The sector is expected to grow from USD 250 billion to USD 300
billion by 2028 and USD 1 trillion by 2040" (source not named in the AR
text). This is the only market-size number management attaches, even
loosely, to the Chemicals business's operating backdrop.

**Credibility read: BROAD.** This is the size of the whole Indian chemical
industry (agrochemicals, dyes and pigments, petrochemicals, bulk chemicals,
polymers, everything) — not the bio-glycols / glycol-ether / green-solvent /
industrial-gas niche IGL actually competes in. IGL's own AR repeatedly
stresses IGL is "the only supplier at scale globally" of bio-based EO/glycols
and "India's largest and only manufacturer for bio-glycol ethers" (AR p.44,
p.49) — i.e., a specialist inside a vast generalist number. Using this figure
as a TAM claim would overstate IGL's addressable market by roughly two orders
of magnitude (see Section 2 triangulation). This is a definitional mismatch,
not necessarily company dishonesty — no other AR/presentation text tries to
connect this figure directly to BSPC's own opportunity — but it is the only
number available for the formal mgmt-claim-vs-conservative-estimate ratio the
stage protocol requires, and is treated here as such.

Management does give explicit medium-term financial (not market) ambitions,
which are weighed but not anchored as TAM in this section (they are used in
Section 5E as a cross-check against the SOM-implied growth rate):
- Chemicals (= residual India Glycols Ltd, Chemicals + Gases): "Aspiration to
  achieve ₹400 Cr EBITDA in the next 4-5 years" on top of an FY27 outlook of
  "EBITDA in excess of ₹200 Cr (including dividend from JV)" — implying a
  revenue target of roughly ₹2,500 Cr in the same window (Investor
  Presentation p.12).
- IGL Spirits (Potable Spirits + Bio-Fuel combined): "aspiration to achieve
  ₹1,000 Cr EBITDA in the next 4-5 years," against an FY27 outlook of
  "EBITDA in excess of ₹500 Cr" (Investor Presentation p.10).
- Ennature Bio Pharma: "Aspiration to achieve ₹130-150 Cr EBITDA in the next
  4-5 years," against an FY27 outlook of "EBITDA in excess of ₹50 Cr"
  (Investor Presentation p.13).
These are management aspirations, not anchored evidence, per the injected
brief, and are treated that way throughout.

## SECTION 2: TAM ESTIMATION — RESIDUAL INDIA GLYCOLS (Chemicals + Gases)

Current annual revenue for this entity: BSPC gross segment revenue ₹1,202 Cr
FY26 (AR MD&A segment table, AR p.34-35; corroborated by Investor
Presentation p.24, "FY26 Revenue stood at Rs. 1,203 crores") + Industrial
Gases ₹46 Cr FY26 (AR p.45) = **₹1,248 Cr combined FY26 revenue**, used as
the base for all share/CAGR arithmetic below.

### Method 1 — Top-down, sum of relevant India sub-markets

IGL's product basket spans several distinct chemical categories rather than
one homogeneous market, so Method 1 is built as a sum of India-market sizes
for each category IGL actually sells into, each individually sourced:

| Glycol ethers (paints & coatings application only — partial, understates true total) | USD 338.5 Mn = **₹3,241 Cr** | Web search aggregation citing a glycol-ethers market report's India/paints-&-coatings line item (accessed Aug 2026, base year not independently confirmed — flagged) | L |

Staleness check: all components are within 1-2 years of run date; none
triggers the >2-year "STALE" flag. The AR's own 2022 global-MEG base figure
(USD 24.16 Bn, AR p.43) is 4 years old at run date and is used only to derive
a growth rate, never as a headline number, consistent with the staleness rule.

### Method 2 — Bottom-up, glycol ethers volume cross-check

Global glycol ethers market: 0.94 million tonnes in 2025, growing to 1.17
million tonnes by 2030 (4.5% volume CAGR), valued at USD 8.7 Bn in 2025 (web
search aggregation of multiple glycol-ether market reports, accessed Aug
2026) → implied average global price ≈ USD 9,255/tonne. Applying India's
observed ~14.1% global share of the paints-&-coatings application specifically
(same source as the Method 1 partial figure) as a rough proxy for India's
share of total glycol-ether demand across ALL applications (an extrapolation,
not a sourced fact — flagged): 0.94 Mt x 12% (mid-point of a 10-14% band) =
~113,000 tonnes India volume x USD 9,255/tonne = USD 1.04 Bn = **₹9,991 Cr**.
This is materially higher than the Method 1 paints-only figure (₹3,241 Cr)
because it attempts to capture all end-uses, not one application. The two
numbers bracket a plausible India glycol-ethers TAM of ₹3,200-10,000 Cr;
conservative-bias rule keeps the lower figure in the Method 1 sum above, and
this method is shown only as a directional cross-check, confidence L.

### Method 3 — Peer revenue aggregation: NOT MEANINGFULLY APPLICABLE

IGL's own AR repeatedly claims near-monopoly status in its specific niches —
"only manufacturer in India to use a continuous process" for glycol ethers
(AR p.24, Investor Presentation), "only producer of ethylene based ethers in
India" (AR p.44), "largest Bio-Based speciality chemicals manufacturer,
pioneer in bio-based EO... and only supplier at scale globally" (Investor
Presentation p.12). If true, peer revenue aggregation inside the bio-niche
would sum to roughly IGL's own revenue, and aggregating the much larger
conventional-petrochemical peer set (Reliance Industries and importers from
China/Middle East) would simply double-count the Method 1 MEG top-down
figure. Note per B07 (Emerging Moat, this run): these "only manufacturer"
scarcity claims are self-reported and have no independent peer verification —
flagged there and carried forward here as a caveat on how much weight to put
on IGL's stated competitive exclusivity.

### Method 4 — Import substitution (qualitative)

AR text repeatedly ties IGL's near-term pricing power to import disruption:
"the imports of MEG in India were adversely impacted [by the Middle East
conflict] which has led to a hike in the prices & availability constraint"
(AR p.43); glycol ether export competitiveness is described as under pressure
from "a reduction in feedstock costs in China... enabling Chinese
manufacturers to offer more competitively priced products" (AR p.44); and a
2026 India-US tariff reduction (50% to 18% on certain chemicals) is cited as
improving IGL's competitiveness against Chinese exporters in the US market
(AR p.48-49). No sourced figure exists for the rupee value of India's MEG or
glycol-ether import volume, so a quantified import-substitution uplift to TAM
is **NOT FOUND**. Directionally, this is a two-sided driver: import
disruption helps domestic pricing (tailwind), while Chinese cost advantage
pressures IGL's own export share (headwind) — both already partially visible
in the segment's revenue decline (see Section 4B).

### Method 5 — Growth-differential benchmark (in place of per-capita)

Per-capita specialty-chemical consumption benchmarks vs China/global were not
found in available sources (**NOT FOUND**). As a substitute directional
benchmark, the AR itself contrasts India's MEG demand growth (domestic CAGR
7-8%) against the global MEG market's 3.2% CAGR (AR p.43) — i.e., management's
own framing is that India is a catch-up growth market roughly 2.3-2.5x the
global growth rate. This supports using the higher end of India-specific
growth assumptions in Section 4, but does not by itself change the TAM level.

### 3B — SOM at 3 and 5 years

Current share of SAM: ₹1,248 Cr / ₹20,753 Cr = **6.0%**.

BSPC's own 3-year revenue trend argues against an aggressive share-gain
assumption: gross segment revenue fell from ₹1,626 Cr (FY24) to ₹1,341 Cr
(FY25) to ₹1,202 Cr (FY26) — a cumulative 26% decline (AR p.35), driven by
global MEG oversupply and Chinese glycol-ether cost pressure (Section 2,
Method 4). This is a company LOSING share in a market that itself grew
modestly over the same period. The standard "normal" 1-2 pp/3-year share-gain
band is applied (not the aggressive 3-5pp band, which the rules reserve for
demonstrated capacity + execution — neither is evidenced here beyond the
already-commissioned, small NSU unit):

- **SOM 3-year:** current share 6.0% + 1.5 pp = 7.5% of SAM = 0.075 x 20,753
  = **₹1,559 Cr**
- **SOM 5-year:** current share 6.0% + 3.0 pp = 9.0% of SAM = 0.090 x 20,753
  = **₹1,870 Cr** (the 3pp cumulative gain over 5 years sits at the low end of
  the "aggressive" band, justified only by the NSU capacity ramp and margin
  mix-shift already visible in FY26; it is not pushed higher given the
  revenue decline above)

**Implied revenue CAGR, arithmetic shown:**
- Yr3: (1,559 / 1,248)^(1/3) − 1 = (1.2492)^0.333 − 1 ≈ **7.7%**
- Yr5: (1,870 / 1,248)^(1/5) − 1 = (1.4984)^0.20 − 1 ≈ **8.4%**

### 3C — Capacity cross-check against B07 capex-embedded growth

B07 (Emerging Moat, this run) reports `capex_embedded_growth_pct: 1` —
i.e., disclosed committed capex across the whole company embeds only ~1%
annualised growth, and B07 names the NSU and malt-plant capex amounts as
NOT disclosed (input_gaps). Applying that 1% figure over 5 years:
₹1,248 Cr x (1.01)^5 ≈ ₹1,312 Cr — the revenue disclosed capex can plausibly
support by year 5.

SOM 5-year (₹1,870 Cr) exceeds this capacity-implied ceiling by **≈₹558 Cr**.
**The gap is real and the SOM is the optimistic side of the ledger** unless
IGL discloses further capacity additions beyond the already-commissioned NSU
(7,500 MT/yr) — none are named with a capex figure in the injected corpus.
This is consistent with B07's own flag that NSU/malt-plant capex amounts and
timelines are undisclosed. `capacity_check: "gap of approx. ₹558 Cr by
year 5 vs B07's 1% capex-embedded growth trajectory; SOM is the optimistic
side absent undisclosed capacity"`.

## SECTION 5: SUMMARY & RUNWAY — RESIDUAL INDIA GLYCOLS

### 5A — Funnel

TAM (conservative, India) ₹33,910 Cr → SAM (five filters applied) ₹20,753 Cr

### 5B — Runway assessment

- Revenue headroom (SAM ÷ current revenue): 20,753 / 1,248 = **16.6x**
- TAM growth rate (weighted average of Method 1 sourced components, weights =
  component TAM value): MEG 7.5% domestic CAGR (AR-cited) x ₹12,160 Cr +
  glycol ethers 4.5% volume CAGR x ₹3,241 Cr + green solvents 7.1-8.15%
  (midpoint 7.6%) x ₹4,625 Cr + industrial gases 6.3% CAGR x ₹13,884 Cr, all
  ÷ ₹33,910 Cr = **≈6.7%**
- Company CAGR vs TAM: BSPC segment revenue actually FELL at roughly a -12%
  CAGR over FY24-FY26 (₹1,626 Cr → ₹1,202 Cr, AR p.35) while the underlying
  TAM grew ~6.7%/yr over the same period — **the company is losing share in
  a growing market**, the opposite of the SOM's forward assumption of modest
  share gain. This gap between demonstrated trend and the assumed 1.5-3pp
  forward share gain is the single most important caveat on this section.
- Years to saturate SAM at the SOM 5yr growth rate (8.4% CAGR): from ₹1,870
  Cr, reaching ₹20,753 Cr would take roughly ln(20,753/1,870)/ln(1.084) ≈
  **30 years** — SAM saturation is not a near-term constraint; capacity (3C)
  is the binding constraint instead.

### 5C — Runway classification: **MODERATE**

The "standard matrix" referenced in the stage instructions was not itself
reproduced in this stage's prompt; the classification below applies reasoned
judgment consistent with the five named tiers (MASSIVE/STRONG/GOOD/
MODERATE/LIMITED) in ascending headroom-and-growth order. Revenue headroom
(16.6x) alone would support a STRONG or higher reading. Two things pull this
down to MODERATE: (1) TAM growth is single-digit (6.7%), not the double-digit

alone does not capture. The capacity gap named in 3C (SOM exceeds
capex-embedded capacity by ~₹558 Cr at year 5) is a further reason not to
read this as STRONG.
