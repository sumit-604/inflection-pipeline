## PATTERN PASS FINDINGS

### P3-1. Pass 1's CSR "contradiction" with CARO dissolves once Note 36's
own brought-forward line is read — DOWNGRADE from Red Flag to Clean 🟢

Pass 1 (finding #4) read Note 36 as showing a Rs 0.18 Cr shortfall
(required Rs 344.05 lakh vs spent Rs 326.19 lakh, FY26) that contradicted
the CARO annexure's statement that CSR was fully spent with no unspent
amount. Note 36's own table has two further rows Pass 1's summary did not
carry through (Note 36, p.178-179):

| Row | FY26 Rs Lakh | FY25 Rs Lakh |
|---|---|---|
| Amount required to be spent | 344.05 | 281.23 |
| Amount spent | 326.19 | 291.19 |
| **Surplus (Shortfall) at the end of the year** | **(17.86)** | **9.96** |
| **Total of previous years' surplus (shortfall) b/f** | **20.69** | **10.73** |

FY26's own-year spend fell short of its own-year requirement by
Rs 17.86 lakh — Pass 1's number was correct as far as it went. But the
company carried forward a Rs 20.69 lakh surplus from FY25 (itself built
from a Rs 9.96 lakh FY25 own-year surplus plus a Rs 10.73 lakh balance
brought forward from FY24). Netting the FY26 shortfall against the
brought-forward surplus leaves a **closing cumulative surplus of
Rs 2.83 lakh** — this exact figure is independently confirmed in the
Board's Report Annexure E, Annual Report on CSR Activities ("the excess
amount available for set-off is Rs. 2.83 Lakhs," p.65). Section 135(5)'s
set-off proviso makes this netting the correct basis, so the CARO
statement ("the Company has fully spent the required amount... no
unspent CSR amount") is accurate on the basis Note 36 itself discloses.
This is not a contradiction; it is a complete disclosure that Pass 1's
extraction summarised too narrowly. **Corrected rating: 🟢 Clean** — the
company's CSR note and its CARO representation are internally consistent
once both rows of Note 36 are read together, which is itself a mild
positive for disclosure quality (the reconciling detail is there, if a
reader follows it through).

Forward-looking nuance carried as a 🟡 watch, not a red flag: the
cumulative cushion that absorbed FY26's shortfall is now down to
Rs 2.83 lakh, near zero. A second consecutive current-year shortfall in
FY27 would exhaust the buffer and could trigger an actual unspent-CSR
transfer obligation under Section 135(5)/(6). Worth a one-line watch in
next year's notes pass, not an issue today.

### P3-2. The GDAL non-controlling-interest equity event is relabelled
between the FY25 AR and the FY26 AR for the identical entry, and its true
multi-year scale is larger than either single-year figure suggests 🟡

Pass 1 (finding #7) read the FY26 AR's Consolidated Statement of Changes
in Equity and reported a FY25 "Investment by NCI" row of Rs 3.46 Cr
(Rs 346.43 lakh) moving NCI equity from Rs 31.53 Cr to Rs 35.79 Cr,
flagging the investor's identity and pricing as undisclosed.

Cross-checking the FY25 AR's own Consolidated Statement of Changes in
Equity (a document this run holds as backward context) shows the
**identical row, identical rupee figures** (Capital Reserve -242.68,
Retained Earnings -3.77, Attributable-to-owners -246.45, NCI +346.43,
Total Equity +99.98, all Rs lakh), but labelled **"Acquisition (sale) of
Non Controlling Interest"**, not "Investment by NCI" (FY25 AR,
Consolidated SOCE, p.204). The FY26 AR's comparative-year row for the
same transaction is captioned "Investment by NCI" (FY26 AR, Consolidated
SOCE, p.192). Same numbers, two different transaction descriptions in
consecutive annual reports, with no note anywhere explaining the change
in language. "Acquisition (sale) of Non-Controlling Interest" and
"Investment by NCI" are not interchangeable under Ind AS 110 — the former
implies a change-of-stake transaction between the parent and existing
NCI holders, the latter implies fresh capital raised by the subsidiary
from an outside investor. Which actually happened is not resolvable from
either AR's notes alone.

The FY25 AR also shows an earlier, larger, and entirely separate NCI
build in FY24 (year ended 31 March 2024) that neither Pass 1 nor Pass 2
surfaced because both worked from the FY26 AR's own two-year comparative
window: NCI equity moved from nil (1 April 2023) to Rs 31.53 Cr (31 March
2024), driven substantially by a Rs 31.19 Cr (Rs 3,118.67 lakh) "Investment
by NCI" line plus a Rs 97.11 Cr "Share issued by subsidiary" line, both in
FY24 (FY25 AR, Consolidated SOCE, p.204 equivalent region). Cumulative NCI
equity across three FY26-AR-visible balance-sheet dates: nil (Apr-2023) →
Rs 31.53 Cr (Mar-2024) → Rs 35.79 Cr (Mar-2025) → Rs 37.65 Cr (Mar-2026,
FY26 AR Consolidated SOCE, p.192, no further NCI equity movement recorded
in FY26 itself). **The true cumulative minority-equity build in GDAL since
April 2023 is roughly Rs 37.65 Cr, not the single-year Rs 3.46 Cr figure
visible from a FY26-AR-only read** — and across all three years, in
neither AR is the minority investor's name, the stake percentage sold or
issued, or the valuation basis disclosed. This is the single highest-value
Halt-1 verification item this pass surfaces: LBF2 (GDAL ownership economics)
cannot be resolved from the notes; live verification (company filings,
MCA records, or direct management query) is required to identify the NCI
holder and the terms.

## F. ONE-LINE NOTES VERDICT

The notes reveal moderate accounting practices, clean on tax and capital
transparency but concerning on cash conversion and subsidiary disclosure.
Key concern: inventory and net debt both growing far faster than revenue
while GDAL burns cash funded entirely by debt, against an undisclosed
minority investor and an invisible layer of subsidiary-level related-party
flows. Key strength: transparent tax reconciliation, clean share-capital
and EPS disclosure, no covenant breaches or exceptional items, and a
genuinely disciplined 45.1% cut in parent-level promoter remuneration.
Overall accounting quality: 5/10.
