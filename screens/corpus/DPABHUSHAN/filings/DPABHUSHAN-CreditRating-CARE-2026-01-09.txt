===== PAGE 2 =====
Rationale and key rating drivers: ratings derive strength from experienced promoters in
jewellery retail, "strong operational track record of over eight decades under the brand
'DP Jewellers' in Madhya Pradesh and Rajasthan, healthy growth in scale of operations and
moderate profitability," prudent working capital management, comfortable financial risk
profile, adequate liquidity. Constrained by profitability susceptible to volatile gold
prices, inherent regulatory risks, and a highly fragmented and competitive gems and
jewellery (G&J) industry.
Rating sensitivities:
Positive: "Significant volume-driven-growth in scale of operations supported by new
store addition and improvement in profit before interest, lease rentals, depreciation,
and taxation (PBILDT) margin above 6% on a sustained basis."
Negative: decline in sales volume with total operating income (TOI) falling below
Rs 2,000 crore and PBILDT margin below 3% sustained; operating cycle elongating beyond
75 days; overall gearing deteriorating beyond 1x sustained.
Analytical approach: Standalone. Outlook: Stable.

===== PAGE 3 =====
Key strengths:
"Growing scale of operations and improving profitability" — TOI grew 42% YoY to Rs
3,312 crore in FY25 from Rs 2,340 crore in FY24. H1FY26 TOI Rs 1,508 crore, broadly flat
versus Rs 1,509 crore in H1FY25, "however, sales volume declined due to lower industry
demand amid rising gold prices." Company opened one store in Ratlam in April 2025 and
"plans to add two more stores in FY26, followed by additional 14 new stores in the next
two years." PBILDT margin improved to 5.34% in FY25 from 4.38% in FY24. In H1FY26,
PBILDT margin rose to 8.62% "due to inventory gains amid rising gold prices," expected
to normalise to 6-7% for the full year.
"Comfortable capital structure and debt coverage indicators" — overall gearing 0.46x as
on 31 March 2025 (0.72x as on 31 March 2024). Low long-term debt. Raised Rs 42.08 crore
on preferential basis and Rs 25.64 crore from convertible warrants, aggregating Rs 67.74
crore up to June 2025, used for new store openings and working capital. Interest
coverage improved to 10.37x FY25 (7.38x FY24). Total debt to gross cash accruals
(TD/GCA) 1.52x FY25 (2.56x FY24).

===== PAGE 4 =====
"Prudent working capital management" — inventory turnover ratio 5.63x FY25 (5.84x
FY24). Inventory managed centrally from Ratlam head office with a replenishment model
for gold-price hedging.
"Experienced promoter and well-established operational track record of over eight
decades" — founded 1940 by Late Dhulchand Kataria; sole proprietorship, then
partnership, converted to public limited company in 2017; listed on NSE November 2017.
Managed by third and fourth generation: Ratanlal Kataria, Anil Kataria, Santosh Kataria
and Vikas Kataria. As on 30 September 2025, 11 showrooms, 50,650 sq ft combined retail
area.
Key weaknesses: highly competitive and fragmented G&J industry limiting pricing
flexibility. Gold is over 90-95% of total cost and subject to sharp volatility;
inventory replenishment model provides partial mitigation. Regulatory risk: gold is
India's second-largest import bill after petroleum; sector vulnerable to import-tariff
and disclosure-threshold changes.
Liquidity: Adequate. Long-term debt to be fully repaid by FY26. Average working capital
utilisation ~68% for the 12 months ended September 2025. Operating cycle 54 days FY25
(54 days FY24). Free cash and bank balances Rs 19.06 crore as on 30 September 2025.
Current ratio 1.77x as on 31 March 2025 (1.67x as on 31 March 2024).
