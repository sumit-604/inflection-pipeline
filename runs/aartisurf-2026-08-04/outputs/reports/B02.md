### 1. ECL cross-note contradiction — RESOLVED (verified against source text directly)

Pass 2 flagged that Note 6.2 (ECL allowance roll-forward, standalone p.112 PDF /
consolidated Note equivalent) shows, for the FY24 comparative column: "Balance at
the beginning of the year 128.39", "Movement in Expected Credit Loss Allowance —
(nil)", "Balance at the end of the year 128.39" — i.e., literally zero ECL movement
in FY24. But Note 31 (Other Expenses, standalone and consolidated, p.127/181 PDF)
and the Cash Flow Statement addback (both standalone p.109/8059 and consolidated
p.11756) independently and consistently disclose **"Provision for Expected Credit
Loss / Allowance for expected credit loss made: 16.86 FY25 / 58.42 FY24"**.

Verified directly against source text (three independent locations: Note 31
standalone, Note 30 consolidated, and both Cash Flow Statements) — all three show
₹58.42 lakh as the FY24 P&L charge, none show a different number. Note 6.2's FY24
comparative column is the outlier and is internally self-contradictory on its own
terms: it shows the FY24 *opening* balance (128.39) identical to the FY24 *closing*
balance (128.39), which is also identical to the FY25 opening balance — i.e., the
FY24 column of Note 6.2 appears to repeat the same static figure across all three
lines rather than showing a true FY23→FY24 roll-forward (which, if the ₹58.42 lakh
charge is correct, should show an FY23 opening balance of roughly ₹69.97 lakh
[128.39 − 58.42], a movement of 58.42, and a closing balance of 128.39).

**Resolution: this is most likely a drafting/template error in Note 6.2's FY24
comparative column** (a cell copied from the FY25 opening balance instead of the
true FY23 opening figure), not a sign of an undisclosed write-back or a real
accounting inconsistency in the P&L charge itself — the P&L-side figure (₹58.42
lakh) is corroborated in three independent places in both standalone and
consolidated statements. However, this cannot be fully confirmed from the text
alone (the "amount written off during the year" line is nil in both years in Note
6.2, ruling out a write-off explanation), so it is flagged as a genuine, unresolved
note-drafting/disclosure-integrity defect rather than closed out entirely. 🟡 Watch
— disclosure integrity, not an earnings-quality or cash concern (the ECL
*allowance* balances themselves, ₹128.39 lakh FY24 and ₹145.26 lakh FY25, are
consistent everywhere they appear — Note 6, Note 6.2, and the receivables ageing
schedule).

### 2. Pattern-level: vague disclosure sits directly next to granular disclosure

Cross-referencing note style across the note set: RPT (Note 38), borrowings (Note
14/18), and contingent liabilities (Note 34) are all disclosed with named
counterparties, exact rates, and tenure-level granularity. By contrast, the single
largest determinant of revenue durability — the two customers now representing
71.0% of total product sales (Note 36/35, "Information about major customers") —
is disclosed with zero counterparty names, no payment-term specificity beyond the
general 15-90/15-135 day ranges in Note 24.1(b), and no contractual protection
detail. This is a genuine disparity in disclosure granularity between routine
compliance items (RPT, borrowings) and the single most decision-relevant
disclosure in the entire note set. Not new in substance (Pass 1 already rated
customer concentration a Red Flag) but the *comparative vagueness* against the
rest of the document is a pattern-level observation that reinforces, rather than
duplicates, that finding.

2. Can you characterize (without necessarily naming) the two customers now accounting for 71.0% of total product sales — contract tenure, pricing/renewal terms, and any contractual protections — and what is driving the concentration increase from 64.0% to 71.0% YoY?
3. Note 6.2's FY24 comparative column shows a nil ECL allowance movement while Note 31 (Other Expenses) and the Cash Flow Statement both show a ₹58.42 lakh FY24 P&L charge for ECL — can you confirm whether the Note 6.2 FY24 column contains a drafting error, and reconcile the two?
4. With MSME trade payables up nearly 5x YoY and total trade payables up 36.9% against revenue growth of only 11.7%, is this a deliberate treasury decision, and can the company confirm on a transaction-level (not just annual-bucket) basis that all MSME payments remain within the 45-day statutory window?
5. Given the Deferred Incentive Income balance is ₹10.57 Cr and growing (+22.0% YoY), what is the expected multi-year run-rate of this non-cash income recognition into P&L, and is the FY25 one-time ₹6.34 Cr state grant likely to recur (in full, reduced, or zero form) in FY26?

### E. NOTES-BASED RED FLAGS

- **Earnings management / aggressive accounting signal**: heavy reliance on
  non-recurring items — a one-time state government grant (₹6.34 Cr), a one-time
  insurance claim (₹1.94 Cr), and an exceptional insurance gain (₹4.20 Cr) —
  together equal to 59.0% of reported standalone PBT. Strip these out and PBT
  would have fallen ~74% YoY versus the reported ~36%. Management's own Note 43
  Ratio Analysis commentary attributes the ROE/margin decline solely to "higher
  input cost," which does not acknowledge that one-time gains partially offset
  that deterioration — this is the most consequential earnings-quality finding
  across all three passes.
- **Undisclosed risk indicators**: (i) subsidiary Aarti HPC's net assets have
  fallen below the parent's cost of investment with no evident impairment test or
  disclosure against the company's own stated policy; (ii) the ECL allowance
  roll-forward (Note 6.2) does not reconcile with the P&L ECL charge disclosed
  elsewhere (Note 31) for FY24, unresolved; (iii) "Other Provisions" (a ₹1.33 Cr
  YoY swing) and "non-deductible expenses" in the tax reconciliation (₹5.09 Cr,
  24.1% of PBT) are both undisclosed in composition.
- **No evidence found of**: revenue recognition manipulation, channel stuffing
  proven from the notes alone (inventory buildup is flagged as a Watch item, not
  confirmed channel stuffing), off-balance-sheet financing, related-party
  self-dealing at non-arm's-length terms, or going-concern doubt.
