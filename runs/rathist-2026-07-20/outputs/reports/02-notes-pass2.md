### 1. 🔴 Promoter-group identity of the company's own preference shareholders and largest equity shareholder is confirmed by Note 11 itself, yet omitted from the Related Party Transactions note
Note 11's own sub-table "Promoters/Promoter Group shareholders holding during the year" (p.80) explicitly lists, as Promoter/Promoter Group:
- Equity: Pradeep Kumar Rathi (9.11%), Udit Rathi (1.34%), **PCR Holdings Private Ltd (26.41%)**, and a previously-unidentified **DBG Leasing & Housing Limited (4.44%, 38,32,472 shares, down from 4.51% PY)**.
- Preference: **Char Investment and Trading Limited (72.57% of preference pool)** and **Lenzing Poly Packs Limited (27.43%)** — i.e., effectively 100% of preference capital.

This directly resolves the open question Pass 1 flagged at Note 11 ("whether Char Investment, Lenzing Poly Packs or PCR Holdings are promoter-linked entities is NOT FOUND IN DOCUMENT"). It is now confirmed IN DOCUMENT: yes, they are promoter group. But the Related Party Disclosure (Notes on Accounts Item 12, p.92) lists only Smt Sushila Rathi as a "Related Party (Individual)" and gives no entity-level related-party listing for PCR Holdings, Char Investment, Lenzing Poly Packs, or DBG Leasing & Housing — despite Note 11's own table confirming their promoter-group status a page earlier in the same financial statements. This is a genuine internal inconsistency between two notes (Note 11 vs. Notes on Accounts Item 12) rather than a mere disclosure gap, and it is more material than Pass 1's original framing suggested: nearly the entire preference-share capital base and the single largest equity block are promoter-group-controlled entities whose transactions/balances with the company (if any beyond share subscription) are not evaluated under Ind AS 24 in this AR. (Note 11, p.79-80; cross-ref Notes on Accounts Item 12, p.92.)

### 2. 🟡 New 6.63% shareholder (ASG Trading Pvt Ltd) appeared during the year, not identified as promoter-group and unexplained
Note 11's "details of shareholders holding more than 5% shares" table (p.79) shows **ASG Trading Pvt Ltd holding 56,40,573 shares (6.63%) as at 31 March 2025, versus 0.00% (nil holding) at 31 March 2024** — a new >5% shareholder appearing within the year. (Note 11, p.79.)

### 3. 🔴 Auditor's CARO Annexure-A ties the undisclosed "surety for others" specifically to Section 185/186 (director-interested party) transactions, which appears to contradict the Corporate Governance Report's own representation
Notes on Accounts Item 18 (p.93) discloses only: "Surety given for others, amount not ascertained as company has not maintained any such records" — flagged by Pass 1 as a record-keeping gap. Re-reading the Auditor's Report Annexure-A item 4 (p.70) adds a materially different dimension: "...the Company has not granted any loans, made any investment, given any guarantee or security... to any companies, firms or other parties **except surety with Sales Tax department for other Companies as covered under section 185 and 186 of the Act** over and above the limits prescribed under these sections." Sections 185/186 of the Companies Act govern loans/guarantees/investments to parties in which directors are interested. This means the unquantified "surety for others" is specifically surety extended (via the Sales Tax department, presumably for other-company tax liabilities) to entities that fall under the director-interest provisions — i.e., promoter/director-linked companies, not arm's-length third parties. This appears to directly contradict a separate representation in the Corporate Governance Report (Other Disclosures, "Particulars of Loans, Guarantees and Securities," p.62): **"During the financial year under review, the Company have neither advanced any loans nor given any guarantees and/or provided any securities, whether directly or indirectly to firms/companies in which directors are interested."** Both statements are in the same Annual Report; one (Governance Report) denies any guarantee to director-interested entities, the other (Auditor's CARO Annexure) confirms an unquantified surety exists that is specifically categorized under the director-interest sections. This is a disclosure contradiction, not merely a gap, and upgrades the Item 18 finding from 🟡 to 🔴. (Notes on Accounts Item 18, p.93; Auditor's Annexure-A item 4, p.70; Corporate Governance Report "Other Disclosures" xi, p.62.)

### 4. 🔴 Cash Flow Statement carries a ₹63.23 Cr FY24 financing outflow ("Receipt/Waiver Short Term Borrowings") that is unexplained by, and far larger than, all disclosed debt-waiver items combined
The Cash Flow Statement's Financing Activities section (p.85) includes a line "Receipt/ Waiver Short Term Borrowings" of **₹506.00 Lakh (FY25) versus ₹(6,323.34) Lakh, i.e. an outflow of ₹63.23 Cr, in FY24** — the single largest financing cash flow line item disclosed for FY24, exceeding even the ₹110.69 Cr gross share capital proceeds that year. No note in the financial statements explains this figure or cross-references it. It is far larger than the sum of every debt-waiver item Pass 1 identified elsewhere in the AR: the ₹12.76 Cr General Reserve credit "on account of Waiver of Term Loan by Lenders" (Note 12, p.81) plus the ₹19.84 Cr "Waiver of Short Term Borrowings from Lenders" exceptional P&L item (Note 27, p.84) sum to only ₹32.6 Cr, roughly half the ₹63.23 Cr cash flow figure. This implies a materially larger legacy-debt repayment/settlement/waiver event took place in FY24 than what the notes disclose in aggregate, with no reconciling note anywhere in the document. (Cash Flow Statement, p.85; cross-ref Note 12 p.81, Note 27 p.84.)

### 5. 🔴 Auditor's CARO Annexure-A confirms finished-goods/raw-material physical verification is done on an ESTIMATION basis, not full count — direct new evidence bearing on the Note 6 inventory red flag
Auditor's Annexure-A item 2 (Inventories, p.70): "the physical verification of inventory (excluding stocks lying with third parties) has been conducted by the management at reasonable intervals. **The physical verification of finished goods and raw material has been done on estimation basis**, from time to time, due to the nature of the business and the cost involved and discrepancies, if any, were properly dealt with in the accounts. In respect of goods inventory lying with third parties, these have **substantially** been confirmed by them." This was not picked up in Pass 1 and materially reinforces the existing Note 6 Red Flag (finished goods +145.9% against 2.0% revenue growth, no write-down disclosure): the ₹34.41 Cr finished-goods balance underlying that swing is itself verified only by estimation, not full physical count, and third-party-held inventory is only "substantially" (not fully) confirmed. This adds a verification-reliability dimension to the existing inventory concern rather than standing alone. (Auditor's Annexure-A item 2, p.70; cross-ref Note 6, p.78, and Note 23, p.83.)

### 6. 🟡 CARO tax-dispute table ties out precisely to three of four Contingent Liability categories — but a ₹11.88 Cr income-tax dispute bucket sits outside Pass 1's ₹29.01 Cr contingent-liability aggregate
Auditor's Annexure-A item 7(b) (p.70-71) itemises statutory dues not deposited due to dispute by forum and period. Summing by category: Excise/Service Tax ₹317.15+133.32+77.21+239.61 = ₹767.29 Lakh = **exact match** to Notes on Accounts Item 2(d) Excise/Service Tax contingent liability of ₹7,67,29,699; Sales Tax ₹13.70+458.25+44.51 = ₹516.46 Lakh = **exact match** to Item 2(c) VAT/Sales Tax ₹5,16,45,541; GST ₹257.90+58.22+24.21 = ₹340.33 Lakh = **exact match** to Item 2(e) GST ₹3,40,33,408. This is a positive tie-out (🟢 in isolation) that Pass 1 did not perform. However, the same CARO table also discloses **Income Tax Act 1961 disputed dues of ₹11.85 Lakh (penalty/late fees, AY2014-15) + ₹21.99 Lakh (expenditure additions, AY2021-22/2022-23) = ₹33.84 Lakh**, plus a separate footnote: **"Income tax AO(s) made additions amounting to Rs.1,188.17 lacs for AY 2013-14 & 2019-20 and the same were adjusted with carry forward losses... as of now there is no tax demand or liability against these orders."** None of these income-tax figures were rolled into Pass 1's ₹29.01 Cr / ≈21.2%-of-net-worth contingent liability aggregate, which covered only VAT/excise/GST/civil-labour buckets (the Notes on Accounts Item 2 narrative income-tax items (a)-(h), p.90-91, disclose the same individual additions — ₹10.20 Cr AY2013-14 + ₹1.68 Cr AY2019-20 ≈ the CARO's ₹11.88 Cr figure — narratively but without a rupee total, which is why Pass 1 treated them as "🟢 routine, adequately narrated" rather than quantifying them into the aggregate). Even though management represents these are covered by carry-forward losses with no live demand, the gross disputed-addition quantum (₹11.88 Cr, ~8.7% of net worth) was not counted in Pass 1's headline contingent-liability percentage and should be treated as a supplementary quantified exposure. (Auditor's Annexure-A item 7(b), p.70-71; cross-ref Notes on Accounts Item 2, p.90.)
