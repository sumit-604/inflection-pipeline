## BUSINESS UNDERSTANDING NARRATIVE

Keltech makes industrial explosives at 12 licensed plants, most of them in mining districts. Explosives were 86% of FY26 revenue, in cartridge and bulk emulsion form, plus accessories such as detonating fuse and cast boosters. A mine or quarry cannot break rock without them, and only licence holders under the Explosives Act and the Ammonium Nitrate Rules may make them. Perlite was about 11% of revenue: an expanded mineral sold as filter aid to edible oil and pharma makers, as cryogenic insulation and as horticulture mix. The buyers are public sector miners such as MOIL, new mine developer operators, dealers, cement and quarry users, and export buyers. No customer reaches 10% of revenue, and the top ten took about 21% in FY25. Public sector bulk orders go to the lowest bidder, all three listed peers call that work thin or negative margin, and MOIL and other PSU mining customer results and deduction notices show where risk purchase and powder factor deductions cut the realised price. The run did not establish qualification cycles or switching costs. Present demand follows rock moved, so the live signals are Coal India and Singareni overburden removal and coal output, and cement, limestone quarry, metal ore and infrastructure output. Ammonium nitrate price and anti dumping or import duty actions set the spread, because materials take about 67% of revenue. The run puts the Indian industrial explosives market at ₹8,000 Cr to ₹13,525 Cr, growing about 7% a year, but H1 FY27 sales rose 32% with no volume disclosed, and peers say much of this year's growth is price. The forward checks are PESO licence validity, new licences and explosives export approvals, and peer commentary on domestic volume, pricing and TNT supply from Solar, Premier and Apollo IDL. The new TNT plant has no named buyer, so defence ammunition filling demand and any named TNT buyer is the only route to outside TNT revenue. If the plant feeds Keltech's own boosters instead, it adds no outside demand. The explosives line holds a licence and plant footprint position of medium durability, but every rival holds the same licence type, so it is a cost of entry and not an edge. The emerging moat scan found no category at Strong or Moderate, and no evidence for talent asymmetry (Category 21) or a cannibalisation barrier (Category 22). Perlite has no moat the run could evidence, and the TNT build is documented as spending, not as a scarce capability.

## Thin margin explosives maker bets on TNT

Keltech has made explosives since 1977 and belongs to the Goa based Chowgule group. FY26 revenue was ₹532 Cr (₹53,206 lakh) and profit after tax ₹28.7 Cr (₹2,866 lakh), up 15%. Explosives volume rose 8.4% to 63,154 tonnes. Exports were ₹83 Cr (₹8,284 lakh), 15.6% of revenue, and the company discloses no market, product or margin for them. The FY26 EBITDA margin was about 7.7% on the screener basis, against 10.2% at Premier Explosives and 27.6% at Solar Industries. The headline understates the year: after the ₹8.8 Cr (₹884.72 lakh) of one time FY25 items comes out, the margin rose from 6.1% to 7.7%.

The change underway is a TNT plant at Garamsur. CRISIL puts the cost at about ₹190 Cr, funded by a ₹135 Cr Canara Bank term loan that runs to September 2034, with operations from April 2027. The company's own words, in a Board's Report approved in May 2026, are that the imported nitration technology will be "commissioned fully in 2027". Capital work in progress rose from ₹38 Cr to ₹63 Cr (₹3,800.82 lakh to ₹6,280.63 lakh) between March and September 2026, and that line also holds a silo project. On the widest count about 40% of the cost is spent, and at least ₹114 Cr remains against ₹25 Cr paid in H1 FY27. No filing names a capacity, a licence category, a product or a buyer. Two readings fit. In the first, TNT feeds Keltech's own cast boosters and earns commercial explosive economics; the annual report wording and CRISIL's "established clientele" phrase favour it. In the second, Keltech sells TNT into a tight defence energetics market; ₹190 Cr is large for captive use at this scale, so that reading stays open. The licence category, and the product and customer the company names at commissioning, will separate them.

Gate 0 scores 63 of 160 as stated and 64 on the framework verifier's recount, AVERAGE either way. The core score of 53 of 100 sits 7 points under the GOOD line, and three NOT FOUND items (pledge, payables history, a listed player census) score zero and could add up to 15 points. The cash block scores 7 of 20: FY25 operating cash flow was negative ₹17.6 Cr, FY26 capex was ₹86 Cr, and free cash flow from FY18 to FY26 sums to negative ₹5.6 Cr. ROCE was 21.1% in FY26 and 25.9% in FY25. The explosives segment margin, on the results filing basis, fell four quarters in a row from 9.00% in Q4 FY25 to 4.64% in Q3 FY26, then recovered to 6.19% and 6.74%, for 6.44% across H1 FY27. That fall began before the new plant started in March 2026. One reading is an ammonium nitrate cost lag, since Solar passes about 60% of a cost rise through at once and the rest a quarter later. The other is share bought on price in lowest bid tenders, which would hold the margin near 6% to 7%. The Q3 FY27 margin, against 4.64% a year earlier and 9.00% in Q4 FY25, separates them. Q2 FY27 EBITDA excluding other income was 8.16%, while H1 FY27 depreciation rose 28.7% and finance cost 76.3% against profit growth of 13.4%.

Cash conversion is the weakest part of the record. H1 FY27 operating cash flow was ₹6.4 Cr (₹638.39 lakh) against profit of ₹16.3 Cr (₹1,633.10 lakh), a ratio of 0.39, or 0.59 once bank deposit movements are taken out. FY26 cash flow of ₹48.5 Cr (₹4,850.02 lakh) beat profit on every cut, but FY25 was negative, and both years move with bank balance lines. Trade payable days rose from 67.8 to 76.1 between Q4 FY26 and Q2 FY27, and the filings do not say how much of that is capital creditors for the plant. So the cash read stays INDETERMINATE. Receivables over six months rose to 7.08% of gross from 4.25%. The inventory Keltech reports to its bank consortium ran ahead of its books in all four quarters, by 23.7% to 43.6% of the book figure (19.2% to 30.3% of the reported figure), against a 10% tolerance, and the March 2026 return equals the March 2025 balance sheet figure. The auditor calls the gap not material and reports an internal control exception on period end close; accounting quality scores 5 of 10. On the other side, the opinion is unmodified, CRISIL rates the bank lines BBB+, and no covenant breach or default appears.

Management holds no calls and issues no presentations, so credibility rests on filings against the annual report and the rating rationale. The grade is C. Of ten checkable items, 4 were delivered, 6 are partial or pending, and none missed, but the delivered items test rating agency expectations, such as net worth of ₹153 Cr against about ₹145 Cr, not multiyear company promises. Disclosure slips recur. FY25 Perlite volume appears as 24,584 tonnes in one annual report and 18,704 tonnes in the next, with no restatement note. The Board's Report says exports grew 7% while the notes give 11%. The annual report's explosives segment result sits ₹4.6 Cr (₹464.31 lakh) above the results filing basis. In April 2026, in the middle of the TNT build, the MD and the Vice-Chairman took executive roles at an unnamed group company; they drew ₹4.1 Cr (₹410.26 lakh), 14.3% of FY26 profit.

The promoter read is CAUTION. A letter of intent dated 27 October 2026 moves 40.99% of the equity from Chowgule and Company to Vijay V Chowgule under a 2021 family settlement that went through arbitration. Total promoter holding stays at 54.33%, flat for 12 quarters, and aggregators show no pledge, though the filed pattern was not retrieved. He becomes the direct holder of about 45%, is 79, and has no named successor. Media reports, not filings in the corpus, describe an Enforcement Directorate FEMA enquiry with summonses in 2024 and 2026 and no charge, an ICRA cut to BB+ Issuer Not Cooperating in February 2026, and a ₹15 Cr loan agreement with promoter group company Dolphin Investment. Four of five independent directors sit on group company boards. Against that, the promoter built the plant base, funded the growth with bank debt and no dilution, and grew profit through a five year family dispute; only the return on the new capital is unproven.

The peers confirm two things and contradict one. Premier, Solar and Apollo's IDL all describe Coal India and Singareni bulk as lowest bid work with thin or negative margins. All three are adding capacity, led by Solar's ₹2,050 Cr FY27 programme. Solar alone confirms ammonium nitrate pass through, and Apollo alone describes IDL, which had "presently negative EBITDA" in May 2026. The contradiction is on dates: five of five dated peer energetics projects slipped one to three quarters, and Premier's Katepally plant moved from FY26 end to water trials in September 2026. No peer states a TNT price or buyer. Apollo's TNT licence covers its own industrial explosive needs, which weakens the case for a second merchant TNT supplier.

The runway class is GOOD, but the sizing is thin. The market estimate rests on one research page and one unsourced blog. The ₹10,507 Cr serviceable market is a ceiling, since only exports were removed. Keltech holds about 3.6% of it, with 28 times headroom. The central case grows revenue about 9.5% a year for three years, or 13.1% if share gains one point. Installed capacity is not disclosed, so the 24% volume rise the central case needs by year three cannot be checked, and TNT sits outside every number. H1 FY27 volume in tonnes would separate the price story from the share story, and no filing gives it.
