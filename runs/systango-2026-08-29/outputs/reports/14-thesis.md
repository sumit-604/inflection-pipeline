## NARRATIVE (plain English)

Systango is a cheap, clean-looking small IT-outsourcing business with a governance
shadow over its cash. The numbers pass every valuation gate: 33% ROCE, net cash of
Rs 80.8 a share, and an operating business the market prices at 7-8x while the
framework earns it 16.8x. On those numbers, every case beats 25% a year.

That is exactly why the name is a watch, not a buy. When a model shows no
downside, the risk is the thing the model cannot see. Two things it cannot see
sit at the centre here. First, the transition is a story, not a fact: revenue,
margin and return on capital are all flat looking forward, and only cash
conversion points up, and even that turns on one number in the coming annual
report. Second, a UK entity called DBX flipped from a customer to a
promoter-linked shareholder, and nobody has disclosed who owns it. That entity
sits next to the cash that is 40% of the value.

So most of the return, if it comes, is the market re-pricing a cheap stock, not
the business getting better. That is a discount closing, not a compounding engine.
Two dated events settle it: the annual report in September and the half-year print
in November. Until both clear, the honest action is to watch, size nothing, and
let the proof arrive.
