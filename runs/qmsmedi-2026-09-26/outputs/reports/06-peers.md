## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

### 2A Demand environment
All three peers describe an improving demand backdrop for CY2025/FY26 relative to a slower FY24-FY25: INDGN cites the pharma industry growing "9% versus 6.4% in CY2024" and a "5%-8% CAGR" outlook through 2028-29, driven by anti-obesity, immunology and oncology; ENTERO notes IPM (Indian Pharma Market) growth moving from a 7-9% trailing range back up to 10-12% in the last two quarters, though management "cannot isolate any specific factor" for the jump; POLYMED describes strong Indian private-hospital demand (20-25%+ growth) and international demand as "intact" even where blocked by logistics (Middle East). None of the three peers frames this recovery around a GLP-1-driven patient-support boom of the kind QMS's guidance leans on — the improving demand each peer cites is broader and less singularly GLP-1-attributed than QMS's framing implies. This is a mild complication, not a contradiction: the macro tailwind is real, but QMS's specific narrative (a GLP-1-driven PSP demand surge) is narrower and more concentrated than what any peer independently describes for the wider industry.

### 2B Pricing and input costs
No clean read-across to QMS's margin narrative exists across the peer set, because the three peers face structurally different cost drivers. POLYMED (a manufacturer) reports a genuine, quantified input-cost shock: "almost a 20% increase" in crude-linked raw material and packaging costs from Q4 FY26 into Q1 FY27, offset by 3-5% price hikes and rupee depreciation (its business is majority export). ENTERO's margin expansion is mix-driven (rising MedTech share, which "plays a more commercial role" and earns "10%+ EBITDA margins" vs pure pharma distribution's much thinner margin), not input-cost driven. INDGN's margin moves are wage-cycle and discretionary-investment driven (annual July increment cycle, deliberate GTM/GenAI investment), not raw-material or pricing related. None of the three describes a margin story resembling QMS's own (services-mix shift that should lift margin but has instead seen margin fall for three straight quarters per B05) — the peer set neither confirms nor refutes QMS's margin narrative; it simply shows margin drivers in this space are heterogeneous and company-specific.

One data point is directly relevant to LBF1 (services vs. distribution margin): ENTERO discloses that pure pharma distribution earns single-digit EBITDA margins (~4-5% company-wide) while playing a fuller "commercial role" (demand generation, not just fulfilment) in MedTech distribution lifts margins into "10%+" territory — "at least double of normal distribution margin" per Prabhat Agrawal (Q4 FY26 call). INDGN, a pure services company, runs an EBITDA margin band of roughly 17-20% historically (guided back to 19-20% by Q4 FY27). This corroborates the structural logic behind QMS's own SOTP framing (B04: Saarathi/services standalone net margin ~20.7% vs parent/products ~4.4%) — services genuinely earn a different margin regime than distribution/products in this adjacent industry — even though QMS itself has not yet delivered the margin uplift its mix shift implies.

### 2C Capex cycle
All three peers are simultaneously investing/expanding, not holding or cutting: INDGN is running GTM, GenAI-platform and M&A investment (BioPharm, Warn, Cake) funded from a strong cash balance; ENTERO closed 7 acquisitions in FY26 (~₹1,000cr of MedTech revenue) before deliberately pausing new M&A for FY27 to "consolidate, integrate and organically build"; POLYMED is running ₹200-296cr/year of capex (new plants in Mitrol, Haridwar, Noida/YEIDA) plus cross-border M&A (PendraCare, Citieffe, a small Brazil entry) under its "PolyMed 3.0 — Ascent" plan to double revenue by FY30. This is an industry-wide capacity/investment race across Indian pharma-adjacent services, distribution and devices in 2025-26, not a lone-expander situation. QMS's own front-loaded hiring and Saarathi consolidation sit inside a genuinely sector-wide "invest ahead of revenue" pattern — though QMS's scale (₹173cr FY26 revenue) is two to three orders of magnitude smaller than any of these three peers, so the comparison is directional, not a scale match.

### 2D Competitive mentions
No peer mentions QMS Medical Allied Services, Saarathi, or any unmistakable description of QMS's business by name across all 12 transcripts (confirmed by full-text search on "QMS Medical," "QMS Medi," and close variants — zero hits). No corroborating or contradicting competitive signal exists in either direction.

### 2E Risks peers discuss that QMS does not
- **Chinese dumping / zero-duty ASEAN import competition on medical devices.** Raised repeatedly and specifically by POLYMED across all four calls ("China is dumping in every industry... they are getting products at 0 duty in India"; an antidumping investigation into Chinese/Malaysian dialyzer imports was filed and is under government review by Q1 FY27). This is directly relevant to QMS's Products segment, which resells third-party imported devices (3M, Heine, Rossmax) — yet no QMS concall in the B05 corpus (Q1 FY26 through Q1 FY27) raises Chinese import competition, dumping, or ASEAN-FTA duty arbitrage as a risk to its Products line. This is a real gap: an industry-wide device-import risk that QMS management has never addressed on any call.
- **New labour code implementation, one-time cost impact.** Both ENTERO (₹6.1cr exceptional PAT impact, Q3 FY26) and POLYMED (₹6.8cr exceptional cost, Q3 FY26) flagged one-time costs from India's new labour code (gratuity/leave-encashment provisioning change) in the same quarter. QMS's own concalls (per B05) never mention the labour code as a cost driver, despite QMS's own Q1 FY27 employee-cost jump (₹3.2cr to ₹11.2cr YoY, per B04) being unexplained beyond "front-loaded hiring." Whether the labour code contributed to QMS's own cost jump is untested — worth a direct management question.
- **Explicit, quarter-by-quarter disclosure of minority-interest/put-option mechanics on partial-stake acquisitions.** ENTERO discloses, every quarter, the percentage ownership, call-option valuation basis, and time horizon (2-5 years) for buying out minority stakes in its acquired subsidiaries, and fields detailed analyst questions on this without evasion. This is a useful governance contrast for LBF3 (Saarathi price ladder): QMS has been comparatively less transparent about the economics, related-party financing (ICD) terms, and interest rate on its own partial-stake structure with Saarathi, per B05's finding that management "declined to disclose Saarathi standalone revenue/profitability separately" when asked directly.
- **Working-capital intensity, LBF2 cross-check.** ENTERO (pure distribution) runs net working-capital days of ~59-66 (declining trend, guided toward ~60); POLYMED (manufacturer) runs a materially higher cash-conversion cycle of ~140 days, reflecting inventory-heavy, IP-driven manufacturing. QMS's own working-capital position (B00 LBF2: receivables ₹67.3cr, inventory ₹45.9cr at ~181 inventory days, against FY26 revenue of ₹172.9cr) implies a working-capital intensity that sits well above even POLYMED's manufacturer-grade cycle, despite QMS being framed as a hybrid distribution/services business that should, on ENTERO's evidence, run closer to a leaner distribution-style working-capital profile. This reinforces B04's WC-intensity flag ("high") with an external comparator: QMS's inventory days look structurally elevated even against a full device manufacturer, let alone a pure distributor.

## PART 4: TRIANGULATION SUMMARY

- Claims verified: 0 of 5.
- Claims partially verified: 2 of 5 (Claim 3 — renewal/escalation industry norm, direction only; Claim 5 — GLP-1 demand tailwind, direction only).
- Claims contradicted: 1 of 5 (Claim 2 — supply-chain/shipping disruption in Oct-2025-Mar-2026). This is a priority item for synthesis.
- Claims unverifiable: 2 of 5 (Claim 1 — $70bn PSP market/spend-shift; Claim 4 — Practo/online-platform threat).

**Single most consequential contradiction:** POLYMED's Q2 FY26 call describes global shipping transit times to Europe IMPROVING (Suez Canal reopening cut transit from 2.5 months to 1 month) during the exact window QMS blames for its H2 FY26 product-revenue slowdown, while POLYMED's own later, genuine logistics disruption (Gulf war/Middle East) starts only from around Q4 FY26 onward and runs in the opposite trade direction (Indian exports blocked, not Indian product imports delayed). This directly undercuts QMS's external-blame framing and reinforces the B05 finding that QMS's excuse pattern skews toward unacknowledged, company-specific misses.

**Single strongest independent confirmation:** ENTERO's granular, four-quarter-consistent GLP-1 commentary (named manufacturer relationships with Eli Lilly and Wegovy, an expected influx of generic manufacturers once semaglutide comes off patent, direct-to-patient online distribution, and a quantified ~5% share of category value) independently confirms that GLP-1/anti-obesity is a genuine, structural demand driver in the Indian healthcare-adjacent market during this exact period — corroborated at the macro level by INDGN's Q1 FY27 statement that anti-obesity is the "primary growth driver" among top global pharma companies.

**Overall assessment:** The peer set complicates QMS's narrative more than it supports or undercuts it wholesale. It genuinely supports the GLP-1 demand thesis (direction, not magnitude) and the services-vs-distribution margin logic behind QMS's SOTP framing. It directly undercuts QMS's specific external-blame explanation for its H2 FY26 product-revenue miss. And it leaves QMS's two most distinctive, hardest-to-check claims (the $70bn PSP market anchor and the Practo/online-disruption dismissal) entirely untested, because no peer in this set operates close enough to those specific claims to confirm or deny them. Symmetrically: nothing here proves QMS's PSP thesis is wrong, but nothing here proves it right either, beyond the general tailwind.

## PART 5: CROSS-PEER HYPOTHESIS

All three peers are running simultaneous, sizeable investment programmes (INDGN's GTM/GenAI/M&A spend, ENTERO's ~₹1,000cr FY26 MedTech M&A binge followed by a deliberate FY27 pause, POLYMED's ₹200-296cr/year capex plus cross-border M&A) funded from strong balance sheets (IPO/QIP proceeds or accumulated cash), and each independently reports a margin trajectory that has run behind its own guidance or historical band during the investment phase, with management attributing the gap to "investment now, payback later" dynamics rather than to demand weakness. None of the three peers frames this as a sector-wide phenomenon — each explains its own margin shortfall in company-specific terms (INDGN: wage cycle plus deliberate GTM investment; ENTERO: MedTech integration costs before synergies land; POLYMED: crude-linked raw-material inflation). But the combined pattern — three unrelated healthcare-adjacent Indian companies, all richly capitalised from recent IPOs/QIPs, all investing ahead of revenue in 2025-26, all missing or slipping their own near-term margin guidance during the build phase — suggests a **sector-wide financing pattern**: capital markets have funded an investment supercycle in Indian healthcare-adjacent services/distribution/devices that is now running a systematic lag between capital deployment and margin realisation, wider than any single company's stated timeline. This is testable: if margin-guidance slippage continues to recur across INDGN, ENTERO and POLYMED for another 2-3 quarters in the same direction (guided-margin date pushed out again), the sector-wide financing-lag hypothesis strengthens; if margins normalise on the originally guided schedule across all three, it weakens. For QMS specifically, this reframes (without excusing) its own three-quarters-running margin-guidance slippage (B05, flagged MEDIUM) as potentially sitting inside a broader capital-markets-driven investment cycle rather than being purely a QMS-specific execution failure — though QMS's failure to acknowledge the slippage explicitly, unlike all three peers who name their own margin misses directly, remains a QMS-specific governance and disclosure gap, not something the sector pattern excuses.
