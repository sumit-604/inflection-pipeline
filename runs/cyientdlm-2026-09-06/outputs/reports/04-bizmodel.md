## UPFRONT: THIS IS A TWO-ENTITY STORY

Before any model decode, one fact governs everything below. Cyient DLM Limited (the
listed India parent, "standalone") and Cyient DLM Inc. (the US subsidiary, which itself
owns Altek Electronics Inc., acquired Oct-2024) are, right now, running in opposite
directions. Reading the consolidated numbers alone hides this.

| Metric | Standalone (India parent) | Consolidated (Group) |
|---|---|---|
| FY26 revenue | Rs9,426.52mn, down 29.9% YoY (AR p.135, Note 33) | Rs12,614.85mn, down 16.99% YoY (AR p.171, Note 34) |
| FY26 PAT | Rs563.24mn, down 26.6% YoY (AR p.171, Note 35 / p.110 P&L) | Rs732.82mn, UP 7.65% YoY (AR p.171, Note 35) |
| India-geography revenue | Rs1,113.15mn, down 84.4% from Rs7,137.15mn (AR p.135) | Rs1,218.90mn, down 83.2% from Rs7,256.92mn (AR p.171) |

The swing is entirely the US side. Cyient DLM Inc.'s share of consolidated profit went
from a Rs84.69mn LOSS in FY25 to a Rs246.69mn profit in FY26 (AR p.171, Note 35). Altek
Electronics Inc. (the US sub-subsidiary) earned less in a full FY26 (Rs31.32mn PAT) than
in roughly six months of FY25 post-acquisition (Rs39.70mn PAT) (AR p.171, Note 35) — its
own trajectory is soft even as the wrapper around it (Cyient DLM Inc.) swung to a large
profit, most likely reflecting the one-off Rs195.75mn earn-out reversal booked at the
Cyient DLM Inc. level (AR p.54, p.169, Note 33.1.4). Every revenue stream and margin
line below is tagged [INDIA] or [US] where the disclosure allows; where it does not,
that is stated.

# SECTION 1: THE BUSINESS MODEL IN PLAIN ENGLISH

## 1A. One-line description

Cyient DLM builds complex, safety-critical electronic assemblies (circuit boards, box
builds, cable harnesses, precision machined parts) for customers in aerospace &
defence, medical devices, industrial and automotive, who supply the design (Build-to-
Print) or increasingly let Cyient DLM co-design the product (Build-to-Spec), and pays
per unit or per program milestone (AR p.11, p.771 [text line ref]).

## 1B. The money flow chain, by revenue stream

**Stream 1 — Build-to-Print (B2P) manufacturing** [both India and US]
Customer's finished engineering drawings and bill of materials → Cyient DLM sources
components (700+ suppliers, AR p.55) and assembles/tests to spec → delivers a
finished PCB assembly, box build, cable harness or machined part → customer (OEM or
Tier-1 in A&D/medical/industrial/auto) → pays per unit/shipment, largely point-in-time
revenue recognition (AR p.144, Note 20: "goods transferred at a point in time," 89.7% of
standalone revenue in FY26).

**Stream 2 — Build-to-Spec (B2S) design-led manufacturing** [primarily India, ramping]
Customer's performance requirement (not a finished drawing) → Cyient DLM's
engineering teams design, prototype, qualify and then manufacture the product,
retaining influence over the Bill of Materials → delivers a qualified, production-ready
system plus ongoing manufacture → customer pays via a long-duration program
contract (9-20 years disclosed for two named B2S wins, AR p.7) with revenue partly
recognised over time as design/engineering milestones complete (AR p.144, Note 20:
"services transferred over time," 10.3% of standalone revenue in FY26 — this is the
best available proxy for the design/services component; it is not a clean B2S-only cut).

**Stream 3 — Aftermarket / value-added services** [not separately quantified]
Existing installed base and long product lifecycles → obsolescence management,
last-time-buy support, value engineering, localisation, sustenance manufacturing →
delivered to the same OEM customers as an extension of an existing program → billed
as part of ongoing program revenue, no standalone revenue line disclosed (AR p.25).
NOT FOUND as a separately quantified stream; treat as embedded in B2P/B2S totals.

**FLAG 1 — internal inconsistency in the AR's own B2S disclosure.** The BRSR Section 16
table (AR p.62-63) states B2S is 25% of turnover in FY26. The SET-framework infographic
elsewhere in the same AR (p.7) states "B2S revenue, FY26: 6%" with an explicit FY27
target of "DOUBLE-DIGIT" share. A company cannot credibly target "double-digit" share
for FY27 if it already disclosed 25% for FY26 — that would be a decline, not the growth
narrative management is telling everywhere else (chairman's letter p.9, MD&CEO letter
p.10, MD&A pp.51-53 all describe B2S as small and ramping). The 6% figure is corroborated
independently by the dollar-revenue split ($133.1M B2P / $8.8M B2S = 5.8%, AR p.61) and
by the standalone Ind-AS revenue disaggregation showing "services transferred over
time" (the closest proxy for design-led revenue) at only 10.3% of standalone revenue (AR
p.144, Note 20). Treat 25% as unreliable; use 6% (FY26) trending to "double-digit" (FY27
target) as the working number, and note this is a single-line data-quality defect inside
the AR that should be raised with management, not resolved by inference.

## 1E. The chai-stall-uncle version

Think of Cyient DLM as a very skilled tailor who works only for the most demanding
customers: airlines, hospitals, defence ministries. Most of the time, the customer
brings the exact pattern (drawing) and the tailor just cuts and stitches it perfectly,
fast, and gets paid per garment. That is Build-to-Print, most of the business today.
A smaller but growing part of the shop is different: some customers now say "we trust
you, design the suit yourself to fit our needs," and pay the tailor a long-term retainer
for years of repeat orders. That is Build-to-Spec, the higher-margin future the company
is chasing but which is still a small slice of the shop's till. The tailor also just opened
a second shop in America (through an acquisition) which had a rough patch and is now
doing much better, while the original India shop's local counter sales collapsed almost
completely as one big customer's order finished. The overall family business looks fine
on paper because the American shop's turnaround masks the Indian shop's slump — but
they are two different shops with two different stories.

## 2D. Industry lifecycle stage

Global EMS: mature but still growing (5-6%+ blended CAGR, Investor Presentation
slide 6). India EMS specifically: early-growth / expansion phase (17.5% CAGR to 2032,
AR p.50), riding China+1 diversification and defence indigenisation. Cyient DLM sits
in the high-complexity, high-reliability sub-segment of a growing industry, but the
company's own FY26 revenue fell 16.99% (consolidated) even as the industry grew —
a company-specific execution/order-timing issue (completion of a large defence order,
AR p.27) layered on top of a structurally growing market.

## 3A. Ignore-these-track-these

| Commonly tracked ratio | Verdict | Why |

## 3D. Unit economics — the physics of the business

Defining "one unit" precisely is hard because the company sells four distinct product
categories at very different price points (a cable harness at Rs196mn/16% of units-
worth vs a box build at Rs3,843mn/30%). The best available per-program unit economics:

| Element | Reading |
|---|---|
| Unit | One manufacturing program/contract (a qualified customer part number or assembly), NOT a single physical unit — programs vary from single-board PCBAs to multi-rack cabinet box builds (AR p.24) |
| Revenue per unit | NOT FOUND at the individual-program level; only aggregate product-line revenue is disclosed (AR p.24) |
| Cost drivers | Direct material (59.63% of consolidated revenue FY26, AR p.54) dominates; import dependency (85-90% of components, AR p.50) is the single biggest structural cost driver outside company control |
| Volume drivers | Order intake (Rs18,430mn FY26, +90% YoY, AR p.10) and program ramp timing (new logos entering mass production from FY28-29 per four anchor customers, AR p.49, p.51) |
| Price drivers | Largely set by customer negotiation and cost pass-through clauses on B2P (AR p.55); B2S carries genuine pricing power via BOM/design ownership but is currently ~6% of revenue |
| Incremental margin / operating leverage | Management's own stated thesis: 50-60% capacity utilisation today means incremental revenue should absorb fixed costs efficiently (AR p.10, p.51-52). This is UNPROVEN at scale — FY26 revenue fell and normalised EBITDA margin fell 10.2% YoY in the same year (AR p.27), the opposite of what the operating-leverage thesis predicts, though the decline was on a smaller revenue base, which cuts both ways |
| Key lever | Mix shift toward B2S and toward Box Build/Mech categories (higher engineering content) rather than commodity PCBA, PLUS working capital discipline, which the company has explicitly NOT delivered for four straight years (NWC 63→145 days, AR p.27) |

PRIMARY: Relative valuation (EV/EBITDA on normalised earnings) against AVALON/KAYNES/SYRMA.
SECONDARY: DCF, with explicit modelling of the WC drag as a cash-flow haircut, and the
order-book conversion rate as a cross-check on the growth assumption.
TERTIARY: Sum-of-the-parts (India standalone vs US Cyient DLM Inc./Altek), given the
scale of the two-entity divergence documented in this report.
Cycle stage that matters for valuation: this is a company entering a capacity-utilisation
upcycle (50-60% utilised, order book growing) after a demand air-pocket (large defence
order completion); the valuation window should weight the order-book-implied FY27
recovery, not the depressed FY26 print, but ONLY if the WC deterioration does not
continue to eat the cash that recovery would otherwise generate.

## 4D. Highest-value questions for management

1. **Which is the real B2S share of FY26 revenue: the BRSR-disclosed 25%, or the infographic-disclosed 6%?**
