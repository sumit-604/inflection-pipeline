## SECTION 3: THE 22-CATEGORY SCAN

**Family A — Product & Technology**

- **A1 rare manufacturing capability** — NO EVIDENCE FOUND. CMS is a services platform, not
  a manufacturer; the category does not apply cleanly.
- **A2 patent and IP pipeline** — NO EVIDENCE FOUND. No patent filing, filing trend, or
  licensing revenue is mentioned anywhere in the AR or four concalls. This is a real gap
  given the "proprietary technology" language used throughout the AR — the company narrates
  a technology moat but discloses zero IP protection for it. Carried to optionality
  register.
- **A3 process innovation** — Strong. "AI-led route optimisation let us reduce routes by 10%
  while we tightened network investment" (D, AR p.4, Chairman's letter), corroborated
  independently in the MD&A cost discussion: "Route optimisation (10%+ routes cut), gig
  transition, and automation pulled these costs down as a percentage of revenue in Q3 and
  Q4, supporting margin recovery" (D, AR p.51). Two independent AR sections, one number.
  Time to materialise: already realised (FY26).
- **A4 product platform / modular architecture** — Weak. ALGO MVS and ALGO OTC are two
  named modules under one platform brand (D, AR p.7), and "Deepen the Platform" is named as
  one of four FY30 strategic pillars with a stated goal of "modular solutions that enable
  clients to add new CMS services seamlessly" (C, AR p.19) — but no SKU count, launch
  frequency, or cross-sell revenue metric is disclosed to test the claim. Time to
  materialise: 2-3 years (cross-sell engine described as "next phase" post FY26
  reorganisation).

**Family B — Supply Chain**

- **B1 backward integration / RM security** — Moderate. "We own the full stack: hardware,
  software, AI models," with an in-house chipset (CMS Edge), AI modules and IoT panel built
  end-to-end for HAWKAI rather than bought from a third-party camera/sensor vendor (D, AR
  p.5, p.7; Inv. Pres. slide 19). This is real vertical integration into a hardware
  component, reducing vendor dependency for the Vision AI product specifically. Time to
  materialise: already built; scale impact on group economics not separately quantified.
- **B2 qualification lock-in** — NO EVIDENCE FOUND. No IATF/AS9100/ISO-class certification,
  design-in language, or customer-funded tooling is disclosed.
- **B3 supply chain network effect** — NO EVIDENCE FOUND in the classic fragmented-supplier-
  aggregation sense. The closest analogue — ALGO MVS as a "single command centre" unifying a
  bank's multi-vendor ATM estate — is scored under D2 (digital platform) instead, to avoid
  double-crediting the same fact through two categories.

**Family C — Customer**

- **C1 customer ecosystem / embedded relationships** — Strong. Three of India's largest
  banks signed integrated, multi-year, multi-product contracts within roughly 18 months:
  SBI ₹1,000cr / 10-year integrated cash outsourcing mandate, ~90%+ live (D, AR p.53);
  HDFC Bank ₹400cr TCV / 5-year / 6,000 ATMs, live from April 2026 (D, Inv. Pres. slide 6);
  ICICI Bank integrated deployment ~90% live, ALGO go-live FY27 (D, AR p.5, p.53). Each
  bundles cash logistics with software and/or Vision AI, which is the embedded-relationship
  test. Time to materialise: largely already live; full-year revenue contribution from FY27.
- **C2 customer concentration improving** — Moderate. SBI's share of revenue fell from 24%
  (FY25) to 20% (FY26); private-sector banks/retail rose from 26% to 30%; PSU banks (ex-SBI)
  rose 16% to 22%; managed-service-provider revenue fell from 34% to 28% (D, Inv. Pres.
  slide 17). Chairman's letter corroborates: "Today, 52% of our revenue comes from large
  private banks, other PSU banks and retail customers, versus a concentrated mix from SBI
  and large MSPs in prior years" (D, AR p.4). Graded Moderate rather than Strong because
  this is risk-mitigation, not a growth driver in its own right, and SBI at 20% plus a
  10-year contract term is still a real single-counterparty exposure.

**Family D — Data & Digital**

- **D1 proprietary data asset** — Strong, the single best-evidenced item in this scan. The
  ALGO currency-forecasting engine "uses predictive analytics and machine learning on 16
  years of transactional data across 150,000+ touchpoints to determine the right amount of
  cash to load into each ATM at the right time" (D, AR p.7). The Securens acquisition (₹75cr
  per AR narrative, "~₹80cr" per the Vision AI infographic — figures not fully reconciled in
  corpus) added "10+ years of AI-trained data that CMS would have taken years to accumulate
  independently" (D, AR p.5, p.17). Management's own framing — "where our moat is our
  proprietary incident dataset" (C, AR p.5) — is a claim layered on top of the (D) facts
  above; I score the underlying data asset (D) and treat the "no competitor can replicate
  this in five years or ten" line (AR p.4) as (C), unverifiable from this corpus alone.
  Replicability assessment: plausible in principle (the touchpoint count and multi-year span
  are genuine switching-cost-like barriers to a new entrant), but the corpus contains no
  evidence of what a well-funded unlisted peer (Hitachi Payment Services, FSS pre-acquisition,
  Writer Business Services) has or has not accumulated — this is a real unknown, not a
  disproof. Time to materialise: already in production use.
- **D2 digital platform** — Strong. ALGO MVS runs 68,000/63,000 ATMs (source figures differ
  slightly between AR narrative sections, both around 63-68k) with ~40% ATM-software market
  share, entire SBI network (D, AR p.5, p.53). HAWKAI monitors 50,000+ sites in real time
  across 500,000+ devices, 36%+ BFSI Vision AI market share (D, AR p.19; Inv. Pres. slide
  19). Digital revenue is the Technology & Payments segment, 16% of services revenue FY26 up
  from 7% FY22, targeted >20% by end-FY27 (D, AR p.19, p.52). Lock-in: multi-year contracts
  bundling the software layer with cash logistics. Time to materialise: live and scaling.

  to 1.5% of revenue (D, AR p.7-8). Real and disclosed, but 105 people is a small base

  than Moderate on materiality, not on evidence quality. Time to materialise: ongoing.
- **F2 execution moat** — NO EVIDENCE FOUND as a *moat* (net negative on balance). Cross-
  referencing B05: promise-delivery record over four quarters is 1 delivered / 5 partial / 3
  missed, credibility grade B. Two promises were silently dropped without acknowledgment
  (ATM count target 74,000-75,000; DSO normalisation by end-March 2026), and the SBI RFP
  slipped from February to April to December 2025 before closing. Against this, margin
  guidance was beaten and raised in the same breath as a revenue cut (LBF-3), which is
  genuine execution strength on the cost side. On balance the documented record shows more
  slippage than delivery on capacity/topline commitments specifically (the F2 test), so this
  does not score as an emerging moat; it scores 0 and is flagged as a risk instead (see 6B).

**Family G — Financial & Structural**

- **G1 war chest** — Scored 0 here by design, not by absence of fact. Zero net debt for over
  a decade, ~₹442cr net cash post-buyback, and self-funded growth are real (per company
  memory / Gate 0 Block D 20/20) but this is *existing*, mature balance-sheet quality already
  credited at Gate 0. Crediting it again here would double-count the same quality through two
  mechanisms, which the operating rules forbid. Note for context only: FY26 capex nearly
  tripled and the ₹168cr buyback consumed cash, so the war chest itself is not currently
  *growing* — a further reason not to score it as an emerging item.
- **G2 WC improvement trajectory** — Scored 0; this is a negative, not a moat. DSO rose 116→
  126 days FY25→FY26; WC days rose 81.9→95.3 (per B01, sourced to AR p.99 ratios and
  audited results). Working capital is deteriorating, the opposite of what this category
  tests for.

**Family H — Ecosystem & External**

- **H1 industry consolidation beneficiary** — Moderate, deliberately not Strong (see 6B/AGS
  assessment below for the full reasoning). AGS Transact, a large listed competitor, exited
  the market during FY26 (D, AR p.51: "a large, listed competitor exited... this ultimately
  benefited CMS by validating its integrated model and driving market share gains"). CMS
  gained approximately 200bps of cash-logistics market share (D, AR p.53). IPPB awarded CMS
  a 1,000-unit ATM replacement contract in the wake of the disruption (per company memory,
  not independently re-verified in this corpus). Set against this: the same AR section
  states the near-term impact "was severe," that smaller service providers faced capital
  pressure, that private banks cut ATM sites, and that the outsourced ATM base itself
  *contracted* from 140,000 to 120,000 machines (D, AR p.51) — this directly explains part of
  CMS's own FY26 topline miss. The CEO's own words on the Aug-2026 call: "AGS impact was both
  positive and negative" (D, Concall_Aug_2026). This is a genuinely two-sided, unresolved
  dynamic, not a clean tailwind.
- **H2 strategic partnerships** — NO EVIDENCE FOUND. Will Poole's board appointment (ex-
  Microsoft CVP, Capria Ventures co-founder, effective 10-Aug-2026, D) is a governance
  signal, not a JV, licensing deal, or strategic equity investor relationship, so it does not
  meet this category's bar.
- **H3 ESG moat** — NO EVIDENCE FOUND. Renewables/DG-set reduction and Vision-AI-for-energy-
  monitoring items exist in the AR (D) but are immaterial scale and not linked to any
  customer ESG requirement or rating upgrade that drives business.

  cannot structurally match pay) has zero evidence anywhere in the corpus. Both legs fail;

- **I2 cannibalization barrier (Category 22)** — Scored 0. Applying the test to every moat
  claimed above: for D1 (data asset) and C1 (bank relationships), the honest answer to "what

  channel-exclusivity argument was found that a specific named competitor would have to break
  its own economics to cross. 0 is correct.

## SECTION 6: TIMELINE, RISKS & COMBINED ASSESSMENT

### 6A. Moat evolution timeline

- **Next 12 months (to Aug-2027):** SBI/HDFC/ICICI full-year contribution; FSS integration
  synergy (management targets H2 FY27 start); currency-supply normalisation to pre-shock
  fulfilment; Tech & Payments crossing 20% of services revenue (end Q4 FY27 target); PSU/IBA
  repricing decision (end Q2 FY27 target). Key milestone to watch: whether Q2 FY27 services
  revenue responds in lockstep with any national cash-fulfilment recovery (the falsification
  test already set in company memory Section 8).
- **12-24 months:** FY28 mix shift toward the FY30 revenue-split aspiration; clarity on
  whether the ~200bps AGS-driven share gain proves durable or gets contested by an unlisted
  peer (Hitachi, FSS, Writer) scaling into the vacated share; non-BFSI HAWKAI scale-up
  (EV/QSR/retail) moving from pilot-scale to material revenue.
- **24-36 months:** FY30 aspiration checkpoint — ₹3,750-3,950cr services revenue, Tech &
  Payments at 20-22%, ROCE and EBITDA margin quality maintained through the mix shift.
- **3-5 years:** full platform maturity; if the mix shift is delivered and durable, the
  destination-PE question (Section 1B territory, not this stage) becomes whether the market
  re-rates the earnings mix, not just the earnings level.
