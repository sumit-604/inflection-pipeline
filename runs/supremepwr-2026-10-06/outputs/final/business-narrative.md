# SUPREMEPWR business narrative (phase 1)

Supreme Power Equipment Ltd, NSE SME (Emerge). Run runs/supremepwr-2026-10-06, dated 2026-10-06. This is a phase 1 file. The valuation paragraph is left out by design, because valuation is phase 3 work. Figures are Rs Cr unless stated.

## Business understanding narrative

Supreme Power makes transformers to each buyer's drawing, and goods were 99.6% of FY26 standalone sales. A transformer steps voltage up or down at a substation, a solar or wind farm, or a local pole, and a power network cannot run without one. The company builds power transformers for substations, distribution units for local networks, and inverter duty units for solar and wind sites. The run could not split FY26 revenue by product, because the presentation chart has no readable labels, but the Rs 590 Cr order book at 13 August 2026 was about 77% power, 18% distribution and 5% inverter duty units. Buyers are state utilities such as TANGEDCO, TNPDCL, KSEB and KPTCL, EPC contractors in Karnataka, Telangana and Maharashtra, renewable builders such as Siemens Gamesa, and an unnamed Navratna company that placed a Rs 61 Cr inverter duty order. Each order comes through a tender, and a utility buys only from vendors it has approved, so approval is an entry ticket that does not stop the buyer from choosing another approved vendor next time. Present demand comes from substation building, which the CEA monthly transformation capacity data tracks, and from state work, which the TNPDCL / TNEB distribution transformer replacement tenders and the KPTCL and KSEB substation transformer tenders track. India added 113,013 MVA of transformation capacity in FY26, 90% of target, and the FY27 target is 158,339 MVA. Demand should grow with RDSS distribution spending, now extended to March 2028 and tracked through RDSS sanction and progress, and with transformer sales that CRISIL expects to grow 10 to 11% a year. Peer order books and capacity at Voltamp, Shilchar, Danish Power and TARIL give a quarterly outside check on that demand. The newest and largest order source is a group of unnamed Hyderabad buyers, who placed Rs 128 Cr of the Rs 196 Cr won in Q1 FY27, and no filing names them. The move into 220 kV power transformers depends on one outside event, the CPRI 220 kV / 160 MVA type test certificate, which management expects in December 2026 or January 2027. The Emerging Moat scan scores 8.3, below the line for an emerging moat, and only rare manufacturing capability and state vendor approvals reach Moderate. Talent asymmetry and cannibalisation barrier (Categories 21 and 22) score zero. The distribution line has no moat, because it sells into tenders against a long tail of regional makers and CRISIL says tender competition forces aggressive bids. The power line will have a moat only if the 220 kV test passes, and Shilchar and Danish Power are type testing for the same class in the same window.

## Full order book, slow plant, Danya questions

Supreme Power Equipment makes transformers at Thirumazhisai in Tamil Nadu and, since February 2026, at a second plant at Kannur. It listed on the NSE Emerge platform in December 2023. FY26 consolidated revenue was Rs 182 Cr, up 22%. Profit for shareholders was Rs 20.4 Cr, up only 10%. Over FY23 to FY26, revenue grew 22% a year and profit 24% a year. The order book was Rs 590 Cr on 13 August 2026, 3.25 times FY26 revenue. Government buyers hold 30% of it. Danya Electric, a partnership firm in which the company holds a 90% profit share, carries Rs 88 Cr of that book.

The company wants to climb from distribution units and power transformers up to 25 MVA to 200 MVA, 220 kV power transformers. The plant for that exists. Kannur cost about Rs 95 to 100 Cr and adds 6,000 to 6,500 MVA to Unit 1's 2,500 MVA. It started commercial production on 25 February 2026. The credential does not exist yet. Type tests cover only 25 MVA and 110 kV. The 160 MVA, 220 kV test is planned for December 2026 or January 2027, about half the 1.5 to 2 year norm the CMD himself gave. Orders still lean small. Of Rs 196 Cr won in Q1 FY27, only Rs 37 Cr (19%) is above 25 MVA. Eight of ten orders are 20 MVA units the old plant can build. No filing states how much Kannur produces. The annual report says 45 to 50% use, but its own figures of 1,750 MVA made on 9,000 MVA installed give 19%. The CMD said 20 to 25% for Kannur in August. Q1 revenue growth fits a figure near 10% if Unit 1 was flat. FY26 earned about Rs 10.9 lakh per MVA. Management's Rs 550 Cr peak on 9,000 MVA implies Rs 6.1 lakh, so capacity growth will not turn into revenue growth one for one. On filed evidence, the proof gate has not fired.

Gate 0, the backward quality screen, scores 85 of 160: 70 of 100 on core and 15 of 60 on moat. The class is GOOD, cut one tier from GOOD+ because only four years of data exist. The class is fragile. If plant under construction counts in the fixed asset test, one moat test fails and the class falls to AVERAGE. Return on capital was 28% in FY24, 27% in FY25 and 20% in FY26, while capital employed rose 93%. Q1 FY27 shows the new plant's cost arriving before its revenue. Revenue rose 37.5% to Rs 48 Cr, but profit rose only 9.6% to Rs 4.95 Cr. Employee and other costs went from Rs 2.3 Cr to Rs 6.4 Cr a quarter. Interest went from Rs 0.41 Cr to Rs 1.30 Cr, and depreciation from Rs 0.17 Cr to Rs 0.80 Cr. Depreciation should reach about Rs 1.24 Cr a quarter on the new gross block. Depreciation and interest took about 70% of the Q1 gain in operating profit. Gross margin rose to 28.82% from 23.80% on an analyst's figures. Management had bought oil and copper in March, before peers saw oil prices double. It did not answer the analyst's question on inventory gains. One reading says price clauses now protect margin. The other says Q1 carried a one time gain from cheap stock. Q2 FY27 gross margin separates them.

FY26 operating cash flow was Rs 25.9 Cr against capex of Rs 57.5 Cr. Free cash flow was minus Rs 2.1 Cr in FY25 and minus Rs 31.6 Cr in FY26. Borrowings rose from Rs 18.75 Cr to Rs 49.97 Cr, and the company has no undrawn bank lines. Suppliers and customers funded most of the rest. Trade payables of Rs 62.2 Cr now equal inventory of Rs 62.1 Cr. Payables and customer advances added Rs 45 Cr in the year, 79% of capex. Without that float, operating cash flow was about Rs 2.2 to 3.0 Cr. Standalone inventory doubled while standalone revenue rose 31%. Receivables older than one year rose from Rs 3.4 Cr to Rs 5.9 Cr, with no provision. Debt service cover fell from 10.5 times to 2.4 times on next year repayments. CRISIL upgraded the company to BBB Stable in March 2026. Its note says the business must give long credit and hold large work in progress. It expects receivables to improve as private orders grow, and it names large debt funded capex as a downgrade trigger. One reading says this is a capex peak plus Kannur stock that will turn into sales. The other says the business runs on its suppliers' and customers' money, and that money can go back out. The H1 FY27 balance sheet, due in November 2026, separates them. Capex is not over. A Rs 20 to 22 Cr tank plant on a term loan and a Rs 14 to 16 Cr 220 kV prototype are next, and the CMD says FY28 may need equity. Rs 15.8 Cr of warrant money is due by 27 February 2027.

The concall stage grades management C over three calls: four promises delivered, four partial and three missed. Verifier B checked the table and recounted it at three, three and three. It also added one delivery the table left out. The order book grew from about Rs 311 Cr in February to Rs 588 Cr in May, against the Rs 70 to 160 Cr the CMD's conversion range implied. Every revenue guide landed at or below its floor. FY26 came in at Rs 181.6 Cr against Rs 180 to 200 Cr, and the CMD later said he had first committed Rs 225 Cr. Q1 FY27 came in at Rs 48.2 Cr against Rs 50 to 60 Cr. The FY27 guide fell from more than Rs 300 Cr in February to Rs 275 to 300 Cr in June and Rs 250 to 300 Cr in August. The CMD denied the change on the call. The FY28 guide fell from Rs 400 to 500 Cr in February to about Rs 375 to 400 Cr in June and about Rs 300 to 390 Cr in August. The profit margin band of 10 to 12% held in Q4 FY26 and Q1 FY27, but in August the floor dropped to 9%. The order schedule did not slip the way the concall stage first read it. Rs 412 Cr due by March (June call) and Rs 377 Cr (August call) sit on different start dates, and about Rs 41 Cr of execution in between explains most of the gap. What stands is Rs 377 Cr due against a Rs 250 to 300 Cr guide, which points at execution capacity, not order flow. Two FY27 readings follow. Stated plant potential gives Rs 225 to 275 Cr. Output the plants have already shown gives about Rs 282 to 327 Cr. Q2 FY27 revenue separates them.

The promoter check returns CONCERN, of the structure type, not integrity. The issue is Danya Electric. The company holds a 90% profit share in it, and the MD and the whole time director hold 10%. Danya is rated BB minus. It sells 61% of its turnover to Supreme Power, and Rs 50 Cr of gross trade between the two nets to under Rs 1 Cr. Consolidated profit equals standalone profit, so no unrealised profit on stock moving between them was removed. Supreme Power guarantees Rs 14.70 Cr of Danya debt, 88% of Danya's net worth. Gate 0 scored contingent liabilities at nil, which is right on the consolidated page. The standalone accounts show this guarantee at 13% of standalone net worth. The September 2026 AGM sought authority for Rs 25 Cr of guarantees, Rs 75 Cr under section 185 and Rs 125 Cr of related party trade, against Rs 50 Cr of trade in FY26. It also sought approval "for having given" a guarantee whose board resolution dates from March 2025. The independent audit chair personally guarantees a Rs 4.75 Cr ICICI loan of the company. A non executive director with 47 years in transformer design was paid Rs 15 lakh against a board report that says sitting fees only. He resigned on 13 August 2026. Against this, promoter shares carry no pledge, and MD and WTD pay stayed flat at Rs 60 lakh and Rs 54 lakh for three years. The MD bought Rs 7.6 Cr of warrants, and the audit opinions are clean. Danya's profit slice to the promoters is only about Rs 0.24 Cr a year, so the exposure is the guarantee and the trade loop. The Danya partnership deed and an outside price benchmark would separate a convenience channel for state tenders from a vehicle that moves value.

Shilchar and Danish Power confirm the demand. Both see 3 to 7 years of strong transformer demand and normal CRGO supply. Both sell mainly inverter duty transformers, so they map only in part to Supreme Power's power and distribution units. They contradict three company claims. First, Supreme Power says 80 to 85% of its book carries price variation clauses. Danish puts its own figure near 30%, and Shilchar passed on 50 to 60% of the cost rise on older orders. CRISIL counted escalation clauses on about 50% of a Rs 300 Cr book in March 2026. Second, the CMD says larger units earn 1 to 2 points more margin. Danish says the customer, not the product type, sets margin, and Shilchar expects lower margins on its first 220 kV units. Third, the CMD says few makers build 220 kV units. Shilchar opens a plant for 160 MVA, 220 kV units in April 2027, and Danish has started type tests up to 245 kV. Voltamp and TARIL are also adding high voltage capacity. Shilchar is going from 7,500 to 14,000 MVA, and Danish runs about 11,000 MVA with more planned. Kannur is a small addition inside an industry capacity race. One peer contradiction does not count against the company. Management never claimed 12 to 24 month lead times, and its own lead times match the peers. Both peers also cut their own guides on outside causes.

The market is not the limit. Indian transformer sales run about Rs 31,200 to 36,465 Cr a year, on the Mordor and CRISIL figures. The served market, southern states and units up to 200 MVA and 220 kV, is about Rs 5,383 Cr. The company holds 3.4% of it. The run classes the runway as STRONG, with about 30 times revenue headroom. Capacity and qualification are the limits. The market stage's share path gives Rs 434 Cr of revenue by FY29, which needs about 63% of Kannur's stated potential. It gives Rs 588 Cr by FY31, which needs about 92%. The FY29 path implies 34% a year and sits at the aggressive end. A normal share gain gives Rs 332 Cr and 22% a year. Growth past FY31 needs new land, and management has not bought any. Management's Rs 9.15 lakh Cr headline is a transmission investment programme to 2032, about 29 times the yearly transformer market. Its transformer market figure matches the conservative estimate. CRISIL sees industry operating margin at 8 to 10%, while Supreme Power guides 18 to 20% EBITDA.
