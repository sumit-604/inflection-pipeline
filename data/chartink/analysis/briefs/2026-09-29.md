# Market read, 29 September 2026

This brief comes from the 34 Chartink table files for the 29 September trade
date. All 34 of 34 were present, so every section below has its data. The full
figures sit in the data appendix, `2026-09-29-appendix.html`. Claude Code has no
web access. This brief checks no news and gives no reason for any move. It
describes what the market is doing. It is not advice to buy or sell anything.
The operator decides.

This brief reads Tuesday against the 28 September brief. It was written on
Wednesday, a day late, because the Tuesday run stopped before it saved anything.
Tuesday is the second day of a new week, so the weekly index figures cover only
two sessions. This brief calls them "the week so far" and nothing more. The last
brief named four ways the cautious view would fail. One came true. The VIX closed
back under its thirty-week average. The other three did not. The long-line count
did not recover, the large-cap list did not reach 60, and no thrust day came.
None of the four ways the cautious view would turn worse came true either.

## The picture in one paragraph

Tuesday split the market in two. The fast measures bounced and the slow floor
kept sinking. Big risers beat big fallers 88 to 49, and volume rose above its
year-ago level. The lists of stocks trading above their day's average price
doubled at every size. Yet about six stocks in ten still closed down. The count
of stocks above the two-hundred-day line fell again, to 973, the lowest since 11
June. The market printed 165 new yearly lows against 40 new highs, the most lows
since the March bottom. The microcap index rose about one percent and led every
index. The Nifty slipped again. Fear eased back under its average. This is a
bounce at the short end on better volume, inside a long base that is still
eroding.

## Where the market stands

Size still orders the market, and Tuesday made the gap wider. The microcap index
rose about one percent, the best of 35 indices. It sits about six percent above
its thirty-week trend line. The smallcap 50 sits about three percent above its
own line. The smallcap 250 fell under one percent and sits about one percent
above its line. That cushion is now thin. The midcap 150 fell about one percent
and sits about three percent under its line. The Nifty closed at about 22,716,
down under a third of a percent. It sits about five percent under its thirty-week
line. The tiny companies went up. Everything above them went down.

The story of the last three weeks runs like this. On 7 September, 1,255 stocks
held their two-hundred-day line. On 15 September came the washout, when 343
stocks fell four and a half percent in one session. The long count dropped to
1,036. Buyers lifted it to 1,186 by 23 September. Then it slid. Monday took it to
991. Tuesday took it to 973. So the long line lost ground again on a day when
more stocks rose hard than fell hard. The slow floor did not care about the
bounce.

973 is the lowest reading since 11 June, when the count stood at 906. In the
spring the count sat near this level for weeks, from late April into mid June.
Today about 43 stocks in 100 hold the two-hundred-day line. About 57 sit below
it. A little under half the market held the long floor through most of
September. Now well under half does. The base the market built after the spring
is thinning from underneath.

The short lines tell a different story. About 609 stocks sit above their
twenty-day line, up from 575 on Monday. On washout day that count was 477. About
735 sit above their fifty-day line, up a handful from Monday. On washout day that
count was 706. So the fast lines made a higher low and ticked up. The slow line
made a lower low and ticked down. That split from Monday widened on Tuesday. The
fast lines still say the washout low holds. The slow line says it broke.

The count of new yearly lows is the loudest number tonight. The market printed
165 new yearly lows and only 40 new highs. That is the most lows in one session
since 30 March, the spring bottom. Keep the scale in view. On 30 March the count
was 913. Tuesday is a fraction of that. Still, the lows have now outnumbered the
highs for four sessions in a row, and the gap grew each day. More stocks now sit
within fifteen percent of a yearly low than within fifteen percent of a yearly
high.

Tuesday was a bounce day on better volume. 88 stocks rose four and a half
percent. 49 fell that hard. Turnover rose above its year-ago level for the first
time since 23 September. Light-volume stocks still outnumbered heavy-volume
stocks, but only by about four to three. On Monday it was more than two and a
half to one. That is the best volume mix since 10 August. So buyers came back
with some size behind them. They came back into a narrow set of names. The
advance and decline tile still shows about six stocks in ten closing lower.

The day's action looked like this. About one stock in seven traded four percent
above its prior close during the day. About four in ten of those held the gain
into the close. On Monday fewer than three in ten did. About the same number
traded four percent down during the day. Only three in ten of those closed near
the low, against more than four in ten on Monday. So buying held a little better
and selling stuck a little less. The share of stocks in a tight five-day range
fell to about one in forty, the lowest of the month. The coil that broke on
Monday stayed broken. Stocks are moving, not resting.

Fear eased. The India VIX, the market's fear gauge, fell about two percent to
about 13.4. It now sits about one percent under its thirty-week average. On
Monday it closed just above that average. So fear moved from normal back to a
little under normal. The VIX is still up about ten percent for the week so far.
Gold fell about four percent over the month. The Nifty fell about six percent
over the same month. Both are falling. Gold still falls less.

### For the trader

**1. The bounce is real at the short end.** Big risers beat big fallers 88 to

every size. Buyers came back on Tuesday.

**2. The bounce is not proven.** About six stocks in ten still closed down. The

fast ones. Tuesday lifted only the fast ones.

## What would change this read

The read tonight is cautious with a short-term bounce inside it. The cautious read
fails if the two-hundred-day count climbs back above its washout level of about
1,035 and stays there. It fails if new yearly highs outnumber new lows again for
several sessions, which means lows falling from about 165 back under about 40. It
fails if the large-cap list above the day's average price climbs past about 60 and
holds. It also fails if a thrust day arrives, 300 or more stocks jumping four and a
half percent in one session, on volume above its year-ago level. That would turn
the last two sessions into a shakeout inside a base.

The cautious read turns worse, and the last of the constructive case fails, if the
fifty-day count falls under about 705, its washout-day low. That line is now only
about 30 names away. It fails if the twenty-day count falls under about 475. It
fails if a second washout arrives, 300 or more stocks falling four and a half
percent in one session. It also fails if the count of stocks down twenty percent
in five days climbs past about ten while the VIX rises back above its thirty-week
average of about 13.5. The new-low count is the first place that damage would show,
and it already rose for four sessions running.
