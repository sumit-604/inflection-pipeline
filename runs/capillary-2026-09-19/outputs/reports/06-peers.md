## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

### 2A Demand environment
The peer set is genuinely split, which complicates any single "the sector is booming" or "the sector is struggling" reading of Capillary's own growth. NEWGEN describes a materially cautious FY26: elongated large-deal decision cycles in India and the Middle East, repeatedly pushed-out recoveries quarter to quarter, and management explicitly linking part of the caution to "AI uncertainty" and geopolitical turmoil (Jan/May 2026 calls). INTELLECT describes the opposite: consistent 20%+ LTM growth with management stating "there's nothing right now material of headwinds or material tailwinds" (Manish Maakan, Q3 FY26 call). UNIECOM sits in between: structurally positive on India e-commerce underpenetration, but with real quarter-to-quarter volatility tied to festive seasonality, GST policy changes, and one large top-10 client's business-model exit. None of the three peers describes Capillary's specific customer segment (loyalty/CRM software sold to retail and D2C brands), so this is context, not a direct read on Capillary's own demand — but it shows enterprise/BFSI software demand in India was not uniformly strong across FY26, which is useful as a sanity check against any assumption that Capillary's growth simply rode a rising sector tide.

### 2B Pricing and input costs
Not applicable as a physical input-cost question (all four companies are software businesses). On pricing power specifically: NEWGEN management states AI-driven consumption pricing is "the least amount of headwind" and pricing itself is not under pressure (Rajakumar Vaidyanathan exchange, May 2026 call) — the friction is deal-cycle length, not price. UNIECOM introduced explicit price-escalation clauses in new client contracts and is rolling them out to renewals (multiple calls), directly analogous to Capillary's own inflation-linked contract uplifts (3-8% per 05-concall). No peer reports AI-driven price deflation to date, which is broadly consistent with (does not contradict) Capillary's own margin narrative.

### 2C Capex/investment cycle
This is a genuine three-way split, and Capillary is not a lone expander. NEWGEN is in a cost-optimisation, headcount-shrinking posture (6% YoY headcount reduction, muted hiring) amid demand caution — a "holding" stance. UNIECOM and INTELLECT are both actively ramping investment: UNIECOM explicitly front-loaded sales/marketing/AI/talent spend in H1 FY27 at the cost of near-term EBITDA margin (Kapil Makhija, Apr/Aug 2026 calls), and INTELLECT is mid-way through a ₹130 Cr Purple Fabric investment plus a new ₹20m/year Capex-funded Siruseri campus for AI co-creation (Arun Jain, Nov 2025/May 2026 calls). So this reads as an **industry-wide investment race among the growth-oriented names** (Capillary, UNIECOM, INTELLECT all spending ahead of current revenue on AI/product/M&A), with NEWGEN as the cautious outlier — not a case of Capillary alone racing ahead of a conservative peer set.

### 2D Competitive mentions of Capillary by name
None. No peer transcript mentions Capillary, "loyalty management," "loyalty software," or any unmistakable description of Capillary's business across all 12 calls. This is a clean absence, not evidence either way.

### 2E Risks peers discuss that Capillary's own record does not
- **AI-driven decision-paralysis as a near-term revenue risk** (NEWGEN, multiple calls): large-enterprise buyers explicitly delaying signed-off deals to "wait and see" how AI reshapes their own architecture choices. Capillary's own record states a stable ~9-month deal cycle without naming any AI-driven elongation risk to its own pipeline.
- **Quantified industry-wide AI development-effort compression risk** (INTELLECT, Aug 2026 call): "-60% development effort... major risk for the industry." Capillary's record frames AI purely as a monetisation opportunity (aiRA) with no quantified downside scenario for its own cost base or competitive position.
- **Customer "build it yourself" (vibe-coding) risk, named and rebutted explicitly** (UNIECOM, two calls): a structurally close analogue to the risk a loyalty-software buyer could pose to Capillary (large retail/D2C brands increasingly have in-house engineering capacity), which Capillary's own concalls do not name as a risk category at all.
- **Litigation/contingent-liability disclosure as a governance-adjacent risk** (NEWGEN): an active Qatar Central Bank legal claim (~$1.4m plus compensation) under appeal, volunteered on-call. Not a direct parallel to Capillary's fraud/forensic-audit gap, but shows a peer proactively naming an unresolved legal exposure on the call itself — a contrast point for 05-concall's finding that Capillary under-details its own governance event.
- **Investor pushback on ESOP-driven dilution/free-float creep** (INTELLECT, UNIECOM): repeatedly raised by analysts as a capital-allocation friction. Capillary's own record discloses ESOP expense (Rs 12-15 Cr, 1-2% of revenue) but no analyst has yet raised dilution optics on Capillary's own calls (only 3 quarters of history exist).

## PART 4: TRIANGULATION SUMMARY

- **Claims verified: 1 of 7** (Q3, non-COGS cost growing well below revenue growth — the core margin-expansion mechanism).
- **Claims partially verified: 4 of 7** (Q4 M&A margin-bridge playbook exists elsewhere but at far shallower margin outcome; Q5 FX tailwind direction/order-of-magnitude corroborated by one peer; Q6 AI risk-vs-opportunity direction corroborated but peers are more candid about the risk half than Capillary; Q7 multi-quarter deal-cycle order of magnitude corroborated, exact figures not).
- **Claims unverifiable: 2 of 7** (Q1 loyalty-market-size claim, no peer touches the topic at all; Q2 "top-decile NRR" framing, no peer uses comparable language, and the one peer with a comparable metric frames a lower number modestly).
- **Claims contradicted: 0 of 7.** No peer states the direct opposite of any Capillary claim tested here.
- **The single most consequential near-contradiction**: Q2 (NRR framing). No peer independently supports calling 110%+ NRR "top-decile," and the closest comparable peer (UNIECOM) treats its own lower, unquantified "100%+" retention as ordinary rather than exceptional. This does not disprove Capillary's number but undercuts the self-congratulatory framing around it — worth flagging to synthesis as a "claim exists, benchmark language does not."
- **The single strongest independent confirmation**: Q3 (operating leverage). Two peers in two different sub-verticals (NEWGEN in BPM/ECM, INTELLECT in BFSI platforms) both show cost growth running well below revenue growth by a similar order of magnitude to Capillary's own 12-15-point framing, with AI-assisted productivity cited as a contributing driver in both cases. This is the load-bearing mechanism behind Capillary's margin story, and it is not unique to Capillary — the mechanism is real and observable across Indian listed enterprise software in this period.
- **Overall**: the peer set neither uncritically supports nor undercuts Capillary's narrative; it **complicates it in a specific and useful way**. The mechanical, cost-discipline half of the margin story (Q3) is well corroborated. The two self-comparison claims that lean on market-sizing or benchmark language rather than mechanics (Q1, Q2) find zero independent support anywhere in this corpus. The AI-framing claim (Q6) is corroborated on substance but not on tone: all three peers are markedly more candid about naming AI as a real, sometimes quantified, risk before arguing it out, whereas Capillary's own record leans toward "net positive" without matching that candour — a gap 05-concall itself already flagged and this stage reinforces rather than resolves.

## PART 5: CROSS-PEER HYPOTHESIS

All three peers — NEWGEN (BPM/ECM software), UNIECOM (e-commerce operations SaaS),
and INTELLECT (BFSI platforms) — independently converge on an almost identical
three-part defence when directly challenged on agentic-AI disintermediation risk:
(1) deep trust/governance requirements make AI-built replacements unsafe for
mission-critical, regulated, or high-stakes workflows; (2) switching costs and
ecosystem relationships (built over years) cannot be replicated by an AI agent or
a customer's own engineering team; (3) the maintenance burden of a DIY AI-built
system is understated by whoever proposes it. This script appears verbatim in
spirit across three unrelated sub-sectors, asked by different analysts, in
different quarters, with no evidence any of the three companies coordinated
messaging. The most plausible explanation is not that each management team
independently arrived at the same defence through company-specific analysis, but
that the "will AI replace you" question has become the single most common
challenge posed to Indian listed enterprise-software managements in this period,
and management teams across the sector have converged on the same three-element
answer as the path of least resistance — a sector-wide talking point rather than
a differentiated competitive analysis. This is testable two ways: (a) if further
quarters from any additional Indian enterprise-SaaS name show the same
three-element structure near-verbatim, that confirms convergence; (b) checking
whether Capillary's own "system-of-record vs. system-of-engagement" defence
(05-concall Section 3C, Q3 FY26 call) maps onto the same three elements would
indicate Capillary itself is running the same converged industry script rather
than a Capillary-specific moat argument — which, if true, would mean this
defence carries less differentiating weight than management presents it as
having, since it is now the industry-standard answer rather than a Capillary
insight.
