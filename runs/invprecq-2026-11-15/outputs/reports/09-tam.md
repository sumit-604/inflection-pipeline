## SECTION 1: MARKET DEFINITION

1A Boundaries.
- Product: investment (lost-wax) castings built to customer print, 8 g to 330 kg, 500+ alloys incl. nickel and cobalt superalloys, as-cast or machined (Q2 FY27 deck p.4; AR p.30). Lost-foam is new and sits outside the lost-wax definition (deck p.17).
- Geography: India as the market; exports are 11.82% of revenue (₹22.27 Cr, AR KPI; 2,227.15 lakh Note 23), so Indian production for export is inside the company's revenue but the IMARC India figure's treatment of exports is not stated (NOT FOUND).
- Customers: auto (63.84%), industrials (20.28%), farm (10.02%), A&D (5.86%) on the ₹185.40 Cr KPI base (AR p.22). Medical and energy are named end uses with no revenue split.
- Channel: direct to OEM and Tier 1, built to print. No channel filter applies.
- Price segment: mid-spec ferrous, with a small superalloy and non-ferrous tail. Revenue per tonne ₹11.93 lakh castings (18,579.08 lakh / 1,557 t, derived).
- Excluded: sand, die, centrifugal and lost-foam castings; forgings; machining-only work; the power segment (₹2.66 Cr, 1.41% of revenue).

1B Management's claims.
- AR MD&A (AR p.76): global investment casting market "approximately $ 18–20 billion in 2025–26", CAGR 4.7–5.4% to 2032–34, $25–31 billion by early-to-mid 2030s, A&D 36–46% of global revenue. Source unnamed ("independent industry research").
- H1 FY27 deck (p.16): USD 17.4 bn in 2025, USD 24.9 bn by 2033, about 4.5% a year, A&D the largest use. Source Grand View Research 2026. Check: (24.9/17.4)^(1/8)-1 = 4.6% (derived).
- Two different market bases from one company within six months. The deck figure is the named one; the AR range is unsourced. I carry the deck figure.
- Both claims are GLOBAL. Management gives no India TAM. Credibility read: broad. A global total presented under "demand is growing in the markets our approvals already cover" (deck p.16) says nothing about the portion a Bhavnagar plant can reach.
- AR p.76 also cites India defence exports ₹159 bn FY23 to ₹538 bn FY30, "~17%" CAGR. Own arithmetic: (538/159)^(1/7)-1 = 19.0% (derived). Source unnamed; FY23 base is stale (3+ years). It is a defence export figure, not a casting figure.

Method 1, top-down (India investment casting estimates):
- IMARC: USD 579.1 M in 2025, USD 745.7 M by 2034, CAGR 2.76% 2026-2034. Page "Last Updated July 24, 2026". Segments by process (sodium silicate, silica sol), material (superalloys, steel, aluminium, titanium, others) and application (automotive, aerospace and military, oil and gas, energy, medical, others). No segment shares given on the page. 579.1 x 88.4 = ₹5,119 Cr (derived).
- Market Research Future (MRFR): "roughly USD 1.5 bn in 2024, CAGR about 4.753% 2025-2035" in the overview. IMARC investment casting = 2.6% of that, MRFR = 6.8% (both derived). I found no IIF investment casting share to decide between them. This check does not separate the two.

3A SAM. The five filters could not be quantified from sources:

- Geography: India. No subtraction (exports inside the company's revenue; IMARC treatment NOT FOUND).
- Channel: built to print, direct. No subtraction.
- Customer: IMARC application list includes oil and gas, not a named I&PCL line. Application shares: NOT FOUND (IMARC gives none). MRFR's FAQ gives automotive 0.64 of 1.61 bn (40%) and aerospace 0.25 (16%) but the same page contradicts itself, so I do not use them as filters.
- Capability: weight range, tooling cost and size limits: NOT FOUND.

SAM is therefore carried as the TAM figure, ₹5,119 Cr, labelled a CEILING. TAM > SAM is not shown here. Consequences: headroom and years-to-saturate below are overstated by an unknown amount; SOM share is measured against a denominator that is too large, so the share is understated. The SAM share gap closes only when IMARC's material and application splits are bought or found.

3B SOM. Market grown at IMARC's 2.76% from the 2025 base, with 2025 treated as FY26 (one-year timing mismatch, NOT FOUND whether IMARC's 2025 is calendar or fiscal).
- TAM at 3 years: 5,119 x 1.0276^3 = 5,119 x 1.0851 = ₹5,555 Cr. At 5 years: 5,119 x 1.0276^5 = 5,119 x 1.1459 = ₹5,866 Cr.
- Current share: 185.79 / 5,119 = 3.63%.
- Share-gain rule: 1-2 pp in 3 years normal; 3-5 pp aggressive only with capacity and execution.
- Evidence on pace: H1 FY27 sales annualised = 11,128.77 x 2 = 22,257.5 lakh = ₹222.6 Cr; 222.6 / 5,119 = 4.35%, a gain of 0.72 pp in about one year (derived; H1 doubling ignores seasonality; the year gap uses FY26 share). That pace sits at the top of the normal band (2 pp / 3 years = 0.67 pp a year). So I carry the top of normal, 2 pp in 3 years, as the central path. This is a reading of evidence, not a haircut.

Companywide growth versus market: the company grew 14.0% (castings, FY26) against an India market CAGR of 2.76% (IMARC) and a global 4.5% (Grand View). I&PCL is gaining share, not riding the market. IMARC's 2.76% sits well under the company's two global figures and the MRFR 4.75%-6%. The market growth input to stage 11 is a low one; the SOM growth comes from share, not from the market.

3C Capacity cross-check.
- Installed: 3,060 t (AR p.30; deck p.4 "net castings capacity a year"). The same AR page also prints "annual casting capacity of 1,500 metric tonnes" for the five melting technologies. Test: FY26 dispatch 1,557 t is 50.9% of 3,060 t (derived), matching the deck's "51%" FY26 utilisation. Against 1,500 t it would be 103.8%, inconsistent with the deck's 60% H1 FY27 figure. So 3,060 t is the working base. This narrows B04's "undecidable" gap; the 1,500 t print stays unexplained (likely a melting subset).
- Nameplate revenue at FY26 mix: 3,060 t x ₹11.93 lakh = 36,514 lakh = ₹365.1 Cr (derived).
- Central 3y SOM ₹312.7 Cr needs 31,270 / 11.93 = 2,621 t = 85.6% of nameplate. Sufficient, with 14.4% headroom.
- Central 5y SOM ₹408.4 Cr needs 3,422 t = 111.8% of nameplate. Gap ₹43.3 Cr (408.4 - 365.1) at constant revenue per tonne.
- Aggressive 3y ₹423.8 Cr: gap ₹58.7 Cr.
- Capex: B07 capex-embedded growth is 1.9% (CWIP 383.89 lakh x 0.926 turnover / 18,844.62 lakh), about ₹3.6 Cr of revenue. The unfunded ₹100 Cr approval would add up to 49.1%, or about ₹92.5 Cr, as a ceiling only (B07; funding and timeline NOT FOUND). 92.5 Cr is more than the 43.3 Cr gap, but it is not committed.
- Which side is optimistic: for years 1 to 3, neither. For year 5, the SOM is the optimistic side against committed capacity (1.9%), because the gap is covered only by a plan with no filed funding or timeline, or by revenue per tonne rising 11.8% on mix (408.4 / 365.1 = 1.118). Higher-value vacuum-melted work is the stated route (deck p.17 "Fill the plants" and "Shift the mix") but no filed price or mix split exists. Note that a 100% fill is not a practical ceiling; the 111.8% need is a nameplate figure.

5B Runway.
