## Business Understanding Narrative

**What does it do, and how does it make money?** Hemant Surgical makes,
imports, assembles and sells medical equipment and disposables. Its own words:
the portfolio covers renal care, cardiovascular disease, respiratory disease,
critical care and radiology, and surgical disposables. Some products are made
in India, others imported from Japan, China, France and Australia and processed
in its assembly units. It also runs and maintains dialysis centres (Annual
Report FY2024-25, page 93).

Dialysis is the core. The product list runs from the SWS 4000A dialysis
machine and refurbished machines through dialysers, bloodline tubing,
bicarbonate mixture, RO plants, dialysis beds and chairs, to heparin and iron
sucrose injection (Annual Report FY2024-25, page 24). The company began in the
1980s importing meditapes from JMS Co Ltd of Japan and started manufacturing in
1999 at Achad Industrial Estate.

The money comes from a spread on imported and assembled kit, plus an annuity
from running dialysis centres.

**What is changing?** The top line doubled. Standalone revenue from operations
went from Rs 106.53 crore in FY25 to Rs 230.99 crore in FY26. Profit before tax
went from Rs 10.56 crore to Rs 23.63 crore (Financial Results 15-May-2026,
pages 6 and 7). The company also bought 66.66% of Lifesenz Cancer Research
Labs for Rs 19.98 crore in June 2026, entering precision oncology (Press
Release 09-Jun-2026, page 1).

**Why now?** That is the question this card cannot answer well. The doubling
is concentrated in one half-year: revenue of Rs 167.19 crore in the six months
to March 2026 against Rs 64.62 crore in the six months to September 2025 and
Rs 57.77 crore in the six months to March 2025 (Financial Results, page 13).
No investor presentation and no earnings call exists to explain it.

**What must be true for the thesis to work?** The revenue must turn into cash.
It did not. This is the finding of the card and step 5 sets it out.

**What breaks it?** It may already be broken. Operating cash flow was negative
Rs 32.02 crore in FY26 against positive Rs 4.59 crore in FY25, while profit
before tax more than doubled (Financial Results, page 7).

## 2. Business model and archetype

Import, assemble, distribute and service. Three manufacturing and assembly
units, the first at Achad Industrial Estate from 1999, making dialysis solution
in liquid and powder form and sterilants (Annual Report FY2024-25, page 93).
Product groups are JMS products, healthcare and surgical, dialysis, criticare,
radiology and ophthalmology (Annual Report FY2024-25, page 22).

**Archetype: distributor with a light manufacturing tail.** Supplier
relationships, import terms, working capital and service annuity are the
variables. It is not a spec'd component maker and it has no evident design win.

## 3. Competitive advantages

Weak. The long relationship with JMS of Japan is an asset and the dialysis
centre operations create switching cost for those centres. Neither appears
quantified anywhere in the corpus. Nothing in the documents shows pricing
power, a patent, a regulatory barrier or a cost position. Moat: absent.

## 6. The transition and the quality ladder

No transition is claimed by management anywhere in this corpus. There is no
presentation, no call and no strategy statement.

What the documents show is a diversification, not a climb. In June 2026 the
company acquired 66.66% of Lifesenz Cancer Research Labs for Rs 19.98 crore.
Lifesenz does ex-vivo chemo-sensitivity analysis using patient-derived 3D
cancer cell models. The company's own press release says Lifesenz "is currently
in a growth and development phase... and will take some time to break even",
and that the board evaluated it "through a long-term strategic and objective
lens... rather than near-term profitability metrics" (Press Release, page 3).

FROM R1 or R2, an importer-distributor. TO nothing stated. The ladder does not
apply because no rung is claimed. Record that as the finding: **fits no rung is
a finding, not a gap to paper over.**

## 8. Proof check

There are no quarters. The company reports half-yearly and the latest period
ends 31 March 2026.

What the last two half-years show is a step change in revenue with no
explanation on the record and no cash behind it. H2 FY26 revenue of Rs 166.72
crore was 2.9 times H2 FY25 and 2.6 times H1 FY26. A distributor does not
nearly triple a half-year organically without saying why.

**Name the absence:** no document held tells the reader what drove the second
half of FY26. That is the single missing fact on which everything else turns.

## 12. Verdict card

**PASS.** Do not spend a `/step1` on this name.

The reasoning is one paragraph. Revenue doubled and operating cash went
negative by Rs 32.02 crore. The gap sits in receivables, inventory and
advances, and was funded with short-term debt. The audited cash flow statement
that discloses this does not internally reconcile. There is no presentation, no
earnings call and no credit rating through which management explains any of it.
Into that, the company spent Rs 19.98 crore on a pre-breakeven cancer
laboratory. The framework does not halt a run on company quality, and this is
not a halt. It is a judgment that the evidence needed to build a thesis does not
exist and the evidence that does exist points the wrong way.

**Load-bearing facts that would have to be verified before this is reopened**
1. The cash flow statement. Obtain the audited annual report and reconcile it.
   If it still does not add, that is the whole answer.
2. What drove H2 FY26 revenue of Rs 166.72 crore. Customer, order, product.
3. Receivable ageing and the identity of the largest debtors.
4. The Lifesenz counterparty, the valuation basis for Rs 19.98 crore, and
   whether any promoter or relative held an interest in it.
5. Promoter holding and pledge.

**What would change the view**
- An audited annual report whose cash flow statement reconciles, showing
  positive operating cash flow in FY27.
- A named, verifiable reason for the H2 FY26 step change.
- The company starting to hold earnings calls.

No Role 1 valuation was run and no target price is set. This verdict decides only whether the
name earns a full `/step1`, and it is not a position.
