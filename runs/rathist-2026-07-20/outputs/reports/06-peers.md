## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

**2A. Demand environment.** Both peers frame demand as structurally supportive and infra/construction-led: Scan Steels' presentation (p.34) cites India's low per-capita steel consumption (~100kg vs. world ~220kg+), ~8-9% annual finished-steel demand growth, and the National Steel Policy's 300 MT FY30 capacity target as the backdrop for its own volume-led capacity build. VRAJ's CARE rationale (p.1) expects "sales volumes... to remain healthy in the near-to-medium-term considering steady demand from end-user industries." This is broadly consistent with Rathi's framing at the level of "demand is there," but neither peer corroborates the specific tariff/FTA/budget triggers Rathi names, and VRAJ's own experience shows demand (volume) staying healthy while *realizations* (price) were weak through FY25 and 9M FY26 — a distinction Rathi's own management narrative does not draw out clearly.

**2B. Pricing and input costs.** This is the most informative cross-read finding. VRAJ explicitly attributes margin compression to price, not volume: "PBILDT margin moderated to 13.20% in FY25 from 18.33% in FY24, largely due to a decline of ~10% in average selling prices across all product categories" (p.2), with 9M FY26 PBDIT margin further down to 9.11% "primarily attributable to a decline in realisations across key products" (p.3). This lends some external, independent support to Rathi's own excuse narrative that industry-wide pricing/oversupply pressure — not execution alone — is compressing margins. However, the magnitude gap is stark: even after two years of compression, VRAJ's margin (13.20% then 9.11% PBDIT on 9M FY26 basis) remains more than double to triple Rathi's flat ~4% EBITDA margin held across three consecutive quarters. This suggests industry pricing pressure is real but does not, by itself, explain the scale of Rathi's margin problem — a company-specific gap remains after accounting for the sector-wide price effect peers corroborate.

**2C. Capex cycle.** This is an industry-wide capacity race, not a lone-expander situation, and Rathi is the comparative laggard within it. VRAJ funded a ~Rs 165 crore sponge iron/MS billet/WHRB expansion plus a further ~Rs 49 crore, 15 MWp solar plant via a ~Rs 171 crore FY25 IPO (CARE rationale, p.2, p.4). Scan Steels is pursuing an ~Rs 850 crore multi-year greenfield pellet/DRI/coal-washery/50 MW power buildout (p.6, p.21, p.31), funded 59% ("Rs 500 Cr") by a fresh equity raise "priced at a premium to CMP" and targeting ~4.9x revenue growth (Rs 838cr to Rs 4,133cr) and ~11.7x EBITDA growth (Rs 49cr to Rs 575cr) by FY31 (p.19, p.25, p.31). Against this, Rathi's disclosed capex is an order of magnitude smaller (~Rs 40 crore trailing three years; ~Rs 20 crore+ guided for FY26 — B05 guidance table) and internally funded, and its flagship TMT direct-charging project is already delayed past its own stated completion window. The peer set is scaling aggressively on accessible equity capital; Rathi is not currently able or choosing to do the same.

**2D. Competitive mentions.** No peer mentions Rathi Steel & Power by name or by unmistakable description in either document.

**2E. Risks peers discuss that Rathi does not.** VRAJ discloses geographic concentration risk (majority of customers within Chhattisgarh — p.3), moderate customer concentration (top five customers ~32% of FY25 revenue, down from 37% FY24 — p.3), and moderate group exposure via equity investment and a corporate guarantee extended to a related entity, accounting for ~15% of tangible net worth (p.3). Scan Steels discloses recurring mine and transporter strikes across the Odisha ore/coal belt disrupting raw-material feed, requiring a ~45-day RM buffer stock (p.29), and monsoon-driven (Jun-Sep) raw-material moisture/supply risk requiring pre-stocking ahead of the rains (p.29), plus execution/dilution risk embedded in its Rs 500 crore equity-funded expansion plan (p.31). None of these risk categories — customer/geographic concentration, related-party guarantee exposure, physical supply-chain disruption (strikes, monsoon), or capital-raise dilution risk — appear in Rathi's own B05 disclosure profile, which is dominated instead by execution-credibility issues (unreconciled utilization, flat margin, evasive answers). This is a genuine disclosure gap worth carrying into synthesis as a missing-risks item.

**Single most consequential contradiction:** Q4. **Single strongest independent confirmation:** None of the six claims reaches a clean, unambiguous VERIFIED status with this two-document peer set. The closest to genuine (partial) corroboration is the pricing/margin-pressure finding under 2B: VRAJ's independently-reported ~10% ASP decline and consequent margin compression (18.33%→13.20%→9.11% PBDIT across FY24/FY25/9M FY26) is real, dated, third-party-verified evidence that sector-wide pricing pressure is a genuine phenomenon, not solely a Rathi excuse — though the magnitude gap versus Rathi's flat ~4% margin means it only partially exonerates the company-specific execution question.

**Overall:** The peer set complicates rather than cleanly supports or cleanly undercuts Rathi's narrative. On the positive side, both peers corroborate a genuine infra-led demand tailwind in the broader secondary-steel segment and one peer (VRAJ) independently confirms sector-wide realization/pricing pressure consistent in direction with Rathi's margin-compression excuse. On the negative side, the peer with the most detailed disclosure (Scan Steels) directly contradicts the exclusivity of Rathi's signature hot-charging claim and both peers show cleaner, more consistent utilization disclosure than Rathi provides, and both are executing multi-hundred-crore, equity-funded expansion programs that dwarf Rathi's own capex scale and pace. Three of the six specific claims (stainless-steel oversupply, RM cost ratio, green-steel institutional premium) simply cannot be tested against this peer set because neither document covers the right sub-segment or metric — a coverage gap, not a finding, but one that should be weighted honestly: absence of evidence here is not evidence of absence for those three claims.

## PART 5: CROSS-PEER HYPOTHESIS

**Hypothesis:** The two peers, despite operating in different states (Chhattisgarh vs. Odisha) and being covered by different document types, share a specific and testable pattern that neither discloses explicitly: margin expansion in this secondary/EAF-IF steel sub-segment is being pursued through *equity-funded backward-and-forward integration and captive-power scale*, not through any single proprietary process technology. VRAJ funded its integration (sponge iron→billet→TMT, plus WHRB and solar captive power) through a fresh IPO; Scan Steels is funding its integration (pellet→DRI→billet→TMT/pipe, plus 50 MW captive power) 59% through a fresh equity raise priced at a premium to CMP. Both frame "backward integration" and "captive/renewable power" — generic, replicable levers available to any well-capitalized secondary steelmaker — as their primary margin bridge, and both have already implemented, or are already implementing, hot charging as one small piece of that generic playbook rather than as a standout differentiator.

This suggests the sector may be bifurcating along **capital-market access**, not technology: producers who can tap equity markets (VRAJ, Scan Steels) are scaling integration and power self-sufficiency by an order of magnitude faster than producers who cannot or do not (Rathi, funding a comparatively tiny capex program from internal accruals and bank debt at ~16% cost of debt). If this bifurcation is real, it would predict that Rathi's reliance on claimed process-level differentiation ("India's first and only" hot-charging) is less a genuine moat and more a narrative substitute for the scale and capital-market access its better-funded peers already have — worth testing directly in a future run by tracking whether Rathi ever accesses equity capital markets for its own integration program, and whether its process claims survive scrutiny as peers scale the same generic playbook.
