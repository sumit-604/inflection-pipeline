## BUSINESS UNDERSTANDING NARRATIVE

Titan Biotech ferments and processes animal and plant proteins into biological raw
materials. The products are peptones, yeast and biological extracts, agar, prepared
culture media sold under the TM Media brand, collagen peptides, ox bile extract,
animal nutrition ingredients and plant growth promoters. A peptone is the digested
protein that feeds a fermentation batch or a diagnostic culture plate, and a
customer who has qualified one supplier's grade carries the cost of revalidating
everything built on top of it if the grade changes. The company reports one segment
and publishes no product wise split in three years of annual reports, so the only
revenue division the filings carry is geographic: 61 percent domestic and 39 percent
export in FY26. Customers are named only as classes, never as companies: pharma,
biotech fermentation, nutraceutical, food and beverage, diagnostics, cosmetics,
veterinary and agriculture. No top five or top ten concentration figure exists in any
filing, and the company names customer concentration as a risk without attaching a
number to it. On switching costs the filings are silent, so the only evidence the run
holds is from the peer set, where Vidhi Specialty describes a four to ten year
customer approval cycle for a comparable regulated ingredient and Advanced Enzyme
ties its own registration timelines to the same pattern. Present demand sits with
those eight end market classes and is checkable only from outside the company,
through the downstream signals the market stage named: HiMedia Laboratories revenue
disclosures for the culture media segment, peer quarterly results from Advanced
Enzyme, Fermenta, Vidhi and Nitta Gelatin, and India export trade data by HS code
from DGCIS for peptones, culture media and collagen. The forward drivers are a
market the run sized at Rs 5,040 cr to Rs 6,660 cr growing about 9 percent a year, of
which Titan holds 4.62 percent of the addressable slice, and the externally
verifiable signals for those drivers are the DGCIS monthly export series, US tariff
and HS code notifications from USTR and the Ministry of Commerce, and Department of
Biotechnology and BIRAC policy announcements that test the company's own government
support claim. Competitive advantage cannot be located per line, because the
company publishes no line level revenue at all. On the whole company view the
emerging moat scan scored 2.0 out of 92 and classified the forward signal NONE, with
one live category, export growth, which the company's own filings never explain
causally. Three categories carry documented adverse evidence rather than absent
evidence: the capacity claim contradicted by its own notes, the repeated capex
framing, and the working capital series that does not improve. Categories 21 and 22
were scored and both returned zero. The backward moat block scored 14 out of 60,
with two moat tests confirmed out of twelve, and the two clean adverse findings are a
gross margin 12.7 percentage points below the peer median and a deceleration from a
21.92 percent three year revenue growth rate to 11.65 percent. Stated plainly: no
business line shows an evidenced moat in the filed record, and the culture media
line competes with HiMedia Laboratories, an unlisted peer roughly five times Titan's
size.

## Revenue up 32 percent, cause not disclosed

Titan Biotech makes fermentation and extraction derived biological ingredients at
four plants in Rajasthan, one at Bhiwadi RIICO Phase III, two at Chopanki and one at
Kaharani. FY26 revenue was Rs 206 cr and profit Rs 30 cr on a consolidated basis. The
business is a converter: cost of materials consumed was 48.1 percent of revenue in
FY26 against 48.3 percent in FY25, flat while revenue grew 31.8 percent. Pricing
power reads weak, and raw material pass through is the company's own named top risk
in every one of the three annual reports.

The transition question is whether the FY26 and FY27 acceleration is a climb into
higher value work or a delayed restock after the FY25 trough. FY25 revenue fell 5.7
percent and the operating margin fell to 16 percent from 21 percent. FY26 reported
revenue growth was 31.8 percent, which is 28.1 percent like for like once the freight
gross up is removed, a presentation disclosed only in the exchange results filing
and absent from every annual report note. Exports rose 49.0 percent to Rs 80.33 cr,
39 percent of revenue, against domestic growth of 22.7 percent. Q1 FY27 revenue rose
27.2 percent year on year to Rs 59.2 cr at a 21 percent operating margin. No filing
names a customer, a country, a certification or a product behind any of it.

Gate 0 scores the backward record GOOD. Core 79 out of 100, moat 14 out of 60, grand
total 93 out of 160, with no deal breakers. Blocks run A 16, B 15, C 17, D 19, E 12
out of 20 each. That score is a correction: a payable days calculation had converted
revenue from lakh to crore at 10 times instead of 100 times, which turned a 3.18 day
working capital change into an apparent release. Corrected, the class falls from GOOD
plus to GOOD. Working capital days ran 130.85, 135.39, 150.05 and 127.67 across FY23
to FY26, high in every year and heaviest in FY25, the year revenue fell.

The balance sheet is the clearest part of the record. Borrowings of Rs 5.7 cr
including leases sit against reserves of Rs 173 cr and net worth of Rs 182 cr, debt
to equity 0.03, current ratio 3.28 times, interest cover 42.9 times. Operating cash
flow exceeded profit in both FY26 and FY25. Free cash flow grew from Rs 18.11 cr in
FY23 to Rs 22.99 cr in FY26 through a FY24 dip to Rs 1.67 cr on a capex spike. The
audit opinion is unmodified with zero key audit matters and no adverse CARO remark
for three years running. Against that: the cash conversion cycle ran 220 days in
FY26, finished goods inventory rose 53.2 percent against revenue up 31.8 percent, the
company booked its first ever expected credit loss provision of Rs 36.26 lakh after
nil in FY25 and FY24, receivables more than six months past due rose from 7.6 percent
to 8.4 percent of the gross book, MSME payables rose 291 percent and customer
advances rose 149 percent.

Related party buying is the largest single finding. Audited Note 41(a) of the FY26
annual report itemises related party purchases of Rs 3,877.14 lakh against cost of
materials consumed of Rs 9,916.35 lakh, which is 39.1 percent (Peptech 44.79,
Phoenix Bio Sciences 2,574.32, Stalwart 986.20, Titan Animal 78.30). That audited
figure replaces the Rs 3,683.61 lakh and 37.1 percent the evidence stages carried,
and it is used everywhere in this run. Phoenix Bio Sciences alone is 26.0 percent of
the raw material bill and grew 73.6 percent in the year. Two serving Executive
Directors, Raja Singla and Shivom Singla, sit on the Phoenix board. The Independent
Chairman and Audit Committee Chairman, Rohit Jain, sits on the board of Peptech
Biosciences, an associate his own committee reviews. No arm's length pricing
benchmark for any of it is disclosed in three years of filings. The other side of the
promoter file is clean: pledge zero throughout, no SEBI, criminal, tax or other
regulatory action found, the independent board expanded from four members to five
with the addition of a senior legal professional rather than a replacement, and CFO
and Company Secretary continuity held across all three years. The one individual
promoter who exited in FY26 sold on the open market before the 2026 rally, not into
it.

Management credibility grades C, and the mode explains why. Titan Biotech holds no
earnings calls. There is no management voice in the corpus beyond written annual
report prose. That prose failed both of its substantive tests. The health supplement
development claim runs verbatim through the FY24, FY25 and FY26 Directors' Reports
with no product name, launch date or revenue in any year. The capacity building
sentence for the investing outflow repeats near verbatim across the same three years
while gross plant additions fell from Rs 1,948.49 lakh to Rs 936.88 lakh to Rs 740.08
lakh, and 77 percent of the FY26 outflow, Rs 25.16 cr of Rs 32.55 cr audited, went
into a quoted debt portfolio whose own return fell from 7.04 percent to 3.64 percent.
Mechanical promises were kept: two dividends paid and the 1:5 share split completed
on 20-Feb-2026. Three separate disclosure problems sit alongside: a Rs 186.90 lakh
gap between the MD&A investing figure and the audited cash flow statement in the same
document, a Rs 243.87 lakh reclassification of FY25 balances between the two annual
reports, and a restatement of FY25 return on equity and return on capital across the
same two documents although FY25 profit, equity and debt are identical in both.

The eleven peer transcripts are the only current management voice on this industry
in the corpus, and they push back twice. Advanced Enzyme and Vidhi Specialty
contradict an industry wide demand re-acceleration: Advanced Enzyme called Q1 FY27 "a
softer, more muted start to the fiscal year" on 12-Aug-2026 and its growth swung 26,
2, 22 and 2 percent year on year across four quarters, while Vidhi named "a
noticeable slowdown in demand across several international markets" on 14-May-2026
with FY26 revenue flat to down. They also contradict the idea that Titan's silence on
capacity is normal for the sector. Advanced Enzyme states utilisation of 70 to 75
percent and ties its next capex decision to it. Vidhi states Rs 75 cr to 85 cr of
capex, an 18 month commissioning period and Rs 125 cr to 150 cr of expected revenue
from it. Every peer discloses a utilisation percentage; Titan discloses none, for any
of its four plants, in any of three years. Peers also quantify US tariff exposure,
Advanced Enzyme at Rs 10 cr to 11 cr of EBITDA and Vidhi at 10 to 20 percent of
quarterly revenue, while Titan names no tariff exposure against a Rs 80 cr export
book. What that does to confidence is direct: with no calls, no analyst questions and
no management voice, every forward claim here rests on annual report prose that the
deep read judged promotional and unevidenced, and there is no mechanism by which a
shareholder can ask.

The market is large enough not to be the constraint. The addressable market sizes at
Rs 5,040 cr conservative and Rs 6,660 cr realistic, growing about 9 percent a year,
with the serviceable slice at Rs 4,339 cr. Titan holds 4.62 percent of that slice, so
revenue headroom is 21.65 times and the runway class is GOOD. The obtainable share
work implies Rs 344 cr of revenue at year three and Rs 476 cr at year five, an 18.9
to 19.7 percent compound growth rate that the run states as a mechanical output, not
an endorsement. Two things cut against it. Management's own market claims in the
MD&A sit 12.5 times above the conservative addressable figure, read as inflated.
And the year five path needs roughly Rs 85 cr of cumulative plant investment against
a FY26 run rate of Rs 7.4 cr a year that has fallen for three years.

The pairing at the centre of this run is a decent backward record next to no
evidenced forward climb. Gate 0 says GOOD on ten years of delivered numbers. The
emerging moat scan says 2.0 out of 92 and NONE on the forward signal, with its single
live category unexplained by the company itself. The combined assessment is GOOD.
That is a business that has earned its record and has not yet shown, in anything it
has filed, the mechanism by which it climbs.
