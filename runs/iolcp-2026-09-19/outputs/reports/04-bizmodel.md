## SECTION 1: THE BUSINESS MODEL IN PLAIN ENGLISH

### 1A. One-line description
IOL makes bulk drug ingredients (APIs, led by ibuprofen) and industrial acetyl
chemicals at one integrated site in Barnala, Punjab, using its own chemicals plant
to feed its own API lines and selling the surplus of both to outside customers
worldwide (AR p.6, "About us").

### 1B. Money flow chain, each revenue stream

**Pharma APIs — Ibuprofen (world's largest single product line).**
[Own-made intermediates: Iso Butyl Benzene, Acetyl Chloride, Mono Chloro Acetic
Acid] → [company reacts these at its dedicated Ibuprofen units, Barnala] →
[delivers bulk Ibuprofen powder in drums to generic-drug formulators in 80+
countries] → [formulators pay] → [on trade credit, company-wide debtors turnover
4.14x FY26 i.e. ~88 days, AR Note 49 p.177-178]. Capacity 12,000 MTPA at ~95%
utilisation (Reg 30 announcement 09-Sep-2026, Annexure-1); world's largest

rationale, Jul-2025, p.2).

**Pharma APIs — non-Ibuprofen basket (Paracetamol, Metformin, Clopidogrel,
Pantoprazole, Fenofibrate, Levetiracetam, Lamotrigine, Minoxidil, Losartan).**
[Backward-integrated intermediates + purchased inputs, mostly domestic per CARE
p.2] → [reacted at 11 dedicated/multi-product units] → [delivered globally to
formulators across the same therapeutic-category customer base] → [customer pays

### 1E. The chai-stall-uncle version
IOL first cooks its own base sauces, the acetic-acid family of chemicals, then
uses most of that sauce itself to cook the main dish, bulk medicine powder like
ibuprofen, which it sells to drug companies around the world. It also sells
extra sauce to outside customers such as paint and ink makers. Because it makes
its own sauce, its dish costs less than a rival who buys sauce from someone
else. But its two biggest items, ibuprofen and one type of sauce (ethyl
acetate), sell at world prices it cannot set itself, so profit rises and falls
with global chemical prices more than with its own selling skill. To fix that,
it is learning to cook more, less crowded dishes (paracetamol, clopidogrel,
pantoprazole and friends), and has just agreed to rent out part of its kitchen
to one foreign chemical company and to cook ready-made tablets on contract for
European customers.

### 2D. Industry lifecycle stage

Ibuprofen and Ethyl Acetate sit in a **mature, cyclical commodity phase**: both
are long-established molecules competing on global cost curves (AR SCOT
"Threats": "Volatility in crude-linked chemical raw materials", p.50). The
Indian API industry overall is in a **structural growth phase**: AR MD&A cites
the Indian API market at roughly USD 15 billion in 2026, China-plus-one
sourcing shifts, and PLI-scheme investment exceeding Rs 6,900 Cr commitment
(AR p.46-47). IOL's non-Ibuprofen diversification and the new CDMO/tolling
lines are positioned inside that structural growth phase, but the CDMO and
tolling businesses themselves are **pre-commercial / early-stage** (Reg 30,
09-Sep-2026), with zero track record yet.

### 3D. Unit economics — the physics of the business

**Define one unit:** 1 metric tonne (MT) of API or specialty chemical produced
and sold.

**Revenue per unit:** **NOT FOUND** as a disclosed blended average selling
price. The AR and Investor Presentation report aggregate Rs Cr revenue against
aggregate installed capacity in MTPA (30,726 MTPA API, 1,82,400 MTPA Specialty
Chemicals; Inv. Pres. Q1FY27 slide 13), not a per-tonne realisation. A
company-wide "revenue per tonne" figure would average across a dozen molecules
at very different price points (Ibuprofen vs Paracetamol vs Ethyl Acetate) and
would not be a meaningful single number. Product-level realisation (Rs/kg for
Ibuprofen, Ethyl Acetate, etc.) is **NOT FOUND IN THIS RUN'S AR/INVESTOR
PRESENTATION**; it would come from concall Q&A or external pricing services
outside this stage's document set.

**Margin per unit:** **NOT FOUND** for the same reason (no per-product cost
disclosure).

**Volume drivers:** Capacity commissioning and ramp (Paracetamol Unit-11,
10,800 MTPA, still ramping at 65% utilisation; Ibuprofen 12,000 to 18,000 MTPA
by Dec-2027) and utilisation improvement on existing lines.

**Price drivers:** Global cost-curve pricing for Ibuprofen and Ethyl Acetate
(company is a price-taker on these); product-mix shift toward higher-realisation
non-Ibuprofen APIs lifts the blended average even without any single price
moving.

**Cost drivers:** Raw materials 64.75% of FY26 revenue (Note 27: Acetic Acid
Rs 403.86 Cr, Specially Denatured Spirit Rs 376.84 Cr, plus Toluene, Propylene,
Aluminium Chloride and others), majority China-sourced for the chemicals
segment (CARE rationale p.2); Power & Fuel 6.7% (Note 31); Employee cost 10.7%
(Note 29).

**Incremental margin / operating leverage:** FY26 revenue grew 11.5% while
EBITDA grew 29.3% (Directors' Report p.53), consistent with a largely
fixed-cost, continuous-process plant where utilisation gains drop through
disproportionately to profit. The Chemical segment shows this most sharply:
segment result rose 96.5% (Rs 15.39 Cr to Rs 30.24 Cr) on total chemical
revenue growth of only 7.5% (Rs 1,079.39 Cr to Rs 1,160.53 Cr, Note 39),
implying the margin gain came mostly from spread/utilisation, not volume, i.e.
high operating leverage on a converter cost base (Amendment 17 territory).

### 4D. Highest-value questions for management

1. What is the transfer-pricing basis for the Rs 237.72 Cr FY26 inter-segment chemicals-to-pharma transfer, and does it embed a below-market subsidy from one segment to the other? Reassuring: a disclosed, consistently-applied arm's-length or cost-plus formula. Worrying: an internal costing convention with no external benchmark, or no clear answer.
2. Who are the CDMO formulations "Anchor Customers" and the tolling "leading global chemical company," and what share of each new facility's capacity is contracted versus speculative? Reassuring: named or quantified take-or-pay contracts covering most of capacity. Worrying: undisclosed, uncommitted, single-customer, cancellable-on-notice terms.
3. What specific financing plan, beyond the phrase "internal accruals," backs the Rs 1,200-1,400 Cr greenfield, given FY26 FCF of only Rs 43 Cr? Reassuring: a credible, phased cash-milestone plan. Worrying: no plan beyond the phrase, or a later surprise term loan.
4. What happened to Vasudeva Commercials Limited's 7.94% stake between 31-Mar-2025 and 31-Mar-2026? Reassuring: a clean, disclosed reclassification or sale with no promoter-group absorption. Worrying: silence, or absorption into a promoter entity without disclosure.
5. What is the expected steady-state margin for the two new pre-commercial lines once ramped, compared with existing Pharma/Chemical segment margins? Reassuring: a specific, evidenced margin range. Worrying: only qualitative "will strengthen the portfolio" language.
6. Is the Ibuprofen expansion to 18,000 MTPA volume-led (new customer commitments) or does its economics implicitly require a global price recovery? Reassuring: a volume/customer-committed rationale independent of price. Worrying: the expansion needs a price recovery to pay off.
7. Given the roughly two-month raw-material pass-through lag on majority China-sourced chemicals feedstock, what hedging or lag-reduction mechanism exists today? Reassuring: named contractual pass-through clauses or a shortened lag. Worrying: no mechanism, margin stays exposed every time input prices move.
