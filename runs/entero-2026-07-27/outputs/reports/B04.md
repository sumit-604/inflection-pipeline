## SECTION 1: THE BUSINESS MODEL IN PLAIN ENGLISH

### 1A. One-line description
Entero buys medicines, medical devices and diagnostics from ~3,300 manufacturers (AR p.4: 2,700+ in FY25; Inv. Pres. slide 23: 3,300+ in FY26) and re-sells/redistributes them, at thin margin but very high volume, to ~100,000+ retail pharmacies and ~3,600 hospitals across India (AR p.4, p.12), increasingly layering in a higher-margin "commercial partner" service (marketing + demand generation for brands) and a MedTech distribution vertical on top of the core distribution engine.

### 1E. The chai-stall-uncle version
Imagine a chai-stall uncle who doesn't grow tea leaves or own a tea garden — he just buys tea in bulk from many suppliers and sells small packets to hundreds of tea stalls across the city, making a few rupees on every packet. Entero is that, but for medicines: it buys from ~3,300 manufacturers and resells to ~100,000+ pharmacies and hospitals, keeping only about 9-10 rupees of gross profit on every 100 rupees of goods sold (AR p.18, p.23; Inv. Pres. slide 41). Because the margin per packet is so thin, the uncle needs to sell a LOT of packets and — crucially — needs cash to buy his next batch of tea before his customers have paid him for the last one; that gap is why this business eats "working capital" instead of buying factories. Entero is now also starting to act like the tea company's marketing agent in some cities (getting paid extra for pushing a brand), and has started selling a new product line — diagnostic machines and cardiology devices — where the margin per packet is meaningfully fatter.

## SECTION 2: INDUSTRY DYNAMICS & COMPETITIVE POSITION

### 2A. Five forces, plainly

| Force | Assessment | Helps / Hurts / Neutral |
|---|---|---|
| Competition (rivalry) | Extremely fragmented — ~65,000 distributors nationwide (Inv. | Supplier power (manufacturers) | Manufacturers set trade margins industry-wide (Inv. ### 2D. Industry lifecycle stage and Entero's position
Early-to-mid consolidation phase: organized/large-national distributor share is ~8-10% of the fragmented Indian pharma distribution market today per one cut of the deck's own data, expected to reach "20-30% by FY28" (Inv. Pres. slide 30-31); a second slide in the same deck frames current large/national share at "25-30%" versus "70-75%" traditional/local (Inv. Pres. slide 31) — the two slides use different base-year framings, both are anchored to Inv. Pres. slide 30-31, and the exact current share is therefore presented with a range rather than a single point figure. Either way, Entero (revenue growing 29-30%/yr vs an ~8-16% Indian Pharmaceutical Market, AR p.12; Inv. Pres. slide 16) is a share-gaining consolidator in an early-innings structural shift, not a mature/stable incumbent.

### 3D. Unit economics — the physics of the business

- **Unit**: Rs 100 of goods sold (a proxy for one unit of distribution throughput/GMV).
- **Revenue and cost per unit**: For every Rs 100 of goods sold, cost of goods sold was Rs 89.7 in FY26 (i.e. gross margin 10.3%, Inv. Pres. slide 10), leaving Rs 10.3 gross profit; of that, employee + other opex consumed roughly Rs 6.3, leaving Rs 4.0 EBITDA (FY26 EBITDA margin 4.0%, Inv. Pres. slide 11).
- **Volume drivers**: number of retail/hospital customers (105,300+/3,600+ FY26), SKU count (97,500+), manufacturer relationships (3,300+), and geographic reach (523 districts, 136 warehouses) — all AR p.19/Inv. Pres. slide 15, 23.
- **Price drivers**: trade-discount terms negotiated with manufacturers; mix shift toward the higher-margin commercial/agency and MedTech streams; procurement scale efficiencies (AR p.5, p.23).
- **Cost drivers**: warehousing/logistics opex, employee costs (medical reps for the commercial vertical), and working-capital financing cost — finance costs rose from Rs 33.4 cr (FY25) to Rs 43.9 cr (FY26), Inv. Pres. slide 18/41 — as gross borrowings expanded to fund growth.
- **Incremental margin / operating leverage**: EBITDA rose from Rs 24 cr (FY22) to Rs 266 cr (FY26) as revenue rose from Rs 2,522 cr to Rs 6,591 cr over the same period (both anchored, Inv. Pres. slide 40-41) — an incremental EBITDA margin of roughly Rs 242 cr / Rs 4,069 cr ≈ 5.9% on the revenue added over four years, well above the ~1.0-4.0% average margin band the company reported in any single one of those years. This is the clearest evidence of real operating leverage: a largely fixed warehouse/employee cost base is being spread over rapidly rising throughput, and each incremental rupee of revenue is dropping through at a materially higher margin than the historical average.

### 4C. Quarterly monitoring checklist (12 items)

5. Net Operating Working Capital days (good: declining toward 60; trouble: rising) — Inv. Pres. balance sheet metric.
6. OCF/EBITDA conversion ratio (good: ≥50%, per company's own FY27 target; trouble: <30% or negative) — cash flow statement.
7. Gross + net borrowings growth vs revenue growth (good: borrowings growing slower than revenue; trouble: faster) — balance sheet.
8. Goodwill as % of equity and any impairment (good: stable ratio, no impairment; trouble: rising ratio or impairment charge) — balance sheet.

11. Number and margin-accretiveness of new acquisitions vs prior guidance (good: on-plan, margin-accretive; trouble: dilutive or behind schedule) — acquisitions slide.

### 4D. Highest-value questions for management

1. What proportion of consolidated revenue and gross margin today comes from the commercial/agency model versus pure fulfilment distribution, and what is the internal 3-year target mix? *Reassuring*: a clear, rising, disclosed mix. *Worrying*: inability or unwillingness to quantify a figure the company itself describes qualitatively.

5. What specific KPIs will confirm MedTech is on track for the guided Rs 1,000 cr FY27 revenue, and what happens to blended margins if MedTech scale-up disappoints? *Reassuring*: concrete, acquisition-level tracking metrics. *Worrying*: vague or purely aspirational framing.

7. What is the retention/renewal rate among the top manufacturer relationships by revenue, and has Entero ever lost a large principal to disintermediation or a rival distributor? *Reassuring*: high, disclosed stickiness. *Worrying*: evasive or undisclosed.
