## Business Understanding Narrative

**What does it do, and how does it make money?** Precot runs two businesses
from five plants across four southern states. The first is spinning: 165,000
spindles, 70 tonnes of cotton yarn a day, 100% compact-yarn enabled. The second
is technical textiles: private-label cotton products for the hygiene and
cosmetics market, made by spunlace hydro-entanglement, sold into 18 countries
(Investor Presentation FY2025 Q3, page 9). In FY2024 yarn was Rs 695 crore of
revenue and technical textiles Rs 238 crore, so 74.5% and 25.5% (Investor
Presentation FY2025 Q1, page 16). Exports were 47.3% of revenue. The company
was incorporated in 1962 and filed its 63rd annual report for FY2025.

**What is changing?** Precot stopped losing money on a unit it had run since
1983. On 27 February 2025 the board discontinued the Hindupur spinning unit in
Andhra Pradesh, after seven to eight years of losses driven by high power
tariffs and wage costs. It settled with employees for Rs 11.19 crore and moved
the machinery into its other spinning units, adding 8,400 spindles at
Kanjikode in Kerala (Annual Report FY2025, page 4). At the same time the
technical textile capacity expansion was commissioned in Q4 FY2025.

**Why now?** The first quarter after both actions landed shows a different
company. Q1 FY27 profit before tax was Rs 35.31 crore against Rs 14.91 crore a
year earlier, up 137%. Profit after tax was Rs 26.61 crore against Rs 11.30
crore (Q1 FY27 results, page 7). That single quarter earned 74% of the whole
of FY2026's profit.

**What must be true for the thesis to work?** The Q1 margin must repeat. The
step up is large: EBITDA margin of 20.6% in Q1 FY27 against 13.0% across
FY2026. Cotton spinning does not earn 20% margins through a cycle. Either the
technical textile mix now carries the company, or the quarter caught a
favourable cotton position.

**What breaks it?** Cotton. Precot buys the crop and sells yarn. The FY2025
Directors' Report describes the Cotton Corporation of India buying a third of
the crop as prices fell below the minimum support price, and notes that mills
were in no hurry to procure because supply was abundant (Annual Report FY2025,
page 4). A cotton price spike with weak yarn demand puts the spinning half back
where Hindupur was.

## 2. Business model and archetype

Two engines under one reported segment. The quarterly filings say "the
operations of the company primarily relate to only one reportable operating
segment namely Textiles" (Q1 FY27 results, page 8). The presentations split the
business in two. That disclosure choice hides the mix shift that is the whole
thesis.

Spinning sells a commodity against a published cotton price. Technical textiles
sells a private-label product to global hygiene and cosmetics brands, with
end-to-end capability and customer qualification.

**Archetype: two. Commodity converter for the yarn slice, outsourcing partner
for the technical textile slice.** Any later valuation must split them.
Section 1B v3.7 Amendment 17 binds the yarn slice.

## 3. Competitive advantages

Absent in yarn. Forming in technical textiles. The company's own stated
strategy is product development, capacity expansion in hygiene categories, new
export geographies in MENA and South East Asia, and debt reduction (Investor
Presentation FY2025 Q3, page 27). Nothing there is a moat. The one durable
asset is customer qualification: a private-label supplier to a global hygiene
brand is audited in, and audits are slow to redo. The corpus names no customer,
so concentration is unknown.

## 4. The promoters

The Chandran family, third generation. Ashwin Chandran is Chairman and Managing
Director. Prashanth Chandran is Vice Chairman and Managing Director. T Kumar is
Executive Director and Ravi Kumar Abburu is Director for technical textiles
(Annual Report FY2025, page 3).

Holdings as at 31 March 2024: Ashwin Chandran 19.36%, D Sarath Chandran
individually 13.69%, D Sarath Chandran HUF 10.14% (Annual Report FY2024,
page 118). On 11 June 2025 Ashwin Chandran transferred 1,67,889 shares, 1.40%,
by off-market gift to an immediate relative inside the promoter group. His
holding fell to 17.96%. The filing records no change to total promoter and
promoter-group holding and discloses no encumbrance, pledge, lien or
non-disposal undertaking (SAST disclosures, pages 3, 5 and 7).

Promoter-group total: NOT FOUND. Looked for in the FY2025 annual report note
and the SAST filings.

Skin in the game is a succession transfer, not a purchase. The company pays
dividends: Rs 3 a share for FY2025 and Rs 4 a share for FY2026 (Annual Report
FY2025, page 4; AGM proceedings, page 3).

## 6. The transition and the quality ladder

FROM R1 commodity price-taker, in the yarn line. TO R3 value-added supplier, in
the technical textile line. At company level the move is a mix shift, not a
single-line climb.

The mechanism is stated: "We Aim to be the Market Leader in the Feminine
Hygiene and Cosmetics Business" (Investor Presentation FY2025 Q3, page 20). The
enabling factors named are product mix, constant R&D for value-added products,
China plus one, and export demand for hygiene products.

That quote is from February 2025. Management has published nothing since. A
transition claim 19 months old is a weak claim.

## 8. Proof check

The triggers claim that removing Hindupur and adding technical textile capacity
would lift profitability. The last two quarters move that way. Q4 FY26 profit
before tax was Rs 18.43 crore. Q1 FY27 was Rs 35.31 crore, on revenue 5% lower.
Year on year the EBITDA margin went from 14.3% to 20.6%.

The proof point is present and it is one quarter old. That is the honest
statement. A second quarter at the same margin turns it into a trend.

## 9. Flags from the documents

  quarterly filings give no yarn-versus-technical-textile split. Flag.
- No earnings call has ever been held that this corpus can find. No investor
  presentation since February 2025. Flag the communication gap.
- FY2026 profit before tax fell to Rs 49.94 crore from Rs 67.91 crore in
  FY2025 on continuing operations, even though the loss-making unit had gone.

- The promoter transfer of June 2025 was a gift inside the family. Succession
  is in progress. Not a concern, but it is the context for the holding change.

- **Ugliness: ARTIFACT-OF-CLIMB.** The discontinued-operation losses of

## 12. Verdict card

**PROCEED to `/step1`.**

Of the three textile names screened today this is the only one where a
loss-making asset was actually removed, the replacement capacity was actually
commissioned, and the first clean quarter actually printed. The company is
quiet, cheaply followed and reports one segment where it runs two. That is the
shape of a recognition gap.

**Load-bearing facts a `/step1` must verify first**
1. The Q2 FY27 EBITDA margin. Everything turns on whether 20.6% repeats.
2. The yarn versus technical textile split for FY2026 and Q1 FY27. The company
   does not disclose it. Find it in the cost audit, the annual report segment
   note, or ask.
3. The 21 March 2026 credit rating action and its rationale.
4. Customer concentration in technical textiles. A private-label supplier with
   one brand behind half its revenue is a different business.
5. Whether the 8,400 Kanjikode spindles are running.

**What would change the view**
- A second quarter near 20% EBITDA margin moves this to a full position case.
- A fall back to 13% makes the quarter a cotton artefact and the posture
  reverts to WATCH.
- Any disclosure that technical textiles depends on one customer.

No Role 1 valuation was run and no target price is set. This verdict decides only whether the
name earns a full `/step1`, and it is not a position.
