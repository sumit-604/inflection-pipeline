## The picture in one paragraph

The week ended with the big companies holding the index up while the average stock slipped. The Nifty closed Thursday near 25,510, up about one and a half percent on the week. Banks led the week, and car makers and staples joined them on Thursday. Under that, the short, middle and long trend counts all ended below half the market. The long count fell from 1,149 a week earlier to 1,043. New yearly highs and new yearly lows finished level at 39 each. More stocks now trade near their yearly low than near their yearly high. Thursday's lift was broad but light, on modest turnover, and fear rose with prices. So the bounce off the October low still stands, but it rests on fewer shoulders than it did a week ago. The next move depends on whether the average stock follows the large ones up or drags them down.

## Where the market stands

Picture three clocks on every stock. The 20 day line is the short clock. The 50 day line is the middle clock. The 200 day line is the long clock. A stock above a line is in an uptrend on that clock. On Thursday 1,122 stocks stood above the short line, 1,036 above the middle line and 1,043 above the long line. Each count sits below half the market. A week earlier the short count stood at about 1,700 and the buyers owned it.

The autumn gives these counts their meaning. 23 September was the last day when more stocks stood above the long line than below it. October brought two waves of selling. On 8 October, 324 stocks fell hard in one session. On 21 October, 413 fell hard. Two sessions later, on 23 October, 390 more fell hard, and only 606 stocks held the long line. That was the low of the year so far on this count.

The climb that followed came in two strong bursts. On 4 November, 319 stocks rose hard, and the next day 244 more did. Those were real thrust days, with turnover near 560 and 600 crore. By 12 November the long count reached 1,149 and the short count reached 1,716. That was the high water mark of the bounce.

The week since then gave back part of that climb in two steps. On Monday 16 November, 216 stocks fell hard. Tuesday was quiet. On Wednesday only 71 stocks fell hard, but the fall was wide. About seven stocks in ten closed lower, and the long count slipped to 1,015. Thursday replied with about six stocks in ten closing higher. 76 stocks rose hard and 36 fell hard. The long count climbed back to 1,043.

The short clock lost close to 600 stocks from its peak. The long clock lost about 105. Short trends break first. Long trends follow only if the selling keeps going.

Thursday's lift was calm, not forceful. Turnover came to about 464 crore, against about 518 crore on the same session a year ago. About one stock traded on heavy volume for every three on light volume. That ratio sits in the lower quarter of its own history. Only about a third of the stocks that traded four percent up kept that gain into the close. A relief day looks like this. A thrust day brings 200 or more hard risers on heavy money.

The market is also coiling. About four stocks in ten moved less than three percent from low to high on Thursday. The share of stocks stuck in a tight five day range reached its highest level in twenty sessions. When many stocks go quiet at once, a larger move tends to follow. The quiet itself does not say which way.

A slower kind of damage built up through the week. The share of stocks down ten percent or more over five sessions rose in each of the last five sessions. It started at less than one in a hundred and ended at about one in eighteen. On the other side, only about one stock in a hundred is up fifteen percent over five sessions.

Fear moved the wrong way for a calm market. The India VIX measures what traders pay for protection against large swings in the Nifty. It rose about three and a half percent on Thursday, to about 16.1, while the Nifty rose about one percent. It is up more than forty percent over three months. When protection gets dearer on an up day, some traders buy the rally and the insurance together. One cross asset gap keeps stretching. Gold funds are down about twelve percent over the past month, while the Nifty is up about fourteen. Money keeps leaving the safe asset for the index.

## What would change this read

The read is a bounce off the October low that lost its width this week and kept its height. Banks, car makers and large staples hold the index up, while the average stock has slipped below its short, middle and long lines. The constructive view fails if the slide under the surface continues. A long count under about 1,000 would say Wednesday began a new leg down, from 1,043 on Thursday. A day of 300 or more hard fallers would repeat the October pattern. New yearly lows above about 100 would say sellers have returned in force. A VIX close above about 18 would say fear has taken charge. A sharp fall in banks or autos on heavy volume would remove the groups that carry the index. A long count under about 606, the October low, would erase the whole bounce.

The cautious view fails if the members catch up with the index. The first sign is the short count back above half the market, about 1,170, from 1,122. The second is the long count back above 1,050 and then turning toward half, from 1,043. The third is a wide up day with 200 or more hard risers on turnover above about 600 crore. The fourth is new yearly highs pulling clear of new lows, from level at 39 each. The fifth is a VIX close under 15, from 16.1. The first two signs together, with any one of the others in the same week, would put the repair back on track.
