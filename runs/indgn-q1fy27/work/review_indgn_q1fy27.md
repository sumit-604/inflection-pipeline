**Six mandatory diagnostics:**
1. **Revenue grew YoY?** Yes, +39.7% INR / +26.5% USD. But this bundles acquisitions (Cake, Trilogy, MJL, BioPharm, Warn, Addressable Health — all in Annexure I, L183-196) and currency; the organic constant-currency ex-M&A component is **not disclosed** (deflected Turn 23) → the honest underlying growth rate is unknown (A3-F16-01, FND-07).
2. **Op EBITDA margin YoY?** Contracted: **Q1 FY27 16.39% vs Q1 FY26 20.41% = −402 bps** (operating basis); −330 bps on the deck basis. Unambiguous compression.
3. **Core operating PBT grew YoY?** **No — it fell −4.8%** (130.0 → 123.7). Reported PAT is flat only because Other Income rose +6.9 cr and offset the operating decline. **The flat headline is not real operating health.**
4. **Gap between core-PBT growth and reported PAT growth:** core PBT −4.8% vs PAT −0.2%. Walk: Op EBITDA +18.9 cr; D&A **−22.5 cr** (the dominant drag); Finance costs −2.7 cr; Other Income **+6.9 cr**; Tax −0.8 cr. Net PAT −0.2 cr. The +39.7% revenue converted to a −0.2% PAT because margin compressed −402 bps and D&A doubled.
5. **D&A / finance scaling faster than revenue?** Yes emphatically — D&A +104% vs revenue +40%. Classic M&A absorption/amortisation gap: ROCE compresses until the acquired revenue scales into the amortisation. Amortisation was flagged to step down Rs 50 mn/qtr from Q3 FY27 — **not reiterated on the call** (A3-F17-02).
6. **Other Income concentration changing?** OI/PBT rose 14.5% → 19.0% YoY. FY-level OI is flat-ish; the Q1 spike (22.1 → 29.0) is masking the operating decline. Strip it and core PBT fell.

# SECTION D — PLAIN-LANGUAGE BRIEF (mandatory, four parts)

## D1. SUMMARY NARRATIVE
Indegene grew Q1 FY27 revenue to Rs 1,063 cr, up 39.7% over a year ago and 5.9% over the March quarter [this quarter's filing, L216]. That headline flatters. Profit after tax was Rs 116.2 cr, essentially flat versus a year ago (−0.2%) [filing L238], and the reason it held flat is not operating strength: the core operating profit before other income actually fell 4.8% year on year [computed from filing], and a Rs 6.9 cr rise in treasury/other income filled the hole. Operating margin, which is the entire investment case here, did not expand: on the clean operating basis it was flat at 16.4% versus 16.3% in March, and the CFO himself conceded that once you strip the March quarter's one-off 240 bps currency-hedge drag, margin actually declined sequentially [concall Turn 4]. Year on year the margin fell about 400 bps from 20.4%. Depreciation and amortisation doubled (+104%) as acquisitions get absorbed [filing L223]. The Indian parent on its own shrank this quarter (revenue −7.5% quarter on quarter, PAT Rs 72.1→59.9 cr) [filing L508/L525]; all the consolidated growth came from offshore subsidiaries, and subsidiary earnings are lumpy (their share of group PAT swung from 9.5% to 48.5% in one quarter). Management responded to the soft print by *hardening* its promise — no longer "6 to 8 quarters" but exactly 6, back to 19-20% by March 2027 [concall Turns 14/18]. At the same time it stopped disclosing organic-versus-acquired growth [Turn 23] and refused to quantify AI-platform revenue [Turn 40] — the two numbers that would let an outsider check the story. Cash and investments fell 15.5% year on year to Rs 1,460 cr [deck s22] and there is no cash-flow statement at Q1, so cash conversion is untestable (INDETERMINATE). For the thesis: the primary tripwire — margin expansion — printed negative on the honest underlying basis; it is 1 of the 2 consecutive quarters that would formally fire it. Verdict stays PROCEED WITH FLAGS; Decision Status unchanged (BUY-on-dips, not BUY-now); the real decision waits for the Q2 FY27 margin print through the wage-hike quarter.

## D2. SECTOR INTELLIGENCE
Demand cycle: global pharma is growing mid-to-high single digits, and the top-20 pharma (Indegene's core customers) faster at ~10-12% [concall Turn 3, management estimate, not independently verified]. The structural tailwinds Indegene sells into are real and prior-Notion-confirmed: patent-cliff pressure pushing brands to reimagine marketing spend, drug pipelines near historic highs, and AI compressing content/commercialization cost. Regulation: US drug-pricing/MFN pressure is described as "largely behind" and policy "currently stable," but management newly foregrounded US policy as the primary external risk to watch [Turn 9] — a caveat worth noting because widened caveats often precede softer commentary. Payer/end-market mix is 91.6% biopharma, 75.1% North America [deck s22, s21 — this-quarter filing/deck], so the business is levered to US pharma commercialization budgets. The sector read cuts both ways: the tailwind is genuine and multi-year, but AI-driven price deflation (which management frames as a share-gain opportunity) is the same force that could compress its own pricing if volume gains lag. No analysed peer reported in the ±4-week window, so the macro claims are unverified against an in-universe peer.

## D3. BUSINESS-MODEL INTELLIGENCE
How it makes money: asset-light, nil-debt services to global pharma across commercial (Enterprise Commercial, 70.6% of revenue) and medical (Enterprise Medical, 25.7%) [deck s21, this quarter]; ~60% of revenue is output/outcome-aligned rather than headcount-billed [concall Turn 30], which is the differentiator versus IT-services and agencies (prior-Notion thesis, reaffirmed). Unit economics: revenue per employee ~$77K TTM, "highest in peer group" per management [deck s7 — note this includes third-party-payroll contractors, so it is not a pure direct-employee productivity figure]. Model drift this quarter: the mix is shifting toward large, upfront-cost outcome-based deals where cost lands in the P&L quarters before revenue (the $10mn+ omnichannel deal: cost since Q4 FY26, revenue from Q3 FY27) [Turn 31]. That is the stated reason margins are depressed and the stated engine of the H2 recovery — but it front-loads risk: if the revenue slips, the margin bridge breaks. The other model signal is that growth is increasingly acquired and offshore (parent shrank; D&A doubled; organic split withdrawn), so the "organic compounding" part of the model is currently unverifiable [this quarter's filing + Turn 23]. Segment detail: Enterprise Commercial margin compressed to 16.0% from 21.1% a year ago and is the drag; Enterprise Medical held ~26%; the small "Others" (consulting/clinical) loss widened to −Rs 11.5 cr [filing Note 5, L349-351].

## D4. COMPETITION INTELLIGENCE
Where it wins: Indegene positions as a category of one ("not IT-services, agency, or CRO") on four claimed moats — embedded on the growth-side of client budgets (less exposed to cost-cutting), 27 years of life-sciences domain depth (29% healthcare-background delivery staff), a proprietary GenAI stack (Cortex/Content Super App/Medical Writing), and outcome-aligned pricing [deck s9; concall Turns 3, 35 — mix of prior-Notion thesis and this-quarter reiteration]. Named competitive frame: it takes wallet share from incumbent agencies and CROs as GenAI deflates their economics [Turn 35]. Where it is structurally weaker / the risk to watch: (1) client concentration — top-20 clients are still 66.6% of TTM revenue [deck s22] and the largest clients "have not yet shown up" in the deepening numbers [Turn 3], so the model still leans on a narrow base; (2) the moat is asserted, not yet proven in the margin — if the GenAI "price deflation is a tailwind for us" thesis were fully true, margin would be expanding, and it is not; (3) frontier-model dependence and client data-ownership — management expects large pharma to move to on-prem open-weight models [Turn 37], which could disintermediate part of the AI value it captures. The competitive question the numbers pose: is the margin compression an *investment* that reverses (management's case) or the early sign that GenAI deflation reaches Indegene's own pricing before the volume offsets arrive (the bear case). This quarter did not resolve it; Q2 FY27 operating margin will.
