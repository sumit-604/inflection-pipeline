## Findings

**Finding 1 (MINOR) — Report-metadata mischaracterization of unread pages.**
B06's metadata line says of the third BHAGYANGR transcript: "20 of 23pp read; remaining pages are closing
remarks/moderator sign-off with no incremental substantive content." I read the remaining pages (the file's
last 3, transcript-numbered pp.19–21) and found they contain real Q&A content before the closing remarks:
a working-capital detail (Raj Sarraf: "I roughly have about 30 to 35 days for stocks and 35 days for
debtors"), a fundraise-plan disclosure ("we are looking at a fundraise of roughly about 150 crores," Isha
Agarwal Q&A), and an FY27 volume-growth guidance restatement ("we are looking at 20% volume growth," Isha
Agarwal Q&A) — only the final paragraph is actual sign-off. None of this is directly relevant to the 4
peer_questions, so it does not change any claim verdict, but the "no incremental substantive content"
characterization is inaccurate and should be corrected in the metadata note.

**Finding 2 (MINOR) — Internal call-date inconsistency (filename-derived mislabeling).**
The third BHAGYANGR transcript is dated 02-May-2026 on both its cover letter and title page and covers Q4/FY26
(year ended 31-Mar-2026). B06 correctly labels it "May 2026 call" / "02-May-2026" in Part 1 (Claim 4), Part 2C,
Part 2D, Part 2E, and the Part 3 coverage map — but in Part 2A ("BHAGYANGR record Rs.2,000+ crore FY26 revenue
with Q4 alone at Rs.735 crore, Jun 2026 call") and Part 2B ("referenced near $1,300/kg equivalent by May 2026
(BHAGYANGR, Jun 2026 call)") it reverts to "Jun 2026 call," apparently inherited from the input filename
(BHAGYANGR-Concall_Jun_2026_Transcript.pdf) rather than the transcript's own stated date. This is not a
fabricated or wrong source — it is the same, correctly-quoted call, just inconsistently dated in two spots.
Should be corrected for internal consistency; does not affect any claim verdict since the same document
underlies both labels.

**Finding 3 (MINOR) — Currency/unit conflation on a supplementary figure.**
Part 2B states copper was "referenced near $1,300/kg equivalent by May 2026 (BHAGYANGR, Jun 2026 call)."
The underlying exchange (Vedansh Agarwal Q&A, May-2026 call: "realization for FY26 was 963... current copper
price is 1300") is in rupees per kg (consistent with the same call's other realization figures stated in
rupees, e.g. "EBITDA per kg for the year is around 43 rupees"), not USD. Labeling it "$1,300/kg equivalent"
misstates the currency. This figure is used only as supplementary industry color in the unprompted cross-read,
not as an anchor for any of the 4 formal claim verdicts, so it does not change any verdict, but it is a
unit/basis error of the kind the framework specifically flags as a priority trap.

**Finding 4 (MINOR) — Slightly loose attribution on the working-capital/copper-price risk item.**
Part 2E cites "VIDYAWIRES, Dec 2025 call, Mihir Manohar Q&A" as one of two anchors for "rising copper price
mechanically inflates working-capital requirements... even when the pricing cycle is fully pass-through/
hedged." Mihir Manohar's actual Dec-2025 question is about FY2025 operating-cash-flow weakness and receivables
build generically; it does not explicitly link the mechanism to rising copper prices the way the BHAGYANGR
citation (Pakshal Jain/Atul Raval, Jan-2026 call: "as the copper prices go up... the working capital cycle
will definitely go up... short-term debts from the bank will go up") does. The BHAGYANGR anchor is squarely
on-point; the VIDYAWIRES anchor is adjacent but not quite the same claim. This softens (not invalidates) the
"both peers acknowledge" framing in Part 2E — it is really one clean confirmation (BHAGYANGR) plus one
loosely-related data point (VIDYAWIRES), not two equally direct confirmations. Does not change any of the 4
formal claim verdicts, since this risk item sits in the unprompted cross-read section, not Part 1.

## No CRITICAL or MAJOR findings
No SUBSTANTIVE peer lacked a real citation. No claim in peer_questions was skipped. No VERIFIED verdict rests
on fewer than 2 independent peer anchors. No verdict was upgraded from silence. No quote I checked was
fabricated, and every quote's attribution (speaker, call, date) I could verify matched the transcript once the
correct call date was used. The four findings above are process/precision issues (metadata accuracy, internal
date-labeling consistency, one currency-unit slip, one loosely-matched secondary anchor) that a diligent editor
should fix, but none of them changes a claim verdict or the overall triangulation conclusion.
