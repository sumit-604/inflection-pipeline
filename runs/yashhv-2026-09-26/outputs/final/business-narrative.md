# YASHHV business narrative (phase 1, evidence only)

Yash Highvoltage Ltd. Run 2026-09-26. Phase 1 lite: no valuation content. The paragraph on what the valuation concluded is left out on purpose. Phase 3 writes it.

## Business understanding narrative

Yash Highvoltage makes transformer bushings. A bushing is the insulated sleeve that carries high voltage current through the wall of a transformer tank without a flashover or a fire. Every transformer carries six or seven of them, and no substitute product exists. Resin impregnated paper bushings, called RIP or RIS, are 83% of sales and are the dry, newer design that the industry is moving to. Oil impregnated paper bushings, called OIP, are 10% of sales and are the older design, still in demand for replacement and for United States and European exports. High current bushings, wall through bushings and a retrofit and testing service make up the rest, with retrofit at about Rs 16 Cr.

The buyers are transformer makers of the GE and Hitachi Energy class, and utilities such as PGCIL, NTPC, NPCIL and DRDO, which have each approved Yash as a supplier. A utility takes 8 to 10 years to qualify a bushing supplier, because a failed bushing can burn a whole transformer. Once approved, a supplier is hard to displace. The customer list is getting narrower, though. One customer took 18.8% of FY26 sales, about Rs 44 Cr, against two customers near 10% each a year earlier.

Demand today comes from grid and generation spending. The run tracks it through the CEA National Electricity Plan transmission capex targets, the PGCIL capex and repair budget, and the order books of transformer makers such as BHEL, CG Power, Hitachi Energy India, GE Vernova and Siemens. All three peers confirm the demand at the transformer level for the same period. Hitachi Energy India reports a record order backlog, and Vilas Transcore says there is no slowdown in transformer makers.

Growth should come from three places. The first is more transformers, visible in MNRE renewable capacity addition data and the CEA targets. The second is import substitution, visible in the India bushing import value under HS code 85049010, which the run could only confirm up to 2023. The third is exports, where Weidmann, Electrolink and the new Yash HV USA office are the signals to watch, alongside Quality Power's disclosures on the Sukrut Electric joint venture.

The competitive edge sits in customer approvals, not in cost or brand. The RIP and OIP lines both carry a real switching cost from the long qualification cycle, and the run confirmed that mechanism from the annual report. The RIP line has no cost edge yet, because about 95% of its cores are still imported from Europe. The new Vadodara plant is meant to change that, but it has not started trial production. The moat scan rates backward integration and the China plus one export channel as its two leading forward categories, but neither has produced revenue. Network effects do not exist here, the talent asymmetry test found no evidence, and cost advantage and brand could not be scored because peer financial data came out empty. The retrofit line has no separate moat the run could establish.

## Bushing maker grows fast, cash lags behind

Yash is a Vadodara bushing maker that listed on the BSE SME board in December 2024 and reports half yearly. FY26 revenue rose 57% to Rs 235 Cr. EBITDA margin rose from 23.1% to 25.7%, and PAT rose 74% to Rs 37 Cr. The order book reached over Rs 400 Cr at March 2026, up from Rs 150 Cr a year earlier and Rs 250 Cr at September 2025. OIP capacity went from 6,000 to 10,000 units a year inside FY26. Exports more than doubled to Rs 17 Cr but are still only 7% of sales.

The transition is from a bushing assembler that imports its most important input to a maker of its own cores at 550 kV. The Vadodara greenfield plant carries that claim. It was due by March 2026. Management now guides trial production by the end of Q2 FY27 and commercial output three to four months later. No call named that as a delay. The capex figure moved from Rs 85 to 90 Cr to "Rs 100 Cr plus", and an analyst cited Rs 153 Cr that the chairman neither confirmed nor denied. The core supply contract with the former Swiss partner runs only to mid 2027, so the plant and the bridge supply now end close together.

The backward score is GOOD at 88 of 160, with a core score of 67 and five of twelve moat tests confirmed. Cash is the reason it is not higher. FY26 operating cash flow was Rs 8.8 Cr against PAT of Rs 37 Cr, and free cash flow was minus Rs 50 Cr after Rs 59 Cr of capex. Inventory rose 125% while revenue rose 57%. Working capital days climbed from 79 in FY22 to 119 in FY26. On the other side, debt to equity is 0.17, suppliers were not stretched, customer advances rose more than fourfold, and no promoter share is pledged. Part of the cash gap is a plant that is not yet earning. Part of it has no explanation on the record. For 12 quarters in a row the stock reported to the bank differed from the books, mostly in one direction and widest at each year end, and only 2 quarters carry a reason. The statutory auditor confirmed this under CARO. The FY25 operating cash figure also changed by Rs 34 lakh between two audited annual reports with no reconciliation.

Management delivers the headline numbers and blurs the rest. FY26 revenue beat the 25 to 30% growth guide, and all six promise checks the verifier ran held up. Against that, the same May 2026 call gave FY27 as 40 to 45% growth, which is Rs 329 to 341 Cr, and also as Rs 360 to 400 Cr of invoicing, and never reconciled the two. The same call gave two different reasons for the FY26 margin gain. The story of who owns the RIP core technology changed across calls. Past growth was restated upward from about 25% to 38 to 40%, and a promise of no further capex till 2030 turned into a Rs 150 Cr equity raise. Segment margin data given in January 2025 was refused in later calls. The credibility grade is C. The promoter verdict is TRUSTWORTHY: legal, pledge and share conduct are clean, while the managing director's pay doubled to Rs 2.9 Cr, 53 times the median employee, in a year of weak cash.

The peers complicate the story more than they confirm it. They confirm tight bushing and insulator supply and the transformer demand wave. They do not confirm Yash's market share, its global market size figure, or its claim of seven to eight years without a price war. Hitachi Energy India and Quality Power describe rules that bar Chinese material from border countries, against Yash's statement that Chinese bushings were "never restricted". By August 2026 both peers report four Chinese transformer and GIS makers allowed to bid with 60 to 65% local content. A customer channel on Yash's own call said RIP capacity in India has come up, and four domestic RIP core localisers now exist. Quality Power expects input costs to hit margins while Yash says it is protected. The largest single finding came from Vilas Transcore: Yash's founder promoter, Mr Ramachandran, now runs a new Vilas backed bushing venture covering 12 to 400 kV. Vilas announced it two days before Yash's May 2026 call, and Yash did not mention it.

The market is large against Yash's size. The addressable bushing market is about Rs 17,400 Cr, inside a global total of Rs 22,600 to 27,000 Cr growing about 5.6% a year. Yash holds about 1.35% of it, so the runway class is STRONG. A three year obtainable revenue of about Rs 585 Cr implies 35.5% annual growth, near management's 40 to 42% guide. The five year figure of about Rs 996 Cr runs past the combined plant ceiling of Rs 635 to 677 Cr at flat prices, so it needs higher prices per unit or more capacity. The annual report quotes a global market size 1.8 times smaller than the one in the prospectus, both investor decks and the calls, and management has never reconciled the two.
