### 2C. Growth embedded in capex — the arithmetic

Mechanical calculation as specified (total capex under execution × historical fixed asset
turnover):
- Net fixed assets (PP&E, net block) at 31-Mar-2026: Rs 1,282.82 Lakh = Rs 12.83 Cr
  (AR Note 10, 📄).
- FY26 revenue from operations: Rs 26,935.66 Lakh = Rs 269.36 Cr (AR/RHP, 📄).
- Historical fixed asset turnover (FY26, year-end basis) = 269.36 / 12.83 ≈ **21.0x**.
- Capex under execution (the only IPO-monitored, committed figure): Rs 10.30 Cr.
- Mechanical implied incremental revenue = 10.30 × 21.0 ≈ **Rs 216 Cr, or ~80% of
  current revenue.**

**This number is flagged as an artifact, not a usable signal.** Susan's fixed-asset base is
tiny relative to revenue because 38.38% of FY26 revenue is aluminium-rod/wire trading
(RHP itself calls it "low-margin," "low entry barriers," RHP p.24) which requires almost
no fixed assets to generate. A 21x turnover ratio built on a revenue base one-third of
which is pass-through trading is not the same thing as a 21x turnover on a manufacturing
engine; applying it to a manufacturing capex line overstates the implied revenue by a
wide margin. 🔍 analyst inference.

**Cross-check via disclosed physical capacity** (more defensible, still directional only):
- Manufactured cable revenue FY26 (LT + HT + MVCC, excluding trading and excluding
  winding wire/conductors) = Rs 7,954.74 + 380.10 + 1,918.60 = **Rs 10,253.44 Lakh
  = Rs 102.53 Cr** (RHP p.139, 📄).
- Disclosed capacity addition is +60% (7,500 → 12,000 km/yr, RHP/Chartered Engineer
  certificate, 📄).
- If cable revenue scaled proportionally with the +60% capacity addition at full
  utilisation: incremental cable revenue ≈ 0.60 × Rs 102.53 Cr ≈ **Rs 61.5 Cr, or ~22.8%
  of FY26 total company revenue (Rs 269.36 Cr).** 🔍 analyst inference, cross-check basis.
- Caveat: this assumes full utilisation of the new capacity, which the 3-year utilisation
  history (Unit I 55-84%, Unit III 64% in year one) does not support as a base case.

**capex_embedded_growth_pct is reported at 22.8% (the capacity cross-check), not the
80% FAT-mechanical figure, because the FAT figure is a known artifact of the trading-line
revenue mix and would mislead any downstream stage that consumes it at face value.**

customer churn rather than a genuine broadening of the manufacturing customer base.

## SECTION 4: REGULATORY & POLICY TAILWINDS (category R1)

**4A. Regulatory approvals in pipeline**: None beyond the certifications already held
(Section 3, B2). No pending BIS/regulatory application disclosed as "in progress."

**4B. Government policy tailwinds**: The Revamped Distribution Sector Scheme (RDSS),
a Central Government scheme, has sanctioned projects totalling approximately Rs 2.81
lakh crore across 46 DISCOMs in 28 States/UTs as of the RDSS dashboard, January 2026
(RHP p.135, citing Infomerics industry report, 📄 for the scheme figures themselves,
though the intermediary source is a company-commissioned industry report — tier
caveat applies per B00). Saubhagya 2.0 (rural/urban electrification) is named alongside
RDSS as a demand driver. Susan participates via DISCOM vendor registrations under
RDSS, MUJY (Jharkhand) and MKVSY Ph-II (state scheme) (AR p.10, 📄). Duration:
multi-year (RDSS execution horizon extends beyond FY27-28 per scheme design).
Enrolment status: Susan is already vendor-registered and can bid. **Competitors share
the benefit identically** — RDSS, Saubhagya 2.0 and BIS-based tendering are open to any
qualified cable manufacturer, including named larger peers (DYCL, VIDYAWIRES,
DIACABS). No PLI scheme, tax incentive or import-substitution measure specific to
wires/cables was found in the corpus.

**4C. Regulatory moat assessment**: ACTIVE (the scheme is sanctioned and executing
now, not merely proposed), but it is a sector-wide tailwind rather than a company-specific
moat. Time to kick in: immediate (Susan already bids into RDSS-linked tenders per the
order-book mix disclosed at the Q1 FY27 call, ~50:50 government/private). Sustainability:
tied to the scheme's own multi-year execution horizon, not to any durable feature unique
to Susan; if a peer wins share within the same RDSS pool, Susan's position does not
improve relative to peers even as the market grows.
