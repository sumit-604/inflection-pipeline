## SECTION 1: MARKET DEFINITION

### 1A. Precise boundaries

**Product scope**: Cloud-native SaaS platform for media operations, spanning
three funnels that map 1:1 to Amagi's own divisions:
- **Cloud Modernization** (~19% of FY26 revenue): migration of broadcast/cable
  network operations from on-premise, hardware-intensive infrastructure to
  cloud-based systems (cloud playout, master control).
- **Streaming Unification** (~56% of FY26 revenue): unified cloud platform for
  OTT/FAST/CTV channel creation, scheduling, management and distribution
  across SVOD/AVOD/FAST business models.
- **Monetization & Marketplace** (~25% of FY26 revenue): CTV/FAST ad-tech —
  ad insertion (SSAI), ad-ops, revenue-share, and content-licensing
  marketplace tools.

**Explicit exclusions**: legacy on-premise broadcast hardware (the >$270bn
global broadcasting market itself is NOT the TAM, only its cloud-software
layer is); pure content production/creation spend; gross linear TV or CTV
*advertising* spend (Amagi monetizes via software/platform fees and revenue
share on top of ad spend, not the ad spend itself — see Section 2 flag);
consumer OTT subscription revenue (Netflix/Prime-type SVOD fees); generic
CDN/telecom infrastructure not bundled into Amagi's stack; DIY/indie
FAST-channel tools priced for creators rather than enterprise broadcasters.

**Geographic scope**: Global. Amagi revenue by FY26 geography: Americas
72.9%, Europe 17.3%, Asia-Pacific 6.7%, Middle East 1.7%, India 1.4% (source:
IPO Prospectus, geographic revenue table, FY23-FY25 trend, run-date extension
to FY26 divisional splits in Q4 FY26 investor presentation). This is a
global, Americas-led business — India is immaterial to Amagi's own book
despite the "India small/microcap" collector-label context; TAM/SAM/SOM here
are sized globally, with regional splits shown where the source data
supports it.

**Customer scope**: Enterprise B2B — broadcasters, cable networks, content
owners/studios, OTT platforms, FAST operators and CTV distributors (400+
content owners/platforms, 407 cumulative distributors per FY26 investor
presentation). Not consumer-facing.

**Channel scope**: Direct enterprise sales plus hyperscaler/platform
partnerships (AWS Marketplace; distribution integrations with Roku, VIZIO,
Samsung and similar CTV platforms cited in customer case studies).

**Price segment**: Enterprise SaaS — subscription + usage + revenue-share
hybrid pricing, bespoke/negotiated per contract (per-channel pricing not
disclosed, B04 flag); 35 customers >$1m ARR (FY26).

### 1B. Management's own TAM claim

Source: IPO Prospectus, "Industry Overview — Overview of the relevant market
for Amagi — TAM and SAM" (1Lattice Report, data as of 31 Dec 2024), and Q4
FY26 investor presentation (21 May 2026, page 7).

- **TAM = US$16.9bn (CY2024)**, sum of three funnels: Cloud Modernization
  (cloud broadcasting software) $6.2bn + Streaming Unification (video
  streaming software, within a $36.9bn ad-supported streaming market) $3.7bn
  + Monetization & Marketplace (CTV advertising software) $7.0bn.
- **SAM = US$5.1bn (CY2024)**, sum of segment SAMs: $1.7bn + $1.6bn + $1.8bn.
  Grew from $2.2bn (CY2019) at 18.3% CAGR; projected to reach $9.6bn by
  CY2029P at 13.3% CAGR (2024-2029P).
- Investor presentation (more recent, 21 May 2026) restates only the Cloud
  Modernization piece: **"$1.9bn SAM, +11% Y/Y"**, alongside two other
  headline stats not framed as TAM/SAM at all: "48% of US TV viewing time"
  (streaming share, Nielsen) and **"$42.5bn global CTV ad spend, growing
  >10% annually"** (eMarketer/WPP).
- Separately, in press commentary (not the prospectus), Amagi's CEO states
  **"~10% cloud penetration"** of broadcast/cable workloads as of 2024,
  expected to reach 40-60% by 2029 — a workload-migration metric, distinct
  from the software-spend SAM/TAM ratio (30.2%, see Section 2).

**Credibility read**: The formal $16.9bn TAM / $5.1bn SAM funnel disclosure
(prospectus) is **reasonable** — methodologically transparent (three
named, separately-sourced funnels that sum cleanly), consistent across the
prospectus's own five separate mentions, and — per Section 2 below —
corroborated by independent third-party market-research ranges. The $1.9bn
Cloud Modernization SAM figure in the investor presentation is also
reasonable in magnitude ($1.7bn CY2024 × ~1.12 ≈ $1.9bn) but re-labels the
segment's **trailing** CY2019-2024 CAGR (11.7%) as "+11% Y/Y" **forward**
growth, when the prospectus's own **forward** CY2024-2029P CAGR for the same
segment is lower, 8.4% — a minor communications-quality flag, not a
magnitude flag. The **$42.5bn global CTV ad-spend** figure is **broad to the
point of being the wrong number entirely** if read as Amagi's TAM: it is
gross ad dollars flowing to publishers/networks, not the software/platform
fee pool Amagi can capture (~2.5x the formal $16.9bn TAM); it belongs in
Section 4 (growth driver / demand-side context), not in Section 2 (TAM
estimate).

## SECTION 2: TAM ESTIMATION, MULTIPLE METHODS

### Method 1 — Top-down (1Lattice three-funnel, cross-checked independently)

Base case is management's own funnel disclosure (Section 1B): **TAM =
$16.9bn (CY2024)**, **SAM = $5.1bn (CY2024)** → **$9.6bn (CY2029P, 13.3%
CAGR)**.

Independent web cross-checks (all searched 12 Jul 2026):
- **Cloud/broadcast software segment** (comparable to Cloud Modernization,
  SAM $1.7-1.9bn per 1Lattice): third-party CY2025-26 estimates run somewhat
  higher — Cloud TV Market $2.15-2.74bn (Fortune Business Insights, 2025-26),
  Cloud Broadcasting Solutions $3.8bn (2026, MarkWide Research), Broadcast
  Automation Software $3.07bn (2026, Research and Markets). These use wider
  scope boundaries (include scheduling/automation tools beyond pure cloud
  playout) so are not directly substitutable, but confirm the segment is
  real and of the right order of magnitude ($2-4bn), not smaller.
- **FAST market** (content+ad revenue, broader than Amagi's software-only
  scope but same underlying growth engine as Streaming Unification):
  third-party CY2026 estimates cluster $12-15bn (Mordor Intelligence
  $14.3bn; Data Bridge $15.2bn; Research and Markets $14.9bn), roughly
  consistent with the prospectus's own Linear OTT (FAST) revenue trajectory
  ($6.6bn CY2024 → $13.3bn CY2029P at 15.1% CAGR).
- **CTV advertising**: eMarketer's 2026 US CTV ad-spend forecast is ~$38bn;
  Amagi's cited global figure of $42.5bn implies the US is ~89% of the
  global CTV ad market, which is plausible given the US's disclosed 78%
  share of the *CTV advertising software* SAM specifically (prospectus) —
  broadly consistent, though this is the ad-spend pool, not Amagi's TAM
  (see 1B flag).
- **Peer revenue aggregation** (Method 3 below) yields an independent
  ~$4.5bn estimate against management's $5.1bn SAM claim — corroborating,
  not contradicting.

**Conclusion**: no independent source offers a more comprehensive,
better-sourced *global three-segment software TAM* than the 1Lattice funnel
figures, and every partial independent cross-check corroborates rather than
contradicts them. TAM Method 1 = **$16.9bn (CY2024), Confidence: M** (single
underlying research house, but internally consistent and externally
corroborated on every sub-piece checked). STALE flag: data is dated 31 Dec
2024 — 19 months old at run date, under the 2-year threshold but aging;
flagged for refresh at next cycle.

### Method 2 — Bottom-up (FAST channel count × per-channel spend; partial, Streaming Unification only)

Addressable unit: one FAST/linear channel requiring cloud-managed
playout+scheduling+distribution. Global FAST channel count (1Lattice, same
prospectus): **9.7K (CY2024) → 33.2K (CY2029P)** — note this counts
channel-distributions across platforms, "which may not all be unique" per
the source's own caveat.

Amagi's own per-channel economics are **NOT FOUND** (B04: bespoke,
negotiated pricing, not disclosed per-channel; only cumulative "channel
deliveries," 9,425 FY26, which conflates unique channels with repeat
distributor deliveries — this metric cannot be turned into a clean
revenue-per-channel figure). Falling back to disclosed third-party
entry-tier cloud-playout pricing ($3,600-$24,000/yr per channel, per vendor
websites TVU Channel, Veset, FAST Channels TV) as a **floor**, since these
are indie/SMB tools, not Amagi's enterprise-grade deployments:

- 9.7K channels (CY2024) × $24,000/yr (top of entry-tier range) = **$233m**
  — floor estimate for the FAST-channel-management sub-segment alone.
- This is far below the prospectus's $3.7bn Streaming Unification TAM
  (CY2024), which is expected — Amagi's real customers are enterprise
  broadcasters on multi-hundred-thousand-to-multi-million-dollar contracts
  (35 customers >$1m ARR), not $250-2,000/month indie tools. Scaling the
  per-channel price up ~10-15x to an enterprise-average tier lands in the
  $2.3-3.5bn range — same order of magnitude as the $3.7bn prospectus
  figure for this one funnel.

**Confidence: L.** This method is structurally thin (assumption-heavy
multiplier, single-funnel scope, no Amagi-specific unit economics available)
and is used only as a **sanity check**, not a competing TAM estimate: it
confirms the Streaming Unification TAM is not absurd, nothing more.

### Method 4 — Import substitution: NOT APPLICABLE

This is a global software TAM, not a domestic-manufacturing-vs-import
scenario; there is no consumption/domestic-production/import framework that
applies to a globally-delivered SaaS platform. Skipped by design, not by
omission.

### Method 5 — Global benchmark (regional catch-up)

Prospectus regional split of the combined SAM: USA ~66% ($3.4bn) of the
$5.1bn CY2024 total, Europe ~15% ($0.8bn), rest-of-world ~19% ($0.9bn). By
CY2029P, USA share is projected to *decline* to ~63% ($6.1bn) while Europe
holds ~16% ($1.6bn) and rest-of-world rises to ~21% ($1.9bn) of the $9.6bn
total — implying the US grows at a modest ~11.9% CAGR while rest-of-world
(the earlier-stage, catch-up geographies) grows faster, ~15-16% CAGR. Amagi
itself is *more* US-concentrated (72.9% Americas FY26) than the SAM's US
share (66% CY2024) and materially under-indexed in Asia-Pacific (Amagi 6.7%
vs SAM's broader "other regions" ~19%) — a whitespace/expansion lever
(Section 5D), not a TAM subtraction. This method is not independent of
Method 1 (same 1Lattice source) so is used only to validate the internal
growth-rate structure, not as a sixth number.

## SECTION 3: SAM & SOM

### 3A. SAM

Management's own SAM definition already embeds the relevant filters:

1. **Product fit**: TAM ($16.9bn, all broadcast/streaming/CTV-ad spend
   including legacy hardware, in-house builds and non-software services) →
   SAM narrows to the cloud-software/SaaS-addressable layer only = $5.1bn
   (69.8% of TAM excluded — legacy hardware capex, DIY/in-house systems,
   non-software services).
2. **Geography**: SAM is already global, matching Amagi's global footprint;
   no further subtraction (Amagi is present in every region the SAM covers,
   though under-indexed in some — a growth lever, not an exclusion, Section
   5D).
3. **Channel**: SAM assumes cloud/API/SaaS delivery, matching Amagi's GTM
   (direct enterprise sales + AWS Marketplace + platform partnerships); no
   further subtraction.
4. **Customer**: SAM covers broadcasters, content owners, OTT/FAST/CTV
   operators — matches Amagi's disclosed customer base (400+ content
   owners/platforms); excludes sub-enterprise indie/creator-tier channels,
   consistent with Amagi's enterprise focus, already embedded in the SAM
   definition.
5. **Capability**: Amagi's product portfolio spans all three SAM funnels
   (Cloud Modernization, Streaming Unification, Monetization & Marketplace)
   — full capability match, no further subtraction.

**SAM = $5.1bn (CY2024) = ₹48,705 Cr.** SAM as % of TAM = 48,705 ÷ 161,395 =
**30.2%.**

### 3B. SOM at 3 and 5 years

**Current share of SAM.** Amagi FY26 revenue ≈$173m (see FX note). SAM
trended to "today" (mid-2026, +1.5yr from the CY2024 base at 13.3% CAGR) =
$5.1bn × (1.133)^1.5 ≈ $6.14bn. Current SAM share = 173 ÷ 6,140 = **2.8%.**

**Share-gain trajectory.** Amagi's revenue CAGR (23% cc / 30% reported,
FY26) is meaningfully faster than the market's 13.3% SAM CAGR — genuine
share gain, not just riding the market — and is happening in a market the
company itself describes as fragmented with legacy hardware-first
incumbents (Evertz, Grass Valley, Harmonic) still transitioning to cloud
(Harmonic's FY2025 total revenue fell 47% YoY, consistent with legacy
hardware-first vendors losing ground — an ambiguous but directionally
supportive signal, flagged for monitoring in Section 4B). Per the
share-gain rules, this sits at the **upper end of the "normal" 1-2pp/3yr
band** rather than the "aggressive" tier (no competitor exit or acquisition
evidenced; capacity is not a constraint per 3C) — chosen deliberately over
a more aggressive assumption to stay consistent with the conservative-bias
instruction and with the fact that Amagi's own YoY growth is already
showing mild deceleration (FY24→FY25 +32.3%, FY25→FY26 +29.5%).

- **3yr**: current share 2.8% + 1.0pp = 3.8% of SAM(yr3). SAM(yr3) ≈
  CY2029P disclosed figure, $9.6bn (directly sourced, ~3 years from run
  date). SOM_3yr = 3.8% × $9.6bn = **$364.8m = ₹3,484 Cr.**
- **5yr**: current share 2.8% + 2.0pp = 4.8% of SAM(yr5). SAM(yr5)
  extrapolates 2 years beyond the disclosed CY2029P horizon at the same
  13.3% CAGR (an unverified extension beyond the sourced forecast window,
  flagged as an input gap): $9.6bn × (1.133)^2 ≈ $12.3bn. SOM_5yr = 4.8% ×
  $12.3bn = **$590.4m = ₹5,638 Cr.**

**Implied revenue CAGR (arithmetic, from FY26 revenue ₹1,506 Cr)**:
- 3yr: (3,484 ÷ 1,506)^(1/3) − 1 = **32.3%**
- 5yr: (5,638 ÷ 1,506)^(1/5) − 1 = **30.2%**

Both sit close to, and the 5yr figure just below, Amagi's own current
reported growth rate (29.5% FY26 YoY) — a self-consistent, credible SOM that
does not require the company to accelerate beyond its demonstrated
trajectory, appropriate for a formal handoff to Stage 11's revenue-growth
cross-check.

### 3C. Capacity cross-check

B07 capex-embedded-growth is **not applicable** — asset-light SaaS, capex is
a rounding error (B04: PP&E 1.2% of total assets). The only relevant
capacity commitment is the **AWS Technology Agreement: ₹24,176.20 million
(≈₹2,417.6 Cr) over 6 years (May 2025-Apr 2031)**, averaging ~₹403 Cr/yr.

This is a **minimum-spend floor for volume/discount pricing, not a usage
cap** — cloud infrastructure scales elastically with AWS; there is no
physical ceiling analogous to factory capacity. Cross-checking against the
SOM: at SOM_5yr revenue (₹5,638 Cr) and the disclosed FY25 technology &
cloud-infrastructure-plus-support cost ratio (28.59% of revenue, B04,
upper-bound proxy since it bundles cloud infra with support costs), implied
annual cloud/infra-linked cost in that single year ≈ ₹1,612 Cr — well above
the AWS commitment's *annual average* of ₹403 Cr, but that is expected and
unproblematic: the commitment is a floor, and actual usage-based spend
already runs several multiples of it today (FY25 cost base of 28.59% × FY25
revenue ≈ ₹332 Cr on cloud+support alone). **No capacity gap; both SOM and
the AWS commitment are consistent** — the AWS agreement was structured
knowing revenue would scale well past the floor.

**capacity_check = sufficient.** The real constraint on SOM achievability
is GTM/execution capacity (sales, customer success, R&D throughput to win
and onboard enterprise logos) — not infrastructure. One genuine risk worth
naming alongside this, already flagged in B04: AWS can terminate the
agreement on 2 years' notice with **no reciprocal notice obligation** on
Amagi's side — a hyperscaler-dependency risk that could indirectly disrupt
SOM delivery if triggered, distinct from the capacity math itself.

- **Entries/exits**: no major disclosed entrant/exit events beyond the

### 5B. Runway assessment

- **Revenue headroom** = SAM ÷ current revenue = ₹48,705 Cr ÷ ₹1,506 Cr =
