## SECTION 3: THE BULL CASE (Why anyone would buy)

Presented in full and weighed honestly. Every positive is real; none of them clears the valuation or governance gate at Rs 152.

**3A. Quality evidence.** Net cash Rs 138.93 Cr (cash Rs 208.57 Cr less borrowings Rs 69.64 Cr), Rs 31.25 per share, on a Rs 152 stock (B10). Zero promoter pledge, formally confirmed FY26 (B08). Asset-light model, capex only Rs 4.71 Cr FY26 (B10). Operational ROCE 16.2% after stripping Rs 179.22 Cr of undeployed IPO cash (deliberation ruling 16; B10). Counterweight: statutory ROCE is 11.25%, statutory ROE 9.14%, cumulative CFO/PAT FY23-FY26 is 8.1%, and FY26 CFO was Rs 0.48 Cr against PAT Rs 38.36 Cr, a 1.25% conversion (B01, B10). The clean balance-sheet line is IPO-funded, not operating-cash-generated (B07 FLAG-CASHCONVERSION).

1. Order book above Rs 900 Cr at 30-Jun-2026, 2.19x FY26 revenue, H2-weighted, 18-24 month conversion. Conviction M. Confirms on H2 FY27 revenue acceleration; kills on another guidance cut below 20% (B05 trigger 1).

## NARRATIVE (plain English)

Fabtech builds pharma factories in the Gulf and Africa on a turnkey basis, and it does the job asset-light. The order book is real at more than Rs 900 Cr, the Saudi and Africa wins are documented, the balance sheet holds Rs 139 Cr of net cash, there is no promoter pledge, and domestic institutions have been nudging their stake up. That is the whole of the good news, and it is genuine.

The problem is the price and the plumbing. At Rs 152 the stock already trades at the exact 13x forward multiple the operator approved as its destination, so there is nothing left to re-rate into. To make 25% a year from here the company would have to grow earnings faster than 25% for three straight years, and both the market it can actually reach (about 14% growth) and its own mixed delivery record (guidance cut twice in two quarters) say that will not happen. Underneath, the cash does not convert (about one paisa of operating cash for every rupee of profit last year), and roughly 42 paise of every rupee of pre-tax profit is interest and forex on the IPO pile rather than money earned building plants. On top of that sits a promoter concern serious enough that the framework caps the name at AVOID on its own.

So the verdict is AVOID, and it is an honest one, not a reflexive one. The business is worth watching, because one fact could change the picture: if the IPO cash gets deployed, operational returns and the growth premium both lift together. But that same single lever is also the single point of failure. The place to get interested is not Rs 152. It is the Rs 63 to Rs 81 zone, and only if cash conversion has actually turned and the governance overhang has cleared. Until then, do nothing.
