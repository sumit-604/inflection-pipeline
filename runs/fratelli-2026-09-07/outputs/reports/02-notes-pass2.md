1. PRIORITY: ECL WRITE-BACK (Rs 250.77 lakh) vs GROSS BAD-DEBT WRITE-OFF
   (Rs 733.86 lakh) — RECONCILED
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Note 30 (Other Expenses, consolidated, p.176/179): the "Bad debts and short
recoveries" line reads, in full: Bad debts and short recoveries Rs 733.86
lakh (gross, FY26) / Nil (FY25); "Less: Provision adjusted out of above
written off" Rs (250.77) lakh (FY26) / Nil (FY25); NET line Rs 483.09 lakh
(FY26) vs Rs 49.29 lakh (FY25).

Reconciliation: the Rs 250.77 lakh is the SAME figure as "Amount written
back during the year" in the ECL allowance movement (Note 10(b), p.163,
and Note 36(C), p.186, which oddly uses the verb "written off" for the
identical Rs 250.77 lakh event that Note 10(b) calls "written back" — a
labelling inconsistency between two notes describing the same movement,
🟡). What actually happened: of the Rs 733.86 lakh of receivables
physically written off (removed from the books) in FY26, Rs 250.77 lakh
was covered by, and consumed, the PRE-EXISTING ECL allowance (which is why
the balance-sheet provision fell from Rs 858.81 lakh to Rs 608.04 lakh with
"Amount provided for during the year" showing Nil — the provision shrank
because it was drawn down against an actual write-off, not because risk
improved). The REMAINING Rs 483.09 lakh of the Rs 733.86 lakh gross
write-off (733.86 - 250.77 = 483.09) had NO prior provision at all and hit
the P&L as a fresh, un-provisioned bad-debt charge — receivables that had
never been flagged doubtful were written off in full within the same
year, i.e. the ECL provide-then-write-off lifecycle was bypassed for the
larger part of this year's write-off. 🔴 FLAG: a Rs 4.83 cr direct,
zero-notice bad debt charge (9.8x FY25's Rs 0.49 cr) sitting alongside an
IMPROVING ageing mix (Pass 1, Section 4) and a FALLING credit-impaired
balance is an internally tense combination — it reads as either a
customer whose exposure was concentrated in the "not due"/"<6 months"
buckets going bad suddenly, or an out-of-cycle judgemental write-off not
signalled by the ageing schedule at all.

Cross-reference worth flagging as a hypothesis, NOT a confirmed fact (the
notes do not name any customer or link the two disclosures): the single
customer contributing to Group revenue fell from Rs 5,243.04 lakh (17.3%
of revenue) in FY25 to Rs 2,103.64 lakh (11.6%) in FY26 (Note 37, p.188,
already flagged in Pass 1) — a Rs 3,139.40 lakh revenue drop from one
customer in the same year a Rs 733.86 lakh gross bad-debt write-off
occurred. A lost or downsized large customer leaving bad debt behind is a
plausible, unconfirmed explanation and should be a direct management
question rather than an inferred fact.

2. PRIORITY: DIRECTORS' REPORT FY25 COMPARATIVE LOSS FIGURES — RESOLVED,
   MATERIAL INTERNAL INCONSISTENCY FOUND
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

🔴 MAJOR NEW FINDING. The Directors' Report "KEY FINANCIAL HIGHLIGHTS"
table (Annual_Report_FY2026.pdf, p.42, printed p.39) shows, for
"Consolidated FY 2024-25": Revenue from Operations Rs 17,844.09 lakh, Loss
Before Tax Rs (1,759.79) lakh, Loss After Tax Rs (1,283.82) lakh. These
figures do NOT match the AUDITED Consolidated Statement of Profit and Loss
FY25 comparative reported later in the SAME annual report (Annual
Report_FY2026.pdf, p.177 area / Financial Statements section): Revenue
from operations Rs 30,209.66 lakh, Loss before tax Rs (2,269.40) lakh,
Loss after tax Rs (1,706.26) lakh.

Two independent checks prove this is an error in the Directors' Report,
not a matter of a different (equally valid) basis:

(a) ARITHMETIC: the Directors' Report "Consolidated FY25" figures equal
the TRUE audited consolidated FY25 figures MINUS the standalone parent's
own FY25 figures, exactly: Revenue 30,209.66 - 12,471.59 (standalone FY25
revenue, same table) = 17,738.07 (close to 17,844.09; the residual Rs
106.02 lakh gap matches the intra-Group elimination Pass 1 noted at Note
37). PBT: 2,269.40 - 509.61 (standalone FY25 PBT loss, same table) =
1,759.79 EXACTLY, matching the "Consolidated" figure shown. PAT: 1,706.26
- 422.45 (standalone FY25 PAT loss) = 1,283.81, matching the Rs 1,283.82
shown (rounding).

(b) CROSS-STATEMENT: Note 48 "Additional information pursuant to Schedule
III" (Annual_Report_FY2026.pdf, p.200-201) discloses Fratelli Wines Pvt
Ltd's own FY25 "Share in loss" as Rs (1,283.81) lakh, 75.24% of the FY25
consolidated total loss of Rs 1,706.26 lakh. This is the exact figure the
Directors' Report mislabels as the total "Consolidated" FY25 loss — the
Directors' Report has, in substance, reported the WINE SUBSIDIARY'S OWN
standalone contribution to the consolidated result, omitting the parent's
own standalone loss and any consolidation adjustments, and labelled it
"Consolidated."

(c) PRIOR-YEAR CHECK: the FY2025 AR's own Directors' Report (Annual_
Report_FY2025.pdf, p.44, printed p.41) reported the CORRECT true
consolidated FY25 figures in the equivalent table one year ago: Revenue
Rs 30,209.66 lakh, PBT Rs (2,269.40) lakh, PAT Rs (1,706.26) lakh — proving
the company knows how to state this correctly and did so last year. This
year's Directors' Report substituted a different, smaller number set for
the same audited fact pattern.

CONSEQUENCE: the Directors' Report narrative claims "revenue from
operations on a consolidated basis stood at Rs 18,128.65 lakhs, compared
with Rs 17,844.09 lakhs in the previous financial year, representing an
increase of 1.59%" (p.42/39). Against the TRUE consolidated FY25 revenue
of Rs 30,209.66 lakh, the real YoY movement is a DECLINE of 40.0% (Rs
30,209.66 lakh to Rs 18,128.65 lakh), driven by the wind-down of the
legacy agro/steel trading book (already flagged in Pass 1, Finding 1, as a
segment-note-level observation; this finding shows the SAME distortion has
migrated into the headline Directors' Report growth claim itself, which a
reader would ordinarily trust without checking the segment note). This is
a disclosure-quality finding of the first order: the single most visible
number in the annual report (the Directors' Report's own "1.59% growth"
claim) is not comparable on the stated basis.

3. PRIORITY: PROMOTER WARRANT LAPSE — SHARE COUNT AND MONEY TRACED;
   INJECTED "39.5%" FIGURE IS WRONG
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

🔴 CORRECTION TO INJECTED FACT (same pattern as Pass 1's Other Expenses
correction). Note 13(e) standalone (Annual_Report_FY2026.pdf, p.108-109)
and Note 15(e) consolidated (p.166): of the 5,57,650 fully convertible
warrants allotted to promoters on 23-Aug-2024 at Rs 300 each (25% upfront
= Rs 75/warrant, balance 75% = Rs 225/warrant due on conversion, 18-month
SEBI ICDR conversion window expiring 22-Feb-2026):
- CONVERTED: 1,94,500 warrants (34.9% of the total, NOT 60.5% as the
  injected "39.5% forfeited" figure implies).
- LAPSED/FORFEITED: 3,63,150 warrants (65.1% of the total) — the majority,
  not a minority, of the promoter commitment walked away.

The injected "39.5%" is therefore WRONG and materially UNDERSTATES the
severity of the promoter walk-away; the corrected figure is 65.1%
forfeited. Treat 39.5% as disproven, in line with the standing correction
protocol already established for the Other Expenses figure in Pass 1.

Money traced: total committed value if fully converted = 5,57,650 x Rs300
= Rs 1,672.95 lakh. Actually received: Rs 418.24 lakh (25% upfront on ALL
5,57,650 warrants, FY25) + Rs 437.63 lakh (balance 75% on the 1,94,500
CONVERTED warrants only, FY26) = Rs 855.87 lakh total cash in across both
years. Money contractually available but NEVER brought in due to the
lapse = 3,63,150 x Rs225 = Rs 817.09 lakh — almost exactly equal to the
amount that WAS brought in, i.e. the promoters brought in barely half of
what they originally committed to. The forfeited 25% deposit on the
lapsed warrants (Rs 272.36 lakh) was retained by the company but credited
to Capital Reserve (Note 16, p.167), not to share capital or securities
premium — zero shares were issued against it.

WHO exercised and who lapsed: the standalone RPT note (Note 30(B)(i),
p.118-119) discloses conversion proceeds by name for FY26: Gaurav Sekhri
(MD) Rs 67.50 lakh, Aarti Sekhri Rs 67.50 lakh, Puja Sekhri Rs 67.50 lakh,
Chin Min Developers Pvt Ltd Rs 67.50 lakh, Shobha Sekhri Rs 167.63 lakh —
summing to Rs 437.63 lakh, i.e. these five named related parties account
for the ENTIRE 1,94,500-warrant conversion (30,000 warrants each for the
first four, 74,500 for Shobha Sekhri, at Rs225/warrant balance). This is
independently corroborated by the 5%-plus shareholder table (Note 13(f),
p.109): Aarti Sekhri's holding rose by exactly 30,000 shares (42,66,256 to
42,96,256) and Puja Sekhri's by exactly 30,000 shares (33,16,821 to
33,46,821) between FY25 and FY26 — matching the conversion counts exactly.
Because these five named related parties converted their ENTIRE
disclosed allotment, the 3,63,150 lapsed warrants must belong to OTHER,
non-related-party (or at least non-KMP-disclosed) allottees of the same
23-Aug-2024 preferential issue, whom the notes do not name. 🟡 worth a
management question: who were the other warrant holders and why did they
not convert.

4. PRIORITY: GOING CONCERN / DTA REVERSAL vs Rs 114.50 CR GUARANTEE
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

🔴 NEW FINDING. The standalone Going Concern note (Note 39, p.129-130) and
its consolidated equivalent (Note 44, p.198) both discuss the Holding
Company's revenue decline, its "adequate surplus funds and liquid
resources," and management's plan to pursue "new business opportunities,"
concluding a going-concern basis is appropriate. NEITHER note mentions,
discusses, or stress-tests the Rs 114.50 cr corporate guarantee the
Holding Company gave to the wine subsidiary's bankers in the SAME year
(Pass 1, Finding 2) — the single largest financial commitment on the
standalone balance sheet is absent from the standalone entity's own
going-concern assessment. This matters because the Holding Company's own
CASH balance is Rs 9.24 lakh (standalone, unchanged FY25 to FY26, per the
Directors' Report, p.42/39) and consolidated Group cash fell 84.5% to just
Rs 7.78 lakh at FY26-end (Note 38 consolidated Capital Management, p.189,
vs Rs 50.09 lakh FY25) against Rs 119.74 cr of Group borrowings — if the
guarantee were ever called, the Holding Company has no liquid resources of
its own to meet it; its guarantee capacity rests entirely on the paper
value of its own investment in the entity it is guaranteeing, a circular
support structure the going-concern note is silent on. 🟡 the "adequate
surplus funds" language in Note 39/44 is not reconciled anywhere against
this Rs 7.78-9.24 lakh actual cash position.

Separately, the auditor's CONSOLIDATED report (Annual_Report_FY2026.pdf,
p.~131-132 of the audit report section) carries an explicit "Emphasis of
Matter" drawing attention to Note 44 on the Holding Company's revenue
decline, opinion "not modified" — and the IDENTICAL paragraph is then
repeated verbatim under a separate "Key Audit Matters" heading immediately
below it (an apparent drafting duplication: the same text appears under
two different report headings that are supposed to serve different
purposes under SA 701/SA 706). By contrast, the STANDALONE auditor's
report for the same entity, same year, states explicitly "there are no
key audit matters to be communicated" — i.e. the auditor treated the
consolidated and standalone views of the identical underlying fact
(revenue decline, going concern) differently across the two opinions in
the same signed report set. 🟡 disclosure-quality / audit-documentation
observation, not an opinion qualification.

The DTA reversal (Pass 1 Finding 4) is corroborated at the standalone
level with full component detail (Note 8, p.104-105): the entire Rs 353.46
lakh DTA balance was reversed to Nil, composed of Provision for doubtful
debts Rs 88.04 lakh, Bought-forward losses Rs 241.39 lakh, PP&E timing
differences Rs 10.29 lakh (opening year only), employee benefits Rs 4.27
lakh and Others Rs 9.47 lakh — a full, unqualified reversal across every
DTA component, not a partial write-down of one questionable item. This
detail sharpens rather than changes Pass 1's finding: management's own
tax note is now unambiguously stating zero probability of future
standalone taxable profits over any recoverable horizon, a more
pessimistic and specific judgement than the going-concern note's language
in the same document.

5. ADDITIONAL NEW FINDINGS FROM THE FULL RE-READ
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

- 🔴 Note 39(A)(iii) consolidated (p.189-190; standalone equivalent Note
  35(A)(iii), p.127): a penalty order under Section 270 of the Income Tax
  Act "for misreporting of income" amounting to Rs 663.03 lakh (Rs 6.63
  cr) was issued for AY 2018-19, on appeal to CIT(Appeals); management
  "does not expect any liability." This specific item sits inside the
  aggregate "Income Tax cases Rs 11.49 cr" figure Pass 1 already totalled,
  but Pass 1 did not name it. A formal "misreporting of income" penalty
  order (Section 270A category) is qualitatively more serious than an
  ordinary quantum dispute and is worth naming to the operator separately
  from the aggregate.
- 🟡 Note 45 consolidated, "Pre-operative expenditure attributable to
  bearer plants pending to be capitalised" (p.196-197) — a note Pass 1 did
  not cover at all. The uncapitalised cost pool grew from Rs 157.21 lakh
  (FY25 closing) to Rs 278.50 lakh (FY26 closing), +77.1% YoY, with ZERO
  capitalisation to Bearer Plants in FY26 (vs Rs 44.15 lakh capitalised in
  FY25). The pool includes capitalised interest of Rs 43.82 lakh and
  capitalised depreciation of Rs 36.62 lakh added straight to the
  carry-forward balance rather than expensed. This is legitimate
  agricultural accounting for vineyards not yet at commercial maturity,
  but a rising, non-crystallising balance for two straight years, with
  capitalisation stalling to zero this year, is worth watching for future
  impairment risk if the underlying vines take longer to mature than
  planned.
- 🟡 Standalone-only Note 37 "Financial Ratios" (p.126-129, mandatory Ind
  AS/Schedule III ratio disclosure Pass 1 did not cite) shows extreme
  standalone-level ratio deterioration explained entirely by the
  wind-down of the parent's own trading book: Net Profit Ratio -1338.08%
  (from -3.39%), Debt Service Coverage Ratio -0.83 (from -0.07, variance
  +1,079%), Trade Receivables Turnover down 97.6%, Trade Payables
  Turnover down 84.8%, each with a management remark attributing it to
  "decline in operating activities." No new fact beyond what Pass 1
  already established qualitatively, but this is the company's own
  quantified admission, in a mandatory disclosure table, of the scale of
  the standalone entity's collapse — useful as a direct citation rather
  than an inferred conclusion.
- 🟡 Both the standalone and consolidated "Additional Regulatory
  Information" notes (Note 43(k) standalone p.131-132; Note 47(j)
  consolidated p.196-197) and the Independent Auditor's Report itself
  (both standalone and consolidated, p.~124/131) disclose that the Tally
  Prime accounting software's audit-trail (edit log) feature does not
  operate "at the database level" — a known industry-wide Tally
  limitation, not unique to this company, but a technical item of
  non-compliance with the MCA's audit-trail mandate that the auditor
  independently echoes in their own report rather than relying solely on
  management's representation.
- 🟢 New, reassuring: the standalone auditor's report states explicitly
  that "the Company has not raised loans during the year on the pledge of
  securities held in its subsidiary" (clause 3(ix)(f) of the CARO Order,
  p.~120) — no promoter or company share pledge financing structure is in
  place. Worth noting as a genuine clean item given the corporate
  guarantee and related-party lending activity flagged elsewhere.
- 🟡 Consolidated cash and cash equivalents fell 84.5% YoY, from Rs 50.09
  lakh (FY25) to Rs 7.78 lakh (FY26) (Note 38, Capital Management, p.189)
  — a number Pass 1 did not state in absolute terms (Pass 1 covered the
  ratio movements but not the underlying cash balance). Against Rs 119.74
  cr of Group borrowings, the Group is running with what is, in practical
  terms, no cash buffer of its own, entirely dependent on the cash-credit
  facility renewing on demand (Pass 1, Finding 6).
