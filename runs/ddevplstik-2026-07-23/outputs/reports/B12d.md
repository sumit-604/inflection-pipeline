## 1. Was the peer-fit judgment (Apcotex is not a real comparable) defensible?

Yes, on the central point. I re-read all four APCOTEXIND transcripts independently and
grepped them for DDev's core product/segment vocabulary (cable, wire, XLPE, HFFR, "PVC
compound", BESS, solar cable, DDev/DDEV). Apcotex operates in synthetic latex (SBR,
styrene-acrylic), nitrile latex, and NBR solid rubber sold into paper, carpet, construction
chemicals, textile, tire and medical-glove end markets — confirmed directly from the
transcripts (e.g. APCOTEXIND Q2 FY26 call, 6-Nov-2025, Q&A with Rudraksh Raheja: "the
contribution still remains at about 30:70... latex... paper and construction... 16% to
18%. Carpet textiles... 11% to 12%. Nitrile latex... 15% to 16%. Tires, tire cord... 10%").
None of this is cable & wire, HFFR/XLPE compounding, or BESS pack/module assembly. B06's
core conclusion — four of six B05 questions are UNVERIFIABLE for genuine peer-fit reasons,
not because DDev's claims are weak — is well supported and is not an effort-avoidance
shortcut: the report shows real, extensive engagement with all four transcripts (specific,
verified quotes throughout, an honest Part 5 "no cross-peer hypothesis" call rather than a
forced one, and a materially informative negative finding on Q4 capex).

However, one specific factual claim underpinning this judgment does NOT hold up, detailed
in Finding 2 below: B06 states "never once" for a list of keywords including "battery"
across all four transcripts. That specific claim is false; "battery" appears in the
Q1 FY26 (31-Jul-2025) call as well, in a directly claim-relevant context that was never
folded into the Q4 analysis.

anything (no product detail, and stage 6's injected inputs per its own prompt file are

with B06's execution) — but it is a genuine, sourced, unused data point that a synthesis
stage or a future run with a BEPL transcript should be pointed toward. Flagged as MINOR,
scoping caveat noted.

## FINDINGS

**Finding 1 — MAJOR — Unanchored/fabricated BEPL business characterization, doubly
mis-cited.**
Location: B06.md lines 88 and 119 (also B06-peers.yaml peer_coverage_map BEPL row, which
inherits the same claim via B06.md's prose even though the YAML row itself is terser).
B06 states: "BEPL, which per its screening CSV is a synthetic rubber/latex/emulsion
polymer producer, is at least nominally closer to some of DDev's polymer-processing
character than Apcotex" and, in Part 3: "Per Stage 5, BEPL is nominally the more
product-adjacent of the two named peers (synthetic rubber/latex/emulsion polymer)."
I checked both cited sources directly:
- The screening CSV: every BEPL-*.csv file in `inputs/screening/` was read in full. None
  contains any product/business description text at all — only numeric financial-statement
  rows (Sales, Raw Material Cost, Net Block, CWIP, etc.) and Screener.in boilerplate. The
  words "rubber", "latex", "emulsion", "ABS", "synthetic" appear zero times across all
  twelve screening CSVs in this run (grep-confirmed).
- Stage 5 (B05.md / B05-concall.yaml): grep-confirmed zero occurrences of "rubber",
  "latex", "emulsion", "ABS", "synthetic", or "Bhansali" anywhere in either file. Stage 5
  names BEPL only as an assigned peer ticker, with no business description.
So the specific characterization is attributed to two sources, neither of which actually
contains it — a fabricated anchor presented as sourced fact. This also happens to be
substantively questionable on general-knowledge grounds (Bhansali Engineering Polymers is
publicly known primarily as an ABS/SAN engineering-resin producer, not a rubber/latex/
emulsion-polymer company), though that external check is outside this run's anchored
evidence and is noted only as a caveat, not as the basis for the finding. The finding
itself rests entirely on the two false in-run citations. This matters because the claim
directly inflates the "genuine coverage gap"/"genuine blind spot" framing around BEPL that
Part 1 Q4 and Q6 carry forward, and that framing is likely to propagate into synthesis
uncritically if not caught.

**Finding 2 — MAJOR — "Never once" claim about Apcotex's vocabulary is factually false;
a directly claim-relevant peer statement was left unused inside a SUBSTANTIVE-marked
transcript.**
Location: B06.md lines 18-19 ("Across all four transcripts, Apcotex never once uses the
words cable, wire, XLPE, HFFR, PVC compound, BESS, battery, solar cable, or DDev") and the
Q4 answer (B06.md lines 61-69), which cites only the Q4 FY26 (7-May-2026) "battery
binders" R&D mention as the closest linguistic proximity to "battery" in the dataset.
This is incorrect. The word "battery" also appears in the APCOTEXIND Q1 FY26 call
(31-Jul-2025, filed 5-Aug-2025), Q&A between Dhaval Shah and Abhiraj Choksey (textcache
`inputs__peer-concalls__APCOTEXIND-Concall_Aug_2025_Transcript.txt`, lines 413-421):
"Sir, does your product go in for the application of this, new-age manufacturing... [PCBA/
electronics]... Anywhere there is an application of your product?" / "There is an
application for, in batteries, in these car batteries, I think specialty application.
Unfortunately, it is not a big market in India. I think, the large battery plants are in
China and Japan, US... it is not a big market in our geographic strengths."
This is a directly claim-relevant statement to Q4 (peer capex/entry eroding DDev's
competitive runway in the battery-adjacent space) that was never surfaced anywhere in
B06's Part 1 Q4 answer, Part 2 cross-read, or Part 4/5 summary — even though the Q1 FY26
transcript is marked SUBSTANTIVE in the Part 3 coverage map and the report claims to have
read it "in full." It also directly falsifies the specific "never once" wording used to
support the "zero overlap" framing. This does not flip Q4's overall verdict (the
application is described by Apcotex's own management as small, geography-mismatched, and
unrelated to BESS pack/module assembly — arguably even less concerning than the Q4 FY26
"battery binders" mention already cited), but the report's own claim of exhaustiveness
("closest linguistic proximity... in the entire dataset") is demonstrably incomplete, and
a careful reader relying on B06's "never once" framing would be misled about how clean the
zero-overlap finding actually is.
