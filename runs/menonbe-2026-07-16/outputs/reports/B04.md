## SECTION 1: THE BUSINESS MODEL IN PLAIN ENGLISH
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

### 1A One-line description

Menon Bearings makes the small, precision-machined metal parts that sit *inside* engines, gearboxes and
brakes — bi-metal bearings, aluminium die-cast components, and brake friction material — and sells them
to vehicle and engine makers (OEMs), their aftermarket dealer networks, and export customers, earning a
manufacturing margin on parts too specialised and approval-intensive for most buyers to make in-house
(Board's Report, "engaged in the business of manufacturing of Auto Components," AR p.35/45; Inv. Pres.
slide 19).

### 1E The chai-stall-uncle version

Think of Menon Bearings like the person who makes the exact bushings and washers that keep a
diesel engine's crankshaft spinning smoothly for ten years without seizing — nobody outside the factory
ever sees the part, but the tractor or truck simply will not run without it. It took decades to get
"approved" by the big engine makers (John Deere, Tata Motors), the way a trusted family mechanic earns
your business over years, not one good repair. Now it is also branching out — casting aluminium parts
(like a second kitchen making a different dish, aluminium instead of steel-copper alloy) and making brake
linings (a third kitchen, still small). The tricky part: raw material (steel, aluminium) prices swing, and
Menon can only raise its own prices with a delay after signing new contracts — so when metal gets
expensive, there's a lag before Menon gets paid back for it, like a shopkeeper who agreed last month's
price with a supplier and can't reprice the shelf overnight.

### 2D Industry lifecycle stage

Indian auto-component industry: mature-but-still-growing (Indian auto component industry "estimated to
cross US$95-100 billion in turnover" FY27, MD&A AR p.54). Menon's own three sub-businesses sit at
different lifecycle points: Bi-Metal bearings is a **mature, cash-generative core** (oligopoly position,
established margins); Alkop is in a **scale-up/growth** phase (capacity +40% machining expansion in-year,
targeted revenue ₹40cr→₹95cr by FY27, Inv. Pres. slide 33); Braking is in an **early ramp/emerging** phase
(sub-scale, pre-Railways-approval, target margin not yet achieved). This is a company using a mature core
to fund two growth call options — a structurally sensible capital-allocation pattern, though one that
currently shows up as rising consolidated capex and working-capital intensity rather than free cash flow
(B03 Phase 3).

| Cost drivers | Steel/aluminium/metal-sheet commodity prices (45-50% of operating cost, CRISIL rationale p.2); energy costs (mitigated by in-year solar/energy-efficiency capex, Inv. Pres. slide 16); labour (219 permanent staff, AR p.~63) |
| Incremental margin / operating leverage | FY26 showed genuine positive operating leverage at the standalone level: revenue +28.5% while employee cost grew only +6.4% and operating expense +22.1% (both below revenue growth), driving PBT growth of +60.6% (B03 Phase 3C) — consistent with a capacity-utilisation story where newly added fixed capacity absorbs incremental volume at a lower marginal cost, though part of the FY26 margin improvement is separately explained by the Alkop-hive-off accounting effect (Board's Report Item 14, AR p.60) rather than pure operating leverage, so the two effects should not be conflated when projecting forward incremental margins |

### 4B Valuation method applicability

| Method | Applicability to this business | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| EV/EBITDA | Strong fit — capital-intensive, cyclical manufacturer with an active, debt-funded capex cycle across three divisions of different margin/scale; EV/EBITDA is capital-structure- and depreciation-timing-neutral, which matters given CWIP just converted to operating assets (₹9.10cr→₹0.09cr, B03 Phase 3B) and interest cost is rising with the capex-linked borrowings | **PRIMARY** |
| P/E | Reasonable fit — earnings quality is reasonably clean this cycle (no exceptional items either year, DuPont-confirmed margin-driven not leverage-driven ROE improvement, B03 Phase 3B), and P/E is the metric most peer small-cap auto-ancillary comps and the operator's own screening likely use; but standalone-vs-consolidated and Alkop-hive-off distortions (Board's Report Item 14, AR p.60) mean any P/E multiple must be applied to *consolidated, normalised* earnings, not a raw current-year print | **SECONDARY** |
| Sum-of-the-parts (SOTP) by segment (Bearings/Alkop/Brakes) | Directionally attractive given three genuinely distinct margin/lifecycle profiles (Bearings mature ~22% margin core; Alkop scale-up ~21% target; Brakes early-ramp ~12% target, Inv. Pres. slide 33) — but there is **no audited segment P&L** to build it on (single-segment accounting policy, AR p.107); any SOTP must rely entirely on unaudited Investor Presentation revenue splits and management-guided margin targets, which caps its reliability to a cross-check, not a primary valuation | **TERTIARY** |
| Not applicable / not primary | **DCF** — near-term FCF is genuinely unstable (rising WC intensity, active multi-year capex across three divisions, FY26 net cash flow −₹7.40cr consolidated) making multi-year free-cash-flow forecasts unreliable inputs right now; **Dividend Discount Model** — flat ₹2/share dividend, ~15-20% payout, this is a growth-reinvestment story not an income one; **Asset-based/replacement value** — not a distressed, holdco, or break-up situation | listed as not_applicable |

**Cycle stage that matters for valuation**: this is a **mid-capex-cycle** story — Bi-Metal and Braking
capacity increases are already commissioned (FY26), Alkop capex continues into FY27-28 (Inv. Pres.
slide 18); valuation timing should weigh whether the market is pricing in utilisation catch-up on capacity
already built (Bi-Metal/Braking) versus capacity still being built (Alkop), and whether the FY26 standalone
receivables/cash-conversion stretch (B02/B03 top finding) is a one-year capex-linked feature or the start
of a structural WC deterioration — the next 2-3 quarters of Note 31 ageing data and CFO/PAT trend are the
key inputs to that judgment (Stage 11's remit per the pipeline's Section 1B v3.3 exit-multiple framework).

### 4C Quarterly monitoring checklist

1. Consolidated revenue growth vs. prior-year quarter, and vs. delivered capacity growth (Inv. Pres.
   slides 8-9) — good: growth tracking/exceeding capacity growth; trouble: growth lagging capacity added.
2. Consolidated EBITDA margin (Inv. Pres. slide 8) — good: holding at or above the 18.65-20.39% CRISIL
   two-year band; trouble: sustained fall below ~18%.
3. Segment/product revenue mix (Bimetal/Alkop/Braking %, Inv. Pres. slide 10) — good: stable-to-rising
   Bearings share or Alkop margin catching up; trouble: rising low-margin mix without margin convergence.
4. Export revenue % of total (Inv. Pres. slide 11) — good: tracking toward the guided 37% FY27 level;
   trouble: stalling or reversing.
5. Standalone receivables ageing (>6-month bucket %, DSO) — good: reverting toward FY25's 6.8%/85 days;
   trouble: continued rise past FY26's 22.2%/114 days.
6. Consolidated CFO/PAT ratio — good: recovery toward/above 100%; trouble: a second consecutive
   sub-70% year.
7. Consolidated working capital as % of revenue — good: stabilising near FY25's ~25%; trouble: continued
   rise past FY26's ~34.7%.
8. Consolidated cash & bank balance trend — good: stabilising; trouble: a third consecutive year of decline
   (already down FY25 and FY26).
9. Alkop utilisation % and per-MT realisation, where disclosed in concalls — good: utilisation and
   realisation both rising toward peer levels; trouble: utilisation stalling below ~60% despite continuing
   capex.
10. Menon Alkop AOC-1 Reserves & Surplus (annual, but check interim commentary) — good: narrowing
    toward positive; trouble: further widening negative, raising Ind AS 36 impairment risk on the parent's
    ₹2,823.50L carrying cost.
11. Whether a formal Ind AS 37 Contingent Liabilities table appears, covering the ₹29cr guarantee —
    good: added; trouble: continued omission.
12. Interest coverage ratio and D/E (Board's Report Item 13) — good: holding near 0.25x D/E, >10x
    coverage; trouble: D/E rising past ~0.5x as capex continues.
13. Any KMP/leadership changes following the March-2026 MD+CFO transition — good: none; trouble:
    further turnover within 12 months.
14. CRISIL surveillance action referencing the final FY26 audited print (post 14-May-2026 board adoption)
    — good: reaffirmation citing audited FY26 receivables data; trouble: a downward rating action citing
    "sizeable stretch in the working capital cycle" (CRISIL's own listed downward sensitivity, rating
    rationale p.2).
15. Brakes division: dynamometer/Railways-approval status and any quantified revenue guidance replacing
    the current unquantified "significant turnover increase" language (MD&A, AR p.55).

### 4D Highest-value questions for management

1. What is driving the standalone-only receivables deterioration (>6-month bucket 6.8%→22.2%, DSO
   85→114 days), and has the ECL matrix been recalibrated? — **Reassures**: a small number of identified,
   collectible accounts with a credible collection timeline and a recalibrated ECL policy. **Worries**: a
   broad-based, unexplained stretch with no ECL response.
2. What is the current utilisation rate and per-MT realisation trend at Menon Alkop, and what is the
   credible path to closing the gap with "larger players" (₹4-4.5 lakh/MT vs. current ₹2.0-2.2 lakh/MT)? —
   **Reassures**: a specific product-mix/EV-parts roadmap with utilisation already rising. **Worries**: capex
   continuing with no utilisation or realisation improvement.
3. Why is there no Ind AS 37 Contingent Liabilities table for the ₹29cr Menon Alkop guarantee, and what
   is the group's total guarantee/dispute quantum? — **Reassures**: a straightforward disclosure-format gap
   to be fixed in FY27. **Worries**: reluctance to quantify or disclose the group's full contingent exposure.
4. Has the ₹2,823.50L Menon Alkop investment been tested for impairment given the subsidiary's negative
   reserves? — **Reassures**: a value-in-use test showing the investment is recoverable given the growth
   plan. **Worries**: no test performed, or a test that would show impairment if performed honestly.
5. What are the commercial terms of the ₹457.22L advance to Menon Brakes, and why did interest income
   fall 50.9% the same year the advance grew 44.9%? — **Reassures**: a disclosed, arm's-length interest
   rate/tenor consistent with a genuine working-capital facility. **Worries**: an indefinite, interest-free
   related-party subsidy with no repayment discipline.
6. What quantified revenue/margin milestone would confirm the Braking division's dynamometer/Railways
   investment is paying off, and by when? — **Reassures**: a specific number and date replacing "significant
   turnover increase." **Worries**: continued vague guidance despite ongoing capex.
7. Given the auto-sector concentration (80-90% of revenue, CRISIL) and CV/tractor-cycle exposure, what
   specific steps is management taking to diversify the end-market mix beyond exports and Alkop's
   EV-adjacent push? — **Reassures**: a credible, quantified diversification plan (oil & gas, industrial,
   railways). **Worries**: reliance on "exports growing" alone as the diversification answer.
