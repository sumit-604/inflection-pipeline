## SECTION 1: MARKET DEFINITION

### 1A — Precise boundaries

**Core compounding market**
- **Product scope**: polymer-based cable, wire, and allied compounds sold to third parties — PVC compounds, PE-based compounds (XLPE, Sioplas, Semicons, HFFR), engineering plastic compounds, and Antifab/filled compounds/masterbatch. Excludes the finished cable/wire product itself (DDev's customer market, not DDev's own market) and excludes compound produced and consumed captively/in-house by backward-integrated cable makers (not a third-party-addressable transaction).
- **Geographic scope**: India domestic consumption is the primary scope for the quantitative TAM below (matches the only third-party sizing found). DDev's ~24% export revenue (55+ countries) sits **outside** this domestic figure — see flag below; this makes the domestic-only TAM a conservative under-count of DDev's true addressable opportunity, consistent with the pipeline's conservative-bias instruction.
- **Customer scope**: cable and wire manufacturers (Apar, Havells, KEC, KEI, Paramount, Polycab ≈22% of DDev revenue per Inv. Pres. p.17), plus white-goods/FMEG, packaging, and footwear compound buyers (smaller Antifab/Engineering slice).
- **Channel scope**: direct B2B, short-cycle (≤90-day export contracts), no distributor layer of consequence.
- **Price segment**: full range, commodity PVC through certified high-voltage XLPE/HFFR.
- **Explicit exclusions**: finished cable/wire market (₹1.2–2.2 lakh Cr class, per Mordor/presentation — DDev's customer's market, not DDev's), captive/backward-integrated compounding, BESS (sized separately below).

**BESS market**
- **Product scope**: containerized lithium-ion (LFP, prismatic) BESS pack/module assembly and integration — cell procurement, pack/module build, BMS/EMS integration, containerization. Excludes land, civil works, grid interconnection, and EPC design/labour, which sit with developers/EPC contractors, not an assembly-model supplier like DDev (per B04's own description of DDev's "assembly-led model").
- **Geographic scope**: India (utility, C&I, data-centre, solar-plus-storage and standalone-storage segments); no export ambition disclosed yet.
- **Customer scope**: SECI, NTPC, POWERGRID, state utilities/DISCOMs, and EPC/integrators (Sterling & Wilson, Enrich, Orange, and ~30 named private counterparties per the Feb-2026 concall) — DDev sells DC/AC containers to these, it is not itself an IPP or EPC.
- **Explicit exclusion**: pumped-storage hydro (part of India's total storage target but a wholly different, non-DDev-addressable technology and value chain).

## SECTION 2: TAM ESTIMATION — CORE COMPOUNDING MARKET, MULTIPLE METHODS

### Method 1 — Top-down (India wire/cable compound market value)
Independent source (IndexBox, 2026 estimate, via web search): *"In 2026, the India Wire Compounds And Cable Compounds market is estimated to consume between 650,000 and 800,000 metric tonnes of formulated compounds, representing a market value of USD 1.8–2.2 billion... domestic compound production capacity is concentrated in Gujarat, Maharashtra, and Tamil Nadu, with an estimated 400,000–500,000 MT of annual compounding capacity."*
- $1.8 Bn × 9,600 = **₹17,280 Cr**
- $2.2 Bn × 9,600 = **₹21,120 Cr**
- **Method 1 range: ₹17,280–21,120 Cr (2026).** Confidence: **L** — single non-transparent aggregator source (IndexBox reports are template-generated market estimates, not a named analyst methodology); no second top-down source found specifically for the *compound* market (as opposed to the much larger *finished cable* market — see the flag under Section 2 cross-check below).

### Method 2 — Bottom-up (unit = 1 tonne of compound)
Total addressable volume: 650,000–800,000 MT (2026, same IndexBox source as Method 1 — not fully independent of Method 1, flagged).
Price per tonne: heterogeneous by mix.
- Conservative (commodity-weighted) floor: ₹60,000/tonne (approximated from IndiaMART bulk PVC/XLPE compound listings, ₹38–100/kg spot/retail range — itself a weak, small-lot-vendor price signal, flagged L confidence)
- Upper bound: DDev's own FY26 blended realised price, ₹1,45,136/tonne (Inv. Pres. p.26; DDev skews toward higher-value XLPE/HFFR mix vs. a PVC-heavy market average, so this is a deliberate upper bound, not a market average)
- 650,000 MT × ₹60,000/MT ÷ 1,00,00,000 = **₹3,900 Cr**
- 800,000 MT × ₹1,45,136/MT ÷ 1,00,00,000 = **₹11,611 Cr**
- **Method 2 range: ₹3,900–11,611 Cr.** Confidence: **L** (wide range reflects genuinely heterogeneous product mix; volume source shared with Method 1).

### Method 3 — Peer revenue aggregation (incl. unorganised estimate)
Named organised competitors found:
- DDev Plastiks: ₹2,953 Cr (FY26, audited, CRISIL rating + Q4 results)
- Shakun Polymers (part of Orbia, Mexico; cable/wire-focused compounder): revenue "over ₹500 Cr" (FY24, Tofler — **STALE, FY24 base**)
- KLJ Polymers and Chemicals Ltd: revenue ₹1,900 Cr (FY24, Tofler — **STALE, FY24 base**); KLJ's compound book spans plasticizers and PVC compounds for multiple end-uses beyond cable, so a 40–50% cable-allocation is assumed (**estimate, not a documented split** — NOT FOUND as a disclosed segment) → ₹760–950 Cr cable-attributable
- Organised top-3 subtotal: ₹2,953 + 500 + (760 to 950) ≈ **₹4,213–4,403 Cr**
- Unorganised-sector addition (pipeline convention: 30–60% of India market for this class of good; management itself names unorganised PVC-segment competition in the May-2026 concall): applying 40–60% unorganised share, Total = Organised ÷ (1 − unorg%)
  - At 40% unorganised: 4,300 ÷ 0.60 = **₹7,167 Cr**
  - At 60% unorganised: 4,300 ÷ 0.40 = **₹10,750 Cr**
- **Method 3 range: ~₹7,100–10,800 Cr.** Confidence: **L**, and **known to under-count** — IndexBox's own capacity note above ("400,000–500,000 MT of annual compounding capacity" spread across "various producers" in Gujarat/Maharashtra/Tamil Nadu) implies materially more organised capacity than the three named players capture; regional/smaller organised compounders are missing from this method by construction.

### Method 4 — Import substitution
**NOT usable / NOT FOUND** as a quantified subtraction. A real, named, but unquantified import-competition signal exists: *"in XLPE... the competition is from overseas players like Dow and Borealis"* (Rajesh Kothari, May-2026 concall) — this is qualitative evidence that a slice of India's high-voltage/EHV XLPE demand is still import-served, but no import-volume, import-value, or domestic-production-vs-import-split data was found in any injected document or via web search specific to cable compounds (as opposed to PVC resin feedstock, which is a different, upstream market). Carried as a qualitative risk/opportunity note in Section 4, not a TAM adjustment.

### Method 5 — Global benchmark
Global polymer compounding market (Inv. Pres. p.20, cross-checked): "$67 Bn (2023) → $115 Bn (2031E), 6.5% CAGR." A different-scope independent figure (Grand View Research, via web search): India *plastic* compounding market "$4,016.3 mn (2023) → $7,797.5 mn (2030E), 9.5% CAGR" — broader than cable-only (auto, packaging, appliance compounding included), directionally useful only.
Assuming wire/cable is 10–15% of global polymer-compounding demand (a standard large end-use segment) and India commands 5–8% of that global cable-compound pool (China+1/certification tailwinds argue for the higher end, India's still-modest share of global manufacturing argues for the lower end):
- Global cable-compound TAM: $67 Bn × 10% = $6.7 Bn; $115 Bn × 15% = $17.25 Bn
- India share: $6.7 Bn × 5% = $0.335 Bn; $17.25 Bn × 8% = $1.38 Bn
- $0.335 Bn × 9,600 = **₹3,216 Cr**; $1.38 Bn × 9,600 = **₹13,248 Cr**
- **Method 5 range: ₹3,216–13,248 Cr.** Confidence: **L** (three stacked assumption layers).

### Triangulation table (core, ₹ Cr)

| Method | Range | Confidence | Staleness |
|---|---|---|---|
| M1 Top-down (IndexBox) | 17,280–21,120 | L | Not stale (2026 basis) |
| M2 Bottom-up (volume×price) | 3,900–11,611 | L | Volume base shared with M1; price floor unsourced |
| M3 Peer aggregation | 7,100–10,800 | L | Peer revenues FY24-based (STALE, ~2yr old); known under-count |
| M4 Import substitution | NOT FOUND | — | — |
| M5 Global benchmark | 3,216–13,248 | L | RRKabel/GVR underlying reports 2023-vintage (STALE) |

**Divergence, explained, not averaged**: three of four usable methods (M2, M3, M5) cluster in a ₹3,200–13,300 Cr band; M1 (the only true purpose-built market-value figure, but single-sourced) sits materially higher at ₹17,280–21,120 Cr. This gap is symptomatic of genuine data scarcity for a niche India B2B intermediate-goods sub-sector — no CRISIL/ICRA/IBEF sector report specific to *cable compounds* (as distinct from the well-covered *finished cable* market) was found anywhere in this run. Illustrating the scope-confusion risk directly: Mordor Intelligence's own *finished* "India Wire & Cable Market" figure is $23.13 Bn (₹2,22,048 Cr) for 2026 — 10–20x the size of any of the *compound* estimates above, and ~18x DDev's own management-cited ₹1,20,000 Cr FY27E finished-cable chart, underscoring how differently "the market" gets defined across sources. Every core-TAM number in this report should be read as directional (L confidence throughout), not precise.

**Conservative estimate: ₹7,100 Cr** (M3 low bound — chosen because it is the most transparently *built* figure, even though it is a known under-count by construction, rather than M2's floor, which rests on an unsourced price assumption).
**Realistic estimate: ₹12,000 Cr** (near the convergence zone of M2-high/M3-high/M5-high, below M1's single-sourced outlier-high).

**Management's claim vs. conservative estimate**: the only internally-consistent way to monetise management's "~2.5 lakh tonnes, ~1/3rd share" claim is to price the *stated whole-market tonnage* at DDev's own realisation (since DDev's own 1/3-share arithmetic is already shown above to be broken and cannot itself be used as a multiplier without compounding the error): 2,50,000 MT × ₹1,45,136/tonne ÷ 1,00,00,000 = **₹3,628 Cr implied market-size claim**.
- **mgmt_claim_cr = ₹3,628 Cr**
- **mgmt_claim_ratio = 3,628 ÷ 7,100 (conservative estimate) = 0.51x**
- Per the standard rubric (>2x inflated, within 1.5x reasonable, below 1.5x "unusually conservative"), 0.51x reads as **"unusually conservative."** That read must **not** be mistaken for a positive credibility signal here: it is the arithmetic output of a **stale, internally-inconsistent statistic** (Section 1B), not evidence of deliberate management modesty — management's own claim, taken at face value, describes a smaller market than this report's own bottom-up/peer-aggregation/global-benchmark methods independently triangulate to. The qualitative finding (stale, internally inconsistent, unusable as a TAM anchor) is the load-bearing conclusion; the numeric ratio is reported for completeness only.

## SECTION 3: SAM & SOM

### 3A — SAM (core)
Applying the five filters to the **realistic TAM (₹12,000 Cr)**:
- **Product fit**: ~100% — TAM is already scoped to DDev's own product range (PVC/XLPE/Sioplas/Semicons/HFFR/Engineering/Antifab).
- **Geography**: ~100% — TAM is India-domestic only by construction (exports sit outside this figure, see 1A; no filter needed here since export upside is simply not counted, which is conservative).
- **Channel**: ~100% — market transacts B2B direct, matching DDev's own model.
- **Customer**: −15% — captive/backward-integrated compounding by large cable makers for commodity PVC (acknowledged directly by management, May-2026 concall: "a lot of people have gone for backward integration... producing their own compound") is not realistically capturable by DDev.
- **Capability**: −5% — DDev is not yet certified for the top of the voltage range (132kV certification targeted, 220kV still R&D per B07's optionality register; 440kV/HVDC undated) and does not yet hold all US UL listings (2 of 3 pending, targeted from Jun-2026).
- **Combined filter: 80% of TAM.**
- SAM (realistic) = 0.80 × ₹12,000 Cr = **₹9,600 Cr**
- SAM (conservative) = 0.80 × ₹7,100 Cr = ₹5,680 Cr
- **`sam_pct_of_tam` = 80%**

**Current SAM share**: ₹2,953 Cr (FY26 revenue) ÷ ₹9,600 Cr = **30.8%**. Sanity note: this happens to land close to management's own "~1/3rd" framing even though the specific volume/tonnage statistic behind that claim is independently discredited (Section 1B/2) — i.e. DDev plausibly *is* a genuinely large player in the ~15–35% range depending which TAM anchor is used (14–17% under Method 1's higher figure, 25–40%+ under the Method 2/3/5 cluster), even though the specific disclosed statistic used to make that claim does not hold up on its own math. Using the conservative TAM (₹5,680 Cr SAM) would imply a >50% share, which is implausible given named organised competition (Shakun/Orbia, KLJ) plus acknowledged unorganised-sector rivalry in PVC — further evidence the conservative anchor likely under-counts, and the realistic figure is the better base for the funnel below.

### 3B — SOM at 3 and 5 years (core)
DDev is **already a large/dominant player** (~31% share on the realistic base), not an early-stage share-taker in a fragmented market — the standard "1–2pp normal / 3–5pp aggressive" share-gain framework applies toward the lower-to-middle of its range here, since most of the "easy" unorganised-to-organised formalisation upside DDev can realistically reach has already been captured. Fresh capacity (₹350–400 Cr core capex, Bhiwadi XLPE 48,000 MTPA online Apr-2026, HFFR to 20,000 MTPA and PE compound +25,000 MTPA by FY27 per Inv. Pres. p.33) supports gains toward the upper end of "normal," not full "aggressive."
- **Share gain assumption**: +2.0pp over 3 years, +3.5pp over 5 years (SAM growing throughout at the Method-1/2-implied ~9% CAGR, midpoint of IndexBox's cited 8–10% band).
- SAM (yr3) = ₹9,600 Cr × 1.09³ = **₹12,432 Cr**; share = 30.8% + 2.0pp = 32.8% → **SOM_3yr (core) = ₹4,078 Cr**
- SAM (yr5) = ₹9,600 Cr × 1.09⁵ = **₹14,771 Cr**; share = 30.8% + 3.5pp = 34.3% → **SOM_5yr (core) = ₹5,066 Cr**
- **Implied core revenue CAGR** (from FY26 base ₹2,953 Cr): yr3 = (4,078/2,953)^(1/3) − 1 = **11.4%**; yr5 = (5,066/2,953)^(1/5) − 1 = **11.4%**
- Sanity cross-check: management's own FY30 core target (₹5,000 Cr) implies an FY26–FY30 CAGR of ~14%. This report's independently-derived core SOM CAGR (11.4%) sits modestly *below* management's own guidance — an appropriately conservative, credible output, not a forced match.

### BESS sizing (separate runway)

**TAM (BESS, equipment/assembly scope)**:
- *Method 1 (capacity-target bottom-up)*: CEA's National Electricity Plan targets **41.65 GW / 208.25 GWh of BESS by FY29-30** (independently verified via web search — Central Electricity Authority planning documents; this figure is **H confidence**, a government primary source, and corroborates the presentation's own "~208 GWh by 2030" claim almost exactly). Valued at DDev's own disclosed unit economics (~₹800–900 Cr revenue per GWh of assembled capacity — internally consistent: Rakesh Tiwari's Feb-2026 concall math of "$100/kWh × 1 GWh ≈ ₹950 Cr" checks out arithmetically and sits in the plausible range for 2025-26 global containerized BESS system pricing):
  - 208.25 GWh × ₹800 Cr/GWh = **₹1,66,600 Cr**
  - 208.25 GWh × ₹900 Cr/GWh = **₹1,87,425 Cr**
  - This is a **cumulative FY26–FY30 build-out opportunity** (a stock concept, not an annual run-rate) for the equipment-supply scope specifically (cells+pack+BMS/EMS+container — DDev's actual scope — not full EPC/land/civil).
- *Cross-check against the full-value-chain figure*: the presentation's own "~208 GWh by 2030 (~$32 Bn value)" (Inv. Pres. p.10) converts to $32 Bn × 9,600 = **₹3,07,200 Cr** cumulative *full system value* (cells+PCS+BOS+EPC+land+soft costs). DDev's equipment-scope estimate above (₹1,66,600–1,87,425 Cr) is ~55–61% of this — plausible, since batteries+PCS+enclosure typically represent 55–65% of total installed BESS cost globally, with the remainder being EPC/land/soft costs DDev does not capture.
- *Method 2 (independent annual-market cross-check)*: third-party India BESS *annual* market-size estimates for 2030 range widely by scope — $1.2 Bn (27% CAGR case) to $1.5 Bn (2025) → $7.5 Bn (2030, 33% CAGR case) [general market-research aggregators, **L confidence**, scope not transparent]. Converted: $1.2–7.5 Bn × 9,600 = **₹11,520–72,000 Cr annual by 2030**. Applying DDev's own ~55–65% equipment-scope filter: **≈₹6,900–43,200 Cr annual equipment-scope opportunity by FY30** — brackets a GWh-based single-year cross-check reasonably (if ~40–60 GWh of the 208 GWh cumulative target is added in FY30 alone, at ₹800–900 Cr/GWh that is ₹32,000–54,000 Cr for that single year), reinforcing that the cumulative Method-1 figure is the right order of magnitude.

**SAM (BESS)**: DDev's Phase-1 committed capacity (5 GWh by FY30, Ahmedabad) represents 5/208.25 = **2.4% of the CEA's national cumulative BESS capacity target** — this is DDev's own capacity-committed slice of the equipment-scope TAM, i.e. SAM ≈ TAM × DDev's addressable geographic/customer/capability fit (assumed ~100% within its committed capacity envelope, since DDev is targeting the exact utility/C&I/government customer set the CEA target describes) = **₹4,000–4,700 Cr cumulative** (5 GWh × ₹800–900 Cr/GWh, i.e. DDev's own Phase-1 ceiling).

**SOM (BESS), using management's own interim guidance as the trajectory, then haircut for execution risk**: management guides ₹200–250 Cr FY27 (H2 only, partial year) → ~₹800–900 Cr FY28 (≈1 GWh) → ₹2,000–2,500 Cr FY30 (Phase-1 target, ≈5 GWh capacity, implying ~55–65% utilisation of the built capacity that year, not 100% — internally consistent with the cost-per-GWh assumptions above).
- **3-year point (~FY29)**: linear interpolation between the FY28 (₹800–900 Cr) and FY30 (₹2,000–2,500 Cr) guided checkpoints gives ≈₹1,550 Cr; B07 flags twice-revised BESS guidance and only a "Moderate" execution moat (F2), so a 15% haircut is applied: **SOM_3yr (BESS) ≈ ₹1,300 Cr**.
- **5-year point (~FY31)**: one year beyond the explicit FY30 target, assuming modest further utilisation gains within the fixed 5 GWh Phase-1 ceiling (max theoretical ≈₹4,250 Cr at 100% utilisation and ₹850 Cr/GWh): **SOM_5yr (BESS) ≈ ₹2,600 Cr**.

### 3C — Capacity cross-check

**Core**: FY27 guided volume (2,31,000 MT, ~15% growth) at DDev's own FY26 blended realisation (₹1,45,136/tonne, held flat conservatively) implies FY27 core revenue ≈ 2,31,000 × 1,45,136 ÷ 1,00,00,000 = **₹3,353 Cr** — within 0.4% of management's own FY27 guidance of ~₹3,340 Cr, validating the per-tonne modelling approach used throughout this report. Total installed capacity post-Bhiwadi is "beyond 3,15,000 TPA" (operator context; Inv. Pres. p.7-8). At the same blended price, 100% utilisation of that capacity ≈ ₹4,572 Cr; 80% utilisation ≈ ₹3,657 Cr. This report's **SOM_3yr (core), ₹4,078 Cr, sits between the 80% and 100% utilisation marks** — achievable at ~89–90% blended utilisation of *current* installed capacity, or comfortably so once the disclosed further capex (₹350–400 Cr core, HFFR to 20,000 MTPA and PE compound +25,000 MTPA by FY27) lands. **Capacity check: sufficient — no gap; SOM and the capex plan are mutually consistent**, not one more optimistic than the other.

**BESS**: SOM_3yr/5yr above (₹1,300–2,600 Cr) require only 1.5–3 GWh of throughput against the committed 5 GWh Phase-1 capacity (₹150–200 Cr capex, internal accruals, per B07) — well within the physical envelope. **Capacity check: sufficient.**

### 4C — Market structure
- **Core**: DDev (self-styled "India's largest listed polymer compound manufacturer"), Shakun Polymers (Orbia-owned, ≥₹500 Cr, cable/wire-focused, targeting 1,00,000 MT by 2030 from ~70,000 MT today), KLJ Polymers and Chemicals (₹1,900 Cr, broader plasticizer/PVC-compound scope), plus acknowledged unorganised-sector competition concentrated in commodity PVC. Top-3 organised concentration within the *organised* segment is high (DDev clearly the largest of the three named), but total market concentration is diluted by the unorganised estimate (40–60% of market per Method 3) and by captive/backward-integrated compounding at large cable makers. **Structure: consolidating within organised segment, fragmenting at the commodity-PVC edge** (new entrants Adani/UltraTech backward-integrating there). Competition is differentiation-based (certification, reliability, tailor-made solutions) at 11kV+/specialty grades, price-based at commodity PVC. Import share: material at the high-voltage/EHV end (Dow, Borealis) per management's own admission, immaterial at commodity grades.
- **BESS**: nascent, pre-consolidation. Named entrants: JSW, Premier Energies/Page Digitek (per concall), various cylindrical-cell players; DDev is early but not first-mover (management concedes others have "just announced" or are further along on prismatic-cell lines). Structure: fragmenting/land-grab phase, price competition not yet the dominant dynamic (differentiated by reliability/certification per management, echoing the core-business qualification-moat playbook).
