Rationale 
The ratings assigned to the bank facilities of Sakuma Exports Limited (SEL) factors the 
extensive experience of promoters of more than two decades in agriculture commodities 
enabling strong supplier and customer relations. The ratings also derive strength from 
the comfortable financial risk profile as indicated by comfortable capital structure and 
debt coverage indicators. Also, the rating factors the prudent forex hedging and inventory 
procurement policy followed by the Company. However, the rating is constrained by 
product concentration risk (primarily trading of sugar), decline in revenues and 
profitability, increasing working capital intensity with shift in revenues towards 
domestic large corporates, exposure to agro-climatic risks and the inherent seasonality of 
the agricultural commodities business, changes in government policies and regulatory 
framework which had intensified with the geopolitical scenario around the globe. 
 
Outlook: Stable 
The Stable Outlook reflects the expectation that the Company will continue to improve its 
operating performance, supported by diversification of revenues across other agri 
products and established business relationships. The outlook also considers the 
improvement in profitability in Q1FY27 and expectation that the Company will maintain 
a stable financial profile over the medium term.

Key Rating Drivers with Detailed Description 
Strengths 
• Comfortable financial risk profile  
SEL’s financial risk profile continues to be comfortable as indicated by capital structure which 
stood comfortable with overall gearing at 0.01x and TOL/adjusted TNW at 0.26x as on 31st 
March 2026 (FY25: 0.15x and 0.21x and FY24: 0.29x and 0.40x). The capital structure is 
supported by strong adjusted networth of Rs. 748.86 crore as on 31st March 2026. Further, the 
total debt comprises of working capital borrowings considering the trading nature of business 
operations which declined with the decline in revenues and total debt stood at Rs. 8.80 crore 
(including lease liabilities) as on 31st March 2026. The lower debt levels led to lower interest 
costs leading to comfortable interest coverage ratio (4.28 times in FY26). Going forward, the 
capital structure is expected to remain comfortable considering the strong netwroth base. 
 
• Prudent forex hedging policy and inventory procurement policy 
SEL actively monitors its forex exposure and inventory procurement which mitigates the 
fluctuation in forex prices and commodities to an extent. The Company factors natural hedge 
and forward contracts to mitigate its forex risk. To manage the inventory price volatility risk, 
the Company places back-to-back orders with its vendors, enters in sale agreement and 
participates in commodity exchanges. The Company does not carry the inventory risk and is 
also able to pass on the price volatility to its customers. Thus, the Company is able to mitigate 
the forex movement and inventory price movements to a large extent by consistently following 
its prudent risk mitigation policies.

• Long track record of business operations with experienced management 
SEL was started as a partnership firm in 1999 by Mr. Chander Mohan Malhotra and his son - 
Mr. Saurabh Malhotra and was subsequently converted into a public limited company in 
August 2005. Mr. Saurabh Malhotra has an experience of 20+ years of managing the business. 
The long track record and experience of the promoters aid the Company to sustain business 
through business cycles. Further, the Company services reputed clientele including Britannia 
Industries Limited, ITC (Imperial Tobacco Company) Limited, Coco Cola, Parle Products 
Private Limited amongst others thereby providing revenue visibility and mitigating credit risk 
of the customers. 
 
Weaknesses 
• Product concentration risk 
SEL’s product concentration has remained very high with sugar accounting for majority of its 
revenue which is subject to government policy for exports. With the ban on sugar exports 
(earlier implemented from October 18, 2023 with the ban continuing in FY25), the Company 
initiated domestic institutional business which provided volumes and revenue visibility but led 
to lower profitability compared to exports. The government subsequently permitted sugar 
exports for the 2025–26 sugar marketing year (subject to quotas) in November 2025. However, 
this policy was completely reversed on May 13, 2026, when the Directorate General of Foreign 
Trade (DGFT) issued an immediate, absolute ban on sugar exports until September 30, 2026. 
Going forward, the Company plans to broaden its product mix by entering the trading of 
ethanol and agro products including wheat flour, maize, edible oil, corn amongst others.  
 
• Decline in revenues and profitability 
The revenues of the Company had declined by ~25% in FY26 on account of decline in volumes 
with product concentration for trading of sugar. SEL’s margins remain structurally thin given 
the trading nature of operations, exposure to agro-climatic risks, and the inherent seasonality 
of the agricultural commodities business. The margins declined sharply in FY26 with EBITDA 
margin at 0.42% and PAT margin at 0.55% (0.61% and 0.64% in FY25). The PAT margin was 
supported by non-operating income comprising of interest income, mark to market gain on 
forward contracts and profit on sale of investments. However, the profitability improved in 
Q1FY27 with EBITDA at Rs. 3.90 crore and PAT at Rs. 2.82 crore (Q1FY 26: Rs. 3.19 crore

• Increase in working capital intensity; albeit stood moderate 
The working capital intensity of the Company increased in FY26 with operating cycle at 106 
(FY25: 78 days) on account of increase in debtor days to 93 days (FY25: 62 days) and inventory 
days to 33 days (FY25: 21 days). The increase in debtor is primarily on account of shift of 
revenues to large domestic players and expected to remain at current levels. The inventory is 
maintained at around one month and expected to remain the same. Further, the creditor days 
continues be low at 20 days (FY25: 6 days) as the Company primarily purchases at discounted 
prices from factories against advance payment. However, the overall working capital 
utilizations stood low despite the increase in working capital intensity as primarily was funded 
through internal accruals. Going forward it remains critical for the Company to prudently 
manage its working capital requirements with the diversification of revenues across other 
products. 
 
• Agri commodities trading susceptible to government policies 
The trading of agricultural commodities is susceptible to Government regulations. Any change 
in the Government policy with respect to procurement price or duties levies on import/export 
may affect the viability of the trading operations. Currently, the major contributor of revenue 
is sugar which is subject to demand supply scenario across the globe coupled with changes in 
Government policy thereby exposing the Company to regulatory risks. Also, going forward, 
the Company plans to initiate operations in other agro products and ethanol which will continue 
to be exposed to the external risk factors. Further, the agro products are exposed to climatic 
conditions and preference by the farmers basis the cyclicity of the crop. Thus, it remains critical 
for the Company to diversify its revenue base across products and prudently manage its product 
mix with the changing external environment.

Liquidity –Adequate 
The liquidity profile of the Company stood comfortable and is expected to remain 
adequate over the near to medium term, supported by healthy cash accruals vis-à-vis its 
interest obligations. Further, the unencumbered cash/bank balance stood at Rs. 17.80 
crore as on 31st March 2026 and the Company had cash balance in working capital fund-
based limits on an average for the past 12 months ending June 2026 providing liquidity 
cushion and financial flexibility.

Downward Factors 
- 
Deterioration in capital structure and working capital intensity leading to impact 
on liquidity profile. 
-

About the Company 
Sakuma Exports Limited (SEL) was started as a partnership firm in 1999 by Mr. Chander 
Mohan Malhotra and his son -Mr. Saurabh Malhotra and was subsequently converted into a 
public limited company in August 2005. It is engaged in international trading and domestic 
trading activity. The Company has specialized and created a niche for itself in the area of export 
of agro-based commodities. The Company is in trading of sugar, peanuts, red split lentils, de-
oiled cakes (soya, sunflower etc.) onions, maize, wheat flour, seasame seeds, chilli, rice, castor 
oil, chickpeas, etc. However, the primary revenue contributor is from trading of sugar.
