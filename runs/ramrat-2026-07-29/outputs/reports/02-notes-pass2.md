## NEW FINDINGS

1. 🔴 **Consolidated operating cash flow swung to NEGATIVE ₹(9,299.42) Lakhs in FY26 versus +₹22,733.31 Lakhs in FY25** (Consolidated Cash Flow Statement, p.264), despite Operating Profit before working-capital changes of ₹27,566.52 Lakhs and PBT of ₹15,296.88 Lakhs. The swing is entirely working-capital driven: Trade receivables absorbed ₹24,736.15 Lakhs, Inventories absorbed ₹25,240.65 Lakhs, partially offset by Trade payables release of ₹21,607.96 Lakhs, leaving cash generated from operations of only ₹(4,680.56) Lakhs before tax and ₹(9,299.42) Lakhs after tax paid (₹4,618.86 Lakhs). This is the hard cash-flow-statement confirmation of the inventory/receivables build-up Pass 1 flagged only via ratios (Note 8, Note 9, Note 37) — it should be the anchor evidence for FLAG-CASH, not a supporting data point.

2. 🟡 Lease Liabilities show a striking **carrying-amount-vs-fair-value gap**: carrying amount ₹5,103.45 Lakhs vs fair value ₹1,465.83 Lakhs at 31.03.2026 (Note 40(a)(iii), p.240) — a ~71% divergence, versus a near-parity gap the prior year (₹786.92 Lakhs carrying vs ₹746.27 Lakhs fair value, ~5%). No explanation is given in the notes. This coincides with the new 95-99 year Bhiwadi land sub-lease and 20-year Jamnagar wind-mill sub-lease recognised during the year (Note 46(b), p.246, already flagged by Pass 1 for size but not for this valuation anomaly). Worth a management question on the discount-rate/valuation methodology used for these ultra-long-tenure leases.

3. 🟢 **Consolidated P&L confirms the exceptional item is a distinct P&L line**, resolving Pass 1's open question: "Profit before exceptional item and tax ₹16,041.94 Lakhs, Less: Exceptional item — Statutory impact of new labour codes (Note 33(d)) ₹356.43 Lakhs" (Consolidated Statement of P&L, p.261). Note the consolidated figure (₹356.43 Lakhs) differs from the standalone figure (₹333.01 Lakhs, Note 38(a)(7)) by ₹23.42 Lakhs, presumably Tefabo's own portion — this reconciliation is not spelled out anywhere in the notes reviewed.

4. 🟡 **Consolidated P&L quantifies the Epavo JV loss for the first time**: "Add: Share of (Loss)/Profit from Joint Ventures ₹(388.63) Lakhs" (Consolidated P&L, p.261), reducing Profit before Tax from ₹15,685.51 Lakhs to ₹15,296.88 Lakhs. This directly answers the question Pass 1 raised (Section 6) about current-year Epavo profitability given its 51.8% equity decline — Epavo is confirmed loss-making at the Group's 50% equity-accounted share in FY26, not just historically.

5. 🟡 **Personal-guarantee base is wider than Pass 1 captured**: alongside Tribhuvanprasad and Mahendrakumar Kabra, the newly appointed FY26 executives Hemant Kabra and Hitesh Vaghela also stand as personal guarantors — Term Loan guarantees ₹3,465.69 Lakhs each, secured Working Capital guarantees ₹7,000.00 Lakhs each (Note 35, p.232). Additionally, Shreegopal Kabra (brother, non-executive, close family member) guarantees ₹38,600.00 Lakhs of secured working capital jointly with Tribhuvanprasad (unsecured guarantee of ₹7,200.00 Lakhs in FY25 reduced to nil in FY26). Governance dependency on family personal guarantees is materially broader across the promoter family than the two names Pass 1 identified.

6. 🟡 **Executive Directors' Commission on profit is a separate, fast-growing item**: ₹554.03 Lakhs (FY26) vs ₹248.31 Lakhs (FY25), +123.1%, embedded within "Accrued Salary & Benefits" (Note 15B footnote, p.217) and cross-referenced at Note 35 (p.229, 231). This is additive to — not the same as — the ₹903.87 Lakhs managerial remuneration Pass 1 already flagged (+102.0%), meaning the total cash cost of executive compensation is compounding on two separate lines simultaneously. Separately, Commission to Independent Directors also more than doubled: ₹38.66 Lakhs vs ₹18.30 Lakhs, +111.3% (Note 35, p.229).

7. 🟡 **"Interest accrued and due" (i.e., overdue, unpaid interest) rose from ₹67.41 Lakhs to ₹157.88 Lakhs, +134.2%** (Note 15B, p.217; confirmed again in Note 40(a), p.238), while "Interest accrued but not due" was roughly flat (₹188.18 Lakhs to ₹159.29 Lakhs). A near-doubling of overdue-but-unpaid interest is a mild cash-stress signal Pass 1 did not surface, consistent with the broader working-capital and leverage strain already identified.

8. 🟡 **EPCG export-obligation contingency is disclosed only as a Deferred Income note, not in the formal Contingent Liabilities table**: Note 18.1 (p.219) states that duty saved on capital-goods imports under the EPCG scheme (₹1,225.60 Lakhs deferred income balance, FY26, up from ₹1,050.11 Lakhs) is contingent on the Company meeting prescribed export obligations over a specified period; failure would require repayment of the duty saved plus interest. This unquantified, off-Note-30 contingency is relevant given Pass 1's own finding that export mix is falling (~7.4% of revenue, down from ~7.9%) — a widening gap between export-obligation commitments and actual export performance would be a real, if currently unquantified, liability.

9. 🔴 **CFO turnover within the fiscal year**: Shri Rajeev Maheshwari was appointed Chief Financial Officer effective 1 June 2025 and resigned effective 31 March 2026 (Note 35(a), p.227) — a complete CFO tenure and departure inside the same reporting year, concurrent with the Section 132 tax search/reassessment overhang (Pass 1 finding #1) and the rapid three-appointment expansion of the promoter-family executive bench (Pass 1 finding #7). CFO churn during a year with an active tax search and rapid executive turnover is a governance signal Pass 1 missed entirely.

10. Factual correction to Pass 1: the HUF that underwent partition is **Mahendra Kumar Kabra (HUF), partitioned w.e.f. 19 March 2025**, per the related-party list (Note 35(c), p.228) — not "Kabra Shreegopal Rameshwarlal HUF" partitioned 19 March 2026 as stated in Pass 1. Kabra Shreegopal Rameshwarlal HUF is a separate, continuing related party. This should be corrected in the Pass 3 consolidation.

11. 🟡 **CSR and "social welfare" spend routed through director-controlled entities**: the Company's CSR obligation (₹157.00 Lakhs, Note 34(a), p.227) is fulfilled entirely via Hema Foundation, "a Charitable Trust in which two Directors of the Company are Trustees." Separately, the Company paid ₹314.95 Lakhs (FY26, vs ₹285.00 Lakhs FY25) to non-profit organisations "in which Directors of the Company are Trustees/Office Bearers" (Note 34(b), p.227). Combined, roughly ₹4.72 Cr of company money in FY26 flows to entities controlled by the Company's own directors — outside the formal Ind AS 24 related-party disclosure (Note 35) since these are CSR/donation channels, but relevant to a governance and related-party-fairness assessment that Pass 1 did not raise.

12. 🟡 **Other Income is inflated by a large, growing net FX gain that is not the mirror image of borrowing-related FX cost**: Foreign Exchange Gain (Net) of ₹1,000.48 Lakhs sits in Other Income (Note 23, p.222; +46.2% YoY), representing ~52% of total Other Income (₹1,924.67 Lakhs) and ~6.6% of standalone PBT (₹15,109.20 Lakhs) — while a separate "Net loss on foreign currency borrowing transactions and translation" of ₹198.20 Lakhs (nil in FY25) sits in Finance Costs (Note 27, p.223). Investors assessing quality of earnings should net these two FX lines together rather than take Other Income at face value; this gross-up/earnings-quality point was not raised in Pass 1.

13. 🟡 ESOP performance-vesting condition is **management-set, not externally benchmarked**: 70% of options vest on "Performance Based" conditions tied to "Annual Consolidated EBITDA target approved by the Board of Directors from time to time and notified to the Participating Employee" (Note 52(A), p.248) — a self-set internal hurdle rather than an independent or peer-relative metric, a mild governance-quality point on incentive design not raised in Pass 1. Separately, 18,000 options expired due to resignation during FY26 (Note 52(D), p.249), consistent with the personnel turnover already noted (item 9 above).

14. Minor/immaterial but a mandatory disclosure Pass 1 did not mention: **relationship with a struck-off company** — "Associated Suppliers and Assistance Co. Private Limited," a struck-off entity, received dividend of ₹1.00 Lakh in FY25 with ₹3.94 Lakhs unclaimed dividend outstanding as at 31.03.2025 (now nil, Note 48, p.247). Amounts are trivial but the disclosure itself is a compliance-history data point. Also noted for completeness: Trade or Investment in Crypto Currency or Virtual Currency — ₹NIL both years (Note 49, p.247), a standard negative disclosure.

15. Full Note 37 ratio panel (p.233-234) adds detail beyond Pass 1's D/E and DSCR citations: Current Ratio declined from 1.18x to 1.15x (-2.54%, mild liquidity compression, not previously flagged); Net Profit Ratio improved 1.98% to 2.20% (+11.10%); ROCE improved 20.92% to 23.55% (+12.59%); Return on Equity rose 15.52% to 20.71% (+33.42% variance — the company's own >25%-variance explanation, alongside Debt-Equity, attributes this to the Bhiwadi ramp-up). Only two of the eight disclosed ratios (D/E and ROE) crossed the 25% mandatory-explanation threshold; both were explained by the company with the same underlying driver (Bhiwadi commissioning).

Minor note-reference correction: the Custom Duty/Stamp Duty provision movement (opening ₹18.56 Lakhs, closing ₹0.89 Lakhs) that Pass 1 attributed solely to "consolidated Note 39" is also disclosed at the **standalone** level as Note 43 (p.245) with identical figures — a housekeeping correction for the Pass 3 consolidation, not a new substantive finding.

## PASS 2 NEW FINDINGS SUMMARY

The most consequential item this pass is the **consolidated cash flow statement**: operating cash flow was negative ₹(9,299.42) Lakhs in FY26 against positive ₹22,733.31 Lakhs in FY25, driven by the receivables and inventory build-up Pass 1 identified only through ratios. This is hard, quantified evidence that should anchor FLAG-CASH. Second in importance is the confirmation that the Epavo JV posted a loss (₹388.63 Lakhs share) in FY26, resolving a Pass 1 open question. Governance-adjacent new findings include a same-year CFO appointment-and-resignation, a wider circle of family personal guarantors than previously identified, a fast-growing Executive Director profit commission running alongside (not instead of) the already-flagged remuneration jump, and CSR/donation flows to director-controlled trusts and NGOs. A factual correction is also flagged: the HUF partition in Note 35(c) belongs to Mahendra Kumar Kabra (19 March 2025), not "Kabra Shreegopal Rameshwarlal HUF" (19 March 2026) as stated in Pass 1. An unexplained lease-liability fair-value/carrying-value anomaly and an EPCG export-obligation contingency sitting outside the formal contingent-liabilities table round out the list.
