# Karnika Industries: H1 FY27 in plain words (17 Oct 2026)

Sources: the committed review, the three A5 audits and work/notion-thesis.md. No agent was re-run.

1. **First look.** There is no Notion page, no company file and no spear on record. This is a first look, with no earlier analysis to compare against.

2. **What the company does.** Karnika makes children's clothes in Howrah. It sells them in bulk to a few large buying houses, which pass them on to shops. It also owns 75% of Kidcity, a smaller arm that sells through store counters and online partners.

3. **What happened this half.** Management said sales grew 52% in the September quarter. That figure compares the whole group this year with Karnika alone last year, before Kidcity joined. On a fair basis, Karnika's own sales grew about 17%. Part of that is Karnika selling to its own subsidiary. Sales to outside customers may have grown only about 6%, though this is our estimate.

4. **What improved.** Profit after tax rose about 15% in the quarter and about 46% for the half. Kidcity reported a strong profit margin. The company has almost no factory assets, because outside workshops do the processing.

5. **What got worse.** The core business grew profit only 7% in the quarter. About half of the profit gain came from other income, such as interest and the sale of old machines. The operating margin fell by about two points. Costs for outside processing and staff rose.

6. **Cash and balance sheet.** This is the weak spot. The business used cash in the half, even though it reported Rs 18 Cr of profit. Customers now take about 142 days to pay, up from about 110. Debt rose, and the company also took Rs 12 Cr of warrant money. At the same time it doubled its loans to outside companies to Rs 31 Cr. The filing does not name the borrowers.

7. **Side bets.** The company bought 40% of Elementa Lifespaces and agreed to buy 26% of a sports academy. Neither is a clothing business. Elementa already shows a loss.

8. **What management promised.** Management targets 30 to 35% yearly growth to FY28, a margin of 17 to 20%, and a new chain of airport and mall stores. Today it has one store. Debt will stay at this level, and new equity will fund the stores. The call gave few hard numbers. The launch date of the first store moved within the same call, and the airport story changed three times. Three statements on the call conflict with the filing. One example: the CFO said Kidcity has no customers who owe it money, but the accounts suggest they owe at least Rs 35 Cr. We rate the guidance as evasive.

9. **Red flags in plain words.** The accounts carry careless errors. The minority holders' share of Kidcity's profit was worked out on the wrong number in the first quarter. Last March's group balance sheet seems to leave out an investment loss. Earnings per share were not adjusted for the bonus issue, so they look like they halved when profit rose. Kidcity was bought cheaply, for about one year of its profit. Its margin doubled after Karnika began supplying it, so prices between the two companies may decide where profit lands. A quarter of Kidcity's profit goes to an owner nobody has named.

10. **Open items.** Our final check left three points unfixed. None of them changes the verdict. First, last year's base already included a full half of sales to Kidcity. Second, the investment loss error may sit only in this filing's comparison column, which makes it less serious. Third, Kidcity's small positive cash flow rests on paying its own suppliers late. Some of that unpaid money may be owed to Karnika itself.

11. **What it means.** The verdict is to proceed with flags. Sales really are growing, but part of the growth is sales to its own subsidiary, and profit is not turning into cash. Money is going into unnamed loans and side bets. There is no thesis, no position and no valuation. The name needs answers before anyone puts a value on it.

12. **What to watch next.** The most important question is who borrowed the Rs 31 Cr and whether they are linked to the promoters. Next comes how much of the money owed by customers was collected in October. The decisive reading is the FY27 annual result, due by about end May 2027. If customers pay within about 110 days and cash follows profit, the story improves. If payment slips past about 142 days, or cash flow stays negative, the bear case holds. We have 37 questions ready for the company.
