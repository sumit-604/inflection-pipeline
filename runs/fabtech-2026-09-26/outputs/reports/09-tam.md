## SECTION 1: MARKET DEFINITION

### 1A Precise boundaries

**Product scope (IN):** Turnkey engineering, procurement, installation and
validation (EPIV) of pharmaceutical, biotech, vaccine and animal-health
manufacturing facilities: cleanrooms, process equipment (bioreactors,
purification, filling), containment systems, and utility systems (Process,
Air, Water — Fabtech's own "PAW" framing, Inv. Pres. Q1 FY27 p.4). Also
in-scope: standalone equipment supply and installation/commissioning
services sold without the full turnkey wrap (8.9% of FY26 revenue, B04).

**Product scope (OUT):** Drug R&D, drug manufacturing itself (Fabtech
builds the plant, does not make the medicine), hospital/clinical
construction outside pharma manufacturing, pure equipment manufacturing
sold without an EPC/installation wrap by third parties, and the newly
disclosed general MEP/civil contracting line in Saudi Arabia via the 51%
Specialized Contracting Activities LLC (SACE) entity — this is a boundary
EXPANSION beyond the pharma-plant niche and is treated separately as a SAM
expansion lever (Section 5D), not folded into the core TAM.

**Geographic scope:** MENA/GCC (UAE, Saudi Arabia) and Africa (Kenya,
Morocco, West and North Africa) are the served core, ~89% of FY26
consolidated revenue per the audited AR MD&A (AR p.44 txt); India ~10-11%.
A Q1 FY27 investor-presentation slide states "international revenue share
of approximately 55%," which conflicts with the audited AR figure — this
inconsistency is carried from B04 (FLAG-DATA-INCONSISTENCY) and the AR is
treated as authoritative here. Company operates across 60+ countries per
its own description (Inv. Pres. Q1 FY27 p.14) but current revenue
concentration is UAE 27.9%, Saudi Arabia 18.2%, India 10.0%, Kenya 9.6%,
Morocco 4.8%, rest of world 29.6% (Inv. Pres. Q1 FY27 p.12). Management's
own claim (below) extends the addressable geography to "the West"
(developed markets); Fabtech discloses zero West revenue today, so the
West slice is treated as aspirational optionality, not current SAM.

**Customer scope:** Pharmaceutical, biotech, vaccine and animal-health
(veterinary) manufacturers — private sector and government/donor-linked
(e.g., Africa CDC/Gavi-backed vaccine plant programmes). No consumer or
retail customer.

**Channel scope:** Direct tender/bid-won turnkey contracts and joint
ventures/local entities (the 51% SACE Saudi structure, the 49%/33.33%/~16%
UAE entities named in the investor presentations, p.5 both decks). No
distributor or resale channel.

**Price segment:** Disclosed individual project values cluster
USD 7.0-7.8 million (Rs 63.6-65.5 Cr) for full-scope turnkey contracts
(West Africa OSD facility, Saudi Animal Vaccine project, Inv. Pres. FY26
p.7) and Rs 49-52 Cr for a smaller veterinary facility — call it a
Rs 50-65 Cr band per full project. A separate, lower "average revenue
booked per project per year" figure of ~Rs 7.6 Cr (RHP-derived, B04) is a
different measure — annual revenue recognised, not total contract value —
and is not conflated with it here.

**Explicit exclusions:** Non-pharma industrial EPC generally (semiconductor
or data-centre cleanrooms, which Fabtech's own strategy slide names as an
adjacency ambition — Inv. Pres. Q1 FY27 p.16 "China+1 strategies" — but
with zero disclosed revenue), and the general MEP/civil Saudi line
(boundary expansion, Section 5D).

### 1B Management's own TAM claim

"North of $30 billion" for pharmaceutical-emerging-market (MENA, Africa,
Southeast Asia) infrastructure over the next 10 years, and "more than
$70 billion" for the West over the same period (Aug-2026 concall, Aman
Anavkar; carried from B05/06-peers). No published source is cited on the
call, no definition of "infrastructure opportunity" is given (unclear
whether this is cumulative capex, addressable EPC/turnkey spend, or the
entire pharma facility spend including in-house builds), and — materially
— this figure is NOT reproduced anywhere in the company's own commissioned
CRISIL MI&A report (Sep-2025), which instead gives MEA pharmaceutical
capex of $9-10 billion (2020-2024, actual) rising to $11.5-12.5 billion
(2025-2029P) — an order of magnitude different framing (annual/period
capex vs a flat $30bn headline) with no visible reconciliation between the
two documents. **Credibility read: BROAD.** The claim is directionally
consistent with real, CRISIL-documented MEA capex growth (Section 2,
Method 1) but is unsourced, undefined in scope, and diverges from the
company's own commissioned industry data. Peer concall cross-read (06-peers
Q1) found no peer corroborating the specific dollar figures — directional
support for "MENA/Africa and Africa are a live capex theme," no
quantitative confirmation.

## SECTION 2: TAM ESTIMATION, MULTIPLE METHODS

All methods below use the CRISIL MI&A report commissioned by the company
for its Sep-2025 IPO (research/CRISIL_Industry_Report_2025-09_company_
commissioned.txt, also reproduced as RHP Industry Overview,
prospectus/FABTECH_RHP_2025-09-22.txt pp.179-195). This report is
NON-ANCHORED for company-specific facts (per B00) but is the best
available third-party industry lead; it is ~1 year old at run date
(dated Sep-2025, run date Sep-2026) — inside the 2-year freshness window,
not stale, but approaching it; flagged for a refresh check at the next run.

### Method 1: Top-down (global and MEA pharma capex, CRISIL)

Global pharmaceutical capex 2025-2029P: $500-550 billion cumulative
(CRISIL, RHP p.187 txt), i.e. **$100-110 billion/year** average.
Subtraction 1 — R&D-only capex: US pharma majors earmark "~20% of overall
capex to R&D activities" (CRISIL, RHP p.189 txt; US-specific, applied here
as the only disclosed R&D-share benchmark, a directional proxy, not an
MEA-specific figure — flagged as an input gap). Facility-build-eligible
capex (buildings, plant, equipment — Fabtech's actual addressable scope)
≈ 80% of the total: **$80-88 billion/year**, global, 100%-capture TAM
ceiling. Per rule 6 (conservative bias), take the lower bound: **$80
billion/year ≈ Rs 6,80,000 Cr/year** (at an illustrative ~Rs 85/USD; FX
rate not independently anchored in the corpus, flagged).

MEA-specific cut (the geography Fabtech actually serves, ~89% of
revenue): CRISIL gives MEA pharmaceutical capex of $9-10 billion
cumulative 2020-2024 (actual) rising ~1.3x to **$11.5-12.5 billion
cumulative 2025-2029P** (RHP p.190 txt) — i.e. **$2.3-2.5 billion/year**
average. Applying the same 80% facility-eligible cut (no MEA-specific R&D
split disclosed, same US-benchmark proxy applied, flagged): **$1.84-2.0
billion/year** facility-build-eligible MEA capex. Conservative (lower):
**$1.84 billion/year ≈ Rs 15,600 Cr/year.**

No source discloses what share of this capex pool actually flows through
third-party turnkey EPC providers (like Fabtech) versus in-house/self-
build by large pharma majors — **NOT FOUND**, named as an input gap. This
caps Method 1 at the capex-eligible pool, not a further-refined
"addressable-to-turnkey-providers" TAM; the gap is carried forward, not
estimated.

### Method 2: Bottom-up (project-unit build)

Unit: one turnkey pharma/biotech/vaccine/animal-health facility project,
12-36 months typical cycle (Inv. Pres. Q1 FY27 p.14). Disclosed full
contract values: West Africa OSD facility USD 7.05 million (~Rs 63.6 Cr);
Saudi Animal Vaccine project USD 7.8 million (~Rs 65.5 Cr); North Africa
veterinary facility Rs 49-52 Cr (Inv. Pres. FY26 p.7). Average of these
three disclosed project values ≈ **Rs 60 Cr/project** (≈USD 7.1 million at
~Rs 85/USD).

Number of addressable project-equivalents per year in the MEA facility-
build-eligible capex pool (Method 1, conservative: $1.84 billion/year ≈
Rs 15,600 Cr/year) ÷ Rs 60 Cr/project ≈ **~245 project-equivalents/year**
across MEA. This is a rough proxy (it treats all facility capex as if
awarded in discrete Fabtech-sized project units, which understates the
count of very large mega-plants and overstates the count of small
maintenance-linked spend; flagged) but gives a bottom-up TAM of
**245 × Rs 60 Cr ≈ Rs 14,700 Cr/year** — this triangulates closely with
Method 1's Rs 15,600 Cr/year (within 6%), a reasonable convergence given
the independence of the two approaches.

### Method 3: Peer revenue aggregation — PARTIAL, NAMED GAP

CRISIL's own competitor list for "pharmaceutical turnkey engineering
solution providers" (RHP p.183-184 txt, sourced to Crisil Intelligence)
names: Telstar (part of Azbil Group), Fabtech Technologies Ltd, Abu Dhabi
International Medical Services (ADI), Exyte GmbH, Pharma Access Pvt Ltd,
Nicomac Taikisha Clean Rooms Pvt Ltd, Hvax Technologies Ltd, Airtech
Systems (India) Pvt Ltd, Integrated Cleanroom Technologies Pvt Ltd, Lotus
Technicals Pvt Ltd. None of these (other than Fabtech) has disclosed
revenue anywhere in the corpus — **NOT FOUND** for nine of ten named
competitors (mostly private companies).

One data point from web search: Exyte GmbH (a named competitor, though its
scope spans semiconductor, data-centre and battery cleanrooms alongside
biopharma, a broader mandate than Fabtech's pharma-only niche) reported
group revenue of approximately €7.4 billion in its most recent disclosed
"record year" (Exyte newsroom, exact fiscal year not confirmed in this
search pass — WEB SEARCH, not independently dated against a filing).
Fabtech's FY26 consolidated revenue (~Rs 411 Cr, ≈USD 47 million / ≈€44
million) is under 1% of Exyte's scale — illustrating that the disclosed
competitor set spans from micro-cap regional specialists (Fabtech) to a
global engineering major, not a homogeneous peer band. This makes a
summed "peer revenue aggregation" TAM unreliable: aggregating Exyte's
global, multi-industry revenue with unknown private-company revenues would
either wildly overstate the MEA-pharma-only TAM (if Exyte's full revenue
is counted) or be arithmetically impossible to complete (nine of ten
competitors have no disclosed financials). **Method 3 status: NOT USABLE
as a standalone TAM estimate; retained only as qualitative evidence that
the addressable market's competitive tiers run from sub-scale regional
players to multi-billion-euro global majors**, which itself bears on
Fabtech's realistic SOM ceiling (Section 3B).

The peer set used elsewhere in this pipeline (SETL, HLEGLAS, PRAJIND — per
B00) are explicitly NOT direct competitors in this niche (06-peers.md:
"None of the three peers is a pure-play export turnkey pharma-plant EPC");
their FY26 consolidated revenues (PRAJIND ~Rs 3,168 Cr, HLEGLAS ~Rs 1,353
Cr, SETL ~Rs 774 Cr, per screening/ Data_Sheet CSVs) are shown here for
scale context only, not summed into any TAM figure.

### Method 4: Import substitution / localisation (qualitative, no TAM figure)

Not a classic import-substitution TAM (Fabtech is not substituting an
imported product with a domestic one), but a structurally analogous
dynamic applies: MEA governments are increasingly requiring LOCAL
manufacturing partners for pharma-facility tenders (Saudi Vision 2030,
"Made in Saudi," local-content rules; RHP pp.190-193 txt), which
progressively substitutes foreign/non-local EPC providers with locally-
incorporated ones — the exact mechanism behind Fabtech's own 51% SACE
Saudi entity and the "local-entity" framing behind its claimed win-rate
improvement (B07). This is a genuine demand-shift driver (Section 4A) but
does not itself resize the TAM; it reallocates who captures it.

### Method 5: Global benchmark (cleanroom-market cross-check)

Independent web search (not from the CRISIL corpus document) puts the
**global cleanroom construction market** at approximately USD 5.4 billion
(2023) growing at ~6.9% CAGR to 2032, with pharmaceutical/biotech cited as
the largest and fastest-growing end-use segment (MarketsandMarkets/Grand
View Research industry summaries, web search, 2026-09-26 — WEB SEARCH,
STALE-RISK: base year 2023, three years old at run date, flagged STALE per
the staleness rule; direction only, not a headline number). This is a
narrower slice than Fabtech's full scope (cleanrooms only, excluding
process/utility equipment) and is global, not MEA-specific, so it under-
counts Fabtech's addressable niche; it is presented as a sanity check on
order of magnitude only — a multi-billion-dollar global cleanroom-specific
market is consistent with, and does not contradict, the CRISIL-derived MEA
capex figures used above.

optimistic, consistent with the BROAD credibility read in Section 1B.

## SECTION 3: SAM & SOM

### 3A SAM

Starting from the global 100%-capture TAM ceiling (Rs 6,80,000 Cr/year,
conservative), apply the five filters:

1. **Geography filter (dominant cut):** Fabtech derives ~89% of revenue
   from MENA/GCC/Africa (AR p.44 txt) and discloses zero West revenue
   despite the management claim naming a $70bn Western opportunity.
   Restrict to the MEA facility-eligible capex pool computed in Section 2:
   Rs 14,700-16,900 Cr/year. This single filter accounts for essentially
   all of the reduction from TAM to SAM.
2. **Product filter:** Already embedded in the Method 1/2 "facility-
   eligible" cut (R&D-only capex excluded); no further product-line
   subtraction evidenced (RHP notes MEA capex skews toward new-build over
   maintenance capex, RHP p.190 txt, which if anything supports rather than
   shrinks Fabtech's addressable share).
3. **Customer filter:** No disclosed split between donor/government-linked
   projects (Africa CDC/Gavi/AfDB-backed) and private pharma-major
   projects within the MEA pool; both are within Fabtech's demonstrated
   customer set (RHP; Inv. Pres. FY26 wins). No further cut evidenced.
4. **Channel filter:** No disclosed % of MEA pharma capex self-executed
   in-house by large multinational pharma majors (who sometimes run their
   own engineering divisions) versus outsourced to third-party turnkey
   providers like Fabtech — **NOT FOUND**, named as an input gap that would
   shrink SAM further if quantified; not estimated here.
5. **Capability filter:** Fabtech's disclosed project-value band (Rs
   50-65 Cr per full contract) suggests it competes for mid-scale
   projects, not the largest mega-plants (which the Exyte-scale global
   majors likely capture) — a real but unquantified capability ceiling.

**SAM (conservative): Rs 14,700 Cr/year. SAM as % of TAM: ≈ 2.2%**
(14,700 / 6,80,000). The geography filter alone explains ~98% of the
TAM-to-SAM reduction; product/customer/channel/capability filters are
directionally real but unquantified (named gaps, not folded into a lower
number without evidence).

### 3B SOM at 3 and 5 years

Current SAM share = FY26 revenue ÷ SAM = Rs 411 Cr ÷ Rs 14,700 Cr =
**2.80%**.

Share-gain rule applied: the order book has been FLAT at Rs 900-926 Cr for
11 consecutive months (Jul-2025 to Jun-2026) despite continuous new-win
claims in exactly the highest-scoring geographies (B05 LBF1, B07
FLAG-ORDER-BOOK-PLATEAU), and concall guidance credibility is graded C
(three consecutive cuts, never framed as a cut, B07 FLAG-GUIDANCE-
CREDIBILITY). This evidence argues against applying the "aggressive"
3-5pp share-gain band (which the prompt reserves for cases with
demonstrated capacity and execution) and toward the low end of "normal"
(1-2pp in 3 years):

- **3-year SOM:** current 2.80% + 1.5pp (midpoint of normal band, not the
  top of it, given the flags above) = 4.30% of SAM = 0.0430 × Rs 14,700
  Cr = **Rs 632 Cr**. Implied 3-year revenue CAGR: (632/411)^(1/3) - 1 =
  **≈15.4%**.
- **5-year SOM:** current 2.80% + 2.5pp (upper-normal, not aggressive,
  reflecting real but execution-capped Saudi/Africa momentum) = 5.30% of
  SAM = 0.0530 × Rs 14,700 Cr = **Rs 779 Cr**. Implied 5-year revenue
  CAGR: (779/411)^(1/5) - 1 = **≈13.6%**.

**This SOM-implied revenue CAGR (13.6-15.4%) sits materially BELOW both
management's own FY27 guidance (20-25% organic growth) and the strategy's
25% CAGR target.** The gap is not necessarily a contradiction — SAM-share-
gain math and single-year guidance measure different things, and Section
5D below names real, unmodelled SAM-expansion levers the company is
pursuing — but it is the FORMAL cross-check this stage exists to produce,
and it argues that management's guided growth requires either (a) SAM
expansion beyond the MEA core computed here (West entry, Saudi civil/MEP,
animal health scale-up), or (b) an aggressive (3-5pp) share-gain rate that
the flat order book does not yet evidence.

### 3C Capacity cross-check

B07's capex-embedded-growth figure is **NOT APPLICABLE**: Fabtech is an
asset-light turnkey EPC business with FY26 PPE additions of Rs 470.81 lakh
against Rs 41,077.18 lakh revenue (1.1%) — there is no capex-driven
physical-capacity ceiling to test SOM against (B07).

The real capacity constraint evidenced in the corpus is NOT physical plant
capacity but **execution and working-capital capacity**. Two load-bearing
facts bind here: (i) the order book has been flat at Rs 900-926 Cr for 11
months despite a claimed Rs 9,300+ Cr enquiry pipeline and Rs 3,800+ Cr
"hot lead" pipeline (Inv. Pres. Q1 FY27 p.13) — meaning either conversion
capacity or delivery pace, not demand, is the binding constraint; (ii)
cash conversion is weak (consolidated CFO ≈Rs 0 FY26 on PAT ≈Rs 38 Cr,
debtor days 182, per B00 LBF2) — scaling revenue toward the Rs 632-779 Cr
SOM band at a constant 182-day debtor cycle would require incremental
trade receivables of roughly Rs 100-165 Cr (182/365 × incremental revenue
of Rs 221-368 Cr over current), which FY26's near-zero operating cash flow
does not show evidence of being able to self-fund.

**Gap/optimism call:** the SOM revenue trajectory (Rs 632-779 Cr by
year 3/5) is the side of the equation carrying the unresolved risk, not a
physical capacity ceiling. If working-capital conversion does not improve
(must-track metric per B04: CFO/PAT crossing 0.5x), the SOM figures above
are the optimistic assumption, contingent on financing (debt or further
equity) to fund the receivables build that the growth requires.

### 4C Market structure

Ten named competitors in the CRISIL-sourced list (RHP p.183-184 txt), spanning
from sub-scale regional specialists to Exyte GmbH (a multi-billion-euro
global engineering major with a broader semiconductor/data-centre/biopharma
mandate). No organised-vs-unorganised split is quantified for this niche —
it reads as a project-tender market of named specialist firms, not a
classic fragmented-informal-sector market (the standard 30-60% unorganised
assumption used elsewhere in Indian GARP analysis does not cleanly apply
here and is not forced onto this niche). Price vs differentiation: RHP
frames competition on project-management experience, regulatory navigation
and cost control rather than price alone, but Fabtech's own margin
narrative (contribution margin volatility 37.6% to 46.7% in one quarter,
Inv. Pres. Q1 FY27) suggests real price/scope competition on individual
tenders. Entries/exits: not disclosed for the specific niche; Fabtech's own
recent entries (Animal Health, Saudi civil/MEP via SACE) show the segment
boundary itself is actively shifting. Import share trend: management
claims a "no Indian/Chinese competitor" position, PARTIALLY CONTRADICTED by
peer evidence showing Indian peers (SETL) describe mixed Indian/
international competitive sets in adjacent categories (06-peers Q6) — the
claim likely overstates differentiation as a structural moat (B07
FLAG-I2-ZERO-BY-DESIGN reaches the same conclusion independently: Saudi
localisation is an execution lead, not a structural barrier).

```
  Rs 14,700 Cr/year   (≈2.2% of TAM)

        v

```

### 5B Runway assessment

- Revenue headroom = SAM ÷ current revenue = 14,700 ÷ 411 = **35.8x**.
- TAM (SAM-level) growth rate: MEA pharma capex CAGR 4.5-5.5% (2024-2029P,
  CRISIL) — take the lower, conservative **4.5%/year**.
- Company CAGR vs TAM: FY26 booked revenue growth was 25.8% YoY
  (screener), materially faster than the 4.5% SAM growth rate — Fabtech is
  **gaining share, not merely riding the market**, though the flat order
  book (LBF1) casts doubt on whether that share gain is continuing at the
  same pace into FY27.
- Years to saturate SAM at current (FY26) growth rate: ln(35.8)/ln(1.258)
  ≈ **15.7 years** — a long runway on paper, though sustaining a 25.8%
  CAGR for 15+ years is not a realistic base case and is shown here only
  as the arithmetic check the prompt requires.

### 5C Runway classification: **STRONG**

Revenue headroom of 35.8x would place this at the top end of the standard
matrix (approaching MASSIVE), but the classification is downgraded one
notch to STRONG given: (i) SAM growth itself is modest (4.5-5.5%, not a
booming category); (ii) the order-book-plateau and cash-conversion flags
(B07, B00 LBF1/LBF2) mean the realistic near-term SOM CAGR (13.6-15.4%)
sits well below headline growth claims; (iii) capability filter (project-
size ceiling vs global majors) is a real, if unquantified, constraint on
how much of the theoretical headroom is genuinely reachable.

### 5E Final output card

- **TAM:** Rs 6,80,000 Cr/year (conservative, global, 100%-capture,
  facility-eligible pharma capex) — Fabtech's actual addressable slice of
  this is a small, geography-bounded fraction (SAM below).
