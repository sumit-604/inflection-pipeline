## NOTE 17 (Other Non-Current Liabilities) / NOTE 21 (Other Current Liabilities) —
## DEFERRED GOVERNMENT INCENTIVE INCOME BALANCE — MISSED ENTIRELY BY PASS 1
(Standalone Note 17, p.120 PDF; Note 21 p.122 PDF; Consolidated Note 16/20 near-identical)

| Item | FY25 (₹Lakh/₹Cr) | FY24 (₹Lakh/₹Cr) | YoY % |
|---|---|---|---|
| Other Non-Current Liabilities — Deferred Incentive Income | 945.64 / 9.46 | 755.09 / 7.55 | **+25.2%** |
| Other Current Liabilities — Deferred Incentive Income | 111.16 / 1.11 | 111.16 / 1.11 | 0.0% |
| **Total Deferred Incentive Income (both lines)** | **1,056.80 / 10.57** | **866.25 / 8.66** | **+22.0%** |

- 🟡 Watch, and a **material nuance to Pass 1's Red Flag #2** (the ₹12.48 Cr one-time
  items / 59% of PBT finding). Cross-referencing the accounting policy (Note B.4(g)
  "Government Grants," p.106 standalone): grants tied to purchase/construction of
  non-current assets are "recognised as deferred revenue in the balance sheet and
  transferred to Profit and Loss on a systematic and rational basis over the useful
  lives of the related assets." The Deferred Incentive Income balance is **growing, not
  being drawn down** — the company continues to accrue new capital/incentive-linked
  government subsidy each year that it will recognise into future P&L over time. This
  means the state incentive income stream (the ₹745.63 lakh/₹6.34 Cr FY25 line Pass 1
  flagged) is **not purely one-time** at the balance-sheet level — there is a ₹10.57 Cr
  pool of deferred incentive income still to be released to future P&L, which is itself
  growing. Pass 1's framing ("strip these out and PBT would have fallen ~74%") is
  directionally sound for FY25's cash receipt, but the existence and growth of this
  deferred balance means a real (if modest, non-cash) tailwind to reported earnings
  will likely persist in coming years — worth flagging to the stage that assesses
  earnings quality/sustainability rather than treating the entire grant income as fully
  one-off and non-recurring.
- The revenue-recognition policy (Note B.4(f)(iv)) separately confirms "Industrial
  Promotion Incentive granted by State Government is recognised when claim in respect
  of Entitlement is made & admitted after close of yearly Sales Tax Assessment" —
  i.e., this is a Madhya Pradesh state industrial-promotion scheme (consistent with the
  company's Pithampur, MP manufacturing location), not a one-off ad hoc grant.

## NOTE 6.2 — ECL ALLOWANCE MOVEMENT vs NOTE 31/30 OTHER EXPENSES — CROSS-NOTE
## RECONCILIATION GAP — MISSED ENTIRELY BY PASS 1
(Standalone Note 6.2, p.112 PDF; Note 31 Other Expenses, p.127 PDF; Consolidated Note 30,
p.181 PDF)

- Note 6.2 (ECL allowance roll-forward) discloses, verbatim: **"Balance at the beginning
  of the year 128.39 [FY25] / 128.39 [FY24]"** and **"Movement in Expected Credit Loss
  Allowance 16.86 [FY25] / — [FY24]"**, ending balance 145.26 FY25 / 128.39 FY24. Read

  movement, opening = closing = 128.39).

  **"Provision for Expected Credit Loss: 16.86 [FY25] / 58.42 [FY24]"** — i.e. a
  ₹58.42 lakh charge hit the FY24 P&L for ECL provisioning.
- 🟡 Watch — **These two notes do not reconcile for FY24**: a ₹58.42 lakh P&L charge is

  year. Either the Note 6.2 FY24 comparative column is a drafting/template error
  (repeating the FY25 opening figure instead of showing a true FY23→FY24 roll-forward),
  or ₹58.42 lakh of ECL provisioning in FY24 was offset by an equal and undisclosed
  write-back/write-off that does not appear in either note (the "amount written off"
  line in Note 6.2 shows nil for both years). This is exactly the kind of inter-note
  inconsistency Pass 3's pattern-search should re-test; flagging here as a genuine new
  observation from the note-by-note re-read.

Only the three ratios that breach the mandatory 25% disclosure threshold (ROE, Net
Profit ratio, ROCE) carry management's one-line explanation ("largely on account on
higher input cost") — this itself is consistent with, and reinforces, Pass 1's Section
12 finding about that explanation being incomplete (it omits the one-time-items effect).
But two more findings are genuinely new here:

- 🟡 Watch — **Current Ratio fell 12.4% (1.35 from 1.54) and DSCR fell 15-17%** (to
  1.32x). Neither was covered anywhere in Pass 1. A DSCR of 1.32x, falling from 1.56-58x,
  combined with the net gearing rise Pass 1 already flagged, is a genuine liquidity
  deterioration signal — both fall short of the 25% mandatory-explanation threshold
  individually but tell a consistent story with the other working-capital findings above.
- 🟡 Watch — **Net Capital Turnover ratio rose ~20%** (10.07x from 8.40x), which reads as
  a positive efficiency signal in isolation, but the ratio's denominator (working capital
  = current assets − current liabilities) is **shrinking because the current ratio is
  deteriorating**, not because revenue efficiency improved. This is a case where a ratio
  moving in the "good" direction is actually a symptom of the same liquidity tightening
  flagged above, not an independent positive — worth flagging so later stages don't read
  it naively as a working-capital-efficiency improvement.
- 🟡 Watch — **Denominator-choice inconsistency across two "leverage" ratios in the same
  note set**: the Capital Management note (Note 39.1/38.1, already cited by Pass 1) shows
  Net Gearing Ratio (net debt ÷ year-end total equity) rising **0.42 → 0.46, +9.6%**,
  while the Ratio Analysis note (Note 43/42, same annual report) shows a similarly-named
  "Net Debt-Equity ratio" (net debt ÷ *average* equity) rising only **0.47 → 0.48,
  +1.3-1.6%**. The averaging convention in the second calculation mechanically dampens
  the visible YoY movement to well under the 25% disclosure threshold that would force a
  management explanation, even though the year-end-basis version (the more standard way
  investors read leverage) rose nearly 10%. Not evidence of intent, but a genuine
  pattern-level observation: the softer-looking ratio is the one management chose to
  formally test against the 25%-change disclosure rule.
