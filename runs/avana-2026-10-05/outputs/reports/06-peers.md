## PART 1: CLAIM-BY-CLAIM VERIFICATION

### Q1. Gross margin fall in H2 FY26 / H1 FY27 at relay and panel makers, and cause
- Claim: AVANA gross margin 47.80% to 40.61% (H2 36.61%); MD&A says no significant change (B05 peer_questions; B03; RES p10).
- Verdict: PARTIALLY VERIFIED (direction only; magnitude not matched).
- Peer evidence:
  - DANISH Nov-25 call, page 3: an analyst states "there was about 1-1.5% drop in gross margins" in H1. MD, page 4: "right now we do not see any significant challenge", margins on "similar lines" to the last 1-1.5 years. He does not dispute the drop. Page 21 (Shivam Talwar): the new capacity "had some factor to it" and H1 revenue was hit by an "abnormally high monsoon".
  - DANISH May-26 call, page 10: an analyst puts FY25 EBITDA margin at 21% and FY26 at 19%. The MD's reply puts 19-20% as sustainable (the 21% was perhaps an H2 figure). Page 5: FY26 EBITDA "approximately 19%" despite "commissioning and the ramp up costs".
  - DANISH May-26, pages 7, 14-15 (H1 FY27 read): West Asia war, transformer oil up "100% plus", copper and aluminium up. Q1 FY27 margin "somewhere be impacted"; older firm-price orders hit; new orders re-priced 5% to 15-18% (page 18). No number given for the hit.
  - MARINE (sheet): raw material cost / sales 70.9% FY25 to 70.5% FY26 (543.65/767.10; 618.36/876.94). Quarterly PBT margin Q1 FY27 9.2% vs Q1 FY26 9.3% (23.75/259.16; 15.47/166.98). Flat. Mix differs (marine, data centre).
  - SPCL (sheet): raw material cost / sales 83.7% FY25 to 82.0% FY26 (110.74/132.36; 127.42/155.40). Improved, not worse.
  - SPCL presentation page 37: H1 FY25 EBITDA margin 17.5% vs 21.0% H1 FY24 (-356 bps). Out of period. Shows margin swings of this size occur in panels in a growth half.
- Peers silent: none fully silent. MARINE and SPCL give no attribution text.
- Net read: The only call evidence shows a 1-2 point margin give-up at a capacity-ramp peer, attributed to ramp cost, seasonality and (from Q1 FY27) commodity shock. AVANA's 7.2 point gross fall (47.80 to 40.61) is three to seven times that and is not mirrored in MARINE or SPCL cost ratios. Peers do not explain AVANA's fall by a sector-wide input spike in FY26. They do give a sector reason for pressure in H1 FY27. AVANA's MD&A "no significant change" has no peer support.

## PART 4: TRIANGULATION SUMMARY

- Claims verified: 0 of 9.
- Claims partially verified: 7 (Q1, Q3, Q4, Q5, Q6, Q7, Q8). Unverifiable: 2 (Q2, Q9).
- Claims contradicted: 0. No peer states the opposite of an AVANA claim. Note for synthesis: peers do contradict the AVANA MD&A framing "no significant change" in margin as a sector-neutral statement, since the only call peer reports margin pressure (a few points) and AVANA's own gross margin fell 7.2 points. This is a framing gap, not a peer-stated contradiction of a claim in the handoff.
- Most consequential finding: AVANA's working-capital and cash profile matches SPCL (the nearest panel peer: stock 157 to 188 days, receivables 79 to 136 days, CFO -22.81, debt up) and not DANISH (64 and 78 days, CFO +42.50). AVANA inventory 164 days and receivables 96.7 days put it on the SPCL side of the split. Since AVANA's IPO capex was 23.9% deployed (B05), the working capital drain is the live use of IPO money. This is a peer-pattern point, not a verdict on AVANA's own cash flow.
- Strongest independent confirmation: DANISH's slip pattern (Q3) and over-nominal output in the peak half (Q8). Both support AVANA's claim that slips and above-nameplate output occur in this industry. Both come from one peer, hence PARTIAL.
- Overall: The peer set complicates the AVANA narrative. It supports the direction of sector demand and of schedule slips, and gives a plausible reading of 886 vs 600 panels as a shift-basis effect. It does not support the size of AVANA's growth (36.4% vs peers 14-22%), gives no sector reason for a 7.2 point gross margin fall in FY26, and shows the working-capital pattern that peer SPCL paid for with cash flow and debt. Against that, DANISH shows that a clean execution (IPO capex fully deployed, cash positive) is possible in the adjacent segment, which is what the AVANA thesis needs and has not yet delivered. Evidence is thin: one transcript peer in an adjacent segment, two decks, and no relay peer.

cross_peer_hypothesis: Post-IPO SME electrical equipment makers absorb cash into working capital faster than they deploy IPO proceeds into capex. DANISH FY25 (IPO year) CFO -14.40, SPCL FY25 -8.55 then FY26 -22.81 with borrowings 5.25 to 37.88, and AVANA's FY26 stock and receivable build with IPO capex 23.9% used (B05). Only DANISH escaped, in its second year (CFO +42.50), by hitting commissioning dates and shortening receivable days. Test: AVANA's H1 FY27 cash flow and IPO utilisation certificate. If receivable plus inventory days stay above ~160 and ~95 with capex utilisation under 50%, the pattern holds. If KIADB commissions and working-capital days fall, AVANA follows DANISH. Basis is two SME peers on sheet data, so low confidence. No single peer states this.
