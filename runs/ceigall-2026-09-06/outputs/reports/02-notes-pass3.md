### 1.1 Assets/liabilities held for sale (Rs 5,431.08m / Rs 3,302.65m, Note 19(a)/(b) consolidated) — RESOLVED, entity identified with numeric tie-out

Directors' Report, "State of Affairs of the Company" (sheet 34, printed
p.64): "In line with its asset monetization and capital recycling strategy,
the Company has divested its Special Purpose Vehicle (SPV), namely, Ceigall
Malout Abohar Sadhawali Highways Private Limited, in terms of the Share
Purchase Agreement dated June 03, 2026 and First Amendment to Share Purchase
Agreement dated June 16, 2026, [under which] 100% equity shares of Ceigall
Malout Abohar Sadhuwali Highways Private Limited held by Ceigall India
Limited together with Ceigall Infra Projects Private Limited, a wholly owned
subsidiary of the Company, has been transferred to Neo Infra Income
Opportunities Fund, managed by Neo Alternative Asset Managers Private
Limited (the 'Purchaser') on June 16, 2026."

Annexure-1 (Form AOC-1), "Additional Details," item 2 (sheet 40, printed
p.76): "Names of subsidiaries which have been liquidated or sold during the
year: Ceigall Malout Abohar Sadhuwali Highways Pvt. Ltd.: Since the binding
offer has been entered into with Neo Asset Management Private Limited and
the same has been classified as asset held for sale."

Annexure-2 (project-status annexure), item ii (sheet 41, printed p.78):
"Status of the Project: The project has been completed, and the Company has
being divested to Neo Infra Income Opportunities Fund, by way of Share
Purchase Agreement on 16/06/2026."

NUMERIC TIE-OUT (the identification is not only narrative): the AOC-1
Part-A table (sheet 39, printed p.74) gives Ceigall Malout Abohar Sadhuwali
Highways Private Limited's standalone Total Assets as Rs 5,431.08 million —
an EXACT match, to the rupee, of consolidated Note 19(a)'s "Assets
classified as held for sale" of Rs 5,431.08 million. The same AOC-1 row
gives Total Liabilities of Rs 4,069.94 million, which does not exactly match
Note 19(b)'s Rs 3,302.65 million; the residual (~Rs 767 million) is plausibly
the Ind AS 105 fair-value-less-costs-to-sell remeasurement or a
consolidation-basis difference (the AOC-1 figure is the SPV's own standalone
balance sheet, not the group's held-for-sale carrying value), but this
residual is NOT itself explained by any note read. 🟡 Watch, resolved on
entity identity, open on the liabilities-side gap.

What remains genuinely NOT FOUND IN DOCUMENT after this full search: the
sale consideration, the expected or realised gain/loss on divestment, and
any Ind AS 105 fair-value-less-costs-to-sell disclosure. This is the first
hard evidence of the stated capital-recycling strategy executing (Corporate
Overview, sheet 32, printed p.58: "The Company's first such transaction —
the divestiture of its Malout-Abohar HAM asset — marks the start of what is
intended to be a sustained, repeatable capital monetisation cycle"), but the
economics of the transaction are undisclosed in this Annual Report. The
transaction date (SPA 3-Jun-2026, completion 16-Jun-2026) sits AFTER the
31-March-2026 balance sheet date but BEFORE the report's signing date
(8-Aug-2026) — i.e. it is properly treated as a subsequent event that
confirms rather than creates the year-end held-for-sale classification. This
is a direct question for management (consideration, gain/loss, use of
proceeds) rather than an accounting defect.

### 1.2 Consolidated vs standalone FY25 bank-guarantee inconsistency (Rs 3,411.75m vs Rs 8,403.35m) — CONFIRMED, UNRESOLVED

Searched the Directors' Report (sheet 34, "Borrowings" section, item 18,
sheet 36 printed p.68: "As on March 31, 2026, your Company's (Standalone)
fund-based facilities availed stood at Rs 4,122.68 million and non-fund-
based credit facilities availed stood at Rs 10,280.34 million" — this
CONFIRMS the standalone FY26 non-fund-based/guarantee figure of Rs 10,280.34
million independently of the Notes, but gives no FY25 comparator and no
consolidated figure), the MD&A (sheets 26-33, no contingent-liability or
guarantee reconciliation content at all), and the Notice of AGM (sheets
133-151, no reference to guarantee reconciliation; the RPT resolutions
there concern loan/investment ceilings for FY2026-27, not FY25/FY26
contingent liabilities). NO note anywhere in the 151-sheet document
reconciles standalone and consolidated contingent liabilities.

VERDICT ON THIS ITEM: the inconsistency Pass 2 confirmed at tighter
resolution (consolidated FY25 bank guarantees Rs 3,411.75m sitting at less
than half the standalone FY25 figure of Rs 8,403.35m, while the two bases
converge almost exactly in FY26: Rs 10,286.34m consolidated vs Rs 10,280.34m
standalone) is CONFIRMED as a genuine, anchored, unreconciled inconsistency
in the filed accounts, not a resolution artefact and not explainable from
any document in this corpus. It is elevated here to a direct management
question and a named verification target for the live-web stage (a
possible explanation — a large SPV-level guarantee that existed at
standalone FY25 but was eliminated or reclassified on consolidation, and
which unwound by FY26 — cannot be tested without further disclosure).

was specifically on IPO-related expenses — the same cost pool the Company
separately represents (Note 54, IPO-utilisation) as "fully accounted for."
No quantum, penalty, or interest figure is disclosed for the DGGI matter
anywhere in the document (NOT FOUND IN DOCUMENT). This finding sits outside
the Notes to Financial Statements strictly defined, but was encountered
directly during the Pass 3 targeted search the run brief authorised, and is
too material to omit.

5. Beyond the Rs 89.65m procurement fraud, what is the status and quantum of the DGGI GST search action (14-May-2025) on wrongful ITC availment tied to IPO-related expenses, and has any penalty or interest since been assessed?

## E. NOTES-BASED RED FLAGS

- Earnings management signal: none found in revenue timing itself (PoC input method is standard for the industry), but the reverse-factoring-inside-trade-payables treatment and the "deemed investment" reclassification of related-party loans both have the effect of presenting the balance sheet more favourably than a like-for-like peer would.
- Aggressive accounting: nil impairment provisioning against a rapidly growing Rs 28.7bn non-cash receivable-type asset base (Contract Assets + RUSCA) is the most quantitatively significant item.
- Undisclosed risk indicators found and now surfaced: the RUSCA note (missed entirely by Pass 1), the held-for-sale note's missing narrative (only found via the Directors' Report/AOC-1), and the DGGI search action (found via the Secretarial Audit Report, outside the Notes proper but material).
- Governance red flags: procurement fraud with qualified IFC opinion; separate DGGI search action; a step-down subsidiary auditor resignation combined with two years of cash losses at that entity; three CEO-level changes in ~12 months.

## F. ONE-LINE NOTES VERDICT

The notes reveal concerning accounting practices. Key concern: Contract Assets and Service Concession receivables have grown to roughly 71% of one year's revenue with nil impairment provisioning, while contingent liabilities, MSME payment-term stretching, and two separate governance/compliance failures (procurement fraud and a DGGI GST search) all landed in the same year. Key strength: the related-party SPV funding web and the IPO-proceeds utilisation are both fully and formally disclosed, and the underlying order book (up 71.7% YoY) reflects genuine operating growth, not manufactured revenue. Overall accounting quality: 3/10.
