## 1. RPT ASSET-SALE ECONOMICS - DEEPENED (challenges Pass 1's framing)

Pass 1 flagged the Rs 3,397.39 lakh FY26 related-party asset sales as a
red flag with "no independent valuation... disclosed." Cross-referencing
Note 1 (PP&E rollforward, AR p.93/131) against Note 34 (Exceptional Item,
AR p.106/144) and Note 40 (RPT, AR p.108/146) gives the actual economics
of each disposal, which Pass 1 did not compute:

- **Office Premises** (sold to Mrs. Pranika Jain Rs 700.00 lakh + Mrs.
  Sreelekha Jain Rs 700.00 lakh = Rs 1,400.00 lakh total, Note 40): Note 1
  shows FY26 gross-block "Buildings" deduction of Rs 802.64 lakh against
  accumulated-depreciation deduction of Rs 337.36 lakh, i.e. **net book
  value (WDV) of Rs 465.28 lakh disposed**. Rs 1,400.00 lakh proceeds
  minus Rs 465.28 lakh WDV = **Rs 934.72 lakh profit, exactly matching
  Note 34's "Profit on sale of Office Premises."** The office premises
  were sold to family members at **3.01x net book value** - this single
  transaction supplies 92% of the entire Rs 1,014.75 lakh exceptional
  item. (Note 1/34/40, AR p.93, 106, 108) 🟡 Watch, refined: a sale at 3x
  book value is a PROFIT to the company, not a loss - the arithmetic runs
  the opposite direction from a classic "cheap sale to insiders"
  pattern. Real-estate book value in a 60-year-SLM/WDV schedule is
  routinely a fraction of current market value after decades of use, so
  a large gain over book does not by itself establish an unfair (or a
  fair) price. Absent an independent valuation of the *specific* office
  premises (only the *remaining* investment property carries an
  independent valuer's opinion, Note 4(iii)), fairness relative to true
  market value - not book value - remains unverified either way. Pass 1's
  framing should be read alongside this: the exceptional gain is real and
  large, but its size argues against (not for) the assets having been
  sold cheap.
- **Vehicles** (sold to Sajal Jain Rs 109.00 + Pranika Jain Rs 28.39 +
  Sreelekha Jain Rs 415.00 = Rs 552.39 lakh total): Note 1 shows FY26
  gross-block "Vehicles" deduction Rs 1,117.76 lakh against accumulated
  depreciation deduction Rs 525.35 lakh, i.e. **WDV disposed of Rs 592.41
  lakh**. Rs 552.39 lakh proceeds minus Rs 592.41 lakh WDV = a loss of Rs
  40.02 lakh, reconciling (to the lakh) with Note 34's Rs 40.01 lakh
  "Loss on sale of Vehicles." Vehicles were sold at 93.2% of book value -
  an ordinary used-vehicle disposal, not an aggressive-looking RPT.
  (Note 1/34/40, AR p.93, 106, 108) 🟢 Clean (routine, reconciles exactly).

**Net effect on Pass 1's Red Flag #1**: the RPT-sale red flag survives,
but its substance narrows to one transaction (Office Premises, Rs 1,400
lakh proceeds / Rs 934.72 lakh gain) rather than the whole Rs 3,397 lakh
disposal pool, and the direction of the gain (large profit over book, not
a loss) is evidence that cuts against, not for, an underpriced insider
sale - the open question is whether Rs 1,400 lakh represents true FMV,
not whether it was a giveaway.

disposals of Rs 4,129.95 lakh (opening Rs 7,981.43 lakh -> closing Rs
3,851.48 lakh), with zero accumulated depreciation on investment property

"asset sales to the exiting family" narrative (Pass 1's framing) applies
fully to the Office Premises (100% RPT) and Vehicles (100% RPT) disposals,
but only partially (about 34% by value) to the largest single disposal
line, Investment Property. The counterparty for the remaining ~Rs 2,805
lakh: NOT FOUND IN DOCUMENT (Note 4 gives no counterparty detail; this
would need Board's Report / Reg 30 disclosure, outside Notes scope).

## 4. NOTE 41 FAIR VALUE HIERARCHY: THE LEVEL 3 FIGURE IS THE SUBSIDIARY
INVESTMENT, NOT THE MERCEDES BENZ PREFERENCE SHARES
(directly corrects a Pass 1 finding)

Pass 1 stated the Mercedes Benz Financial Services Pvt Ltd unquoted
preference shares (Rs 1,041.30 lakh, Note 5(a)) are "classified Level 3
fair value (Note 41)." Re-reading Note 41 (Financial Instruments, AR
p.109 standalone / p.147 consolidated) shows this is not correct:

- The STANDALONE Note 41 table lists only two itemised Level-1 rows
  ("Shares" Rs 1,171.91 lakh, "Mutual funds" Rs 18,535.82/19,770.45(FY25
  err, see below) lakh) and a single un-itemised "Level 3" amount of
  Rs 1,040.93 lakh that appears ONLY in the Total row, in BOTH FY26 and
  FY25 (unchanged) - the note never states what asset this Rs 1,040.93
  lakh represents.
- Rs 1,040.93 lakh matches, to the rupee-lakh, Note 5's Investment in
  Subsidiary (Indoborax Infrastructure Pvt Ltd, carried at COST per
  accounting policy (e), unchanged both years) - NOT the Mercedes Benz
  preference shares, which are Rs 1,041.30 lakh (a different, merely
  similar-looking number).
- Proof: the CONSOLIDATED Note 41 (AR p.147), where the parent's
  investment in the (now line-by-line consolidated) subsidiary is
  eliminated on consolidation, shows Level 3 = nil ("-") in BOTH years,
  while consolidated "Shares" Level 1 stays at exactly Rs 1,171.91 lakh -
  identical to the standalone figure. If the Level 3 balance had been the
  Mercedes Benz preference shares (which are NOT eliminated on
  consolidation, since they sit at the parent), consolidated Note 41
  would still show a Level 3 balance. It does not. This confirms the
  standalone Level 3 amount is the subsidiary investment.

This means the truly unquoted, Level-3-appropriate Mercedes Benz
preference shares (Rs 1,041.30 lakh, ~2.7% of net worth) are not shown
anywhere in the Level 1/2/3 split - they must sit inside the Rs 1,171.91
lakh "Shares" Level 1 total (Note 5(a)'s own total nets to exactly Rs
1,171.91 lakh only if the unquoted Mercedes Benz stake, ~Rs 1,041.30
lakh, is combined with ~Rs 130.61 lakh of genuinely quoted shares - Note
5(a)'s own footnote "Aggregate amount of quoted investments Rs 130.61
lakh" confirms only Rs 130.61 lakh of the Rs 1,171.91 lakh total is
actually quoted). 🔴 Red Flag (disclosure quality, corrects Pass 1): Note
41 (a) fails to itemise its own Level 3 line, (b) includes a
cost-carried, non-fair-valued subsidiary investment inside a table
titled "recurring fair value measurements," and (c) appears to
misclassify a materially unquoted equity instrument (Mercedes Benz
preference shares) as Level 1. (Note 5/5(a)/41, standalone AR p.96,
109; consolidated AR p.134, 147)

## 5. RETURN ON EQUITY RATIO (NOTE 45(d)/46(d)) IS POPULATED WITH EPS
FIGURES, NOT A COMPUTED ROE% (new finding - systematic, not a one-off)

Standalone Note 45(d) "Return on Equity Ratio" (numerator stated as "Net
Profit after Tax," denominator "Average Shareholder's Equity") reports
15.50 (FY26) and 13.05 (FY25). These exactly equal standalone Basic/
Diluted EPS (Note 36: Rs 15.50 and Rs 13.05). A true ROE using the
stated formula is approximately 13.8% for FY26 (Net Profit Rs 4,973.97
lakh / average equity of Rs 35,927.51 lakh = 35,927.51 avg of Rs
38,455.18 and Rs 33,399.83) - materially different from the 15.50
disclosed. The identical pattern repeats in the CONSOLIDATED Note 46(d):
reported 15.67/13.25, exactly equal to consolidated Basic/Diluted EPS
(Note 36 consol: Rs 15.67/13.25), not a computed ROE%. 🟡 Watch
(accounting-quality/disclosure-integrity finding): the same
mis-population occurs in both the standalone and the consolidated
ratio note, indicating a systematic error in preparing the Schedule
III-mandated ratio disclosure, not a single typo. All other ratios in
Note 45/46 (current ratio, inventory turnover, ROCE, ROI, etc.) check
out arithmetically against the underlying notes and are not affected.
(Note 45(d), standalone AR p.113; Note 46(d), consolidated AR p.151)

## 6. CREDIT RISK NOTE IS SILENT ON THE SINGLE LARGEST CONCENTRATED
EXPOSURE (sharpens Pass 1 Red Flag #2)

Note 43(A) Credit Risk (AR p.111 standalone / p.149 consolidated) states:
"the Company is not exposed to any significant credit risk exposure to
any single counterparty or any company of counterparties having similar
characteristics... The Company has very limited history of customer
default, and considers the credit quality of trade receivables that are
not past due or impaired to be good." This qualitative narrative appears
only in the context of "trade and other receivables" and never mentions,
qualifies, or excludes the Rs 509.04 lakh Inter Corporate Deposit to
Radius Estates Projects Pvt Ltd - a single counterparty under active
IBC insolvency proceedings, 1.26% of total standalone assets, and the
Company's single largest non-trade credit exposure. The dedicated
Ind AS 107 credit-risk note - the note whose specific purpose is to
disclose concentration risk - is silent on this exposure; it is
disclosed only in Note 6 (loan schedule) and Note 47 (contingent
liability-adjacent note). 🔴 Red Flag (sharpens Pass 1 #2): the gap is
not just "no ECL provision" (Pass 1's finding) but that the note
structurally designed to surface concentration risk omits the company's
one clear concentration.

## 7. SUBSIDIARY RAN THE SAME TREASURY ROLL-UP AS THE PARENT, AT SMALLER
SCALE (new finding)

Comparing standalone vs consolidated Note 7 (Other Non-Current Financial
Assets): "Fixed Deposit with banks" was Rs 790.00 lakh standalone but Rs
1,890.00 lakh consolidated at FY25 - a Rs 1,100.00 lakh difference
attributable to the subsidiary (Indoborax Infrastructure Pvt Ltd) holding
its own bank FDs. By FY26 this gap closes to nil (both standalone and
consolidated show Rs 205.00 lakh). Over the same period, consolidated
Note 9 (current investments, mutual funds) exceeds the standalone total
by Rs 1,234.63 lakh at FY26 (Rs 19,770.45 lakh consol vs Rs 18,535.82
lakh standalone) - a gap of similar size to the FD reduction. Read
together, this indicates the wholly-owned subsidiary independently rolled
roughly Rs 1,100-1,235 lakh of its own bank fixed deposits into mutual
funds during FY26 - the same treasury move Pass 1 already flagged at the
parent level (Finding #4), now shown to have also occurred inside the
subsidiary. 🟡 Watch (adds colour, not a new red flag): Form AOC-I's own
"Investments" line for the subsidiary shows only Rs 0.54 lakh (AR p.153),
which does not reconcile with the ~Rs 1,235 lakh of mutual fund exposure
implied by the consolidation differential - likely a narrower AOC-I
definition of "Investments" (e.g. investments in other companies'
shares only, excluding treasury mutual funds/current investments), but
the note itself does not explain the gap. NOT FOUND IN DOCUMENT
(reconciling explanation).

## 8. LEAVE ENCASHMENT ACTUARIAL TABLE: CURRENT SERVICE COST EXACTLY
EQUALS BENEFITS PAID (new finding, numerical anomaly worth verification)

In the Leave Encashment (unfunded) actuarial table (AR p.105/143), FY26
"Current service cost" is Rs 110.18 lakh and FY26 "Benefits paid" is also
exactly Rs (110.18) lakh - an exact match between two figures that are
independently derived (one a forward actuarial accrual for the whole
active workforce, the other actual cash paid to departing employees) and
would not normally coincide to the rupee. Separately, the DBO
roll-forward shows an actuarial (gain) of Rs (35.06) lakh as a component
of the obligation movement, but the "Expenses for the year" sub-table's
"Net actuarial (gain)/loss recognised in the year" row for Leave
Encashment shows nil, and "Total Expenses Recognised" of Rs 110.18 lakh
equals current service cost alone - the Rs (35.06) lakh actuarial gain
does not appear to flow through the stated P&L total. 🟡 Watch: this may
be a coincidence of a pay-as-you-go unfunded scheme experiencing a large
KMP-exit-driven payout in the same year (plausible given the
23-Jan-2026 leadership change, as Pass 1 already suggested), or it may
be a note-preparation figure duplicated across two cells. Not large
enough (Rs 110-146 lakh range) to be material to the P&L, but the
internal arithmetic of this specific actuarial table does not fully
tie out from the disclosures given. NOT FOUND IN DOCUMENT (an explicit
reconciliation of the actuarial-gain treatment). (Note 30, AR p.105/143)
