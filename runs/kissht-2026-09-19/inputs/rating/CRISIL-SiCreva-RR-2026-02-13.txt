Detailed Rationale
C risil Ratings has upgraded its ratings on the bank loan facilities and debt instruments of Si Creva Capital Services Pvt Ltd (Si Creva) to 'C risil A- /Stable/C risil A 1'  from 'Crisil BBB+/Stable/Crisil A2+’ . Crisil Rating s has also assigned its   ‘ C risil A-/Stable ’   rating to the   Rs 2 00 crore   Non-Convertible Debentures ( NCD )   of Si Creva.
C risil Ratings has withdrawn   its ratings on redeemed NCDs aggregating Rs 1 59 crore (See 'Annexure - Details of Rating Withdrawn’ for details) on receipt of requisite documentation for redempti on and at client’s request. The withdrawal is in line with the Crisil’s Ratings’ policy on withdrawal of ratings.
Si Creva is a wholly owned subsidiary of OnEMI Technology Solutions Ltd (OnEMI). Both the entities together are herein referred to as Kissht group. The group primarily offers personal loans and has started disbursing loan against property (LAP) loans too, from fiscal 2025.
The rating action is driven by increased scale of operations while maintaining healthy profitability. The group has seen a strong growth with assets under management (AUM) increasing to Rs 5,533 crore as on September 30, 2025 from Rs 4087 crore as on March 31, 2025 and Rs 1,268 crore as on March 31, 2023. Personal loans comprised around 96% of the AUM as on September 30, 2025 and LAP formed the remaining 4%.
The group’s earnings profile has remained comfortable with consolidated profit after tax (PAT) of Rs 122 crore for the first half of fiscal 2026, translating into a healthy return on managed asset ( RoMA ) of 4.7 % as agains t PAT of Rs 161 crore and 4.4% of RoMA for fiscal 2025. The group is expected to maintain similar profitability levels over the medium term too. In terms of asset quality , gross non-performing assets (GNPA) stood at 2.95%, and write-offs and FLDG cost for trailing 12-months (as % of AUM) was at 8.5% as on September 30, 2025 . With tightened underwriting and strengthened collection processes, delinquencies are expected to improve and will remain a monitorable.
The overall ratings   also continue to   reflect   heal thy capitalization of the group and scalable business model. Nevertheless, these strengths are   partially   offset by   the   inherent vulnerability of asset quality metrics given the   borrower   segment and modest resource profile.
Analytical Approach
Si Creva is a wholly owned subsidiary of OnEMI Technology Solutions Limited (OnEMI), which was incorporated in 2016 as a technology platform running under the brand name – Kissht. Crisil Ratings has considered the consolidated business and financial risk profile s o f the group ( OnEMI and Si Creva ) as there are significant operational and managerial linkages between the companies.

Key Rating Drivers - Strengths
Healthy c apitalisation m etrics
Capitalisation metrics remain healthy supported by regular capital infusion and healthy internal accruals. The networth of the group remained comfortable at Rs 1,173 crore as on September 3 0 , 2025. Gearing too   was comfortable at 1. 7 times as on same date and is expected to remain under 3   times on a steady-state basis. Capitalisation metrics are expected to be further bolstered post completion of listing of parent OnEMI Technology Solutions. The company received SEBI approval in January 2026 for its upcoming IPO, which includes a fresh issue of up to Rs 1,000 crore. Of this, Rs 750 crore of capital is expected to be infused into Si Creva and remaining up to Rs 250 crore will be used for general administrative purposes.
Crisil   Ratings   expects networth to be supported by timely capital raise and profits, thus providing a cushion against asset-side risks.
Scalable   business model aided by technology-based sourcing and assessment
For providing unsec ured loans, t he group operates through a branchless business model with end-to-end operations i.e. from origination to disbursements happening digitally. The group generates its leads from two sources – digital channels and offline merchant partnerships . With sourcing being   done   online, the customer acquisition cost is lower. In addition to this, the entire risk assessment process is also digitalized , with minimal manual intervention, thereby resulting in lower turnaround time.
Starting fiscal 2025, the gr oup increased it’s focus on longer-tenor loan products from short-term product offerings. This resulted in increase average loan tenor from 2.9 months in f iscal 2024 to 9.7 months in f iscal 2025. Given the shift in product strategy, there was slow - down in disbursements in the first quarter of fiscal 2025 post which the disbursements p icked up. Total disbursements for fiscal 2025 and H1 fiscal 2026 stood at Rs 9,858 crore and Rs 5,742 crore, respectively.
Consequently, the AUM has grown to Rs 5, 533 crore a s on September 30 , 2025 when compared to Rs 4,087 as on March 3 1 , 2025 (Rs 2,604 crore as on March 30, 2024). Growth in AUM has also been aided by the growth in co-lending/business correspondence partnerships which have gone up to Rs 2,717 crore (49% of AUM) as on September 30, 2025 from Rs 1,129 crore as on March 31, 2024 .
The group has also started loans against property (LAP) business under a branch-led model, establishing over 75 branches as of September 2025. While this is at a nascent stage with AUM of Rs 226 crore as on September 30, 2025, the group plans to scale this business over the near to medium term.
Comfortable earnings profile
Besides on-balance sheet loan book, the group has also grown its AUM through partnerships with other financial institutions under the co-lending model. Given that Si Creva acts as a s ourcing non-banking financial company ( NBFC ) in all of its co-lending arrangements, it provides the group an opportunity to earn a higher fee income on its managed portfolio.
For the half year ended September 30, 2025, the group reported a PAT of Rs 122 crore and RoMA of 4.7% ( Rs 161 crore an d 4.4% , respectively, for fiscal 2025) . The group’s net total income (as a % of managed assets) stood at around 32% for the half year ended September 30, 2025 (33% for fiscal 2025). While interest margins came down dur ing the period on account of both reduction in average yields and higher interest expenses given increase in leverage levels , overall income was supported by healthy growth in fee income during the period. Also, with increase in AUM, operating expenses, though remained high, has improved to 14.5% (as % of average managed assets) for the half year ended September 30, 2025 as compared 16.1% for fiscal 2025. Credit costs for the period stood at 8.5% (annualized) as compared to 9% for fiscal 20 25 . The company a lso provides first loss default guarantee (FLDG) for the co-lending partnerships; the same stood at 3.3% for the half year ended September 30, 2025 (1.7% for fiscal 2025).
Going forward, the company’s ability to maintain comfortable profitability metrics while achieving scale, will remain a monitorable.
Key Rating Drivers - We a knesses
Inherent v ulnerability in asset quality owing to risk s associated with the asset class
Gross non-performing assets (GNPA) stood at 2.95% as on September 30, 2025 and was at similar levels as on March 31, 2025 (2.92%). Nevertheless, write-offs and FLDG cost for trailing 12-months (as % of AUM) improved to 8.5% as on September 30, 2025 from 12.2% as on March 31, 2025. This was primarily on account of increased focus towards longer tenure and larger ticket sized loans as comp ared to before. Further, the company has also tightened its underwriting norms and strengthened its collection processes. While these measures have shown early signs of improvement in the collection efficiency, sustained improvement in asset quality will need to be seen.
The group has put in place strong risk management systems and processes. The lending decision is primarily based on the output from the proprietary credit risk model, which is reviewed at regular intervals. Nevertheless, t he group provide s unsecured personal loans, wherein asset quality metrics remain vulnerable to slippages. Therefore, the ability to maintain asset quality while scaling up the loan portfolio remains a key monitorable. Furthermore, given its segment of operations, the group is vulnerable to regulatory changes and any material adverse impact on the business risk profile on account of the same will also be a key monitorable.
Modest resource profile
The resource profile as on September 30, 2025 comprised of 48% NCDs, 31% term loans (19% from NBFCs and ~12% from banks), 18% direct assignments/pass through certificates (PTCs) and 3% commercial papers (CPs). The groups’ cost of borrowings was relatively higher with average cost of borrowing at 13.3% for the half year ended September 30, 2025 (13.7% for fiscal 2025). Ability to raise sanctions from banks and financial institutions at competitive rates remains a key monitorable.
Liquidity   Strong
As on December 31, 2025, the group had unencumbered cash, cash equivalent and liquid investment of Rs 290.4 crore. This is sufficient to cover one month debt repayments. Additionally,  the group’s average   monthly collections   stood at   over   Rs 5 00 crore   over the last six months. Crisil Ratings also understands that   Si Creva will   not   do any incremental disbursements   unless   it h as   adequate   resources to service maturing debt obligations in the near term; this will remain a key monitorable.
Outlook  Stable
C risil Ratings   believes   group   will continue to maintain healthy capitalisation metrics and comfortable earnings profile
Rating s ensitivity factors
Upward factors:
Increase in scale of operations while maintaining healthy asset quality
Sustain ed profitability with RoMA maintained at around 4.5% over the medium term
Downward factors:
Increase in steady-state gearing of above 3.5 times
Adverse movement in asset quality leading to substantial impact on earnings and capitalisation metrics
About the Company
Si Creva is a systemically important non-deposit taking NBFC (ND-NBFC). The company commenced operations in 2018 and is a wholly owned subsidiary of OnEMI, which is the technology platform and is run under the brand Kissht. The group entity is promoted by Mr Ranvir Singh and Mr Krishnan Vishwanathan, who have a prior experience in the financial services and technology domain .
The product offerings include unsecured personal loans with tenure of six months to thirty six months a nd a ticket size of up to Rs 5 lakhs . The group has also started LAP business recently in a branch-led model, offering loans of tenure up to 10 years and ticket size up to Rs 20 lakhs.
