### P2. CONFIRMED (independently re-verified, not inherited) — Note 7 vs Note 23 raw-material gap is real

Re-read directly from the source text this pass, not taken from Pass 2's report:

- Note 7 (Inventories, PDF p.92, printed p.177, exact text): "Raw material ... 19,284.21
  ... 5,367.62" (FY26 / FY25, ₹ Lakh).
- Note 23 (Cost of Material Consumed, PDF p.95-96, printed p.185): "Inventory at the
  beginning of the year 5,367.62 ... Add: Purchase 86,679.20 ... Less: Inventory at the end
  of the year -14,250.98 ... Cost of Material Consumed 77,795.84" (FY26 column; the FY25
  opening ties exactly to Note 7's FY25 raw-material closing balance, and Cost of Material
  Consumed ₹77,795.84 Lakh ties exactly to the P&L face).

Note 23's own arithmetic implies an FY26 raw-material closing balance of ₹14,250.98 Lakh.
Note 7 states the FY26 raw-material closing balance is ₹19,284.21 Lakh. **The gap is
₹5,033.23 Lakh (₹50.33 Cr), confirmed by direct reading of both source tables, not an
extraction defect.** Both notes are individually clean and internally consistent; they
disagree with each other on the same balance sheet line, and no note or auditor commentary
in the corpus explains the difference. Note 2's accounting policy states that gold received
under Gold Metal Loan and gold-lease arrangements "is recognised as inventory with a
corresponding liability at the prevailing gold price on the date of receipt" — i.e. outside
the conventional cash "Purchase" line (Note 2, PDF p.86-87, printed p.169, confirmed by
direct reading this pass). Note 12 shows combined GML + Leased Gold liabilities of
₹6,010.32 Lakh at FY26-end against ₹0 at FY25-end (Note 12, PDF p.94, printed p.183) — the
same order of magnitude as the ₹50.33 Cr gap, though not an exact tie-out (the residual
could be MTM revaluation or timing). **[INFERENCE, not confirmed]**: a material share of
the FY26 raw-material increase is GML/gold-lease stock entering inventory directly against
a liability, bypassing Note 23's purchase-and-consumption roll-forward — which would mean
Note 23's Cost of Material Consumed **understates** true FY26 gold throughput. This remains
the single most load-bearing open question for LBF1 and should go to management directly
(Q1 below) or be cross-checked against quarterly concall gram data at stage 5/6. 🔴

### D. FIVE QUESTIONS FOR MANAGEMENT

1. Reconcile Note 7's FY26 raw-material closing balance (₹192.84 Cr) against Note 23's
   implied closing balance (₹142.51 Cr, per its own Opening + Purchase − Closing roll-
   forward). What does the ₹50.33 Cr gap represent — is it Gold Metal Loan / gold-lease
   stock recognised as inventory outside the conventional Purchase line, per Note 2's own
   policy?
2. What share of FY26's ₹18.60 Cr "Profit Th. MCX Hedging" (booked inside Revenue from
   Operations, nil in FY25) is realised versus unrealised mark-to-market, and what would
   FY26 revenue growth (currently +22.24%) look like excluding it?
3. What drove the 42 Kg unfixed/unhedged gold position (10 Kg Gold Metal Loan + 32 Kg Gold
   on Lease) at 31-Mar-2026 against zero a year earlier, given the same note's own
   description of a "natural hedge" from fixing gold price at the time of sale?
4. What does "Revenue from Non-Retail Operations" comprise, and why did it decline 5.96%
   (₹530.98 Cr to ₹499.32 Cr) in a year Retail Operations grew 27.61%?
5. With total borrowings up 76.8% to ₹290.91 Cr and every facility backstopped by personal
   guarantees and pledged family property, what is the plan to delever, refinance onto
   longer tenor, or raise fresh equity as store count scales toward the FY30 guidance,
   given the ₹600 Cr QIP approved Feb-2025 has been postponed?

### E. NOTES-BASED RED FLAGS

- Earnings management signal: none confirmed as deliberate — but the presentation choice of
  booking MCX hedging gains inside Revenue from Operations (finding 3), combined with the
  auditor's own Revenue Recognition KAM (finding 2), is a genuine aggressive-presentation
  concern worth naming, distinct from outright manipulation.
- Aggressive accounting: the Note 7/Note 23 unexplained gap (finding 1) is the clearest
  candidate; not confirmed as manipulation, but confirmed as a real, unexplained internal
  inconsistency in the audited notes, independently re-verified this pass.
- Undisclosed risk indicator: the 42 Kg unfixed/unhedged gold position (finding 4)
  contradicts the "natural hedge" framing used elsewhere in the same note — a disclosed
  fact that undercuts the company's own risk narrative, not an undisclosed risk per se.
- No evidence found of: round-tripping, layering, undisclosed related-party benefit,
  wilful-defaulter status, struck-off-company dealings, crypto exposure, or a qualified/
  adverse audit opinion (all explicitly negative and clean, Notes 33.16-33.21 and the
  Independent Auditor's Report).

### F. ONE-LINE NOTES VERDICT

The notes reveal moderate accounting practices, granular but not fully self-consistent.
Key concern: an unexplained ₹50 Cr gap between Note 7 and Note 23's raw-material figures,
alongside a ~285x hedging-gain jump booked inside operating revenue. Key strength: an
unqualified audit opinion, immaterial contingent liabilities, a corrected (improving, not
worsening) related-party borrowing trend, and full transparency once the extraction defect
was resolved. Overall accounting quality: 6/10.
