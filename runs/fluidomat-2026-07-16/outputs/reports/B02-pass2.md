## NOTE-BY-NOTE: NEW FINDINGS ONLY

### Note 45a (Related Party roster, p.102) — stale and incomplete in Pass 1

Pass 1 covered the remuneration table (45b) and the non-remuneration transactions (45c) fully, but did not
read the full party roster in 45a against the Corporate Governance Report's current board composition. Doing
so now surfaces two new items:

1. **Stale independent directors still on the related-party roster.** Note 45a (p.102) lists, as related parties for
   FY25: "4. Mr. Praful R. Turakhia — Independent Director; 5. Mr. M. K. Shah — Independent Director; 6. Mr. K.
   C. Jain — Independent Director" — alongside, separately, "16. Mr. Ashok Kumar Patni — Independent
   Director; 17. Mr. Samyak Modi — Independent Director; 18. Mr. Sharad Panot — Independent Director" (Note
   45a, p.102). Per the Corporate Governance Report (p.53, Directors' Profile table), the current independent
   directors — Patni, Panot, Modi — were all appointed 14/08/2023, meaning Turakhia, Shah and K.C. Jain had
   already left the Board before the FY25 reporting period began. Their retention on the FY25 related-party
   roster, run in parallel with the correct current independent directors, is a genuine notes-maintenance lapse:
   the roster was not cleaned up on rotation and effectively double-lists the independent-director related-party
   category (3 stale + 3 current = 6 names for 3 board seats). 🟡 Watch — disclosure-hygiene gap, not a
   financial-impact item (no transactions are attributed to the stale names in 45b/45c).
2. **Two related entities never mentioned in Pass 1.** The roster also names "12. M/S Focus Eye Technocraft
   P. Ltd. — Directors are interested" (already covered in Pass 1) and, separately, **"13. M/S Redwood
   Packaging P. Ltd. — Directors are interested"** and **"15. M/S Manav Enterprises — Director is Partner"**
   (Note 45a, p.102). Redwood Packaging Pvt Ltd corroborates the Corporate Governance Report's director's
   profile table (p.53), which shows both Shri Ashok Jain and Shri Kunal Jain holding outside directorships at
   "Redwood Packaging Private Limited." Neither Redwood Packaging nor Manav Enterprises has any
   transaction value disclosed in Note 45b or 45c for either FY25 or FY24 (i.e., NIL transactions both years). 🟡
   Watch — not a red flag in isolation (zero quantum), but these are two additional related entities the operator
   should be aware exist and should confirm carry no undisclosed dealings, direct or indirect, with Fluidomat.

### Note 27 / Note 36.2 / Note 33 cross-reference with Board's Report — BGR Energy IBC matter (material, new)

The Board's Report (p.37–38, "CASE FILED BY THE COMPANY UNDER IBC, 2016") discloses that Fluidomat
filed an application as **Operational Creditor under Section 9 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016
against BGR Energy Systems Limited on 29.06.2022**. The parties executed a "full and final settlement" dated
08.10.2024, after which the Company filed a withdrawal memo and the Hon'ble NCLT Bench, Amaravathi
disposed of the Company's Petition (IB) No. 80/9/AMR of 2022.

This is a **material omission from the Notes themselves**: nowhere in Note 36.2 (Contingent Liabilities, p.101)
— which discloses only bank counter-guarantees and a Rs 5.32 lakh labour-payment dispute — is this
multi-year (2022–2024) customer insolvency-recovery action against BGR Energy Systems even mentioned,
despite the matter being live across two of the three years covered by this Annual Report's comparative
figures and only resolved mid-way through FY25 (settlement 08.10.2024). 🔴 Red Flag (disclosure adequacy) —
a major receivable-recovery dispute pursued through formal insolvency proceedings against a named
customer should ordinarily surface in the contingent-liabilities/litigation notes or at minimum be cross-
referenced; its complete absence from Note 36 means an investor reading only the Notes (not the Board's
Report) would never learn this existed.

This finding also **newly connects two previously separate Pass-1 observations**: (a) the receivables-ageing
deterioration in Note 11 (2–3yr bucket +128%, >3yr bucket +79.5%, receivable days ~88.6→~113.6), and (b) the
combined "Bad debts/liquidated damages/Tender/Vendor Registration fee" expense line in Note 33 (p.100),
which rose from Rs 38.22 lakh (FY24) to Rs 47.77 lakh (FY25). The Notes do not break out how much of this
combined FY25 line, if any, relates to a settlement discount/write-off on the BGR Energy recovery. **Question
for management**: does the Rs 47.77 lakh (FY25) "Bad debts/liquidated damages" line in Note 33 include any
amount written off or discounted as part of the BGR Energy Systems settlement executed 08.10.2024, and if
so, how much, and against which customer receivable bucket in Note 11's ageing schedule?

### Note 38 vs Board's Report Annexure 3 — unreconciled prior-year FX outgo figure (new)

Note 38 (p.101), "Expenditure in Foreign Currency," shows Travelling Expenses of **Rs 7.79 lakh (FY25) vs Rs
2.87 lakh (FY24)**. Annexure 3 to the Board's Report (p.45, "Conservation of Energy, Technology Absorption
and Foreign Exchange Earnings and Outgo") shows, for the same two years, "Foreign Exchange outgo during
the year in terms of actual outflows" of **Rs 7.79 lakh (FY25) vs Rs 3.60 lakh (FY24)**. The FY25 figures
reconcile exactly (Rs 7.79 lakh both places); the **FY24 comparative does not** — Rs 2.87 lakh in the Notes vs
Rs 3.60 lakh in the Annexure, an unreconciled Rs 0.73 lakh difference between two sections of the same
Annual Report describing what purports to be the same figure. 🟡 Watch — immaterial in quantum, but is
exactly the kind of number-that-does-not-match-across-sections the pattern pass should also test.

### Note 46.13 (Ratios) read together with Board's Report KMP remuneration table — pay-ratio widening (new)

Pass 1 covered the Rupee remuneration amounts in Note 45b but did not connect them to the CEO/KMP
pay-ratio disclosure in the Board's Report (p.34, "Ratio of the remuneration of each director to the median
employee's remuneration"): Shri Ashok Jain (MD) draws **28.40x** the median employee's remuneration; Mrs.
Radhica Sharma **24.99x**; Shri Kunal Jain **24.36x**. The same disclosure states average employee salary
increases ran 7–10% for the year, against a **12.85%** average increase in managerial remuneration (Board's
Report p.34, item D). Combined with Note 45b's KMP remuneration table, this quantifies a widening pay gap
between the three promoter-executive-directors and the Company's 200-person workforce (Board's Report
p.33, item C). 🟡 Watch — not unusual for a promoter-controlled small-cap, but a data point the investor should
have precisely, not just impressionistically from the Rupee totals.

### Note 17(i)/17(iii) — promoter insider share purchase during the year (new, mildly positive)

comparison window. Net effect is roughly neutral, but the Kunal Jain accumulation is a small positive
insider-alignment signal not previously flagged.

### Note 29.1 / Note 37 — import dependency now effectively zero (new, minor, positive)

Note 29.1 (p.98) shows imported components fell to **0.00% of raw material consumed in FY25** (Rs 0.00 lakh)
from **0.04% in FY24** (Rs 0.85 lakh); Note 37 (p.101) confirms C.I.F. import value of raw material components
at Rs 0.00 (FY25) vs Rs 0.73 lakh (FY24). Fluidomat's raw-material base is now effectively 100% domestically

### Note 46.15 — explicit no-layering representation, correctly anchored (clarification, not new in substance)

Pass 1 discussed the auditor's Rule 11(e) "no funds layering to/from Ultimate Beneficiaries" representation
from the Auditor's Report but did not anchor the parallel standalone Note. **Note 46.15 (p.104)** independently
states the Company "has not advanced or loaned or invested funds... to any other person(s) or entity(ies),
including foreign entities," and has not received funds from any funding party "with the understanding
(whether recorded in writing or otherwise)" of onward layering. This is the Notes-level mirror of the auditor's
statement and should be cited to Note 46.15 specifically going forward. No change to conclusion — still 🟢
clean — just a completeness/anchoring correction.

# PASS 2 NEW FINDINGS SUMMARY

Ten new items surfaced on this re-read, the most significant being: (1) a related-party roster (Note 45a) that
retains three departed independent directors alongside the three current ones, and newly identifies two
related entities (Redwood Packaging Pvt Ltd, Manav Enterprises) with NIL disclosed transactions that Pass 1
missed entirely; (2) a materially under-disclosed multi-year IBC recovery action against a named customer
(BGR Energy Systems Limited, filed 2022, settled October 2024) that appears nowhere in Note 36's contingent
liabilities despite running concurrently with the receivables-ageing deterioration already flagged as a red flag
in Pass 1, and which raises an unanswered question about whether the FY25 "bad debts" expense line
absorbed any settlement discount; (3) an unreconciled Rs 0.73 lakh FY24 comparative-figure mismatch
between Note 38 and Board's Report Annexure 3; and (4) a CEO/KMP pay-ratio and pay-growth-differential
data point (28.40x median employee pay; managerial pay growing 12.85% vs employee pay growing 7-10%)
that sharpens, with hard numbers, the RPT concentration concern Pass 1 raised only qualitatively. The
remainder are minor completeness or positive-direction items (insider share purchase, falling import
dependency, NRI dividend trend). None of these new items overturn Pass 1's overall reads; the BGR
Energy/Note 36 omission is the one item that should be elevated into the Top 15 at Pass 3 consolidation and
is itself the strongest new contribution to the receivables/cash-conversion concern already driving a
FLAG-CASH candidate.
