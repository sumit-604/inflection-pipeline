PASS 2 NEW FINDINGS (not covered in Pass 1)

1. **Parent company's share of consolidated profit collapsed from 97% to 6% — the sharpest possible quantification of the margin-migration story Pass 1 flagged qualitatively.** The Ind AS 112 mandated "share in profit or loss" table (Note 50(a), consolidated, p.279) shows: Parent (Tatva Chintan Pharma Chem Ltd) contributed 97% / ₹29.348 Cr (₹293.48 Mn) of consolidated PAT of ₹30.354 Cr in FY24, but only 6% / ₹0.327 Cr (₹3.27 Mn) of consolidated PAT of ₹5.713 Cr in FY25. Tatva Chintan USA Inc. contributed 25%/₹1.431 Cr (0%/₹0.091 Cr FY24); Tatva Chintan Europe B.V. contributed 56%/₹3.202 Cr (2%/₹0.521 Cr FY24); consolidation eliminations added a further 13%/₹0.753 Cr (1%/₹0.394 Cr FY24). Cross-check: 0.327+1.431+3.202+0.753 = 5.713 Cr, ties exactly to Pass 1's reported consolidated PAT. 🔴 Red Flag — investor importance: highest of Pass 2. This single table is the most decisive evidence yet that in FY25 nearly the entire group's profit was generated outside India by the wholly-owned trading subsidiaries, while the Indian manufacturing parent's own profit contribution evaporated to near-zero, even though the parent still holds 97% of consolidated net assets (₹721.31 Cr of ₹738.82 Cr, same note). This sharply raises the stakes on Pass 1's Red Flag #4 (transfer-pricing/margin-migration question) — this is no longer a marginal-improvement pattern, it is a near-total profit-share inversion in one year.

2. **Management's own standalone ratio-variance note independently confirms and quantifies the subsidiary-margin-migration pattern: "Return on equity investments in wholly owned subsidiaries (WOS)" rose 525% (4.41% → 27.55%).** (Note 50(l), standalone, p.203) This is a ratio the company itself is required to flag and explain because the variance exceeds its own 25% materiality threshold: "Return on equity investments in wholly owned subsidiaries has been increased due to increase in profitability of wholly owned subsidiaries during the year ended 31 March 2025" (numerator: net profit after tax of WOS; denominator: average shareholder's equity of WOS). 🔴 Red Flag — investor importance: high; this is a company-disclosed, company-computed metric (not an analyst derivation) that corroborates Finding 1 above using an entirely separate note and calculation basis. Same note also discloses, and Pass 1 did not fully quote: Net profit ratio -99% variance (0.09% FY25 vs 7.75% FY24) and Return on capital employed -104% variance (-0.24% FY25 vs 5.58% FY24) — Pass 1's summary conflated these two distinct standalone ratios into one garbled reference ("standalone PBT margin -0.24% ROCE"); Pass 2 corrects this: ROCE is -0.24% (negative) FY25 vs 5.58% FY24, and net profit ratio is a separate line at 0.09% FY25 vs 7.75% FY24. 🟡 Watch (correction/completeness, not a new substantive risk).

3. **Company began capitalising internally-generated product-development costs onto the balance sheet for the first time this filing cycle, coinciding with R&D capex roughly doubling.** Note 6 "Other intangible assets" (consolidated, p.235) shows a new "Intangible assets under development" cost column of ₹3.610 Cr (₹36.10 Mn) added in FY25 (nil in FY24), against which ₹0.686 Cr (₹6.86 Mn) of amortisation was charged in FY25, giving a net carrying value of ₹2.924 Cr (₹29.24 Mn) as at 31 March 2025 (useful life for "Product development" disclosed at 5 years per Note 2(f), p.223) — up from a ₹0 balance in this category the prior year. Separately, Note 7 "Intangible assets under development" (p.235) shows a NEW work-in-progress balance of ₹2.775 Cr (₹27.75 Mn) built up during FY25 (from a ₹3.610 Cr FY24 balance that was fully transferred out/completed), funded by cost of materials consumed ₹0.511 Cr, employee benefits ₹1.251 Cr and other expenses ₹1.013 Cr. This directly correlates with Note 32(ii): R&D capital expenditure ₹4.445 Cr (₹44.45 Mn) FY25 vs ₹2.219 Cr (₹22.19 Mn) FY24 (+100.3%), R&D revenue expenditure ₹8.394 Cr vs ₹7.942 Cr (+5.7%), under the Holding Company's DSIR-approved (s.35(2AB)) R&D unit. 🟡 Watch — investor importance: medium; two-sided read. Positive: the company is investing more, not less, in new-product R&D even as reported earnings collapsed, consistent with a specialty-chemicals GARP transition thesis (product pipeline investment through the down-cycle). Caution: this is the first year the company has capitalised (rather than fully expensed) internally-generated development costs at any scale — a change in accounting outcome (though amounts, ₹2.775–3.610 Cr, are small relative to FY25 PBT of ₹7.579 Cr, so not a major driver of the profit number, but a capitalisation-policy shift worth monitoring for consistency of the "technically and commercially feasible" recognition threshold in future years).

4. **Two relatives of promoter/executive-director KMPs are employed by the company in operating roles and draw compensation, a related-party disclosure Pass 1's related-party section did not name.** Note 42(1)(b) (consolidated, p.267) lists "Ms. Shimoni Chintan Shah — Executive, International Sales" and "Mr. Aryan Shekhar Somani — Management Trainee, Business Development" among the relatives of KMP (daughter of Chairman & MD Chintan N. Shah, and son of Whole Time Director Shekhar R. Somani, by name correspondence). Note 42B(i) (p.268) shows employee-benefits expense paid to them: Ms. Shimoni Chintan Shah ₹0.030 Cr (₹0.30 Mn) FY25 vs ₹0.033 Cr (₹0.33 Mn) FY24; Mr. Aryan Shekhar Somani ₹0.010 Cr (₹0.10 Mn) FY25 vs ₹0.025 Cr (₹0.25 Mn) FY24 (down 60%). Outstanding employee-benefits-payable balances to them: ₹0.02 Cr and ₹0.01 Cr respectively at FY25 year-end (Note 42C, p.269). 🟡 Watch — investor importance: medium; amounts are immaterial in isolation, but this is a governance-relevant disclosure (children of two of the three executive promoter-directors hold company operating roles in sales and business development) that belongs in the related-party narrative alongside the KMP pay-rise finding already in Pass 1's #3.

5. **Institutional (mutual fund) shareholding nearly halved while small/retail individual shareholding rose sharply; promoter holding was unchanged and unpledged.** Note 16(ix) (consolidated, p.243) shareholding pattern: Mutual funds fell from 12.84% (3,002,583 shares, 5 holders) at FY24-end to 5.34% (1,249,741 shares, 2 holders) at FY25-end; Foreign portfolio investors eased slightly, 3.65% (30 holders) to 3.30% (15 holders); total Institutions fell from 16.49% to 8.76%. Individual holders with holdings up to ₹0.20 Mn rose from 8.99% (71,145 holders) to 14.88% (75,486 holders); individual holders above ₹0.20 Mn rose from 1.50% to 2.04%. Promoter and promoter group holding was unchanged at 72.02% in both years with 0.00% change and Note 16(v) confirms none of the promoter shares were pledged or encumbered in either year. 🟡 Watch — presented as a factual ownership-composition shift only (per house rule, institutional ownership level itself is never treated as a risk factor); flagged because the magnitude of the mutual-fund exit (halved) alongside the retail increase is a notable register change worth carrying into the ownership/liquidity discussion, not as a standalone red flag.

6. **The MSMED Act interest-on-delayed-payment disclosure that Pass 1 flagged as "NOT FOUND IN DOCUMENT" does exist — in the standalone notes, not consolidated — and the amount is immaterial.** Standalone Note 22(vi) (p.171) discloses the full statutory breakdown: dues to micro/small enterprises less than 45 days ₹10.818 Cr (₹108.18 Mn) FY25 vs ₹6.571 Cr (₹65.71 Mn) FY24 (i.e., 99.97% of the ₹10.821 Cr FY25 MSME balance is within the 45-day window); dues more than 45 days — principal outstanding ₹0.001 Cr (₹0.01 Mn) FY25 vs ₹0.101 Cr (₹1.01 Mn) FY24; interest due on principal outstanding as above: nil FY25, nil FY24 stated as "-"/₹0.00 Mn; interest due and payable for the period of delay: ₹0.002 Cr (₹0.02 Mn) FY25 vs ₹0.001 Cr (₹0.01 Mn) FY24; interest due and unpaid: nil both years; further interest payable under s.23 of the MSMED Act for disallowance in succeeding years: nil both years. 🟢 Clean — this resolves and downgrades Pass 1's 🟡 Watch item (Section 8, "interest on delayed MSME payments... NOT FOUND IN DOCUMENT"). The disclosure exists at standalone level (consolidated Note 22 omits it — an inconsistency in disclosure completeness between the two statement sets worth a light Pass 3 note, but the underlying substance is immaterial: statutory MSME interest exposure is a rounding error, ₹0.02 Cr Mn-scale, i.e., ₹20,000).

7. **Debt service coverage and net capital turnover ratios both improved materially — a positive counterweight Pass 1 did not surface.** Standalone Note 50(l) (p.203): Debt service coverage ratio improved to 6.35x FY25 from 4.84x FY24 (+31% variance, flagged by management's own >25% threshold), explained as "due to repayment of long-term borrowings and finance cost during the year." Net capital turnover ratio improved to 2.42x FY25 from 1.83x FY24 (+32% variance), explained by "a combination of decrease in current assets and increase in current liabilities (mainly borrowings)." 🟢 Clean/positive — worth including for a symmetric bull/bear read alongside the current ratio and debt-equity ratio deteriorations Pass 1 already flagged from the same note.

8. **Interest-rate sensitivity to a 100bps move more than tripled year-on-year, consistent with the debt-book growth already flagged.** Note 44 (consolidated, p.274) and standalone Note 44 (p.199): a 100bps increase in variable interest rates would reduce FY25 PBT by ₹0.357 Cr (₹3.57 Mn) vs only ₹0.10 Cr (₹1.00 Mn) FY24 — a 3.57x increase in rate sensitivity, mechanically following from the current/variable-rate borrowings rising from ₹100.00 Mn (FY24 rate-exposed base was actually largely fixed; only ₹100.00 Mn variable) to ₹357.49 Mn variable-rate at FY25-end (interest rate profile table, same note). 🟡 Watch — minor quantification supporting Pass 1's Finding #5 (net cash to net debt swing) with a specific earnings-sensitivity number.

9. **Non-current assets are almost entirely held in India (₹570.99 Cr of ₹571.00 Cr, i.e. >99.97%) despite the wholly-owned foreign subsidiaries now capturing a majority of group profit — a geography/asset-vs-profit mismatch that strengthens Finding 1.** Note 41A(ii) (consolidated, p.266): non-current assets within India ₹570.989 Cr (₹5,709.89 Mn) vs outside India just ₹0.015 Cr (₹0.15 Mn) at FY25-end; India ₹520.794 Cr vs outside India ₹0.018 Cr at FY24-end. 🟡 Watch — the trading subsidiaries hold a negligible fixed-asset base (₹1.5–1.8 lakh) yet, per Finding 1, captured 94% of consolidated FY25 profit; this is the expected shape for pure distribution entities but sharpens the transfer-pricing question already raised in Pass 1.

10. **Government grants tied to export incentives were de-recognised in FY25 specifically because the underlying export revenue was deferred under Ind AS 115 — a direct mechanical link to the growing contract-asset/contract-liability balances Pass 1 flagged.** Note 37(A)(ii) (consolidated, p.257): "During the year ended 31 March 2025, the Group has de-recognised government grants aggregating to ₹0.053 Cr (₹0.53 Mn) (31 March 2024: ₹0.004 Cr/₹0.04 Mn), comprising ₹0.039 Cr under the Duty Drawback Scheme and ₹0.014 Cr under RoDTEP... as the associated revenue from export of products has been deferred in accordance with Ind AS 115. Consequently, the government grants linked to such transactions have also been de-recognised." 🟡 Watch — small in absolute ₹ terms but directly corroborates the mechanism behind Pass 1's Finding #10 (deferred contract revenue tripling); worth citing together in the cut-off-risk discussion given the auditor's #1 Key Audit Matter is revenue cut-off.
