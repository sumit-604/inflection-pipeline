## Business Understanding Narrative

Max India is two things under one ticker. First, the legal shell of the old Max Group
split: its sector tag itself reads "Investment Holding." Second, three operating
businesses built inside it under the Antara brand: Residences for Seniors, a
capital-light community developer earning fees from partners (only Dehradun is fully
owned, Concall p.15, p.11); Assisted Care Services, running Care Homes at ~Rs 10-12
lakh capital per bed (Concall p.15); and AGEasy, a branded D2C and marketplace
products business (IP Q1FY27 p.23).

What is changing is whether three young, loss-making businesses become one
profitable group. Consolidated revenue grew 66% YoY in Q1FY27 (IP Q1FY27 p.8), and
the EBITDA loss is narrowing on management's multi-year comparison (Concall p.5,
p.9), though a conflicting figure exists (step 5). Why now: Noida received its
occupation certificate, ending years of delay (Concall p.3); Care Home occupancy
climbs at 5 of 8 homes (Concall p.6, p.9); AGEasy's ad-spend return improved 1.8 to
2.0 QoQ (IP Q1FY27 p.8).

What must be true: Care Homes must reach the 23-26% ROCE claimed at scale (guidance,
IP Q2FY27 p.14), AGEasy must hit its Q4FY27 breakeven target (Concall p.9), and
Residences must keep collecting so the group avoids a raise beyond the $20 million
already flagged (Concall p.16).

What breaks it: cash. Liquidity was Rs 21 cr against losses of Rs 25-36 cr a quarter
(IP Q1FY27 p.8, p.27). A raise has slipped once, and FY26 figures disagree across
sources.

## 2. Business model and archetype
Three engines, two archetypes. Residences earns a development-management fee (Rs
47.69 cr billed ITD on E360 alone, IP Q1FY27 p.13) plus re-sale fees, while a
developer partner funds construction. Care Homes is compared by management to
healthcare, not real estate (Concall p.13); AGEasy runs at 45% online gross margin
(IP Q1FY27 p.23). No single archetype fits: AGEasy is Brand/franchise consumer, Care
Homes leans licence/scarcity (NABH accreditation as entry barrier, Concall p.8),
Residences is closest to an order-book/development-fee model. Parent standalone
earned only Rs 0.65 cr of Q1FY27 revenue (IP Q1FY27 p.8): the holding company is not
yet the engine.

## 3. Competitive advantages
Uneven, and management is candid: "there are only 2 moats... differentiated,
innovated products... and brand. Nothing else is a moat" on AGEasy (Concall p.14), 4
patents granted, 3 filed (Concall p.7). Care Homes lean on 24/7 on-campus staffing
developers "tying up with a nearby nursing home" cannot replicate cheaply (Concall
p.13-14). Residences lean on accumulated wellness-service IP, not the real estate
(Concall p.13). None proven at scale: AGEasy's repeat rate is 10-12%, against a 20%
plus target (Concall p.13). Moat: forming, not yet structural.

## 4. The promoters
Max Ventures Investment Holdings Pvt Ltd is the promoter, holding 50.14% pre-issue as
of 1 Aug 2025 (AR FY25 p.334). Ultimate beneficial owners: the Analjit Singh BAS
Family Trust, Tara Singh Vachani, Piya Singh, Veer Singh (AR FY25 p.335). The
promoter subscribed Rs 22 cr of an Rs 80.36 cr preferential warrant issue at Rs 217;
the larger tranche, Rs 50 cr, went to a non-promoter AIF (AR FY25 p.331, p.335). No
pledge found. The Antara-Contend Builders (Logix Infra) JV for Noida is a flagged
material related party (AR FY25 p.338). Real capital in from the promoter, but most
new money is outside, similar to Goodluck's subsidiary funding.

## 5. Financial trajectory
Q1FY27 consolidated: revenue Rs 68.6 cr vs Rs 41.3 cr Q1FY26 (up 66%), down 4.7% from
Rs 72.0 cr Q4FY26. EBITDA loss Rs 25.0 cr vs Rs 23.2 cr Q1FY26 and Rs 6.8 cr Q4FY26.
Loss after tax Rs 36.3 cr, EPS -Rs 6.9. Net worth Rs 372.2 cr, down from Rs 408.3 cr
at March 2026. Liquidity Rs 21 cr (IP Q1FY27 p.8, p.27-28; Concall p.5). By segment:
Residences Rs 37.36 cr, Assisted Care Services Rs 30.61 cr, parent Rs 0.65 cr (IP
Q1FY27 p.8).
Multi-year, per the concall: consolidated revenue Rs 175/145/190 cr and EBITDA loss
Rs 57/139/121 cr for FY24/25/26 (Concall p.5, p.9). A conflicting figure sits in the
guidance tool, from a document this session could not read directly: FY26 revenue
"closed at INR213.4 crores," up 30% on FY25's "INR164 crores," and EBITDA loss
"INR83 crores versus INR99 crores" FY25 (guidance, O7CIEr p.4), a wide disagreement.
Both readings are shown; neither resolved.
Inflection classification: earnings-led and capacity-led. Revenue grows on new
capacity, but the group is loss-making throughout, and FY26 cannot be pinned down
with confidence.

## 6. The transition and the quality ladder
Management's claim, quoted once: the company is "building an integrated care
ecosystem," taking "a customer view" across residences, care homes and products,
following the same senior consumer through life stages (Concall p.13).
FROM R0, non-operating / accounting-driven: the sector tag "Investment Holding" names
the start, and parent standalone earned only Rs 0.65 cr of this quarter's Rs 68.6 cr
(IP Q1FY27 p.8). A transition FROM R0 must first prove an operating engine exists.
TO claim, by segment: Care Homes targets 23-26% ROCE, 18-20% EBITDA margin at scale,
R3/R4 territory if delivered (guidance, IP Q2FY27 p.14). AGEasy is not built for mass
scale ("perhaps INR1,000 crores" in five years, Concall p.14) but a higher-margin
niche. Residences is a fee-income annuity on real estate the group does not own.
None of these TO claims is delivered: still consolidated-loss-making, and the number
that would settle whether the climb has started, FY26 profitability, is disputed
(step 5). Real execution exists, but no rung is reached.

## 7. Growth-trigger register
- Antara Noida Phase I: occupation certificate received, possession offered on 340
  units, ~75% of Rs 169 cr demand collected; operations commence Q2FY27. Phase II
  approval targeted next, expected to realise most profit at Rs 16,000-18,000/sq ft
  vs Rs 7,000-11,000/sq ft in Phase I. Concall p.3-4; IP Q2FY27 p.11. Status: Phase I
  delivered, Phase II stated.
- Bangalore and Dehradun expansions (~Rs 1,800 cr combined) target the 1.5 mn sq ft
  annual ambition, in diligence (an earlier Bangalore deal's developer "ran into
  financial issues," Concall p.15). Care Home expansion deferred to
  October-November pending occupancy proof (Concall p.4, p.6, p.9). Status:
  underway/gated.
- AGEasy FY27 revenue doubling, ARR trending Rs 120-150 cr by Aug 2026, path to
  breakeven by Q4 FY27. Concall p.9, p.11-13; guidance FY27 Q2 p.10. Status: underway.
- Two negative signals: Chandigarh unwound after Operation Sindoor flight-path
  restrictions; DLF entered senior living, no measured sales impact so far. Concall
  p.15-16. Status: both delivered.

## 8. Proof check
What fired: Noida possession after years of delay (Concall p.3); occupied-bed-days
up 2.3x YoY with 5 of 8 Care Homes on model (IP Q1FY27 p.20-21); AGEasy ROAS up 1.8
to 2.0 QoQ, CM2 from -70/-80% historically to -17% by July (Concall p.9); Dehradun
stays the one fully proven profitable community (Concall p.5).
What has not fired: consolidated profitability. EBITDA loss widened QoQ, Rs 25 cr vs
Rs 6.8 cr Q4FY26, attributed to lumpy fee timing (IP Q1FY27 p.8; Concall p.5). Four
of eight Care Homes are not tracking to plan, and AGEasy revenue fell 18% QoQ after
the March peak, called seasonal but unproven across a full cycle (Concall p.12).
Named proof point: AGEasy breakeven by Q4 FY27, and the four lagging Care Homes.

## 9. Flags from the documents
- **Conflicting FY26 figures** (step 5), a Rs 23 cr revenue gap and Rs 38 cr
  EBITDA-loss gap between the concall and an unreadable guidance-tool source. Needs
  primary-source reconciliation first.
- **Thin liquidity.** Rs 21 cr cash against a Rs 36.3 cr quarterly net loss (IP
  Q1FY27 p.8, p.27). A raise has slipped once, and the $20 mn plan is an estimate,
  not a committed facility (Concall p.16).
- **Lumpy income and repeated delays.** DM fees and re-sale income are "always
  exceptional," not in headline revenue (Concall p.10). An earlier Bangalore deal

- **China sourcing.** 36% of AGEasy's portfolio is China-sourced, a cost risk under
  the "geopolitical situation" cited repeatedly (IP Q1FY27 p.23; Concall p.4).
Not seen: confirmed pledge status, full related-party schedule, auditor's report,
FY26 MD&A commentary.

## 12. Verdict card
**WATCH, with the financial-data conflict as the first item to close.**
Max India is a genuine attempt to turn an "Investment Holding" shell into an
operating senior-care group: Noida possession, climbing Care Home occupancy, and an
AGEasy recovery management can point to in its own metrics. That is the shape of an
R0-to-operating transition the strategy is built to catch early. Not a PROCEED yet,
for two reasons. The ladder asks an R0 name to prove an operating engine exists
before a climb is credited, and the group still loses money at the consolidated
level. And the one number that would show whether the group is getting better or
worse, full-year FY26 revenue and EBITDA loss, disagrees widely between two sources
here, and the more favourable document could not be read directly this session.
Load-bearing facts a deep run must verify first:
1. FY26 revenue and EBITDA loss from the audited annual report, resolving the Rs 190
   cr vs Rs 213.4 cr revenue and Rs 121 cr vs Rs 83 cr loss gaps.
2. The Noida JV with Contend Builders/Logix: economics, ownership split, promoter
   pledge status, and any further related-party terms.
3. Cash runway against the $20 million capital need, and terms of the next raise.
4. Care Home economics at the four homes not tracking to plan, and whether the
   October-November 2026 expansion gate holds.
5. AGEasy's path to Q4FY27 breakeven, against actual CM2 and ROAS.
What would change the view: a reconciled FY26 number showing genuinely narrowing
losses, plus one clean quarter of execution, moves this to PROCEED to `/step1`. A
second data conflict, a further raise, or a stall at the four lagging Care Homes
moves this to PASS. No price here.
