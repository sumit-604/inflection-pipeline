## SECTION 2: TAM ESTIMATION, MULTIPLE METHODS
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

### Segment 1: Bi-Metal engine bearings/bushes/thrust washers (domestic)

**Method 1 — Top-down.** India's overall bearings market is far too broad a starting point
(ball/roller/needle bearings dominate it, and Menon competes in none of those); the closer
top-down anchor is the *India automotive bearing market*: US$503.3 million in 2024, projected to
US$725.5 million by 2033 (Fortune Business Insights / OpenPR, "India Automotive Bearing Market
2025," search result, dated report) → ₹4,832cr (2024) to ₹6,965cr (2033) at ₹96/US$ (FX applied
to a 2024-vintage figure; flagged). This is a market for *all* automotive bearing types (ball,
roller, wheel-hub, plain/engine). Engine plain bearings/bushings are one sub-category; the
off-highway/industrial engine segment alone is cited separately at ~5% of the total Indian
bearings market (search result, Fortune Business Insights companion query), and automotive
overall is ~60% of total bearings demand. No disclosed report gives an exact "engine plain
bearings" % split of the automotive-bearing total, so this stage applies an analyst assumption
of 15-20% (plain/engine bearings + bushings + thrust washers, as distinct from the
higher-value-count ball/roller/wheel-hub categories that dominate automotive bearing spend) —
**flagged explicitly as an unsourced analyst split, not a disclosed segment breakdown**:
₹4,832cr × 15-20% = **₹725cr to ₹966cr** (2024 basis).

**Method 3 — Peer revenue aggregation.** Menon's Bi-Metal segment revenue ₹212.54cr (Inv. Pres.
slide 10, FY26, domestic+export) + Bimetal Bearings Ltd (Coimbatore) total sales ₹296.99cr
(FY26, `inputs/screening/BIMETAL-Data_Sheet.csv` row 11 — independently confirms the ₹244.64cr
FY25 / ₹296.98cr FY26 figures found via web search) — but Bimetal Bearings' disclosed scope
explicitly extends beyond bi-metal automotive engine bearings into "defense and railway sectors"
(web search result), so its revenue is not a clean like-for-like; treated as an upper-bound
comparable. Named "4-player" Indian oligopoly (Menon's own characterisation, Inv. Pres. slide
21) — the other two players (besides Menon and, per web search, likely Bimetal Bearings and
possibly Asiatic Bearing, a small Rajkot-based private/unfunded bi-metal bearing maker) are not
named in any source (NOT FOUND). Combined named-peer revenue (domestic+export) = ₹212.54cr +
₹296.99cr = ₹509.53cr. Applying Menon's own domestic/total revenue mix (65.9% domestic, FY26,
Inv. Pres. slide 34) as a working proxy for the peer set's domestic share (a necessary
simplification, since neither peer discloses a bi-metal-specific domestic/export split): ₹509.53cr
× 0.659 ≈ ₹336cr domestic revenue for the two largest named players. Grossing up for the
unorganised/smaller-player tail (30-60% add-on, standard India assumption per stage
instructions) and the two further named-but-unquantified oligopoly players: **₹700cr to
₹1,300cr** domestic, a wide range that nonetheless brackets management's own ₹1,000cr figure.

**Method 5 — Global benchmark (directional only, NOT used for the headline number).** The
global "crankshaft and connecting rod engine bearings market" was valued at US$3.0 billion in
2025, India CAGR cited at 5.2% within it (Future Market Insights, search result; direct page
fetch returned HTTP 403 and could not be independently re-verified beyond the search snippet —
flagged). If India's share of this global market tracked its ~6-7% share of global vehicle
production, the implied India TAM would be US$180-210 million (₹17,300cr-20,200cr) — **17-20x
higher than management's ₹1,000cr claim**. This divergence is flagged, not averaged in: the
global market almost certainly reflects developed-market OEM-fitted ASPs materially higher than
India's cost-competitive manufacturing/pricing point (Menon's own stated competitive edge is
cost, not price parity with global peers — B04 2C), so this method's output is treated as an
unreliable upper bound, excluded from triangulation per the stage's conservative-bias rule.

**Triangulation table — Bi-Metal:**

| Method | Estimate (₹cr, domestic) | Confidence | Staleness |
|---|---|---|---|
| M1 Top-down (auto bearing mkt × est. engine share) | 725-966 | L-M (unsourced split assumption) | 2024 base year, <2yr, current |

estimate (1,000/900 = 1.11x)**.

### Segment 2: Aluminium die-casting (Menon Alkop, domestic)

**Method 1 — Top-down.** India's automotive parts aluminium die-casting market: US$1.71 billion
in 2025 (Mordor Intelligence, "India Automotive Parts Aluminum Die Casting Market," search
result) → ₹16,416cr at ₹96/US$ (FX flagged as above). Of this, "engine parts" application
commanded a 45.98% share in 2025 (same Mordor source) = ₹16,416cr × 0.4598 = **₹7,548cr**. This
is the closest independently-sourced analogue to management's "aluminium casting products"
domestic claim, since Alkop's current product scope is overwhelmingly engine + industrial
components (not the large body/structural-panel segment, which is growing fastest per Mordor at
8.61% CAGR but sits outside Alkop's disclosed 12kg-part-weight capability).

**Method 3 — Peer/context check.** No single listed pure-play Indian automotive aluminium
gravity/tilt-gravity die-caster of comparable scale was identified in this search round (NOT
FOUND — the aluminium die-casting industry is described by Menon's own B04 Section 2A as "a much
larger, more fragmented" market than bi-metal bearings, consistent with the ₹6,000-7,500cr scale
found here against Alkop's own FY26 revenue of just ₹72.18cr, a <1.5% share). A separate,
narrower "Aluminum Parts Gravity Die Casting Market" (global) was found sized at US$8.15 billion
by 2028 (Mordor/GlobeNewswire, search result) — global, not decomposable to an India figure with
available sources, kept as directional context only, not used in the headline number.

**Triangulation table — Aluminium die-casting:**

| Method | Estimate (₹cr, domestic) | Confidence | Staleness |
|---|---|---|---|
| M1 Top-down (ADC market × engine-parts share) | 7,548 | M (two independently-sourced Mordor percentages multiplied, not a direct single disclosed figure) | 2025 base year, current |
| Management claim | 6,000 | Specific, single-source | May-2026, current |

Conservative estimate = **₹6,000cr** (management's own figure is the lower of the two, taken

**Method 3 — Peer context.** Rane Brake Lining Ltd, the most directly comparable listed Indian

comparable, kept as context only. "About a decade ago there were only four friction product
manufacturers in the country but today there are 15" (web search), confirming a fragmenting,
not consolidating, competitive structure in this segment (Section 4C).

### 3B SOM at 3 and 5 years — arithmetic shown, per segment

**Bi-Metal** (mature, oligopoly — normal 1-2pp share-gain rule applies, per stage instructions):
- Current domestic Bi-Metal revenue (estimate) = ₹212.54cr segment revenue × 65.9% domestic mix
  (Menon's company-wide domestic/total ratio, Inv. Pres. slide 34, used as a working proxy since
  no segment-specific domestic/export split is disclosed) ≈ **₹140.1cr**
- Current share of SAM(Bi-Metal, conservative ₹693cr) = 140.1/693 = **20.2%**
- 3yr share (+1.8pp, normal-range gain given mature/oligopoly status) = 22.0% → SOM = 693×0.22 =
  **₹152.5cr**
- 5yr share (+2.8pp cumulative) = 23.0% → SOM = 693×0.23 = **₹159.4cr**

**Aluminium** (growth-phase, fragmented market, active capex, capacity headroom — supports the
3-5pp aggressive-gain band):
- Current domestic Alkop revenue (estimate) = ₹72.18cr × 65.9% ≈ **₹47.6cr**
- Current share of SAM(Aluminium, conservative ₹1,008cr) = 47.6/1,008 = **4.7%**
- 3yr share (+3.0pp, aggressive band justified by capacity headroom at 60-65% utilisation and a
  fragmented market per B04 2A) = 7.7% → SOM = 1,008×0.077 = **₹77.6cr**
- 5yr share (+5.0pp cumulative, at the top of the aggressive band, contingent on the
  utilisation/realisation catch-up B07 flags as unproven) = 9.7% → SOM = 1,008×0.097 = **₹97.8cr**

**Braking** (early-ramp, high execution risk — conservative gain only, given the documented
guidance-slippage pattern):
- Current revenue = **₹9.09cr** (assumed ~100% domestic; only a small Dubai-distributor export
  channel disclosed, B07 2D)
- Current share of SAM(Braking, conservative ₹157cr) = 9.09/157 = **5.8%**
- 3yr share (+1.0pp only — NOT the aggressive band, given the ~11-month dynamometer/Railways
  slip already realised, B07 F2) = 6.8% → SOM = 157×0.068 = **₹10.7cr**
- 5yr share (+2.0pp cumulative, assuming Railways eventually unlocks within the window) = 7.8% →
  SOM = 157×0.078 = **₹12.2cr**

**Consolidated SOM**: 3yr = 152.5+77.6+10.7 = **₹240.8cr** / 5yr = 159.4+97.8+12.2 = **₹269.4cr**
(both domestic-only, per the TAM/SAM scope established in Section 1).

**Implied revenue CAGR** (domestic-only basis; current domestic revenue base = ₹193.6cr, Inv.
Pres. slide 34, FY26):
- 3yr: (240.8/193.6)^(1/3) − 1 = (1.2438)^(0.333) − 1 = 1.0754 − 1 = **7.5%**
- 5yr: (269.4/193.6)^(1/5) − 1 = (1.3915)^(0.20) − 1 = 1.0684 − 1 = **6.8%**

**Critical scope caveat (flagged, not buried):** this CAGR is built entirely on the
domestic-only market data management itself disclosed (Section 1B) — it excludes any export
TAM/SAM, because no independently-sourced, decomposable India-export-specific bi-metal bearing
or Alkop EV-parts market figure could be found (Section 2). Exports are ~34% of Menon's actual
FY26 revenue and are the single strongest-evidenced growth vector in this pipeline's own B07
scan (E2 "China+1 beneficiary," rated STRONG). **The 7.5%/6.8% figures above are therefore a
domestic-market-only lower bound on achievable company growth, not a full-company growth
ceiling** — a genuine, structurally correct feature of this analysis (not an error), but one
stage 11 must not mistake for "the" SOM-implied CAGR without this context. See Section 5E for
the reconciliation against management's own aspirational CAGR.

### 3C Capacity cross-check (using B07 capex_embedded_growth_pct = 28.9%)

- B07's own arithmetic (Section 2C of that stage): planned incremental capex ₹34.50cr over the
  next ~2 years × historical fixed-asset turnover 2.46x = ₹84.87cr incremental revenue potential,
  i.e. 28.9% of FY26's ₹293.81cr base → **total-company capex-embedded revenue potential ≈
  ₹293.81cr + ₹84.87cr = ₹378.68cr** (roughly a 2-3 year horizon, per B07's own capex timeline).
- To compare like-for-like against this stage's *domestic-only* 3yr SOM (₹240.8cr), gross it up
  to a total-company figure assuming the export share holds at its current ~35% level (a
  simplifying, explicitly-flagged assumption — not itself an independently-sourced export TAM):
  total-company revenue implied by domestic SOM = 240.8 / (1 − 0.35) = **₹370.8cr**.
- **Gap: ₹378.68cr (capex-embedded) − ₹370.8cr (SOM-implied) = +₹7.9cr, i.e. capacity is
  slightly AHEAD of the SOM's revenue requirement** (a ~2% margin, within the noise of both
  estimates' rounding). **capacity_check: sufficient** — capacity is not the binding constraint
  on this stage's 3yr SOM; if anything, the disclosed capex plan is marginally more optimistic
  than the domestic-market-anchored SOM, which is the expected, healthy direction (better to have
  spare capacity than a capacity bottleneck).
- No 5yr-horizon capex figure is disclosed anywhere in the source documents (B07's capex table
  only covers "next ~2 years") — **the capacity sufficiency of the 5yr SOM (₹269.4cr domestic /
  ~₹414.5cr total-company-equivalent at the same 35% export-share assumption) is NOT FOUND and
  would require a further capex commitment beyond what is currently disclosed.** This is a
  genuine forward gap for stage 11 to weigh, not an estimate.

### 5B Runway assessment

- **Revenue headroom** = SAM ÷ current revenue (domestic basis, for scope consistency) =
  1,858 ÷ 193.6 = **9.6x**.
- **TAM growth rate** (blended, conservative-TAM-weighted): Bi-Metal ~4.15% (India automotive
  bearing market CAGR 2025-2033, Fortune Business Insights, search result — a second source,
  MarkNtel, cites 7.0% for 2023-28; the lower figure is used per conservative bias), Aluminium
  7.29% (Mordor, 2026-2031), Braking ~4.8% (global friction-materials CAGR used as an India proxy
  in the absence of an India-specific figure, flagged). Weighted by conservative-TAM share
  (Bi-Metal 10.6%, Aluminium 70.6%, Braking 18.8%): 0.106×4.15 + 0.706×7.29 + 0.188×4.8 = **6.5%**
  blended TAM growth.
- **Company CAGR vs TAM**: Menon's consolidated revenue grew ~21.2% YoY in FY26 (₹293.81cr vs
  ₹242.52cr FY25, screener-Data_Sheet.csv) and domestic revenue specifically grew at a ~10.8%
  4-year CAGR FY22-FY26 (₹128.4cr→₹193.6cr, Inv. Pres. slide 34) — both comfortably above the
  ~6.5% blended TAM growth rate, confirming Menon has been **gaining share**, not merely riding
  the market, consistent with the share-gain assumptions used in Section 3B.
- **Years to saturate SAM at current (domestic) growth**: ln(1,858/193.6) ÷ ln(1.108) =
  2.261 ÷ 0.1026 ≈ **22 years** at the historical ~10.8% domestic CAGR — a long runway, though
  this number should not be over-read: it assumes the current growth rate holds constant for two
  decades, which is unlikely given the EV-substitution risk flagged in 4B and the already-mature
  status of the Bi-Metal core.

### 5C Runway classification

Given: ~9.6x revenue headroom (domestic basis), ~6.5% underlying TAM growth, a company
demonstrably gaining share, but with (a) capability-driven SAM filters already applying a heavy
17-77% haircut per segment (meaning the "easy" TAM is already substantially carved out of this
SAM), (b) a structural long-run EV-substitution risk sitting outside the near-term TAM growth
numbers for the largest, most mature segment, and (c) the weakest segment (Braking) carrying a
demonstrated, repeated execution-slippage pattern (B07 F2) — this stage classifies the runway as
**STRONG** (not MASSIVE: the >9x headroom is real but is measured against a domestic-only,
heavily-filtered SAM in mature/fragmenting-but-not-expanding-explosively categories, not a
new-category or regulatory-unlock-driven step-change; not GOOD/MODERATE either, since genuine,
multi-year, share-gain-supported headroom clearly exists across all three segments).

**Valuation implication line.** Current valuation: Market Cap ₹1,072.65cr ÷ TTM PAT ₹43.93cr
(sum of quarters Sep-2025 ₹6.79cr + Dec-2025 ₹9.25cr + Mar-2026 ₹13.78cr + Jun-2026 ₹14.11cr,
`inputs/screening/screener-Data_Sheet.csv`) = **24.4x TTM P/E** (FY26 full-year trailing basis:
1,072.65 ÷ 38.25 = 28.0x — both bases shown since the two diverge meaningfully on a fast-growing
earnings base). FY26 consolidated EBITDA margin was 21.51% (Inv. Pres. slide 8, B04), against a
CRISIL two-year band of 18.65-20.39% — treated here as a roughly stable ~20-22% margin
trajectory (no strong disclosed catalyst for material margin expansion beyond the current level;
rising Alkop/Brakes revenue share is mix-dilutive given their lower target margins, per B04 3A).

At **7.5%** revenue CAGR implied by this stage's domestic-only SOM (3yr), with a margin
trajectory of **~20-22%** (broadly flat), the earnings growth embedded here is approximately
**7-8% CAGR** (assuming stable margins, earnings growth tracks revenue growth 1:1), which **does
not support** the current valuation of **24.4x TTM P/E** (PEG-equivalent ≈ 24.4/7.5 ≈ 3.2x, well
above the ~1.0-1.5x range typically associated with "growth at a reasonable price").

**This is the central bull/bear tension this stage surfaces, stated plainly**: the market is
pricing Menon on something closer to management's own aspirational **25% CAGR** (FY27 ₹350-360cr,
FY28 ₹500cr targets, both reaffirmed at the Q4/FY26 call, per B07's optionality register) or the
company's actual recent consolidated growth (~21% YoY FY26), not on the domestic-market-anchored,
independently-triangulated SOM this stage can defend (7.5%/6.8%). The gap between management's
25% aspiration and this stage's 7.5% (3yr) SOM-implied CAGR is a ratio of **25/7.5 ≈ 3.3x** —
squarely in "requires verification, not extrapolation" territory. The reconciling factor, if one
exists, is almost entirely the export growth leg (China+1, US/Europe, B07's only STRONG-rated
category) which sits outside the domestic TAM this stage could independently size — meaning
stage 11's valuation call should explicitly weigh how much of the 25% aspiration it is willing to
underwrite from an export market this pipeline could not independently verify the size of,
against a domestic core that this stage's own evidence supports growing at a high-single-digit
rate. Both readings are defensible; conflating them (crediting the 25% aspiration on the back of
a domestic TAM that only supports ~7-8%) would repeat exactly the "TAM=SAM=growth runway" error
this framework exists to catch.
