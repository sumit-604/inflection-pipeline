## 6. THE CATALYST STORY IN PLAIN WORDS

PML is trying to move from being a maker of small magnetic parts for electricity meters and
cars into three larger things at once: specialty alloys, latching relays for smart meters,
and rare earth magnets through its wholly owned subsidiary Quantum Magnetics. One of those
three is working. Two are behind schedule.

**The alloys furnace is the real catalyst, and it is documented.** It was installed and
commercialised in the March 2026 quarter. Alloys went from roughly 1% of sales two years
ago to roughly 10% in FY26 (AR-FY26 p.9). The March quarter grew 46.9% and the June quarter
grew 19.1%. The division holds AS9100D aerospace certification, which is what lets it sell
into aerospace and oil and gas. This is capital that was spent and is now earning, which is
the single best thing in the file. What confirms it: alloys continuing to gain share of
sales in the next two prints. What kills it: order flow not converting, or the furnace
running below the utilisation it needs.

**The relay line is late and the delay is management's own word.** The facility is largely
built and test infrastructure is installed, but customer endurance testing is taking longer
than planned, so commercial ramp is now guided to the second half of FY27 (AR-FY26 p.4 and
p.19). Management describes itself as behind its original timelines. The prize is real,
because a relay is worth several times PML's existing content per meter, and there is a
named licensing partner in REL Developments of the United Kingdom. But the original target
date is not in any document in this run, so the size of the slip cannot be measured. What
confirms it: a named customer qualification completed, or a relay revenue line appearing.
What kills it: a second slip past the second half of FY27.

**The rare earth business made almost no money and is the riskiest leg.** Quantum Magnetics
booked Rs 0.78 Cr of revenue in FY26 against Rs 5.37 Cr the year before, a fall of about
85%, and lost Rs 5.62 Cr. Management attributes this to Chinese export controls on rare
earth materials, which is plausible and is named openly in the annual report. In the same
year the subsidiary drew an unsecured foreign currency loan of Rs 47.81 Cr, which pushed
consolidated debt to equity from 0.17 times to 0.56 times. So the leg of the business that
earns least carries the most new debt. Phase two is guided to Q3 FY27 with first sales in
Q4 FY27. What confirms it: phase two commissioned on schedule and any revenue at all
appearing. What kills it: another year of near zero revenue, or further tightening of
Chinese export controls.

**Working capital has no catalyst at all.** Nobody has announced a factoring facility, a
debtor day target, or a collection programme, and management does not discuss working
capital in the annual report. The only balance sheet action of the year was to triple the
borrowing headroom.

**One sector tailwind, correctly discounted.** India's smart meter programme has installed
65 million of a targeted 250 million meters as of April 2026, so the runway is long and it
is independently corroborated by Salzer Electronics. But a sector tailwind is not a company
catalyst. Every component supplier gets it. What would make it PML specific is winning more
content per meter, which is exactly what the relay is meant to do and has not done yet.

## 7. THE VERDICT

**Composite score +1 out of a possible +8, which is the DEEP WATCH leaning AVOID band.**
One transition is starting, three are flat, none is firing. The Kernex cap is not engaged,
because cash conversion is scored stagnant rather than declining, but it sits one judgment
call away and the alternative reading is set out in full in section 4. The TRIM rule does
not apply, because the four transitions were never all firing backward.

The whole call turns on one thing: **the September 2026 quarter.** The June quarter already
showed revenue up 19.1% and profit down 14.1% standalone and 38.4% consolidated, with no
management explanation on file because the results filing carries no commentary. If the
September quarter repeats that pattern, the alloys recovery is being consumed by the cost
of the capital being spent on the two bets that have not started, and the composite falls.
If it shows profit growing again with receivable days back under 76, margin moves toward
starting and the picture improves materially.

Symmetrically: the bull case is that a real, documented, already earning alloys furnace is
being priced alongside two options that cost money today and could be worth a great deal in
FY28, in a company whose credit rating was upgraded in February 2026. The bear case is that
the capital base doubled while profit did not, three of four transitions are flat, the
subsidiary carrying the new debt earns almost nothing, and nobody has said anything about
working capital.

### THE EARNINGS BASIS QUESTION, for Keerti to decide

Which earnings does the destination multiple get applied to?

**One year forward earnings.** Fits a business whose alloys furnace is already ramping and
whose FY27 will carry a fuller year of it. But FY27 also carries the cost of two lines that

quarter, and the June quarter already showed profit falling while revenue rose. A forward
basis credits a ramp that has not printed, and this management's delivery grade is C.

**Trailing earnings.** Uses FY26, which is audited and real. But FY26 carries a Rs 1.74 Cr
one time labour code charge, and it excludes the alloys furnace's first full year, so it
understates the current run rate.

There is a second choice bundled inside this one: **standalone or consolidated**. Standalone
profit of Rs 20.39 Cr excludes the subsidiary that holds the Rs 47.81 Cr borrowing and lost
Rs 5.62 Cr. Consolidated profit of Rs 14.77 Cr includes it. The gap is 38%, and it moves the
answer more than most of the pillars do.
