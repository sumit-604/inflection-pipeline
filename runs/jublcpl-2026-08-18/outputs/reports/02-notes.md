Method: re-read the notes a third time hunting specifically for (a) cross-statement
number mismatches, (b) contradictions between notes, (c) deliberately thin disclosure

### New pattern-level findings

1. **Cross-statement reconciliation check — PASSES, and sharpens the cash-conversion
   story.** Every cash-flow-derived figure cited in Pass 1 (Finding #3) and Pass 2
   (Finding #10) was independently checked against the printed Statement of Cash Flows
   (pp.101-102) and ties out exactly: net cash from operating activities ₹75.314 Cr
   (FY26) vs ₹131.445 Cr (FY25); working-capital drag (trade receivables/loans/other
   assets) ₹140.029 Cr; direct taxes paid ₹44.008 Cr (FY26) vs ₹19.546 Cr (FY25);
   share-based payment expense ₹4.804 Cr vs ₹2.681 Cr; investment in the new subsidiary
   ₹0.10 Cr; ESOP cash proceeds ₹0.739 Cr. No contradiction found. NEWLY NOTICED in this
   pass: "Operating cash flow before working capital changes" (i.e., cash profitability
   before working-capital and tax timing) actually GREW a healthy +34.0% YoY (₹149.213
   Cr → ₹199.964 Cr, Statement of Cash Flows, p.101) — closely tracking the reported PBT
   growth. This means the operating-cash-conversion collapse flagged in Pass 1 is
   entirely a working-capital and cash-tax-timing phenomenon (subsidy receivable
   buildup + near-doubled cash taxes paid), NOT a decline in underlying cash-generative
   profitability. This materially sharpens (without deleting) the FLAG-CASH finding: the
   quality-of-earnings concern is real and worth monitoring, but it is a balance-sheet/
   timing issue, not evidence the P&L profit itself is being manufactured. 🟡 Watch
   (refines Pass 1 Finding #3).

2. **Minor self-correction, not a company issue.** Pass 1's Section 2 (RPT) cited FY26/
   FY25 continuing-ops PAT as "₹126.36 Cr" / "₹87.87 Cr" when cross-checking KMP
   remuneration growth. The correct figures per Note 50 (EPS, p.163) are ₹127.636 Cr
   (FY26) and ₹88.719 Cr (FY25) — Pass 1's own transcription was slightly off, though the
   +43.9% growth-rate comparison it drew was numerically correct (127.636/88.719 =
   +43.9%). Corrected in this consolidated report; no restatement or company-side
   inconsistency involved.

3. **Generic, unquantified restatement note.** Note 51, the final note (standalone
   p.164): "Previous year figures have been re-grouped and re-arranged wherever
   necessary to conform current year's classification." No line items, no amounts, no
   explanation of what moved. This sits alongside a genuine, material presentation
   change already flagged in Pass 1 (Note 39, p.149): FY26 is the first year P&K
   Fertilizers is reported as its own segment rather than folded into a broader Agri
   segment, and FY25 comparatives were restated onto the new three-segment basis. Given
   that change, a generic non-quantified catch-all restatement note is a disclosure gap
   — an investor cannot verify from the notes alone which other FY25 comparative figures
   moved, by how much, or why. 🟡 Watch.

4. **Going concern language: NONE FOUND IN DOCUMENT.** No going-concern note or
   statement appears anywhere in Note 2 (Basis of Preparation, Material Accounting
   Policies, pp.103-105) or elsewhere in the Notes reviewed across all three passes.
   This absence is consistent with, and not undercut by, the company's improving
   liquidity and deleveraging profile (Section 7 of Pass 1) — flagged here only because
   the pattern-pass protocol requires an explicit confirmation either way.

5. **Aggregated disclosure-selectivity pattern.** Viewed together across all three
   passes, at least four of the year's largest unexplained YoY swings receive ZERO
   narrative explanation in the notes, despite the company providing granular
   quantitative disclosure elsewhere (e.g., full ageing tables, actuarial sensitivity
   tables): Stores & spares inventory +69.7% (Note 11, p.125); "Discounts, claims to
   customers and other selling expenses" +216.5% (Note 29, p.135); "Other financial
   assets — Others" (non-current) ~128x (Note 9, p.124); and a first-appearance ₹1.568
   Cr "past service cost/curtailment" gratuity line item (Note 34B(i), p.140). Each was
   already individually flagged in Pass 1/Pass 2; the PATTERN itself — that narrative
   explanation is consistently absent specifically for the largest swings, cutting
   across unrelated note categories (inventory, opex, financial assets, employee
   benefits) — is a standalone disclosure-quality observation appropriate to this pass.
   🟡 Watch.

6. **Confirmatory cross-checks that raised no new issues:** Note 33 discontinued
   operations (retail business) ties to the Statement of Cash Flows discontinued-
   operations line (NIL FY26 vs ₹(0.547) Cr FY25) with no new activity; Note 50's
   share-issuance footnote (82,193 shares on 13 Oct 2025 + 3,199 shares on 06 Jan 2026 =
   85,392 shares) exactly matches Pass 2's ESOP exercise finding; the Note 30 deferred-
   tax reconciliation's ₹117.003 Cr vs ₹117.056 Cr FY25 PBT figures (already flagged by
   Pass 1 as "immaterial rounding") remain immaterial on this re-check — a genuine but
   trivial (₹0.05 Cr, <0.05%) notes-vs-statement rounding mismatch, not a substantive
   contradiction.

No further contradictions, restated numbers, or subsequent events beyond those already

### D. FIVE QUESTIONS FOR MANAGEMENT

1. What specifically drove the P&K Fertilizers segment's swing from a ₹11.00 Cr loss to a ₹46.17 Cr profit in FY26 (subsidy timing, raw material cost, pricing), and is it structurally sustainable? Given this segment is being demerged, what is management's view of the retained Performance Polymers & Chemicals segment's standalone earnings trajectory post-demerger?
2. What is driving the +92.2% YoY growth in the fertilizer subsidy receivable, what is the government's expected release timeline, and why has the >6-month-overdue bucket of the receivables ageing schedule nearly tripled?
3. What specifically caused "Discounts, claims to customers and other selling expenses" to more than triple YoY (+216.5%) — a change in trade terms, competitive intensity, or one-off settlements?
4. Given Note 48(vi) states there are no MSME dues outstanding beyond 45 days, why does the company recognise interest on delayed MSME payments (doubled YoY) and disclose ₹1.958 Cr of MSME payables in the overdue ageing buckets?
5. What is the expected timeline and completion percentage for the Advance License Scheme export obligation (₹39.626 Cr, +31.2% YoY), given actual export revenue has declined for the year, and what is the estimated exposure to customs duty clawback/penalty if the obligation is not met?

### E. NOTES-BASED RED FLAGS

- Questionable expense classification: a ₹2.284 Cr Labour Code implementation cost — described in the company's own note as subject to further "appropriate accounting effect" as rules evolve, i.e. an ongoing/structural item — classified as "exceptional" and excluded from headline operating results.
- Direct notes-vs-notes contradiction: Note 48(vi)'s boilerplate "no MSME dues outstanding for more than 45 days" versus Note 19.1's ageing schedule (₹1.958 Cr overdue) and the doubled interest-on-delayed-MSME-payments disclosure.
- Undisclosed risk indicator: a rising unmet export obligation under the Advance License Scheme against declining actual exports, with no maturity/completion percentage disclosed anywhere in the notes — a latent contingent duty/penalty exposure not captured in the Contingent Liabilities note.
- Materialised litigation understated by classification: the Shivashakthi Builders partial decree (₹8.00 Cr + 8% p.a. interest, already passed against the company) continues to be presented under "Contingent Liabilities" rather than flagged as a recognised/probable exposure.
- Recurring selective-disclosure pattern: the largest YoY swings across four unrelated note categories (inventory, other opex, other financial assets, employee benefits) each carry zero narrative explanation, despite granular quantitative disclosure elsewhere in the same notes.

### F. ONE-LINE NOTES VERDICT

The notes reveal moderate to concerning accounting practices. Key concern: this year's
entire profit growth came from the P&K Fertilizers segment that is being demerged out,
funded by a subsidy-receivable-driven working-capital deterioration and a genuine MSME
disclosure contradiction. Key strength: transparent deleveraging, conservative ECL
provisioning growth ahead of receivables growth, and clean deferred tax and
related-party loan disclosures. Overall accounting quality: 5.5/10.
