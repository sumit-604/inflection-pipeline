# PREMEXPLN: Premier Explosives Ltd, business narrative (phase 1 lite, run 2026-11-15)

## BUSINESS UNDERSTANDING NARRATIVE

Premier Explosives makes energetic materials, the chemicals that burn or detonate on purpose, at two plants in Katepally and Peddakandukur. About 72% of FY26 revenue came from defence products: solid propellant for missiles, igniters, rocket motors, and the chaff and flares that aircraft release to decoy an incoming missile. Another 9% came from running solid propellant plants for ISRO at Sriharikota under an operations and maintenance contract, and the last 19% came from commercial explosives for mines, such as bulk explosives, cast boosters and detonating fuse. A missile programme cannot easily change its propellant, because the grain is qualified into the design: the company says it supplies all the solid propellant for MRSAM, LRSAM and Astra, the igniters for every Agni stage, and the booster grains for Akash. A mine can change its explosives supplier with little cost, because Coal India and Singareni buy on lowest price. The defence buyers are the Ministry of Defence and the Air Force, Bharat Dynamics, which buys propellant casts and assembles the motors itself, DRDO programmes, ISRO, and foreign defence buyers who hold four signed export orders worth about Rs 475 Cr. These buyers inspect stock before dispatch, and DRDO must clear any new raw material, so a new product or a new input takes months to qualify; one unnamed customer takes 10% or more of sales, and the run did not establish concentration beyond that. Present demand comes from the "MoD/IAF Rs 430 Cr chaff and flare order execution and LD claim", from "BDL and DRDO missile production (Akash, MRSAM, LRSAM, Astra)", from the "ISRO SHAR solid-propellant O&M renewal", worth about Rs 18 Cr a year with 2.5 years left, and on the commercial side from "Coal output and Coal India/Singareni explosives tenders". Growth should come first from defence spending, tracked through "Defence budget, DAC approvals, Positive Indigenisation Lists": the FY27 defence budget is Rs 7.85 lakh Cr, up 15.2%, and about 75% of the Rs 1.39 lakh Cr capital acquisition budget is earmarked for Indian industry. Exports are the second driver, and they convert only through "Export authorisation and buyer import licences": about Rs 400 Cr of the order book waited on licences, and one Rs 18.9 Cr order was cancelled when the licence was refused. Plant availability depends on "PESO licences for Katepally and Peddakandukur", and the pace of the serviceable market shows up in "Solar Industries defence revenue vs Rs 4,500 Cr FY27 target", since Solar is the largest private peer and its defence revenue rose 94% in FY26. Defence products hold the only moat the run found, and it is an existing moat, not a forming one: programme spec in, AS9100D and NABL accreditation, and DSIR recognised laboratories. That lock in does not yet earn price, because FY26 EBITDA margin was 10.0% while the two listed peers earned 28% to 33% on defence work. The forming advantages scored 12.3 in the Emerging Moat scan, 0.3 above the MODEST floor, and a verifier finds that the framework as written gives between 9.3 and 11.4, which is class NONE. The claim to be the only qualified Indian maker of countermeasures is untested, since no peer mentions chaff or flares and Solar opened a chaff plant at Nagpur in 2024. The licence regime raises the cost of entry, but it also shut this company's own plants after accidents, so the scan rated the regulatory advantage weak. Commercial explosives have no moat: more than 40 players compete at thin margins, and the company withdrew from Coal India bulk supply on price. The operations and maintenance line has no moat the run established either, since the Jagdalpur contract was lost on price, and the talent asymmetry and cannibalisation barrier categories both scored zero.

Defence order book waits on execution proof

Premier Explosives makes propellants, rocket motors, countermeasures and mining explosives, and filed its 46th annual report in September 2026. FY26 revenue was Rs 388 Cr, down 7%. Defence and space made 81% of it, and 86% of the September 2026 quarter. The order book was Rs 1,569 Cr in May 2026, about 4 times FY26 revenue, and Rs 1,642 Cr in October 2026, 96% of it defence. Commercial explosives carry less than half a year of orders, so that line books and ships. The company reports one segment, so no filing shows which line earns the profit.

Two changes are underway. The first is ownership. The founding Gupta family moved its 41.33% into the AKS Family Trust in February 2026, on an exemption that said control would not change, and agreed in July 2026 to sell all of it to Apollo Micro Systems for about Rs 1,550 Cr. The 26% open offer was paid on 4 November 2026, and the share purchase closes after competition approval, which the company expects in December 2026. The second change is capacity and product. A Rs 60 Cr programme at Katepally adds RDX and HMX capacity and a large rocket motor plant, and its start date has slipped three times. New mines, drone payloads and ammunition wait on DRDO clearance of an alternate raw material, now expected around February 2027. The operator has not signed the Mental Model, so this run names no starting rung and no target rung.

The backward score is AVERAGE: 62 of 160, with a core score of 53 of 100. It is two periods in one. FY17 to FY23 produced a median ROCE of 8.2% and two years of operating losses. FY24 to FY26 ROCE ran at 17% to 20%. FY26 leaned on filed other income of Rs 42 Cr, of which Rs 34 Cr was a supplier discount on materials bought in an earlier year. One time items made up 64.8% of FY26 PBT, and ROCE without other income was 8.7%. A verifier found two proxies in the score, for ROCE and for capex, and the core score moves between 45 and 57 across readings; the class is AVERAGE in every reading. Debt is low at 0.13 times equity, and net cash was Rs 23 Cr, down from Rs 56.5 Cr.

Cash did not follow profit in FY26. Standalone operating cash flow was negative Rs 1.4 Cr against PAT of Rs 45.8 Cr. Working capital absorbed 5,863 lakh rupees (Rs 58.6 Cr). Trade payables fell from Rs 117 Cr to Rs 42 Cr, finished goods rose 89% while revenue fell 7%, and advances to suppliers doubled. Receivables older than one year rose 33.7% to 1,187 lakh rupees (Rs 11.9 Cr), and dues past six months rose to 25.3% of gross receivables from 20.5%. Balance confirmations sent by the auditor drew no reply. The two years before ran the other way: three year operating cash flow was 1.91 times PAT. One reading says FY26 unwound an earlier payables build and stocked goods for an FY27 ramp. The other says government dues are ageing and the supplier discount may be a credit note, not cash. The H1 FY27 cash flow statement filed with the September quarter results separates the two, and no stage has read it yet.

Management credibility grades D. Over the last four quarters, 14 promises were scored: 2 delivered, 4 partial, 8 missed. FY26 revenue came in at Rs 388 Cr against a Rs 500 Cr guide. The FY27 guide then rose to Rs 600 to 700 Cr, and Rs 600 Cr was kept in August. EBITDA of 15% to 20% was promised on three calls, against about 9.9% in FY26 and 9.3% in H1 FY27, both before other income. H1 FY27 revenue was Rs 248 Cr, 41% of Rs 600 Cr, so the second half needs about Rs 176 Cr a quarter. Two promises held. Other income fell back to Rs 1.8 Cr and Rs 2.5 Cr in the June and September quarters, as management said it would. Delayed dispatches recovered in the September quarter: revenue was Rs 145 Cr, up 42% on the June quarter, at an 11.9% EBITDA margin.

Governance draws a CONCERN verdict, and it scores the family that is leaving. The auditor qualified internal controls over procurement, where one person holds approval. The auditor's CARO report records a whistle blower complaint and a special audit, while the governance report says no complaint was received. SEBI sought information on suspected insider trading in the stock in November 2025. Three fatal incidents hit two plants in about seven months of 2025. Independent directors fell to 3 of 8 after 1 November 2026, with no replacement found. On the other side, the promoter pledged nothing, related party dealings are low, and the exit is a full strategic sale with the open offer already paid.

The peers confirm the backdrop and not the size of the miss. Solar Industries and Astra Microwave both report heavy defence demand, inspection and approval delays, working capital drag and higher input prices. Both earned 28% to 33% EBITDA on defence work and met or beat FY26 guidance. Solar says rise and fall clauses pass about 60% of input cost increases through to the customer. Premier's share of the qualified private defence energetics pool fell from about 19.9% to 10.7% in FY26, as Solar's defence revenue rose 94% and Premier's fell 6.5%. No peer names Premier, and the peer stage logged no formal contradiction. The licence, moat and integration questions that matter most stayed unverifiable, and Bharat Dynamics, the buyer side view, has no transcripts.

The market stage ran partial and sized a large pond around a small business. Vendor reports put the addressable market at Rs 37,637 Cr to Rs 66,263 Cr, but those totals include non energetic content. The serviceable market is Rs 2,949 Cr, of which Premier holds 10.7%, a headroom of 9.4 times; the stage labels the runway STRONG. The three year obtainable revenue is Rs 773 Cr, a 25.8% annual rate, and the five year figure is Rs 1,089 Cr, a 22.9% rate. Capacity is the binding limit: the Rs 600 Cr FY27 guide already exceeds committed capacity of Rs 518.5 Cr by Rs 81.5 Cr, and the gap reaches Rs 254 Cr by FY29. Management gives no market size of its own.
