## BUSINESS UNDERSTANDING NARRATIVE

eMudhra sells digital trust. The product is proof that a person, a device or a
document is what it claims to be, and the proof is a cryptographic certificate.

The first line is Trust Services, 20% of FY26 revenue. eMudhra holds a
Certifying Authority licence under the Indian IT Act. It issues digital
signature certificates and runs an e-signature service. The service processes
well over three lakh transactions a day in banking and financial services. The
customer cannot easily do without it because Indian law requires a licensed CA
to issue a legally valid digital signature. Only a handful of licensed CAs
exist. Management cites about 38% share by value of the Indian digital
signature certificate market, sourced to Frost and Sullivan 2024.

The second line is Enterprise Solutions, sold in India and internationally.
This is software. The main platforms manage the life cycle of certificates
inside a large organisation, issue device and machine identities, run public
key infrastructure, and handle e-passports and digital identity for
governments. The buyer is a bank, a government department or a critical
infrastructure operator. The buying behaviour is a long procurement, a
security review and then a multi-year deployment, which is itself the
switching cost.

Demand today has three named drivers. In Europe, the NIS2 and DORA regulations
force banks and critical infrastructure to prove identity controls. In the
United States, a market rule cutting TLS certificate lifetimes to 47 days by
2029 multiplies the number of certificate renewals a company must manage,
which is a direct volume tailwind for certificate life cycle management. In
Africa and the Middle East, governments are buying digital public
infrastructure stacks covering digital identity, PKI, e-passports and e-sign.

Demand should grow for a structural reason. Certificates expire. Shorter
lifetimes mean more renewals. Renewals at scale cannot be done by hand, so the
customer must buy management software. That is a recurring volume driver, not
a one-time sale.

Where the advantage sits differs by line. Trust Services has the strongest
position, because the CA licence is a regulatory barrier and the share is
large. Enterprise Solutions in India earns a high margin and appears to hold
real pricing power. Enterprise Solutions outside India is the growth engine and
the weakest on margin, and part of the international group is a staffing and
recruitment business, TWO95 International Inc, whose economics are not those of
a software product. This shallow pass did not run a moat scan, so no moat
category is scored.

## THE EARNINGS-QUALITY QUESTION

This is the single most important unresolved item on this name.

eMudhra capitalised Rs 60.1 cr of organic IP in FY26. That is 8.6% of revenue
and 55% of reported profit after tax [COMPUTED]. FY27 research and development
spend is guided at 7-8% of revenue.

Capitalising software development is permitted under Ind AS 38 when the
recognition criteria are met, so this is not an allegation. It is a
measurement question with a large answer. If a substantial part of that Rs 60.1 cr
would be expensed under a stricter reading, reported profit and therefore the
entire price-to-earnings framing changes materially. The amortisation profile
also determines how much of past capitalisation is now flowing back through the
profit and loss statement.

The effective tax rate of 16.2% is a second piece of the same question. A full
run must establish whether it reflects a sustainable structure across
jurisdictions or a timing benefit.

Neither can be settled without the FY26 annual report. Both are named below as
load-bearing facts.

## THE ACQUISITION STRUCTURE

- Cryptas International GmbH, Vienna. eMudhra BV bought 51% for EUR 5.0 million
  cash. An upside payment is due, based on enterprise value at 10 times FY2026

- AI Cyber Forge Inc, United States. Bought by eMudhra Inc for USD 4.80 million
  cash, merged into eMudhra Inc on 6 December 2025.

Source: Outcome of Board Meeting, FY2026, Q4, page 12, notes 3 and 5.

The Cryptas 49% put is a real future claim on cash or equity whose size is not
yet fixed. It rises with Cryptas EBITDA, so the better Cryptas performs the
more the remaining stake costs. Framework rule v3.8 Amendment 18 bars carrying
an unresolved probability slice into an exit. This obligation must be
quantified and placed in the equity bridge before any valuation, not left as a
footnote.

The group now consolidates nineteen entities. That complexity is priced once,
in the discount rate, per Section 1B v3.6 Amendment 13.

## ARCHETYPE AND QUALITY LADDER

The company is a blend and should be declared per line.

- Trust Services: LICENCE / SCARCITY BUSINESS. The economics are licence
  validity, regulated entry, and share of a protected market.
- Enterprise Solutions: closest to BUILD-TO-SPEC / platform software, sold into
  long procurement cycles with deployment switching costs.
- The unexplained ~21% of revenue, probably staffing services, fits neither and
  must be identified before the archetype is declared final.

Quality ladder placement:

- Trust Services. R4 to R5. A licensed CA with about 38% value share sits at
  the scarcity end. But the segment margin fell 500 basis points in FY26, which
  is not what a scarcity asset normally does. This needs explanation.
- Enterprise Solutions India. R4. A 57.5% segment result margin implies real
  pricing power, though the Q4 print fell to 51.9%.
- Enterprise Solutions outside India. R3 at best on a 21.9% margin, and lower
  if the staffing revenue sits here.

The claimed transition: from an India-centric licensed trust business to a
global identity software company. The mix has moved. International is already
64% of revenue.

Here is the problem with the transition as stated. The company is growing
fastest in its lowest-margin geography. On the ladder that is mix moving DOWN,
not up, even as revenue moves up. A climb is defined by pricing power and ROCE
durability, not by size. Until the international segment margin rises toward
the group average, the evidence shows scale, not a rung change.

Section 1B sector cap row: Platform / SaaS / IT services, 45x. The fit is
arguable rather than clean, because a licensed CA is not SaaS and a staffing
subsidiary is not either. If a full run finds the staffing revenue material,
a blended cap is the honest treatment, and the operator must rule on it.

## UGLINESS VERDICT

The ugly optic is margin. Group EBITDA margin fell 190 basis points, Trust
Services fell 500 and international fell 410, all in a year of 35% revenue
growth.

Reading one, ARTIFACT-OF-CLIMB. Two acquisitions landed mid-year at lower
margin and are not yet integrated. Cryptas was loss-making at purchase and is
guided to over USD 1 million of profit in FY27. Cross-selling the CLM platform
into the Cryptas base has deals expected to close within one to two quarters.
Integration costs depress the year they land and lift the years after.

Reading two, STRUCTURAL-FEATURE. International enterprise work carries more
services content and less product content, so it earns a structurally lower
margin, and the faster it grows the lower the group margin goes. The Trust
Services fall supports this reading differently: that is the protected line,
and it lost margin without an acquisition to blame.

The one observation that separates them: whether Enterprise Solutions outside
India prints a segment result margin above 26%, its FY25 level, in any quarter
of FY27. If integration was the cause, it recovers. If mix was the cause, it
does not.

Verdict: UNRESOLVED, leaning ARTIFACT-OF-CLIMB on the international line and
UNEXPLAINED on the Trust Services line. The Trust Services margin fall is the
part no acquisition explains and it is the part nobody has asked about.

## SECOND-ORDER CHAINS

1. International is 64% of revenue and earns a 21.9% segment margin against
   57.5% in India enterprise. Management guides 20-25% annual growth in
   Enterprise Solutions and 15-20% in Trust Services. [INFERENCE] The guided mix
   drives group margin DOWN mechanically, because the fastest-growing slice is
   the least profitable. Hitting the guided 25% group EBITDA margin therefore
   requires the international margin itself to rise, not just volume to grow.
   That single variable, international segment margin, decides whether the
   guidance is arithmetic or hope. It is the one number to watch every quarter.

2. Rs 60.1 cr of organic IP was capitalised in FY26 against Rs 110.05 cr of
   reported profit. R&D is guided at 7-8% of revenue for FY27, so on the guided
   revenue base the capitalised amount grows. [INFERENCE] Reported profit growth
   and cash profit growth will diverge further unless amortisation catches up.
   The gap between the two is the real margin of safety question on this name,
   and it is invisible in the price-to-earnings ratio everyone quotes.

3. The Cryptas 49% put is priced at 10 times trailing EBITDA with a floor of
   one times revenue, exercisable 2028 to 2030. [INFERENCE] Success at Cryptas
   raises the cost of buying the rest of it, and the obligation falls inside the
   operator's own 3-5 year holding window. A shareholder buying today owns a
   business with an undisclosed future cash call attached. Quantify it before
   any position, never after.

4. Trust Services margin fell from 36.4% to 31.4% with no acquisition in that
   segment to explain it. Management commentary describes the franchise as
   performing "steadily" and cites combo certificates and direct retail sales
   as growth drivers. [INFERENCE] A protected, licensed business losing 500
   basis points of margin while growing 32% suggests either price competition
   among licensed CAs, or a deliberate shift to a lower-priced volume product,
   or a change in the channel economics of direct retail. None of these has
   been asked about on a call. This is the highest-value unasked question on the
   name.

5. Interest expense rose from Rs 1.16 cr to Rs 5.07 cr, and two acquisitions
   consumed roughly Rs 76 cr, while the group added a nineteen-entity structure
   with an outstanding upside payment. [INFERENCE] The acquisition programme is
   now a funding commitment, not an opportunistic one, and management has said
   it is looking at further bolt-ons of up to EUR 10-12 million. Verify
   available headroom and whether the growth algorithm depends on continuing to
   buy revenue.
