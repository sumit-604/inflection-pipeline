# Stage 6: Peer Concall Verification — Airfloa Rail Technology Ltd (544516)
Run date: 2026-07-15 | Peers: FRONTSP (Frontier Springs Ltd), TEXRAIL (Texmaco Rail & Engineering Ltd)

Peer segments: FRONTSP supplies coil springs, air springs and forgings to Indian Railways
(suspension/safety components). TEXRAIL manufactures freight wagons, foundry castings, rail
electrification infrastructure and rolling-stock leasing, with a fast-growing diversification
agenda (defense, metro, Kavach, wheels, AI/GCC, real estate). Neither peer is in Airfloa's
apparent niche (rail interior fit-out / door systems / vacuum toilet systems / composite
components implied by B05), so all verdicts below carry an "adjacent segment" caveat per

## PART 1: CLAIM-BY-CLAIM VERIFICATION

### Q1. Is raw material inflation (aluminium +~80% YoY, stainless steel +~60-65% YoY) industry-wide, and are price-variation clauses similarly limited to ~50-60% pass-through?

**Claim (verbatim from B05):** "Is raw material inflation (aluminium +~80% YoY, stainless steel +~60-65% YoY) industry-wide, and are peers' price-variation clauses similarly limited to ~50-60% pass-through? ... Tests whether Airfloa's EBITDA margin compression (25%->20.1%) is sector-wide or company-specific procurement/hedging weakness."

**Verdict: CONTRADICTED** (on the margin-compression consequence; the underlying input-cost inflation direction is corroborated)

**Peer evidence:**
- FRONTSP confirms steel price inflation is real and industry-wide: "I must be candid, steel prices remained elevated through the second part of the year and that did create cost pressure." (FRONTSP-Jun_2026 Transcript, Kapil Bhatia, opening remarks, Q4/FY26 call, Jun 3 2026)
- But FRONTSP not only avoided margin compression, it **expanded** margin materially over the same period the steel inflation occurred: "EBITDA margin improved to 26.80% in Financial Year 26 from 21.47% in Financial Year 25. A significant improvement of 533 basis points." (same transcript, same speaker) The mechanism given: "We responded by negotiating more favorable terms with our vendors and by focusing our execution on high value tenders, we were able to largely offset the margin headwind this way."
- FRONTSP also describes the structural pass-through mechanism differently than Airfloa's framing: existing Railways contracts carry **no** mid-contract price revision ("Railways has a fixed price policy, they don't increase the rate in between the contract"); recovery happens only by re-pricing **future** tenders higher, not via a formal price-variation clause with a fixed pass-through percentage. (FRONTSP-Jun_2026, Garvit Goel exchange) No peer discloses a specific pass-through percentage comparable to Airfloa's "~50-60%."
- FRONTSP flags forward risk consistent with Airfloa's concern but smaller in magnitude: "if steel prices remain firm in FY27, there may be some moderation in margins in the near term" — guided at most 1-2 points of margin give-back, not a 5-point compression.
- TEXRAIL's margin weakness in the same period (FY26 EBITDA margin 10.2%, down from an implied higher FY25 base, full-year revenue -14% YoY) is attributed almost entirely to volume/operating-leverage effects — wagon wheel-set shortages and US tariff-driven export declines — not to raw material input cost inflation. TEXRAIL does not discuss aluminium, stainless steel, or price-variation clause mechanics at all.

**Peers silent:** Neither peer discusses aluminium-specific inflation (Airfloa's stated +~80% YoY) or a quantified stainless-steel input cost move; TEXRAIL is silent on price-variation clause structure entirely.

**Net read:** The peer set weakens rather than supports Airfloa's "sector-wide margin compression" framing. FRONTSP faced the same broad input cost environment (elevated steel prices) in the same period and expanded margin by ~533bps through vendor negotiation and tender selection discipline, while Airfloa's EBITDA margin fell ~490bps (25%->20.1%). This is one data point, not proof, but it shifts the burden toward Airfloa-specific procurement or pricing execution rather than an unavoidable sector-wide headwind — consistent with the B05 flag on this point.

### Q2. Do peers report similarly stretched cash-conversion cycles / negative operating cash flow despite strong revenue growth?

**Claim (verbatim):** "Do peers report similarly stretched cash-conversion cycles / negative operating cash flow despite strong revenue growth? ... Tests whether Airfloa's back-ended execution and negative OCF (-Rs57.45cr FY26) is structural to the sector or company-specific working-capital weakness."

**Verdict: PARTIALLY VERIFIED** (one peer corroborates a structural sector dynamic, one peer contradicts it)

**Peer evidence:**
- TEXRAIL: an investor directly raised this exact pattern and got a candid management admission of a chronic, multi-year EBITDA-to-cash gap: "over the last 6 financial year, my company is having cumulative EBITDA of around Rs. 1,625 crores. But cumulative operating cash flow is only around Rs. 50 crores, sir. So in simple terms, we can see that EBITDA is growing, but not the cash." CFO Kishor Rajgaria's response confirmed rather than disputed the pattern: "in previous 6 years, so our turnover was very low. So we have increased our volume and also our requirement for working capital... that is why in past, you are seeing most of the EBITDA we have used and our remaining cash flow is low. But going forward, we will be getting benefits out of this. So operating cash flow will improve." (TEXRAIL-Concall_Feb_2026 Transcript, Q3 FY26 call, Feb 9 2026) This is a materially worse and longer-running EBITDA-OCF gap than Airfloa's, and management characterizes it as a natural consequence of scaling working capital with revenue growth — i.e., structural to the sector, not unique to Airfloa.
- FRONTSP contradicts the "structural to the sector" framing: it reports no negative OCF anywhere across the four transcripts, funds capex entirely from internal accruals ("we have not taken any term loans since last so many years, and we are able to claw back money from the profit" — FRONTSP-Jun_2026, Sucrit D. Patil exchange) and pays a continuous cash dividend. When an investor raised high trade receivables (a working-capital proxy) in the Jun-2026 call, management's answer implied comfort rather than strain: "Trade receivables are high because our turnover is high... it is all good trade receivables. There is no bad debt at all." (FRONTSP-Jun_2026, Siddharth exchange)

**Peers silent:** Neither peer discloses standalone OCF figures on the call in a way directly comparable to Airfloa's -Rs57.45cr FY26 number; the TEXRAIL figure is a 6-year cumulative disclosure, not an annual one.

**Net read:** The two peers split on this question. TEXRAIL's own investor-flagged, management-confirmed multi-year cash conversion gap lends real credibility to the idea that negative or lagging OCF during a revenue scale-up is a known feature of rail-component/rolling-stock manufacturing (working capital build ahead of collection). But FRONTSP shows the opposite is achievable at a similar or faster growth rate with disciplined receivables and no debt — so this is not universal, and Airfloa's negative OCF cannot be waved off as purely sector-driven. The TEXRAIL admission also cautions that such gaps, once established, can persist for years without a firm turnaround date — a point that should temper optimism about Airfloa's cash conversion resolving quickly.

### Q3. What growth rate, order-inflow pace, and bid-win ratio do peers cite for the national coach-refurbishment programme (~Rs26,000cr) and the Vande Bharat/Amrit Bharat/metro capex cycle?

**Claim (verbatim):** Tests whether Airfloa's FY27 guidance and Rs1,200cr bid pipeline framing (leaning on this sector tailwind) is consensus or optimistic.

**Verdict: UNVERIFIABLE** (for the specific coach-refurbishment programme and bid-win ratio); the broader capex tailwind is independently corroborated (see Part 2/2A below)

**Peer evidence:** Neither FRONTSP nor TEXRAIL mentions a "coach refurbishment programme" or the ~Rs26,000cr figure by name in any of the eight transcripts. Both peers extensively discuss the broader Vande Bharat / Amrit Bharat / LHB / metro capex cycle and cite specific Railways budget allocations (FRONTSP: "Union Budget 2026 allocated a record capital outlay of Rs. 2.65 lakh crore for Indian Railways" — FRONTSP-Concall_May_2025; TEXRAIL: "Union Budget 2026-2027... allocated a record Rs. 2.93 lakh crores" — TEXRAIL-Concall_Feb_2026), but neither cites a bid-win ratio or order-inflow pace specific to refurbishment work.

**Peers silent:** Both peers, on the specific refurbishment programme and its Rs26,000cr sizing.

**Net read:** The general railway capex tailwind (budget allocation growth, LHB/Vande Bharat/Amrit Bharat coach production ramp, metro expansion) is strongly and independently corroborated by both peers as real and multi-year — this supports the demand backdrop Airfloa's guidance leans on. But the specific ~Rs100cr refurbishment order target and Rs1,200cr bid pipeline sizing cannot be triangulated; this claim should stay unverified on its specifics rather than being read as confirmed by the general tailwind.

### Q4. Have peers disclosed comparable Chinese-machinery export-control/end-user-certificate delays affecting capex plans since January 2026?

**Verdict: UNVERIFIABLE**

**Peer evidence:** No peer discusses China-specific export-control or end-user-certificate delays on machinery. FRONTSP mentions actively "negotiating about another line of coil springs and more automated line of manufacturers from China" in the Jun-2026 call with no mention of any delay or certification issue — if anything, this is mild evidence of a *different*, less-constrained sourcing experience with China than Airfloa describes, though it is a different equipment category and too thin to treat as contradiction. FRONTSP's only disclosed hammer-import delay was Ukraine-war shipping related (FRONTSP-Concall_May_2025), not China export control. TEXRAIL discusses Chinese wheel imports as a general sourcing option but no delay.

**Peers silent:** Both peers, on China export-control machinery delays specifically.

**Net read:** No peer evidence either way. The silence is worth noting per the operating rules: if this were a well-known, industry-wide China machinery export-control problem since January 2026, one might expect it to surface in at least one of eight peer calls spanning that period (the FRONTSP Jun-2026 call is contemporaneous), and it does not. This modestly raises the possibility the issue is more idiosyncratic to Airfloa's specific vendor/machinery choice than a broad supply-chain shock, but this is inference from silence, treated cautiously and flagged rather than concluded.

**Net read:** This claim cannot be triangulated with the peer set provided; the segment mismatch is real (adjacent, not identical, businesses) and should be read as a genuine coverage gap rather than a negative signal.

### Q6. Are peers pursuing defense/aerospace diversification at a similarly aggressive R&D intensity, and is that R&D expensed or capitalized?

**Verdict: UNVERIFIABLE** (on capitalization treatment); partial color on diversification intensity

**Peer evidence:**
- TEXRAIL is pursuing an aggressive, disclosed diversification program that explicitly includes defense: "So we chose defense as part of it... the Board has, of course, sanctioned the budget" with a Board-approved defense capex of "INR200 crores" inside a broader "Vision 2030" envelope of "INR1,500 crores to INR2,000 crores" across defense, wheels, metro, propulsion, Kavach, and AI/GCC verticals (TEXRAIL-Concall_May_2026, Sudipta Mukherjee). This confirms that aggressive multi-vertical diversification funded by disclosed capex is not unique to Airfloa among rail-sector peers — TEXRAIL is doing something directionally similar and at larger absolute scale.
- However, TEXRAIL frames this spend as **capex** for new business lines, not as an R&D-intensity ratio (e.g., "8-9% of sales") that is then capitalized — a different accounting characterization than Airfloa's B05-flagged treatment. No peer discloses whether R&D specifically (as distinct from capex) is expensed or capitalized, and neither peer states an R&D-to-sales percentage.
- FRONTSP's defense exploration remains small and early-stage across all four calls: "we are already talking to a consultant" (Nov-2024) through "we have some samples in defence as well. But the defence procedure is a little bit slow... it's taking some time for us to break through" (Jun-2026) — i.e., no capex or R&D-intensity disclosure comparable to Airfloa's step-up.

**Peers silent:** Both peers, on R&D expensing vs. capitalization treatment specifically.

**Net read:** TEXRAIL's evidence partially supports that aggressive defense/diversification investment is an emerging sector pattern, not an Airfloa-only phenomenon, which cuts against reading Airfloa's ambition as uniquely aggressive. But the specific accounting question (R&D capitalized vs. expensed) that drove the B05 flag remains completely untriangulated — no peer discloses this, so the accounting-optics concern stands unresolved rather than confirmed or dispelled.

## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

### 2A. Demand environment
Consensus, not contradiction. Both peers independently describe a strong, multi-year, well-funded railway capex cycle: FRONTSP cites the Rs2.65 lakh crore FY26 Railways budget (FRONTSP-Concall_May_2025) and Rs2.93 lakh crore is separately cited by TEXRAIL for FY27 (TEXRAIL-Concall_Feb_2026), both citing LHB coach ramp-up (6,000->10,000 coaches/year), Vande Bharat, Amrit Bharat sleeper rollout, and metro expansion as durable multi-year demand. This is directionally consistent with Airfloa's growth narrative. One complication: TEXRAIL's own FY26 revenue *fell* 14% YoY despite this favorable demand backdrop, due to wagon wheel-set supply constraints and US tariffs on exports — a reminder that a strong sector tailwind does not guarantee individual-company execution, which is relevant context for weighing Airfloa's own guidance credibility.

### 2B. Pricing and input costs
FRONTSP confirms steel price inflation was a real, industry-relevant pressure in the same period Airfloa reports its margin compression, but FRONTSP's own outcome (margin expansion via vendor negotiation and tender selection, not compression) complicates rather than supports Airfloa's margin narrative — see Q1 above. TEXRAIL's margin trajectory is driven by volume/operating leverage, not input costs, and is not directly comparable on this dimension.

### 2C. Capex cycle
This is not a uniform "industry-wide capacity race" — the two peers show genuinely different postures. FRONTSP is expanding cautiously and incrementally, funded entirely from internal accruals, explicitly framed as bottleneck-clearing rather than a step-change ("small Capex of 7-8 crore... continuous process"; "INR 20 crores to 25 crores CAPEX for all the three divisions" for FY27 — FRONTSP-Concall_Jun_2026). TEXRAIL, by contrast, has announced a large, multi-vertical diversification capex program (Vision 2030 / "Texmaco 2.0") spanning defense, wheels, metro, propulsion, Kavach and AI, sized at Rs1,500-2,000cr over several years, alongside a Rs700cr contingency provision flagged for balance-sheet caution around large new contract risk (TEXRAIL-Concall_May_2026). Airfloa's own profile — a 14-acre new facility, R&D step-up to 8-9% of sales, and a defense/aerospace diversification push — sits structurally closer to TEXRAIL's "lone diversifier" pattern than to FRONTSP's measured capacity-clearing pattern. This is worth flagging: Airfloa is not simply riding an industry-wide capacity build-out; it is making a company-specific strategic bet on diversification, similar in kind (though smaller in scale) to what TEXRAIL is doing, and unlike what FRONTSP is doing.

### 2D. Competitive mentions
No peer transcript mentions Airfloa Rail Technology by name or by unmistakable description in any of the eight calls reviewed. This is a null result, stated plainly per the operating rules — it neither confirms nor undermines Airfloa's competitive positioning claims; it means Airfloa does not register as visible competition to two of its closest listed rail-component peers during this period.

### 2E. Risks peers discuss that Airfloa's B05 does not
- **Wagon wheel-set shortage**, a sector-wide supply constraint from Indian Railways' own wheel-manufacturing capacity, repeatedly disclosed by TEXRAIL as a revenue driver across three consecutive quarters (TEXRAIL-Concall_Aug_2025, Nov_2025, Feb_2026) and referenced by an FRONTSP analyst comparing Airfloa's implied peers Titagarh/Texmaco/Jupiter (FRONTSP-Concall_Nov_2025). This is a different supply-chain risk than Airfloa's China machinery/EUC issue and is not mentioned anywhere in B05 — worth adding to Airfloa's risk register as a sector-level input constraint that could plausibly reach Airfloa's supply chain if it sources any wheel-adjacent components, though this is speculative extension beyond what B05 covers.
- **US tariff exposure on exports** hit TEXRAIL's Foundry division (30% export volume decline in a quarter — TEXRAIL-Concall_Nov_2025/Feb_2026). Not directly relevant to Airfloa's apparently domestic-focused business per B05, but a sector-level geopolitical/trade risk theme absent from Airfloa's own disclosures.
- **A Rs700cr contingency provision and qualified audit opinion** at TEXRAIL (TEXRAIL-Concall_May_2026), taken specifically against unforeseen large-contract execution risk in a volatile geopolitical environment — a governance/disclosure risk theme with no analogue raised in Airfloa's B05.
- **Chronic 6-year EBITDA-to-OCF gap** at TEXRAIL (see Q2) — while this corroborates rather than contradicts Airfloa's own flagged cash-conversion risk, its multi-year persistence at TEXRAIL is itself a risk data point Airfloa's B05 does not carry: the peer evidence suggests this kind of gap, once established, does not resolve quickly.
- **Deflection on Kavach-related questions is not unique to Airfloa.** TEXRAIL was asked directly why it is not visible in Kavach signalling and gave a near-identical evasive, non-committal answer across calls ("We are working on it, we can only say this much now... it is not that it is out of radar" — TEXRAIL-Concall_Aug_2025), mirroring the exact repeated-evasion pattern B05 flagged for Airfloa on its own Kavach-adjacent system. This is worth noting as an industry investor-relations pattern rather than an Airfloa-specific credibility problem, which somewhat tempers (without erasing) the weight of that particular B05 red flag.

## PART 4: TRIANGULATION SUMMARY

- **Claims verified:** 0 of 6 (fully verified — two independent peers corroborating direction and magnitude)
- **Claims partially verified:** 1 of 6 (Q2, cash conversion — split peer evidence)
- **Claims contradicted:** 1 of 6 (Q1, margin compression framing — priority item for synthesis)
- **Claims unverifiable:** 4 of 6 (Q3 specific programme sizing, Q4 China machinery delays, Q5 named competitors, Q6 R&D capitalization treatment)

**Single most consequential contradiction:** Q1. FRONTSP faced the same broadly elevated steel-price environment in the same period as Airfloa's FY26 margin compression, and instead of compressing, FRONTSP's EBITDA margin *expanded* 533bps (21.47%->26.80%) through vendor negotiation and tender selection discipline. This is one peer, in an adjacent but not identical segment, so it is not conclusive — but it directly undercuts the framing that Airfloa's margin drop (25%->20.1%) is an unavoidable, sector-wide raw-material shock rather than a company-specific procurement, hedging, or tender-selection weakness. This should be treated as a priority item for synthesis and valuation stages.

**Single strongest independent confirmation:** Q2. TEXRAIL's own investor-prompted, management-confirmed disclosure of a chronic 6-year gap between cumulative EBITDA (Rs1,625cr) and cumulative operating cash flow (Rs50cr) lends real, peer-sourced credibility to the idea that negative or lagging OCF during a rail-sector revenue scale-up is a structural, not purely company-specific, phenomenon. This somewhat mitigates the severity read on Airfloa's -Rs57.45cr FY26 standalone OCF — though it also warns that such gaps can persist far longer than one or two years without a clear catalyst, which argues against assuming a quick Airfloa turnaround.

**Overall assessment (symmetric):** The peer set complicates Airfloa's narrative more than it supports it, but does not conclusively undercut it either. On the positive side, the broad railway capex tailwind Airfloa's growth thesis leans on is independently and strongly corroborated by both peers via cited government budget figures and coach/metro/Vande Bharat demand commentary — this is genuine, real, and not company-specific optimism. On the negative side, the peer evidence complicates two of Airfloa's central defensive framings: the claim that margin compression is a shared, unavoidable sector-wide raw-material problem (FRONTSP's experience argues otherwise) and, more mildly, the claim that its diversification and R&D-intensity strategy is an ordinary defensive move (TEXRAIL shows aggressive diversification is happening elsewhere too, which cuts both ways — normalizing the strategy while also showing peers who disclose the accounting/capex mechanics more specifically than Airfloa does). Several of Airfloa's most company-specific claims (named competitors, China machinery delays, R&D capitalization treatment, the specific refurbishment programme sizing) remain genuinely untriangulated because the two available peers, while genuine rail-sector operators, sit in adjacent rather than identical sub-segments. Net effect: **complicates**.
