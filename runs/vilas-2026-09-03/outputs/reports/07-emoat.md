### 2C Growth embedded in capex — arithmetic shown

Total capex still under execution as of the run date (i.e. NOT already reflected in the FY26
run-rate, excluding the already-commissioned Unit 3 CRGO/nano/radiator lines): copper
conductors Rs25-30 Cr + HV bushings Phase-1 Rs10 Cr ≈ **Rs35-40 Cr** (midpoint Rs37.5 Cr).

Historical fixed-asset turnover, computed from disclosed figures (Inv. Pres. slide 8, revenue
÷ net PPE excl. CWIP): FY24 = 3,097/327 = 9.47x; FY25 = 3,531/323 = 10.93x; FY26 (post-Unit-3
commissioning, capacity still ramping) = 4,607/814 = **5.66x**.

Management itself separately claimed a 10x turnover for the (much larger) Unit 3 expansion
("It will be 10x, sir... INR75 crores... INR750 crores is the block value" — May-2025 concall,
Miten Shah Q&A, p.18-19) — this is a 🎙️ claim, and the FY26 realised figure (5.66x) came in at
roughly half that, i.e. the company's own forward multiplier has not yet been borne out by the
first year of actual results. This stage anchors on the realised (not claimed) ratio, per
"prioritise hard evidence over promises."

Arithmetic: Rs37.5 Cr incremental capex × 5.66x realised turnover ≈ **Rs212 Cr** implied
incremental revenue potential, once fully utilised (multi-year horizon, not a 12-month figure).
As % of FY26 revenue (Rs460.7 Cr): **≈46%**.

Caveat: this blends two dissimilar product lines (copper's own management-guided revenue/capex
ratio of ~4x-7x — Rs25-30 Cr capex → Rs100-200 Cr peak revenue per Nov-2025/May-2026 concalls —
is lower than the blended 5.66x used here; HV bushings has no revenue guidance at all yet). The
46% figure is a directional upper bound, not a forecast; capex_embedded_growth_pct in the YAML
block carries this number with this caveat attached.

### FAMILY A — Product & Technology

**A1 — Rare manufacturing capability.** Nanocrystalline core processing is management-described
as "highly technical," with only "2 or 3 products technology" mastered so far and a small
5-6-year-old pilot unit in Manesar being scaled (May-2025 concall p.9-10). Actual commissioning
and H2 FY26 sales (Rs3.48 Cr, 30,250 kg) are 📄-adjacent (Inv. Pres. slide 6), the forward
capability claims remain 🎙️. Radiator manufacture required a hired technical team from "India's
number 1 radiator manufacturing company" (unnamed — May-2025 concall p.10). **Strength: WEAK.**
Time to materialise: 12-24 months (nanocrystalline already ramping; further product tiers
2-3 years out per management's own words).

**A2 — Patent and IP pipeline.** NO EVIDENCE FOUND. No patent filings, licensing revenue, or
lab partnerships disclosed anywhere in the AR, concalls, or presentation.

**A3 — Process innovation.** AR's technology-absorption section states generically that
upgrades "yielded... enhanced product quality, increased operational efficiency, controlled
production costs, and reduced waste" (AR p.19) with no quantified yield or unit-cost data.
NO EVIDENCE FOUND (too generic to score).

**A4 — Product platform / modular architecture.** New products (radiator, copper, bushings) are
each accompanied by dedicated new hires and dedicated capex, the opposite of "SKU growth
without proportional R&D." NO EVIDENCE FOUND against this specific definition.

### FAMILY B — Supply Chain

**B1 — Backward integration and RM security.** Explicitly absent — CRGO steel is not
domestically producible in India (AR MDA p.34: "India currently has no domestic manufacturing of
CRGO steel") and VILAS does not backward-integrate. The company instead diversified suppliers
(AR p.36: "onboarded new suppliers... to ensure supply continuity" — 📄 stated action) and
sources from Japan, Korea, Germany, Russia and China (May-2025 concall p.22) plus two domestic
mills, NLMK and TKES, for ~25% of volume (May-2026 concall p.5-6). This is risk mitigation, not
a moat under B1's definition (no captive source, no 5+ year agreement disclosed, no captive
power). NO EVIDENCE FOUND.

**B2 — Qualification lock-in.** ISO 9001:2015 certification is held at all facilities (documented,
generic to the industry). PGCIL approval, if granted, would be a genuine multi-year qualification
asset but is treated under R1 (Section 4) to avoid double-counting the same fact under two
categories. No design-in/customer-funded-tooling language found for CRGO. NO EVIDENCE FOUND
under B2 specifically.

**B3 — Supply chain network effect.** NO EVIDENCE FOUND. VILAS is a single-node manufacturer,
not a platform.

### FAMILY I — Structural Asymmetries (operator directive, 20-Aug-2026)

**I1 — Talent asymmetry.** Leg (a) requires a documented class of unusually capable people
(named patent inventors, ex-DRDO/HAL concentration, or AR remuneration showing above-market pay
for technical hires). VILAS has one named, credentialed individual (Mr. Ramachandran) at a
25%-owned JV entity, with no remuneration disclosure yet — insufficient to establish a "class."
Leg (b) (competitor structurally cannot match the pay) has NO EVIDENCE at all — no PSU
comparison, no failed-poaching commentary, no competitor economics data. **Score: 0.**

**I2 — Cannibalization barrier.** The company's own commentary supplies the cleanest possible
test of this category and it fails it: one transformer OEM (TRIL) DID backward-integrate into
CRGO lamination manufacturing and STILL buys from VILAS today ("even today we are supplying to
TRIL also... backward integration will not solve the problem because that is not their main
business" — May-2025 concall p.5). This means the honest answer to "what must the best-resourced
competitor destroy to copy VILAS" is **nothing** — TRIL tried backward integration and it did
not eliminate the need for VILAS's supply. That is execution lead (quality, delivery, price), not
a cannibalization-protected configuration, which the framework explicitly scores as 0. VILAS's
customers are private companies, not PSUs, so the institutional-rigidity leg of I2 also does not
apply. **Score: 0.**
