### FAMILY C — CUSTOMER

**C1 — customer ecosystem / embedded relationships.** EVIDENCE FOUND,
MODERATE. Two documented data points: "more than seventy percent of our
business comes from customers who have been with us more than ten years"
(Annual_Report_2026, p.37) 📄, and Note 42's major-customer table showing
the top customer's share rising from 21.00% to 24.05% of standalone
revenue year-on-year (Annual_Report_2026, p.102) 📄. Rising share within
an already-decade-long relationship is consistent with deepening wallet
share — more scope from the same client, a genuine embedding signal. But
the category's richer sub-indicators (3+ product cross-sell, ERP/workflow
integration, co-development, AMC revenue, designed-around components) are
absent from the AR entirely; the evidence here is duration plus one
account's rising share, not the fuller embedded-relationship profile the
category describes. Time to materialise: already present, not purely
forward — the emerging element is the deepening trend, not the
relationship's existence. Strength: Moderate. See the constraint-3
discussion under C2 for why this same fact also reads as a risk.

**C2 — customer concentration improving.** NO EVIDENCE FOUND — and the
evidence runs the opposite direction. Note 42 shows the top customer's
share *rising* (21.00% to 24.05%) while the second customer's share fell
below the 10% disclosure threshold (11.00% to 9.98%) (Annual_Report_2026,
p.102). No new-customer addition, no geographic or industry
diversification metric, and no top-5/top-10 aggregate is disclosed. The
category as defined — concentration *declining* — scores 0 because the
documented direction is the reverse.

**The C1/C2 tension, resolved (constraint 3).** The same fact — top-customer
share rising from 21.00% to 24.05% — supports two readings and the AR
gives no basis to fully separate them from filed evidence alone. Reading
A (moat): the client is buying more from TAAL Tech inside a 10-year-plus
relationship, i.e., wallet-share capture, consistent with C1. Reading B
(fragility): revenue is concentrating into fewer hands in a
relationship-dependent T&M business with zero disclosed contract minimums
or termination-notice terms (B02, B03). The observation that would
separate the two readings — whether the growth is broad (new scope lines,
new engagement types within the account) or narrow (one program ramping,
capturable in a single renewal cycle) — is not disclosed anywhere in the
AR, the results filings, or any management-authored document (B05
FLAG-SILENCE: the acceleration itself goes unaddressed). Until that
distinguishing fact surfaces, this scan treats the rising share as
Moderate evidence for C1 and simultaneously as the top risk to C1 (see
Section 6B); it is not double-counted as a separate moat.

## THE SCAN'S HARDEST QUESTION: DOES ANY CATEGORY EXPLAIN THE MARGIN?

TAAL Tech's genuine EBIT margin (revenue less total expenses, Other Income
excluded) is 27.8% consolidated (B02-notes finding #2), roughly double
Onward Technologies (~13% at Rs544cr revenue) and Cyient (~12% at
Rs7,268cr revenue). Working the 22 categories against this question
directly, category by category:

- **A-family (product/technology):** no patent, no process-innovation
  disclosure, no platform. None of these can explain a pricing or
  cost-structure edge because none is evidenced.
- **B-family (supply chain):** not applicable to a labour-hour business;
  no explanatory power.
- **C1 (embedded relationships):** the only *plausible* moat-side
  explanation the corpus supports. A book that is 70%+ ten-year-plus
  tenure could, in principle, mean less competitive re-bidding pressure
  and better realised billing rates than a peer selling into shorter,
  more contested engagements. But the AR discloses no bill rate, no
  utilisation rate, and no per-account margin (confirmed absent by
  B04-bizmodel). **Confirming evidence would be realised bill-rate or
  per-account margin data. The corpus does not carry it.** This reading
  stays a 🔍 inference resting on a 📄 tenure fact, not a documented
  mechanism.
- **F1/F2 (talent/execution):** median remuneration of Rs9.40 lakh p.a.
  (Annual_Report_2026, p.50) argues *against* a premium-talent-commands-
  premium-billing story — the workforce is paid modestly, not richly.
  If a labour-cost explanation exists, filed evidence points toward cost
  efficiency, not talent premium, but no bill-rate-versus-cost bridge is
  disclosed to confirm even that.
- **G1 (war chest):** a balance-sheet fact; explicitly excluded from moat
  scoring by this scan's own rules and has no bearing on the *operating*
  margin question in any case.
- **G2 (WC trajectory):** deteriorating, and a balance-sheet/cash-timing
  matter, not a P&L margin driver — irrelevant to the EBIT-margin
  question, though relevant to earnings quality generally (B01/B02
  FLAG-CASH).
- **H3 (ESG):** no linkage to margin anywhere in the corpus.
- **I1/I2 (structural asymmetries):** both score 0; neither offers a
  mechanism.

**The one candidate explanation the filed evidence does support, and it
is not a moat:** a lean-overhead cost structure. Standalone "Other
expenses" (the SG&A-type line) were Rs1,923.77 lakh against Rs19,010.10
lakh revenue from operations — 10.1% of revenue (Annual_Report_2026,
p.66, p.91). R&D spend was 0.19% of revenue (Annual_Report_2026, p.49).
The company runs no earnings calls, no investor-relations deck, and (per
the AR) a single registered office with a shrinking fixed-asset and
leased-space base (Section 2A). A small, founder-led shop with no
investor-relations apparatus, no material R&D burn, and no physical
capacity to maintain could plausibly carry materially lower overhead than
a Rs544cr or Rs7,268cr listed peer running sales, IR, and multi-site
delivery organisations at scale. This is a 🔍 inference built on 📄
filed cost-line facts, and it explains overhead, not the full spread — it
says nothing about why TAAL Tech's *gross* delivery economics (revenue
less direct delivery cost: employee benefits + cost of technical
subcontract, 60.8% of revenue combined, Annual_Report_2026, p.66, p.90)
would themselves run richer than peers'.

A second candidate — that TAAL Tech's smaller revenue base carries a
richer bill-rate or contract mix than the blended book of a much larger
peer running staffing-augmentation programmes at scale — is a plausible
**revenue-comparability hypothesis**. **Confirming evidence would be
peer segment/contract-mix disclosure (Cyient, Onward Technologies annual
reports or investor decks). This container did not read peer documents
this run; the corpus carries none.** This is flagged to Stage 6's
peer-question handoff (B05-concall, peer_questions), not resolved here.

**Conclusion on the hardest question.** No category in this 22-item scan
supplies a documented, moat-based mechanism for the margin spread. The
one partially-evidenced explanation (lean overhead) is real but is not a
competitive advantage — any similarly small, IR-light engineering shop
could replicate it, and it is exactly the kind of advantage that
compresses as a company scales its own sales and governance apparatus
(which this company's own growth, and its own governance gaps flagged in
B02/B03, may eventually force). **An unexplained margin is not a moat.**
This scan cannot promote the margin premium to evidence of competitive
advantage; it can only name what would test it (bill-rate, utilisation,
per-account margin, peer contract mix) and confirm the corpus does not
carry that evidence.
