# AVANA Electrosystems in ten lines

Recently listed power grid equipment micro cap
Avana Electrosystems is a Bangalore maker of customised control and relay panels rated 11kV to 220kV, protection relays, and substation automation systems reaching 400kV, sold to power transmission and distribution utilities and to industrial and renewable generators. Installed capacity is about 70,000 relays and 600 panels a year. FY26 revenue was Rs 84 Cr, PAT was Rs 11.7 Cr, the balance sheet carried net cash of about Rs 28 Cr, and the shares trade near Rs 146 against a Rs 331 Cr market cap.

What changed is the listing itself. The company IPO'd on the NSE SME platform on 19 January 2026 at Rs 77.50 and sits roughly six months into public life. FY26 equity share capital jumped about 28.7 times, from Rs 0.8 Cr to Rs 22.7 Cr, reserves rose from Rs 21 Cr to Rs 36 Cr, and financing brought in Rs 19.8 Cr, all consistent with the raise. Where that money is going is not disclosed in any document available for this run, and a Rs 4.7 Cr capital work in progress line appeared for the first time in FY26 with no stated project, capacity, or commissioning date.

The revenue record shows a real inflection off a weak base. Sales moved from Rs 22.6 Cr in FY20 down to Rs 15 Cr in FY21 and Rs 20.4 Cr in FY22, then up hard to Rs 53 Cr, Rs 61.5 Cr, and Rs 84 Cr across FY24 to FY26. PAT tracked the same climb, Rs 4 Cr to Rs 8.3 Cr to Rs 11.7 Cr over the last three years. The seven year sales CAGR is about 24.5%. Gate 0 scored the company AVERAGE, because the sub-scale FY21 and FY22 years drag the through-cycle return and profitability blocks even as the recent two years look strong.

Cash conversion is the honest weak spot and the honest unknown at the same time. Operating cash flow swung from minus Rs 0.3 Cr in FY23 to Rs 7.95 Cr in FY26, a seven year high, which is a genuine improvement. Against that, working capital days stretched by about 45.9 days between FY20 and FY26, which is a genuine deterioration and not a data gap. The full window operating cash to profit ratio is only 0.62 times, weighed down by the earlier years. Free cash flow cannot be computed because the screener does not separate capex, and trade payables are not reported on their own, so the cleanest cash quality tests stay open.

Credibility cannot be graded the usual way because no annual report, results filing, or concall exists for the company. The promoter read is CAUTION, not clean and not alarming. There is no SEBI, criminal, or tax action against the company or its four promoters, no pledge found, and an unmodified FY26 audit. Set against that, the track record is only six months old, a KIADB commercial-production deadline was missed by about six years and stands extended to 22 May 2026 with the outcome unconfirmed, and the promoter group disclosure is incomplete on a father-in-law who qualifies under SEBI rules. The NSE also queried the price move from Rs 77.50 to Rs 146 in under six months, and the reply was standard boilerplate.

Peers point to a supportive sector but do not confirm this company. Danish Power and Aartech Solonics concalls describe a demand supercycle in grid modernisation, renewables integration, data-centre power, and national transmission and substation targets running through FY27. None of that is Avana-specific. No Avana document confirms its own order book participation beyond an RHP-sourced order book of Rs 52 Cr as of 30 November 2025 that could not be verified in local sources.

The market opportunity is real and roomy. The domestic addressable market for medium and high voltage control and relay panels, protection relays, and substation automation sits at roughly Rs 1,100 Cr to Rs 1,700 Cr, with a serviceable slice near Rs 800 Cr and about 9.5 times revenue headroom. Runway is GOOD. The tension is that the market-share path implies a 14% to 15% revenue CAGR, below the 24.5% the company has actually delivered, and no management growth claim exists to reconcile the two.

The binding constraint is evidence, not the business. Every subject-company number in this run traces to a single screener data sheet covering FY20 to FY26. There is no annual report, no results, no concall, no rating, no shareholding filing, no prospectus in hand, and no investor presentation. The very identity of the business was recovered through web search, and the manifest sector tag of Pharma or CDMO is a confirmed collection error to be ignored. Five analytical stages were skipped for want of any source to read.
