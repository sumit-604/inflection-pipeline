Run date: 2026-09-07. Twelve peer transcripts (SULA x4, TI x4, SDBL x4) and six peer investor
presentations (SULA/TI/SDBL, Q4FY26 and Q1FY27 each) read in full. Every citation below was
independently located in the .txt file and anchored to the nearest preceding
`===== PAGE N =====` marker at the time of writing; where a quote and a number come from
different pages, both are cited and labelled. This is a rerun; the first run's output was not
read, per instruction.

**Headline finding, stated up front.** Two of stage 5's six peer-verification questions resolve
cleanly to CONTRADICTED by arithmetic on numbers already in the corpus, not by any peer saying
"that is wrong" in words. Fratelli's own audited FY26 revenue (Rs181.29cr) against its own
AR-cited/IMARC-corroborated domestic wine market size (Rs1,917cr-2,320cr, per B09-tam.yaml)
implies a 7.5-9.5% market share. Adding Sula's own disclosed FY26 "Own Brands" revenue
(Rs511.1cr, SULA-Investor_Presentation_Q4FY26_2026-05-06.txt p.10) to Fratelli's, the two
companies together hold roughly 30-36% of the same market — not the "85% or 90%... duopoly"
Fratelli's own management asserts on its own Q1 FY26 call (Concall_Q1FY26_Aug2025_Transcript.txt
p.14, verified page; stage 5 had cited p.13). Separately, Sula's own FY26 WIPS subsidy accrual
(Rs48cr, SULA-Concall_May_2026_Transcript.txt p.10) exceeds Sula's own FY26 PBT (Rs35.4cr,
SULA-Investor_Presentation_Q4FY26_2026-05-06.txt p.10) by 36% — meaning the profitable market
leader's own reported profit is smaller than its subsidy, a fact that recontextualises stage 5's
Fratelli-specific WIPS red flag as a sector-wide structural exposure. Both findings are set out
in full below.

## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

### 2A. Demand environment

No peer describes a broad macro slowdown in discretionary consumer spending, the framing Fratelli's management repeatedly uses to explain guidance misses (stage 5, "external-blame-heavy" pattern). Instead, every disruption named by every peer this period is STATE-EXCISE-POLICY-SPECIFIC, not macro-demand-specific: TI's own estimate that "the industry from our understanding has degrown by around 20%, the IMFL industry" in Maharashtra following the new Maharashtra Made Liquor policy (TI-Concall_Nov_2025_Transcript.txt, p.6); SDBL's Karnataka excise-driven share loss ("we have roughly lost about 5%, 5.5% in half 1... vis-a-vis half 1 of last year," SDBL-Concall_Nov_2025_Transcript.txt, p.10) and a further, separate Madhya Pradesh "operational and regulatory disruption" a year later (SDBL-Concall_Aug_2026_Transcript.txt, p.4); Sula's own Telangana licence-renewal and Karnataka destocking disruptions running through the same window (SULA-Concall_Feb_2026_Transcript.txt, p.7-8, echoing Fratelli's own cited Telangana disruption). Read together, this corroborates the STATE-SPECIFIC-REGULATORY-CHURN half of Fratelli's excuse pattern as a genuine, sector-wide, recurring phenomenon across three unrelated companies and at least four different states over four to five quarters — but it does not corroborate, and nothing in the peer set supports, a general "macro discretionary spending" framing distinct from these specific policy events. Separately, Sula's own management states plainly that the underlying wine category itself has shown only "low single-digit growth" over the last two years (SULA-Concall_Aug_2026_Transcript.txt, p.8: "The last 2 years have not seen much growth in the overall wine industry, neither in domestic nor in imports as per IWSR... low single-digit growth in the overall industry"), which directly conflicts with Fratelli's own multi-year 15-20% industry growth guidance given to its own investors on the same-period Q1 FY26 call ("we... are expecting the industry to grow at about 15% to 20% for the next three to four years," Concall_Q1FY26_Aug2025_Transcript.txt, p.7). This is consistent with, and extends, B09's already-flagged FLAG-CATEGORY-GROWTH-CONTESTED.

### 2B. Pricing and input costs

A genuine, quantified, and unexplained divergence. Sula discloses a severe table-grape cost spike during Harvest 2026 (the same Jan-March 2026 harvest window, and the same state, Maharashtra, that Fratelli's own Akluj estate operates in): "Last year, grape prices shot up to INR 35 a kilo for table grapes" against a typical "INR 15-16 a kilo," "more than doubling of table grape prices compared to previous harvests" (SULA-Concall_Aug_2026_Transcript.txt, p.8), with a quantified ~350-400bps FY26 full-year gross-margin impact and a further ~150bps hit carried into Q1 FY27 (SULA-Investor_Presentation_Q1FY27_2026-08-06.txt, p.10-11). Fratelli's own commentary on the identical Harvest 2026 window is confined to yield and quality only, with no cost reference at all: "Harvest 2026 has been encouraging for us. Despite heavy rains during the season, the vineyard performance will be strong, and it has delivered good yields and more importantly, very good quality grapes" (Concall_Q3FY26_Feb2026_Transcript.txt, p.4). Fratelli's Q4 FY26 call three months later (after the harvest concluded) contains zero mentions of "grape" or "harvest" at all (exhaustive keyword search, Concall_Q4FY26_Jun2026_Transcript.txt). Fratelli's earlier, pre-harvest claim was that grape procurement carries "no major changes in the price... contracts are long-term with farmers... therefore... no major changes in the gross margin profile" (Concall_Q1FY26_Aug2025_Transcript.txt, p.7) — and its own recomputed gross margin did in fact hold up or improve through the year (78.9% Q4 FY26 vs 73% Q4 FY25, per stage 5). Either Fratelli's long-term farmer contracts genuinely insulated it from a spike a much larger same-state peer could not avoid, or its harvest-cost disclosure is materially incomplete; the corpus cannot resolve which, but the total silence during and after the exact harvest window a peer describes as carrying "a huge spike" is itself a finding, feeding 2E below.

### 2C. Capex cycle

Not a uniform picture; the sector splits by what the capital is FOR, not simply expand/hold/cut. TI (post-Imperial-Blue acquisition) and SDBL (Rs570cr Uttar Pradesh greenfield brewery, "Phase 1... expected to be completed by June," SDBL-Concall_Feb_2026_Transcript.txt, p.4, commissioned per SDBL-Concall_Aug_2026_Transcript.txt, p.3) are both in an active CORE-CAPACITY expansion phase — a genuine capacity race in beer/IMFL. Sula, the direct wine comparable, is doing the opposite in its CORE wine business: reducing regular wine capex from a historical "INR 50 crores to INR 60 crores" run-rate to "approximately INR 25 crores" in FY26 (SULA-Concall_May_2026_Transcript.txt, p.9), while simultaneously acquiring a new estate (Chandon) and expanding Wine Tourism resort capacity (SULA-Concall_May_2026_Transcript.txt, p.9-11). Fratelli sits closer to Sula's pattern than to TI/SDBL's: modest core-business capex (~Rs10cr FY26, stage 5) alongside a large, currently-deferred hospitality/wine-tourism capex programme (Rs70-80cr). Fratelli is not a lone expander against a cautious peer set, nor is it part of an industry-wide core-capacity race; it is one of two wine players (with the market leader) redirecting capital away from core wine production and toward tourism/hospitality, a pattern an analyst on Sula's own call framed bluntly as a sub-1x asset-turnover, structurally low-return core business ("so the return on equity is then 12% in the wine business even for our industry leader," SULA-Concall_Nov_2025_Transcript.txt, p.13; Sula's CFO did not dispute the framing, attributing it instead to harvest seasonality of tanks and barrels, same page).

### 2D. Competitive mentions

None. An exhaustive search of all twelve peer transcripts and all six peer presentations for "Fratelli" returns zero matches anywhere. This confirms and extends B09's carried-forward finding (Sula never names Fratelli across four quarters of calls) to the full twelve-transcript, six-presentation set: no peer, in any quarter, names Fratelli, describes it unmistakably, or refers to "the number two player" in any way that resolves to Fratelli specifically. This silence is itself informative given Fratelli's own repeated "duopoly," "market leader and us," and "vast lead over the next in line" framing (Investor_Presentation_Q4FY26_May2026.txt, p.23) — a company positioning itself as one half of a two-player market is not once acknowledged as a competitor by the other half, across four consecutive quarters of that other half's own analyst calls.

### 2E. Risks peers discuss that Fratelli does not

- **Grape/table-grape input cost inflation.** Sula quantifies a ~350-400bps FY26 gross-margin hit and further Q1 FY27 drag from a "more than doubling" of table-grape prices in the same harvest window Fratelli describes only in terms of yield and quality (see 2B). Fratelli's calls carry zero cost-side grape/harvest risk disclosure in any of the five available quarters.
- **WIPS receivable collection and scheme-continuity risk.** Sula tracks its own WIPS outstanding receivable balance and collection cadence every single quarter (Rs85-88cr outstanding across the corpus, see Question 2). Fratelli discloses no receivable balance, collection risk, or scheme-continuity risk for its own WIPS exposure at any point after the single Q1 FY26 mention.
- **Category-wide demand stagnation.** Sula's own management describes the underlying wine category as showing only "low single-digit growth" over the last two years (SULA-Concall_Aug_2026_Transcript.txt, p.8) — a risk to the multi-year 15-20% industry growth rate Fratelli guides its own investors to, never itself named as a risk on any Fratelli call.
- **Core-business asset-turnover / return-profile structure.** Sula's own analyst exchange frames the wine business itself as a sub-1x-asset-turnover, ~12% ROE business at the market leader (SULA-Concall_Nov_2025_Transcript.txt, p.13), a structural feature of wine manufacturing (single annual harvest, tank/barrel utilisation) that Fratelli's calls never address in capital-efficiency terms; consistent with B09's finding that winery utilisation % is NOT FOUND anywhere in the Fratelli corpus.
- **Recurring, multi-state regulatory volatility as an ongoing operating feature, not a one-off.** Three different peers, across four different states (Maharashtra/TI, Karnataka and Madhya Pradesh/SDBL) and multiple quarters each describe fresh regulatory or excise disruption; Fratelli frames each of its own disruptions (Maharashtra, Telangana, Uttarakhand) as a temporary, resolving headwind rather than a structural, recurring feature of operating in Indian state-regulated alcohol retail.

## PART 5: CROSS-PEER HYPOTHESIS

**The organised Maharashtra wine sector's reported profitability is structurally leveraged to a
single state subsidy scheme (WIPS) to a degree that exceeds what any single peer's own
disclosure reveals in isolation, and this is a sector risk, not a Fratelli-specific one.**

No single peer states this. Sula discloses its own WIPS accrual and receivable balance every
quarter as a matter of routine, unremarkable disclosure — it never frames the number against its
own PBT. Fratelli discloses its WIPS figure once and never returns to it. Only by combining the
two — Sula's granular quarterly WIPS numbers against Sula's own filed PBT (Rs48cr accrued FY26
WIPS vs. Rs35.4cr FY26 PBT, a subsidy that is 136% of the market leader's own bottom line) with
the fact that Fratelli's comparable but far smaller and far less transparently disclosed subsidy
exposure was flagged as a red flag in isolation (stage 5) — does the sector-level pattern become
visible: the entire organised, listed segment of the Indian wine industry, not just the
transitioning entrant, currently reports a bottom line smaller than its own state subsidy income.
This is testable and falsifiable going forward: if WIPS accrual growth outpaces PBT growth for
BOTH companies over the next two to three fiscal years, the dependency is deepening sector-wide;
if either company's PBT begins to exceed its WIPS accrual, the underlying wine business is
starting to carry its own weight. It also reframes the policy-continuity risk implicit in both
companies' filings (a Maharashtra government decision to sunset or cap WIPS) from a
company-specific risk into a sector-repricing event that would hit the profitable incumbent
harder in absolute rupee terms than it would hit Fratelli.

seven years (2018-2025), with the further jump to "90%+" coming only from the 2025 Imperial Blue
ACQUISITION, not from continued organic mix shift (same page). Two implications for Fratelli's
own claimed climb: first, TI's actual loss-to-profit timeline ran roughly a decade (2014 distress
to 2024 debt-free status), an order of magnitude longer than the roughly three-year FY24-FY27
window over which Fratelli claims its own transition; second, TI's turnaround was driven primarily
by BALANCE-SHEET engineering (debt restructuring, Rs310cr+ of equity raises) and, more recently,
M&A, not by sustained organic operating-margin improvement of the kind Fratelli's calls describe
quarter to quarter. TI's own explicit strategic pivot away from "long-gestation capex" and toward
an "asset-light model" (same presentation, p.18, "Turnaround and Expansion Strategy") stands in
direct contrast to Fratelli's currently-deferred but still-guided Rs70-80cr hospitality capex
programme. If TI is the right base rate for a loss-to-profit alco-bev transition in India, it
argues for a materially longer and more capital-structure-dependent climb than the one Fratelli's
management describes, not a shorter or purely operating-leverage-driven one.
