**Flagged structural question:** Per web search of MPS's own scheme filings (not disclosed by name in

evidence-grounded, unresolved governance question — recommend the operator obtain and review the
Scheme of Arrangement / valuation report directly.** [❓ web search only, corroborated by the AR's own

### 3B. Capital allocation behaviour

### 5C. Key-person risk and succession visibility

Rahul Arora is simultaneously **Chairman, CEO, and Managing Director**, chairs both the CSR
Committee and the Risk Management Committee, and sits as a member of the Audit Committee — a
significant concentration of committee-level influence in one executive. This remains
technically Companies Act/SEBI-compliant (the Audit Committee itself is majority-independent with an
independent Chairman) but is not best-practice separation of oversight from management. **No named
internal successor to Rahul Arora is currently disclosed.** The 2021 founder-to-son(-successor)
transition was itself well-managed over a disclosed five-year plan — a positive precedent — but there
is no evidence of an equivalent next-generation succession plan today. Combined with the
senior-management churn in 5A directly beneath this single concentrated leader, **key-person risk is
elevated** relative to a company with a more distributed leadership bench.
