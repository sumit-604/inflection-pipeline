### 2B. Excuse pattern analysis

- Only one genuine "miss" required an excuse in this window — the gross margin compression (item 5). Management's explanation blended three factors (customer pricing/renegotiation, minor AI cost, and a "double bubble" cost of running parallel infrastructure during a migration) — this is a **specific, itemized explanation**, not a vague external-blame deflection, and management proactively volunteered the "double bubble" detail even though only price/renegotiation was directly asked about.
- On the two silent misses (headcount KPI, Labour Code clarity), there is no excuse at all — these are **silent drops**, not defended misses. That is a distinct and arguably worse pattern than an over-blamed miss: nothing was said, so no accountability signal either way.
- Revenue-timing volatility (Q3 FY26 call: top-5 customer rev-rec front-loaded into H1 FY26 vs even spread in FY25) was explained proactively and in technical detail (IND AS output-method rev-rec tied to channel go-lives) before an analyst even pressed hard — this reads as a **proactive disclosure**, a positive credibility signal.
- No instance found across either call of management saying "we made a mistake" in as many words; negatives are consistently framed as "speed bumps," "timing," or "not a recurring signal" — language is optimistic-leaning even when conceding a real cost.
- No instance of blaming external parties (macro, competitors, regulation) for a miss; the two genuine misses are silent, and the one partial-miss explanation is internally attributed (own pricing/infra decisions), not externally blamed. This tempers the "self-serving" read somewhat.

**excuse_pattern classification: balanced** — leans toward proactive/internally-attributed explanations when management does explain, but two commitments were simply dropped without any acknowledgment, which is a distinct governance-attentiveness flag rather than a credibility-of-excuse flag.

### 2D. What they are NOT saying

- **Unit economics (LTV/CAC, gross margin payback)** — explicitly asked (Q3 FY26 call, Kawaljeet Saluja) and explicitly declined ("not a metric we officially disclose"). Likely reason: still building investor-facing disclosure discipline post-IPO, and/or the numbers may not yet be flattering enough to volunteer given a mission-critical enterprise-software business model with long sales cycles.
- **Competitor names** — despite repeated direct questions about competitive positioning (Q3 FY26 call, Omprakash Kavadi; Q4 FY26 call, Manish Adukia), management never names a single competitor, describing them only generically as "traditional hardware vendors" and "cloud-native, albeit small" rivals. Likely reason: avoiding giving competitors free press, and/or the claim of "no credible competition" is easier to sustain rhetorically if it is never tested against a named rival.
- **Market share figures** — repeated claims of "grabbing more and more market share" (Q3 FY26 call) with zero supporting percentage, source, or methodology in either call. This is exactly the kind of claim the peer-verification stage should stress-test.
- **NEWSPULSE / AI product economics** — gross margin impact of AI products is repeatedly deferred ("slightly early... we'll be able to share a lot more in the next couple of quarters," Q4 FY26 call) even though this is framed as the single largest strategic priority of the company (webinar, entire session). The size of the pilot pipeline is given only as "tens of POCs," not a number.
- **Marketplace segment metrics** — explicitly asked in Q4 FY26 call, explicitly declined ("we don't kind of separate that growth yet"), despite being flagged as a "big growth area" as far back as the Q3 FY26 call's initial segment description.
- **Headcount-by-function and Labour Code run-rate** — both promised and both silently dropped (see 2A).
- **Hedging policy specifics** — asked directly (Q4 FY26 call, Rohan Nagpal); answer is qualitative only ("we do occasionally transactionally hedge, but we don't have a hedging book... something we're thinking about this year, but nothing material to call out").

## SECTION 3: COMPETITIVE INTELLIGENCE FROM CONCALLS

### 3A. Competitor commentary and credibility check

- **Q3 FY26 call** (Baskar, to Omprakash Kavadi): competitors described in two buckets — "traditional hardware vendors" (public, 50+ years old, transitioning to cloud) and "cloud-first" competitors ("albeit small... from a sophistication and platform strategy standpoint Amagi is quite ahead"). No names given. Credibility check: directionally plausible (legacy broadcast-tech vendors like traditional playout/MCR hardware makers are a known category) but entirely unfalsifiable as stated — no market share numbers, no named comparators.
- **Q4 FY26 call** (Baskar, to Manish Adukia, on AI-specific competition): "we are not seeing anybody specifically coming to what's Amagi's staple today... there is no competition that we see today as we speak." Credibility check: this is a materially stronger claim than the Q3 framing (moved from "we're ahead" to "no competition exists") in the space of one quarter, on a topic (AI/agentic media tooling) that is objectively fast-moving and crowded with new entrants globally. The claim reads as promotional rather than evidenced — flagged for peer verification.
- No mention across either earnings call of any named rival (e.g., legacy broadcast/playout vendors, other cloud-native FAST/CTV infrastructure players). This absence is itself a data point (see 2D).

### 3B. Industry and market intelligence dropped in the calls

- Cloud TAM: $16.9bn, only ~10% of TV channels moved to cloud (Q3 FY26 call, Baskar).
- Cloud modernization SAR: $1.9bn, +11% YoY (Q4 FY26 call, Baskar).
- US streaming = 48% of TV viewing, +4pp YoY; FAST viewership in the US now "as high as Netflix" (Q4 FY26 call, Baskar).
- CTV ad spend: ~$42.5bn, growing ~10% (Q4 FY26 call, Baskar).
- Ad impressions delivered: 13bn in Q3 FY26 (+60% YoY, Q3 FY26 call, Baskar); full-year monetized impressions +62% YoY vs revenue in that segment +36% YoY (Q4 FY26 call, Vijay) — a volume/price divergence explicitly acknowledged as "modest pressure on CPMs... more than offset by volume."
- Customer count trajectory: 396 (FY24) → 463 (FY25) → 492 (FY26); Q4 FY26 alone: 27 gross adds, 30 churned (mostly sub-$1,000 accounts in year-end cleanup) (Q4 FY26 call, Vijay).
- Cost structure: direct costs ~30% of revenue USD-denominated, sales ~20% USD-denominated — a natural currency tailwind on ~$100 of revenue against ~$50 of USD cost (Q4 FY26 call, Vijay, to Rohan Nagpal).
- Cloud TCO savings pitch to customers: "anywhere 30% and above TCO savings" cited as the sales thesis for cloud modernization deals, explicitly caveated as not independently verified customer-side ("I'm sure they don't share the numbers with us") (Q4 FY26 call, Baskar, to Sanjay Ladha).
- Media industry AI thesis (webinar, management view only, not scored): "$2 to $4 of human toil for every dollar spent on technology" cited as the addressable opportunity for agentic products; Jevons' paradox argument that automation expands rather than shrinks the number of jobs/tasks addressed (webinar, Baskar).
