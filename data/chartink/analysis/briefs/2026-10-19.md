## Where the market stands

Think of the market as about 2,260 stocks, each with three trend lines under its price: the 20 day, 50 day and 200 day averages. A stock above a line trends up on that clock. On Monday 853 stocks sat above the long line, a little under four in ten. About 570 sat above each of the other two, one stock in four. The short count fell by about 230 stocks in one session.

The story of the autumn explains why the long count matters most. On 7 August about 1,370 stocks held the long line. That is the high of this whole record. The count then drifted down through August and early September. On 15 September the market had its worst day of the autumn. 350 stocks fell four and a half percent or more, and the long count dropped to about 1,040. A repair followed and peaked at 1,186 on 23 September. The next day the count lost about a hundred stocks. A second, smaller repair peaked at 980 on 6 October.

Then came 8 October. 313 stocks fell hard, and the long count fell to 838, the low of the autumn. A third repair began. The count rose to 880, then 906, then 922. It slipped on Thursday and reached 939 on Friday. That was the peak of the third repair. Monday took 86 stocks back in one session. The third repair had won about a hundred stocks in six sessions. One day erased nearly nine tenths of it.

So the market has a staircase of lower peaks: about 1,370, then 1,186, then 980, then 939. The low side has not yet stepped down. The 8 October low of 838 is still the floor, and Monday closed only 15 stocks above it. A close below it would put a lower low under the lower highs.

Inside the day, sellers controlled the close. About one stock in ten traded four percent above its prior close at some point. Only about one in four of those held the gain. About one stock in four traded four percent below its prior close. Close to half of those closed near their low.

The volume tells a calmer story. Turnover was about 514 crore, against about 481 crore a year ago and about 597 crore on Friday. The share of stocks trading on heavy volume fell to its lowest in four weeks. A panic day brings heavy volume across many stocks. Monday was a broad markdown on ordinary money.

Deep damage stayed rare. Only five stocks are down twenty percent or more over five days, and 16 are up that much. New yearly lows jumped from 58 on Friday to 105, about half the 205 of 8 October. New highs held at 43.

One more fact from the history matters. No day since August has had 250 or more stocks rise four and a half percent. A day like that is a thrust, a broad burst of buying that often starts a real advance. All three autumn repairs climbed on ordinary days, and each one faded.

Fear rose and is not yet high. The India VIX measures what traders pay to protect against big index swings. It rose about five percent to about 14.8, about a quarter higher than three months ago. Gold funds are down about five percent over the month and the Nifty about four.

## What would change this read

The read is a failed repair that sits just above its floor. The volume shows no panic, and a staircase of lower peaks sits overhead. It turns worse if the long count closes under about 838, the 8 October low. That would set a new autumn low and step the staircase down on the low side too. New yearly lows above about 200 would say the same thing. A shock day with about 300 hard fallers would be the third of the autumn. A VIX close above 15 while breadth stays weak would say the worry has reached the index. If heavy volume arrives on a down day, the fall has moved from a markdown to forced selling.

The cautious read fails if the floor holds and the buying comes back broader than before. The first test is the long count back above about 940, Friday's peak, within a few sessions. That would make Monday a shakeout and the floor a double bottom. The second test is a thrust day, with about 250 or more stocks up four and a half percent on heavy turnover. No day since August has had one. The third test is the long count clearing about 980 and holding there. That would break the staircase of lower peaks. A VIX close under 14, with new lows back under about 50, would support all three. State banks rising against the tape is the only sign of that kind of buying tonight. One group does not make the case.
