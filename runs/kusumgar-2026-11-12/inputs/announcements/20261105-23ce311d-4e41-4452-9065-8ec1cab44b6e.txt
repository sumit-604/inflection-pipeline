Kusumgar Limited – Investor Q&A, Q2 FY27 
This Q&A answers the probable questions from analysts and investors. It is filed with the 
stock exchanges together with our results for the quarter and half year ended 30 September 
2026, so that all shareholders receive the same information at the same time. All figures are 
consolidated. Statements about the future carry the risks set out in our safe harbour 
statement. 
Results 
1. How did the quarter go, and how does management see it? We are pleased with the first 
half. Revenue grew 81% year on year in Q2 and 91% in H1, as execution of our aerospace 
and defence solutions contracts continued at a steady pace. Margins were lower than in Q1, 
but remained well above last year’s levels. For the second half, our focus is on timely 
execution, progressing product qualifications with customers in India and overseas, and 
keeping working capital in check. 
Revenue from operations was ₹250.9 Cr, up 81% from ₹138.8 Cr in Q2 FY26 and against 
₹247.2 Cr in Q1 FY27. EBITDA was ₹71.3 Cr at a 28.4% margin, and PAT was ₹40.6 Cr, against 
₹10.9 Cr in Q2 FY26. For H1 FY27, revenue was ₹498.2 Cr and PAT ₹83.2 Cr, against ₹261.2 Cr 
and ₹17.6 Cr in H1 FY26. 
2. What drove the quarter?  Growth came mainly from execution of aerospace and defence 
solutions contracts, alongside steady demand across our other businesses. Revenue was 
broadly in line with Q1, which reflects a more even pace of execution than in the previous 
year. Margins moved due to change in product mix and higher raw material costs. 
3. How did margins move, and why? EBITDA margin, including other income, was 28.4%, 
compared with 31.1% in Q1 FY27 and 22.2% in Q2 FY26. Margins move mainly with product 
mix, operating leverage and raw material prices.  
4. Why does performance vary so much between quarters? A part of our business depends 
on military tenders, and the timing of these tenders is uncertain. We therefore encourage 
investors to look at annual trends. 
5. Can you share revenue by segment, or the domestic and international split, for the 
quarter?  For financial reporting we operate as a single segment, and we do not publish a 
quarterly breakdown by product line or geography. Our offerings are described in our 
investor presentation. 

[page 23]
6. Were there any one-off items for H1FY27? We made an expected credit loss (ECL) 
provision of ₹6.7 Cr, in line with our ECL policy. The provision relates to government 
receivables, which shall be reversed when the amount is collected. 
Business and positioning 
7. How would you describe Kusumgar today? We are a textile-forward materials and 
solutions company, focused on aerospace and defence. We own the underlying materials, 
chemistry, process know-how, which lets us control product performance. 
8. What makes the business hard to replicate? Four things compound over time: 
• Process knowledge built over more than 50 years 
• Qualification cycles that take years and create high switching costs once we are 
approved 
• Exclusive partnerships with global technology leaders 
• The patience to develop products that can take a decade to mature 
9. Who do you compete with?  By strategy, we try to be in product categories that call for 
customisation, technical depth and innovation. In such specialised systems there are few, if 
any, qualified players. In less specialised products, the field is wider, and we compete on 
quality, consistency, specifications and cost. 
Visibility 
10. What is the order book? What is your tender win rate, and which bids are you 
pursuing? We do not disclose an order book, a pipeline of bids, win rates or expected share 
of any tender. At any point our orders sit at different stages: formal purchase orders, letters 
of intent and customer projections. A single number would not be reliable, and most of our 
work is covered by customer confidentiality. 
 
Margins, costs and capital 
11. What margin range is sustainable, and what drove the recent improvement?   We do 
not guide on margins. EBITDA margin, excluding other income as in our prospectus, was 
29.6% in FY24, 25.6% in FY25, 30% in FY26 and 29.7% in H1 FY27. Margins move mainly with 
product mix, operating leverage and raw material prices. The recent improvement came 
from a richer product mix. 
12. How do raw material prices affect you?  Our main inputs are polyester yarn, nylon yarn 
and coating chemicals. We are usually able to pass on most price increases to customers, 
though with a lag. 
[page 24]
13. How indigenous are your products? What is your import content? Fabric development, 
manufacturing, fabrication, integration and testing are done in India, at our own facilities. 
Some specialised inputs are imported. Imported materials were 38.91% of materials 
consumed in FY26, against 27.92% in FY25. We continue to build domestic sources for 
critical inputs. Where domestic supply does not yet meet our customers' specifications, we 
source from qualified overseas suppliers. 
14. Are there any supply chain constraints on execution? None that affect current 
execution. We plan critical inputs against confirmed orders, work with qualified suppliers in 
more than one geography, and have our own risk-mitigation plans to manage supply 
challenges. 
15. How do you manage currency risk? Our treasury team manages currency exposure on 
international sales and imported inputs. We are usually net exporters, which gives us a 
natural hedge. 
16. What are your capex plans?  H1 FY27 capex was ₹44.1 Cr. Our current capacity supports 
the existing business, so capex here is mainly for maintenance and debottlenecking. We 
continue to evaluate investments in new technologies and capabilities, and will disclose any 
significant capex subsequently as and when relevant. 
17. What are normal working capital levels? Are government receivables a risk?  Over a full 
year, our working capital cycle is typically around 100–120 days, but it can move from 
quarter to quarter. A large part of our business is with defence and government customers, 
where payment follows the customer’s inspection and acceptance process, so the timing of 
large deliveries affects quarter-end balances.  
Customers, markets and strategy 
18. How do you invest in technology and R&D? Technology development runs through the 
whole company, not only a separate R&D unit. We have a dedicated technology team, and 
in-house labs for rapid prototyping, application simulation and testing. Much of our 
development is done jointly with customers and global technology partners against specific 
operational requirements. We commercialise new products every year across our solution 
families. 
Shareholders and disclosure 
19. What are the key risks to the business? 
• Timing of product approvals and tenders, which drives quarter-to-quarter variation 
• Trade policy, including tariffs in export markets 
• Our ability to attract and keep specialised technical talent 
The full risk factors are set out in our prospectus. 
[page 25]
20. What is the promoter holding? Are any shares pledged, and when do lock-ins end? At 
listing, the promoters held about 74.7%, and none of their shares were pledged. Under SEBI 
rules, 20% of the post-IPO capital held by promoters is locked in for 18 months from 
allotment, and the balance for six months.  
21. Why publish a written Q&A instead of hosting an earnings call? A written Q&A gives 
every shareholder the same information at the same time, in a form that is easy to refer 
back to. As a supplier to defence customers, we also need to be careful with programme 
details. We welcome meetings with institutional investors. Questions for future editions can 
be sent to pooja.jain@kusumgar.com.
