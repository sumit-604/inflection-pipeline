### 2B. Criminal cases
- **Live prosecution — Drugs & Cosmetics Act, 1940 ("Vicks Gel" case)**: Stemming from a
  2016 inspection at a Mumbai retail point that found expired stock of Vicks Multi Pain
  Relief Gel (manufactured by Akums, distributed by P&G). Charges under Sections 18B,
  18(a)(vi), 22(1)(cca), punishable under Sections 27(d)/28A of the Drugs and Cosmetics
  Act. The Supreme Court dropped charges against an individual P&G director but did **not**
  grant relief to Akums Drugs — the trial continues against the company and P&G Ltd. ✅
  court record (casemine.com judgment) + 📰 (Medical Dialogues, accessed 2026-07-10). This
  is a statutory manufacturing/quality-compliance prosecution against the corporate
  entity, not an allegation of personal promoter fraud, but it is a live criminal matter
  under the company's name and belongs in the record.
- **Chennai Metropolitan Magistrate Court, CC 2673/2020** — "The Drugs Inspector vs. Akums
  Drugs and Pharmaceuticals Ltd (Plant II)," hearing referenced for 28 Feb 2025. Nature of
  the specific allegation NOT FOUND beyond the case listing. ✅ court-record existence
  (mercurylawyer.com case index), ❓ on substance since the underlying complaint text was
  not retrieved.
- No PMLA/ED action, FEMA violation, arrest, or economic-offence charge against the
  promoters personally was found.

### 2C. Tax and revenue — most significant adverse finding of this scan
- **Income Tax search-and-seizure (January 2025)**: IT Department conducted search
  operations ("raid") at Akums' offices and manufacturing units and (per one trade-press
  headline) at "top execs' residences." Stock fell ~6-7% on the day of disclosure, closing
  around ₹581.75 on BSE. Company stated it "fully cooperated" and responded to
  clarifications sought. ✅/📰 (Business Standard, Trade Brains, DrugsControl Media
  Services, Healthcare Radius, accessed 2026-07-10).
- **Block-period assessment orders (disclosed ~May 2026)**: Following the search, Akums
  and subsidiary Akumentis Healthcare Ltd received assessment orders under Section 158BC
  for the block period 1 April 2018 to 12 March 2025 (i.e., a seven-year span). Total
  demand: **₹133.75 crore**, split ₹60.09 crore (Akums standalone) and ₹73.66 crore
  (Akumentis Healthcare), arising from disallowance of expenditure under Section 37(1) of
  the Income Tax Act. Four further wholly-owned subsidiaries — Maxcure Nutravedics, Pure
  and Cure Healthcare, Malik Lifesciences, and Nicholas Healthcare — also received
  assessment orders for the same block period on similar disallowance grounds; specific
  rupee amounts for these four were NOT FOUND. Company states the demands are "not
  sustainable" and intends to appeal; this is the company's own characterization, not an
  independently confirmed outcome. (Sources: scanx.trade, InvestyWise, Medical Dialogues,
  Whalesbook, accessed 2026-07-10) 📰
- A separate, smaller matter: a ₹3.59 crore penalty on subsidiary Maxcure Nutravedics
  (GST/invoicing-related, per the First Appellate Authority filing) was **set aside on
  appeal** in July 2026 — a favorable resolution, reducing the severity of that specific
  item. (Source: scanx.trade, accessed 2026-07-10)
- **Assessment of severity**: a coordinated search-and-seizure operation followed roughly
  16 months later by a group-wide, seven-year block-period demand exceeding ₹133 crore
  across six group entities is a materially significant finding for the "most critical"
  legal section of this framework — distinct in kind from a routine assessment dispute.
  No SFIO referral, PMLA angle, or personal charge against the promoters was found
  attached to this matter; it reads as a corporate tax-expenditure dispute rather than a
  fraud allegation, but the scale and the search-and-seizure origin keep it in the "red"
  tier below.
