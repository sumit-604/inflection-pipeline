## Where the market stands

Think of the market as about 2,250 stocks. Each one has three trend lines: a short one over 20 days, a middle one over 50 days and a long one over 200 days. A stock above a line is in an uptrend on that clock. On Friday 579 stocks sat above the long line, about one in four. Only 253 sat above the middle line, about one in nine. Only 178 sat above the short line, about one in thirteen. The short count has not been this low since 23 March, the worst week of the spring bottom.

The year's story shows why these counts matter. The market made its low in late March. On 23 March only 288 stocks held the long line, and 952 stocks fell hard that day. A week later, on 30 March, new yearly lows passed 900. Then broad buying arrived. On 1 April more than 1,100 stocks rose four and a half percent in one session. On 8 April 878 did. Traders call these thrust days. A thrust day is a session when buying is so wide that almost every part of the market rises at once. After those two days the long count climbed through spring and summer. It peaked at 1,367 on 7 August, the highest in this record.

Autumn has been a staircase down. The count fell through August into a shock on 15 September. A repair took it back to about 1,180 within a week. A second shock came on 8 October and took the count to 837. A third repair reached 937 on 16 October. Then the count fell every session of last week: 852 on Monday, 720 on Wednesday, 673 on Thursday and 579 on Friday. The market lost more than 350 long trend holders in five sessions.
The shape of the week matters as much as the level. Wednesday brought 383 hard fallers. Thursday was calmer, with 127, and it looked like a pause. Friday brought 360 again. A shock, then a quiet day, then a second shock is how a slide looks. A washout looks different: one huge day of selling, then buyers arrive. The last time two days this heavy came so close together was the last week of March. Back then a thrust came within days. No thrust day has come since 12 June.

Inside Friday's session, sellers owned the day. About two stocks in three traded at least four percent below their prior close at some point. About four in ten of those finished near their low. About one stock in eleven traded four percent above its prior close. Only one in four of those kept the gain by the close. That is better than Wednesday, when one in seven kept it, and it is still poor. Almost everything moved, and most of it moved down.

Volume confirms the damage. Turnover reached about 611 crore, against about 431 crore on the same day a year ago. That is the heaviest day since 7 September. The share of stocks on heavy volume, against those on light volume, tripled from Thursday. It now sits near the top of its own history. The market has now had three days with 300 or more hard fallers since 8 October. Heavy volume on a down day means holders chose to sell at any price.

The damage has spread to the far end. More than one stock in four is down ten percent or more over five days. That is the highest share in this whole history. 26 stocks are down twenty percent or more in five days, against seven up that much. About one stock in fourteen now trades more than ten percent below its 10 day average. That stretch is also a record for this data.
New highs have almost gone. Only eight stocks made a yearly high on Friday, the same as Thursday. No day since 24 March had fewer. New yearly lows rose to 453, the most since 30 March. About four stocks in ten now sit within fifteen percent of a yearly low. Only about one in fourteen sits that close to a yearly high.

Fear keeps rising at the index level. The India VIX measures what traders pay to protect against big swings in the Nifty. It rose about seven percent on Friday to about 16.9. It is up about a fifth on the week and more than forty percent over three months. Gold gave no shelter either. Gold funds fell about eight percent over the month, while the Nifty fell about four. The usual safe asset fell harder than the stocks it should protect against.

## What would change this read

The read is a confirmed slide in breadth, on heavy volume, hidden by a steady Nifty. It turns worse if the long count falls under about 450. The count last sat that low on 7 April, a week after the spring bottom. New yearly lows above about 700 would say the same. Only three days in this record passed that mark, and two of them came in late March. Another day of 300 or more hard fallers within the next week would turn a slide into a rout. A VIX close above about 17, with the Nifty falling as hard as the small names, would say the large company shelter has failed. If IT turns down while breadth stays this weak, the index loses its last support.

The cautious read fails if buying returns broad and fast. The first test is a thrust day, with about 250 or more stocks up four and a half percent on heavy turnover. The last one came on 12 June. The second test is the long count back above about 840, the old autumn floor, within a few sessions. The third test is new lows back under about 100 while new highs climb past about 50. A VIX close back under about 15 would support all three. The record stretch below short averages makes a snap back likely soon. A snap back that fails every one of these tests is a rally inside a downtrend. On Friday only the auto parts makers, the jewellers, a few small steel makers and the large drug makers rose against the tide, and four small groups do not make the case.
