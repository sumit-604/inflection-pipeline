# Stage 6: Peer Concall Verification — Karnika Industries Ltd (KARNIKA)
Run date: 2026-07-11 | Model: claude-sonnet-5 | Stage: B06-peers

## Peer set and fiscal calendar note
Three apparel-sector peers were provided, all on an April-March fiscal year matching Karnika's own:
- **CANTABIL** (Cantabil Retail India) — men's/family apparel, largely high-street domestic retail, ~60% own manufacturing / 40% job-work, no kidswear focus.
- **MONTECARLO** (Monte Carlo Fashions) — woollens/apparel, winter-wear led, 100% outsourced manufacturing (explicitly rejected backward integration), small kids/home-textile lines.
- **SPAL** (S.P. Apparels) — integrated yarn-to-garment export manufacturer (spinning, dyeing, garmenting) with a babywear/kidswear niche (Young Brand Apparel, SPUK), predominantly B2B/export to US/UK/EU, plus small India retail brands (Crocodile, Angel & Rocket).

None of the three is a domestic D2C/omnichannel kidswear retailer like Karnika+Kidcity. Where a peer's evidence only partially maps to a Karnika-specific claim, this is stated plainly below rather than stretched.

Twelve transcripts were provided (4 per peer, Q1-Q4 FY26) — this matches the expected "up to 12" and all 12 were read in full.

## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

**2A Demand environment.** Consensus among the two domestic-facing peers (Cantabil, Monte Carlo) is that FY26 demand strengthened through the year, materially aided by the GST rationalization effective September 22, 2025 (both cite it explicitly as a durable tailwind, not a one-quarter blip). SPAL's demand picture is bifurcated: EU/UK demand strong and improving (new customer wins, UK FTA anticipation), US demand disrupted specifically by tariffs (not organic weakness) with a "temporary pause" that eased once the India-US agreement was signed (SPAL, Q3 FY26 call, Feb 13, 2026, p.2-3). No peer describes a domestic demand downturn matching what could be an implicit Karnika excuse for H2 FY26 softness.

**2B Pricing and input costs.** All three peers converge on the same finding: cotton/yarn/wool costs rose meaningfully during FY26, becoming clearly visible by Q4 FY26 (Jan-Mar 2026), and all three passed the increase through via price hikes (Monte Carlo 7-8%, Cantabil unspecified but explicit, SPAL cotton ~INR70,000-75,000/candy plus yarn +5%) without material EBITDA-margin damage. This matches Karnika's stated cost narrative directionally but not in magnitude of margin impact (see Claim 6).

**2C Capex cycle.** All three peers are actively expanding, not holding or cutting: Cantabil opened 91 gross stores in FY26 and capitalized a new ~Rs50-55cr corporate office/warehouse; Monte Carlo continues its 40-45 EBO/year target plus a ~Rs50cr solar power capex program; SPAL is mid-expansion toward 2,000 Sri Lanka machines and resuming its paused Salem (Young Brand) project. This is an **industry-wide capacity race**, not a lone-expander situation — useful context for evaluating Karnika's West Bengal manufacturing/property integration trigger (B05 priority 6): the peer set suggests capex ambition itself is not unusual for the sector, though execution discipline (see Claim 6) varies.

**2D Competitive mentions.** No peer names Karnika, Kidcity, or gives an unmistakable description of either. Monte Carlo's characterization of Zara and Shein as long-standing fast-fashion **competitors** (not customers) is the only tangentially relevant competitive color found (Claim 5).

**2E Risks peers discuss that Karnika does not (per B05).** (i) US tariff exposure quantified precisely and managed via explicit customer/supplier discount-sharing arrangements (SPAL: "one fourth" of tariff burden shared across the value chain, Aug 18, 2025 call, p.6-7) — Karnika's own stalled US order (B05 flag) lacks this kind of quantified tariff/cost-sharing detail. (ii) Minimum-wage/Labour Code compliance cost increases (SPAL Tamil Nadu wage hike, Aug 18, 2025 call, p.7; all three peers discuss new Labour Code compliance) — not mentioned in Karnika's B05 disclosures. (iii) Specific shipping-lane disruption (Strait of Hormuz, SPAL) — Karnika cites "Gulf war" generically without mechanism. (iv) Bangladesh losing EU LDC duty-free status by 2029, a trade-diversion tailwind SPAL is actively positioning for (May 22, 2026 call, p.10) — not discussed by Karnika, though relevant to any China/Bangladesh diversification thesis. (v) Granular return/EOSS provisioning and channel-inventory discipline (Monte Carlo discloses discount % and return % by quarter; Cantabil discloses inventory-day targets) — a level of receivables/inventory transparency Karnika has not matched (consistent with the B05-flagged receivables/AR inconsistency).

## PART 4: TRIANGULATION SUMMARY

- **Claims verified:** 1 of 7 (Claim 2 — cotton/yarn cost shock genuine and sector-wide; partial on the Gulf-war/freight-specific language)
- **Claims partially verified:** 1 of 7 (Claim 3 — "no true integrated peer" holds against two of three peers but is complicated by SPAL)
- **Claims contradicted:** 2 of 7 (Claim 6 — sector EBITDA margins expanded/held while Karnika's compressed sharply; Claim 7 — H2 FY26 demand/discounting was resilient/improving across peers, not weak) — **priority items for synthesis**
- **Claims unverifiable:** 3 of 7 (Claim 1 — kids-apparel TAM/CAGR; Claim 4 — Kidcity footprint; Claim 5 — Zara/H&M/DMart negotiations)

**Single most consequential contradiction:** Claim 6. All three peers held or expanded FY26 EBITDA margins despite absorbing the same genuine cotton/yarn cost inflation and, for SPAL, considerably more severe geopolitical/tariff disruption. Karnika's implied H2 FY26 margin collapse (H1 19.67% to an implied H2 ~11.5%) is far sharper than anything peers experienced, pointing toward company-specific (likely Kidcity-integration-related) execution failure rather than an unavoidable sector-wide margin squeeze.

**Strongest independent confirmation:** Claim 2. SPAL's Q4 FY26 call independently names both a cotton/yarn price increase (with specific rupee figures) and a Gulf-region logistics disruption (Strait of Hormuz) in the same Jan-Mar 2026 quarter Karnika cites, corroborated on the cost side by Monte Carlo and Cantabil in the same quarter. Karnika did not fabricate this macro shock.

**Overall assessment:** The peer set **complicates and partially undercuts** Karnika's narrative rather than cleanly supporting or destroying it. It validates the underlying macro shock (cotton/yarn inflation, Gulf-region freight disruption) as real and contemporaneous across the sector, which gives Karnika's late acknowledgment of the shock some factual grounding — even though the flip from explicit denial (Q2 FY26) to blame (H2/FY26) remains a credibility concern independent of whether the shock itself was real. But the peer set directly undercuts any inference that the shock explains the *severity* of Karnika's margin miss: peers facing the same or worse cost and logistics headwinds preserved or grew margins over the identical period, isolating Karnika's compression as more company-specific than industry-driven. The peer set cannot verify or deny the Kidcity-specific, kids-market-size, or named-retailer claims (Zara/H&M/DMart, HopScotch, Spencer's) because none of the three available peers operate in Karnika's exact domestic kidswear/D2C niche — those claims remain open items requiring different evidence sources.
