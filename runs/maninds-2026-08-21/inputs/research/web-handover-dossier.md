**The analogy (tailor's shop, amended).** A workshop that cuts, coats and certifies steel pipe to a customer's exact specification. It does not set the price of its cloth (steel plate); it buys plate, adds labour and coating, and sells against a signed order. Income depends on how many orders sit in the book, how fast plate turns into pipe, and how quickly it is paid after shipment. The workshop has taken much longer to get paid and its book has thinned, even as it has bought a second workshop overseas with a better customer list and is betting that one fills the gap. An independent credit assessor (not the banker) has looked at the ledger and says the slow payment is tied to two specific shipments now clearing, not a permanent change; the workshop's own ledger has not yet shown the reversal. And the workshop's owners are in front of a tribunal over how they kept the books for six years.

**What the model REJECTS as noise:** headline gross margin (swings with plate); single-quarter YoY revenue (shipment timing); trailing P/E on blended profit (FY25 includes Merino one-off); dividend pattern (build phase); unadjusted ROE/ROA (unrelated quoted-equity treasury); consolidated revenue growth including the ~Rs369cr Merino one-off (FY26 was roughly flat ex-Merino [B12b]).

**Falsifiers of the model itself:** (a) Merino becomes recurring, non-trivial revenue -> add a real second archetype; (b) NPC/Aramco converts to long-term fixed offtake -> NPC line moves toward licence/scarcity archetype; (c) all governance items clear (SAT vacates, Limitless repaid, 206(5) compounded, SC dismissed) -> governance exits the dominant set and the model is re-declared at lower fragility. **Fragility verdict: FRAGILE** (five variables, one company-narrated only, one outside company control).
