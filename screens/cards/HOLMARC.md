## Business Understanding Narrative

**What does it do, and how does it make money?** Holmarc makes scientific and
engineering instruments in Kochi. Its lines cover spectroscopy, microscopy,
optics, motion stages, optical tables and teaching lab kits (AR25 p3). It sells
to research labs, universities, industry and government buyers. In FY25 it
entered 233 government tenders and won 142 (AR25 p24). It sells through 63
domestic dealers and exports to 60 countries (AR25 p18, p24). The money is a
product margin on low-volume, high-mix instruments. Employees cost 46.6% of
revenue in FY25 (AR25 p89).

**What is changing?** The mix is moving up from teaching kits to analytical
instruments. Spectroscopy sales rose 35.52% and analytical instruments 19.87% in
FY25 (AR25 p18). The private-sector share of turnover rose to 42.56% (AR25 p18).
The company also bought a second industrial unit in 2026 (Unit p1).

**Why now?** The company raised IPO money, bought machines and hired ahead of
demand. Staff rose from 290 at IPO to 355 (AR25 p8). The new Edayar unit got its
land-transfer sanction on 10-Sep-2026 (DIC p1). Existing capacity runs at 90%
(Unit p1).

**What must be true?** Productivity must rise faster than headcount. The
chairman sets the bar: "at least 50% production and sales growth by productivity
increase alone" with current staff (AR25 p8). Import substitution must win
private-sector labs, not only tenders.

**What breaks it?** Demand that does not arrive. The chairman wrote that
extra rent and staff were spent "expecting an immediate disproportionate market
growth which unfortunately did not happen" (AR25 p7). He warned of "a sluggish
financial performance in the current financial year", that is FY26 (AR25 p7).
The FY26 numbers that would confirm or refute that are unreadable.

## 2. Business model and archetype

FY25 product sales were Rs 35.97 crore across 13 lines (AR25 p22). The largest,
Rs lakh: optical breadboards and tables 668.88, lab equipment 605.07,
analytical instruments 582.07, physics lab equipment 424.80, opto-mechanics
312.38, motorised stages 263.90, spectroscopy 220.30 (AR25 p22). Domestic goods
sales were Rs 29.87 crore and exports Rs 6.10 crore (AR25 p97).

The margin comes from in-house engineering. The company built 14 capital
equipment units itself in FY25 (AR25 p24, p25). It sells a DRS
spectrophotometer "at half the price of what is being offered by foreign
competitors" (AR25 p8).

**Archetype: no clean fit.** Nearest is build-to-spec component maker for the
opto-mechanics lines. The instrument lines sell like a niche franchise into
institutional budgets. Record "fits no archetype cleanly" as the finding.

## 3. Competitive advantages

Forming. Cost advantage is real: domestic manufacture at half the imported
price (AR25 p8), and self-built production equipment (AR25 p24). Breadth is
real: 646 customers, up from 575 (AR25 p21). Pricing power is absent. The MD&A
names "low-cost imported products" as the threat to "pricing strategies" (AR25
p17). Tender wins at 61% (AR25 p24) show competitiveness on price, not moat.

## 6. The transition and the quality ladder

FROM R2, a cost-advantaged instrument maker winning on price. TO R3, a
value-added supplier of analytical and clinical instruments with spec-in at
private labs. Mechanism: move from teaching kits and tables to spectroscopy,
Raman, PVD optical coating and clinical microscopy (AR25 p7, p8, p22).

Management's claim, quoted once: "Holmarc is committed to becoming a noticeable
manufacturing company in the world scenario in opto-mechatronics instruments
and systems" (AR25 p7).

Two readings. **Climb:** analytical and spectroscopy lines keep compounding and
lift margin. **Stall:** the company added cost for growth that came at 11%.
The observation that separates them: FY26 EBITD margin against FY25's 14.0%.
That number exists and is unreadable here.
