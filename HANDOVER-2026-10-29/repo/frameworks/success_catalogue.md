# SUCCESS CATALOGUE

*Companion to the failure catalogue (Kernex, Tipco, Rappid Valves, Ind Swift).
Created 08-Sep-2026 under Master Project Prompt v3.7, Rule I. Maintained by the
operator, Keerti Kaushik. No agent adds a name.*

## What this is

Names from the operator's own history that looked ugly on trailing numbers and
compounded anyway. The failure catalogue teaches what a value trap looks like.
Alone, it teaches only fear: every transition setup pattern-matches to a failure
name, because a transition setup and a value trap look identical on trailing
numbers. That is the point of the strategy. The success catalogue supplies the
other half of the comparison.

## The rule it serves (Rule I)

Every pattern-match to a failure name must be answered with the nearest success
name and the observable difference between them. Role 3 Section 3 (Value Trap
Test) states three lines:

- Nearest success-catalogue analogue: ___
- What separated it from the failure names at the same stage: ___
- Which of those separators does this company show today: ___

**Enforcement threshold: FOUR names.** Below four, Role 3 states "success
catalogue pending (___ of 4 names)" in the value-trap section and continues. A
catalogue of one or two names is a coincidence, not a comparison set.

**Current count: 0 of 4. Rule I is NOT yet enforced.**

## Who fills it

The operator, from her own history. Not from screeners, not from published
multibagger lists, not by an agent. The value of the catalogue is that she
watched these names at the ugly stage and knows what she saw. A name she read
about later teaches nothing about what was observable at the time.

## Filling notes

- **What it looked like at entry.** The optic that would have put a reader off.
  Low margin, negative cash flow, a promoter nobody covered, a segment in
  decline, a rerating that had not started.
- **What the trailing numbers said.** The specific figures. ROCE, CFO/PAT,
  debtor days, growth. This column is what a screener would have shown.
- **What was actually happening.** The mechanism the trailing numbers could not
  see. Capacity commissioned and not yet filled. A mix shift underway. A
  customer won but not yet shipping. Working capital absorbed by growth, not by
  decay. This is the ARTIFACT-OF-CLIMB reading of an ugly optic (CLAUDE.md
  Transition Decision Matrix, Part B5).
- **What confirmed it, and when.** The observable event that settled it, and how
  long it took from the ugly stage. This column is what makes the catalogue
  usable: it says what to watch for and how long to wait.
- **Outcome.** Plainly. Include the ones that worked slowly and the ones where
  the operator sold early. A catalogue of clean wins teaches as little as a
  catalogue of pure failures.

## What this catalogue is not

It is not a pattern to match forward. A success name does not license a BUY, and
"looks like [success name]" is not evidence. Rule I asks for the OBSERVABLE
DIFFERENCE between the failure names and the success name at the same stage, and
then asks which of those differences the company in front of us shows TODAY.
The answer can be "none of them", and that is the useful answer.
