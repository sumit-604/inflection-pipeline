## SPEAR LOAD-BEARING FACTS — CHECKED FIRST

**(1) Q1 FY27 run rate vs Indonesia launch batch.** Q1 FY27 total revenue Rs 141.8 cr (+85% YoY, +25% QoQ over Q4 FY26's Rs 113.8 cr) (Q1 FY27 call, Sudhir Mirjankar). Transfer Case revenue Rs 76 cr (+93% YoY), 53% of total revenue (Q1 FY27 call, Sudhir Mirjankar). Management states "some 30%" of the 70,000-unit Indonesia contract has been executed to date (Q1 FY27 call, Jitendra Divgi, in response to Sumit Ambekar). Jitendra states "more than 50% of the [Indonesia] business has the potential to become recurring in nature" (Q1 FY27 call, opening remarks) — an assertion, not a delivered fact. When Karan Gupta and Sumit Ambekar directly asked management to strip out Indonesia and quantify domestic/organic transfer case volume, Jitendra could not or did not answer with a number: "I don't have the numbers off the top of my head, but broadly on a revenue basis... transfer case business... is at an all-time high of almost INR75 crores. If you were to annualize that, that will come to INR300 crores" (Q1 FY27 call) — this answers total transfer-case run-rate, not the Indonesia-vs-organic split asked for. **This is a real gap: the single most important question for judging whether the Q1 FY27 beat is a one-off Indonesia batch or a durable re-rated run rate was asked directly and not answered.** Flagged as a red flag below.

**(2) ~Rs 275 cr net cash decline in FY26.** No transcript across all three calls contains any rupee figure for cash, net cash, or net cash movement. Management repeatedly asserts "debt-free balance sheet" and "healthy net cash position" (Q4 FY26 call, Jitendra Divgi: "even after factoring in future expansion plans and increased working capital requirements associated with growth, the company expects to maintain a healthy net cash position") but never quantifies the balance or its year-on-year change in any of the three calls, and no analyst asked about it. **There is nothing reconcilable in the concall record for the ~Rs 275 cr net cash decline claim** — management's language is consistent with net cash staying healthy/rising, not with a large decline, but this is asserted, not evidenced with a number. Flagged as a disclosure gap (2D) and a red flag.

**(3) June 2026 FY25 results resubmission.** No mention of any restatement, resubmission, or correction of FY25 results appears anywhere in the Q4 FY26/FY26 annual call (27-May-2026) or in any other transcript. The FY25 comparator figures cited (FY25 revenue Rs 240 cr, EBITDA Rs 58 cr, PAT Rs 24.4 cr — Q4 FY26 call; 9M FY25 revenue Rs 176 cr — Q3 FY26 call; Q4 FY25 revenue Rs 64.1 cr — Q4 FY26 call) are internally consistent with each other (176 + 64.1 ≈ 240), showing no visible sign of restatement in the numbers used. **NOT FOUND in the concall record; if a resubmission occurred it was not addressed on any of these three calls.**

**(4) Sigma EV transmission SOP in Q2 FY27 after an April 2026 slip.** Traced across all three calls:
- Q3 FY26 call (Feb 2026): the 120-kilowatt/220 Newton-metre transmission for 4 models of a key EV customer is "in the concluding phase," delay attributed to "hiccups the OEM has faced in their vehicle testing," and "as of now, indication that we have is that SOP will be in April of 2026" (Jitendra Divgi). Separately, "Project Sigma" is named as expected to add "at least additional 3,000" units of EV capacity within about 3 months (same call).
- Q4 FY26 call (Jun 2026): the same 90–120kW design (now spanning Punch, Nexon, Curvv, Harrier) is confirmed to have received Production Part Approval (PPAP) from Tata, but vehicle testing "took longer than expected"; Jitendra states "next month or definitely by July, this new model will be going into production" (i.e., a slip from the promised April 2026 date to ~June/July 2026).
- Q1 FY27 call (Aug 2026): EV segment shows "a little bit of a dent in Q1"; "the transition from imported platforms to localized production... has taken longer than initially anticipated"; "we have a PPAP approval today from Tata" (i.e., PPAP reconfirmed/finalised only as of the 12-Aug-2026 call date, later than the "by July" estimate given three months earlier); "Sigma program is progressing towards commercialization" (not yet at SOP); forward run-rate guided to Rs 10–12 cr/quarter vs a prior Rs 6.5–8 cr/quarter base, for "the remaining quarters" of FY27.
**No transcript across the three calls states an explicit "Q2 FY27" SOP date for Sigma.** The verifiable transcript record shows a slip from April 2026 (promised, Q3 FY26 call) to "July 2026" (promised, Q4 FY26 call) to still-not-launched as of the 12-Aug-2026 call, with commercialization described as "progressing" and no new fixed date given. This is a genuine, repeated, three-consecutive-quarter slippage on the same product; if the operator's Q2 FY27 SOP figure comes from a source outside these three transcripts, it is not corroborated here and should be checked at Halt 1.

### 2B. Excuse pattern analysis

Every disclosed miss (EV SOP slippage, EV utilization shortfall) is attributed to the **same external cause repeated verbatim across three quarters**: the OEM customer's (Tata's) vehicle testing taking longer than planned. This is a plausible explanation for a Tier-1 supplier waiting on a customer's PPAP, but it is never paired with any acknowledgment of what Divgi itself might have done differently, and the pattern (identical excuse, three quarters running, without escalating urgency in tone) is a caution flag. No instance across the three transcripts contains a "we made a mistake" statement from management about anything. Hard topics that were raised proactively (the Mahindra 3-wheeler loss, Q4 FY26 call) sit alongside topics raised only under analyst pressure or not at all (order book quantification, net cash figures, the declining export share, the unconfirmed automatic-transmission contract milestone). **Classification: excuse-pattern is external-blame-heavy**, softened by one instance of proactive, non-spun disclosure (the Mahindra 3-wheeler loss).

### 2C. Tone ratings (1–5, with evidence)

- **Transparency: 3/5.** Segment financials are disclosed with good granularity (volumes, realizations, capacity). But order book value, cash-balance figures, and the Indonesia-vs-organic transfer case split were each asked for directly and not given.
- **Specificity: 4/5.** Extremely specific on operational detail — unit counts, kilowatt ratings, BOM line-item counts (80–90 for a transfer case vs 250+ for an automatic transmission), capacity numbers, named competitor OEMs.
- **Consistency: 3/5.** Core segment narrative is consistent, but headline revenue targets escalate quickly quarter to quarter (Rs 1,500 cr → Rs 2,000 cr "potential" language across two quarters) and the export-share and EV-utilization figures move in directions inconsistent with stated targets without acknowledgment.
- **Accountability: 2/5.** No "we made a mistake" instance found in any of three calls; every slip is externally attributed (OEM testing delays, market conditions, geopolitics/Iran war, EV range anxiety).
- **Defensiveness: 3/5.** Handled the toughest question of the three calls (Jai Prakash, Q4 FY26, on reports the Indonesian government was "keeping [orders] on hold") with a long, data-backed rebuttal rather than deflection — a positive data point — but the rebuttal also veered into unverifiable geopolitical claims ("Japanese propaganda," "$500 million savings" for Indonesia) that a peer-verification stage should check.
- **Over-promotion: 5/5 (high).** Persistent grandiose framing throughout all three calls: "historic milestone," "the mother of all markets," "historic crossroad," "leading the revolution in India to create India's first generation of genuine Tier-1 companies" (Q1 FY27 call), plus effusive praise of nearly every analyst's question ("excellent question," "very relevant," "thank you for your compliment"). Per pipeline rules this rating does NOT move the credibility grade, which is set on delivery evidence alone.

### 2D. What they are NOT saying

No analyst asked either.
- **Total order book value (Rs cr).** Asked directly once (Q3 FY26 call, Karan Gupta); deflected with a qualitative, non-numeric answer; never revisited or volunteered in the two later calls.
- **FY25 results resubmission.** No mention anywhere across the three calls (see SPEAR fact 3).
- **Automatic transmission contract status (the July-2026 milestone).** Simply not revisited after the deadline passed.
- **Reconciliation of the "crossed 20% [export] in Q2 FY26" claim (Q4 FY26 call) against the 17-18% (9M FY26/FY26) and 16% (Q1 FY27) blended figures.** Never explained; likely a different denominator or a peak that did not hold, but management does not clarify, and the declining trend into Q1 FY27 is not addressed at all.
