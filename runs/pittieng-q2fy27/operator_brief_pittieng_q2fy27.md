## 1. NARRATIVE

1. Pitti presses electrical steel into thin sheets called laminations. It builds them into the core parts of motors and generators. It also casts and machines frames and shafts for the same customers.
2. Its buyers are large makers of locomotives, generators and heavy motors, in India and abroad. To win them, a supplier must pass long quality checks. Once it passes, the buyer rarely switches.
3. This quarter, sales grew 21% to Rs 578 Cr. Profit after tax fell 15% to Rs 34 Cr.
4. Profit fell because "other income" almost disappeared. This is income from outside the main business. It was Rs 21 Cr a year ago and Rs 2 Cr now.
5. On the call, management linked other income to a Maharashtra state incentive. They said they collect nothing this year and about Rs 40 Cr a year from FY28. We cannot yet tell if this is old income paused or a new income line.
6. The main business did well. Profit before tax, without other income, grew 33%.
7. Cash is the first worry. Half-year operating cash looks strong, but it came from paying suppliers later. Money owed to suppliers almost doubled to Rs 475 Cr.
8. Management said this supplier credit will stay and will help pay for four years of new plants. Net debt is Rs 539 Cr, above the Rs 491 Cr line we set. That test fails.
9. Castings are the second worry. The company sold 3,086 tonnes, two-thirds of our 4,600-tonne line. The foundry runs at about half its capacity. That test also fails.
10. On the call, management changed how it counts the 17,000-tonne castings target, to tonnes made instead of tonnes sold. It did not give the half-year figure on the new count.
11. The Board approved Rs 450 Cr of new capacity, for Rs 740 Cr in all up to FY31. Management put this year's spend at Rs 250-300 Cr. The filing shows Rs 257 Cr already spent in the half.
12. Watch next quarter: castings tonnes sold and made, how much of the supplier credit is really bank-backed debt, and whether the margin recovers when fuel costs pass to customers from January.
