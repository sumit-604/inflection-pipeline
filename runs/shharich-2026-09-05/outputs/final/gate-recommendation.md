## Why the rule fired

Verdict selection rule 1 fired. Two of its three triggers are true. One verifier acceptance
rate sits below 60 (red flag coverage 26). Overall confidence sits below 60 (26, the minimum
of the four components). No source fidelity finding is outstanding and no CRITICAL numerical
finding exists, so the gate is not about wrong numbers.

REWORK judges the ANALYSIS, not the company. The numbers this run printed hold up: the
numerical verifier re-read 54 figures against the source pages and accepted 98.1 percent,
with both of its earlier major findings cleared on re-check. What failed is coverage. An
independent reader working the same annual report and the same eight peer transcripts found
38 flags. This run caught 10, partly caught 7, and missed 21, two of them CRITICAL. Two
claims this run made were not supported by the peer record it cited.

Failing stages: stage 5 (management communication, no concall mode) and stage 6 (peer
verification). Every missed and unsupported item sits in one of those two.

2. Stage 5, Form AOC-2 never read. It declares 8 related party contracts NOT at arm's length

   related party transaction exists (AR pp. 71 to 72, p. 89).
3. Stage 5, the Rs 100 crore related party ceiling. Rs 50 crore of it is for a counterparty
   with nil FY26 transactions and nil turnover; the other counterparty is loss making; and
   the abbreviation SDPL is used for two different companies in one report (AR pp. 16, 35,
   37, 39, 40, p. 7).
4. Stage 5, the delivery credit must be withdrawn or re-evidenced. The Reg 32(7A) footnote
   says net proceeds went towards other purposes considered expedient, the next line declares
   no deviations, and Note 35.24 gives a third version (AR pp. 90 to 91, p. 138). The single
   delivery credit behind the D grade rests on that table.
5. Stage 5, four undisclosed or contradicted items to record: the object clause widening into

6. Stage 5, three numbers endorsed that the audited accounts contradict: board report
   consolidated PAT 411.81 and TCI 410.61 against the audited consolidated P&L 414.95 and

7. Stage 5, the margin excuse must be decomposed. The trading mix explains about a third of

   unit of time, and the second wholly owned subsidiary Shakambhari Chemtech Private Limited,
   which the report never mentions.
8. Stage 6, three peer readings must be re-dated or withdrawn. The claim that both peers
   describe a lag between input cost and price pass through is unsupported: Bodal states pass
   through is in tandem (Bodal Q4 FY25 call 29-May-2025, Mayur Padhya, p. 9 to 10). The cost

   realisations down 21 percent (Shree Pushkar Q3 FY26 call 12-Feb-2026).
9. Stage 6, two verdicts must change. The export step change graded UNVERIFIABLE and called

   tonne India to China H Acid flow from June or July 2025 (Shree Pushkar Q3 FY26 call,
   Punit Makharia, p. 7). And no peer supports an overcapacity narrative understates a
   disclosed 72,000 tonne a year chemical capacity addition plus a 240,000 tonne acid complex

10. Stage 6, the evasion that was missed and the anchors that were wrong. The peer refused
    the volume versus realisation reconciliation across two consecutive quarters, on the exact
    question this run calls its strongest independent confirmation (Shree Pushkar Q4 FY26 call
    19-May-2026 p. 8; Q1 FY27 call 13-Aug-2026 pp. 10 to 11). Separately, the peer verifier
    found two quotes real but mislocated, including the flagship contradicted verdict citation,
    and three off by one page anchors in one transcript, plus two speaker misattributions.

## Monitorables

1. Organic manufactured H Acid revenue, stripped of the resale line. Find it in the product
   disaggregation note of the next annual report, or in any results filing that separates
   sale of manufactured goods from purchase of stock in trade. Double digit organic growth
   supports the volume story; a repeat of about 5 percent under a bigger headline kills it.
2. Other income as a share of pre tax profit. Next profit and loss statement. It was 57.7
   percent in FY26 and should fall back toward 12 to 15 percent. A second year above 40
   percent makes the earnings quality problem structural rather than a one off.
3. Promoter family pay, relatives' salaries and rent as a share of standalone pre tax profit.
   Next related party note and AOC-2. It was 84.9 percent then 112.7 percent. Below 100
   percent says FY26 was a peak; above says it is the new normal. Watch the Rs 6.60 crore
   remuneration ceiling separately, because the ceiling is the intent.
4. Shakambhari Dyechem's first turnover and the capitalisation of its aged construction in
   progress. Next AOC-1 and the property plant and equipment movement note. Nil turnover into
   a third year kills the expansion leg of the thesis outright.
5. Warrant follow through: the 75 percent balance payment inside the 18 month window from
   25-Aug-2026. Reg 30 filings and the next share capital note. Lapsed warrants would say the
   family does not want the equity at that price.
6. Current ratio. Next balance sheet. At or above 1.0 is healthy for this working capital
   profile; below 0.80 is deterioration on top of two sub 1.0 years.
7. Export share and the foreign exchange note together. Next annual report. Watch whether a
   hedging policy appears, whether the export share holds, and whether the risk note still
   denies material currency exposure while 46 percent of sales are exported.
8. H Acid realisation and the India to China trade flow, from the ChemAnalyst price tracker
   monthly and DGCIS trade statistics monthly. This is the only external read that separates
   a price driven quarter from a volume driven one, and it is the question the whole run turns
   on.
