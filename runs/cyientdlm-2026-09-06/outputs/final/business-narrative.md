# BUSINESS UNDERSTANDING NARRATIVE

Cyient DLM builds electronic assemblies that sit inside machines where a failure is not survivable. Printed circuit board assemblies are 52 percent of FY26 revenue at about Rs 655 Cr, box builds are 30 percent at about Rs 384 Cr, precision machined parts are 16 percent at about Rs 203 Cr, and cable harnesses are 2 percent at about Rs 20 Cr. A box build is the finished sub system, the boards and wiring and metalwork assembled into one unit that the customer bolts into an aircraft, a scanner or a machine tool. A customer cannot move this work quickly, because every part number is qualified into a named programme under AS9100, NADCAP, ITAR, ISO13485 or IATF16949, and qualifying a second supplier runs for years.

The buyers are global and regional OEMs and Tier 1 suppliers in four verticals. Aerospace is 43 percent of the FY26 vertical mix, industrial 27 percent, medical 19 percent, defence 9 percent and automotive 2 percent. Honeywell Aerospace and Thales are the named anchor accounts, Deutsche Aircraft is a new design led logo, and an unnamed Japanese eVTOL company holds a nine year programme that reaches mass production late this decade. Buying behaviour cuts both ways: qualification takes years and locks the supplier in, and the top customers are 55.71 percent of consolidated revenue and 70.54 percent of standalone revenue.

Demand today comes from a short list of downstream drivers the run named. BEL repeat orders for the Indian Navy and the Ministry of Defence set the defence vertical order pool. India MoD modernisation and the defence budget allocation set its size. MeitY PLI and ECMS scheme disbursement fund the wider ESDM base the company sits inside. Honeywell Aerospace and Thales order flow drives the aerospace vertical directly.

Demand should grow on drivers that can be checked outside the company. EU ReArm Europe, sized at EUR 800bn, feeds the European defence electronics pipeline that Thales sits on. The US CHIPS Act pulls semiconductor capital equipment subsystem orders. Inside the company the matching evidence is order intake of about Rs 1,843 Cr in FY26, up 90 percent, an order book of about Rs 2,417 Cr, and a full year book to bill of 1.5.

Competitive advantage sits in one place and not the others. Build to Print work, 94 percent of revenue, has no moat of its own: it is a cost pass through model where the customer supplies the drawings and the bill of materials, and the company itself names pass through clauses as its currency hedge. Build to Spec work, 6 percent of revenue, is where design ownership and 9 to 20 year contracts live, and it is too small today to carry the business. The emerging moat scan scored 21 on a scale reaching about 90 and classed the forward position MODEST, with four active categories: qualification lock in rated Strong on documented evidence, customer ecosystem rated Moderate on a claim, improving concentration rated Moderate and caveated as partly a consolidation artifact, and new certifications rated Moderate on documented evidence. The one durable moat, qualification lock in, predates FY26 and is not itself emerging. The AI data centre and robotics lanes carry no named customer and no disclosed revenue, and the scan recorded no active category beyond those four.

US subsidiary carries a shrinking India core

Cyient DLM is a high mix low volume electronics manufacturer with plants at Mysore and Bengaluru, a head office at Hyderabad, and Altek Electronics in Connecticut, acquired in 2024. Cyient Limited owns 52.12 percent and is the sole promoter. FY26 consolidated revenue was about Rs 1,261 Cr, down 17.0 percent. The company listed in FY2023-24, so only four years of annual data exist.

The claimed transition has two legs. The first is a mix shift from Build to Print, where the customer owns the design, to Build to Spec, where Cyient DLM co designs the product and holds influence over the bill of materials. Management sizes Build to Spec at 6 percent of FY26 revenue with a double digit target for FY27 and a claimed margin uplift of 250 to 300 basis points over 12 to 18 months. The same annual report also states 25 percent in its statutory BRSR table. The run ruled for 6 percent, corroborated twice, and the gap is a management question, not something to average away. The second leg is geographic, from an India base to a dual shore India and US footprint.

The FY26 numbers say the two legs are moving in opposite directions. Consolidated PAT rose 7.65 percent while the standalone India parent's PAT fell 26.6 percent. The whole swing is Cyient DLM Inc in the US, which moved from a Rs 8.5 Cr loss to a Rs 25 Cr profit. Standalone revenue fell 29.9 percent and India geography revenue fell 83 to 84 percent. Three non recurring items sit in the consolidated profit without a label: an Altek earn out fair value gain of about Rs 20 Cr, an inventory build credit of about Rs 54 Cr, and about Rs 9 Cr of ESOP reversal. Together they are larger than the entire year on year movement in profit before tax. Altek itself earned less in full year FY26, about Rs 3.1 Cr, than in roughly six months of FY25, about Rs 4.0 Cr.

Cash is where the record is hardest. Cumulative operating cash flow across FY23 to FY26 is minus Rs 25 Cr against cumulative profit after tax of plus Rs 234 Cr. Net working capital days ran 48, 79, 127 and 145 across those four years and reached 161 by the June 2026 quarter. The Gate 0 screen classified the name AVOID on a mechanical cash conversion rule, with cumulative CFO to PAT at minus 0.11. The offsetting facts are real and belong next to that: standalone borrowings are zero, unused credit lines stand at about Rs 417 Cr, interest cover improved to 4.43 times, EBITDA margin rose from 9.0 percent to 10.1 percent, and FY26 operating cash flow itself swung positive by about Rs 116 Cr. Debt service coverage is 0.62 times in FY26 and the direction of the one year move cannot be established, because the two annual reports disclose the same FY25 year two different ways.

What management says against what it does grades C. Two self set commitments failed. Positive year on year revenue growth was promised for the March 2026 quarter, reaffirmed once, and the quarter came in down 13.8 percent. Working capital was promised to normalise by that same quarter and instead worsened, then the promise was replaced by a vaguer target of 100 to 120 days over a couple of years. A specific FY27 growth number of 20 to 25 percent was given and then withdrawn twice under questioning. Altek standalone financials were asked for across three calls and never given. The book to bill promise was kept, the design led anchor customer timeline held, and revenue and margin did arrive one quarter late, at 34.3 percent growth in the June 2026 quarter. The explanations for the misses point outward, to a West Asia war and to memory component lead times, with no admission of a planning error.

The peers cut away the industry wide defence on the one issue that matters most. Avalon improved net working capital from 124 days to 112 days while growing revenue 46 percent, with operating cash flow rising from about Rs 25 Cr to about Rs 57 Cr. Syrma improved from 69 days to 63 days with operating cash flow at 53 percent of EBITDA. Kaynes did deteriorate, and traced it to a named non EMS government receivables subsidiary while its core EMS working capital improved from 83 days to 53 days. All three grew revenue 27 to 46 percent in FY26. Cyient DLM fell 17.0 percent over the same window. Peers do confirm two company claims: about 50 percent US tariff exposure with customer level mitigation, and a memory and component shortage in the January to June 2026 window.

Market size is not the constraint. The run set a conservative TAM of about Rs 88,000 Cr for certified high mix low volume EMS in the four verticals, a serviceable market of about Rs 21,600 Cr, and a 3 year serviceable obtainable market of about Rs 2,053 Cr against current capture of 5.84 percent. Revenue headroom is about 17 times and the runway graded STRONG. The management framing does not survive the same test. The global EMS figure management cites runs at about 70 times the conservative TAM for this company, no peer cites it, and the annual report gives the terminal year two ways. The 5 year obtainable figure also runs about 26 percent past the capacity ceiling implied by the company's own 55 to 60 percent utilisation disclosure.
