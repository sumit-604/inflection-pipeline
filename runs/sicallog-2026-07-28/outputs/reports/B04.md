## SECTION 1: THE BUSINESS MODEL IN PLAIN ENGLISH

### 1A. One-line description
Sical is a post-insolvency, Pristine Group-controlled multimodal logistics operator that gets paid three different ways for three different jobs: digging earth out of the way for coal mines (a contractor), running container yards next to ports (a landlord-operator), and storing and moving other companies' goods in warehouses (a fee-based service provider) — with the mining piece run directly out of the listed parent and the port/rail piece run out of a partly-owned subsidiary.

### 1E. The chai-stall-uncle version
Imagine three different small businesses run by the same family. One brother digs a hole for the coal company and gets paid by how much dirt he moves — steady work if the mine keeps digging, but he doesn't own his own shovels; he rents them from his uncle (that's the Pristine-Malwa equipment lease). Another brother owns a big parking lot right next to the port where ships unload, and every truck or container that parks there for customs-clearance pays him rent — that lot is real estate, hard to copy, and he's the biggest lot-owner in Chennai. The third sister runs rented warehouses where she stores other people's goods and delivers them out for a fee, like a paid-storage-and-delivery service — she doesn't own the buildings, so she can shrink or grow without big bills. The family went bankrupt a few years ago (2021 insolvency), was bought out by a bigger logistics family (Pristine Group, backed by BlackRock money) in 2023, and is now trying to prove the business can stand on its own feet again.

### 2D. Industry lifecycle stage
- Coal OB removal: mature-but-growing niche — India crossed 1 billion tonnes coal production in FY25 (~1,047.6 MT vs ~997.8 MT FY24) with a government target of 1.5 billion tonnes by FY30 (Inv. Pres. slide 35; AR MD&A p.33). Company position: established incumbent (7+ decades), riding a genuine multi-year volume tailwind via a large order book.
- CFS/ICD: mature, slow-growing industry (Rs 60-70bn as of FY25, projected 4-6% CAGR to Rs 80-90bn by FY30, Inv. Pres. slide 36) — Sical's position is a share-gaining/consolidating incumbent within a stagnant overall pool.
- MMLP/rail-linked terminals: early-growth, policy-driven (National Rail Policy target of 45% rail freight share; PM Gati Shakti) — Sical is an early entrant in the South Indian corridor (Inv. Pres. slide 28, 26).
- Warehousing/3PL: high-growth, structurally expanding (organized warehousing to double by FY30 per AR MD&A p.33; 10-13% CAGR per Inv. Pres. slide 37) — Sical is a small, asset-light participant scaling via leased capacity and the newly-added pharma super-stockist line.

### 4B. Valuation method applicability (handoff table for Role 1 valuation stage)

| Method | Applicable? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **PRIMARY: Sum-of-the-Parts (SOTP)** | YES | Three structurally distinct segments with different asset intensity, ownership stakes (SIAL/SMART only 53.6%-owned; Warehousing subsidiary PVLPL 100%-owned; Mining run directly through standalone parent) and different economics (contract-service vs infrastructure-toll vs asset-light 3PL) make a single blended multiple inappropriate. SOTP should value: (i) Mining as a contract-services business against order book/EBITDA, (ii) Terminals (CFS+MMLP) at Sical's *economic* (53.6%-look-through) share against CFS/port-infrastructure peer multiples, (iii) Warehousing/3PL against asset-light logistics peer multiples, then net off standalone parent's negative net worth/debt |
| **SECONDARY: EV/EBITDA (consolidated, cycle-normalized)** | YES, as cross-check | Useful sanity check against diversified logistics peers, but must be **NCI-adjusted** (currently ~51% of consolidated equity sits with minorities, Inv. Pres. slide 31) before comparing to peer EV/EBITDA multiples, and should be normalized for the FY24-FY26 turnaround trajectory (EBITDA grew from Rs 101mn to Rs 783mn, Inv. Pres. slide 7) rather than a single-year snapshot |
| **TERTIARY: Replacement cost / asset-value cross-check for Terminals only** | YES, limited use | Owned CFS land (Chennai ~55 acres, Tuticorin ~16.6 acres, Vizag ~10.2 acres) and the 82-acre Chennai MMLP parcel are scarce, quasi-real-estate assets; a replacement-cost or land-value floor check is a useful sanity check specifically for the Terminals SOTP component, not for Mining or Warehousing |
| Not applicable: DCF as sole/primary method | NOT APPLICABLE as primary | Company is only 3 years post-CIRP with a single audited full profitable year (FY26); reliable multi-year free cash flow forecasting is premature given the volatility already visible (FY24→FY26 EBITDA up 675%, rights issue for compliance not growth) — DCF can be a secondary sensitivity check once segment-level cash flows stabilize, but should not anchor the valuation now |
| Not applicable: P/E on consolidated EPS (unadjusted) | NOT APPLICABLE | Standalone parent carries negative net worth and consolidated EPS blends ~51% NCI-owned earnings; unadjusted P/E would materially misstate value attributable to SICALLOG shareholders |
| Not applicable: Book value / P/B | NOT APPLICABLE | Post-CIRP asset write-downs (FY21-FY23) and accumulated losses (consolidated retained earnings -Rs 2,05,623 lakh, AR p.36) make book value a poor proxy for going-concern earning power |

**Cycle stage that matters for valuation**: The company is in **early-stage post-restructuring turnaround**, not mid-cycle steady state. FY24 (Rs 2,211mn revenue, Rs 101mn EBITDA, 5% margin) to FY26 (Rs 3,857mn revenue, Rs 783mn EBITDA, 20% margin) is a recovery/ramp trajectory, not a normalized run-rate (Inv. Pres. slide 7). Any valuation must explicitly state whether it is using FY26 (recent, but still ramping — MMLP only commissioned Dec-2025) or a forward normalized year, and should not extrapolate the FY24-FY26 growth rate mechanically forward.
