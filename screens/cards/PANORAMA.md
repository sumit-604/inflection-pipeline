## Business Understanding Narrative

Panorama Studios makes films and sells the rights to them. It describes itself
as present "in multiple verticals viz. production, distribution, exploitation
of content", all treated as one segment (Result Q1FY27 p2 note 4). Around the
listed parent sit five subsidiaries: Panorama Studios Pvt Ltd, Panorama Music
Pvt Ltd, Panorama Music Regional Pvt Ltd, Panorama Studios Inflight LLP, and
Brain on Rent LLP (Result Q1FY27 p7). One was renamed from Panorama Studios
Distribution LLP to Panorama Studios Inflight LLP, which points at in-flight
entertainment rights. So the money is made by spending on a film, then selling
theatrical, streaming, music and ancillary rights against it.

What is changing is scale. Consolidated revenue grew 34.3% in Q1 FY27, to
Rs 183.1 crore from Rs 136.3 crore. Consolidated profit grew 263%, to Rs 12.7
crore from Rs 3.5 crore (Result Q1FY27 p3). FY26 consolidated revenue was
Rs 308.5 crore.

Why now: nothing in the corpus says. There is no management commentary of any
kind. A film company's quarter is a release calendar, and the calendar is not
disclosed here.

What must be true: that the slate keeps landing. Operational expenses ran 86.1%
of standalone revenue in Q1 FY27 (Result Q1FY27 p2). That leaves a thin
cushion. A film business earns its return on the difference between what a
title costs and what its rights fetch, and one flop resets a year.

What breaks it: the share count. Paid-up capital went from Rs 14.19 crore at
30 June 2025 to Rs 52.11 crore at 31 March 2026, a 3.67 times increase (Result
Q1FY27 p2). The only explanation offered is a 2:5 bonus issue on 2 December
2025 (Result Q1FY27 p2 note 5). A 2:5 bonus turns 1,418.78 into about 1,986.
It does not turn it into 5,210.71. Roughly Rs 32 crore of face value appeared
by a route this filing does not name. On a per-share thesis, that is the whole
question, and it is unanswered.

## 2. Business model and archetype
One reported segment, media and entertainment (Result Q1FY27 p2 note 4). No
split between production, distribution and exploitation is given, so the
revenue mix that would tell you whether this is a studio or an agency is not
disclosed.
What the P&L shows is a thin-margin pass-through. Standalone Q1 FY27:
Rs 179.8 crore of revenue, Rs 154.8 crore of operational expenses, Rs 2.02
crore of employee cost (Result Q1FY27 p2). Two crore of salary on 180 crore of
revenue means almost nothing is made in-house. This is a company that
commissions, finances and trades rights.
Archetype: **fits no rung of the library, and that is the finding**. The eight
archetypes assume a repeatable unit economic. A film slate has none. The
nearest is the order-book business, where a title is an order and execution
pace matters, but a trailer maker's order book is contracted and a film's
revenue is not. Declare it a **project-slate content business** and record that
the library needs a media archetype before any Stage 11 runs on this name.

## 3. Competitive advantages
Not demonstrable from the corpus. The things that would give a studio an edge,
a library of owned rights, a director or star relationship, a distribution
network, a music catalogue, are all plausible and none is documented here.
What the numbers show cuts the other way. An 86.1% operational expense ratio
says the content cost is nearly all of the revenue. Finance costs ran Rs 11.06
crore consolidated in FY26 against Rs 16.26 crore of pre-tax profit (Result

it earns. The FY23 and FY24 Large Corporate Annexure B2 filings in the Bull AI

years, which is a disclosure obligation for sizeable debt issuers.
A moat is **not evidenced**. Absent, on what is held.

## 12. Verdict card
**WATCH.**
Not a pass, because the business is growing fast and visibly: 34.3% revenue
growth and 263% profit growth in the most recent quarter, on a Rs 308 crore
FY26 base. Not a proceed, because a full `/step1` would run aground inside a
day. There is no balance sheet, no cash-flow statement, no promoter data, no
management commentary in the company's entire filing history, and a 3.67 times
increase in share capital that the company has not explained.

Load-bearing facts to resolve before any `/step1`:
1. What caused the share capital to rise from Rs 14.19 crore to Rs 52.11 crore
   beyond the 2:5 bonus. Preferential allotment, conversion, merger. Until this
   is known, no per-share number means anything.
2. The four SAST Regulation 29(2) disclosures of 17 December 2025.
3. The content amortisation policy, from the FY25 or FY26 annual report.
4. Operating cash flow for FY25 and FY26, to separate profit from capitalised
   film cost.
5. Promoter holding and pledge, absent entirely.
6. Whether a credit rating exists, given the Large Corporate filings.

What would change the view: a clean explanation of the equity expansion, plus
two years of positive operating cash flow, would make this a genuine `/step1`
candidate on a real growth record. Evidence that the share issuance funded
related parties, or that profit is capitalised content, turns this to PASS.
No price, no position. This decides only that Panorama does not earn a
`/step1` until the share count is explained.
