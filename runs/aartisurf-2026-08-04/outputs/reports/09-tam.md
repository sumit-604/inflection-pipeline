## SECTION 1: MARKET DEFINITION

### 1A. Precise boundaries

**Product scope (in):** Anionic, non-ionic and specialty surfactants and blended
specialty ingredients (surfactants, mild surfactants, UV blockers, preservatives,
pearlising agents) sold as B2B raw-material/ingredient inputs to FMCG manufacturers,
per the Sep-2025 investor presentation's own "Industries catered" slide and the
FY2020-21 AR product-portfolio page (Investor_Presentation_1.pdf p.2; Annual_Report_2022.pdf p.4).

**Product scope (out, explicit exclusion):** Industrial & institutional cleaning
chemicals, oilfield surfactants, agrochemical surfactants, textile-processing
surfactants, Synthetic Latex Polymer (SLP)/paints-and-coatings/construction
surfactants. These are large categories inside the generic "surfactants market"
figures that market-research vendors publish, but they are **not** part of
Aarti Surfactants' disclosed customer base (home care + personal care only, per
Investor_Presentation_1.pdf p.2 and p.5) and are excluded from TAM here. This is
the single most important scoping decision in this report: most third-party
"surfactants market" reports size the full multi-application market, which
overstates AARTISURF's addressable opportunity by roughly 2x if used unfiltered
(see Method 1 below).

**Geographic scope:** Primary — India (80% of FY2025 revenue, Investor_Presentation_1.pdf
p.5). Secondary — export markets across 30+ countries in Asia, Europe, Africa,
Americas (20% of FY2025 revenue, same source). Core TAM/SAM built on India;
global export opportunity carried separately as a SAM-expansion lever (Section 5D)
rather than blended into the headline TAM, because export revenue is a minority
and volatile share (B04 flags export % as "L" predictability, falling from 28.2%
FY2020-21 to 20% FY2025).

**Customer scope:** FMCG home-care and personal-care manufacturers (100+
customers per Investor_Presentation_1.pdf p.2); B04 flags two-customer
concentration at 35.0% of FY2020-21 revenue (AR p.80, Note 30) — current-year
concentration NOT FOUND (B04 input_gaps). This customer concentration is treated
as a capability/customer-access constraint on SAM and SOM speed, not a TAM
scoping issue.

**Channel scope:** Direct B2B ingredient supply (no retail/consumer channel
relevant — company sells to manufacturers, not to end consumers).

**Price segment:** Commodity-to-mid-specialty. B04 explicitly rates pricing
power "weak" and the business "cyclical" (B04-bizmodel.yaml).

### 1B. Management's own TAM claim

Neither injected document contains a company-specific TAM, SAM or ₹-crore
addressable-market claim. The Sep-2025 investor presentation (Investor_Presentation_1.pdf,
9 slides) is a headline AGM deck with only financials, milestones and a
"vision to be Global Partner of Choice for FMCG surfactants" — no market-size
figure anywhere in it.

The only quantified market figure attributable to management sits in the
FY2020-21 Annual Report's Management Discussion & Analysis (Annual_Report_2022.pdf
p.9, "Industry Overview — Global Surfactants Market"): *"The global surfactants
market is estimated to reach USD 52.4 billion by 2025 from USD 42.1 billion in
2020, registering a CAGR of 4.5% from 2020 to 2025"* (cited source within the
AR: Markets and Markets Research). This is:
- **Stale**: dated 2020, forecast window (2020-2025) has now fully lapsed as of
  the 2026-08-04 run date — >4 years old, per the staleness rule this may
  inform direction only, never a headline number.
- **Scope-mismatched**: it is the *entire global* surfactants market across
  every application (industrial, oilfield, agro, textile, construction,
  home/personal care combined), not the home+personal-care-only, India-weighted
  slice AARTISURF actually serves.
- **Not a company claim in the ordinary sense** — it is generic third-party
  industry background quoted in the MD&A, not management asserting "this is
  our addressable market."

**Credibility read: BROAD.** Management does not offer a specific, scoped TAM/SAM
claim in either document — itself a flag (see flags list). For the purposes of
the mandated mgmt-claim-vs-conservative-estimate ratio (Section 2), this
generic global figure is used as the best available proxy for an "implied
claim," clearly labelled as such, converted to ₹ Cr at the current spot rate
(₹95.3/USD, 3 Aug 2026, exchangerates.org.uk / tradingeconomics.com) for
comparability: USD 52.4bn × 95.3 ≈ **₹4,99,372 Cr**.

## SECTION 2: TAM ESTIMATION, MULTIPLE METHODS

### Method 1 — Top-down (India surfactants market, filtered to home+personal care)

Third-party estimates for the **India surfactants market** (all applications,
2024 base year) diverge by vendor:
- IMARC Group: USD 2.03bn (2024) → USD 3.85bn (2033), CAGR 6.80% (2025-2033)
  (imarcgroup.com/india-surfactants-market, search-snippet accessed 2026-08-04;
  direct WebFetch to this URL returned HTTP 403, so figures are taken from the
  search-engine synthesis of the page, not the primary page itself — flagged).
- Grand View Research: USD 3,015.0mn (2024) → USD 5,156.8mn (2033), CAGR 6.2%
  (2025-2033) (grandviewresearch.com/horizon/outlook/surfactants-market/india,
  search-snippet accessed 2026-08-04; direct fetch also 403).
- MarkNtel Advisors: USD 2.11bn (2025) → USD 3.32bn (2030), CAGR 9.49%
  (2025-2030) (marknteladvisors.com, search-snippet, 2026-08-04).
- Market Research Future: USD 3.99bn (2024) → USD 10.92bn (2035), CAGR 9.58%
  (2025-2035) (marketresearchfuture.com, search-snippet, 2026-08-04) — treated
  as an outlier/likely-inflated vendor estimate and excluded from the
  conservative/realistic band.

Per conservative bias, the **lower** IMARC figure (USD 2.03bn, 2024) anchors
the conservative case; Grand View (USD 3.015bn, 2024) anchors the realistic
case.

No India-specific application split (home care vs personal care vs industrial
vs oilfield vs agro vs textile vs construction) was found in any source. As a
proxy, the **global** surfactants application split is used (flagged as an
estimate, L confidence): household detergents/home care ≈ 40% share, "dominant"
2022-2024 (fortunebusinessinsights.com / polarismarketresearch.com,
search-snippet, 2026-08-04); industrial & institutional cleaning ≈ 30%+ of 2024
share (same sources); personal care described qualitatively as smaller than
home care/industrial but the fastest-growing category (6.34% CAGR to 2035,
same sources) — no fixed % disclosed, so **personal care share is assumed at
≈17%** (ASSUMPTION, L confidence, derived as a residual after home care 40%,
industrial/institutional 30%+, and other niche applications ~13%).

Home + personal care combined share of total surfactants ≈ **40% + 17% = 57%**,
rounded down to **55%** for conservative bias.

- Conservative TAM = USD 2.03bn × 55% = USD 1.1165bn × ₹95.3 ≈ **₹10,640 Cr** (2024 basis)
- Realistic TAM = USD 3.015bn × 55% = USD 1.65825bn × ₹95.3 ≈ **₹15,801 Cr** (2024 basis)

### Method 2 — Bottom-up (application/ingredient-share cross-check)

Rather than one blended %, size home care and personal care surfactant spend
separately and sum:

- **Home care surfactants (India):** India total surfactants market
  (conservative, IMARC USD 2.03bn, 2024) × 40% global home-care application
  share (proxy, as above) = USD 812mn × ₹95.3 ≈ **₹7,738 Cr**.
- **Personal care surfactants (India):** India Personal Care Ingredients
  market = USD 461.02mn (2024) → USD 766.29mn (2033), CAGR 5.81% (2025-2033)
  (imarcgroup.com/india-personal-care-ingredients-market, search-snippet,
  2026-08-04). This ingredient market spans surfactants, conditioning
  polymers, rheology modifiers, emollients, emulsifiers and others; the same
  source states surfactants are "the largest share of the ingredient type"
  segment. Assumed at 35% of the ingredient-type mix (ASSUMPTION, L
  confidence, mid-point of a plausible 25-40% range for the largest single
  ingredient category): USD 461.02mn × 35% = USD 161.4mn × ₹95.3 ≈ **₹1,538 Cr**.

**Bottom-up combined TAM (India, home+personal care surfactants) ≈ ₹7,738 Cr +
₹1,538 Cr = ₹9,276 Cr** (2024 basis).

This sits below the Method 1 conservative figure (₹10,640 Cr), which — per the
conservative-bias rule — makes **₹9,276 Cr the operative conservative TAM**
used in Section 3 onward (rounded to **₹9,270 Cr**).

### Method 3 — Peer revenue aggregation + unorganised-sector estimate

Known organised-sector home/personal-care specialty surfactant suppliers with
disclosed India-relevant revenue:
- Galaxy Surfactants Ltd (largest listed Indian specialty surfactants/personal-
  care-ingredients maker; plants in India, Egypt, Netherlands): consolidated
  revenue ₹5,248.28 Cr, FY2025-26 (24.26% YoY growth from ₹4,223.67 Cr
  FY2024-25) (whalesbook.com / kotaksecurities.com, search-snippet,
  2026-08-04). Note: this is *global* consolidated revenue, not India-only —
  a meaningful portion is export/overseas-plant revenue, so it overstates the
  India-market contribution of this one peer; NOT FOUND: India-specific
  revenue split for Galaxy.
- Aarti Surfactants Ltd itself: ₹859.13 Cr, FY2026 (screener-Data_Sheet.csv).
- VVF (India) Ltd: oleochemicals-led (fatty acids, fatty alcohols, glycerine,
  soap noodles — largely upstream raw-material feedstock rather than finished
  specialty surfactants), 83% of revenue from the oleochemicals segment
  FY2025; VVF Group global revenue ~USD 863mn (2026) (rocketreach.co /
  icra.in, search-snippet, 2026-08-04) — India-entity-specific revenue for the
  finished-surfactant-comparable portion NOT FOUND.
- Other named players (Godrej Industries' surfactant/AOS line, KLK Oleo India,
  smaller regional manufacturers): revenue NOT FOUND at company-specific
  level.

Known/estimable organised revenue in this space ≈ ₹5,248 Cr (Galaxy, all-in) +
₹859 Cr (Aarti) ≈ **₹6,107 Cr**, materially incomplete given the NOT FOUND
names above. Indian detergent/surfactant-adjacent markets are typically
quoted with an unorganised share around 40% (organised brands "comprise 60% of
the total market," globenewswire.com/researchandmarkets.com detergent-market
coverage, search-snippet, 2026-08-04). Grossing up the known ₹6,107 Cr at an
assumed 55%/45% organised/unorganised split (mid-point, ASSUMPTION) implies a
total India home+personal-care surfactant revenue pool of ≈ ₹6,107 Cr ÷ 0.55 ≈
**₹11,100 Cr** — in the same order of magnitude as, and bracketing, the
Method 1/2 range (₹9,276 Cr - ₹15,801 Cr). This is a useful sanity check
despite its data gaps (Confidence: L, given the missing peer names).

### Method 4 — Import substitution

Attempted but **data NOT FOUND** at the level needed. India is simultaneously
described as "the largest importer" (24% share, Mar 2023-Feb 2024 shipments)
and "the largest exporter" (44% share, same window) of Linear Alkyl Benzene
(LAB) (volza.com, search-snippet, 2026-08-04) — but LAB is an upstream
feedstock intermediate for LABSA/LAS detergent-active production, not a
finished specialty surfactant, and no clean finished-surfactant trade-balance
or import-substitution dataset was found. This method is not used for TAM
sizing; the LAB trade dynamic is noted only as directional context (India is
both a meaningful surfactant-feedstock importer and exporter, i.e., not a
simple net-import-substitution story).

### Method 5 — Global benchmark / per-capita

Global surfactants market, 2025: cluster of current vendor estimates USD
47.4bn (Grand View Research) to USD 52.44bn (Next Move Strategy Consulting),
with most in the USD 49-51bn range (Fortune Business Insights USD 49.71bn;
Knowledge Sourcing USD 50.4bn; Research Nester USD 50.1bn; Globe Market
Research USD 49.5bn) — all search-snippet, accessed 2026-08-04. CAGR to 2030
quoted 4.5%-5.8% across vendors.

Using conservative USD 47.4bn (2025) ÷ ~8.2bn global population (standard UN
DESA-order-of-magnitude estimate, not independently re-verified this run) ≈
USD 5.78 per-capita surfactant spend. Applied to India's ~1.45bn population
(same caveat) → implied full-market India surfactants size at global-average
consumption ≈ USD 8.39bn (all applications) → × 55% home+personal filter ≈
USD 4.61bn × ₹95.3 ≈ **₹43,934 Cr**.

This is a "what if India consumed surfactants at the global per-capita average"
ceiling, roughly **4-5x** the current-consumption-based TAM estimates above —
it illustrates substantial headroom from under-penetration but is **not**
used as the near-term addressable TAM; it is carried into Section 4 (growth
drivers) as evidence for a penetration/per-capita growth driver, not into the
headline conservative/realistic TAM.

ingredient-market estimate built almost entirely from proxy application-share
assumptions rather than segment-specific primary data. The spread is
explained, not averaged away: Method 1 uses a blunter %-of-total-market
filter while Method 2 sizes each application bottom-up with its own
(different) source — the two disagreeing by ~15% is expected given
independent proxy assumptions, not a red flag.

artefact (global, all-application vs India, home+personal-care-only), not
evidence management is deliberately overstating its own opportunity — because
management makes **no explicit scoped claim at all**. That absence is itself
flagged.

## SECTION 3: SAM & SOM

### 3A. SAM

Applying the five filters to the conservative TAM (₹9,270 Cr):

| Filter | Discount applied | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Product fit | 90% | TAM already scoped to home+personal-care surfactants; further haircut for premium bio-based/naturals niche (AR's own "Growing Demand for Bio-Surfactants" section, Annual_Report_2022.pdf p.10) where B04 flags AARTISURF as a "commodity-input" manufacturer, not yet positioned in this premium sub-segment |
| Geography | 100% | TAM already India-scoped; export geography carried as a separate SAM-expansion lever (5D), not double-counted here |
| Channel | 100% | TAM is already an ingredient/B2B market (not a finished-goods retail market), so no channel mismatch |
| Customer | 70% | Two-customer concentration at 35.0% of FY2020-21 revenue (B04, AR p.80 Note 30) signals limited penetration beyond a narrow relationship set; assume ~30% of the customer-scoped TAM sits with FMCG majors locked into larger/global suppliers (Galaxy, multinational chemical majors) that AARTISURF cannot realistically win in the medium term |
| Capability | 80% | Two manufacturing sites (Pithampur MP, Silvassa) with no disclosed capacity/tonnage figures (B07: "No disclosed project name, timeline, or capacity target"); conservative haircut for finite, unquantified installed capacity |

Combined SAM % of TAM = 0.90 × 1.00 × 1.00 × 0.70 × 0.80 = **50.4%**

**SAM = ₹9,270 Cr × 50.4% ≈ ₹4,672 Cr**

Current SAM share = ₹859.13 Cr (FY2026 revenue) ÷ ₹4,672 Cr ≈ **18.4%**.
(This looks high next to typical small-cap TAM-share readings only because
SAM here has already been narrowly filtered down to the home+personal-care
ingredient niche; against the unfiltered India-total-surfactants market or
the global market, AARTISURF's share is well under 1%.)

### 3B. SOM at 3 and 5 years

Share-gain assumption: **+3pp by year 3, +5pp cumulative by year 5** —
the "aggressive" band (3-5pp), justified by (a) the capex/CWIP ramp evidenced
in B07 (CWIP ₹7.64 Cr FY24 → ₹14.65 Cr FY25 → ₹41.03 Cr FY26, ~11%
capex-embedded growth) signalling capacity is being added, and (b) a
meaningfully unorganised India surfactants base (Method 3, ~40-45%) that is
formalising — but capped at the "aggressive" ceiling (not the >5pp band, which
the rules reserve for competitor exit/acquisition, neither of which is
evidenced here) given the ongoing two-customer concentration constraint noted
above.

SAM growth assumption: 6.2% CAGR (Grand View Research's India CAGR, 2025-2033,
the realistic-case growth rate; used here for the funnel to avoid
double-counting conservative bias already embedded in the level).

- SAM (yr 3) = ₹4,672 Cr × (1.062)³ ≈ **₹5,597 Cr**
- SAM (yr 5) = ₹4,672 Cr × (1.062)⁵ ≈ **₹6,311 Cr**
- SOM share (yr 3) = 18.4% + 3pp = 21.4%
- SOM share (yr 5) = 18.4% + 5pp = 23.4%

**SOM (3yr) = 21.4% × ₹5,597 Cr ≈ ₹1,197 Cr**
**SOM (5yr) = 23.4% × ₹6,311 Cr ≈ ₹1,476 Cr**

Implied revenue CAGR from current ₹859.13 Cr (FY2026):
- 3yr: (1,197 / 859.13)^(1/3) − 1 ≈ **11.7%**
- 5yr: (1,476 / 859.13)^(1/5) − 1 ≈ **11.4%**

For context: the company's own disclosed historical CAGR is 14% over six
years (Investor_Presentation_1.pdf p.4, "Milestone" slide), and FY2025 revenue
grew 12% YoY (Investor_Presentation_1.pdf p.6-7) but with **negative operating
leverage** — EBITDA margin compressed from 10.56-10.70% (FY2024) to 7.53-7.56%
(FY2025) despite the revenue growth (B04 flag). The SOM-implied 11.4-11.7%
CAGR sits modestly below the historical 6-year 14% CAGR — a deliberately more
conservative forward read given the customer-concentration and weak-pricing-power
flags already carried from B04, while still assuming continued share gain
above the ~6.2% underlying market growth rate.

### 3C. Capacity cross-check

B07's capex-embedded growth estimate is ~11% (derived from the CWIP ramp,
FY24→FY26; no disclosed capacity/tonnage target, per B07 note).

Converting the SOM trajectory into a capacity-implied revenue path at the
disclosed 11% capex-embedded growth rate: ₹859.13 Cr × (1.11)³ ≈ ₹1,175 Cr
(yr 3) and ₹859.13 Cr × (1.11)⁵ ≈ ₹1,448 Cr (yr 5).

- Year 3 gap: SOM ₹1,197 Cr vs capex-implied ₹1,175 Cr → **gap of ₹22 Cr
  (≈1.9%)**
- Year 5 gap: SOM ₹1,476 Cr vs capex-implied ₹1,448 Cr → **gap of ₹28 Cr
  (≈1.9%)**

**capacity_check: sufficient.** The gap is small (<2% of SOM at either
horizon) and well within the noise of the underlying proxy assumptions used
throughout this report. Neither the SOM estimate nor the capex plan is
clearly the more optimistic side — they are closely matched. The real risk
flagged here is not a capacity shortfall but execution/demand risk: B07's
capex-embedded growth figure has "no disclosed project name, timeline, or
capacity target" (input gap carried from Stage 7), so this near-match should
be read as a coincidence of two independently-derived ~11% figures rather
than confirmed engineering-level capacity adequacy.

## SECTION 5: SUMMARY & RUNWAY

### 5A. Funnel

```
Global surfactants market (all applications, 2025):     USD 47.4bn (₹451,602 Cr)  [conservative]
  -> India surfactants market (all applications, 2024):  USD 2.03bn (₹19,346 Cr)   [conservative, IMARC]
      -> TAM (India, home+personal-care only, ~55% filter):     ₹9,270 Cr  [conservative, bottom-up triangulated]
          -> SAM (x 50.4%: product-fit x geog x channel x customer x capability):  ₹4,672 Cr
              -> SOM 3yr (21.4% of grown SAM):   ₹1,197 Cr  (implied CAGR ~11.7%)
              -> SOM 5yr (23.4% of grown SAM):   ₹1,476 Cr  (implied CAGR ~11.4%)
Current FY2026 revenue: ₹859.13 Cr (screener-Data_Sheet.csv, Sales, 2026-03-31 column)
```

### 5B. Runway assessment

- **Revenue headroom** = SAM ÷ current revenue = ₹4,672 Cr ÷ ₹859.13 Cr ≈
  **5.4x**
- **TAM growth rate** ≈ **6.2%** (Grand View Research India CAGR, 2025-2033 —
  mid-single-digit, not a high-growth secular category at the India-total
  level; global growth is slower still at 4.5-5.8%)
- **Company CAGR vs TAM:** historical company CAGR 14% (6-year, investor
  presentation) vs TAM CAGR ~6.2% → company has historically grown at roughly
  2x the market rate, i.e., **gaining share**, not merely riding the market.
  The forward SOM-implied CAGR (11.4-11.7%) embeds a continuation of this
  share-gain pattern, just at a lower multiple of TAM growth than the
  historical run-rate.
- **Years to saturate SAM at current (historical) growth differential:**
  solving ₹859.13 Cr × (1.14)^t = ₹4,672 Cr × (1.062)^t for t gives
  **t ≈ 24-25 years** — a long runway in absolute terms, consistent with a
  ~5x headroom multiple against a market growing faster than the company
  could realistically saturate it.

### 5C. Runway classification: **GOOD**

Headroom (5.4x) sits at the boundary between the GOOD (3-5x) and STRONG
(5-10x) bands, but TAM growth (6.2%) sits clearly in the GOOD band
(5-8%) rather than the STRONG band (8-15%) — growth is the binding,
lower-scoring leg. Combined with B04's flags on weak pricing power and
cyclicality (which temper the qualitative growth story even where the
quantitative headroom looks stronger), **GOOD** rather than STRONG or MASSIVE
is the more defensible classification. This is not a MASSIVE/land-grab TAM
story; it is a real but moderate, single-digit-growth, niche B2B ingredient
market where the company has demonstrated above-market historical share gain.

### 5D. SAM expansion levers actually being pursued
