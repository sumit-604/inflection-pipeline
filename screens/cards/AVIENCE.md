## Business Understanding Narrative

**What does it do, and how does it make money?** Avience makes and sells medical
diagnostics: in-vitro diagnostic kits, reagents and rapid tests. It also trades
goods and sells services. Its own filing describes revenue as coming from "Sales
of Medical Devices and Consumables" (PROSPECTUS, FY2027, Q1, page 367). The
company was founded in 2019 and listed on the NSE SME platform on 25 June 2026.

**What is changing?** Three things at once. The approved product count is to go
from 88 to 288. A new automated manufacturing facility is scaling. And the
revenue mix has swung almost entirely domestic: exports fell from 18.15% of
revenue in FY25 to 1.01% in FY26 (Investor Presentation, FY2027, Q2, page 28).

**Why now?** The company has put a number on the next year. It targets 60%
revenue growth in FY27, driven by capacity expansion, product launches and wider
distribution, and it names a recent Rs 47.46 crore government and PSU tender win
to be executed within three to four months (Investor Presentation, FY2027, Q2,
page 22). Against FY26 revenue of Rs 52.51 crore, that single order is 90% of a
year's sales.

**What must be true for the thesis to work?** The order must convert and repeat.
A Rs 47.46 crore tender executed in one quarter is not a run-rate; it is an
event. The thesis needs the 288-product portfolio to turn the tender channel
into recurring revenue.

**What breaks it?** Concentration in a tender channel that pays late. Government
and PSU buyers set the price, delay payment, and re-tender annually. A
diagnostics maker whose exports have collapsed to 1% and whose growth comes from
PSU tenders is a price-taker with a working-capital problem, not a brand.

## 2. Business model and archetype

Manufactured goods plus traded goods plus services, sold into Indian hospitals,
laboratories and government buyers. The prospectus is explicit that manufactured
exports carry the best margin: "Exports of Manufactured Goods typically offer
higher margins than domestic sales of manufactured goods or trading activities"
(RHP, FY2027, Q1, page 378). That sentence and the mix table together are the
most important pair on this card, and they point the wrong way. The company has
moved from its highest-margin channel to its lowest.

Operating leverage is real and disclosed. Other expenses fell from 9.41% of
revenue in FY23 to 6.92% in FY25, and turnover rose 2.6 times from Rs 10.92
crore in FY23 to Rs 28.89 crore in FY25 (RHP, FY2027, Q1, page 378).

**Archetype: build-to-spec component maker, diagnostics variant.** The binding
variables are approved product count, regulatory approvals, customer
qualification, and input pass-through.

## 3. Competitive advantages

Absent so far, on this corpus. The company's stated strengths are the product
portfolio and the new facility. Neither is a moat. There is no disclosed pricing
power, no contracted volume, no switching cost, and no scale advantage against
national diagnostics manufacturers.

The one asset that could become a moat is the regulatory approval stack. 88
approved products going to 288 is a real barrier if the approvals are hard and
the portfolio is broad enough to win a full tender basket. This corpus does not
say which regulator, which products, or how long approval takes. Treat it as an
issuer claim.

## 4. The promoters

Four promoters, named and unpledged. Dharam Deo Choudhary 50.57%, Ram Nagina
Choudhary 15.93%, Janardan Pal 10.43%, Deepa Choudhary 10.88% pre-issue,
totalling 87.81%, falling to 64.54% post-issue; with the promoter group,
87.89% pre-issue and 64.59% post-issue (PROSPECTUS, FY2027, Q1, pages 91 and
93).

The governance facts are clean. "None of the shares belonging to our Promoters
have been pledged till date." The entire promoter holding is locked in per SEBI
rules, and Rs 11,28,600 shares, 20.01% of post-issue equity, are locked for
three years (DRHP, FY2027, Q1, pages 90 and 91).

Related-party transactions are disclosed as director remuneration, salary and
wages, and loans taken and repaid, plus sales and purchases with promoter
entities (RHP, FY2027, Q1, page 378). Sales and purchases with related parties
in a company this small deserve a line-by-line read in a deep run.

Post-issue promoter holding of 64.59% is high. Skin in the game is not in doubt.

Margins are high for a diagnostics maker of this size and they improved while
revenue grew only 16%. That is the opposite of the usual small-cap pattern and
it deserves suspicion, not applause: the prospectus's own logic says the
high-margin export channel shrank to 1.01% in the same year that margin rose 320
basis points. Those two facts do not sit together comfortably. The deep run must
reconcile them.

ROCE, leverage and cash-conversion direction: **NOT FOUND.** No balance sheet or
cash flow statement appears in what this corpus holds. That is the largest gap
on this card.

**Inflection classification: margin-led, with the mechanism unexplained.**

## 6. The transition and the quality ladder

FROM R1 COMMODITY PRICE-TAKER. TO R3 VALUE-ADDED / SPEC'D SUPPLIER.

The claimed mechanism is the approval stack. A trader and small manufacturer of
commodity consumables sits at R1. A maker of 288 approved IVD products that a
hospital qualifies and then keeps buying sits at R3.

Management's claim, quoted once: "Target to expand to 288+ approved products
from 88+ currently" with "60% Revenue Growth" in FY27 (Investor Presentation,
FY2027, Q2, page 22).

This is a two-rung claim in one step, and the ladder's base rate is one rung per
two to three years. Per the rung-jump rule, a claimed multi-rung leap requires
extraordinary proof and is itself a red flag. Nothing in this corpus is
extraordinary proof. **Flag carried.**

## 8. Proof check

**The proof gate has not fired, and it cannot yet.** The company listed on 25
June 2026. The latest financial period in this corpus ends 31 March 2026, which
is before listing. No post-listing quarter exists to test. The two-period test
the framework requires cannot be run.

What can be said: FY26 delivered 16.1% revenue growth. The FY27 target is 60%.
The step from 16% to 60% rests on one named order and a facility, neither
observed converting.

The named absence: there is no quarter, anywhere in this corpus, that shows the
Rs 47.46 crore order being executed.

## 11. Posture: the Transition Decision Matrix

- **Proof gate: NOT FIRED.** No post-listing period exists.
- **Ugliness: ARTIFACT-OF-CLIMB, provisionally.** The genuinely ugly fact is the
  export collapse. Read as a deliberate reallocation to a large domestic tender
  opportunity, it is an artifact. Read as losing export customers, it is
  structural. **This corpus cannot tell the two apart**, and the classification
  is therefore provisional.
- **Recognition gap: OPEN.** Rs 179.20 crore of market capitalisation against
  FY26 profit of Rs 8.75 crore is 20.5 times trailing. That prices the delivered
  16% growth, not the targeted 60%.

**Posture: RESEARCH / WATCH.** Proof not fired, ugliness provisionally an
artifact, gap open. The framework's reading is exact: narrative until the proof
gate fires. Not a trade. Watch the gate.

## 12. Verdict card

**WATCH.** Not PROCEED. The reason is not the business, which screens well on
price and growth. It is that a full `/step1` run on a company with no balance
sheet, no cash flow, no credit rating and no post-listing quarter would spend
the heavy machinery on an issuer's own prospectus.

Load-bearing facts a deep run must verify first, once H1 FY27 reports:
1. **Whether the Rs 47.46 crore order was executed and collected.** Executed is
   half the test. Collected is the test.
2. **The FY26 balance sheet and cash flow.** Receivable days and net debt are
   unknown today.
3. **Why exports fell from 18.15% to 1.01%** while margin rose. Lost customers
   or chosen reallocation. Everything on this card turns on the answer.
4. **The approval count.** 88 approved products is a stated figure with no
   regulator named. Verify the number and the issuing authority.

What would change the view to PROCEED. One reported half-year showing the order
executed, receivable days under 120, and revenue tracking the 60% path. That
report is due around November 2026.

What would move it to PASS. Exports falling further with margin falling too,
which would show the FY26 margin was mix-timing rather than operating leverage.

No price. No position.

| Growth on FY26 | +60% |
| Market capitalisation | Rs 179.20 crore |
| **Forward PE** | **12.8x** |
| **Basis tier** | **A — Guided** |

Derivation. Management targets "60% Revenue Growth" for FY27 with a date and a
named driver set (Investor Presentation, FY2027, Q2, page 22), which is tier A
on the revenue leg. FY26 revenue of Rs 52.51 crore grown 60% is Rs 84.02 crore.
The FY26 PAT margin of 16.7% is held flat, which is an assumption and is
labelled as one: the company guides no margin. That gives FY27 PAT of Rs 14.0
crore.

Market capitalisation is the Bull AI company record at 22 September 2026, a
service record, not a filed document.

**The warning this step exists to carry.** 12.8x forward on 60% growth is the
cheapest growth-adjusted number in this run, and it is the least supported. The
revenue leg is guided but unproven by any post-listing period; the margin leg is
my assumption, not the company's; and the whole is built on one tender order
that may not repeat. Hold revenue growth at the delivered FY26 rate of 16.1%
instead, and FY27 PAT is Rs 10.2 crore, a forward PE of 17.6x on 16% growth.
That is the same company at the same price and it is an ordinary screen result.
Read this step with steps 8, 9 and 11, never instead of them.
