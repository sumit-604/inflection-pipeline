## Business Understanding Narrative

**What does it do, and how does it make money?** Antelopus Selan produces crude oil and gas from small onshore fields in India. It sells at international crude prices in US dollars (Q2 res, p.6, note 2). It holds nine contract areas in five basins (AR26, p.15). Five produce. Four sit in development (IndRa, p.3). Four Cambay fields carry the business: Bakrol and Karjisan sold 750 and 758 boepd of the 1,656 boepd total in Q2 FY27 (Q2 deck, p.6). The contracts leave the company more than 60% net entitlement (AR26, p.15). Costs are 80% to 85% fixed (IndRa, p.3). So each extra barrel falls almost straight to EBITDA.

**What is changing?** Ownership and ambition. Antelopus Energy, a private-equity-backed team founded in 2018, took control of Selan and merged into it (AR26, p.15). The merger record date was 5-Jul-2025 (Q4 res, p.8, note 6). Blackbuck Energy Investments went from 30.46% to 69.93% (AR26, p.146). The new team drills hard. Average sales rose from 1,107 boepd in Q2 FY26 to 1,656 boepd in Q2 FY27, and October ran at 1,950 boepd (Q2 deck, pp.6, 8). The plan is 2,500 boepd at the FY27 exit and 5,000 boepd by FY29 (Q2 deck, p.4).

**Why now?** H1 FY27 profit was Rs 111.61 crore against Rs 23.03 crore a year earlier (Q2 res, p.3). The ten-well Bakrol frac campaign finished in Q2 (Q2 deck, p.5). Seven new KG fields arrived in the DSF Round IV award, with contracts signed 22-Oct-2026 (Q2 res, p.7, note 9).

**What must be true?** Volumes must reach 2,500 boepd and keep climbing. The government must extend the Bakrol, Lohar and Karjisan licences past 2030 and Cambay past 2029 (AR26, p.16). Crude must not fall back hard from the levels that lifted FY27.

**What breaks it?** A licence extension that does not come, or comes on worse terms. A crude price reversal. Or wells that decline faster than new ones arrive: one key Bakrol well already "hit productivity issues" (Q2 deck, p.5).

## 2. Business model and archetype

One segment: production of oil and gas (Q2 res, p.6, note 3). The FY26 mix was about 82% oil and 18% gas (FY26 deck, p.4).

Licence years from AR26, p.16; Q2 split from Q2 deck, p.6.

Development assets: D-31 offshore Mumbai (to 2039), D-11 offshore Mahanadi (to 2039), Duarmara in Assam at 50% (to 2032) (AR26, p.16), Elao in Gujarat (IndRa, p.3). The new DSF IV blocks add seven KG fields and four Cambay discoveries (Q2 deck, pp.3, 7).

The margin comes from three things. First, fixed costs spread over more barrels. Second, favourable fiscal terms. Third, royalty: royalty and cess fell from Rs 54.9 crore in FY25 to Rs 44.1 crore in FY26 on higher revenue (FY26 deck, p.7). India Ratings credits a government cut in royalty rates (IndRa, p.3).

**Archetype: licence/scarcity business.** The variables are licence validity, renewal risk, a price the company does not set, and a capacity cap set by reservoir and approvals. Section 1B v3.7 Amendment 17 does not bind, because this is not a converter. But price exposure is total: the company takes the crude benchmark.

## 3. Competitive advantages

**Cost advantage: present, on mature fields.** The company works fields others left: frac, workovers, infill drilling. Its stated pillars are "revitalising mature oilfields" and "monetising stranded and discovered hydrocarbons" (AR26, p.15). Karjisan wells "pay back within 1 year" (Q2 deck, p.6). Bakrol has approval for "up to 55 more wells" (Q2 deck, p.6). That is a runway, not a moat.

**Scarcity: forming.** Licences are scarce. The company won "2 highly contested" DSF IV licences (Q1 deck, p.3). The new fields sit next to its own infrastructure, so capex should be lower (Q2 deck, p.7).

**Pricing power: absent.** The company sells at the international crude price (Q2 res, p.6, note 2).

**Verdict: a cost and execution edge is present; a moat is absent.** The edge is the team's playbook. It is not protected by the asset.

## 6. The transition and the quality ladder

**FROM: R1, commodity price-taker.** Pre-merger Selan ran a few mature Cambay fields. EBITDA margin was 44% in FY24 (IndRa, p.3). Volumes sat near 1,000 boepd (Q2 deck, p.4).

**TO: R2, cost-advantaged producer.** The claim is a multi-basin portfolio run at low cost per barrel, with margin from cost position, not price. The mechanism is fixed cost spread over rising volume, plus a fiscal regime above 60% entitlement.

Management's claim, once: "Road to 2,500 boepd in FY'27, entirely self-funded" (FY26 deck, p.4).

Ladder check. One rung. That fits the base rate. R2 asks for durable mid-teens ROCE. The screener record shows ROCE of 19.9% (market-data-2026-10-26.md, a service record). Durability rests on licence life, which is the open question in step 9.

## 8. Proof check

**The proof point is volume, and it moved.** Q2 FY27 sales of 1,656 boepd were 50% above Q2 FY26 (Q2 deck, p.8). October ran at 1,950 boepd (Q2 deck, p.6). Profit tracked: Q2 FY27 PAT was 4.9 times Q2 FY26 (Q2 res, p.3).

**Two softer signals.** First, Q2 volume was 2.9% below Q1. The deck had said frac impact would show "from Q2 onwards" (Q1 deck, p.4). It showed only from mid-Q2, and one key well underperformed (Q2 deck, p.5). Second, Karjisan fell from 991 boepd in Q4 FY26 to 834 in Q1 FY27 after "flash production" (Q1 deck, p.7). New-well flush is not the plateau.

**Name the gap.** Reaching 2,500 boepd at exit needs about 28% more than October's 1,950. Q3 and Q4 FY27 are the test. No transcript exists to test the claim in Q&A.

## 9. Flags from the documents

- **Licence life against booked life.** Bakrol, Lohar and Karjisan licences run to 2030, Cambay to 2029 (AR26, p.16). From 1-Oct-2025 management added a further ten years to the amortisation life of those fields "estimated on a conservative basis" (Q2 res, p.6, note 4). The auditor placed an emphasis of matter on this change (AR26, p.106). The 2P reserves are booked only to the current licence end (AR26, p.15). The producing Cambay fields hold 2.89 mmbbl of gross 2P oil (AR26, p.16). Most of the 10.68 mmbbl total is undeveloped Duarmara at 9.90 mmbbl (AR26, p.16).
- **Second emphasis of matter:** the FY25 restatement for the merger (AR26, p.106). Key audit matter: impairment of Rs 597.57 crore of oil and gas assets and CWIP, including "chances of extension of lease period" (AR26, p.107).
- **Synergia dispute.** Synergia announced that a shareholder "refused to sanction" the Cambay SPA. The company called that "factually misleading" and said it needs "to reassess the valuation parameters" (Synergia note, p.1). The Rs 4.73 crore exclusivity fee was written off (Q2 res, p.6, note 6). The operating partner on Cambay is now in public dispute.
- **Elao impairment** of Rs 10.00 crore, pending DGH approval (Q2 res, p.7, note 8).
- **One-offs in profit:** the Rs 6.50 crore cess refund (Q2 res, p.7, note 7).
- **Concentration.** Two fields carry 91% of Q2 sales (Q2 deck, p.6). India Ratings names this a weakness (IndRa, p.2).
- **Pay** at 12% of profit (step 4).
- **Dilution.** ESOP allotments of 1,25,462 shares in Q2; 83,076 vested options remain (Q2 res, p.6, note 5). Small.
- No qualification, no pledge, no related-party trade with the holding company.

## 10. Independent check: the credit rating

India Ratings assigned **IND A/Stable/IND A1** to Rs 300 crore of proposed bank facilities on 09-Jun-2026 (IndRa, p.1). This is the first rating in the corpus.

Strengths named: a sizeable reserve base, a diversified asset base, and EBITDA rising in FY27 (IndRa, p.2). The agency notes that in-place volumes of 195.13 mmbbl oil and 1,371.65 bcf gas far exceed 2P "because 2P reserves reporting is restricted to the duration of the current RSC/PSC validity period" (IndRa, p.3). It adds that "current production relative to reserves is quite low, making the ramp-up in production crucial" (IndRa, p.3).

Weaknesses: concentration in two assets, crude price dependence, and geological risk (IndRa, p.2). Its FY27 view leans on crude at "USD90-100/bbl in the short term because of the ongoing West Asia conflict" (IndRa, p.4).

Positive triggers include "extension of PSCs" (IndRa, p.4). So the agency treats the extension as an upside, not a given. The books treat it as a base case. The two readings differ, and the gap matters for step 13.

## 11. Posture: the Transition Decision Matrix

- **Proof gate: FIRED on volume.** Sales up 50% year on year, profit up nearly five times, self-funded (step 8). Not yet fired on the 2,500 boepd exit.
- **Ugliness: ARTIFACT-OF-CLIMB, with one structural question.** Negative FY26 free cash flow is capex for the climb, and it turned in H1 FY27. The licence end in 2029-2030 is structural until the extension is granted.
- **Recognition gap: CLOSED.** Market cap is Rs 4,195 crore and the price is 3.3 times its 52-week low (market-data-2026-10-26.md). The step 13 forward PE is about 19x. The R2 neighbourhood is 15x to 17x. The market already pays for the TO rung.

**Posture: EARNINGS-ONLY.** The climb is real and priced. Returns must come from EPS growth alone. If the licence extension is refused, the ugliness turns structural and the cell becomes AVOID.

## 12. Verdict card

**WATCH.**

The team delivers. Volume, cash and the rating all moved the right way. But three of the forward legs are not in hand: the extension, the crude price and the 2,500 boepd exit. The market has already priced the climb. A `/step1` now would spend its depth on a question that two quarters and one government decision will answer.

**Load-bearing facts to verify first**
1. The status of PSC extensions for Bakrol, Lohar, Karjisan and Cambay under the Oilfields (Regulation and Development) Amendment Act 2025 and the December 2025 rules (Q2 res, p.6, note 4). Without it, the ten-year amortisation assumption has no grant behind it.
2. Q3 and Q4 FY27 average sales against the 2,500 boepd exit (Q2 deck, p.6).
3. Realised price per barrel by quarter. The deck does not print it; India Ratings gives only FY averages (IndRa, p.4).
4. Who stands behind Blackbuck Energy Investments, and that fund's exit horizon (AR26, p.15).

**What would change the view**
- Up to PROCEED: a granted extension, plus Q3 FY27 sales above 2,200 boepd at a stable price.
- Down to PASS: an extension refused or offered on worse fiscal terms, or Q3 sales below Q2's 1,656 boepd.

## 13. Forward view

**Basis tier: B, run-rate**, cross-checked against a Tier A guide.

- **FY+1 (FY27) bottom line: about Rs 223 crore.** H1 FY27 PAT of Rs 111.61 crore (Q2 res, p.3), annualised. The two one-offs roughly offset: a Rs 10.00 crore impairment in Q1 against a Rs 6.50 crore cess refund in Q2, both before tax (Q2 res, p.7). The company files no seasonality warning; Q1's IOCL custody gap was "a once-a-year timing issue" (Q1 deck, p.5).
- **Tier A cross-check.** The deck guides FY27 EBITDA at more than twice FY26's Rs 167.5 crore, so above Rs 335 crore (Q2 deck, p.4). H1 delivered Rs 183.1 crore (Q2 deck, p.8). The run-rate is consistent with the guide.
- **Growth:** about 149% against FY26 PAT of Rs 89.61 crore (Q2 res, p.3).
- **Forward PE:** Rs 4,195 crore / Rs 223 crore = **about 18.8x**. Market cap from screener.in, 26-Oct-2026, a service record, not a filed document.

**Read it with steps 5, 8 and 9.** Three things flatter this number. Crude in H1 sat on a West Asia premium that India Ratings calls short term (IndRa, p.4). The amortisation change adds about Rs 29 crore of pre-tax profit a half-year (Q2 res, p.6, note 4). And H2 volume growth, if delivered, would push profit above the run-rate. The first two are the downside to the estimate. The third is the upside. At USD 69/bbl and without the amortisation change, the multiple would be materially higher; that number is not computed here.
