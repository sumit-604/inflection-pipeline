# Foil converter scaling fast, cash conversion unproven

GSM Foils makes aluminium pharma foils, the blister and strip foil that pharmaceutical companies pack tablets and capsules in, out of Vasai near Mumbai. Two promoters run it, Sagar Bhanushali as Chairman, Whole Time Director and CFO in one seat, and Mohansingh Parmar as Managing Director. The company listed on the NSE Emerge SME board in May 2024. FY26 revenue was Rs 258 Cr, PAT was Rs 20 Cr, and the shares trade near Rs 278 for a market value around Rs 391 Cr. A new ROPP caps line started in June 2026.

The story on offer is a multi plant scale up. Vasai runs near full, an Ahmedabad plant is ramping, ROPP caps Unit 3 commenced through an MoU with AAPL Solutions, and an export unit called Unit 4 is referenced but not yet confirmed in any filing. Management guides FY27 revenue to Rs 400 to 450 Cr, roughly a doubling off a base with no cross quarter track record yet. Q1 FY27 revenue was Rs 97 Cr, which tracks the exit run rate but has not shown acceleration.

The backward scorecard reads AVOID at 71 of 160, and it helps to see why. Return on capital scored full marks and growth scored full marks. The score is dragged down by cash conversion, which scored zero, and by data gaps tied to a company two years post listing with no pre IPO history to source. AVOID here is a backward data label, not an investment call.

Operating cash flow has been negative for three straight years while reported profit rose every year. FY25 operating cash flow reconstructs to about minus Rs 17 Cr against reported PAT of Rs 9.65 Cr, and that reconstruction exists only because the cash flow statement is absent from the filed annual report. Trade receivables ran from Rs 7 Cr to Rs 34 Cr to about Rs 94 Cr across FY24 to FY26, faster than the 93 to 228 percent revenue growth, funded by short term borrowings up 295 percent. Cash on the balance sheet fell to Rs 0.24 Cr.

Management earns a C on credibility. It beat its own FY26 revenue guide of Rs 230 to 250 Cr. Against that, it guided Ahmedabad utilisation at 40 to 50 percent twice and delivered 25 to 30 percent with no explanation, its Ahmedabad monthly revenue missed its own guide by about half, and a Lamitubes backward integration plan was announced then quietly reversed. Direct questions about cash generation were deflected across two calls.

Only one supplied peer, CGRAPHICS, is a true segment comparison. CGRAPHICS confirms the industry backdrop GSM describes, the March 2026 aluminium spike, the Hindalco linked pass through, 90 to 150 day payment terms from large pharma, and negative operating cash flow as a standing condition. It also complicates the margin claim, since CGRAPHICS ran EBITDA near 10.5 percent in the same window GSM reports 11.5 percent. GSM specific claims, a top three position among Tier 2 converters, a Rs 4 to 6 Cr capex threshold, and a merchant exporter channel to Yemen, Vietnam, Europe and the US, are unverifiable against any supplied source.

The addressable market is genuinely large. The served market is around Rs 5,120 Cr, GSM holds roughly 5 percent, and revenue headroom is close to 20 times. The caveats are real too. Management's cited TAM figures are the wrong denominator, 30 to 100 times larger than the market GSM actually serves, and the claim that the market doubles in five years is not corroborated by any independent series, which show 1.5 to 1.7 times growth over the relevant spans.

The disclosure picture scores 4 of 10. The cash flow statement, the statement of changes in equity, the contingent liabilities note and the capital commitments note are all absent from the FY25 annual report despite three certifying parties attesting to two of them. A related party, Sanjiya Metal Corporation held by a KMP, appears in no RPT table while the auditor's CARO clause certifies full RPT disclosure. A legacy GSM Foils LLP tax liability is being settled through directors' personal accounts, unquantified. On the other side, there is zero promoter pledge across two dilutive raises, and domestic institutions entered the register from zero to 2.46 percent. Promoter holding fell from 73 percent to 54 percent over the same window, and the mechanism of the June 2026 step down remains unconfirmed.
