### 2B. Excuse pattern analysis

There is exactly one miss with a clear excuse-pattern read (item #1 above: the
revenue decline) and it falls into the **silence** category — no external blame, no
self-credit, no admission, nothing. This is distinct from and arguably a bigger
information gap than "external-blame-heavy," because investors relying solely on
these filings get zero causal narrative for a ~19% top-line decline.

By contrast, the one clearly new, negative, non-forecastable item that did appear
during the year — the New Labour Codes' Rs.46.26 lakh exceptional gratuity
cost — was handled with unusual proactive specificity: management named the exact
mechanism ("change in the definition of 'wages'"), quantified the impact precisely
across two consecutive filings (Rs.45.17 lakh recognised in Q3, revised to Rs.46.26
lakh full-year in Q4), and stated they would keep monitoring rule notifications and
adjust accounting treatment as needed (Q3 FY26 results, Note 2; Q4 FY26 results, Note
2). This is **external-blame that is legitimately external** (a government-mandated
labour law consolidation) and disclosed with more transparency than the revenue miss
received.

Net pattern: **honest and proactive on a small, externally-forced, easily
quantified cost item; silent on the large, harder-to-explain revenue miss.** No
instance of "we made a mistake" language exists in any source reviewed. No hard
topic (the revenue decline) was raised proactively; it was not raised at all in the
sources available.
