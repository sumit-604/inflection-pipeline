## BUSINESS UNDERSTANDING NARRATIVE

Two businesses sit inside one listed company. The Extrusion Machinery Division made 69.8 percent of FY26 revenue, Rs 314.89 Cr of Rs 451.05 Cr (AR FY26 Note 38 p.105-106). It designs and assembles the machines that turn plastic granules into pipe, blown film, sheet and compounds, and a pipe maker cannot make pipe without one (AR FY26 p.35-36). The Battery Division, called Geon and formerly Battrixx, made 30.2 percent of FY26 revenue, Rs 136.11 Cr (AR FY26 Note 38 p.105-106). It buys lithium ion cells from global suppliers, designs the pack, the battery management system and the housing, and assembles at Chakan near Pune. It does not make cells (AR FY26 p.36). Extrusion customers are pipe, film and flexible packaging makers, mostly Indian, buying engineer to order machines against an advance and milestone payments (AR FY26 p.35-36); exports were Rs 57.52 Cr, 12.8 percent of FY26 revenue (AR FY26 Note 38 p.106). Battery customers are electric two and three wheeler makers, plus retail buyers of a direct to consumer inverter battery launched in FY26 (AR FY26 p.4-5, p.36). Concentration has already cost money: one customer was 19.11 percent of FY26 revenue, and Hero Electric, owing Rs 30.39 Cr, entered NCLT insolvency in December 2024 (AR FY26 Note 38 p.106; Note 9 p.87-88). Neither annual report gives churn, retention or qualification lock in data, so switching costs are not established.

Machine demand today rides on water infrastructure spending, and the company names Jal Jeevan Mission disbursement delays as the cause of its FY26 decline, tracked here as the downstream candidate Jal Jeevan Mission 2.0 fund disbursement pace (AR FY26 p.36). Battery demand today rides on India EV retail registrations, up 24.6 percent in FY26, tracked as the downstream candidate India EV retail registrations by category (AR FY26 p.32-33). Forward, the two markets size to Rs 6,135 Cr conservative and Rs 10,480 Cr realistic and grow about 15.9 percent a year, against a served share of 7.35 percent and 13.6 times revenue headroom. Three outside signals test that runway: Union Budget capex allocation on the Jal Jeevan Mission and PLI-ACC lines, PLI-ACC and PM E-DRIVE disbursement milestones, and the lithium carbonate and cell price index. The company is not capturing the market yet. Consolidated revenue fell 5.45 percent in FY26 while both cited markets grew, and the battery line is smaller than it was two years ago: segment revenue fell 52.3 percent from Rs 266.16 Cr in FY24 to Rs 126.98 Cr in FY25, then printed Rs 136.11 Cr in FY26 (AR FY25 Note 38 p.104; AR FY26 Note 38 p.105-106). The emerging moat scan scored 9 of 92 and classified NONE, and the machine line carries no moat: the ~40 percent market share claim appears only in the FY25 report and gross margin fell from 38.9 percent to 35.64 percent (AR FY25 p.37; AR FY26 p.37). The battery line carries none either, because management calls its own model technology agnostic and asset light, a design any entrant can copy, and the Dec 2023 deck claims of ARAI AIS-156 accreditation, an IATF approved facility and a 90 percent plus design win rate appear in neither annual report (AR FY26 p.36; Dec 2023 deck p.16, p.22). The last two scan categories agree: category 21, cannibalisation barrier, found no evidence, and category 22, regulatory tailwinds, scored weak because Jal Jeevan Mission 2.0, PM E-DRIVE and PLI-ACC reach every competitor equally.

## Machine cash funds a halved battery business

Kabra Extrusion Technik made Rs 451.05 Cr of revenue in FY26, down 5.45 percent, and lost Rs 5.37 Cr consolidated and Rs 2.44 Cr standalone (AR FY26 p.37, p.122, p.17). The 43 year old extrusion machinery business still earns: segment result Rs 50.75 Cr on Rs 314.89 Cr of segment revenue, 16.1 percent, down from 19.3 percent (AR FY26 Note 38 p.105-106). The six year old battery business does not: segment result minus Rs 43.35 Cr on Rs 136.11 Cr of segment revenue, a loss ratio of 31.9 percent against 20.1 percent the year before (AR FY26 Note 38 p.105-106). About Rs 250 Cr is sunk into the roughly 7 GWh Chakan plant, and management puts its revenue ceiling at Rs 1,500 Cr plus at optimal levels, which the FY26 run rate fills to about 9 percent (AR FY26 p.36, p.37). Capex has been commissioned: capital work in progress fell from Rs 50.35 Cr to Rs 12.21 Cr and depreciation rose 89.9 percent to Rs 29.57 Cr (AR FY26 p.83; Note 28 p.94).

The battery growth number does not mean what the report implies. Battery segment revenue fell 52.3 percent between FY24 and FY25, from Rs 26,616.45 lakh to Rs 12,698.12 lakh, and the segment result swung from a profit of Rs 651.55 lakh to a loss of Rs 2,553.28 lakh (AR FY25 Note 38 p.104). Neither annual report narrates that collapse. The FY25 report instead calls Geon a business "gaining traction from EV OEMs" in the same document (AR FY25 p.38, p.4). The FY26 report's 7.2 percent battery growth therefore runs off a base that had already halved. It is also not a like for like number: the segment revenue basis changed between the two reports without disclosure, because the FY25 comparative of Rs 48,983.14 lakh includes other income and the FY26 figure of Rs 45,099.83 lakh excludes Rs 2,367.55 lakh of it (AR FY26 Note 38 p.105 against P&L p.67 and Note 23 p.95; AR FY25 Note 38 p.104). The same defect makes the 13.2 percent extrusion decline not like for like.

Gate 0 scored the backward record 22 of 100 on the core and 25 of 160 grand total, and classified it AVOID. This is the backward scorecard class, not a valuation decision, and no valuation has run. Both would read worse if FY26 repeats. The cause is narrow and traceable, not a broad quality collapse. Take the battery segment out and the machine business is still profitable.
