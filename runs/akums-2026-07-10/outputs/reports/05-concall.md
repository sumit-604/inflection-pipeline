WEAKENING/GUIDANCE MISS, with the explanation (deliberate deprioritization of certain HQs) surfacing only under questioning, not proactively.
- **European contract timeline**: Q2 FY26 "commercial supplies around March/April 2027" → Q3 & Q4 FY26 calls reframe as "FY28" (which begins April 2027) — consistent once fiscal-year framing is applied, not a real slip, but the switch from calendar to fiscal-year language across calls could mislead a reader who doesn't track precisely.

## SECTION 3: COMPETITIVE INTELLIGENCE FROM CONCALLS

### 3A. Management commentary on competitors, with credibility check

Management rarely names competitors directly. The one notable instance: in the Q2 FY26 call, analyst Ankit Minocha noted that "smaller peers with 20% of your market are reporting slight margin expansion" versus Akums' sharp margin decline. Management's response ("I think it's fair, while we are continuously monitoring our gross margins, which continue to do better than our peers...") conceded the point rather than dismissing it, and gave a granular quantified explanation (new-plant EBITDA drag of INR17cr in H1). This is a credibility-positive response — acknowledges rather than deflects a direct peer-comparison challenge. No competitor was named by ticker or company name in any of the three transcripts.

### 3B. Industry and market intelligence

- **IPM (Indian Pharma Market) volume growth**: flat in Q2 FY26; ~1%, 1.5% cited in Q3 and Q4 FY26 calls as the overall market backdrop against which Akums' double-digit CDMO growth is being measured — a large divergence flagged for peer verification (Section 4B).
- **API price trend**: top-200 APIs down ~8% YoY in Q2 FY26; full-FY26 basket down ~20-25% (Q4 FY26 call); cephalosporin-specific COGS improved from ~91% (Q2 FY26) to ~87% (Q3 FY26); early FY27 (Q1) prices rebounded "single high-digits" due to Middle East-linked crude/solvent inflation (per analyst Sajal Kapoor, confirmed by management in Q4 FY26 call).
- **Schedule M enforcement**: implementation deadline (Dec 31, 2025) has passed; management declines to characterize on-ground impact (Q3, Q4 FY26 calls) despite earlier framing it as a tailwind (Q2 FY26 call).
- **MIP (Minimum Import Price) on Pen G / 6-APA**: newly implemented (raised in Q3 FY26 call); management characterizes as industry-wide, not expected to be a differentiator for Akums specifically.
- **Cough syrup regulatory scrutiny** (2025 "syrup incident"): drove demand surge and "alternate vendor development" (AVD) inquiries into Akums (Q2 FY26 call) — a positive but unquantified tailwind, never revisited with hard numbers in Q3 or Q4.
- **GLP-1 market**: acknowledged as "large and massive opportunity" but pricing "very volatile and going down south" — management still evaluating entry (Q4 FY26 call only).
- **Zambia pharma market**: ~US$200m currently (mostly imported, government procures >80%), expected to grow to over US$300m by 2030 (Q2 FY26 call).

### 3D. Customer and order book signals

- CDMO customer base steady at >1,500 customers (Q2 and Q4 FY26 calls), KAM-based sales model with 100+ sales staff, ~15-20 clients per KAM (Q2 FY26 call).
- Extraordinary Q3/Q4 FY26 volume growth attributed explicitly to "existing customers only... not new geographies or new customers" (Sahil Maheshwari, Q4 FY26 call) — a concentration signal: growth is coming from wallet-share deepening, not customer acquisition diversification.
- European CDMO: single MNC partner, sole supplier from Plant 2 for one established oral-liquid brand, six-year-plus contract to December 2032 — a single-customer/single-plant concentration for a key growth pillar.
- Pipeline of 8-10 European dossiers (Q2 FY26 call), evolving into a target of "eight to ten global customers" for CDMO over the next 2-3 years (Q4 FY26 call) — early-stage, uncontracted pipeline, not yet wins.
- Trade generics: deliberate, multi-year scale-down from ~INR400cr+ five years ago to just INR100cr in FY26 — a conscious "capital reallocation" exit disclosed candidly in Q4 FY26 call (Avnish Tiwari question), not a customer loss under duress.
- Zambia: single government/tender counterparty relationship, US$50m cumulative over the first two years, explicit acknowledgment that "local manufacturers will get an upper hand in tender bidding" once the local plant is operational — a geographic/counterparty concentration risk.

**Governance note (not a formal red flag, flagged for other stages)**: The Q3 FY26 call management roster lists both "Mr. Sanjeev Jain – Managing Director" and "Mr. Sandeep Jain – Managing Director" (co-MD structure), while the Q2 and Q4 FY26 call rosters list only Sandeep Jain as MD. This appears to be a one-quarter listing anomaly or a temporary co-MD arrangement; worth a governance/related-party cross-check in later stages.

### 4B. Questions for peer verification (formal handoff to Stage 6)

1. **Question**: Akums cites Indian Pharma Market (IPM) volume growth as flat in Q2 FY26 and ~1-1.5% in Q3/Q4 FY26 calls, versus its own reported double-digit CDMO volume growth (16%+ Q3, 25%+ variance Q4) in the same period. **Why it matters**: a large divergence between claimed market growth and company growth is either genuine share gain or a sign of one-off/restocking effects not disclosed. **Check peers**: Innova Captab (INNOVACAP) and Cohance (COHANCE) as closest CDMO comparables; Windlas Biotech (WINDLAS) for domestic-formulation volume trend cross-check.
2. **Question**: Management claims top-200 API basket prices fell ~8% YoY in Q2 FY26 and ~20-25% over full FY26, with a subsequent single-high-digit rebound in early FY27 attributed to Middle East-linked crude/solvent inflation. **Why it matters**: this raw-material trend directly drives Akums' cost-plus CDMO/API margins; independent corroboration is needed. **Check peers**: Piramal Pharma (PPLPHARMA) and Cohance (COHANCE) for API/raw-material cost trend and cephalosporin-specific pricing; Innova Captab (INNOVACAP) for comparability of cost-plus CDMO margin impact.
3. **Question**: Management states the extraordinary CDMO volume growth is broad-based from existing customers (not new customer wins), and speculates (without confirming) that it may reflect share shift from smaller/non-compliant manufacturers ahead of Schedule M enforcement. **Why it matters**: this is the single largest unresolved driver behind FY26's earnings beat and management itself says the cause "is still to be thought through." **Check peers**: Innova Captab (INNOVACAP) and Windlas Biotech (WINDLAS) as domestic CDMO/formulation peers — if they report similar volume acceleration, it confirms an industry-wide Schedule M effect; if not, it points to an Akums-specific (and less durable, or less explicable) gain.
4. **Question**: Akums guided FY26 capex of INR222cr (delivered) and an FY27 target of INR300cr, largely for oral-solids expansion, injectable-facility ramp-up and European-market readiness, alongside a US$45m Zambia JV investment (51% share) and the EUR35m/yr European CDMO contract. **Why it matters**: establishes whether Akums is capex-cycling ahead of demonstrated demand or in line with a genuine sector-wide capacity cycle. **Check peers**: Innova Captab (INNOVACAP) and Cohance (COHANCE) for whether the broader CDMO sector is in a similar capacity-expansion cycle.
