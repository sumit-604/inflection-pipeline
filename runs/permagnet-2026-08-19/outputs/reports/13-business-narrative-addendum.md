# BUSINESS UNDERSTANDING NARRATIVE

Permanent Magnets Ltd makes precision magnetic and current-sensing parts that sit inside other companies' products. Electricity-meter components bring about 40% of sales: shunts, current transformers, and flux concentrators that let a meter measure and bill current accurately. Auto sensing parts bring about 23%, magnetic assemblies that sense current, torque, and position inside vehicles. Specialty alloys bring about 10%, nickel, cobalt, and ZAMAK grades carrying AS9100D aerospace certification, and a mixed 16% comes from renewables, electrical, medical, aerospace, and food lines. Each part is built to a customer's own specification, and the customer qualifies it before buying, so a rival must be re-tested from the start.

Customers are original equipment makers, not end users. The largest disclosed class is the top-3 global electricity-meter manufacturers, though the run did not name them. The company says it supplies about 50% of tier-1 global auto manufacturers, and alloys go to oil and gas, aerospace, and industrial buyers. Long qualification cycles lock a supplier in once approved, and customer concentration was not disclosed.

Present demand rides India's RDSS smart-meter rollout, run by the Ministry of Power and REC, which pulls metering-component orders. 65 million of a 250 million meter target were installed as of April 2026, and Genus Power Infrastructures, India's largest listed installer at about 30% share, is the closest listed proxy for that pull-through. Global light-vehicle and EV production drives the auto line. The China NdFeB rare-earth licence regime, set by MOFCOM, gates the subsidiary's planned magnet line, and REL Developments Ltd of the UK licenses the latching-relay technology behind a planned new line.

Forward demand ties to signals a person can check: 185 million meters were still to install toward a March 2028 completion date, auto and EV volumes track through SIAM and global vehicle trackers, and MOFCOM policy decides whether the NdFeB line can ship at all. The moat sits in the mature lines: metering components and alloys hold qualification lock-in and held certifications, rated moderate and present in the moat scan. The RDSS tailwind is real but sector-wide, not company-specific, since listed peers ride the same rollout. Relays are pre-commercial, guided to H2FY27 and already behind their first timeline, so no relay moat is proven yet. The NdFeB magnet line booked zero revenue in FY26 and stays pre-commercial, so it carries no proven moat today.
