## HEADLINE

Roofs margin surge carries a mixed group.

Standalone revenue Rs 824.32 Cr, up 10.0% year on year (Q1 FY26 Rs 749.18 Cr) and up 31.9% versus the balancing-figure Q4. Standalone operating EBITDA Rs 88.32 Cr against Rs 35.47 Cr, margin 10.71% against 4.73%, roughly a 600 bps step up. Standalone PAT Rs 49.69 Cr against Rs 19.68 Cr, EPS Rs 65.89 against Rs 26.10. The profit is operations driven, not other income: other income fell to Rs 9.01 Cr from Rs 21.74 Cr, and core PBT excluding other income went to Rs 58.21 Cr from Rs 4.97 Cr.

Consolidated tells the harder truth. Consolidated revenue Rs 1,174.02 Cr, up 11.6%, consolidated PAT only Rs 9.40 Cr against a Rs 1.32 Cr loss a year ago. The gap between Rs 49.69 Cr standalone PAT and Rs 9.40 Cr consolidated PAT is the overseas flooring business. The auditor states six subsidiaries carried a net loss after tax of Rs 39.61 Cr for the quarter (consol review report para 7, L1030-1038).

Read: three moving parts.

1. Roofs, the fibre cement roofing legacy, is the engine. Revenue up 16.9% and PBT up 52.2%, margin widening from 13.3% to 17.4%. On roughly Rs 223 Cr of net capital it earned Rs 89.68 Cr in one quarter. This one segment produces more than the group's entire consolidated PBT.

2. Pipes and Walls are improving. Walls PBT margin more than doubled to 5.4%. Pipes narrowed its loss to Rs 5.34 Cr from Rs 14.61 Cr, consistent with integration of the five amalgamated pipe companies.

3. Floors (Parador, the German laminate and flooring operation) is the drag and it worsened. Revenue up 12.1% but the loss doubled to Rs 37.41 Cr from Rs 17.95 Cr, margin from minus 5.9% to minus 11.0%. Floors ties up Rs 1,261 Cr of segment assets and Rs 941 Cr of segment liabilities, Rs 320 Cr net, the group's largest capital block, and it loses money every quarter.

The capital-allocation contrast frames the strategic action below: the profitable, capital-light domestic building-products business subsidises a capital-heavy loss-making European flooring business.

## STRATEGIC AND STRUCTURAL ITEMS

- **New capex approved (Annexure I, Board Outcome).** Greenfield Fibre Cement Board plant at Hyderabad. Addition 72,000 metric tons per annum on an existing 1,32,000 TPA base, a 54.5% capacity increase. Estimated investment Rs 167 Cr including land, funded by internal accruals and borrowings, to be added within 24 months. Existing utilisation stated at 80%. This adds to the winning Roofs and Walls domain, so the capital direction matches where the returns are.
- **Parador impairment already taken.** FY26 carried a Rs 74.20 Cr exceptional provision for diminution in the value of the investment in BirlaNu International GmbH, formerly HIL International GmbH, the Parador holding entity (Note 5b, L378-382), partly offset by a Rs 39.41 Cr gain on assets held for sale. The write-down flags continuing impairment risk on the European flooring business, which the segment numbers above confirm is still deteriorating.
- **Clean Coats amalgamation pending.** Scheme to merge Clean Coats Private Limited (already a wholly owned subsidiary from 11 Nov 2025) into BirlaNu is before the NCLT. Hyderabad bench listed 13 Aug 2026, Mumbai bench final hearing 1 Oct 2026 (Note 7). Not yet in these numbers on a merged basis.
- **Pipes amalgamation effective.** Five pipe companies (Crestia Polytech, Topline Industries, Aditya Poly Industries, Aditya Polytechnic, Prabhu Sainath Polymers) merged w.e.f. 5 Apr 2024, effective 10 Mar 2026, pooling of interest, prior periods restated (Note 8). The transferor set posted Q1 FY26 revenue Rs 37.30 Cr and a Rs 8.17 Cr loss, so it was margin dilutive at entry; the narrowing Pipes loss is the integration playing out.
- **Supercor Nigeria.** Dormant since November 2015, carried at NIL after a Rs 1.43 Cr provision, RBI unique identification number suspended (Note 9). Legacy tail, immaterial.
- **Identity.** HIL Limited renamed BirlaNu Limited (CK Birla group). Scrip BIRLANU, BSE code 509675. Paid-up equity Rs 7.57 Cr, face value Rs 10, unchanged across all periods, so EPS moves are earnings, not share count.

## SYMMETRIC READ

**Bull.** Standalone operating EBITDA margin up about 600 bps to 10.71% and one quarter now exceeds all of FY26. Roofs, the largest and most profitable segment, is compounding both revenue (up 16.9%) and margin (up about 400 bps). Pipes losses are halving and Walls margins are doubling, so three of four segments are moving the right way. The profit is operational, not other income, and there is no exceptional item flattering the standalone number. Fresh Rs 167 Cr capex is directed at the highest-return part of the business.

**Bear.** Consolidated PAT is only Rs 9.40 Cr because Floors (Parador) lost Rs 37.41 Cr in the quarter, a loss that doubled year on year, and it consumes the largest single block of group capital at Rs 320 Cr net. The Rs 74.20 Cr FY26 impairment on the same European entity says the problem is structural, not seasonal. Pipes is still loss-making. Q4 comparability is limited by the balancing-figure caveat, and the new capex is part-funded by borrowings against a business whose consolidated free cash generation is held back by the European drag.

## FLAGS FOR THE OPERATOR (decision stays human)

1. **Parador / Floors deterioration.** Quarterly loss doubled to Rs 37.41 Cr and margin fell to minus 11.0% on Rs 320 Cr of net capital, one quarter after a Rs 74.20 Cr impairment. Watch for a further write-down or a disposal or restructuring decision. This is the single largest determinant of consolidated earnings.
2. **Standalone vs consolidated divergence.** Rs 49.69 Cr standalone PAT versus Rs 9.40 Cr consolidated PAT. Any thesis must be built on the consolidated figure, because that is where the flooring loss lives.
3. **Capex funding and leverage.** Rs 167 Cr greenfield, part debt funded. No cash flow statement is in a quarterly Reg 33 filing, so net debt movement is not verifiable here. Confirm against the balance sheet and any concall guidance when available.
4. **Clean Coats merger.** NCLT hearings 13 Aug and 1 Oct 2026. Track the effective date and the pro-forma effect, since Clean Coats currently contributes to standalone other income via dividend.
