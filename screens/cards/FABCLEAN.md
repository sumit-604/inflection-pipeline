## Business Understanding Narrative

**What does it do, and how does it make money?** Fabtech builds cleanrooms
turnkey. It designs and installs the partitions, the ceilings and the precision
HVAC that make a room clean enough to manufacture a drug, a solar cell or a
semiconductor in. It reports its order flow by two business verticals,
cleanroom partitions and HVAC systems, and by customer sector (Corporate
announcement 12-Jun-2026, page 1). The money is project margin on turnkey
contracts.

The company grew up on pharmaceuticals. The prospectus says so plainly:
"Currently, the majority of our revenue is derived from pharmaceuticals and
allied industries" (RHP, page 144).

**What is changing?** The customer. In the order book at 31 May 2026,
renewable energy is Rs 184.42 crore, pharma Rs 135.18 crore, data centres
Rs 27.17 crore and semiconductors Rs 0.50 crore, of a consolidated Rs 354.74
crore. Renewable energy is now 52% of the book and pharma is 38%. In the
pipeline of Rs 541.16 crore under active consideration, data centres alone are
Rs 250.00 crore (Corporate announcement 12-Jun-2026, page 1).

The company states the strategy directly: "Evolving from pharma-centric
operations to multi-sector capabilities, including data centers, solar
manufacturing, semiconductors, and more" and "From cleanrooms to complete
precision HVAC, AHU, and specialized design-engineered systems. Adding MEP
capability" (Investor Presentation FY2026 Q2, page 10).

**Why now?** Because the order book is compounding fast and the company
publishes it monthly. It secured Rs 70.82 crore of orders in April 2026 and
Rs 153.05 crore in May 2026. The book went from Rs 229.25 crore at 30 April to
Rs 354.74 crore at 31 May, a 55% rise in one month.

**What must be true for the thesis to work?** The new sectors must pay like the
old one. This is the load-bearing question of the whole card and step 6 sets it
out.

**What breaks it?** Solar capex. Renewable energy is a cyclical, price-fought
capital-goods customer. Pharma cleanrooms are validated, regulated and sticky.
A book that shifts from one to the other is growing and may be getting worse at
the same time.

## 2. Business model and archetype

Turnkey project execution. Revenue is contracts won multiplied by execution
pace. Two verticals: cleanroom partitions took Rs 112.40 crore of the May order
intake and HVAC systems Rs 40.65 crore (Corporate announcement 12-Jun-2026,
page 1).

Growth is partly bought. The company has acquired 26% of Advantek, which makes
air handling units, and subscribed to compulsorily convertible preference
shares in Kelvin representing 33.33% on conversion; Kelvin integrates critical
HVAC (RHP, page 144). The consolidated FY26 total income of Rs 221.72 crore
against standalone Rs 143.39 crore shows that 35% of the group's income sits
outside the parent (Investor Presentation, page 21).

**Archetype: order-book business.** Order inflow, book-to-bill, execution pace,
working capital and margin on backlog are the variables.

## 3. Competitive advantages

Partial. In pharma, a cleanroom contractor with a validated track record is
hard to displace mid-programme, and the prospectus leans on exactly that:
"track record delivering of project execution, deep industry knowledge...
integrated manufacturing process and support of larger group company(ies)"
(RHP, page 144). Integration into air handling units through Advantek and
Kelvin is a real cost and delivery advantage.

In renewable energy and data centres the company is a new entrant bidding
against incumbents. Moat: present in pharma, absent in the sectors that now
carry the book.

## 6. The transition and the quality ladder

Here is the honest reading, and it is not the flattering one.

FROM R3, a value-added spec'd supplier to pharmaceutical customers, where
validation and regulatory qualification give real switching cost. TO a
multi-sector turnkey contractor whose largest customer group is now renewable
energy capex.

**That may be a step down the ladder, not up.** Solar module and cell plants
are built in competitive tenders against a capex budget. Data centre shells
are the same. Neither carries the validated, audited incumbency that a
pharmaceutical cleanroom carries. The order book has grown and diversified;
nothing in the corpus shows that the margin on the new book matches the margin
on the old one.

Management's claim, quoted once: "We're focused on shaping a turnkey cleanroom
company that can serve multiple high-growth industries, while staying true to
the quality and commitment that define us" (Investor Presentation FY2026 Q2,
page 23).

That is a growth claim, not a returns claim. Record the ladder reading as
**contested, pending sector-wise margin disclosure.**

## 8. Proof check

The triggers claim order growth and sector diversification. Both show, and they
show monthly. The book rose from Rs 229.25 crore to Rs 354.74 crore in a single
month, and the pipeline rose from Rs 407.16 crore to Rs 541.16 crore over the
same month.

The book at 31 May 2026 is 1.62 times FY26 revenue of Rs 219.32 crore. For a
project business, that is a full year and a half of visible work.

**Name the gap:** the financial proof stops at 31 March 2026 because the
company reports half-yearly. The order book proof runs to 31 May 2026. Four
months of order flow after that are unobserved, and no revenue or margin has
yet been reported against the renewable-energy book.

## 9. Flags from the documents

- **No sector-wise margin.** The company reports order intake by sector in
  detail and reports margin only in aggregate. The single most important
  question about this business is therefore unanswerable from its own
  disclosure. Flag, and make it load-bearing.
- Consolidated total income of Rs 221.72 crore against standalone Rs 143.39
  crore. A third of the group sits in entities whose ownership the corpus does
  not fully set out. Flag.
- A separate listed group company, Fabtech Technologies Ltd, exists, and the
  prospectus cites "support of larger group company(ies)". Related-party flows
  between two listed entities are a governance question. Flag, not
  investigated.
- Finance cost rose from Rs 0.40 crore to Rs 1.43 crore and depreciation from
  Rs 0.62 crore to Rs 1.78 crore in one year. Working capital in project
  businesses is where the money goes. Flag.
- Half-yearly reporting under the SME platform. Flag the reporting frequency
  against the framework's two-quarter proof test.
- The held screener.in CSV exports in the run folder are empty files. Flag as a
  collector defect.
- Promoter holding, pledge and credit rating NOT FOUND.

## 11. Posture: the Transition Decision Matrix

- **Proof gate: FIRED on growth, NOT FIRED on quality.** Order intake, book and
  pipeline all compound and are published monthly. No evidence exists that the
  new sectors earn what pharma earned.
- **Ugliness: ARTIFACT-OF-CLIMB.** Doubling employee cost, tripling finance cost
  and tripling depreciation in a year of 46% revenue growth is the cost of
  building execution capacity.
- **Recognition gap: CLOSED on delivered earnings.** Price basis, not a corpus
  document: Bull AI company record, market capitalisation Rs 588.43 crore.
  FY26 consolidated profit after tax was Rs 15.82 crore and earnings per share
  Rs 12.84. The market already pays a full growth multiple for a project
  contractor. It does not obviously pay for the Rs 354.74 crore book.

**Posture: RE-RATING LIVE on the book, PRICED NARRATIVE on the multiple.** The
two readings resolve on one disclosure: margin by sector.

## 12. Verdict card

**PROCEED to `/step1`.**

The reason is the evidence quality, not the price. A small company that
publishes its order book, its pipeline, its sector split and its vertical split
every single month hands an analyst the forward series that most names in this
screen refuse to give. Rs 354.74 crore of book against Rs 219.32 crore of
annual revenue is a real, checkable inflection. A `/step1` can build a
revenue bridge from filed monthly data rather than from management guidance.

Proceed with the ladder question stated up front, not buried. The book grew by
moving from pharma into solar. That is diversification and it may also be a
quality downgrade. The run must settle it.

**Load-bearing facts a `/step1` must verify first**
1. **Margin by sector.** Renewable energy against pharma, on won orders. This
   decides whether the transition is a climb or a slide.
2. Promoter identity and holding, and every related-party flow between this
   company and Fabtech Technologies Ltd.
3. The Advantek and Kelvin structures: what is consolidated, what is
   associated, and what drives the Rs 78.33 crore gap between consolidated and
   standalone income.
4. Order book at 30 September 2026 and the intervening monthly announcements.
5. Receivable and retention-money days on renewable-energy contracts. Solar
   developers pay differently from pharma companies.
6. Whether any single order is more than a fifth of the book. The May intake
   alone was Rs 153.05 crore.

**What would change the view**
- Sector margins showing renewable work at or above pharma margins moves this
  to a full position case.
- Renewable margins materially below pharma turns the growth into a
  quality slide and the verdict to WATCH.
- A stalling order book, or a receivable build in H1 FY27.

No valuation was run. No target price. This verdict decides only whether the
name earns a full `/step1`, and it is not a position.
