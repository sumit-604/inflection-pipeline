## SECTION 1: MARKET DEFINITION

### 1A Precise boundaries

- **Product scope**: generic pharmaceutical formulations — tablets,
  capsules, dry syrups/suspensions, liquids, ointments, injectables
  (vials/ampoules) — plus, through subsidiaries, medical devices (IV
  cannulas, IV infusion sets), nutraceuticals, and cosmetics. Core scope for
  Methods 1-3 below is the **generic-formulations export/domestic
  business** (≈79% of FY26 consolidated revenue per B04); devices,
  nutraceuticals, and cosmetics are sized separately and qualitatively in
  Section 5D because no India-specific market-size figure for them survived
  the search budget (see input_gaps).
- **Geographic scope**: India domestic market + export to distributors in
  **semi-regulated / rest-of-world markets** — Africa, Latin America, CIS,
  and Asia, 46 countries as of FY26 (AR p.15 [page 15], deck p.10). Explicitly
  **excludes** the highly-regulated US/EU/UK/Japan generics market: TLL's
  corpus shows no registration base or revenue there (B04; AR company
  overview).
- **Customer / channel scope**: B2B, distributor-led. TLL does not sell
  direct-to-pharmacy or direct-to-patient; "we partner with distributors for
  market access" (deck p.8).
- **Price segment**: mid/low-cost generic, not innovator/branded/patented.
- **Explicit exclusions**: US/EU/UK/Japan regulated generics; biologics and
  complex generics/CDMO-for-innovators (not TLL's model per B04); China
  domestic market.

## SECTION 2: TAM ESTIMATION, MULTIPLE METHODS

### Method 1 — Top-down (macro industry)

- Indian domestic pharmaceutical market: independent estimate **USD 60.32
  billion in 2026**, growing to USD 79.74 billion by 2031, CAGR ≈5.74%
  (source per AR: "Independent market forecasts… Mordor Intelligence,"
  AR p.15 [page 15]). Management/government's own stated *ambition* (USD 130
  billion by 2030, USD 450 billion by 2047) is explicitly a policy aspiration
  cited in the AR, not an independent forecast — held separately, not used
  as a TAM input (would inflate the number on an unevidenced basis).
  In Cr: USD 60.32bn × ₹88.1/USD ≈ **₹5,314 Cr… no** — corrected: USD
  60.32bn × 88.1 = **₹5,31,419 Cr** (₹5.31 lakh Cr).
- India pharmaceutical exports, FY26 actual: **USD 31.11 billion ≈ ₹2.74
  lakh crore** (AR p.15 [page 15], STALE-free, FY26 data).
- Of exports, web search finds roughly **50% go to highly-regulated markets
  (US/EU)**, the balance to semi-regulated/RoW markets TLL actually serves
  (medicaldialogues.in / IBEF cross-check, Sep-2026 search). Applying that
  split: semi-regulated-relevant export TAM ≈ ₹1.37 lakh Cr.
- **Method 1 TAM = domestic (₹5.31 lakh Cr) + semi-regulated exports (₹1.37
  lakh Cr) ≈ ₹6.68 lakh Cr (~USD 76 bn).** This is the macro ceiling: the
  entire Indian-pharma-plus-relevant-export universe, all players, all
  channels. Confidence: **M** (named third-party sources, but a wide funnel
  three subtraction-steps from TLL's actual footprint). Staleness: 2026-dated
  primary figures, not stale.

### Method 2 — Bottom-up (company-anchored proxy; flagged limitation)

A true population/prescription-level bottom-up (addressable patients ×
penetration × price) is **not available**: no per-capita consumption data
by target country and no per-unit price data exist in the corpus (B04:
"revenue_per_unit: NOT FOUND"). As a substitute, this method uses TLL's own
disclosed unit — the **registered product-country pair**:

- FY26 pharma-formulations revenue (parent + TNS) ≈ ₹101.9 Cr (AR p.26-27
  [page 16], standalone revenue crossing ₹100 Cr) against **1,091 products
  registered across 46 countries** (AR p.16 [page 16]).
- Implied average revenue per registered product ≈ ₹101.9 Cr ÷ 1,091 ≈
  **₹0.093 Cr (₹9.3 lakh) per registration**, at TLL's current stage of
  registration maturity (many registrations are recent and not yet
  monetised — this average understates a mature registration's revenue).
- TLL discloses **2,534 registrations in process**, taking the total
  registered-plus-pipeline base to **3,625 products** (AR p.26, "the Company
  continues to widen its offering, which now spans 3,625 products").
- At the *current* average revenue-per-registration, full monetisation of
  all 3,625 would imply ≈ **₹337 Cr**.

This ₹337 Cr is **not a market TAM** — it is TLL's own registration-pipeline
revenue ceiling at today's yield per registration, i.e. a company-specific
number, not "total revenue opportunity at 100% capture" by any player. It is
retained as a **sanity check on SOM** (Section 3B), not as a TAM headline.
Confidence: **L** for TAM purposes; flagged explicitly.

## SECTION 3: SAM & SOM

### 3A SAM

Starting from the conservative TAM (₹6,541 Cr, Method 3), apply the five
filters:

1. **Product fit**: 100% retained. Method 3's peer set is already pure-play
   generic-formulations exporters/domestic sellers — matches TLL's core
   product scope exactly (devices/nutraceuticals/cosmetics sit outside this
   SAM; see 5D).
2. **Geography fit**: peers CAPLIPOINT, INNOVACAP, and especially SENORES
   carry some highly-regulated-market (US/EU) exposure that TLL does not
   have (company memory: SENORES "grows by emerging-market registrations
   plus acquired plants and CDMO work" with a US tilt). Estimated cut:
   **-15%** for regulated-market revenue embedded in the peer sum that TLL
   structurally cannot address. ₹6,541 Cr × 0.85 ≈ **₹5,560 Cr**.
3. **Channel fit**: 0% cut — all four companies (including TLL) run a
   distributor-led B2B model.
4. **Customer fit**: 0% cut — same B2B distributor customer type across the
   peer set.
5. **Capability fit**: not applied here (capacity/registration-pace
   constraints are a *capture-rate* question, addressed in SOM/3C, not a
   structural-exclusion question).

**SAM ≈ ₹5,560 Cr, i.e. ≈85% of conservative TAM.**

### 3B SOM at 3 and 5 years

Current share of SAM: ₹129.02 Cr ÷ ₹5,560 Cr ≈ **2.32%**.

Share-gain rules applied: TLL is mid-build on new capacity (Parenterals,
Wellness commissioning; Mediquip and TNS ramping) — this qualifies for the
**aggressive band (3-5pp in 3 years)**, but B04's FLAG-SUBSIDIARY-GAP (three
of five subsidiaries pre-revenue or near-zero against stated peaks) argues
for the **conservative end** of that band, not the top.

- **3-year SOM**: +3pp share gain → 2.32% + 3.0% = 5.3% of SAM → 0.053 ×
  5,560 ≈ **₹295 Cr**.
  Implied revenue CAGR, FY26 (₹129.02 Cr) → FY29 (₹295 Cr):
  (295/129.02)^(1/3) − 1 ≈ **31.8%**.
- **5-year SOM**: +5pp share gain (top of the aggressive band, allowing more
  time for Parenterals/Wellness to season) → 2.32% + 5.0% = 7.3% of SAM →
  0.073 × 5,560 ≈ **₹406 Cr**.
  Implied revenue CAGR, FY26 → FY31:
  (406/129.02)^(1/5) − 1 ≈ **25.8%**.

Both figures sit close to the strategy's 25% CAGR hurdle — noted, not
adjusted for.

**LBF3 test — management's "triple in three years" vs SOM**: implied FY29
revenue from tripling = 3 × ₹129.02 Cr ≈ **₹387 Cr** (implied CAGR ≈44.2%).
Against the conservative 3-year SOM of ₹295 Cr: **ratio = 387 ÷ 295 ≈
1.31x**. By the standard read (within 1.5x = reasonable), management's claim
is **not wildly inflated relative to a bottom-up share-gain SOM** — but this
1.31x gap is not small either, and the corpus's own subsidiary delivery
record (Mediquip ~39% of its stated peak after ~1.5 years operating; TNS
Pharma only ~14% of its stated peak years after "commercial operation";
Parenterals/Wellness/Elements still near-zero) argues the *composition* of
that extra 31% (i.e., the gap between ₹295 Cr and ₹387 Cr) leans on faster
subsidiary ramp-up than the company has yet demonstrated anywhere in its own
five-subsidiary history. **mgmt_claim_read: reasonable, with a named
execution-history caveat** (not "inflated" outright, because 1.31x clears
the 1.5x inflated-line; not "conservative").

### 3C Capacity cross-check

B07 (Emerging Moat capex-embedded-growth figure) is not yet available
(stage 7 running in parallel). Substituted per task instruction: FY26 AR
consolidated CWIP and the Aug-2026 deck capacity table.

- **CWIP**: consolidated Capital Work-in-Progress closing balance FY26 =
  ₹3,391.07 lakh = **₹33.91 Cr** (AR Note 8 (PP&E), [PDF page 63];
  arithmetic check: opening ₹1,879.29 lakh + additions ₹1,565.50 lakh −
  deletions ₹53.72 lakh = ₹3,391.07 lakh, ties exactly).
- **Installed monthly production capacity** (two-shift basis, deck p.9
  [page 9]): Tablets 120 Mn, Capsules 120 Mn, Dry powder bottles 3 Mn,
  Ointments 3 Mn, Liquid bottles 3 Mn, Injectables 10 Mn.
- **Group capacity utilisation: 40%** (deck p.8 [page 8], stated KPI,
  "two-shift operations" basis).

Using utilisation as a rough linear proxy for revenue-to-capacity (imperfect
— ignores price/mix shifts — but the only available cross-check): at 40%
utilisation, FY26 revenue is ₹129.02 Cr; at 100% utilisation of the *same,
already-installed* capacity, revenue would scale to ≈ ₹129.02 Cr ÷ 0.40 ≈
**₹322.5 Cr**.

- **3-year SOM (₹295 Cr) sits below this ₹322.5 Cr ceiling.** Existing
  installed capacity, even before Parenterals/Wellness contribute
  meaningfully, can physically support the 3-year SOM. **Capacity check,
  3yr: sufficient.**
- **5-year SOM (₹406 Cr) exceeds the ₹322.5 Cr ceiling by ≈₹84 Cr.** That
  gap must come from new subsidiary capacity — principally TLL Parenterals
  (stated peak revenue ₹200 Cr, currently ₹0, "revenue contribution expected
  from FY27 onwards," deck p.13) and TLL Wellness (stated peak ₹10 Cr,
  currently ₹0.12 Cr). Closing an ₹84 Cr gap from Parenterals alone would
  require it to reach **~42% of its own stated ₹200 Cr peak within 5
  years** — a faster ramp than **any** of TLL's existing subsidiaries has
  achieved: Mediquip (the best performer) reached ~39% of its stated peak
  after roughly 1.5 years of ownership, and TNS Pharma has been commercially
  operating since 2024 (deck p.10, "Strategic growth" timeline) yet sits at
  only ~14% of its stated ₹40 Cr peak by FY26. **Gap: ≈₹84 Cr. The SOM (not
  the capex plan) is the optimistic side of this cross-check** — the
  installed-and-committed capacity plan is plausible on its own terms; it is
  the 5-year SOM's assumption that Parenterals delivers faster than the
  company's own subsidiary track record that carries the risk.

### 5B Runway assessment

- **Revenue headroom** = SAM ÷ current revenue = 5,560 ÷ 129.02 ≈ **43.1x**.
- **TAM/SAM growth rate**: the *macro* domestic market grows at ~5.74% CAGR
  to 2031 (independent estimate, AR p.15); the *niche* (Method 3 peer set,
  the more relevant denominator) grows faster — CAPLIPOINT and INNOVACAP
  each compounded **~15-19%** FY22-26 (screener arithmetic). Using the niche
  figure: **TAM growth ≈16%** (average of the two mature comparables).
- **Company CAGR vs TAM**: TLL's own ~56% FY22-26 revenue CAGR (deck p.7)
  is **more than 3x the niche's own growth rate** — TLL is unambiguously
  **gaining share**, not riding the market.
- **Years to saturate SAM** at the SOM-implied growth rate (~26-32%): ln(43.1)
  ÷ ln(1.30) ≈ **14 years** (illustrative; ignores that SAM itself is growing
  ~16%/yr, which pushes the true saturation point further out still).

### 5C Runway classification: **STRONG**

Headroom (43x) and a niche growing faster than the macro market both argue
for the top of the matrix. Not classified **MASSIVE**: this SAM is a
specific, evidenced niche (registration-led semi-regulated exporter), not an
unconstrained blue ocean, and capture requires country-by-country
registration and subsidiary capacity to physically materialise (Section 3C:
the SOM's own 5-year figure already runs ahead of installed capacity).
**STRONG** reflects genuine large headroom with real, named execution
friction, consistent with the conservative-bias instruction.

### 5D SAM expansion levers actually being pursued

- **Domestic pharma** (already inside SAM/TAM as computed): share of
  revenue up from 30% to 49% FY25→FY26 (AR p.16) — the clearest, already-
  running lever.
- **Medical devices** (Trident Mediquip: IV cannulas, infusion sets): global
  IV-cannula market ≈USD 2.5-4.1 billion (2024, multiple sources diverge by
  scope) growing at ~5.5-7.5% CAGR to 2033-34; global IV-infusion-set market
  growing ~8.2% CAGR 2026-2032 (web search, this stage). **India-specific
  breakdown NOT FOUND** within the search budget — directional only, low
  confidence, not added to the headline SAM.
- **Nutraceuticals** (TLL Wellness): India nutraceutical market
  ≈USD 37-43 billion (2026 estimates vary; ~₹3.3-3.8 lakh Cr at the AR-
  implied rate), CAGR ~10.5% to 2030 (web search, this stage). TLL Wellness
  FY26 revenue: ₹0.12 Cr (B04) — **large adjacent TAM, immaterial current
  penetration**; genuine option value, not near-term SOM headroom.
- **Cosmetics** (TLL Elements, "YES" brand): India cosmetics/beauty-personal-
  care market ≈USD 25.6-33.9 billion depending on source/scope (web search,
  this stage), growing high-single-digits. TLL Elements FY26 revenue:
  ₹0.02 Cr, loss-making (B04) — same read as nutraceuticals: **large
  adjacent market, currently negligible penetration.**
- None of these three levers is added to the headline TAM/SAM/SOM figures
  above; they are named because management is visibly pursuing them (five
  subsidiaries), and because their eventual scale, if delivered, is the
  mechanism by which "triple in three years" would have to work beyond the
  core pharma-formulations SOM computed in 3B.
