Genuine grower priced far above earned value

OBSC Perfection makes precision engineered metal components to customer drawings for automotive Tier 1s and OEMs. Founded in 2017 and run out of Pune, it listed on NSE Emerge in October 2024 and trades near Rs 666 for a market value around Rs 1,720 Cr, about 64 times trailing earnings. Automotive is 86% of sales, marine 6%, defence 6%, and a small renewables and others bucket 2%. Named customers include ZF, Tenneco, JTEKT and Tata AutoComp, most of them far larger than OBSC itself. Its main raw material, bright bar steel, is sourced structurally from a promoter controlled entity, Omega Bright Steel.

The company is widening from a single domestic auto vendor into a diversified precision supplier across defence, marine, aerospace, humanoid robotics and exports. The order book grew from about Rs 290 Cr in FY24 to Rs 700 to 750 Cr in FY25 to more than Rs 1,200 Cr in FY26, a filed figure to be executed over the next five to six fiscals. New capacity is coming up at Sanand on a dedicated Tenneco line, plus a Supa plant and added stamping and forging lines. Top five customers are 50% to 55% of revenue and the largest single customer 15% to 20%, so the growth is real but concentrated.

FY26 revenue was about Rs 220 Cr, up 54%, with PAT around Rs 27 Cr and an EBITDA margin near 20%. Gate 0 places the company at AVERAGE on a core score of 52 out of 100, with full marks on growth and a strong balance sheet score, held down by weak cumulative cash conversion and by a data gap where the promoter holding and pledge pages of the annual report could not be read. Cash is the sore point. FY26 operating cash flow was negative about Rs 2 Cr against positive PAT of Rs 27 Cr.

The cash gap traces to a working capital build that ran ahead of sales, funded by fresh debt and a preferential equity issue rather than internal cash. The rating agency CRISIL, with full account access through FY26, frames the 223 day gross current assets as working capital intensive operations and long credit periods to export customers, with no bad debt flag. On that evidence the strain reads as growth induced rather than a collection failure, which holds the cash penalty neutral in the valuation. The single number that would overturn that read is the over 12 month receivables bucket, which the corrupted annual report never let anyone see.

Return on capital moved the wrong way, from 33% in FY24 to 19% in FY25 to about 15% in FY26 on a year end basis, because the October 2024 IPO raised about Rs 57 Cr and bloated the capital base faster than profit grew. Management earns a credibility grade of B. It delivered 54% growth against a guide above 40%, brought the Sanand facility on, and grew the order book as claimed across two calls. Against that it broke its own promise of quarterly disclosure and held only one further call in twelve months, let an AS9100D aerospace certification slip past its implied timeline without acknowledging it, and trimmed a medium term margin ambition from four to five points down to about 1% for FY27. The promoter record reads CAUTION rather than clean, on a structural related party dependency, no non compete, and a recent Rs 12.45 Cr related party asset purchase whose pricing fairness is undisclosed.

Four listed auto parts peers, Divgi TorqTransfer, Precision Camshafts, RACL Geartech and Talbros Auto, corroborate the demand backdrop of a China plus one export tailwind, strong commercial vehicle volumes, and an industry wide capex race that makes the plant buildout look normal. One claim they contradict matters. OBSC framed its margin guidance trim as forced by tariffs, the West Asia conflict and shipping costs; three peers facing the same cost pressure in the same window reaffirmed their margin guidance rather than cut it.

The addressable market is large and the company small inside it. TAM sits at Rs 61,000 to 70,000 Cr, the served market about Rs 18,600 Cr, and OBSC holds roughly 1.2% of that served market today, a runway classed as MASSIVE with headroom near 85 times. The three year served opportunity implies a 39% revenue CAGR, though that number sits on the optimistic side of the disclosed capex plan and the Supa cost was never disclosed.

Valuation is where the case stops. A four pillar build sets the earned exit multiple at 16.5 times on the conservative required return track and 20.1 times on the additive track, against about 64 times today. Base fair value three years out is roughly Rs 404 to Rs 492, and even the bull case near Rs 620 sits below the current price. Fair re engagement is Rs 207 to Rs 252, with a margin of safety near Rs 166, roughly a third of Rs 666. The return hurdle STOPs and the probability weighted three year return from here is about minus 10%, so the call is a buy on the business and an avoid on the price, held on a deep watchlist. The devil's advocate case is weakened but alive, and it destroys the valuation safety outright, because the entry zone is derived from the same contested multiple it is meant to protect against.
