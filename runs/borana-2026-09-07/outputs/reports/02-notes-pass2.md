4. EPS WEIGHTING — DEEPENED, THE COMPANY'S OWN STATED RATIONALE FOUND AND QUOTED
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

The FY26 AR's Note 39(g)/40(g) (AR pp.126-127) contains the Company's own explanation, quoted in full:

"During the financial year ended March 31, 2025, the Board of Directors...vide resolution dated November 16,
2024 proposed and agreed to increase the issued share capital of the Company from...1,99,37,295 equity
shares...to...2,66,45,295 equity shares...The above proposed increase...was approved by the members...at
their Extra Ordinary General Meeting held on October 10, 2024...the same was also intimated to the
Registrar of Companies...on October 18, 2024. However, the process of Initial Public Offer (IPO) was
completed and the company was listed on stock exchanges as on May 27, 2025. Accordingly, the effect of
issued share capital which got effective in the closure date of financials for F.Y. 2025-26 has given effect
to in the current financial statements. Accordingly, same is considered to be Adjusting Event."

This is a materially clearer picture than Pass 1 had. It shows: the EGM approval (10-Oct-2024) and board
resolution (16-Nov-2024) that authorised the CAPITAL INCREASE both occurred in FY25, well before the IPO. But
the actual issuance of the 6,708,000 new shares for CASH CONSIDERATION only happened on IPO completion,
27-May-2025, roughly 57 days into FY26. The Company's own note treats the full post-increase share count
(26,645,295) as outstanding for the WHOLE of FY26 — i.e. it appears to be treating a fresh cash issue as
though it were a bonus-style capitalisation event (which alone would justify full-year weighting under Ind
AS 33 para 28), when the underlying event was a fresh issue for cash via IPO, which Ind AS 33 para 19-21
requires to be time-weighted from the date shares are issued and consideration receivable. The note's own
language ("Adjusting Event") is the closest the Company comes to a stated rationale, and it does not resolve
the distinction between authorising a capital increase (no cash) and completing an IPO (cash consideration,
dated 27-May-2025).

Cross-check against the exchange filing: runs/borana-2026-09-07/inputs/results/Results_Q4FY26_and_FY26_2026-
03-31.pdf states Basic and Diluted EPS Rs 24.35 (FY26) and Rs 20.14 (FY25) — IDENTICAL to the AR figures. The
questionable weighting was therefore used consistently in both the exchange-filed results and the AR, not an
AR-only drafting artefact.

Illustrative recomputation (this analyst's own Ind AS 33 time-weighting, using the Company's own disclosed
day-count facts, NOT a note-disclosed figure — presented to size the issue, mirroring Pass 1's own EBITDA
reconciliation method): weighting 19,937,295 shares for ~57 days and 26,645,295 shares for the remaining
~308 days of a 365-day year gives a time-weighted average of approximately 25,597,744 shares — about
1,047,551 fewer shares (-3.9%) than the 26,645,295 the Company used. Applying FY26 PAT (Rs 6,461.20 lakh per
the Return on Equity ratio note, Note 40 item 4, AR p.128) to this corrected share count implies Basic EPS of
approximately Rs 25.2-25.3, versus the Rs 24.35 reported — i.e. if the weighting is wrong, reported EPS
appears UNDERSTATED by roughly 3-4%, not overstated. This is directionally the opposite of what a red flag
usually implies (an aggressive company would prefer to inflate, not deflate, EPS), which is itself worth
noting: whatever produced this outcome does not look like an EPS-inflation motive; it looks like a mechanical
application of the "Adjusting Event" logic without distinguishing a capitalisation issue from a cash issue.
🔴 Red flag stands, now with the Company's own stated rationale on record and quantified magnitude, both of
which sharpen it from a suspicion into an evidenced Ind AS 33 question for management and the auditor. (Note
39(g)/40(g), AR pp.126-127; Note 32, AR p.115; Results_Q4FY26_and_FY26_2026-03-31.pdf)

CORRECTION A — Inventory Turnover Ratio: Pass 1 stated "6.42x FY26 vs 3.96x FY25" and separately flagged this

which matches the Board's Report's "Inventory Turnover Ratio 6.26/7.23" exactly. 🟢 This RESOLVES, not

the same direction as the raw-material stocking build Pass 1 identified from the balance-sheet numbers. There

flagging the raw payables balance collapse (Rs 376.88 lakh to Rs 23.19 lakh) as "unusual...worth a question."

Neither correction changes the underlying facts Pass 1 identified from the raw rupee balances (inventory
