### Part 1 — Summary narrative (what happened and what it means)

JNK sells into refining, petrochemicals, fertilisers, steel and hydrogen — process industries whose capex cycle drives its order book **[N]**. The demand read this quarter is mixed: management points to a ~Rs 6,000 Cr bidding pipeline split 50:50 domestic/export **[Q]**, consistent with the India refinery/petrochemical capex tailwind the thesis relies on (T3) **[N]**, but the order book itself declined sequentially and a marquee export order collapsed on a licensor-approval barrier **[Q]** — a reminder that in this industry technical approvals and proven references are the real entry barrier, and that JNK's export reach is partly gated by the technology licensor (JNK Global / others). ### Part 3 — Business-model intelligence

JNK designs, engineers, fabricates and commissions fired heaters, reformers and cracking furnaces, increasingly bundled with waste-gas systems (flares/incinerators), hydrogen infrastructure and solar EPC **[Q]**. It earns on project execution against an order book, so revenue is lumpy and quarter-shape is seasonal (Q1 is the trough) **[Q]**. Unit economics this quarter: gross margins are healthy (standalone gross profit is a large share of revenue) but operating EBITDA margin is only 10.5% standalone / 8.8% consolidated ex-other-income **[Q]**, below the 13-16% the thesis targets **[N]** — the model's profitability is real but thin and still maturing. Model-drift signals this quarter: (a) growth is being sourced increasingly from a 51%-owned subsidiary (Chemdist) that is currently loss-making and dilutive **[Q]**; (b) the board is widening the charter into marine/offshore heavy-lift and turnkey EPC for steel/cement/mining **[Q]** — a capability the company does not yet demonstrably own, funded by unspecified "internal accruals and/or borrowings." The core fired-heater franchise remains the profit engine (standalone Rs 13.55 Cr PAT) — the risk is that management's attention and capital rotate away from it before the adjacencies pay.

### Part 4 — Competition intelligence

JNK's structural advantage is the strategic partnership with JNK Global (Korea) for design know-how and references, plus a scarce approved-vendor status behind high technical entry barriers **[Q/N]**. That same partnership is the competitive dependency: ~82% of the Rs 1,961 Cr order book was linked to JNK Global **[N]**, and this quarter a "direct" export order still died on a licensor's approval **[Q]** — evidence that JNK is not yet fully independent of its technology licensor, which can price to favour the Korean parent **[N]**.
