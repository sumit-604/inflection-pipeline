## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

**2A Demand environment.** All three peers describe robust, broad-based demand across auto/EV, industrial, aerospace/defence, health-tech and (for Kaynes/Syrma) government-linked programmes, with no peer describing a demand slowdown. Kaynes: "we don't see anybody saying that we are going to cut down, including the public sector enterprises or the government" (KAYNES Q1 FY27 call, Aug-2026, p.19). Syrma: "I personally don't foresee demand side to be a constraint in the coming quarters" (SYRMA Q1 FY27 call, Aug-2026, p.3). This is broadly consistent with CYIENTDLM's own demand framing. What is NOT consistent is delivered growth: while CYIENTDLM's consolidated revenue FELL 17.0% in FY26, all three peers GREW revenue substantially over the same period — Avalon +46% (AVALON Q4 FY26 call, May-2026, p.3), Kaynes +33.2% (KAYNES Q4 FY26 call, May-2026, p.2), Syrma +27% (SYRMA Q4 FY26 call, Jun-2026, p.5). If sector demand was uniformly strong, as all four companies (including CYIENTDLM) describe, CYIENTDLM's FY26 revenue decline is harder to attribute to demand and points more toward execution/supply issues specific to Cyient DLM (which is consistent with its own stated reasons — West Asia delays, approvals, memory lead times — but stands out in magnitude against a peer set that grew through the same conditions).

**2B Pricing and input costs.** All three peers describe a near-complete cost pass-through mechanism for tariffs and component-price inflation, with a lag, and are explicit that ABSOLUTE margins are largely protected even when PERCENTAGE margins are optically compressed by the pass-through gross-up (Avalon ~100-110bps; Syrma explicitly walks an analyst through the mechanics: "if my chip is Rs. 60 and I am selling it at Rs. 100... I sell it at 140 to the customer. 140 is not 40%," SYRMA Q1 FY27 call, Aug-2026, p.9). This is consistent in direction with CYIENTDLM's margin narrative but more granular and quantified than anything CYIENTDLM has disclosed on tariffs specifically (see Q3 above).

**2C Capex cycle.** This is an industry-wide capacity race, not a lone expander. Avalon is adding a new Chennai plant and acquiring land for a decade-long expansion (AVALON Q1 FY27 call, Aug-2026, p.4). Kaynes has committed over INR1,200cr combined to OSAT and PCB backward integration with government subsidy support (KAYNES Q1 FY27 call, Aug-2026, p.11). Syrma is building an INR800cr-plus PCB facility in Andhra Pradesh plus Bangalore/Pune/Jodhpur expansions and a new Kaga JV (SYRMA Q1 FY27 call, Aug-2026, p.5, p.13). All three cite government schemes (ECMS, PLI, ISM 2.0) as co-funding the buildout. This is a capacity race across the EMS sector broadly, driven by policy tailwinds, not an isolated bet by any one name.

**2D Competitive mentions.** No peer names Cyient DLM or gives an unmistakable description of it in any of the twelve transcripts. One relevant adjacent-competitor mention: Syrma's IIFL analyst raises Larsen & Toubro's INR50bn EMS capex announcement as a new-entrant competitive threat (SYRMA Q4 FY26 call, Jun-2026, p.15) — a competitive-intensity risk CYIENTDLM does not appear to discuss in its own record.

**2E Risks peers discuss that CYIENTDLM does not.** (i) Governance/disclosure-control risk: Kaynes spent an entire call (Dec-2025) rebutting a short-seller/forensic accounting report covering goodwill recognition, related-party-transaction notes, and receivables aging — a governance-quality risk class CYIENTDLM's record does not surface. (ii) New-entrant competitive intensity from large conglomerates (L&T), as above. (iii) AI-data-centre demand competing for semiconductor supply, named explicitly by Syrma as a driver of the current chip shortage (SYRMA Q1 FY27 call, Aug-2026, p.15) — CYIENTDLM blames the shortage on West Asia/logistics but does not connect it to AI-driven chip demand. (iv) Government-tender/long-cycle receivable risk in adjacent business lines (Kaynes' metering subsidiary) as a distinct risk category from core EMS execution risk. (v) Rare-earth-specific input constraints, named by Syrma (SYRMA Q2 FY26 call, Nov-2025, p.5) as distinct from general component shortages.

## PART 4: TRIANGULATION SUMMARY

- Claims verified: 2 of 7 (Q3 tariff exposure/mitigation; Q4 memory/component shortage).
- Claims partially verified: 3 of 7 (Q2 order-growth magnitude; Q5 AI-data-centre/margin claim; Q6 aerospace/defence moat).
- Claims contradicted: 1 of 7 (Q7 NWC/cash-conversion trend) — this is the priority item for synthesis.
- Claims unverifiable: 1 of 7 (Q1 global EMS TAM figure).

**The single most consequential contradiction.** Question 7. CYIENTDLM's rising net-working-capital and negative cumulative operating cash flow (FY23-26) cannot be read as a sector-wide, growth-induced feature of the EMS business model. Two of three peers (Avalon, Syrma) show IMPROVING working capital and consistently POSITIVE, GROWING operating cash flow while growing revenue 27-46% YoY over the identical FY25-FY27 window. The one peer with genuine cash-conversion deterioration (Kaynes) attributes it specifically and repeatedly to a single non-core, government-receivables-heavy subsidiary, with its core EMS business improving over the same period. This evidence weighs toward the STRUCTURAL classification for CYIENTDLM's cash-conversion problem rather than GROWTH-INDUCED, which is the more forgiving reading management has implicitly offered.

**The single strongest independent confirmation.** Question 3. Avalon's granular, repeatedly quantified tariff disclosure (>50 unique tariff rates, >99% customer recovery, precise optical-versus-absolute gross-margin math, and the 50%→18% rate reduction after the trade deal) independently confirms that ~50% pre-deal US tariff exposure was real and material for India-based EMS exporters, and sets a disclosure bar CYIENTDLM has not met despite being asked twice to quantify its own exposure in rupees.

**Overall assessment.** The peer set complicates more of CYIENTDLM's narrative than it supports. It corroborates the two claims most exogenous to company execution — industry-wide tariff exposure and industry-wide component/memory shortages — giving CYIENTDLM's stated reasons for its Q4 FY26 miss real, if partial, cover. But on the claims closer to company execution and disclosure quality, the peer record is neutral-to-unfavourable: order-book growth magnitude is not the peer norm one way or the other; the AI-data-centre margin claim is made without a mechanism where a comparable peer explicitly declines to make the same claim; the aerospace/defence "moat" argument is supported only in its narrower (barrier-to-new-entrants) form, not in the stronger (durable-margin-for-incumbent) form CYIENTDLM implies; and, most importantly, the working-capital/cash-conversion trend that anchors the Gate 0 AVOID classification runs in the opposite direction at two of three peers, undercutting any "this is just what growth in this industry costs" defence.

## PART 5: CROSS-PEER HYPOTHESIS

Across the combined peer set, working-capital and operating-cash-flow quality does not track revenue growth rate, export mix, or company scale — the three variables analysts typically use to explain EMS cash conversion. It tracks something narrower and more specific: exposure to GOVERNMENT-TENDER-LINKED, long-cycle, milestone- or installation-based receivables (smart metering, defence programme payments tied to government release cycles), versus exposure to private-sector, contract-manufacturing-style receivables (industrial, automotive, consumer, aerospace box-build for private OEMs).

Kaynes is the clean natural experiment: its core EMS book (private-sector customers) improved NWC from 83 to 53 days over FY24-FY26 even as the company scaled rapidly, while its GridCrest/Iskraemeco smart-metering subsidiary (government-tender receivables) dragged consolidated NWC to 122+ days and consolidated OCF to -INR600cr in the same year. Avalon and Syrma, whose revenue is almost entirely private-sector EMS/ODM (Syrma's own smart-metering exposure is small and deliberately capped — "we are selective about customers... I can grow several times what I'm doing today in the energy metering business. But I don't want to land in a situation where the sales cycle does not result into a sort of a short-term cash flow cycle," SYRMA Q3 FY26 call, Feb-2026, p.14), show improving cash conversion throughout.

Testable hypothesis: an EMS company's cash-conversion trajectory is better predicted by the SHARE of revenue tied to government-tender/milestone-payment customers than by its headline growth rate. If CYIENTDLM's aerospace/defence customer mix carries a meaningfully higher government-payment-cycle component than a typical private-sector EMS peer, this framework predicts its NWC deterioration is structural to that customer mix — not a temporary cost of scaling — and would not be expected to self-correct through revenue growth alone, only through a change in customer/payment-term mix. No single peer states this axis explicitly; it only emerges from comparing Kaynes' internal bifurcation against Avalon's and Syrma's clean, private-sector-dominated books.
