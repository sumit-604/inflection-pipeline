Niche polymer maker shedding its fertilizer half

Jubilant Agri and Consumer Products makes adhesives, latex and food polymers on one side and single super phosphate and bulk NPK fertilizers on the other. It sits inside the Jubilant Bhartia Group. The polymer side sells Jivanjor and Charmwood wood adhesives through about 1,450 distributors and 31,000 retailers, and it is the number one vinyl pyridine latex maker in India and number two globally outside China. The company listed on 14 February 2025 through a reverse composite scheme, not an IPO, so there is no prospectus and the backward record runs only across the annual report years. FY26 revenue grew about 21 percent and net profit about 44 percent.

The company is splitting in two. The Board approved demerging the entire Agri division on 4 November 2025 into a new entity, Jubilant Agri Solutions. Exchange approvals came in April 2026, the NCLT first motion order in July 2026, and shareholder and creditor meetings are set for 5 September 2026. When it completes, the fertilizer and agri nutrient segments leave, the residual company is renamed Jubilant Industries Ltd, and what remains is the Performance Polymers and Chemicals business, about 65 percent of FY26 revenue.

Almost all of FY26 profit growth came from the segment that is leaving. P&K Fertilizers swung from an Rs 11 Cr loss to an Rs 46 Cr profit, a swing that accounts for essentially the entire year over year rise in segment profit. The retained polymer core grew segment EBIT only about 0.8 percent and audited segment revenue about 7.5 to 8.75 percent. Management also put 93 percent of FY26 capex into the retained polymer segment, so it is investing behind the half it keeps, not the half that carried this year's earnings.

The balance sheet is genuinely stronger. Debt to equity fell from 0.17 to 0.06, interest cover rose from 8.9 times to 23.9 times, and gross margin gained about 200 basis points. Gate 0 scores 86 and lands GOOD+, with a STRONG moat read on distribution, brand and niche scale. The forward looking emerging moat scan is thinner at MODEST, driven mostly by that balance sheet war chest rather than any category defining expansion, so the combined read stays GOOD+.

Cash conversion fell hard and then explained itself. Operating cash flow was 59 percent of profit in FY26 against 149 percent in FY25, and free cash flow dropped about 63 percent. The cause is not weaker profit. Operating cash flow before working capital changes still grew 34 percent. The drag is a 92 percent jump in the government fertilizer subsidy receivable and a 125 percent rise in cash taxes paid. Most of that receivable leaves with the demerger, but until it unwinds the timing explanation stays unproven.

Management delivered on some promises and went quiet on the biggest one. There are no earnings calls, so credibility grades C on a small sample. It commissioned the Samlaya adhesive plant on time with independent filing corroboration, missed its Agri inventory sell down and blamed the monsoon, and has cleared every demerger gate on schedule so far. Against that, the Chairman's letter praised the Agri business and the group's diversification and never once mentioned that the same Board he chairs had already approved demerging that entire division.

Peers back the recent excuses and undercut the older story. Rivals confirm the Q1 FY27 problems were real and sector wide: a monsoon rainfall deficit near 40 percent through June, and West Asia shipping disruption that hit exports across the industry. But the FY26 claim of subdued global latex demand does not hold, because Apcotex posted record export volumes and EBITDA up 48 to 61 percent over the same window. The separate claim of market share gains across all businesses cannot be checked either way, since the closest peers were also growing fast.

The runway is long but capacity is the near term ceiling, not the market. The addressable market for the retained polymer business runs to roughly Rs 47,000 Cr against a current share near 2.5 percent, about 40 times headroom. That headroom is real, yet the binding constraint over the next few years is plant capacity, not demand, and management's own constructed market figure reads inflated by roughly 15 times. Promoter conduct sits at CAUTION with real transition evidence on both sides: an outside professional CEO hired from Samsung, zero pledged shares, and an independent director review of the demerger fairness, set against a Rs 1,250 Cr borrowing committee with no independent directors, key management pay up 64 percent against a 10 percent average raise, all corporate social spend routed through the family's own foundation, and a 2018 insider trading penalty on the two founders at a different group company.
