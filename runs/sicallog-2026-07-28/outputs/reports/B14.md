## SECTION 3: THE BULL CASE (Why Buy) — carried honestly

**3A. Quality Evidence.** A genuine operating inflection is on the FY26 tape.
Core EBITDA margin expanded from 9.7% to 20.4% (B01/B11, audited FY26). Debt
fell hard: consolidated debt/equity 4.1x (FY25) to 1.6x (FY26), via the rights
issue plus non-core asset sales (B08). CFO grew ~7.9x over three years, Rs 6.61cr
(FY23) to Rs 52.29cr (FY26) (B01). These are not narrative claims; they are in
the filings.

**3B. Growth Triggers (ranked by conviction, B05).**
1. SECL Porda-Chimtapani overburden-removal contract, Rs 3,422cr over ~140
   months, secured Q4 FY26 (B05, B07). It anchors a Rs 4,600cr order book at
   11.9x FY26 revenue (B07). Conviction Medium. Confirmed by FY27+ mining-segment
   revenue at run-rate; killed by execution delay or tender dispute.
2. Chennai MMLP rail terminal, commissioned Dec-2025 on the guided timeline (the
   one delivered management promise, B05), ramping toward a stated 50 to 70
   rakes/month. Conviction Medium. Confirmed by rake-traffic disclosure reaching
   the range; killed by utilization stalling.
3. Delevering optionality: Rs 111.30cr land held-for-sale earmarked for debt
   repayment within 12 months (B05, B11), plus a possible refinance of the 12.5%
   related-party debt.

**3C. Moat Assessment.** Existing moats are location/land scarcity (owned CFS
land plus the ~82-acre Chennai MMLP parcel) and CFS customs-licensing barriers
(B04). Emerging moats are narrow but documented: customer-embedded mining
relationships (C1, Strong), Chennai geographic first-mover (E1, Strong), a
single-project execution record (F2, Moderate) (B07). Direction: modestly
expanding at the terminal, but Gate0 confirmed 0/12 durable moats and EM stays
MODEST at 17.4, below the 25 threshold that would earn a Pillar 3b premium.

**3D. Management Quality.** Post-insolvency sponsors are professional and
non-family (ex-CONCOR/IRTS backgrounds), replacing the fraud-tainted
Coffee Day / Chidambaram chain; board refreshed with three new independent
directors; parent PLIL filed a confidential SEBI DRHP for ~Rs 1,200cr, which
would subject the ultimate holdco to IPO-grade governance (B08). This is real
transition evidence. Delivery record is Mixed (grade C): 1 delivered, 2 partial,
1 missed; the QIP-to-rights pivot was unexplained and the CFO resigned 9-Jul-2026
mid-ramp with no disclosed succession (B05).

**3E. Market Opportunity.** TAM Rs 94,200cr to Rs 164,000cr, SAM ~Rs 64,000cr,
current SAM share only ~0.6%, so revenue headroom is ~166x (B09). The mining
order book alone (Rs 4,600cr) is ~15% of Mining SAM already booked, a credible
signal. Runway is classed MASSIVE, but B09 itself flags this is low penetration
of a large pool, not a near-term capture guarantee, and the SOM-implied 38.5%
3yr CAGR is an upper bound off a tiny base.

**3F. Peer Positioning.** Against GDL (~9 to 11x EV/EBITDA), Allcargo (~7.5x),
KTIL (~12.2x, distressed), Sical trades at ~16.1x actual EV/EBITDA (B11). It is
middle-of-pack operationally but priced at ~2.3x the peer-anchored fundamental.
The premium is not justified by owners' clean ~1% pre-exceptional ROE.

**The genuine open question for the devil's advocate:** the SOTP credits the
owned Chennai-park and terminal land at BOOK value only. Market land value is
NOT FOUND (B11). A large verified re-valuation of that land could lift the
Terminals slice and its NCI-adjusted owners' share materially. This is the one
lever that could move the SOTP, and it is currently un-priced in either
direction.

## NARRATIVE (plain English)

Sical Logistics looks, at a glance, like exactly the transition-alpha setup the
strategy hunts: a post-insolvency logistics operator with professional new
sponsors, margins that doubled, debt that more than halved, and a Rs 4,600cr
order book that covers revenue nearly twelve times over. Strip the one line that
matters and the picture changes. The FY26 profit is a land sale. Take out the
Rs 55.59cr one-time gain and the listed parent lost money on operations, and the
profitable part of the group, the Chennai terminals, is only about half owned by
the shareholders who buy this stock. So an investor at Rs 107 is paying about
sixteen times EV/EBITDA and roughly six-and-a-half times book for a business
whose owners' clean earnings are negative, whose largest single risk (a pledged
promoter block at 56.75%) is past the level the framework treats as a
deal-breaker, and whose entire growth story leans on one coal-sector counterparty.

Every valuation path lands in the same place. The sum-of-the-parts, which is the
right lens because it isolates the ~49% of the terminals that owners do not own,
puts fair value near Rs 22 today, and even that credits the land only at book,
with market land value unknown. The approved exit multiple, applied to a
generous forward earnings ladder, puts a three-year target between Rs 29 and
Rs 42. The bull case, everything clicking, still lands at Rs 68, which is a loss
from Rs 107. The expected return is minus thirty-eight percent a year and the
Hurdle Ratio stops the analysis cold. The honest bull point to keep on the shelf
is the land: a real market re-valuation of the owned Chennai parcels could lift
the terminals value and the owners' share of it, and that number is genuinely not
found rather than proven small. That is the one thing worth watching, alongside
whether the parent can earn an operating profit without selling something.
Until price meets Rs 12 to Rs 15 and the parent shows two clean quarters and the
pledge comes down, the answer is do not buy.
