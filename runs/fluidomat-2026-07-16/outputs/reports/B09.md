## SECTION 1: MARKET DEFINITION

### 1A. Precise Boundaries

**Product scope (IN):** Hydrodynamic (fluid) power-transmission couplings — fixed-speed
(delay-fill, soft-start) and variable-speed (scoop-controlled) — used to connect a prime
mover (electric motor / IC engine) to driven equipment, for smooth start-up, load
de-clutching, torsional-vibration damping and stepless speed control. Includes hazardous-area
(ATEX / Zone 0-1, water-filled, steel-body) variants and IC-engine drain-nozzle couplings
(FNIC/FNCT series) for off-highway and mobile equipment. Power range up to ~6,000 kW
(Fluidomat's stated max; larger marine/utility-scale units up to tens of MW exist globally
but are outside Fluidomat's addressable range and are excluded).

**Product scope (OUT):** Electronic variable-frequency drives (VFDs) — a substitute
technology, sized separately below as the substitution boundary (Section 1A "Substitution
boundary"); rigid/flexible mechanical couplings (gear, disc, jaw types) that do not use a
hydrodynamic fluid circuit; automotive torque converters and automatic-transmission fluid
couplings (a vastly larger but functionally and commercially distinct market serving
passenger-vehicle OEMs, not industrial EPCs/PSUs); gearboxes, belts, and other power
transmission products.

**Geographic scope:** India first (headline TAM), global shown separately. Within India,
demand is nationwide (plants in MP, Odisha, Chhattisgarh, Gujarat, Maharashtra, coastal
refinery/port belts) — Fluidomat's single Dewas (MP) plant + 4 sales offices (Mumbai, Delhi,
Chennai, Kolkata) already covers the relevant geography structurally.

**Customer scope:** Original-equipment purchase by power generation (thermal/coal), steel,
cement, mining, material handling (port/coal handling), petroleum & refinery, chemical &
fertilizer, and off-highway/IC-engine-drive OEMs and end-users (PSUs, EPCs, private
conglomerates) — i.e., B2B capital-goods buyers, not retail/individual consumers.

**Channel scope:** Direct engineering sales + OEM/EPC vendor-empanelment relationships
(NTPC, Coal India, ONGC, BPCL, HPCL, Adani, L&T, Tata, Vedanta type accounts per
investor-presentation client list, p.16). No significant retail/distributor channel for this
product category.

**Price segment:** Full range — Fluidomat's own products span roughly ₹1 lakh (small
fixed-speed couplings for pumps/fans) to several tens of lakh per unit (large variable-speed,
ATEX-rated units for mining/power). No explicit ASP disclosed by the company (data gap).

**Explicit inclusions:** New-equipment fluid couplings sold with fresh plant/machinery
installations; aftermarket spares, retrofit, repair/AMC revenue on the 60,000+-unit
installed base (per investor presentation p.5,18 — "60,000+ couplings installed worldwide,"
alternatively broken out as "5,000+ variable speed | 60,000 fixed speed units" on p.5, an
internal inconsistency in the company's own disclosure that we flag rather than resolve).

**Explicit exclusions:** VFDs and any electrical/electronic speed-control alternative;
automotive torque converters; non-hydrodynamic couplings; unrelated power-transmission
products (gearboxes, belts, bearings) even when sold by the same diversified competitors
(e.g., Ghatge Patil Industries, which sells fluid couplings as one line within a broader
automotive-components/foundry business).

**Substitution boundary (VFDs):** Electronic VFDs are the dominant alternative technology
for variable-speed drive applications (fans, pumps, conveyors). VFDs offer finer speed
control and, for many duty cycles, better part-load efficiency, and their hardware cost has
fallen steadily. Fluid couplings retain a durable niche where: (a) the environment is
hazardous/dusty/humid and solid-state electronics are a reliability or certification risk
(ATEX zones, underground mining, cement raw-mill, coal handling); (b) torque
shock-absorption / torsional-vibration damping is itself the requirement, not just speed
variation (crushers, IC-engine drives); (c) very high inertia starts are needed without
inrush-current penalty; and (d) capex-sensitive retrofits favour the lower first cost and
simpler maintenance of a mechanical coupling over a VFD + motor-rewind package. The
Indian VFD market is estimated in the low-single-digit-billion-USD range (Grand View
Research India VFD outlook: ~US$0.49bn, 2025) — roughly 5-9x the size of our conservative
India fluid-coupling TAM estimate (below), underscoring that fluid couplings are a
shrinking-share niche within total variable-speed drive spend, not a market growing in line
with total industrial automation capex. This is treated as a Section 4 risk (disruption/
substitution), not folded into the TAM.

Search sources blocked full-text access (403 on maximizemarketresearch.com, 6wresearch.com,
expertmarketresearch.com, grandviewresearch.com India-VFD page) — figures above are drawn
from search-engine result snippets, a secondary-source limitation flagged in the search log.

Taking the **conservative (lowest credible) global figure of ~US$1.07bn (2025)** and the
**upper end of ~US$1.9bn (2025)** as bookends:
- Global TAM conservative: US$1.07bn × ₹86 = **₹9,200 Cr**
- Global TAM upper/realistic: US$1.9bn × ₹86 = **₹16,340 Cr**

**India share of global demand:** No report gave an explicit India-consumption percentage.
A trade-data proxy exists (India accounts for ~32% of global fluid-coupling *export
shipment units* — 1,959 of 6,079 units, per aggregated customs-data-based search
snippets) but this measures India's manufacturing/export competitiveness, not India's own
*consumption* share, and cannot be used directly. As a demand-side proxy, we instead use
India's share of the global installed base of the end-industries fluid couplings serve:
India represents roughly 8-11% of global steel and cement production capacity, and a
smaller (~5-7%) share of global coal-fired thermal generation capacity (directionally, not
a specific cited figure — NOT FOUND as a single sourced percentage; treated as an
estimate). Applying a **5-9% India demand-share band** to the global bookends:
- India TAM (conservative): ₹9,200 Cr × 5.4% ≈ **₹500 Cr**
- India TAM (realistic): ₹16,340 Cr × 5.5% ≈ **₹900 Cr**

Confidence: **Low-Medium (L-M)**. Staleness: global base reports are current (2025 base
year); the India-share overlay is an estimate, not a sourced percentage — flagged.

### Method 2 — Bottom-up (addressable units × realization)

Addressable unit: one fluid coupling sold (new build or replacement) into an Indian
industrial installation per year.

- Fluidomat's own current manufacturing capacity: **1,500 units/year**, running at **~96%
utilization** (both figures per FY2025-26 Investor Presentation, "Capex & Expansion Plan,"
p.22 — "existing utilisation already at ~96%"), expanding to **3,500 units/year** by
~FY2028-29 via the ₹35 Cr capex programme.
- If Fluidomat — one of a handful of Indian organized-sector players in a niche where
"only 5 countries globally" have indigenous design capability (investor presentation p.5) —
holds an estimated 25-40% share of India's *organized* new-unit demand (a reasoned
estimate, not sourced; Fluidomat does not disclose market-share data), implied total
organized India annual unit demand = 1,440 (current output) ÷ 0.25-0.40 ≈ **3,600-5,760
units/year**.
- Adding an unorganized/import-substitute layer (per pipeline convention, 30-60% of
Indian industrial-component markets are unorganized; we take the lower end, ~25-35%,
given this is a technically certified, ATEX/PSU-vendor-approved niche with real barriers
to informal-sector entry) brings total India annual unit demand to a **4,500-8,000
units/year** range.
- Realization per unit: back-calculated crudely from Fluidomat's own P&L — FY26 "Sale of
products" revenue ₹71.53 Cr (₹7,153.05 lakh, Note 27) against ~1,440 units effectively
produced/shipped implies an average revenue-per-unit of **~₹5.0 lakh**, but this figure
commingles new units and spares (per B04 handoff: "new couplings + spares, commingled,
NOT separately disclosed") and spans a very wide power range (small pump/fan couplings
to 6,000 kW mining units), so we widen the assumed realization band to **₹5-12 lakh/unit**
to reflect the true product-mix spread.
- India TAM (units-based) = 4,500-8,000 units × ₹5-12 lakh = **₹225 Cr (low×low) to ₹960
Cr (high×high)**; taking the middle of each band (6,250 units × ₹8.5 lakh) gives a point
estimate of **~₹530 Cr**.

Confidence: **Low (L)** — built almost entirely on assumed market-share and realization
bands rather than sourced data; useful as a sanity check on Method 1's magnitude, not a
standalone anchor.

### Method 4 — Import substitution

Not straightforwardly applicable: India is a **net exporter** of fluid-coupling
manufacturing capability, not an importer. Fluidomat's own raw-material/component import
dependency is negligible — "Component consumed – imported" was **0.00% in FY25** and a
trivial 0.04% in FY24 (AR Note 29.1), and India is cited as the largest exporter of fluid
couplings globally by shipment count (~32% of a 6,079-unit sample, per search-snippet
trade data). The relevant "substitution" dynamic in India is therefore *organized-for-
unorganized* formalization (captured in Methods 2/3) rather than classic import
substitution. We do not use this method for a standalone TAM figure; it instead supports
the "import substitution" growth driver in Section 4A.

### Method 5 — Global benchmark (per-capacity comparison)

Using India's share of global installed capacity in Fluidomat's core end-markets as a
sense-check on the Method 1 share overlay:
- Steel: India ~220 MTPA of ~1,878 Mt global crude steel capacity ≈ **~10-11%** share
(current status per National Steel Policy tracking, mid-2026).
- Cement: India ~370 Mt of ~4,100 Mt global cement capacity ≈ **~9%** share (approximate,
not independently re-verified this run — carried from general industry knowledge, flagged
as an estimate).
- Coal-fired thermal power: India's share of global coal generation capacity is smaller in
relative terms (~5-7%, directional) because China dominates global coal capacity; but
India's *incremental* coal-capacity growth (88-100 GW planned addition by 2032-35, per
Ministry of Power context) is a larger share of *global incremental* coal capacity, since
most other major economies are retiring rather than adding coal plants.

This confirms the 5-9% band used in Method 1 is a reasonable order of magnitude,
tilted toward the lower end for thermal power (a large chunk of Fluidomat's own end-market
exposure) and higher for steel/cement. Confidence: **Medium (M)** for the directional
check, **Low (L)** for precision.

### 3B. SOM at 3 and 5 Years

**Current SAM share:** FY26 revenue ₹72.5 Cr ÷ SAM ₹275 Cr = **~26% current share of SAM.**
This is a plausible, not-implausible figure for an established (55-year) niche player with
five decades of accumulated installed base and PSU vendor relationships — it is
*not* being treated as a low-share "story stock," which matters for calibrating how much
share-gain room realistically remains.

**Share-gain trajectory (per standard rules):**
- 3-year: Normal share gain is 1-2pp; Fluidomat qualifies for the **aggressive 3-5pp**
band because it has committed, funded capacity expansion (1,500 → 3,500 units/year,
i.e. +133%, via the ₹35 Cr internally-funded capex programme, FY26-FY29) — capacity *and*
execution evidence both present. We take **+4pp**, i.e. share moves 26% → 30% by year 3.
We explicitly do **not** invoke the ">5pp only on competitor exit/acquisition, or faster
where unorganized share >40% is formalizing" exception, because we have no sourced data
on the unorganized share of this specific niche (Method 3 gap) to support that stronger
claim.
- 5-year: incremental **+3pp** more (26% → 33% cumulative, +7pp over 5 years), reflecting
that the capacity expansion completes within the window and gives two further years to
convert it to share, but not accelerating beyond the "aggressive" ceiling given
unconfirmed unorganized-share dynamics.

**SAM growth assumption:** TAM growth rate of **~7%/year** (blended from steel-capacity
CAGR required to hit 300 MTPA by 2030-31 from ~220 MTPA today, ≈6.4%/year; thermal
coal-capacity CAGR implied by 88-100 GW addition over ~8 years on a ~283 GW base,
≈3.6%/year; and faster cement/mining/material-handling capex growth pulling the blend
up — see Section 4A for driver-level evidence). SAM compounds at the same 7%.

| Year | SAM (₹Cr, @7%/yr) | Fluidomat share | SOM (₹Cr) |
|---|---|---|---|
| FY26 (base) | 275 | 26% | 72.5 (= actual FY26 revenue) |
| FY29 (3-yr) | 275 × 1.07³ = 337 | 30% | **101** |
| FY31 (5-yr) | 275 × 1.07⁵ = 386 | 33% | **127** |

**Implied revenue CAGR, shown arithmetic:**
- 3-year: (101 / 72.5)^(1/3) − 1 = (1.393)^0.333 − 1 ≈ **11.7%**
- 5-year: (127 / 72.5)^(1/5) − 1 = (1.752)^0.20 − 1 ≈ **11.9%**

This is a **formal handoff to Stage 11**: the SOM-implied revenue CAGR is ~12% at both
horizons — well below the strategy's 25% CAGR transition-alpha target, and also below
what a naive reading of management's "enormous demand" framing would suggest. Stage 11
should cross-check its own revenue-growth assumptions against this ~12% figure and
require explicit justification for anything materially higher.

### 3C. Capacity Cross-Check

No formal B07 capex_embedded_growth_pct figure was handed off to this stage (input gap —
noted in YAML). We instead cross-check directly against the capacity data disclosed in
the AR/investor presentation: capacity rises from **1,500 to 3,500 units/year** by
~FY28-29 (₹35 Cr capex, internal accruals, 40% capital subsidy eligible, per investor
presentation p.22 and AR MD&A p.36).

Using the Method 2 realization band (₹5-12 lakh/unit, midpoint ~₹8.5 lakh), 3,500
units/year of *theoretical* capacity implies a maximum revenue ceiling of roughly
**₹175-280 Cr/year** — comfortably above the SOM_3yr figure of ₹101 Cr (FY29, the same
year capacity expansion completes).

**Conclusion: capacity is sufficient.** The capex plan is, if anything, **the more
optimistic side of the comparison** — management is building capacity for a revenue
outcome (~₹175-280 Cr ceiling) well above what our conservative SOM math implies
(₹101 Cr by FY29). This is consistent with either (a) management genuinely expecting
faster share-gain/export/aftermarket traction than our conservative SOM assumes, or (b)
capacity being sized with a margin of safety / longer payback horizon in mind, or (c) the
company hedging against underlying market-growth or export-recovery scenarios more
optimistic than our base case. We do not resolve which; we simply flag the gap (capacity
supports ~1.7-2.8x more revenue than our SOM_3yr figure) for Stage 11 and Stage 13 to
weigh against margin/execution evidence developed elsewhere in the pipeline.

## SECTION 5: SUMMARY & RUNWAY

### 5A. Funnel Diagram

```
GLOBAL TAM (conservative-realistic): ₹9,200 Cr  —  ₹16,340 Cr   (~US$1.07bn – US$1.9bn)
        │  (India ≈ 5-9% of global demand, estimated)
        ▼

        │  × ~55% (5-filter SAM haircut: product/geography/channel/customer/capability)
        ▼

        │  × current share ~26% (FY26 actual)
        ▼
CURRENT REVENUE (FY26 actual):          ₹72.5 Cr
        │  → share gain +4pp (3yr, aggressive/capacity-backed) → 30% of grown SAM (₹337 Cr)
        ▼

        │  → share gain +3pp more (5yr) → 33% of grown SAM (₹386 Cr)
        ▼

```

### 5B. Runway Assessment

- **Revenue headroom** = SAM ÷ current revenue = ₹275 Cr ÷ ₹72.5 Cr ≈ **3.8x**.
- **TAM growth rate** ≈ **7%/year** (blended steel/thermal/cement-cycle estimate, see
Section 3B).
- **Company CAGR vs. TAM:** FY26 company revenue growth was **~0%** (₹72.46 Cr vs. ₹72.18
Cr FY25) — currently *lagging* the ~7% estimated market growth rate, i.e. Fluidomat is
presently losing relative share, not gaining it, on a trailing basis. The forward SOM
math (Section 3B) assumes this reverses via the capacity expansion; that reversal has not
yet shown up in reported numbers.
- **Years to saturate SAM at the SOM-implied ~12% CAGR:** ln(3.8) / ln(1.12) ≈ 1.335 /
0.1133 ≈ **~12 years**.

### 5C. Runway Classification

**MODERATE.** Revenue headroom of ~3.8x sits in the moderate band (below the 5x+ typically
associated with "GOOD" or higher classifications), TAM growth is mid-single-digit-to-
high-single-digit (not explosive), and the company's own trailing growth is currently flat
— i.e., below the market's growth rate, not above it. This is not a runway-constrained
"LIMITED" situation (there is real, multi-year headroom and a funded capacity plan), but it
is also nowhere near "MASSIVE" or "STRONG" — the ₹11+ lakh crore framing in management's
own materials is not supported by the underlying served-market math.

### 5D. SAM Expansion Levers Actually Being Pursued

1. **Capacity/product-mix upgrade (funded, in progress):** ₹35 Cr capex raising capacity
1,500 → 3,500 units/year with an explicit shift toward "higher-range coupling units" —
credible lever, already committed. Potential SAM-capture addition: difficult to size
precisely without a sourced ASP-mix figure, but directionally supports the higher end of
our SOM band rather than a wholly separate addition.
2. **Aftermarket/spares monetization (stated intent, not yet separately monetized):**
Management explicitly states intent to "expand aftermarket services and spares business,
which provides annuity-like revenue streams" on a 60,000+-unit installed base (management
commentary, investor presentation p.21) — but spares/services revenue is **not separately
disclosed** (0.8% of revenue is tagged "Services" per B04 handoff; the rest is commingled
"Sale of products"). If even a modest 2-3%/year replacement-cycle rate applied to the
installed base at a ₹1-3 lakh average spares ticket, this could represent a currently
under-monetized pool worth roughly **₹120-360 Cr** of *cumulative addressable* aftermarket
spend — a real, identifiable lever, but one the company has not yet demonstrated it is
capturing at scale (no separate disclosure exists to confirm current capture rate).
3. **Export/geographic expansion (stated intent, but currently moving backward):** Capex
rationale cites entering new geographies, but export share has *declined* from ~5% to ~3%
of revenue over the two most recent disclosed years — this lever's credibility is weak
until the trend reverses.

Levers 1-2 are credible and could plausibly add **~₹100-200 Cr** to the effective SAM over
a 5-7 year horizon if executed; lever 3 should not be counted until the export trend
inflects. We do not fold this into the base-case SOM figures above (which already reflect
a reasonably aggressive share-gain assumption), but flag it as identifiable upside for
Stage 11/13 to weigh.

### 5E. Final Output Card

- **India TAM:** ₹500 Cr (conservative) – ₹900 Cr (realistic)
- **Global TAM:** ₹9,200 Cr – ₹16,340 Cr (US$1.07bn – US$1.9bn)
- **SAM:** ₹275 Cr (~55% of conservative India TAM)
- **Current SAM share:** ~26%
- **SOM (3-yr, FY29):** ₹101 Cr | **SOM (5-yr, FY31):** ₹127 Cr
- **SOM-implied revenue CAGR:** ~11.7% (3-yr) / ~11.9% (5-yr)
- **Revenue headroom:** 3.8x | **Runway class: MODERATE**
- **Management's TAM claim vs. conservative estimate:** ~2,200x — **inflated** (management

- **Capacity check:** sufficient — the FY26-FY29 capex plan (3,500 unit/year ceiling,
implying a ~₹175-280 Cr revenue capacity) exceeds the SOM_3yr figure of ₹101 Cr; **the
capex plan is the more optimistic side of this comparison**, not the SOM.

**Valuation implication line:** At **~12% revenue CAGR** implied by SOM, with a margin
trajectory of roughly **30%** (normalising down from FY26's 35% EBITDA margin toward the
FY23/24 historical band of ~27%, before any operating-leverage benefit from the capacity
expansion), the earnings growth embedded here is on the order of **~10-13% CAGR**, which

FY26 PAT, per injected task context; note the FY26 investor presentation itself states a

PAT — a ~3-week-old, lower figure that we flag as an internal inconsistency rather than
resolve). Either reading places the stock on a growth-to-multiple mismatch versus the
pipeline's 25% CAGR transition-alpha target, absent either (a) SAM-expansion-lever
execution (Section 5D) materially exceeding our base case, or (b) multiple compression.
