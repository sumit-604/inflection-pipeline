## Business Understanding Narrative

Graphite India bakes petroleum coke into electrodes and sells them to steel
mills. An electric arc furnace melts scrap by passing current through graphite
rods, and those rods burn away. Every tonne of steel made this way consumes
electrode. The customer is the furnace, the product is consumed in use, and
demand is a direct function of how much scrap the world melts.

That makes this a spread business. The company buys needle coke and sells
electrode, and it earns the difference. It does not set either price. Both are
global and both move without asking. FY26 net sales were Rs 2,852 cr, up 11.4%,
and management attributes the growth to "increase in volumes with flat
realizations" (doc MSTZX2 p.4). Volume rose. Price did not.

What is changing is capacity and utilisation, not the nature of the business. Q4
FY26 capacity utilisation reached 104%, against 81% in Q4 FY25 (doc MSTZX2 p.6).
The plants are full and running past nameplate. So management is adding more:
"The first phase of electrode capacity expansion is expected to be commissioned
in the current financial year" (doc MSTZX2 p.6).

Why now, in management's telling, is steel. It forecasts Indian demand rising
7.4% in 2026 and 9.2% in 2027 (doc MSTZX2 p.6), and global steel demand growing
2.2% to 1,762 million tonnes in 2027 (doc MSTZX2 p.8).

What must be true for a shareholder is that price follows volume. Full
utilisation with flat realisation produces a thin result. FY26 EBITDA was
Rs 375 cr and net profit Rs 171 cr on Rs 2,852 cr of sales (doc MSTZX2 p.6).
That is a 13.2% EBITDA margin and a 6.0% net margin [both DERIVED].

What breaks the case for this strategy is simpler than any of that. There is no
transition here. The company is not claiming to climb a rung. It is claiming a
better year in the same business, and it holds Rs 3,767 cr of cash net of gross
debt while it waits (doc MSTZX2 p.4).

## 2. Business model and archetype

Graphite India makes graphite electrodes for electric arc and ladle furnaces,
plus a smaller carbon and speciality range. Hindustan Aeronautics buys carbon
brushes and graphite bushes for aerospace (doc SAewkD p.27), which shows a high
value niche exists alongside the commodity.

Archetype: **commodity converter.** The library's four markers are the right
lens. Spread is needle coke in against electrode out. Utilisation is 104% in
Q4FY26. Cycle position is the open question. Cost curve rank is NOT FOUND.

The group is wider than the Indian plants. Graphite Cova GmbH is a wholly owned
step down German subsidiary (doc ul3lMB p.41). Graphite International B.V. holds
51.806% of General Graphene Corporation in the United States (doc ul3lMB p.208),
and General Graphene is listed as an associate of the group (doc ul3lMB p.198).
Graphite Vicarb India Limited also appears (doc ul3lMB p.197).

Revenue split between electrodes, other carbon products and the German
operation: NOT FOUND.

The one economic engine is utilisation against a fixed cost base. Electrode
plants are capital heavy and their margin swings violently with volume and
price. At 81% utilisation the business is ordinary. At 104% it is better. At
104% with rising realisation it would be very good, and that combination is not
in FY26.

The second engine, and it is not operating, is the cash. Rs 3,767 cr of net cash
sits on the balance sheet against a Rs 14,349 cr market value. Management named
"treasury income impact" as a factor in the FY26 profit outcome (doc MSTZX2
p.6). Investment income is material to reported earnings.

## 3. Competitive advantages

Real at the industry level, thin at the company level.

The industry barrier is genuine. Needle coke is scarce, electrode plants take
years to build and qualify, and the number of credible global suppliers is
small. That barrier protects every incumbent equally. It is not an advantage
Graphite India holds over its peers.

Cost advantage: not evidenced. Cost curve position is NOT FOUND, and it is the
only advantage that matters for a converter.

Pricing power: no. Realisations were flat in a year when volumes rose and
utilisation hit 104%. A business with pricing power raises price when its plants
are full. This one did not.

Switching cost: low. Electrodes are qualified by grade and diameter, and mills
run multiple suppliers.

Scale: moderate globally, and the scale that matters is cost, which is unknown.

Scarcity: the needle coke input is scarce, and that cuts both ways. It protects
the industry and it squeezes anyone without integrated supply. Whether Graphite
India has secure coke supply is NOT FOUND, and it is a question worth asking.

The one genuinely differentiated asset is the aerospace and speciality carbon
line, evidenced by the HAL relationship. Its size is NOT FOUND, and on the
revenue split available it cannot be material.

Verdict: **moat absent at the company level.** The industry has barriers. This
company has no demonstrated edge inside them.

## 6. The transition and the quality ladder

FROM R1, commodity price taker. TO R1.

This is the finding of the card. **No rung migration is claimed anywhere in the
reachable record.** Management's forward statements are a capacity expansion and
a set of steel demand forecasts. Neither is a change in the kind of business.

Management's claim, quoted once: "The first phase of electrode capacity
expansion is expected to be commissioned in the current financial year" (doc
MSTZX2 p.6).

More capacity in the same product, sold at a price the company does not set, is
growth at the same rung. Under the ladder that is an earnings event, not a
re-rating engine.

A case for R2, cost advantaged converter, could exist if the company sits low on
the global cost curve. Its rank is NOT FOUND. Flat realisation at 104%
utilisation argues against pricing strength but says nothing about cost.

The one genuine rung optionality is General Graphene Corporation, the 51.806%
held United States venture (doc ul3lMB p.208). Graphene is a different material
with different economics. Its revenue, losses and stage are all NOT FOUND. Treat
it as an unpriced option of unknown size, and as a possible cash drain.

## 12. Verdict card

**WATCH.** Revised 2026-09-10 from PASS. Still do not spend a full `/step1`.

Read the change precisely, because only half of the original verdict moved.

**What has not changed.** Graphite India is not climbing the quality ladder. It
is a global commodity converter selling into a price it does not set, and no
rung migration is claimed anywhere in the corpus. On the strategy's own first
question this name still fails, and a 30% price rise does not alter that. An
industry wide price rise lifts every producer at once. It creates earnings; it
creates no advantage.

**What has changed.** The original PASS leaned on one observation: realisations
stayed flat while utilisation went from 81% to 104%, which I read as proof that
no pricing power existed. That reading was wrong in an important way. Flat
pricing at full plants was not evidence of a structurally weak business. It was
a cycle at its bottom. The card itself named the disproof, and the disproof has
arrived from outside the company.

The mechanics are worth stating plainly. At 104% utilisation there is no spare
volume, so a price rise flows to profit with very little cost against it. That
is why electrode earnings move in multiples rather than percentages, in both
directions. FY26 EBITDA of Rs 375 cr on Rs 2,852 cr of sales is a 13.2% margin
carrying a large fixed cost base. Price is the whole operating leverage.

**Why WATCH and not PROCEED.** Three reasons, and they are about timing, not
about the business.

First, the price rise is announced, not realised. Electrode contracts settle
over quarters. Nothing in any filed document yet shows this company's
realisation per tonne rising.

Second, the market has already moved. The stock rose as much as 18% on the news
to a 52-week high. Buying a cyclical after the sector has repriced is the
opposite of the recognition gap this framework hunts.

Third, and unchanged from the original verdict, this is a cycle trade. It needs
entry price and cycle timing, which this framework is not built to answer, and
it does not suit a 25% CAGR target held over three to five years. A converter
bought at a cycle turn can double and can also give it all back.

Load-bearing facts, now for a run rather than for the file:
1. Realisation per tonne, disclosed, quarter by quarter. This is the whole thing.
2. Volume in tonnes, and how much of the book is contracted versus spot. A
   producer locked into old contracts captures the rise late.
3. Needle coke cost and whether supply is contracted or spot. A price rise
   driven by input inflation is worth far less than one driven by shortage.
4. Position on the global electrode cost curve.
5. Treasury income as a stated line, separated from operating profit. A material
   part of the Rs 171 cr FY26 net profit is investment income on the cash pile.
6. General Graphene Corporation. Revenue, losses and funding commitment.

What would change the view to PROCEED:
- Realisation per tonne rising in this company's own disclosure, with the
  contracted share of the book stated. That converts an industry announcement
  into this company's earnings.
- A disclosed cost curve position in the lowest quartile, which would move the
  name from R1 to R2 and make the climb real rather than cyclical.
- A price that reopens a gap. The name got more expensive on the day the news
  broke, and the framework buys gaps, not headlines.

What would return it to PASS:
- Evidence that the announced rise is not converting into Indian realisations,
  or that it is being consumed by needle coke and energy inflation.

No valuation, no target price and no position is set here.
