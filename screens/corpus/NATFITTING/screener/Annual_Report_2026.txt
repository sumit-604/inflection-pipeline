Communication to Shareholders
Building the Next Phase of Growth
Dear Shareholders,
FY 2025-26 was the year in which several strategic decisions set in motion over the preceding two years 
came to completion, and in which the Company’s next phase of growth began to take visible shape.
The Company achieved its highest-ever revenue, and - on a like-for-like basis excluding last year’s one-time 
exceptional gain - its strongest operating profit in at least five years. That improvement was not incidental. It 
reflects the benefit of the consolidation undertaken in FY 2023-24 and FY 2024-25 (the sale of the Thekkalur 
unit and relocation of foundry operations to Vedasandur), which has now had two full years to translate into 
better utilisation, tighter cost control and a stronger product mix.
The single most significant development of the year was the completion of the change in the Company’s 
promoter group. M/s Himgiri Castings Private Limited, together with persons acting in concert, is now the 
Company’s promoter, bringing over 40 years of experience in the pipe fittings and castings industry. Because 
castings are one of the Company’s most critical raw material inputs, this is not a change in ownership alone 
— it is a strategic alignment that gives National Fittings Limited direct access to deep casting expertise for 
the first time in its history.
Three initiatives now under way translate that alignment into concrete growth for the Company:
●	
Backward integration into castings — two fully integrated manufacturing locations under direct 
ownership. The proposed merger of M/s Banil Castings Private Limited and M/s Avisa Private 
Limited into National Fittings Limited — approved by the Board on 22nd May, 2026 and now 
progressing through statutory and judicial approvals — brings the Company’s principal casting 
supplier directly onto the Company’s own balance sheet. On completion, National Fittings Limited 
will have two manufacturing locations — Goa and Gujarat — each 100% fully integrated, meaning 
each independently carries out both casting and finishing, and each under the Company’s direct 
ownership and control. Neither location is dependent on the other for casting or finishing. This gives 
the Company genuine diversification and de-risking of its manufacturing supply chain, with both 
ends of the process under its own direct control at two separate sites, rather than depending on 
a single site or an external, related-party casting supplier. This is expected to secure raw material 
supply and remove a layer of related-party dependency that has historically sat outside the listed 
entity.
●	
New capacity in Gujarat — a 100% fully integrated facility in its own right, and a step-change 
in scale. The Company to build a new, fully integrated manufacturing facility at Village Mudhela, 
Gujarat, independently capable of both casting and finishing, with a total capacity of 2,500 MT per 
month (approximately 30,000 MT per annum) — over four times the Company’s current capacity of 
600 MT per month (approximately 7,200 MT per annum). Gujarat was selected over the Company’s 
existing Tamil Nadu locations for the ready availability of power, labour and raw material — 
availability that Tamil Nadu does not offer to the same degree. Together with the Goa location under 
direct ownership through the Banil Castings merger — also 100% fully integrated — this gives the

that comes with depending on a single site or on an external, related-party casting supplier.
●

office from Tamil Nadu to Gujarat, completed this year with all statutory approvals in place, positions 
the Company’s legal seat alongside its newest and largest planned manufacturing investment.

are to complete the Banil Castings and Avisa merger, commission the Gujarat facility on schedule, secure 
the domestic approvals described in the next Section, and convert the resulting capacity, supply security 
and market access into sustained, profitable revenue growth — increasingly from India’s own infrastructure

Strengthening Focus on the Indian Domestic Market
National Fittings is, to the Board’s knowledge, the only 100% Made-in-India OEM manufacturing grooved 
piping products of this kind in the country. Despite that, and despite the exceptional pace of India’s own 
infrastructure investment over the past decade, the Company has not been at the forefront of that growth. A 
meaningful share of the domestic market the Company is capable of serving has instead gone to imported 
products. The Board considers closing that gap — converting the Company’s genuine Made-in-India status 
into actual order share — the single most important reason to build a much stronger domestic presence, and 
this is now the Company’s central strategic priority.
The Company’s export business has historically been anchored in the Gulf Cooperation Council (GCC) 
region, and the GCC remains, and will continue to remain, a strategically important market for the Company. 
That said, the regional conflict that erupted in the Gulf on 28 February 2026 — since eased by an April 
ceasefire and a June diplomatic framework — caused real disruption this year, and underlined that growth 
cannot rest on any single market alone, however important that market is to the Company. Building a stronger

MANAGEMENT DISCUSSION AND ANALYSIS REPORT
1. Financial and Operational Performance
FY26 was a year of strong operational progress for National Fittings Limited, reflecting the benefits of 
the strategic initiatives undertaken over the past two years.
Revenue from Operations increased by 16.98% to ₹91.40 crore, while Total Income increased by 
18.61% to ₹96.19 crore, representing the highest annual revenue achieved by the Company. Improved 
demand across domestic and export markets, coupled with better operational efficiencies and enhanced 
manufacturing utilisation, contributed to this growth.

The Company’s underlying profitability improved significantly during the year. Profit Before Tax before 
exceptional items increased by 85.8%, from ₹7.02 crore in FY25 to ₹13.04 crore in FY26, demonstrating 
the positive impact of operational consolidation, improved cost management and a stronger product mix.
Reported Profit Before Tax and Profit After Tax are lower than the previous year as FY25 included an 
exceptional gain arising from the sale of manufacturing assets as part of the Company’s restructuring 
programme. Accordingly, the reported profits for FY25 and FY26 are not directly comparable. On a like-
for-like basis, the Company’s core operating performance improved substantially during the year.
The Company continued to maintain a strong financial position with shareholders’ funds increasing to 
₹89.05 crore and cash and cash equivalents remaining healthy at ₹44.23 crore, providing adequate 
financial flexibility to support future growth opportunities.
2. Strategic Developments
FY26 marked an important milestone in the Company’s strategic evolution.
The successful completion of the change in the Company’s promoter group following receipt of all 
regulatory approvals introduce a promoter group M/s Himgiri Castings Private Limited with over 40 years of 
experience in the pipe fittings and castings industry. This represents a strong strategic alignment with the 
Company’s core business, as castings constitute one of the principal raw material inputs for its products. 
The new promoter group’s extensive manufacturing expertise is expected to strengthen the Company’s 
capabilities through greater backward integration, improved manufacturing processes, enhanced quality 
consistency and stronger supply chain resilience.
During the year, the Board also approved a Scheme of Arrangement for the merger og M/s Banil Castings 
Private Limited and M/s Avisa Private Limited with National Fittings Limited. The proposed merger is 
currently progressing through the statutory approval process and is expected to further strengthen the 
Company’s manufacturing platform through greater operational integration.
The proposed integration is expected to improve coordination between casting and downstream 
manufacturing operations, optimise procurement, enhance production planning and strengthen long-term 
competitiveness while supporting sustainable and profitable growth.
3. Market Environment
Demand conditions improved during FY26, contributing to the Company’s highest-ever annual revenue. 
Export markets continued to remain an important contributor to business, while domestic infrastructure 
investments created additional growth opportunities.
We continue to focus on specialised grooved piping products where our quality, manufacturing capabilities, 
certifications and customer service differentiate us from lower-cost competition. Our ability to manufacture 
technically demanding and specialised products enables us to maintain longstanding customer relationships 
across domestic and international markets.
The Company also continues to benefit from increasing awareness of quality standards, growing 
infrastructure investments and the continued adoption of reliable mechanical piping systems across 
industrial and commercial applications.

Geopolitical Environment
The Middle East remains one of the Company’s important export markets. While infrastructure investments 
across the region continue to present significant opportunities, ongoing geopolitical developments have 
the potential to affect project execution schedules, customer purchasing decisions, logistics and freight 
costs. We continue to work closely with our customers and distribution partners to minimise any potential 
disruptions while maintaining the flexibility to respond to changing market conditions.
International Trade
Recent changes in tariff policies and global trade dynamics have the potential to alter competitive 
conditions across several export markets. While these developments may create new opportunities for 
Indian manufacturers in certain regions, the eventual response of competing suppliers through pricing, 
sourcing and market strategies remains uncertain. The Company continues to closely monitor these 
developments and remains committed to maintaining its competitiveness through product quality, operational 
efficiency and customer service.
Foreign Exchange
As an export-oriented business, the Company remains exposed to fluctuations in foreign exchange rates. 
Management continues to closely monitor currency movements while maintaining a balanced approach 
between domestic and export sales to mitigate the impact of exchange rate volatility. Raw Material and 
Supply Chain Castings remain one of the Company’s principal raw material inputs. The completed promoter 
transition and the proposed integration of casting operations are expected to strengthen supply chain 
resilience, improve manufacturing coordination and reduce long-term procurement risks through greater 
backward integration. The Company also continues to diversify its supplier base and optimise inventory 
management to minimise supply chain disruptions.
Outlook
FY26 represents the completion of one phase in National Fittings Limited’s evolution and the beginning 
of another.
The operational restructuring undertaken over recent years has translated into stronger operating 
performance, improved profitability and record revenues. The successful transition to the new promoter 
group introduces significant industry expertise that complements the Company’s existing strengths, while 
the proposed merger of Banil Castings Private Limited and Avisa Private Limited is expected to further 
strengthen manufacturing integration and operational efficiency.
Although geopolitical developments and evolving global trade policies continue to create uncertainty, we 
believe the Company is well positioned to navigate these challenges through its strengthened balance 
sheet, integrated manufacturing strategy, diversified customer base and continued focus on quality and 
customer service.
As we enter FY27, we remain committed to building on the strong foundation established over the past 
several years. Our priorities continue to be operational excellence, profitable growth, manufacturing 
integration and long-term value creation for our shareholders and all other stakeholders.

Meaning of Internal Financial Controls over Financial Reporting
A company’s internal financial control over financial reporting is a process designed to provide reasonable 
assurance regarding the reliability of financial reporting and the preparation of financial statements for 
external purposes in accordance with generally accepted accounting principles. A company’s internal financial 
control over financial reporting includes those policies and procedures that (1) pertain to the maintenance 
of records that, in reasonable detail, accurately and fairly reflect the transactions and dispositions of the 
assets of the company; (2) provide reasonable assurance that transactions are recorded as necessary to 
permit preparation of financial statements in accordance with generally accepted accounting principles, and 
that receipts and expenditures of the company are being made only in accordance with authorizations of 
management and directors of the company; and (3) provide reasonable assurance regarding prevention 
or timely detection of unauthorized acquisition, use, or disposition of the company’s assets that could 
have a material effect on the financial statements.
Inherent Limitations of Internal Financial Controls over Financial Reporting
Because of the inherent limitations of internal financial controls over financial reporting, including the 
possibility of collusion or improper management override of controls, material misstatements due to error 
or fraud may occur and not be detected. Also, projections of any evaluation of the internal financial 
controls over financial reporting to future periods are subject to the risk that the internal financial control 
over financial reporting may become inadequate because of changes in conditions, or that the degree 
of compliance with the policies or procedures may deteriorate.

1.2	Significant accounting policies
a)	 Statement of compliance
	
The Standalone Financial Statements comply, in all material aspects, with Indian Accounting Standards 
(‘Ind AS’) notified under Section 133 of the Companies Act, 2013 (‘the Act’) read with Rule 3 of the 
Companies (Indian Accounting Standards) Rules, 2015 and other relevant provisions of the Act.
b)	 Basis of Preparation
	
These financial statements have been prepared in accordance with the Generally Accepted Accounting 
Principles in India (‘Indian GAAP’) to comply with the Accounting Standards specified under Section 
133 of the Companies Act, 2013, read with Rule 7 of the Companies (Accounts) Rules, 2014 and the 
relevant provisions of the Companies Act, 2013. The financial statements have been prepared under the 
historical cost convention on accrual basis, except for certain items which are measured at fair value.
	
Fair value is the price that would be received to sell an asset or paid to transfer a liability in an orderly 
transaction between market participants a the measurement date, regardless of the price is directly 
observable or estimated using another valuation technique.
	
All assets and liabilities have been classified as current or non current as per the Company’s normal 
operating cycle and other criteria set out in the Schedule III of the Companies Act, 2013 and Ind AS, 
based on the nature of products and the time between the acquisition of assets or processing and their 
realisation in cash and cash accruals
c)	 Property, plant and equipment

e)	 Leases
	
The determination of whether an arrangement is (or contains) a lease is based on the substance of the 
arrangement at the inception of the lease. The arrangement is, or contains, a lease if fulfillment of the 
arrangement is dependent on the use of a specific asset or assets and the arrangement conveys a right 
to use the asset or assets, even if that right is not explicitly specified in an arrangement.
	
(i) As a lessee
	
A lease is classified at the inception date as a finance lease or an operating lease. Leases of property, 
plant and equipment where the company, as lessee, has substantially all the risks and rewards of 
ownership are classified as finance leases.
	
Leases in which a significant portion of the risks and rewards of ownership are not transferred to the 
Company as lessee are classified as operating leases. Payments made under operating leases are 
charged to profit or loss on a straight-line basis over the period of the lease unless the payments are 
structured to increase in line with expected general inflation to compensate for the lessor’s expected 
inflationary cost increases.
	
(ii) As a lessor
	
Leases are classified as finance leases when substantially all of the risks and rewards of ownership 
transfer from the Company to the lessee. Amounts due from lessees under finance leases are recorded 
as receivables at the Company’s net investment in the leases. Finance lease income is allocated to 
accounting periods so as to reflect a constant periodic rate of return on the net investment outstanding 
in respect of the lease.
	
Lease income from operating leases where the company is a lessor is recognised in income on a 
straight-line basis over the lease term unless the receipts are structured to increase in line with expected 
general inflation to compensate for the expected inflationary cost increases. The respective leased 
assets are included in the balance sheet based on their nature.

Financial instruments, Financial Assets, Financial Liabilities
	
Financial assets and financial liabilities are recognised when a Company becomes a party to the 
contractual provisions of the instruments.
Initial Recognition
	
Financial assets and financial liabilities are initially measured at fair value. Transaction costs that are 
directly attributable to the acquisition or issue of financial assets and financial liabilities (other than 
financial assets and financial liabilities at fair value through profit or loss and ancillary costs related to 
borrowings) are added to or deducted from the fair value of the financial assets or financial liabilities, as 
appropriate, on initial recognition. Transaction costs directly attributable to the acquisition of financial 
assets or financial liabilities at fair value through profit or loss are recognised immediately in Statement 
of Profit and Loss.
Classification and Subsequent Measurement: Financial Assets
	
The Company classifies financial assets as subsequently measured at amortised cost, fair value through 
other comprehensive income (“FVOCI”) or fair value through profit or loss (“FVTPL”) on the basis of 
following:
	
the entity’s business model for managing the financial assets and
	
the contractual cash flow characteristics of the financial asset.
	
(i)	 Amortised Cost
	
	
A financial asset shall be classified and measured at amortised cost if both of the following conditions 
are met:
 	
	
—	 the financial asset is held within a business model whose objective is to hold financial assets in 
order to collect contractual cash flows and
	
	
—	 the contractual terms of the financial asset give rise on specified dates to cash flows that are 
solely payments of principal and interest on the principal amount outstanding.
	
(ii)	 Fair Value through other comprehensive income
	
	
A financial asset shall be classified and measured at fair value through OCI if both of the following 
conditions are met:
	
—	 the financial asset is held within a business model whose objective is achieved by both collecting 
contractual cash flows and selling financial assets and
	
—	 the contractual terms of the financial asset give rise on specified dates to cash flows that are solely 
payments of principal and interest on the principal amount outstanding.
	
(iii)	Fair Value through Profit or Loss
	
	
A financial asset shall be classified and measured at fair value through profit or loss unless it is 
measured at amortised cost or at fair value through OCI.
	
	
All recognised financial assets are subsequently measured in their entirety at either amortised cost 
or fair value, depending on the classification of the financial assets.

Classification and Subsequent Measurement: Financial liabilities
	
Financial liabilities are classified as either financial liabilities at FVTPL or ‘other financial liabilities’.
	
(i)	 Financial Liabilities at FVTPL
	
	
Financial liabilities are classified as at FVTPL when the financial liability is held for trading or are 
designated upon initial recognition as FVTPL. Gains or Losses on liabilities held for trading are 
recognised in the Statement of Profit and Loss.
	
(ii)	 Other Financial Liabilities:
	
	
Other financial liabilities (including borrowings and trade and other payables) are subsequently 
measured at amortised cost using the effective interest method.
	
	
The effective interest method is a method of calculating the amortised cost of a financial liability and 
of allocating interest expense over the relevant period. The effective interest rate is the rate that 
exactly discounts estimated future cash payments (including all fees and points paid or received 
that form an integral part of the effective interest rate, transaction costs and other premiums or 
discounts) through the expected life of the financial liability, or (where appropriate) a shorter period, 
to the net carrying amount on initial recognition.
Impairment of financial assets
	
Financial assets, other than those at FVTPL, are assessed for indicators of impairment at the end of 
each reporting period. The Company recognises a loss allowance for expected credit losses on financial 
asset. In case of trade receivables, the Company follows the simplified approach permitted by Ind AS 
109 – Financial Instruments for recognition of impairment loss allowance. The application of simplified 
approach does not require the Company to track changes in credit risk. The Company calculates the 
expected credit losses on trade receivables using a provision matrix on the basis of its historical credit 
loss experience.
Derecognition of financial assets
	
The Company derecognises a financial asset when the contractual rights to the cash flows from 
the asset expire, or when it transfers the financial asset and substantially all the risks and rewards 
of ownership of the asset to another party. If the Company neither transfers nor retains substantially 
all the risks and rewards of ownership and continues to control the transferred asset, the Company 
recognises its retained interest in the asset and an associated liability for amounts it may have to pay. If 
the Company retains substantially all the risks and rewards of ownership of a transferred financial asset, 
the Company continues to recognise the financial asset and also recognises a collateralised borrowing 
for the proceeds received.
	
On derecognition of a financial asset in its entirety, the difference between the asset’s carrying amount 
and the sum of the consideration received and receivable and the cumulative gain or loss that had been 
recognised in other comprehensive income and accumulated in equity is recognised in profit or loss if 
such gain or loss would have otherwise been recognised in profit or loss on disposal of that financial 
asset.
Derecognition of financial liabilities
	
A financial liability is derecognized when the obligation under the liability is discharged or cancelled or 
expires. When an existing financial liability is replaced by another from the same lender on substantially

Credit risk
