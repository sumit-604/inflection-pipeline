## PART 2. Unprompted cross-read

### 2A Demand environment
Split, and none matches a boom. CAPLIPOINT: group +10-11% in FY26 quarters, +20% in Q1 FY27 on a tender, with "gestation and consolidation" language for Mexico and Chile (Q3 FY26, p.8; Q1 FY27, p.5). It is capacity-limited, not demand-limited: "our current challenge is not a lack of orders but a lack of capacity" (Q1 FY27, C.C. Paarthipan, p.3). SENORES: emerging-market +21-30% with a strong second half, regulated +28-42% (Q2 FY27, p.4; Q1 FY27, p.5). LINCOLN: 12-18% (Q3 FY26, p.3). Against this, KPL's guide of 720+ Cr on H1 343.6 Cr (B05) implies growth well above any peer except Senores' early quarters. No peer mentions an industry-wide LATAM or Africa demand boom (silence noted: not one peer claims market growth).

### 2B Pricing and input costs
CAPLIPOINT holds gross margin at 59.8% and reprices its own stock (Q1 FY27, p.6; Q4 FY26, p.14). SENORES lifts emerging-market realisation per unit but gives up margin on competitive beta-lactam (Q4 FY26, p.10; Q2 FY27, p.7). LINCOLN gives no 2026 pricing (Q3 FY26). Input inflation is mild and logistics-driven (oil, shipping). KPL's Q1 gross margin of 46.2% against a 52.1% Q2 (B05) is a larger swing than any peer reports; the peer pricing story does not explain it.

### 2C Capex cycle
Not a lone expander and not an industry-wide race. CAPLIPOINT is the one large expander: about ₹1,000-1,100 Cr of capex, 17 injectable lines in 2-3 years, all from internal cash and zero bank loans (Q4 FY26, p.18; Q1 FY27, Vivek Partheeban, p.4-5, p.11). SENORES is calibrating: capex ₹100-120 Cr in FY27, sterile injectables downsized, oral solids prioritised (Q1 FY27, p.10-13). LINCOLN wants a new unit or an acquisition to pass ₹750-800 Cr on its current base (Q3 FY26, p.13-14), with capex funded internally. The common pattern is expansion funded from cash. KPL's net borrowing of +15.8 Cr in H1 (B05) differs from Caplin and Lincoln, which describe their capex as debt-free.

### 2D Competitive mentions
None. No peer names KPL or describes it. One cross-peer reference exists: an analyst contrasts Lincoln with Caplin on owning warehouses (Q3 FY26, Saket Saurabh, p.12). The silence is itself information: KPL does not register in these peers' competitive conversation.
