### 2B. Excuse pattern analysis

- **External-blame instances**: Middle East war and geopolitical disruption were cited repeatedly across the Q4 FY26 and Q1 FY27 calls to explain margin compression (RMC cost +33% YoY, execution cost +43% YoY, Q4 FY26 call), UAE segment weakness ("genuine regional headwinds," Q1 FY27 call), freight/logistics cost increases, and delayed order conversions. This is a materially real external factor (an actual regional conflict), but it is also the dominant explanatory frame in both later calls, used alongside language crediting the team's own execution ("our integrated in-house model enabled us to navigate these disruptions far more effectively than fragmented EPC structures," Q1 FY27 call) — i.e. blame the war for the miss, credit the team for the resilience.
- **Honest-admission instance**: Aasif Khan, Q3 FY26 call, on the stock's post-listing fall: "the stock has crashed, leading to the loss of shareholder trust. Honestly speaking, we were not very communicative, and we have realised that people..." — a genuine, unprompted self-critical admission. This is the single clearest accountability moment across all three calls.
- **Deflection instances**: stock-price/investor-confidence question (Q4 FY26 and Q1 FY27 calls) answered with "the stock price is something which you know is beyond the management's control" rather than a concrete plan; order-book segment/geography split requested in all three calls and never given as hard numbers; revenue-recognition-method question answered with process description ("we are debating, we are deliberating") rather than a date.
- **Silence**: the auditor's Key Audit Matter on receivables overdue beyond 365 days (Rs 58.9 Cr, per task briefing) and the FY26 jump in provisioning were never raised by management nor asked by any analyst across all three calls, despite receivables continuing to climb on-call (Rs 204.34 Cr at Mar-26 → Rs 215 Cr at Jun-26, explicitly discussed by an analyst without the >365-day aging ever coming up). Undisclosed related-party land/lease arrangements (per task briefing) were likewise never mentioned in any of the three calls.
- **Pattern check**: Do they blame externals while crediting themselves? Yes, this is the dominant pattern in the two most recent calls. Do they ever say "we made a mistake"? Yes, once (Q3 FY26 communication admission) — but it is not repeated or built upon in later calls. Do they raise hard topics proactively or only under analyst pressure? Overwhelmingly the latter — related-party-transaction percentages, IPO-fund deployment delay, receivables levels, and the revenue-recognition transition all surfaced only because an analyst asked.
- **Classification: external-blame-heavy**, tempered by one genuine early admission that was not sustained as a pattern in subsequent calls.

## SECTION 3: COMPETITIVE INTELLIGENCE FROM CONCALLS

### 3A. What management says about competitors, with credibility check

Management states repeatedly (Q3 FY26 and Q4 FY26 calls) that Fabtech has "no direct or indirect listed peers in the SME or main board in India," and that its real competition is European players, not Indian or Chinese firms — "we don't compete with Asians. We compete with Europeans" (Q1 FY27 call). This is a favourable, differentiation-maximising framing that has not been tested against any named competitor on any call — management "usually refrain[s] from talking about competition... we prefer not talking about European companies" (Q3 FY26 call). **Credibility check**: plausible given the specialised turnkey pharma-EPC-in-MENA/Africa niche, but entirely self-reported and untested; this is exactly the kind of claim the peer-concall stage should verify against Anup Engineering, HLE Glascoat, Ion Exchange, and Praj Industries, all of which touch adjacent process-equipment / EPC segments (see peer_questions below).

### 3B. Industry and market intelligence dropped in the calls

- Pharma emerging-market opportunity sized by management at "over north of $30 billion" over the next 10 years (MENA/Africa/Southeast Asia), with the West "a different ballgame, more than $70 billion in the next 10 years" (Q1 FY27 call, Aman Anavkar) — no third-party source cited.
- Saudi Vision 2030 GCC localization: "local content and in-kingdom presence are increasingly hard qualification criteria... not just preferences" (Q1 FY27 call) — cited as the direct driver of the SACE local-entity investment.
- Post-COVID "medicinal sovereignty / medicinal independence" theme cited consistently across all three calls as the structural demand driver for MENA/Africa/GCC pharma manufacturing capacity build-out.
- Animal health / veterinary pharmaceutical manufacturing flagged as an emerging, previously-unanticipated growth vertical in Africa ("a big industry in Africa, which we thought will happen much later, but it's happening now," Q3 FY26 call), later evidenced by actual order wins (Botswana, BioBago Saudi).
- Raw-material-cost (RMC) inflation of ~33% YoY and execution-cost inflation of ~43% YoY in FY26, attributed to Middle East war-driven freight and logistics disruption (Q4 FY26 call).
- Egypt currency devaluation cited as a market-specific risk requiring geographic diversification to manage (Q4 FY26 call, Ashwani Singh).
- Development finance institutions (DFIs), World Bank agencies and European funding agencies cited as a growing funding source for pharmaceutical-emerging-market capex, benefiting Fabtech's project pipeline (Q1 FY27 call).

### 3D. Customer and order book signals

- **New wins across the three quarters**: West Africa tablet-capsule turnkey facility ($7.05M, 23-27 month execution); Saudi animal-vaccine facility ($7.8M, 11-month timeline); North Africa EU-GMP veterinary formulations facility (Rs 49-52 Cr, targeted Q1 FY27 completion); GCC cell & gene therapy and oncology projects; UAE vaccine fill-finish and medical-device projects; Botswana veterinary vaccine manufacturing facility (Rs 31.23 Cr, Q1 FY27); BioBago Saudi veterinary vaccine facility (~$7M, Q1 FY27); one civil order in Saudi via the newly acquired SACE entity (closed ~10 days before the Q1 FY27 call).

Do peer companies cite comparable market-sizing/growth-rate estimates for pharma EPC/cleanroom capex? *Why it matters*: sole source is management's own unattributed claim (Q1 FY27 call); it underpins the entire long-term growth narrative and the Rs 1,000 Cr+ by 2030 target. *Check peers*: Anup Engineering, HLE Glascoat, Ion Exchange, Praj Industries.
2. **Raw material / cost inflation claim** — RMC cost +33% YoY and execution cost +43% YoY in FY26, attributed to Middle East war and freight disruption. Do peer EPC/process-equipment companies report similar cost inflation over the same period, or is this company-specific? *Why it matters*: tests whether the margin-compression explanation is sector-wide or a company-specific procurement/hedging weakness. *Check peers*: Anup Engineering, HLE Glascoat, Ion Exchange, Praj Industries.
3. **Competitive-moat / no-Indian-peers claim** — Fabtech states it has no direct/indirect listed Indian peers and competes only with European players. Do Anup Engineering, HLE Glascoat, Ion Exchange, or Praj Industries compete for any of the same MENA/Africa turnkey pharma infrastructure tenders? *Why it matters*: tests a potentially self-serving differentiation claim that could overstate competitive moat/pricing power. *Check peers*: Anup Engineering, HLE Glascoat, Ion Exchange, Praj Industries.
4. **Market-share / ticket-size growth claim** — Average order ticket sizes have grown from $1.5-3M to $7-10M, with orders now up to $70M. Are peer companies seeing similarly rising average deal sizes in MENA/Africa pharma capex, or is this specific to Fabtech's localization/GCC strategy? *Why it matters*: tests whether this reflects a sector-wide demand shift (medicinal sovereignty theme) or company-specific execution. *Check peers*: Anup Engineering, HLE Glascoat, Ion Exchange, Praj Industries.
5. **Capex-cycle / cash-conversion claim** — Fabtech has not resolved its shift from shipment-based to percentage-of-completion revenue recognition across three consecutive quarters. What revenue-recognition method and typical receivable/working-capital cycle do peer EPC/turnkey process-equipment companies use, and is Fabtech's ~120-day working-capital cycle and rising receivables (Rs 204 Cr to Rs 215 Cr in one quarter) unusual for the sector? *Why it matters*: directly tests the cash-conversion credibility question flagged by the auditor KAM (Rs 58.9 Cr overdue >365 days). *Check peers*: Anup Engineering, HLE Glascoat, Ion Exchange, Praj Industries.
6. **Localization-mandate claim** — Management says GCC/Saudi "local content" rules are shifting from preference to hard qualification criteria under Vision 2030, driving the need for majority-local subsidiaries like SACE. Do peer companies with Middle East exposure report similar localization mandates, and how are they responding? *Why it matters*: tests whether Fabtech's capital-intensive local-entity strategy (Rs 24 Cr+ approved investment) is a necessary sector-wide adaptation or a discretionary, aggressive capital-allocation choice. *Check peers*: Anup Engineering, HLE Glascoat, Ion Exchange, Praj Industries.
