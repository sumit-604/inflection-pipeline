1. CASH FLOW STATEMENT vs NOTES — QUANTIFIED CASH-QUALITY FINDING (new; not covered in Pass 1,
   which scoped to Notes only and did not read the Cash Flow Statement)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
🟡 **Watch — most significant new finding of this pass.** The Cash Flow Statement (p.95) contains a
labelling/extraction artefact on its "Cash Generated from Operations" line that, read literally, would
show a NEGATIVE ₹2.40cr (240.0 lacs) for FY26 versus +₹5.436cr (543.6 lacs) FY25. This does not
reconcile arithmetically with "Net Cash generated from Operating Activities" one line below it
(₹23.892cr FY26 / ₹30.697cr FY25) net of "Direct Taxes paid" (₹9.720cr FY26 / ₹8.883cr FY25) —
reversing the tax add-back gives a TRUE "Cash Generated from Operations" of ₹33.612cr FY26 and
₹39.580cr FY25 (23.892+9.720=33.612; 30.697+8.883=39.580), which does tie exactly to "Operating
Profit before Working Capital Changes" (₹36.012cr FY26, ₹34.144cr FY25) plus the individual
working-capital adjustment lines (Trade Receivables, Other Current Assets, Inventories, Trade
Payables & Provisions, Other Current & Financial Liabilities). The printed (240.0)/543.6 figures are
in fact exactly the SUM of those five working-capital adjustment lines, not the true operating-cash
total — an extraction/formatting defect on that one row, not a genuine negative operating cash flow.
**Flag for the orchestrator: verify this row against the PDF/source filing before using CFO in any
downstream ratio.**

Correcting for the defect, the reconstructed cash-quality numbers are real and material:
- Net working-capital movement swung from a **+₹5.436cr tailwind** to operating cash in FY25 to a
  **-₹2.400cr headwind** in FY26 — a ₹7.836cr net negative swing in one year. Driven by: Inventories
  -₹1.998cr (FY26) vs +₹2.881cr (FY25); Trade Receivables -₹1.297cr vs -₹2.242cr; Trade Payables &
  Provisions -₹0.650cr vs +₹0.918cr; partly offset by Other Current & Financial Liabilities +₹1.730cr
  vs +₹0.097cr (this offset is largely the customer-advance build, see Section 4 below, not a genuine
  payables improvement).
- **Cash conversion of profit deteriorated**: true CFO-before-tax / PBT = 90.1% FY26 (33.612/37.302)
  versus 115.5% FY25 (39.580/34.271). The company went from generating MORE cash than accounting
  profit to generating LESS, in a single year, even as PBT rose +8.9% and PAT rose +8.6%.
- **Net Cash from Operating Activities fell -22.2% YoY** (₹23.892cr FY26 vs ₹30.697cr FY25) while PAT
  rose +8.6% — a genuine quality-of-earnings divergence, and a sharper, quantified version of Pass
  1's qualitative "cash conversion cycle widening from both ends" observation (Note 7/8/17).
- This is the single number that most concretely substantiates a FLAG-CASH designation; Pass 1's
  receivable-days/inventory/payable-days findings were individually 🟡 Watch, but this shows they
  cost the company real cash in FY26, not just a lengthening ratio.

2. NOTE 38(a) CREDIT RISK — INTERNAL CONTRADICTION MISSED IN PASS 1 (Note 38, p.115-116)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
🟡 Watch. Pass 1 flagged the zero-ECL fact; re-reading the two adjacent sentences in the same
sub-note shows an internal policy-vs-practice contradiction not called out before: "the Company uses
a matrix to compute the expected credit loss allowance for trade receivables. The provision matrix
takes into account available external and internal credit risk factors and Company's historical
experience for customers" is immediately followed by "(i) The company has not made any provision
for expected credit loss on trade receivables and other financial assets, based on the management
estimates." No matrix, no loss-rate-by-ageing-bucket, and no explanation of how a stated
matrix-based methodology produces an output that management then overrides to nil are disclosed.
The note claims a systematic method and reports a management-judgment override in the same
breath — a disclosure-quality gap distinct from, and sharper than, Pass 1's "adequacy cannot be
tested" framing.

3. DIRECTOR REMUNERATION DOUBLING — GOVERNANCE CONTEXT RESOLVED (cross-reference outside strict
   Notes scope, but directly resolves the Pass 1 Note 26/34 flag; sourced from the Secretarial
   Auditor's Report and the main Independent Auditor's Report bound in the same AR)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
🟡 Watch (re-characterised, not cleared). Two facts materially change how the +120% remuneration
jump (₹1.80cr → ₹3.96cr, Note 26 p.108 / Note 34(iii) p.112) should be read:
- The Secretarial Auditor's Report records that at the 16th AGM (18-Aug-2025, within FY26), members
  approved, **by Special Resolution**, reappointment of all three promoter-directors — Jogindersingh
  Jaswal as Chairman & Managing Director, Ketan Ramani and Pritesh Ramani as Whole-time
  Directors — each for a fresh three-year term (source line context ~p.54 of the bound AR, Secretarial
  Auditor's compliance report section). This is the corporate action that produced the FY26 revised
  pay structure (each director's remuneration moved from ₹0.60cr to ₹1.32cr, Note 34(iii)).
- The Independent Auditor's Report, Section 197(16) statement (main audit report, p. ~96 of the
  bound AR pagination, ahead of Annexure A): "the remuneration paid by the Company to its directors
  during the year is in accordance with the provisions of section 197 read with Schedule V to the
  Act."
- **Net effect on the flag**: this is not an opaque, unsanctioned insider payment — it is
  shareholder-approved (special resolution) and auditor-opined as Section 197/Schedule V compliant.
  The economic-scrutiny concern from Pass 1 stands on its own terms regardless of legal compliance:
  ₹3.96cr to three related, promoter-family directors is 14.3% of FY26 PAT, and the quantum jump
  (120% in one year, tied to fresh 3-year terms rather than phased increases) concentrates
  post-IPO profit growth into promoter compensation faster than it flows to minority shareholders.
  Recharacterise the Watch from "governance/self-dealing risk" to "quantum reasonableness given
  legal compliance is confirmed."

4. EMPLOYEE COST DECOMPOSITION — DIRECTOR PAY IS 81.5% OF THE TOTAL INCREASE (Note 26, p.108)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
🟢 Clean on the residual, sharper read of an existing flag. Total Employee Benefits Expense rose
₹2.649cr YoY (₹4.754cr → ₹7.403cr, +55.7%). Decomposing the increase: Director Remuneration alone
contributed ₹2.160cr of that increase (81.5% of the total). The remaining ₹0.489cr increase splits
across Salary/Wages/Bonus (+₹0.321cr, +13.9%, ₹2.317cr→₹2.638cr), Gratuity+PF/ESIC (+₹0.027cr),
and Staff Welfare Expenses (+₹0.141cr, +96.6% off a small base, ₹0.146cr→₹0.287cr). Rank-and-file
cost growth (total employee cost excluding director remuneration) was +16.6% YoY (₹2.954cr →
₹3.443cr) against flat 1.0% revenue growth — a genuine cost-inflation signal on its own, but far less
dramatic than the headline 55.7% total-employee-cost growth suggests once director pay is stripped
out. New precision on Pass 1 finding #2; not a new direction of concern.
