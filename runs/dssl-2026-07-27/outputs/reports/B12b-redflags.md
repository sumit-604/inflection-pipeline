## PART 1 — INDEPENDENT RED-FLAG LIST (from raw transcripts only)

Each anchored (call, speaker, PDF page). "IND-n" numbering is mine.

**IND-1 — Q3 margin assurance directly contradicted next quarter.**
Q3 FY26, Dharmesh Anjaria, PDF p10 (Neeraj Dalal Q&A): "we actively manage input costs and
currently do not see material pressures that would impact margins... strong OEM partnerships...
mitigate the price volatility." One quarter later, EBITDA margin falls from 11.9% (Q3) to ~9.0%
(Q4), blamed on supply-chain cost escalation. A clean promise-vs-outcome contradiction.

**IND-2 — Q3 "back-to-back / pricing already agreed" protection claim also contradicted.**
Q3 FY26, Management, PDF p11-12 (Madhur Rathi Q&A on RBI/hardware pass-through): "we have
back-to-back arrangements already with all the IT vendors... The pricing is already agreed... so
that our margins don't take a hit." Q4 shows margins did take a hit from the same hardware/
supply-chain channel. This is a second, distinct Q3 assurance (mechanism-level) broken in Q4,
separate from IND-1's general "no material pressure" statement.

**IND-3 — Unit economics of the As-a-Service/DaaS/CBaaS pivot withheld across BOTH calls.**
Q3 FY26 p6-7 (Ankush Agrawal on ROCE), p16 (Bhavana Jain-adjacent on ROA); Q4 FY26 p6
(Gunit Singh: "no IRR number... it's internal record. So I'm sorry we're not disclosing"), p8
(Ankush Agrawal on gross margin, refused). Investor states outright (Q4 p8): "as an investor it
would be very difficult for us to judge these new deals... you're not able to share the payback or
ROCE or IRR neither... any kind of gross margin understanding." A 2+ quarter repeated evasion on
the single most important number for a capex-heavy model pivot.

**IND-4 — Segment / order-book breakup refused both quarters, management self-references the
prior refusal.** Q3 p5-6 (Madhur Rathi, segment margins & order-book split), p15 (Bhavana Jain,
order-book bifurcation); Q4 p5 (Gunit Singh, told "as we have already explained in our last
Investor Call... for confidentiality reasons we do not give out breakup"). Repeated evasion.

**IND-5 — All forward revenue/margin guidance refused both quarters** under a stated no-guidance
policy (Q3 p4-5, p11, p16; Q4 p5, p13-14). Consistent policy, but it means every forward number
in the thesis is unverifiable against management.

**IND-6 — Net-vs-gross working-capital framing.** Q3 p12-13 (Bhagya Bansal): management admits
"the debtor days are increasing" but pivots to "net working capital cycle... we've been able to
improve." Q4 p9-10 (Akash Jain): trade receivables Rs301cr→Rs602cr over 4 years (Rs437→602,
+38% YoY per Aditya p12), payables Rs219→443cr, yet management leads with "net working capital
days... 14 days... now actually 17 days... very efficient." Management consistently presents the
flattering net figure and never volunteers a gross DSO number.

**IND-7 — Payable days 75→134 funding working capital; "pay OEMs early" deflected.** Q4 p10
(Akash Jain): "days payable increased... around 75 days now up to 134 days... healthy cash
balance... why not make early payments?" Management: credit period is a benefit, won't pay early.
The WC "efficiency" is substantially a stretched-payables artifact.

**IND-8 — Data-centre/cloud mix decelerated 37%→34%** in the single best year for the sector.
Q3 p5 ("14% to 37% as we are today"); Q4 p8 (Abhi Jain): "data center revenue has been flattish
this year... last year was flattish" vs a 50% CAGR history; management: "34% of our revenues
coming from data center," calls it "timing," not "flattening."

**IND-9 — FY26 revenue growth 12% vs historical ~20% CAGR, "blip" framing left uncontested.**
Q4 p8 (Abhi Jain): "FY26 was a blip... came down to a 12%ish otherwise we have been maintaining
20% CAGR." Management does not reaffirm the higher trajectory; cites project-timing instead.

**IND-10 — Scripted "temporary blip / short-term cost pressure / normal quarterly variation"
line repeated near-verbatim to FIVE Q4 analysts.** Ankush Agrawal (p7), Akash Jain (p11), Aditya
(p12), Saurabh Gupta (p13-14), Dhirendra Patro (p16). A rehearsed-deflection pattern on the one
material miss. (B05 counted four; the count is actually five — Akash Jain also received the line.)

**IND-11 — BHEL order dropped without explanation.** Q3 p6 (Ankush Agrawal Q&A): "BHEL has
started adding to the revenue." Q4: not mentioned once. A specifically-flagged new contributor
disappears with no update.

**IND-12 — Competitor-naming retreat.** Q3 p4-5: engaged candidly when analyst named Unified
Data and its 15-20% margins. Q4 p11 (Akash Jain): "whom do you consider as your nearest
competitor?" → "I would rather not name them on a public platform." A specificity retreat on
competitive positioning across quarters.

**IND-13 — Q4 PAT flat YoY despite +22% revenue growth; opex-model earnings quality question.**
Q4 p7 (Ankush Agrawal): "this quarter we have seen very healthy revenue growth... however the
PAT hasn't grown... clearly on account of increased depreciation and finance cost because of the
opex deals." Management's answer conflates sequential and YoY and never squarely resolves why
PAT did not grow with gross margin "pretty stable." For a GARP/earnings-growth thesis, flat
quarterly PAT on 22% revenue growth from the lease-heavy model is a distinct earnings-quality
signal, not just an EBITDA-margin story. (Implied Q4 PAT ≈ Rs19cr: FY26 Rs85cr − 9M Rs66cr.)

**IND-14 — Fixed-asset figure discrepancy Rs158cr (analysts) vs Rs68cr PPE (management), with a
quantification dodge.** Management opening, Q4 p4: "property, plant and equipment from INR8
crores in FY25 to INR68 crores." But Gunit Singh (p5-6) and Saurabh Gupta (p13) both read the
balance sheet/presentation as "fixed assets... from INR9 crores... to INR158 crores." Management
never reconciles the ~Rs90cr gap (almost certainly PPE Rs68cr + ROU/lease assets), and when
Saurabh asks twice to "quantify the amount," management dodges: "That is already mentioned"
(p14) — it was not. The true gross balance-sheet inflection (~Rs158cr) is ~2.3x the Rs68cr figure
management led with, materially understating the capital intensity of the pivot.

**IND-15 — Recurring "don't judge us sequentially, project-based" deflection used in BOTH
quarters to bury sequential weakness.** Q3 p9 (Bhavya Bansal): revenue −3.3% QoQ and PAT −3.6%
QoQ; management deflects with "we are in a project nature of business... quarter-on-quarter is not
a right matrix." Q4 p11 (Akash Jain): same "our business cannot be judged sequentially" line for
the margin drop. The Q3 sequential revenue/PAT decline is itself never surfaced as a data point;
the same script neutralised it in both quarters.

**IND-16 — Admission that Q4 margin pressure is partly a commercial choice, not purely external.**
Q4 p16 (Dhirendra Patro Q&A): "if it is a good acquisition customer, we may decide to win over
the customer... because of these reasons also the short-term margin pressures can come in." This
nuances — and partly undercuts — the "purely external, unavoidable AI-hardware supply chain"
narrative given to every other analyst: some of the compression is deliberate below-cost bidding
to acquire customers.

Minor / noted but not scored as an independent flag:
- RBI order size ambiguity: Q4 opening cites RBI EAP "~Rs249cr" (p2), but analysts Ankush (p6)
  and Binoy (p15) both reference "the RBI order... roughly Rs750cr" and management does not
  reconcile the Rs249 vs Rs750 gap on-call. B05 resolves this as two separate orders (EAP Rs249cr
  + Private Cloud Rs750cr won 4-May-2026) using the operator digest; the transcript alone leaves
  it unreconciled. This is a source-of-number question that belongs to Verifier A, not a management
  red flag, so I do not score it.
