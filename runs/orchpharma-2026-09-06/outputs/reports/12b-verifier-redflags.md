## PART 1: INDEPENDENT RED-FLAG LIST

### Group A — The earnings base does not reconcile, and no combined base exists

**A1. Dhanuka Laboratories EBITDA was refused across three consecutive quarters, and no
combined FY26 EBITDA is disclosed anywhere in the corpus.**

- Q2 FY26 (Nov_2025, p.11, Manish Dhanuka), asked by Sagar Arya for H1 EBITDA: "I don't have
  the EBITDA, it's non-listed. I would not have the EBITDA ready in this."
- Q3 FY26 (Feb_2026, p.10), asked by Rupesh Tatiya: Manish gives a band, "It remains same 5%,
  6%... Same what has been traditionally been between 5% to 8%", which Mridul immediately
  disclaims: "We don't calculate actually it's unlisted entity, right? So these are very,
  very broad estimates at this point."
- Q4 FY26 (Jun_2026, p.10), asked by Sagar: "EBITDA numbers, I don't have at this point of
  time. The audit is going on." Asked again by Rupesh (Jun_2026, p.16): "EBITDA PAT is
  currently being evaluated because that's a non-listed entity takes slightly more time.
  Revenue is INR450 crores."
- Q1 FY27 (Aug_2026): the merger is now effective and FY25/FY26 are restated combined, yet
  no combined FY26 EBITDA figure is given, and the standalone-to-combined earnings bridge is
  never drawn.

Four analysts, three consecutive quarters, one recurring question, no computed answer, on an
entity that is roughly 36% of combined revenue (Rs 450 cr of Rs 1,233 cr) and whose margin
determines the combined margin base. This is a repeated evasion in the rubric's sense, and it
leaves the corpus with no reliable combined FY26 earnings base at all.

**A2. The three disclosed combined FY26 figures do not reconcile to the standalone FY26
figures given three months earlier.**

Disclosed combined (Aug_2026, p.3 and p.4, Manish Dhanuka): revenue Rs 1,233 cr; gross margin
"approximately 4% to 32%"; "Combined employee and other operating expenses were broadly flat
at INR353 crores in FY26".
Constructed: Rs 1,233 cr x 32% = Rs 394.6 cr gross profit, less Rs 353 cr = **~Rs 42 cr
implied combined FY26 EBITDA**.
Disclosed standalone (Jun_2026, p.3, Manish Dhanuka): "FY '26 EBITDA stood at INR101 crores".
Adding Dhanuka at management's own stated 5-8% band on Rs 450 cr gives Rs 123-137 cr combined.

The gap is roughly Rs 81-95 cr and is never bridged. I do not claim to know which figure is
right; several innocent explanations exist (other income, a different gross-margin definition,
reclassification on restatement). The red flag is that the only forward-comparable basis, the
restated combined basis, has no stated earnings number and no reconciliation to the basis it
replaced. Stage 11 has no anchored combined FY26 EBITDA to build from.

**A3. The Q3 FY26 quarterly EBITDA figure contradicts the 9-month figure in the same
paragraph.**

Feb_2026, p.3, Manish Dhanuka, consecutive sentences: "EBITDA for the quarter was at 6% versus
17% last year. For the 9-month period, EBITDA stood at INR58 crores compared to INR115 crores
last year with a margin of 10% versus 17% last year."

- Q1 FY26 EBITDA Rs 14 cr and Q2 FY26 EBITDA Rs 6 cr (Nov_2025, p.3: "EBITDA for the quarter
  was INR6 crores compared to INR14 crores for the last quarter").
- 9M Rs 58 cr less Rs 20 cr = **Rs 38 cr implied for Q3, i.e. 18.4% of Rs 207 cr, not 6%.**
- Run the other way: 6% x Rs 207 cr = Rs 12.4 cr, giving 9M of Rs 32 cr, not Rs 58 cr, and a
  9M margin of 5.6%, not the 10% stated in the same sentence.
- The FY26 full-year figures settle which one is the outlier: Rs 101 cr less Q4's Rs 42.3 cr
  (Jun_2026, p.3) = Rs 58.7 cr for 9M, corroborating Rs 58 cr and isolating the "6%".

**A4. Combined Q1 FY26 EBITDA is stated below Orchid's own standalone Q1 FY26 EBITDA.**

Aug_2026, p.4: "EBITDA improved to INR25 crores in Q1 of '27 compared with EBITDA of INR10
crores in Q1 of '26" — combined basis. But Orchid standalone Q1 FY26 EBITDA was Rs 14 cr
(Nov_2025, p.3). Combined includes Dhanuka on top of Orchid, and Dhanuka is stated to run at
5-8% EBITDA margins, so combined should be at or above Rs 14 cr, not Rs 10 cr. Unexplained,
unasked. The direction of the discrepancy flatters the headline: a Rs 10 cr base makes Q1 FY27
a +150% improvement; a Rs 20 cr base would make it +25%.

### Group B — Framing that hides the sequential direction

**B1. Q1 FY27 revenue fell roughly 19% sequentially. The call presents only the YoY
comparison against the trough quarter, and an analyst's factually wrong sequential premise is
left uncorrected.**

Management (Aug_2026, p.4): "we have begun financial year '27 on a better footing. Revenue
from operations increased to INR304 crores in Q1 '27 from INR263 crores in corresponding
quarter last year, representing growth of approximately 15%."

Constructed Q4 FY26 combined, all components disclosed:
- Orchid standalone Q4 FY26 Rs 238 cr (Jun_2026, p.3)
- Dhanuka Q4 FY26 = FY26 Rs 450 cr (Jun_2026, p.10) less 9M Rs 305 cr (Feb_2026, p.10) =
  Rs 145 cr
- Intercompany elimination implied by Rs 811 cr + Rs 450 cr = Rs 1,261 cr against restated
  combined Rs 1,233 cr (Aug_2026, p.3) = Rs 28 cr for the year, ~Rs 7 cr pro-rata per quarter
- **Q4 FY26 combined ~Rs 376 cr, against Q1 FY27 combined Rs 304 cr: ~19% sequential decline.**

Q1 FY26 (Rs 263 cr) was the trough quarter, the one management itself later described as
carrying the maximum inventory loss (Jun_2026, p.8: "the first quarter, the loss was the
maximum"). Measuring growth against it, and never mentioning the sequential direction, is a
choice.

Worse, on the same call (Aug_2026, p.8) Nishita of Sapphire Capital says "in this in Q1, we
had a 15% Q-o-Q growth" — she has read the YoY number as sequential. Management does not
correct her: "Yeah, we hope to have better sales on Q-o-Q basis." A materially wrong premise
about the direction of the business was left standing.

**B2. The "historical" regulated:non-regulated baseline was restated downward one quarter
after the actual mix fell.**

- Nov_2025, p.13, Mridul Dhanuka: "Normally, the split for Orchid has always been 40-60, 40%
  regulated, 60% unregulated."
- Feb_2026, p.3, Manish Dhanuka: "our regulated versus nonregulated mix has **historically**
  been one-third regulated and two-third nonregulated. However, for this quarter, the split
  stands at approximately one-fourth."
- Jun_2026, p.9: "Our trend has been one-third, two-third... For this year, whole year, 30% is
  regulated and 70% is ROW."

The stated history moved from 40% to 33% in the quarter the actual number printed 25%. That
converts a 15-point shortfall into an 8-point shortfall. Regulated mix is, on management's own
account (Feb_2026, p.7), the primary swing factor in gross margin, which makes the baseline
the load-bearing number.

### Group C — The Exblifep disclosure pattern

- Nov_2025, p.6, Mridul Dhanuka: "I think we'll have the number by the next quarter... So

Three quarters, same question, and the answer travelled from "we will report it" to "policy
is we do not". The flagship asset has never had a rupee or euro figure attached to it on any
call in this corpus.

**C2. Europe growth rates were restated for two separate quarters, and a decelerating restated
series is characterised as a J-curve.**

| Quarter | Stated at the time | Restated later |
|---|---|---|
| Q3 FY26 | "growth has been more than 200%" (Feb_2026, p.3) | "approximately 300% in Q3 of '26" (Aug_2026, p.4) |
| Q4 FY26 | "about fourfold improvement over the previous quarter" (Jun_2026, p.4) | "170% in Q4 of '26" (Aug_2026, p.4); "in Q4, over Q3, it was 175%" (Aug_2026, p.11) |
| Q1 FY27 | "about 50%" (Aug_2026, p.4) | — |

Both restatements move; the Q4 one moves down by more than half. The Aug_2026 call also gives
Q4 as two different numbers (170% and 175%) eight pages apart. The restated series runs
300% -> 170% -> 50%, which is decelerating on every step, and Mridul describes it (Aug_2026,
p.11) as "we are seeing this J-curve continue to play out". A J-curve accelerates. Percentage
growth on an undisclosed base, restated downward, labelled with the wrong curve, is three
compounding disclosure problems on one line item.

**C3. The $1-2bn lifetime figure is retro-dated to 2021, contradicting what was said in

- Nov_2025, p.11, on whether the "$250 million product" number still holds, Mridul: "At this

- Jun_2026, p.7: "We still maintain the lifetime sales would be between $1 billion to $2

  mentioned earlier on the call, $1 billion to $2 billion lifetime sales."
- Aug_2026, p.11: "our long-term guidance on this is remaining the USD1.1 billion to USD2
  billion **that we came up with in 2021**."

A number said in November 2025 not to exist is, nine months later, attributed to 2021. The
range also drifts from $1-2bn to $1.1-2bn. Neither the basis switch (peak-annual to lifetime)
nor the provenance is ever reconciled.

**C4. Ex-India Exblifep is supplied from Allecra's legacy Chinese CMO, disclosed only under
direct questioning, and it contradicts the acquisition framing.**

Nov_2025, p.4, Manish Dhanuka, announcing the acquisition: "This is a transformative
development for Orchid. It gives us **full control** over the regulatory and commercial
strategy for this innovative antibiotic worldwide... **All future economic benefits**,
including the double-digit royalties from Advanz in Europe will now accrue directly to us."

Aug_2026, p.7, only when Tarun Krishna asks who manufactures for the licensees, Manish
Dhanuka: "we have acquired the asset from Allecra, and they already had a CMO in China. They
have a long-term agreement of providing the product from their CMO in China. So the supplies
will start from that CMO itself, but we will have our margin in the supplies... hopefully,
after two years, we would be able to supply some percentage from our side."

So for at least two years, Orchid's international Exblifep economics are a margin on a Chinese
contract manufacturer's output under an inherited long-term agreement, not own-manufacturing
economics. This materially changes the margin structure of the flagship asset, and it adds a
China supply dependency to the one molecule whose story is de-risking away from China. It was
never volunteered in three prior calls.

### Group D — 7-ACA, the Rs 750 cr decision

**D1. The captive/third-party split reversed, and the reversal was denied when challenged.**

Jun_2026, p.12, Vishal Manchanda: "your presentation suggests 75% will be used in-house. And
I think -- my understanding is you had guided 25% was something you were looking to use
in-house and the rest you will sell to third parties. Is there a change here or..."
Mridul Dhanuka: "**No, no, it's the same.** So Vishal, what we meant earlier was 25% is our
existing consumption and rest of it would be added to the downstream processing systems."
Aug_2026, p.6, Mridul: "our long-term guidance on this is 80% in-house use and 20% selling to
third party."

An analyst identified a reversal from 25% captive to 75-80% captive. The answer asserted
continuity rather than acknowledging a change of plan. The distinction is not cosmetic: it
determines whether 7-ACA is a merchant-sales revenue line or a captive cost saving, which is
exactly the fork Stage 11 has to take when crediting the project.

**D2. The PLI entitlement has shrunk to about two years because of project slippage, and the
question about it was answered without engaging the risk.**

Aug_2026, p.14, Ankur Chedda: "is there any risk to the PLI benefit due to the **slippage in
the timeline**, or has that matter been settled with the government?"
Manish Dhanuka: "PLI, we'll probably be entitled for 2 years. Based on the current scenario,
we'll be entitled for 2 years." Asked about an extension: "We will maybe try for extension
only once the plant gets started."

Two things happen here. The premise "slippage in the timeline" is not disputed, which is an
implicit confirmation. And a truncated incentive entitlement on the single largest capital
commitment in the plan is disclosed in one sentence, never quantified, and never revisited.

**D3. An 800x scale-up jump sits behind an 80-100% first-year utilisation target, with the
adverse peer precedent volunteered by management itself.**

Aug_2026, p.6, Mridul: "our ramp-up plan is to go to about 80% to 100% by the end of the first
year." Aug_2026, p.10, same speaker: "We've guiding 12 months, but... I have said that again
earlier that Aurobindo looking at other experience, it might be longer... We've been
successfully been able to commercial -- scale up our existing pilot plant by 20x, and we have
to another do about 800x from there to Jammu, but it's a little unpredictable."

I record this as candid rather than evasive. It is still a red flag on the plan, not on the
people.

**D4. The price-stability premise underpinning the capex is contradicted in mechanism by two
peers in this corpus.**

Orchid's premise (Aug_2026, p.6, Mridul Dhanuka): "in 7-ACA, that joint cooperation or
whatever you want to call it was never happened, and the prices have been stable. So,
therefore, I said I don't see much room for China to drop the prices." And p.6: "The pricing
in 7-ACA has been stable over the last 10 to 12 years. The weighted average is about USD60."

Peer evidence pointing the other way, both in this corpus:
- GRANULES-Concall_May_2026_Transcript.pdf, p.8, K.P. Chigurupati, on DCDA, where Granules is
  "the only DCDA manufacturer outside China": "what has happened during the process is, the
  Chinese competition, **they started reducing prices drastically**. And we have been trying
  to do better than them or at least meet those prices." This is an in-window, first-hand
  account of an Indian company attempting China-substitution in a chemical intermediate and
  being met with drastic price cuts.
- KOPRAN-Concall_Feb_2025_Transcript.pdf, p.8, Sanjay Dosi: "since India is going very
  aggressive on development of KSMs, whether synthetics or fermentation, we are seeing a trend
  where **China is dropping prices of KSMs**... So overall, I look at it as **the next
  battlefield will shift from APIs to KSMs**."

The claim actually at stake is not "what has 7-ACA cost historically" but "will China cut when
Orchid enters". On that question the peer set is not silent, and it points against the
premise. Kopran also puts a national timeline on fermentation-KSM self-sufficiency far longer
than Orchid's ramp plan (Feb_2025, p.8, Surendra Somani: "India still has a long way to go.
Maybe it will be more like a 5, 7 year time cycle"), and notes the peer relies on government
protection to make the economics work — while Orchid's PLI entitlement is down to two years
(D2).

**D5. Committed capex against disclosed liquidity is never addressed on any call, and never
asked.**

Disclosed commitments: Rs 750 cr for 7-ACA and USD 20-25 million for Cefiderocol (Aug_2026,
p.8, Mridul Dhanuka), plus ~Rs 50 cr for fill-finish (Jun_2026, p.8). Disclosed funding: a
Rs 450 cr planned 7-ACA facility with Rs 170 cr drawn, and Rs 75 cr of cash, Rs 60 cr of it
QIP money (Feb_2026, p.7). No call in the corpus addresses how the balance is funded, and no
analyst asks. The words "debt" and "cash" appear **zero times** in the entire Q1 FY27
transcript (verified by search).

## PART 3: PROMISE-DELIVERY SPOT CHECKS

Six checks. For each: did the earlier call actually contain the promise, and does the later
call actually show the stated outcome?

**SC1. "Q3 FY26: 7-ACA mechanical completion by September 2026 -> MISSED, revised to Q1
CY2027, ~4-6 month slip." — WRONG PAIRING.**
Promise confirmed: Feb_2026, p.4, Manish Dhanuka: "We continue to target mechanical completion
by September." Reinforced at p.12, Mridul: "September is mechanical completion and then first
commercial should take a quarter or 2 from that, water trials in 1 quarter and commercial
production in the next."
Outcome: Jun_2026, p.4: "still committed to the commissioning in the first quarter of calendar
year 2027." Aug_2026, p.5: "commissioning and the first commercial batch by March of '27."
The problem: B05 compares a **mechanical completion** milestone against a **commissioning**
milestone. On Feb_2026's own arithmetic (September + water trials one quarter + commercial the
next), first commercial lands around March 2027 — which is exactly what Aug_2026 says. The end
date did not slip between Feb_2026 and Aug_2026. What actually happened is worse in kind and
smaller in size: the September mechanical-completion milestone was never reported against on
either subsequent call, neither confirmed nor withdrawn. B05's "~4-6 month slip" is an
inference the transcripts do not evidence; the silently abandoned intermediate milestone is
the real, anchored finding.

**SC2. "Q3 FY26: licensing deal every quarter -> MISSED, admitted in Q4 FY26." — CONFIRMED.**
Promise: Feb_2026, p.12, Manish Dhanuka: "we are very hopeful maybe every quarter, at least
one or two announcements should happen."
Outcome: Jun_2026, p.6, Mridul Dhanuka: "I was hoping that today, we could have announced at
least one, but unfortunately, we don't have the definitive agreement signed yet." Direction and
characterisation both correct. I add one item B05 missed: Jun_2026, p.15, asked whether all
five named markets would be signed within six months, Mridul answered "I would say **50% will
be signed in for sure**." By Aug_2026 one of five (Russia) is signed and nobody on the call
holds him to it.
