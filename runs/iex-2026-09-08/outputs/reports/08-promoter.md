### 1D. History before the current structure — MATERIAL, NOT ROUTINE
IEX's absence of a promoter today is not an organic outcome. It is the
direct result of a regulator-forced exit of the company's ORIGINAL
promoter roughly a decade before this run.

IEX was founded and originally promoted by Financial Technologies (India)
Ltd (FTIL, controlled by Jignesh Shah), which held close to 26% of IEX at
one point. FTIL's group company National Spot Exchange Ltd (NSEL)
defaulted on a Rs5,574cr payment obligation in 2013. Following that
default, the Forward Markets Commission and SEBI ruled FTIL "not fit and
proper" to hold a 2%-or-more stake in any exchange, including commodity
and power exchanges. CERC directed FTIL to divest its entire IEX stake by
20-Jul-2014. Jignesh Shah resigned from the IEX board in 2014. FTIL
completed the IEX divestment in stages through 2014-2015.
📰 MEDIA REPORTED, multiple corroborating Business Standard / DNA India
reports (search log below), consistent across sources, not corroborated
inside this run's own corpus (the AR does not narrate its own 2014
history).

None of the current directors or KMP (Goel joined as MD/CEO Jan-2014,
the same year FTIL's exit was ordered; Bajaj and Harlalka joined later
in operating roles) were part of the FTIL-era control group. This is
history to weigh, not a current adverse finding against anyone on the
present board. It is reported here because Section 1D calls for founder
history and unexplained gaps, and "the company has no promoter" is itself
an unexplained gap unless this event is named. The takeaway: IEX's
promoter-less structure was born out of a serious governance failure
event at its own controlling shareholder, subsequently repaired by
divestment and professionalisation, not out of an unusually clean design
from listing.

  CONCLUDED, with findings and remedial action taken, as of 23-Jul-2026.
- The BRSR (filed as a standalone exchange document 14-Aug-2026, the
  same date the full Annual Report was filed): "the Company received a
  complaint under the said mechanism, which was reviewed and
  investigated in accordance with the established procedures.
  Appropriate actions were taken, wherever required." STATUS: also
  reads as CONCLUDED, as of the 14-Aug-2026 filing date.

**Resolution.** The two sets of disclosures are not, on close reading,
directly contradictory: they carry different as-of dates. The financial
statements and CARO annexure are dated 23-Apr-2026 (results-approval
board meeting) and say ongoing as of that date. The Board's Report and
the BRSR are dated 23-Jul-2026 and 14-Aug-2026 respectively (a later
board meeting ahead of the AGM) and describe the same matter as
concluded with findings and action taken. Read as a timeline, the
investigation appears to have run from sometime in FY26 through to
some point between 23-Apr-2026 and 23-Jul-2026, when it concluded.

What the evidence does NOT establish, and what stops this from clearing
to a clean "resolved" finding:
1. The Annual Report bundles BOTH the stale "ongoing" note (Notes 46/49,
   CARO xi(c)) and the newer "concluded" narrative (Board's Report,
   BRSR) into the SAME document, filed 14-Aug-2026, without a
   reconciling cross-reference or an updated note. A reader of Notes 46
   or 49 alone, which is where a financial-statement user would look
   first, receives stale information as at the filing date.
2. No document anywhere in the corpus or in web search names the
   officials, states what "appropriate actions" were taken (warning,
   reassignment, termination, recovery of funds), states whether any
   business was in fact diverted and at what value, or states whether
   the matter was reported to any external regulator (CERC, SEBI, MCA).
   "Do not have a material impact on the financial statements" is a
   financial-statement materiality judgment, not a governance-adequacy
   judgment.
3. Web search under multiple queries (see searches_performed) found no
   media coverage, no SEBI filing, and no court record naming this
   whistleblower matter; it appears to have generated no external
   scrutiny distinct from the regulatory disclosures themselves.
4. Given the CARO framing, "certain officials" plural and "potential
   diversion of business" are both serious characterisations. The
   combination of an unnamed subject, an unquantified allegation, and
   two internally inconsistent status statements in the same filed
   document is a genuine, named governance flag. It does not, on the
   facts available, rise to a deal-breaker (no fraud finding, no
   material impact stated, no auditor qualification beyond CARO's
   standard whistleblower-complaint disclosure clause), but it should
   not be treated as closed either.

**Placement.** This is recorded as a named FLAG-PROMOTER item (not a
deal-breaker) because the pipeline's own deal-breaker list does not
contain a "unresolved/inconsistent whistleblower status" trigger, and
because no material financial or fraud impact was found. It is the
decisive fact holding the verdict at TRUSTWORTHY rather than EXEMPLARY.
