## Business understanding narrative

ASM designs and now builds complex capital equipment for the industries that are being built out in India: semiconductors, electronics, solar, aerospace. It began in 1992 as an engineering-services firm, doing design work for clients. It still does that, but the larger part of the business is now design-led manufacturing, where it makes the machines it designs. It earns from projects that run 18 to 24 months, so it has long forward visibility on what it will build.

What is changing is the centre of the business. Manufacturing was 12% of revenue in the first half of FY25, 58% a year later, and 74% by the June 2026 quarter. The company is turning from a services house into a manufacturer, and it is bolting AI and wireless design onto that through two acquisitions.

Why now: India's semiconductor and electronics push, a ₹510 crore memorandum with the Karnataka government with the land already allotted, and three new plants commissioned in FY26.

For the thesis to work, the order book must justify the plants, the manufacturing margin must hold as the mix shifts to it, and the coming equity raise must not dilute away the per-share gain.

What breaks it: the stock trades above 100 times earnings, so the climb may already be paid for; there is no order-book number in any document; and the company is raising equity twice in six months.

## 2. Business model and archetype

Two segments. Engineering R&D services (ER&D), design work for clients, and design-led manufacturing (DLM), building the equipment. In the June quarter DLM was 73.6% of revenue and ER&D 26.4%. (`presentations/ASM-InvestorPresentation-Q1FY27-2026-08-06.txt`, page 7.) The consolidated group is ASM plus nine subsidiaries in the US, Singapore, UK, Japan, Thailand and Vietnam. (`results/ASM-Q4FY26-audited-results-2026-05-09.txt`, page 3.) Customers are in semiconductor, electronics, solar, aerospace and engineering. Plants at Dabaspet near Bangalore, in Chennai and in Vietnam. (`annual-report/Annual_Report_2026.txt`, pages 10 and 175.)

Archetype: **outsourcing partner turning into a build-to-spec component maker**. The economics run on capacity fill, contract stickiness and content per programme, moving toward customer capex cycle and design-win pipeline.

## 3. Competitive advantages

Real and forming. Three decades of ER&D depth is the barrier: the company's own framing is that "ER&D supports our DLM," so the design capability wins the manufacturing work. (`concalls/Concall_Nov_2025_Transcript.txt`.) That gives spec-in and switching cost: once ASM designs a customer's equipment, it is placed to build it, and the customer does not re-tender casually. Long visibility confirms the stickiness: management says "we have visibility of at least 18 months and in some cases, 24 months." (Same source.) The semiconductor and ESDM tailwind, plus a government memorandum, add a scarcity of qualified Indian design-and-build partners. On the ladder this is a genuine R3, a value-added spec'd supplier, reaching toward R4 as the semiconductor content deepens.

## 4. The promoters

Promoters hold 57.96%, across five holders. (`annual-report/Annual_Report_2026.txt`, page 54.) The chairman is M.R. Vikram, a chartered accountant with 45 years in audit and capital markets, a non-independent director; the managing director is Rabindra Srikantan. (Same file, pages 3 and 17.)

Two things to weigh. Promoter holding at 58% is moderate, not the 75% of the other two names, and it is falling: the company placed shares and warrants in the March 2026 quarter and has approved a further raise, so the promoter share dilutes as the growth is funded by equity. That is the honest tension in an otherwise clean promoter picture: the founders are building aggressively, and paying for it partly with their own stake.

## 5. Financial trajectory

Accelerating, earnings-led, margin rising (`presentations/ASM-InvestorPresentation-Q1FY27-2026-08-06.txt`, pages 6 to 8):

- FY25 revenue ₹289 crore, EBITDA margin 16.4%, PAT ₹26 crore.
- FY26 revenue ₹529 crore, up 83%, EBITDA margin 19.1%, PAT ₹61 crore.
- Nine months to December 2025 revenue up 126% year on year. (`presentations/ASM-InvestorPresentation-Q3FY26.txt`, page 6.)
- June 2026 quarter revenue ₹199 crore, up 62%, EBITDA ₹46 crore at 23.2%, up 80%, PAT about ₹27 crore, pre-tax profit ₹39 crore up 82%.

Return on capital about 25% (Trendlyne parameters). Inflection type: **earnings-led, real, and margin-expanding**. This is the cleanest financial trajectory of the three.

## 6. The transition and the quality ladder

The claim, from the managing director: the company is "pivoting very rapidly into DLM and for that, the ER&D is a very key part of the DLM initiative." (`concalls/Concall_Nov_2025_Transcript.txt`.) The FY26 report frames it as "becoming an AI-Powered Design-Led Manufacturing for capital equipment." (`annual-report/Annual_Report_2026.txt`, page 6.)

Delivered mechanism: the manufacturing share went 12% to 58% to 74% in eighteen months, three plants were commissioned, and two acquisitions added AI and wireless design. Ladder: FROM R2 to R3, an engineering-services and spec supplier, TO R3 to R4, a design-led manufacturer with semiconductor stickiness. The climb is under way and partly delivered.

## 8. Proof check

The numbers back the story. Revenue up 83% in FY26 and 62% in the June quarter, margin rising from 16% to 23%, DLM at 74%, plants commissioned, land allotted. The proof gate has fired on delivery. Two caveats sit in the same documents. The mix shift costs margin: an analyst on the November call noted manufacturing's rise "is dragging the gross margin a bit," with services showing "a slight degrowth in the revenue also and much more decline in the margin," and management did not dispute it. (`concalls/Concall_Nov_2025_Transcript.txt`.) And there is no order-book figure anywhere to size the delivered growth against the planned capex.

## 11. Posture: the Transition Decision Matrix

- Proof gate: FIRED. Plants delivered, revenue and margin delivered, mix shifted.
- Ugliness: the mix-shift margin drag is an ARTIFACT of the climb, a temporary optic as manufacturing scales, not structural decay.
- Recognition gap: CLOSED. The price is up 52% this year, near its high, at a PE above 100. The re-rating has largely happened.

Posture: **EARNINGS-ONLY**. The climb is real and already priced, so returns from here ride EPS growth, not a further re-rating. Actionable only if earnings can clear the hurdle without help from the multiple, which at a PE above 100 is a high bar.

## 12. Verdict

Candidate for /step1: **PROCEED**.

Of the three this is the clearest documented rung climb, and the only one whose recent numbers are earnings-led rather than cyclical or asset-driven. The reason to run the deep pipeline is precisely the tension the shallow read cannot resolve: a real climb at a price that has already paid for it.

Load-bearing facts a full /step1 must verify first:
1. The order book, undisclosed everywhere in the corpus. A ₹510 crore capex and a ₹500 crore raise are being sized against a number no filing gives.
2. The dilution. How much of the per-share story survives the ₹500 crore raise, and on what terms.
3. Whether DLM margins hold as manufacturing becomes the whole business, which the November call already showed pulling on gross margin.

What the deep run decides: whether EPS growth can grow into a PE above 100, or whether the recognition gap is closed and the return is spent. The transition is not in doubt; the price is the question.
