### 1. NARRATIVE

1. WeWork India leases office buildings, fits them out, and rents desks to companies on contracts of about 28 months. The Embassy Group is the promoter.
2. Sales grew fast. Revenue from operations rose 33.6% on a year ago, to Rs 767.8 Cr. Desks rose 27% to 145,300 and members rose 33%.
3. The growth is volume, not price. Revenue per member stayed flat. Renewals now price below the escalations in old contracts.
4. The company reports its headline on its own "IGAAP equivalent" basis. No auditor reviews that basis. On it, Q2 profit was Rs 65.8 Cr, with no tax charged.
5. The audited Ind AS profit was Rs 20.4 Cr. Interest, fund gains and lease exit gains supplied Rs 18.6 Cr of the Rs 20.5 Cr pretax profit.
6. Without that income, the core business made about Rs 1 Cr before tax. On billed revenue, it lost about Rs 0.3 Cr.
7. Three headline claims do not rebuild from the filed statements: net cash of Rs 23.1 Cr, ROCE of 34.5%, and the order book cover. The filed balance sheet shows net cash near zero before leases.
8. Lease liabilities are Rs 5,233 Cr, which is 15 times book equity. A deferred tax asset makes up 90% of that equity.
9. Cash flow looks better than profit. But a Rs 137 Cr inflow from deposits and other liabilities paid for all of the gain. Without it, cash after rent and capex got worse.
10. The call carried two new risks. A large customer of seven years moves to its own campus in FY28. The promoter pledge release promised last quarter did not happen.
11. Occupancy fell to 83.7%, down 114 bps in the quarter and 320 bps since March. The March 2027 desk target is now a 150,000 to 155,000 range. The company reached 150,000 on 5 October.
12. Watch Q3 FY27 occupancy, due by 14 February 2027. At 85% or above, the new centres fill on time. At 82.5% or below, growth outruns demand.
