## SECTION 1: MARKET DEFINITION

### 1A. Precise boundaries

**Product scope.** Forged and precision-machined metal components sold to OEMs on a build-to-spec basis: crankshafts (core product), railway wheels, empty ammunition shells, lifting hooks, undercarriage components, brake components, hydraulic motors, turbine blades (Concall PPT Jun 2026, [[PAGE 5]]). BALUFORGE is a component maker, not a converter or a finished-vehicle/equipment maker (B04-bizmodel.yaml, archetype = "build-to-spec component maker"). Excluded: raw steel/billet supply (an input, not an output), finished vehicles/engines/equipment, and the aftermarket/spares retail channel — every disclosed revenue stream is classified "build-to-order unit sale" (B04), not aftermarket.

**Geographic scope.** Global. Exports are 83.4% of consolidated FY2025 revenue; the company supplies 25+ OEMs across 80+ countries (AR 2024-25, [[PAGE 27]] financial highlights; Concall PPT Jun 2026, [[PAGE 7]]).

**Customer scope.** OEMs in six named end markets: legacy auto/commercial vehicle, agriculture, oil & gas, defence/aerospace/railway, heavy engineering & industrial machinery, power generation (task brief; B04 revenue_streams).

**Channel scope.** Direct OEM design-in relationships (15-20 year customer lifecycle per B04 archetype_basis), not distribution or retail.

**Price segment.** Precision-engineered, certified components (IATF 16949, ISO 9001; defence approvals for 180+ products) — not commodity/uncertified castings.

**Explicit inclusions.** Forged + machined components across the six named verticals, wherever sold (India + export).
**Explicit exclusions.** (i) OEM in-house/captive forging capacity (not contestable by a merchant supplier); (ii) small-arms cartridge ammunition (BALUFORGE's disclosed line is large-calibre shells/artillery, not cartridges); (iii) airframe/jet-engine aerospace components requiring AS9100/NADCAP, which BALUFORGE does not hold (Stage 7 finding — the single Alpha Aircraft Systems Inc. order is the sole aerospace data point); (iv) the broader "auto industry," "railway industry," or "defence industry" markets themselves (those are end-product markets many times the size of the forged-component slice a supplier like BALUFORGE can address — conflating the two is exactly the TAM=SAM error this section is built to avoid).

## SECTION 2: TAM ESTIMATION, MULTIPLE METHODS

### Method 1 — Top-down (global and India forging market)

Global forging market (all metals, all end industries, all suppliers including OEM captive), 2025: estimates range **USD 85.9 Bn to USD 104.2 Bn** across three research houses (Vantage Market Research, Spherical Insights, unnamed third; WebSearch "global forging market size 2025 billion USD automotive share forecast"). Global automotive-forging sub-segment, 2025: **USD 25.0 Bn to USD 55.0 Bn** (four-way divergence: Fortune Business Insights, OpenPR/unnamed, Future Market Insights, Global Insight Services — same search). This is a very wide spread; flagged.

India metal forging market, 2025: three IMARC-family estimates diverge materially: **USD 5.6 Bn, USD 7.7 Bn, USD 9.44 Bn** (WebSearch "India forging market size 2025 billion USD IMARC report crores" and "…IMARC report crores"). At the Sept-2026 spot rate of ₹94.4/USD (tradingeconomics.com, WebSearch "USD INR exchange rate September 2026", 4-Sep-2026), this is **₹52,864 Cr to ₹89,114 Cr**. This figure includes OEM in-house/captive forging, which is NOT contestable by a merchant supplier like BALUFORGE — this is the key reason Method 1's topline sits well above Method 3 below, and Method 1 is used here only as a directional ceiling, not the operative TAM.

STALE check: all figures above are 2024-2026 vintage; none exceed the 2-year staleness threshold as of the 2026-09-06 run date.

### Method 2 — Bottom-up (illustrative, ammunition/shell line only — the only sub-vertical with disclosed unit economics)

BALUFORGE has commercialised a 360,000 shells p.a. empty-shell/large-calibre ammunition line (Concall PPT Jun 2026, [[PAGE 8]]). A disclosed order — "₹100 Cr order for 155mm shells, ships 10,000 units" (WebSearch, scanx.trade) — implies an approximate ₹1 lakh/shell realisation IF the full order was for 10,000 units (this is an assumption; the press item does not state the order's total unit count with certainty, so treat this ASP as illustrative, single-data-point, NOT a verified average selling price). At this illustrative ASP, 360,000 shells p.a. at full utilisation implies a revenue ceiling of **~₹360 Cr p.a.** for the shell line specifically. This is a useful sanity check for the defence-vertical SOM only (Section 3C); it cannot be extended to the other five verticals, none of which disclose comparable unit/price data. Bottom-up for auto/CV, agriculture, oil & gas, heavy engineering, or power generation: **NOT FOUND** — no per-unit volumes or realised prices are disclosed by BALUFORGE for any of these lines, and no third-party source ties a rupee ASP to BALUFORGE's specific product mix in these verticals.

## SECTION 3: SAM & SOM

### 3A. SAM — five filters applied to TAM

Base: TAM conservative ₹31,000 Cr (Method 3).

| Filter | Retained | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Product fit | 85% | Excludes small-arms cartridge ammunition and full jet-engine/airframe aerospace scope not in BALUFORGE's disclosed product list |
| Geography | 100% | TAM base (Method 3) already sums India-based forgers' revenue including their export books — no further cut needed |
| Channel | 100% | Direct-OEM design-in matches the TAM base's own channel (the same peers sell the same way) |
| Customer/qualification depth | 45% | BALUFORGE holds 25+ OEM relationships; the peer pool collectively serves a much larger OEM base, and 15-20yr design-in cycles (B04) mean most of that pool is not realistically reachable in the near term. **Stated as analyst judgement, not a sourced figure** — this is the filtering exercise the method calls for, not a data point that can be "found." |
| Capability/certification | 90% | Ammunition/shells and rail lines are commercially qualified today (180+ approved products, AR [[PAGE 26]]); the broader aerospace slice is excluded per the product-fit filter above, so the remaining haircut here is small and reflects residual defence-programme qualification risk (unnamed MoU counterparty, Stage 7) |

Compound retention = 0.85 × 1.00 × 1.00 × 0.45 × 0.90 = **34.4% → 34%**

**SAM (conservative) = ₹31,000 Cr × 34% = ₹10,540 Cr → ₹10,500 Cr (rounded).**
SAM (realistic, TAM ₹38,000 Cr × 34%) = ₹12,920 Cr — shown for context; conservative case carried into the YAML per the framework's Conservative Bias rule.

**Current SAM share** = FY25 audited consolidated revenue ÷ SAM = ₹924 Cr ÷ ₹10,500 Cr = **8.8%**. This is directionally sensible: BALUFORGE is a small player next to Bharat Forge alone (₹8,844 Cr, ~9.5x BALUFORGE's revenue) within the same TAM pool.

### 3B. SOM at 3 and 5 years

Share-gain rule applied: BALUFORGE has a genuine capacity-expansion story underway (Section 3C) and a real order-book tilt toward higher-value defence/aerospace/railway work (order book ~50% defence/aerospace/railway vs. 13% of FY26 *revenue* — Concall PPT Jun 2026, [[PAGE 12]]/[[PAGE 28]]), which argues for the higher end of the "normal" 1-2pp/3yr band or the low end of "aggressive" 3-5pp. Against that: management guided 40-45% FY26 revenue growth and delivered 19.9% (Stage 5), and three peers contradicted the company's own explanation for its Q4 FY26 decline while posting their own strongest FY26 quarter in that same period (Stage 6) — a documented execution-credibility gap. Net: **2pp gain by Year 3, 4pp cumulative gain by Year 5** — the upper end of "normal," not the aggressive band, given the execution track record.

SAM share: 8.8% (current) → 10.8% (Yr3) → 12.8% (Yr5)

TAM/SAM growth rate: blended by FY26 revenue-share weight across the six verticals, using each vertical's sourced/estimated growth rate — auto/CV (36% weight) at the global automotive-forging low end ~5%; agriculture, oil & gas, heavy engineering, power generation (20%+16%+10%+5% = 51% weight, no independent vertical rate found) proxied at the India-forging-market rate ~8%; defence/aerospace/railway (13% weight) at the independent India-ammunition CAGR ~10.8% (Kenresearch-style estimate, WebSearch "India defence indigenization…"):

0.36×5 + 0.51×8 + 0.13×10.8 = 1.80 + 4.08 + 1.40 = **7.28% → 7% (rounded)**. This is an **analyst blend of sourced component rates**, not itself a single sourced figure — flagged as such.

Grown SAM: ₹10,500 Cr × 1.07³ = ₹12,860 Cr (Yr3); × 1.07⁵ = ₹14,730 Cr (Yr5)

**SOM Yr3 = 10.8% × ₹12,860 Cr = ₹1,389 Cr → ₹1,390 Cr**
**SOM Yr5 = 12.8% × ₹14,730 Cr = ₹1,885 Cr**

Implied revenue CAGR from FY25 audited base (₹924 Cr):
- Yr3: (1,390 / 924)^(1/3) − 1 = **14.6%**
- Yr5: (1,885 / 924)^(1/5) − 1 = **15.3%**

This SOM-implied CAGR sits **below** management's own historical guidance (40-45% FY26, delivered 19.9% — Stage 5) and **below the actual FY26 delivered growth rate of 19.9%,** and well below the strategy's 25% CAGR hurdle. It is the disciplined, share-gain-rule-bound answer, not an extrapolation of one strong or one weak year.

### 3C. Capacity cross-check

**Mandatory caveat, carried forward exactly as instructed:** B07's capex_embedded_growth_pct of 50% is **NOT a fixed-asset-turnover-derived number.** Stage 7 states the literal turnover method produced an unusable result — an artefact of 2020 reverse-merger deemed-cost accounting plus a used-machinery purchasing strategy — and substituted a forging-capacity-ratio proxy instead (forging capacity 100,000 → 150,000 MTPA = +50%; machining capacity 45,000 → 80,000 MTPA = +78%), flagged approximate because **no utilisation percentage is disclosed for either old or new capacity, in either stage.** The SOM revenue figures above (₹1,390 Cr Yr3 / ₹1,885 Cr Yr5) are derived independently through the SAM-share-gain waterfall in 3B and do **not** depend on this proxy at all. Only the feasibility check below draws on it, and it assumes full utilisation of nameplate capacity, which is unverified — treat this section as directional only.

- Forging-capacity ceiling (+50%, full ramp to 150,000 MTPA): ₹924 Cr × 1.50 = **₹1,386 Cr**
- Machining-capacity ceiling (+78%, full ramp to 80,000 MTPA): ₹924 Cr × 1.78 = **₹1,645 Cr**

**Yr3 SOM (₹1,390 Cr)** sits almost exactly at the forging-capacity ceiling (₹1,386 Cr) — a ₹4 Cr gap, immaterial given the rounding involved. **Broadly sufficient.**

**Yr5 SOM (₹1,885 Cr)** exceeds even the more generous machining-capacity ceiling (₹1,645 Cr) by **₹240 Cr**, and exceeds the forging ceiling (₹1,386 Cr) by ₹499 Cr. No capacity expansion beyond 150,000 MTPA forging / 80,000 MTPA machining is disclosed anywhere in the corpus. **The Yr5 SOM, not the capex plan, is the optimistic side of this comparison** — closing the ₹240 Cr gap requires either further capacity investment not yet announced, or a mix-shift toward higher-₹/tonne product (defence/aerospace/precision machining) beyond what is already embedded in the 3B growth assumption, since no per-tonne realisation figure is disclosed to verify how much of that shift is already priced in.

## SECTION 5: SUMMARY & RUNWAY

### 5A. Funnel

```
TAM (conservative, Method 3)              ₹31,000 Cr
  → SAM (34% of TAM, 5 filters applied)   ₹10,500 Cr
    → Current revenue (FY25 audited)      ₹924 Cr    (8.8% of SAM)
    → SOM Yr3 (10.8% of grown SAM)        ₹1,390 Cr  (CAGR 14.6% from FY25 base)
    → SOM Yr5 (12.8% of grown SAM)        ₹1,885 Cr  (CAGR 15.3% from FY25 base)
```
(TAM realistic case: ₹38,000 Cr → SAM ₹12,920 Cr, shown for context; conservative case carried through per framework rule.)

### 5B. Runway assessment

- **Revenue headroom** = SAM ÷ current revenue = 10,500 / 924 = **11.4x**
- **TAM growth rate** ≈ **7%** (blended, see 3B)
- **Company CAGR vs. TAM**: FY26 actual growth was 19.9% (Stage 5) against a ~7% blended TAM growth rate — the company is currently **gaining share**, though at a materially slower pace than its own 40-45% guidance implied, and the SOM's disciplined forward estimate (14.6-15.3%) is itself well below that one-year actual.
- **Years to saturate SAM**: at the SOM-implied ~15% CAGR, ₹924 Cr reaches ₹10,500 Cr in **~17 years**; at the FY26 actual 19.9% rate, **~13 years**. Either way, a long runway relative to a 3-5 year hold horizon.

### 5C. Runway classification: **STRONG**

Basis: double-digit revenue headroom (11.4x), positive but modest TAM growth (~7%), and a company that is demonstrably gaining share (19.9% actual vs. ~7% TAM growth) — but **confidence on this classification is MEDIUM, not HIGH**, because the headline TAM rests primarily on Method 3 (peer aggregation with two stated estimate-additions) given that four of six served verticals (51% of FY26 revenue) have no independently-sized addressable market anywhere in public research.

### 5D. SAM expansion levers actually being pursued

- **AS9100/NADCAP accreditation** (not yet held, Stage 7): if achieved, would reverse most of the product-fit/capability haircut applied to the aerospace slice of the defence/aerospace/railway vertical — illustratively, reversing roughly the residual 10-15% capability haircut on that slice could add on the order of ₹1,500-2,000 Cr to SAM (**illustrative, not a sourced or precise figure** — flagged as such, not carried into the YAML).
- **In-house forging integration** (outsourcing being replaced by in-house capacity, Concall PPT Jun 2026 [[PAGE 6]]): this is a margin-capture lever, not a SAM-expansion lever — noted distinctly so the two are not conflated.
- **Geographic expansion** (NATO supply chain, Europe/Middle East): incremental effect on SAM is likely modest, since the Method-3 TAM base already credits India-based forgers' full export reach.
- **"New Energy/E-Mobility" segment claim**: unaudited, unsupported by any segment/product disclosure (B04 flag) — **excluded** from any SAM-expansion credit until evidenced.
