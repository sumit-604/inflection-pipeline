# Max India Ltd (MAXIND)

Three senior care build outs draining treasury

Max India runs three senior focused businesses inside one listed holding company, plus a treasury and business investments segment that holds the group cash. Antara Senior Living sells residences (Noida, Dehradun, Gurugram) and earns development management fees and maintenance annuity on them. Assisted Care runs 485 care home beds across 8 facilities in NCR, Bengaluru and Chennai, together with a Care at Home service. AGEasy sells senior consumer products across 91 products and 159 SKUs. The shares trade at Rs182, a market cap of Rs959 Cr, and the company is deeply loss making.

FY26 revenue grew 31%, the first annual increase after revenue fell in 4 of the prior 6 years. The consolidated net loss was about Rs122 Cr and the EBITDA loss narrowed to about Rs83 Cr from about Rs100 Cr the year before. The Gate 0 scorecard reads AVOID at Core 22 of 100 and Grand Total 24 of 160, with zero confirmed moats: every return on capital and every growth line scores zero because revenue declined through the build out years while the group funded Antara communities, the care home network and AGEasy from rights issue and warrant proceeds rather than operating cash. Median ROCE across the window is negative 21%. This is a mechanical backward score on a company still in its investment phase, and it does not halt the analysis.

Cash is the pressure point. Treasury fell to about Rs58 Cr by March 2026 from about Rs320 Cr nine months earlier, against a full year EBITDA loss near Rs83 Cr. Cumulative operating cash outflow over FY20 to FY26 is more negative than cumulative accounting loss, so the cash burn runs ahead of the reported loss. Consolidated trade receivables grew about 349% in the FY25 accounts while revenue fell 17%, with the expected credit loss allowance essentially unchanged, though 97% of that receivable still aged within 6 months. A planned Rs200 Cr to Rs250 Cr fundraise remains undated and, per management, contingent on market sentiment.

Management says more than it has delivered on the operational commitments. The delivery record across the last three calls is 2 delivered, 3 partial and 4 missed of 9 tracked promises, which earns a credibility grade of C. The care home target of 500 beds was quietly cut to 485 and slipped 2 to 3 quarters. The 1.5 million square feet per year residences commitment came in at 1.1 million. The fundraise timeline softened from a specific 6 to 9 month window to "sometime this year" as treasury fell from about Rs105 Cr to about Rs58 Cr. Against that, the EBITDA loss improvement landed on plan, Estate 361 launched on time in December 2025, and the delayed Noida partial occupancy certificate did eventually arrive in May 2026.

Governance carries anchored concerns alongside genuine repair. Shareholders rejected the special resolution renewing Chairman Analjit Singh's FY26 compensation, 59.82% in favour against the 75% required. A Max Towers floor sale to promoter linked Max Estates went through at Rs105 Cr versus an independent valuation of Rs65.5 Cr obtained about 7 months earlier, and related party evaluation is the auditor's own Key Audit Matter at both standalone and consolidated levels. Two independent directors, Sharmila Tagore and Rohit Kapoor, resigned mid term within one calendar year. A 2023 care home fire that killed two residents carries a media reported licensing allegation absent from the company's own safety narrative. On the other side, a long running family oppression dispute at the promoter entity was settled in January 2025, a former McKinsey senior partner joined as an independent director, and the promoter converted rather than lapsed its July 2026 warrants.

Peers confirm the demand backdrop but sharpen the risks. Ashiana Housing, the closest listed analogue, names Gurugram and Pune oversupply and a management bandwidth constraint on multi city expansion that MAXIND does not raise for its own footprint. Max Estates independently corroborates the DLF entrant claim and Noida Sector 105 pricing, though realised residential appreciation across the peer set runs 16% to 29%, not the 2 to 3 times MAXIND cites for Sector 150 Phase 2. GPT Healthcare's new Raipur hospital saw its own breakeven guidance slip from 12 to 15 months to 24 to 36 months, the most direct cautionary read on Antara's untested care home maturity curve. Management's claims of sector "deals at established multiples", the China sourcing cost pressure on AGEasy, and the AGEasy RoAS trajectory each remain unverifiable against the provided peers.

The market opportunity is large and the runway is long. The realistic addressable market across senior housing, assisted and home care, and senior products sums to about Rs62,500 Cr, with a serviceable slice near Rs17,500 Cr, of which MAXIND holds about 1.2%. That leaves roughly 82 times revenue headroom and a runway classed as massive, with a 3 year serviceable obtainable market near Rs738 Cr and a 5 year figure near Rs1,089 Cr. Management's own market claim of about Rs115,800 Cr sits at 1.5 times the independent estimate and reads as reasonable, but the investor presentation's unattributed "about USD 30bn opportunity" slide is roughly 4.6 times the sum of parts and carries no source, date or method.
