## 1. MENTAL MODEL DECLARATION (SIGNED — operator, 30-Aug-2026, v2.1)

**Archetype: CONTRACTED INFRASTRUCTURE OPERATOR.** CMS owns the machines, runs the cash, monitors the sites, and is paid a fixed monthly fee for five to ten years by a small number of large banks.

Per line: ATM Management Solutions (58%) contracted infrastructure operator; Retail Solutions & Currency Logistics (26%) outsourced labour network, legacy archetype being pruned; Technology & Payment Solutions (16%) platform layered on the existing network AND a hardware-displacement wedge; hardware resale (~7% of consolidated) no archetype, pass-through, deliberately shrinking.

**Permanent structural feature of the archetype, not a risk-register item:** CMS physically custodies third-party money. Embezzlement Rs 120.53m / 217.22m / 125.35m across FY24-FY26; ADT-4 fraud filings under s.143(12) in two consecutive years; separate provision line for ATM cash shortages; auditors unable to confirm audit trail for billing software or for the general ledger post-migration 1-Sep-2025 [FILED]. Small against Rs 14 lakh crore handled annually. Permanent, scales with volume, and distinguishes this archetype from asset-light outsourcing peers (Quess, UDS).

**The transition.** FROM: own the machine, paid PER TRANSACTION — depreciation runs continuously, revenue stops when volumes stop. Both risks, neither protection. This is the model that killed AGS Transact. TO: own the machine, paid a FIXED MONTHLY FEE on five-to-ten-year contracts.

**What changed is the PRICING, not the ownership.** CMS entered ATM and cash-recycler deployment in 2013 via the Nautilus Hyosung partnership and won a Rs 255 Cr / 2,300-recycler SBI mandate in January 2015 [SECONDARY — Business Standard, siliconindia, verified]. A second strand runs alongside: from SELLING machines to banks (hardware line, -25.9% FY26) to OWNING AND LEASING them (contracted rentals +117.6%).

**The two halves are one contract.** Fixed fee is how CMS is paid; owning the machine is what CMS had to do to win it. A bank accepts a fixed fee only if the supplier carries equipment risk; a supplier funds equipment only if the fee is contracted long enough to earn it back. This is also why segment reporting broke — in one integrated fee there is no honest way to separate machine rental from cash logistics from monitoring. Management's May-2026 statement that margin splitting is "not possible and feasible" is a CONSEQUENCE OF THE MODEL, not an evasion.

**The analogy.** A tower company. Own the passive infrastructure, rent it to a few large creditworthy counterparties on long contracts, accept lower return on capital in exchange for contracted revenue, build ahead of revenue so returns dip during the build and recover as tenancy fills. The analogy is directional and imperfect — Indus Towers has one asset type and near-monopoly economics; CMS has three businesses and rents machines under competitive tender.

**Operator reading: NECESSITY EXECUTED FROM STRENGTH.** The old model was dying — India's ATM withdrawal VALUE has fallen three years running at an accelerating rate (see S1). Banks changed the tender terms and CMS complied. Clearest evidence this was not a free choice: CMS won the flagship SBI mandate as **L1**, lowest bidder, after having been the only qualified participant in a first round that was scrapped [MGMT — Feb-2026 call]. The strength is equally real: post-AGS, banks tightened credit across every MSP and weaker players hit a funding wall; balance sheet became the qualification to compete; CMS has zero debt, ~Rs 600 Cr cash, [ICRA]AA+/A1+.

**Investment consequence, binding on the deliberation:** because the move was FORCED rather than chosen, do NOT assume destination returns match origin returns. A company that moves voluntarily moves because the destination is better; a company that moves because it must accepts whatever the market allows. Current spread 16.6% post-tax ROCE against ~13% cost of equity. Approximately three points. Positive but thin.

**Ugliness classification: ARTIFACT OF CLIMB, with a named treadmill risk.** Upgraded from UNRESOLVED on evidence post-dating the Phase 1 gate recommendation. Treadmill risk retained: CWIP Rs 1,134.71m at year end with Rs 2,809.83m added during FY26 (FY24 CWIP Rs 147.30m), so the build has not finished; FY26 capex overshot its own Nov-2025 guidance of ~Rs 300 Cr by roughly 17%.

**Fragility: HIGH.** Not on variable count but on disclosure withdrawal. Three withdrawals in twelve months: segment profitability (promised Nov-2025, reversed May-2026); HAWKAI/ALGO standalone revenue (refused every quarter asked); Return on Net Worth dropped from the FY26 MD&A ratio table and replaced by post-tax ROCE, so no like-for-like multi-year return series exists.

**PROOF GATE: consolidated post-tax ROCE above 22% by FY28, with capital employed flat and capex at or below the guided Rs 100-125 Cr.**

FY27 is NOT the test year. Q1 FY27 D&A annualises to ~Rs 2,900m against FY26's Rs 2,076m, so depreciation PEAKS in FY27. Judging FY27 as the recovery year produces a false negative. Secondary gate: organic Technology + Managed Services external revenue growth at 15% or better, EXCLUDING FSS and Securens revenue.

**Gates explicitly rejected, with reasons — do not reinstate:** EBITDA margin (the mix shift flatters it; FY26 printed 24.1% while ROCE fell 8.6 points, and Q1 FY27 repeated it with EBITDA +190bps while EBIT fell 14.1% to 10.3%); Managed Services segment margin (set by an undisclosed transfer price that moved by 72% of the segment's total profit in one year); Technology % of services revenue (purchasable — FSS at ~4% of FY27 services revenue clears a naive 20% gate with zero organic delivery); segment-level ROCE (same transfer-price corruption).

**Recovery arithmetic, binding on Role 1.** Cash Management + Managed Services capital employed rose Rs 12,123m to Rs 16,141m while segment result fell Rs 5,379m to Rs 4,378m; return on capital 44.4% to 27.1%. Profit returning to the FY25 PEAK restores only 33.3%, not 44.4%. Restoring 44.4% requires profit 33% ABOVE the all-time high. **The recovery is partial by construction. Do not model a return to FY25 returns.**

**Dominant variables (7):** (1) consolidated post-tax ROCE, 16.6%, >22% by FY28 passes / <18% past FY28 fails; (2) capital employed vs capex guidance, flat = build, rising = treadmill; (3) contracted lease rentals receivable, Rs 8,126m +117.6%; (4) loss allowance cover vs aged receivables, **0.29x standalone** against 4-6x for four prior years; (5) ATM useful life, now 10 years from 7 — further extension is a warning, impairment is a confession; (6) transaction-linked BLA as % of revenue, <10% [SECONDARY, UNPINNED]; (7) ATMs running ALGO, ~65,000 at ~40% share — track MACHINES, not revenue.

**What the model REJECTS as noise:** whether cash is dying (fixed-fee contracts have broken the link on the new book; CIC grew 12% regardless); market size (9.1x revenue headroom, runway STRONG, execution is the constraint); EBITDA margin (in an asset-owning business it is the number BEFORE the cost of the assets); segment-level returns (unusable, see §3 S4); promoter alignment (no promoter exists — structural, not a disclosure failure); DSO as a trend (see §3 S3); currency-in-circulation as a primary KPI (rises with nominal GDP regardless).

**Transition falsifier.** Capex comes in at the guided Rs 100-125 Cr, capital employed goes flat, contracts reach full ramp, and ROCE still does not clear 20% by FY28. Then CMS is buying assets that earn roughly its cost of capital — it converted a high-return labour business into a zero-spread rental book because it had no choice, preserving revenue while destroying returns.

**Business falsifier.** National cash fulfilment has recovered from 70% to 80-85%. If it normalises and CMS revenue does not respond, the external-shock framing is dead and the decline is structural.

**The tension being underwritten.** The transition thesis is a claim that revenue is moving from one segment to another. Management's disclosure position is that the segments are not separable. Both cannot be strongly true. If the business genuinely is one integrated thing, there is no ladder to climb — only a company that got less profitable in FY26 and may or may not get more profitable again.

## 2. VERTICAL FINDINGS (verdicts only, with tiers)

NPCI NFS quarterly confirms a three-year accelerating decline in value: FY24 -0.7%, FY25 -4.5%, FY26 -7.2%, all PRE-DATING the FY27 currency shortage [FILED — NPCI Retail Payments Statistics]. **Thesis consequence: transaction pricing loses ~7%/yr before competing for anything; fixed monthly fees sit on a base GROWING 1.3%/yr. This is the external quantification of why the pricing transition was a necessity. CMS ATM Management grew 5.3% in FY26 while the pond's money fell 7.2% — a twelve-point gap and real evidence the share-gain story works.**

**V2 — Encroachment.** UPI average ticket Rs 1,263 (July 2026) against ATM Rs 5,412; **ticket ratio 0.233, DOWN from 0.247 a year earlier** [FILED — NPCI + RBI PSI]. Longer series: UPI ticket Rs 1,662 (FY23) to Rs 1,300 (FY26), -22% over three years. The rails are SEPARATING, not converging. Digital is moving DOWN-market. What is actually happening is not classic substitution: people withdraw the same or slightly larger amounts LESS OFTEN (volume -8.9%, value -5.3%). Replenishment frequency is driven by VALUE, so the metric that hurts CMS falls at 5-7%, not 9%. **RISK RETIRED: micro-ATMs 1,328,246, DOWN 9.4% YoY; AEPS withdrawals FLAT for four years (FY23 1,235mn txns / Rs 3,344bn vs FY26 1,213mn / Rs 3,228bn)** [FILED — RBI PSI + NPCI]. **V3 — Counterparty concentration.** SBI 65,175 machines, **55.7% off-site**, Rs 76,930 crore withdrawal, Rs 118.0 lakh per machine; = **31.1% of the national bank estate and 35.2% of withdrawal value** [FILED — RBI bank-wise ATMView, July 2026]. **V6 — Policy optionality.** Taxation and Other Laws (Amendment) Bill, 2026 PASSED BY PARLIAMENT 10-Aug-2026 [FILED — PIB/ANI]. Credibility note: the Finance Ministry publicly called MDR-on-UPI reports "completely false, baseless, and misleading" in June 2025 and passed the enabling law fourteen months later.
