India's largest CDMO awaiting export margin catalysts

Akums is the largest domestic pharma contract manufacturer in India, making finished dosage forms for branded pharmaceutical companies. CDMO is 80% of revenue at a 13.4% EBITDA margin. The rest splits across domestic branded formulation at 10.2%, API at 4.2%, international branded formulation at 3.3%, and trade generics at 2.3%. The business is asset heavy, works on high working capital, holds weak pricing power, and runs cyclical. Installed capacity is about 5,059 Cr units at 44% utilization, so most of the plant sits idle. Market cap is Rs 11,052 Cr at Rs 702 a share.

Two real developments are meant to shift the mix toward higher margin export and branded revenue. One is a European CDMO Plant 2 contract worth EUR 35m a year running to December 2032, with commercial supply starting FY28. The other is a Zambia joint venture of US$45m at 51% Akums, with plant commissioning slipped to FY29 and a US$25m India to Zambia supply leg guided by the end of Q2 FY27. The Plant 2 EU GMP approval arrived in January 2026. Neither development has booked revenue yet, and stage work reads these as two events, not two independent moats.

Gate 0 scores 58 of 160, which classifies AVERAGE with a THIN moat and 1 of 12 moat tests confirmed. Two loss years drag the history: FY22 single year ROCE was minus 17.36% and FY24 PAT was minus Rs 4 Cr, both tied to abnormal Other Expenses spikes of Rs 565 Cr and Rs 434 Cr around the IPO period rather than operating collapse. Revenue compounded 10.4% and PAT 17.6% across FY15 to FY26 on positive endpoints. The Emerging Moat scan lands at 23.5 of 80, MODEST, just under the strengthening line.

FY26 reported operating cash flow of Rs 1,181 Cr looks strong until you strip it. About Rs 954 Cr of it is a one off European contract advance under the company's own adjusted definition. Organic adjusted OCF fell from Rs 465 Cr in FY25 to Rs 227 Cr in FY26. Net working capital days worsened from 91 to 105. Adjusted OCF to EBITDA conversion fell to 34.9% from 90.7% a year earlier. Finance cost rose 172% year on year despite IPO proceeds going toward debt repayment.

Management earns a C on credibility. It delivered CDMO margin and volume ahead of guidance, turned trade generics EBITDA positive, kept capex inside the guided range, and made a rare honest admission that FY26 API was a year of miss. Against that, it missed domestic branded formulation growth badly at 2.9% versus a mid teens guide, ran a non monotonic API turnaround under continuous improving language, and deflected the same two questions across all three quarters: what it will do with the cash surplus, and whether Schedule M enforcement has actually won it share. On the promoter side, an Income Tax search and seizure hit in January 2025, a Rs 134 Cr block period tax demand followed across the group, and a live Drugs and Cosmetics Act prosecution continues. Offsetting that are 0% promoter pledge throughout, a credentialed independent board, a blue chip IPO anchor book, and DII ownership rising from about 7.4% to 14.3%.

Peers confirm two of Akums' claims and contradict a third. The API price decline through FY26 and the Middle East linked rebound into Q4 both corroborate across Cohance, Innovacap and Windlas, and the sector capex cycle is real rather than an Akums outlier domestically. But the story of an industry wide 25% CDMO volume surge does not hold: Cohance's underlying Pharma CDMO growth was early single digit after adjusting for destocking, Windlas ran 18 to 23% and Innovacap 6 to 10% organic. Akums' own 25% variance is an outlier its management admits it cannot fully explain.

The market runway is genuine and mid sized. Domestic CDMO TAM sits at Rs 13,880 to 18,580 Cr, SAM at Rs 11,630 Cr, and the five year SOM at Rs 7,348 Cr, giving 3.3x revenue headroom and a GOOD runway class. The management TAM claim ratio of 1.34 reads reasonable. The catch is that the SOM implied CDMO CAGR of 15.7 to 16.1% runs well above the FY26 actual CDMO growth of 8.6%, so the runway assumes reacceleration rather than extrapolation of what is printing today.
