Note 22(e), Consolidated Financial Statements, p.203 ("Details of shares held by

  shares (10.46%) at 31-Mar-2026 — a reduction of 24,00,000 shares, ~34.5% of its

🟡 Rating: Watch. Both Invesco and Smallcap World Fund could have simply sold

holding, if any. Two independent institutional investors reducing below a 5%
threshold in the same year as a large non-promoter trust selling a third of its
stake is a pattern worth checking against price action and any block-deal
disclosures (BSE/NSE) in a later stage — this is exactly the kind of signal the
input_gaps flagged "shareholding (MEDIUM)" and "announcements (MEDIUM)" for.

NEW FINDING 5 — The Anand Chemiceutics "98.3% goodwill" deal is partly a
mechanical artefact of put-option liability accounting, not pure overpayment
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
Note 46 VII(c), Consolidated Business Combinations, p.219, read together with
Note 3(B)(i) Material Accounting Policy on written put options, p.192-193.

- For the Anand Chemiceutics deal (51.51% stake, ₹1,728.44M consideration), the
  "Total identifiable net assets acquired" was only ₹56.16M, despite the deal
  bringing in real Trade Receivables of ₹1,796.72M and Inventories of
  ₹1,103.11M. The reason: "Other Financial Liabilities" of ₹1,965.39M was booked
  at acquisition — a liability of a scale that dwarfs every other line in the
  acquisition balance sheet.
- Note 3(B)(i) states the Group's policy explicitly: where it has written a put
  option over NCI shares, "the Group accounts for the put option as derivative
  liability with corresponding debit to other equity at inception." The
  ₹1,965.39M Other Financial Liabilities figure is consistent with this being
  the day-one fair value of the written put option over the retained 48.49%
  non-controlling stake in Anand Chemiceutics, not an operating liability.

🟡 Rating: Watch, with a correction to how Pass 1 Finding 1 should be read. Pass
1 flagged that "87-100%+ of consideration is booked as goodwill" for three FY26
deals including Anand Chemiceutics as a red flag on M&A pricing discipline. That
remains true for Ramson (negative net assets, pure overpayment signal) and for
Ace Cardiopathy (87.4%, no comparable liability distortion visible in Note 46
V). For Anand Chemiceutics specifically, the extreme 98.3% ratio is
substantially inflated by an accounting mechanic (the put option derivative
liability), not solely by the price paid for unrecognisable customer/vendor
relationships. The underlying acquired business (₹1,796.72M receivables +
₹1,103.11M inventory funded for ₹1,728.44M) looks closer to asset value than
the headline ratio implies. This does not clear the deal of scrutiny — the put
option itself is a real, growing (Note 45, below) liability — but it changes
which number an investor should worry about (the put option fair value
trajectory, not the goodwill percentage in isolation).

tracked alongside it, not in isolation.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
Note 45(A), Consolidated Fair Value Measurements, p.213. Pass 1 (Finding 5)

- Derivative liability (Put option): ₹335.67M non-current + ₹89.04M current =

- Derivative asset (Call option): ₹169.32M at 31-Mar-2026 (non-current only)
  versus ₹56.58M non-current + ₹45.48M current = ₹102.06M at 31-Mar-2025.
- Both are confirmed 100% Level 3 (unobservable inputs) fair value measurements
  — no independent market check is possible on either balance.

🟡 Rating: Watch — a refinement, not a contradiction, of Pass 1 Finding 5. The
balance-sheet derivative position (₹424.71M net liability closing) is smaller
than the P&L-adjacent equity movement Pass 1 cited (>₹1.5bn), because most of
the FY26 equity swing was a ONE-TIME initial-recognition entry on the new
Anand Chemiceutics put option (see New Finding 5) rather than a revaluation of
a continuing balance. The continuing Level 3 balance to track going forward is
the smaller ₹424.71M figure, which itself will keep moving with no external
verification available.
