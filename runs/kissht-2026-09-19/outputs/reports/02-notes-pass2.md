## PART A — THE FIVE RE-CHECK ITEMS

### A1. The 150-DPD write-off figure — CONFIRMED NOT FOUND, in either document
Searched the RHP's full text for every DPD/write-off/asset-classification passage (the accounting-policy
notes at RHP p.278 (standalone-equivalent set) and RHP p.350 (consolidated set), the ECL staging note, the
collections-strategy business section, and the risk-factor glossary). The RHP's write-off accounting policy is
worded IDENTICALLY to the AR's: "The Company/Group writes off financial assets, either partially or in full,
when there is no reasonable expectation of recovery based on past experience and assessment of the
borrower's financial condition" (RHP p.278, p.350). The RHP's own default/staging definition also states 90
DPD as the default/Stage-3 trigger and 30 DPD as the Stage-2 trigger — same as the AR. The word "150" does
not appear anywhere in the RHP attached to a DPD or write-off concept; the one "150" hit near write-off
language is unrelated ("150,071 Series D1 CCPS," a share-allotment number). 🟡 Watch, elevated from Pass
1's "flag for live verification": this is now a confirmed gap across BOTH filed documents available in this
container, not an AR-specific omission. If a 150-DPD trigger exists, it is an operational/internal policy never
disclosed in either the AR or the RHP notes; live verification (results-filing analyst annexures, the CRISIL
rating rationale, or a direct company confirmation) is the only path to it. (RHP accounting policy pp.278,
350; RHP Note 41/impairment section pp.~247-249 in AR-equivalent pagination.)

### A2. Corporate guarantee-to-net-worth ratio — the RHP shows a FOUR-YEAR ESCALATING PATTERN, not
a two-year jump
Pass 1 (Finding 1) compared only FY25 to FY26 (160% to 228%). The RHP's Note 36 (Contingent Liabilities,
consolidated-restated, p.~325) gives the full "Corporate guarantee issued on behalf of subsidiary" line for
four points: FY23 Rs 3,895.17 mn, FY24 Rs 7,024.42 mn, FY25 Rs 12,217.64 mn, 9M-Dec-2025 Rs 17,344.82
mn — the FY25 figure (Rs 12,217.64 mn) matches the AR's standalone Note 32 FY25 comparative EXACTLY,
confirming the two documents describe the same line item. Cross-referencing against the parent's own
standalone net worth (RHP Schedule III note, "Parent" net assets column, p.332-333: FY23 Rs 5,540.86 mn,
FY24 Rs 6,718.19 mn, FY25 Rs 7,643.59 mn; AR Note 31: FY26 Rs 9,640.57 mn) gives a guarantee-to-net-worth
ratio that has risen EVERY YEAR for four straight years:
| | FY23 | FY24 | FY25 | FY26 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Guarantee (Rs mn) | 3,895.17 | 7,024.42 | 12,217.64 | 21,982.06 |
| Parent net worth (Rs mn) | 5,540.86 | 6,718.19 | 7,643.59 | 9,640.57 |
| Ratio | 70.3% | 104.6% | 159.8% | 228.0% |
🔴 Red flag, escalated from Pass 1: this is not a one-year anomaly ahead of listing. The parent's guarantee
exceeded its own net worth for the first time in FY24 (104.6%) — two full years before the IPO — and has
compounded roughly 55-70 percentage points every year since, entirely independent of the listing event.
The IPO did not create this structure; it was already running at 160% of net worth before the IPO priced.
(RHP Note 36 p.325/333, Note 47 Schedule III p.332; AR standalone Note 32 p.88, Note 31 p.88.)

### A3. FY25 ECL release vs FY26 charge — the RHP shows a FOUR-YEAR BUILD-RELEASE-REBUILD CYCLE
Pass 1 (Finding 4) flagged the FY25 release (Rs 1,652.04 mn) against the FY26 charge (Rs 495.27 mn) as an
unexplained ~Rs 2.1 billion swing confined to the two years shown in the AR's comparative column. The
RHP's Note 26-equivalent (Impairment on financial instruments, p.~318) gives the same line for FOUR years,
and the FY25 figure matches the AR exactly (Rs (1,652.04) mn release on standard assets, Rs 540.56 mn NPA
charge, Rs (1,111.48) mn net) — confirming it is a real, correctly-carried number, not a typo or restatement
artefact:
| (Rs mn, ECL against standard assets, net) | FY23 | FY24 | FY25 | FY26 (AR) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Charge / (release) | 443.57 | 2,603.50 | (1,652.04) | 495.27 |
The book was scaling fast in FY24 (a large provisioning build as AUM grew), then FY25 released nearly all of
that build back through P&L, then FY26 rebuilt a smaller charge. 🟡 Watch, same rating as Pass 1 but now
evidenced as a recurring pattern of building and releasing large provisions in alternating years rather than a
single unexplained swing — still with no narrative explanation for the FY25 release in either document. This
strengthens the case for a specific management question on what changed between FY24's build and FY25's
release (a model recalibration, a macro-overlay unwind, or a genuine credit-quality improvement each carry
very different implications for FY27 provisioning).

### A4. Useful-life change (5→10 years, in-house software) — RHP shows the change predates the AR by
at least the RHP's own filing date, and was in effect for the interim stub used to price the IPO
The RHP's accounting-policy note (p.278, standalone-equivalent set; p.350/351, consolidated set), dated for
restated financial information through the nine months ended 31 December 2025, carries the IDENTICAL
sentence: "Software developed in-house is amortized over estimated life of 5 years... till the previous
financial year. However, beginning from current financial year software developed in-house is amortized
over estimated life of 10 years..." "Current financial year" in the RHP's own framing is FY26 (the year
commencing 1 April 2025), the same year the AR reports. This means the change was adopted at the START
of FY26 — roughly nine months before the AR was signed (27-May-2026) and well before the IPO itself
(listed 8-May-2026) — and was already embedded, unquantified, in the interim numbers investors saw in
the RHP when the issue priced. 🟡 Watch, nuanced from Pass 1: this reframes the change from "a policy
shift disclosed only at AR sign-off in the IPO year" to "a policy shift adopted at the start of the IPO year and
carried, still unquantified, through the RHP into the AR" — the change had roughly nine months of investor
visibility before listing, but neither document has EVER quantified its P&L effect. The absence of
quantification is now confirmed as a genuine, sustained gap across two independent filings, not a one-off
oversight fixable by checking a second document.

### A5. Comparative-figure regrouping — the RHP's OWN restatement schedule shows the only quantified
regrouping identified (FY23-FY25 window) was immaterial
The RHP carries a mandatory SEBI-ICDR "Material Restatement Adjustments — Material Regroupings"
schedule (p.334) that the AR's boilerplate Note 43/46 has no equivalent for. It discloses exactly one
quantified regrouping across the entire FY23-FY25 restated window: FY24 revenue from operations was
reduced by Rs 0.17 mn and other income increased by Rs 0.17 mn (a reclassification between the two lines,
not a change to any total). 🟢 This narrows, rather than closes, Pass 1's flagged concern: for FY23-FY25, the
"regrouped and reclassified, wherever considered necessary" language in Note 43/46 evidently covered
nothing larger than a rounding-level reclass — genuinely immaterial. It does NOT cover the FY26-vs-FY25
comparative regrouping the AR's own Note 46 (p.93/131) refers to, since the RHP predates FY26 audited
figures by over a year; that specific comparative-year regrouping remains unquantified in the only document
that would carry it (the AR), and stays open as a residual (very likely immaterial, based on the FY23-FY25
precedent, but not confirmed).

## PART B — NEW FINDINGS FROM THE FULL RE-READ (AR notes not extracted in Pass 1)

All four sit inside Consolidated Note 41 "Financial Risk Management" (pp.127-129), a note Pass 1 read in
depth only for its Impairment Assessment / ECL-staging sub-section (section B7 of the Pass 1 report). The
Liquidity risk, Price risk, and Interest rate risk sub-sections of the same note were not extracted.

### B1. Undrawn bank credit lines rose almost 31x
Consolidated Note 41B, Financing arrangements (p.128): undrawn bank credit lines were Rs 1,910.65 mn at
31-Mar-2026 versus Rs 61.72 mn at 31-Mar-2025, a +2,996% increase. 🟢/🟡 Not a red flag on its own — a
much larger untapped liquidity cushion is a normal post-fundraise position — but the SCALE of the jump
(almost 31x on a near-zero base) is itself a data point worth naming: a year ago the Group had almost no
undrawn headroom, and the IPO/balance-sheet strengthening changed that completely within twelve
months. Worth a one-line management question on how much of the new headroom is IPO-proceeds-linked
versus organically negotiated with lenders. (Consolidated Note 41B, p.128.)

### B2. The liquidity risk note discloses only the LIABILITY side of the maturity ladder
Consolidated Note 41B's "Maturity Pattern" table (p.128-129) gives the full contractual-maturity bucket
breakdown for trade payables, debt securities, borrowings, lease liabilities, and other financial liabilities —
but no equivalent maturity table for financial ASSETS (the loan book) appears anywhere in the notes. For an
NBFC-ML whose core business is borrowing short/medium and lending across a mixed personal-loan/LAP
book, an investor cannot construct an asset-liability maturity gap from the notes alone; a true ALM picture
would need Si Creva's RBI-mandated ALM return (a document not in this container). 🟡 Watch — a
disclosure gap, not a demonstrated mismatch; flag for live verification if the RBI ALM statement becomes
available. (Consolidated Note 41B, p.128-129 — absence noted against the full note range.)

### B3. Interest rate risk exposure is small and the book is fixed-rate matched
Consolidated Note 41D (p.129): the Group states loans to customers are fixed-rate ("hence there is no
interest rate risk on loan exposure"); only Rs 3,277.90 mn of Rs 23,961.02 mn total borrowings (13.7%) is
floating-rate, up from Rs 1,538.09 mn (10.2%) in FY25. A 0.5% rate move affects pre-tax P&L by only Rs 16.39
mn (FY26) versus Rs 7.69 mn (FY25). 🟢 Clean — no material floating-rate funding cost squeeze on a fixed-
rate lending book is evidenced by the numbers disclosed; this is a genuinely reassuring finding Pass 1 did
not surface, and belongs in the balance of the accounting-quality picture alongside the red flags above.
(Consolidated Note 41D, p.129.)

### B4. Standalone-level customer concentration (74.7% in three counterparties, Pass 1 Finding 9) could not
be cross-checked against RHP history
Pass 1 flagged the AR's standalone Note 40 (three customers = 74.7% of standalone revenue) as apparently
contradicting the Group-level "no customer >=10%" statement, calling it reconcilable but worth naming. This
pass attempted to check whether the standalone-level concentration is a longstanding pattern or new in
FY26, using the RHP as the second source. It could not: the RHP's Financial Statements section contains
only "Restated CONSOLIDATED Financial Information" throughout (every note heading confirmed
"Consolidated," no parallel standalone-only note set exists in the RHP). NOT FOUND IN RHP — this remains
open and unverifiable from the documents in this container; the AR's Group-level Note 44 (p.126) does
independently confirm "no revenue from transactions with a single external customer amounted to 10% or
more" for the period ended 31 December 2025 and FY25/FY24/FY23 (RHP p.333, line matching AR
consolidated Note 44 wording exactly) — so the CONSOLIDATED side of Pass 1's Finding 9 is confirmed as a
multi-year pattern (no Group-level concentration in any of the four periods checked), even though the
STANDALONE side of the same finding cannot be cross-checked across years.
