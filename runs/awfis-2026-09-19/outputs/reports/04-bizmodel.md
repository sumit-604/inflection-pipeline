## SECTION 1: THE BUSINESS MODEL IN PLAIN ENGLISH

### 1A. One-line description
Awfis leases or co-funds office floors from building owners, fits them
out, and re-lets desks, cabins and managed offices to companies by the
month, plus a design-and-build arm that fits out offices for clients
directly (AR p.5-6, p.10).

### 1B. Money-flow chain, one chain per revenue stream

**Stream 1 — Co-working space on rent and allied services (the core
business, 82.8% of FY26 revenue from operations, see 1C):**
[Awfis secures a floor from a landlord under Straight Lease or Managed
Aggregation] → [Awfis fits it out with desks, cabins, meeting rooms,
café, IT infra] → [it delivers ready-to-use seats/cabins/managed offices
plus allied services (TechLabs IT support, Awfis Go, Café, events) to
enterprises, GCCs, SMEs, start-ups] → [the paying party is the occupying
company, billed per seat/cabin/managed-office under a signed licence
agreement with a lock-in] → [payment is monthly in advance, backed by an
upfront security deposit (₹3,514.58mn / ₹351.5 Cr standalone at FY26,
Note 36(b), AR p.116)] (AR p.5-6, p.10-11, p.31-32; Inv. Pres. p.19-21).

**Stream 2 — Construction and fit-out projects (Transform by Awfis /
Design & Build, 17.2% of FY26 revenue, being carved into the wholly
owned subsidiary Awfis Transform Pvt Ltd, ATPL):**
[A corporate client (or Awfis itself, for its own new centres) commissions
an office fit-out] → [Transform designs and executes space planning,
interiors and project execution] → [it delivers a completed fitted-out
office] → [the paying party is the external client (59% of Transform
FY26 revenue, up from 46% FY25) or Awfis' own co-working business (the
balance, an internal/related-party flow)] → [payment is an initial
advance of 15-20% of contract value plus milestone payments] (AR p.14,
p.3800-3814 MD&A; Note 40 p.122-123).

### 1E. The chai-stall-uncle version
Think of Awfis like a hotel that doesn't own its building. It rents a
whole empty floor from a landlord — sometimes splitting the renovation
bill with the landlord instead of paying full rent (that's Managed
Aggregation). It fits the floor out with desks and cabins, like a hotel
fits out rooms. Then it rents out individual desks by the month to
companies, the way a hotel rents rooms by the night. Every company that
moves in pays a security deposit upfront, real cash Awfis can use
immediately, while it owes rent to the landlord on its own separate
schedule. That gap, cash collected from desk-renters today versus cash
owed to the landlord later, is where this business actually makes or
loses money; the accounting profit number hides that gap almost
entirely (see Section 3, cash EBITDA bridge).

Growth stage: flex space penetration is rising structurally (21% of CY26

## SECTION 3: FINANCIAL METRICS THAT MATTER FOR THIS BUSINESS MODEL

### 3A. Ignore-these-track-these

| Commonly tracked ratio | Why it is MISLEADING/IRRELEVANT here |
|---|---|
| Reported EBITDA margin (36.8% FY26, AR p.11) | Ind AS 116 keeps lease rent out of EBITDA (it appears as D&A and finance cost instead); the Q1 FY27 bridge shows cash EBITDA is 10.1%, a 28.1pp gap (Inv. Pres. p.20-21) |
| Net Debt/Equity or gross "Borrowings" ratio | Screener/reported "Borrowings" is ~97% lease liability, not financial debt (B01 FLAG-LEASE-ACCOUNTING); actual financial debt is ₹509.31mn (₹50.9 Cr) FY26 (Note 36, AR p.116-117) against a net-cash position |
| Interest coverage on reported EBITDA | Numerator (pre-rent EBITDA) and denominator (includes lease interest embedded in "finance cost") are both distorted by the same accounting standard; AR's own MD&A defines a separate "ex-Ind-AS-116" interest coverage (~11x, AR p.34) that should be used instead |
| ROCE as stated in AR front-matter ("60%+", AR p.6, p.9) | Uses an undefined "Cash EBIT" numerator inconsistent with the Schedule III-mandated Note 41 ratio (11.87% FY26 standalone) — a ~5x unreconciled gap inside the same document (B03 FLAG-ROCE-DEFINITION). The MD&A does define its own formula elsewhere (Ind AS EBITDA less actual lease payments ÷ average capital employed, AR p.34) — use that formula consistently, not the headline number alone |
| Gross margin proxy (Revenue − Material Cost)/Revenue | No real cost-of-goods-sold concept for a lease-and-sublet service business (B01 M9 note); not a genuine brand/pricing read here |
| Current ratio / quick ratio | Negative working capital is a designed, cash-generative feature (client deposits collected upfront, payables run longer) — not distress; standalone net current liability position did widen 58.3% YoY (Note 2 going-concern paragraph, B02 finding), which is the more relevant signal than the static ratio |
| Fixed-asset turnover on gross block including right-of-use assets | Mixes owned PP&E with capitalised lease rights; not comparable across peers with different Straight Lease vs MA mix (the presentation's own 1.5x figure, Inv. Pres. p.8, uses "revenue from co-working ÷ average closing gross block" — confirm which gross-block definition before cross-company use) |
| P/E on reported PAT | Effective tax rate is ~0% both years (large DTL/DTA netting, Note 39, B02 finding) and FY25 PAT included a one-off ₹251.02mn Awfis Care exceptional gain (Note 29/30) — reported PAT quality needs adjustment before multiple comparison |

| Incremental margin / operating leverage | The company's own bridge shows incremental revenue does not translate 1:1 into incremental cash EBITDA while a portfolio is absorbing lease-cost resets (H1 FY27 margin pressure from the 2021-vintage lease cohort's 5-year reset, timing-lagged 3-4 quarters behind the pass-through to tenants) — genuine operating leverage exists once a centre is mature, but the reported year can mask it | Q1 FY27 concall, p.16 of 16 |

Illustrative (not company-actual) prospectus unit economics for context: under the industry-section hypothesis, a Managed Aggregation centre generates ~22% EBITDA margin and ~78% ROCE for the operator with a ~16-month payback, versus ~32% margin, ~33% ROCE and a ~36-month payback under Straight Lease, at a 70:30 owner:operator fit-out cost split in the MA case (Prospectus p.181-183). These are illustrative model assumptions from the IPO prospectus's industry section, not Awfis-reported actuals, and should not be read as current company economics.

### 4B. Valuation method applicability

| Method | Applicable? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| DCF (cash-flow basis, FCFE/FCFF built on cash EBITDA and actual lease cash outflow, not reported Ind AS 116 EBITDA) | **PRIMARY** | Only method that naturally reconciles the reported-vs-cash EBITDA gap (28.1pp in Q1 FY27) and captures the timing lag between landlord resets and tenant pass-through described in 3D/4A |
| EV/EBITDA or EV/Sales, cash-EBITDA basis, cross-checked against Smartworks/IndiQube/DevX on the same normalised basis | **SECONDARY** | Reported-EBITDA multiples are not comparable across peers with different MA/SL mix and lease-accounting elections; must be rebuilt on a common cash basis first |
| EV per operational seat / EV per sq ft | **TERTIARY** | Capacity-based cross-check common among flexible-workspace investors; useful sanity check on the DCF's implied per-seat value, not a standalone driver |
| Reported EV/EBITDA (Ind AS 116, pre-rent) | NOT APPLICABLE | Materially overstates cash-generating capacity; see 3A |
| P/E on reported, unadjusted PAT | NOT APPLICABLE without adjustment | ~0% effective tax rate and a one-off FY25 exceptional gain both distort it; usable only after normalising for cash tax and one-offs |
| Asset-based / NAV / SOTP | NOT APPLICABLE | Awfis owns negligible real estate; nearly all footprint is leased or co-invested (MA), so there is no owned-asset base to value on a NAV basis |
| Cycle stage that matters for valuation | Growth-stage flex-penetration tailwind (Section 2D/2E) sets the volume trajectory; the near-term cash-margin inflection (whether cash EBITDA margin climbs off ~10% as the 2021-vintage lease resets wash through and MA share rises) is the variable that should set the entry multiple, per the Section 1B destination-PE framework downstream. NOTE: the Section 1B exit-multiple determination is out of scope for this stage; this handoff names methods only. |

### 4C. Quarterly monitoring checklist (10-15 items)

1. Cash EBITDA and cash EBITDA margin (Inv. Pres. "Cash EBITDA Profile" slide) — good: rising toward/above the FY27 guide; trouble: flat or falling below 10%
2. Mature (>12mo) vs blended occupancy, and the gap between them — good: gap narrowing, both rising; trouble: gap widening or mature occupancy itself falling
3. Net seat adds (gross adds minus exits/closures) — good: net positive, tracking toward FY27 guide; trouble: net negative for 2+ quarters
4. MA vs Straight Lease vs Partial MO supply mix — good: near the ~60:40 MA:SL target; trouble: SL share rising involuntarily (higher fit-out capex burden)
5. Monthly net churn rate — good: stable near 1.5-2%; trouble: rising, or a repeat large single-client exit
6. Co-working revenue growth YoY — good: mid-20s%+; trouble: deceleration below seat-capacity growth
7. Transform (D&B) third-party revenue and % of segment — good: rising, as in FY26 (46%→59%, AR p.34); trouble: reversing or total segment revenue continuing to shrink
8. Top-10 customer receivable concentration — good: stable/falling from 61.66%; trouble: further rise
9. Lease liability maturity, especially the <1-year bucket, versus client lock-in maturity — good: stable ALM gap; trouble: widening near-term mismatch
10. Financial (non-lease) debt and net cash position — good: net cash maintained; trouble: rising financial borrowings or falling cash
