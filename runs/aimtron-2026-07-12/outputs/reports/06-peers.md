### Q5 — EMS peers show margin erosion in defence contracts (Aimtron implies many EMS peers don't make money in defence)

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Claim | EMS peers show margin erosion in defence contracts, consistent with Aimtron's implication that "does defence business but many don't make money" |
| Verdict | **CONTRADICTED** |
| Peer evidence | Centum is the most disclosure-rich peer on this exact point and shows the opposite pattern: CFO Sundararajan Parthasarathy, Q3 FY26 (Feb 2026 call): "the BTS segment, we carry a margin profile of around 20% at EBITDA level, whereas the EMS segment across industries... is fairly pegged at between 9% and 10%." BTS (Build-to-Spec) is Centum's near-entirely defense/space business; EMS is the lower-margin contract-manufacturing side. Defense-specific program margins were also quoted directly: "in this business, we expect to be at 20% to 25% EBITDA margin" for BTS radar/navigation defense programs (Q3 FY26 call). This directly contradicts the framing that defence contracts erode EMS margins — for Centum, defence/BTS is structurally the *higher*-margin segment, not the lower. Partial nuance: Cyient DLM's now-completed large legacy defense order was specifically characterized as low-margin ("a large customer order that has come to an end completely... the backlog now contains higher-margin orders, signaling a favorable shift" — Q1 FY26 call), which is contract-specific evidence in Aimtron's favor, but it describes one expiring order, not a sector pattern, and Cyient DLM's own commentary frames the shift toward non-defense-heavy mix as margin-*accretive*, i.e., the defense order itself was the outlier low-margin item, not defense generically. |
| Peers silent | Avalon and Vinyas do not disaggregate margins by defense vs. non-defense segment specifically enough to independently test this claim; Vinyas's business is ~85-90% defense/aerospace by order book with EBITDA margins of 9-11% guided to rise with mix shift toward system integration — this is at minimum ambiguous, neither confirming nor contradicting margin erosion from defence specifically since there is no non-defense baseline within Vinyas to compare against. |
| Net read | The clearest independent evidence (Centum, which explicitly separates defense/BTS from EMS margins across three consecutive quarters) shows defence contracts as the *more* profitable segment, not less. This directly undercuts Aimtron's implied claim. The one piece of supporting evidence (Cyient DLM's low-margin legacy defense order) is a single-contract data point being actively replaced by higher-margin orders, not evidence of a sector-wide pattern. ## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

**2A Demand environment.** Consensus, not split or contradiction: every peer describes a strengthening EMS/ESDM demand environment across FY26, with Avalon revising guidance upward three separate times within the year (ending at 46% actual growth against an initial 23-25% guide), Centum standalone delivering ~25% growth with "over 2.3x" order book growth over three years, Cyient DLM sustaining a book-to-bill ratio above 1 for three consecutive quarters with record order intake, and Vinyas delivering 43% H1 growth against a 30-35% multi-year guide with FY26 order inflow of Rs960cr. This is broad, multi-peer corroboration of the industry tailwind Aimtron's narrative rests on — the sector-wide picture supports rather than undercuts that portion of the framing. The one caveat, noted above (Q6), is that Aimtron's own pipeline is described as static against this backdrop of peer order-book growth.

**2B Pricing and input costs.** Peers broadly confirm a pass-through pricing model as sector standard, with absolute gross margins protected even as tariff/commodity grossing-up produces optical percentage compression (Avalon: ~100-110bps optical GM% drag from tariff pass-through despite unaffected absolute margins, three consecutive quarters). Centum is the standout independent source validating a specific, live input-cost theme matching Aimtron's semiconductor/DRAM narrative: AI-datacenter-driven memory component tightness increasing lead times industry-wide (May 2026 call). Vinyas corroborates general semiconductor supply chain tightness easing over a 3-4 month horizon. This is consistent with, not contradictory to, Aimtron's margin-protection narrative — though no peer provides the specific import-mix percentages Aimtron cites.

**2C Capex cycle.** This is an industry-wide capacity expansion, not a lone-expander situation. Avalon: Chennai brownfield Phase 2, new export-focused Chennai plant, ~Rs50-60cr/year sustained capex. Centum: KIADB Aerospace Park groundbreaking, new manufacturing lines, 80+ new product introductions in FY26. Cyient DLM: factory automation/digitization program, Mysore land acquisition for a new owned factory, though running at only 50-60% utilization currently (implying room to grow revenue before further major capex is needed). Vinyas: SMT line capacity expansion aiming to roughly double current capacity, additional 25,000 sq ft for system integration, Rs30cr capex tranche already deployed. All four peers are expanding capacity concurrently with the demand upcycle described in 2A — this is a sector-wide capacity race consistent with, not an outlier relative to, Aimtron's own capex plans.

**2D Competitive mentions.** No peer transcript mentions Aimtron by name or by unmistakable description in any of the eleven substantive calls reviewed. No peer mentions L&T's ESDM entry either (see Q3). This is a null result in both directions — no positive competitive intelligence, favorable or unfavorable, was surfaced.

**2E Risks peers discuss that Aimtron's own materials (per the stage-5 claim set) do not appear to name.**
- **Customer/order concentration and single-large-customer dependency risk:** Cyient DLM's growth was materially disrupted for multiple quarters by the completion/delay of a single large defense order (BEL-linked), with a stated inability to predict when a repeat order would be re-issued — a live illustration of concentration risk in defense-linked EMS revenue.
- **Dual-shore/geographic diversification cost:** Avalon's US manufacturing operations ran at a sustained EBITDA/PAT loss for multiple years (quarterly losses of Rs5-14cr, narrowing but persistent through FY26), a real, quantified cost of the geographic diversification strategy that is easy to underweight in a bull case.
- **Overseas subsidiary/M&A execution risk:** Centum's Canada and France subsidiaries required a full year of restructuring, discontinuation, and over Rs200cr of exceptional impairments/write-offs; Cyient DLM separately expensed $17.75mn on a failed M&A evaluation. Both illustrate that inorganic/geographic expansion in this sector carries real, sometimes large, execution risk.
- **Component/geopolitical supply risk beyond China:** Middle East conflict disruption (Israel-Iran, and separately Gaza-region tensions) disrupted air-freight routing and costs for Cyient DLM across multiple quarters; Vinyas discloses a specific 10-15% dependency on Israeli-sourced components for critical defense programs, a geopolitical exposure distinct from the China-centric framing in Aimtron's own sourcing claim.
- **Regulatory/labor cost risk:** Centum quantified a new-labor-code incremental cost impact (Rs33 lakh in one quarter); Cyient DLM separately flagged a new-wage-code one-off cost. Neither is large in isolation, but both indicate a live regulatory cost line peers are tracking explicitly.

## PART 4: TRIANGULATION SUMMARY

- **Claims verified (full VERIFIED bar — 2+ peers, direction and magnitude): 0 of 6.** None of the six claims met the strict bar of two or more independent peers corroborating both direction and rough magnitude on the specific figure tested. Three claims reached PARTIALLY VERIFIED (Q1, Q2, Q6), two reached CONTRADICTED (Q4, Q5), and one is UNVERIFIABLE (Q3).
- **Claims contradicted: 2 of 6 (Q4, Q5) — priority items for synthesis.**
- **The single most consequential contradiction:** Q5 — defence contract margins. Centum's disaggregated, multi-quarter disclosure shows its defense/BTS business running at 20-25% EBITDA margin versus 9-11% for its EMS business — the opposite of Aimtron's implied "does defence business but many don't make money" framing. If defence contracts are structurally the higher-margin segment across the peer set (as Centum's clean data shows), Aimtron's implicit competitive-differentiation argument on defence profitability needs re-examination, not restatement.
- **The single strongest independent confirmation:** the AI-datacenter-driven memory/DRAM component tightness Centum discloses independently (May 2026 call) is a close, dated, and specific match to the semiconductor/DRAM shortage element of Aimtron's Q2 claim — a peer volunteering the same supply-side dynamic, unprompted, in the same period, with no incentive to support Aimtron's narrative.
- **Overall:** the peer set **complicates** more than it supports or undercuts Aimtron's narrative wholesale. The macro backdrop Aimtron describes — a growing EMS/ESDM demand environment, industry-wide capacity expansion, and cost pass-through as standard practice — is genuinely and broadly corroborated across all four peers, giving real support to the sector-level framing. But two of Aimtron's more specific, differentiating claims do not hold up against peer disclosure: the SMT-line productivity figure is contradicted by a directly comparable, size-similar peer (Vinyas) citing a 5-6x higher per-line revenue figure, and the defence-margin-erosion framing is contradicted by the peer with the cleanest disaggregated disclosure (Centum), which shows defence as the higher-margin segment. Both should be treated as priority flags for synthesis rather than dismissed as immaterial peer-mix differences, since both bear directly on capacity-driven growth assumptions and on the defence-exposure profitability thesis respectively.
