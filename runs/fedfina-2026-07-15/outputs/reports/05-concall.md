Context for the reader: this is a company mid-turnaround. FY25 saw a credit-cost problem
concentrated in small-ticket LAP (ST LAP), driven by an under-invested, largely
agency-outsourced collection function. FY26 is explicitly framed by management as a
"rebuild year" for ST LAP and a "credit cost year" (target 1% ± 10bps), with cost-to-income
and opex efficiency pushed out to FY27. Gold loans are the engine funding this rebuild.

### 2B. Excuse pattern analysis

For the one area where management has had to explain a genuine miss — the ST LAP
credit-cost/collection problem — the pattern is consistently **honest-admission**, not
external-blame. Repeated verbatim across all three calls: "it was our issue," "our
collection team's inadequacy," "not necessarily an environment issue... it was our
issue" (Q3, responding to Prolin Nandu/Rahul). Management proactively volunteers that
Fedfina was "one of the first companies" to flag ST LAP stress (Q1 call) and names the
specific weak geographies (Maharashtra, Tamil Nadu) unprompted by Q3.

Where the pattern weakens is on items management would rather not revisit: the ST LAP
credit-scorecard commitment, the MT LAP BRE pilot, and the CRAR/Tier 2 update are
handled by **silence** rather than an active excuse — they are simply not brought up
again, and analysts did not press on any of the three across the transcripts reviewed.
This is a milder but real deflection pattern: honest and proactive on the headline
turnaround narrative (credit cost, secured mix), quiet on smaller specific commitments
that didn't pan out or aren't ready to discuss.

Management does say "we made a mistake" in substance repeatedly (framing the ST LAP
problem as self-inflicted), which is a positive credibility signal. Hard topics (the
ST LAP stress, the write-off/ARC sale mechanics, the labor-code cost hit) are raised in
opening remarks, not only under analyst pressure.

### 2D. What they are NOT saying

- **CRAR / Tier 2 capital**: disclosed each quarter through Q2 FY26 (22.4% → 21.64%,
  with an explicit promise to supplement Tier 2 in H2 FY26), then **not mentioned at
  all in the Q3 FY26 call** — precisely the quarter in which leverage jumped most
  (D/E 3.78 → 3.99) and gold-loan disbursal hit an all-time high (₹7,853cr). Likely
  reason for silence: capital adequacy may be tightening as leverage grows into the
  gold opportunity, and management would rather not draw attention to it before the
  promised Tier 2 raise materializes.
- **ST LAP credit scorecard/BRE rollout**, targeted for Q3 FY26 in the Q1 call: never
  mentioned again. Likely reason: the rollout may have slipped alongside the broader
  collection in-housing delay, but management chose not to flag it rather than report
  a second miss on the same workstream in the same call.
- **MT LAP BRE/scorecard pilot**: introduced as "under evaluation" in Q1, then dropped
  from all subsequent commentary, even though MT LAP is described elsewhere as the
  "pristine" part of the book — plausible reason is lower priority once ST LAP
  consumed the risk-team's bandwidth.
- **FY27 credit cost / opex-to-assets guidance**: explicitly deferred to Q4 FY26 when
  directly asked (Sandeep Joshi, Q3 call). Not evasive per se (guidance genuinely not
  yet due), but stacks with the pattern of resisting a numeric commitment ahead of time.
- **Geographic branch-mix discrepancy** flagged by Renish (ICICI Securities) in Q1: no
  correction or follow-up appears in Q2 or Q3 transcripts.

## SECTION 3: COMPETITIVE INTELLIGENCE FROM CONCALLS

### 3A. Competitor commentary and credibility check

- Management repeatedly frames gold-loan competition as **hyper-local**: "you don't
  match your yields with every player, you match it with the predominant player" in a
  given state (Q3, responding to Sonal Minhas). This is a reasonable but unverifiable
  claim — worth checking against Manappuram's own state-level color.
- Fedfina disputes an analyst's characterization that its LTV is "higher" than
  non-bank peers, claiming onboarding LTV of 70-71% vs regulatory caps of 80-85% (Q3,
  Jagadeesh Rao). Credibility check: internally consistent with the company's own
  declared portfolio LTV trend (64% Q1 → 62% Q2 → 59.3% Q3), which is falling, not
  rising — supports the "conservative" framing, but needs peer corroboration.
  (Sonal Minhas, Q3, named Muthoot and Manappuram explicitly as the comparison set.)
- Management states larger gold players (cited as guiding ~20% AUM growth) were
  referenced by an analyst (Yash Dantewadia, Q1) without naming which peer explicitly
  said 20% — Fedfina counter-guided 25% Y-o-Y with existing branches. Unverified claim
  about peer guidance, flagged for peer-stage verification.

### 3B. Industry and market intelligence

- Gold: industry tonnage growth cited by management at 10-15% over the last two years,
  "higher than 15% in one of the years" (Q1); reiterated as a 10-12% CAGR target
  going forward (Q3).
- RBI gold-loan LTV guidelines: December 2024 draft was "pretty strict"; revised final
  guidelines were "a little more relaxed" (up to 85% LTV for certain ticket sizes),
  but Fedfina states it has **not yet adopted** the relaxed LTV as of Q3 FY26 —
  deliberately staying on the stricter December framework.
- MFI-sector stress is cited as spilling into the sub-₹5 lakh and ₹5-7 lakh ST LAP
  ticket band (Q3, Parvez Mulla) — an industry-wide claim, not company-specific,
  explicitly distinguished from the Maharashtra/Tamil Nadu stress which management
  calls "our issue," not a market issue.
- New RBI co-lending guidelines effective January 1, 2026 cited as the reason for an
  accounting-methodology shift from direct assignment (DA) income to co-lending income
  recognition (Q3 call).
- Labor code regulation change drove a one-off ₹3.9cr cost impact in Q3 FY26
  (retiral-benefit catch-up from the new 50%-of-CTC basic-wage requirement); management
  states future recurring impact is uncertain and will be benchmarked against how
  peers respond in the April wage cycle.

### 3D. Customer and order book signals

- Business-loan (unsecured) book fully exited via two tranches of 100% assignment
  (₹770cr in Q1, ₹115.6cr in Q2), residual book down to under 0.6-0.9% of the balance
  sheet by Q3 — a clean, verifiable wind-down with disclosed 0-DPD proportion (>75% of
  the Q2 residual book).
- ARC sale of deep-delinquent ST LAP/HL pool (₹79.5cr gross, including ₹41cr already
  written off) for ₹32.6cr upfront cash in Q2 — a real deleveraging/cleanup event, not
  a cosmetic one, though it compressed PCR optically to ~32% from ~38-40%.
  the PCR compression, and the flow-through to Q3's PCR recovery to 32.3%, are
  consistent and traceable across the two calls.
  no concentration/renewal data was disclosed at the customer level in any of the three
  calls (typical for a retail secured-lending book).
- Geographic mix: management describes a deliberate pivot toward Western/Northern
  gold-loan markets and away from the South (already dominated by larger players),
  while mortgage AUM is pushed into "white space" geographies competitors haven't
  penetrated — consistent narrative across Q1 and Q3, though the Q1-flagged
  "geographic branch mix" data discrepancy (Renish) was never resolved on record.
