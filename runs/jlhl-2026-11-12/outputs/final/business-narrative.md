# Jupiter Life Line Hospitals Ltd (JLHL): business narrative, phase 1

Run 2026-11-12. Phase 1 lite. Evidence stages only. No valuation has run, the Mental Model is not signed, and this file carries no entry zone, no decision and no destination PE. Figures are in Rs Cr unless marked Rs Mn (10 Rs Mn = 1 Rs Cr).

## BUSINESS UNDERSTANDING NARRATIVE

Jupiter owns and runs four hospitals in Thane, Pune, Indore and Dombivli, with 1,259 operating beds at September 2026. In-patient care earns 78% of the Rs 1,500 Cr FY26 revenue: a patient is admitted for cardiac, cancer, orthopaedic, neuro or transplant care and stays about four days. Out-patient consultations, scans and lab tests earn 17.5%, and pharmacy, cafeteria and the Thane hotel share the last 4%. A patient who needs a transplant, a cath lab or radiation therapy cannot get it at home or at a small nursing home, so this work has weak substitutes.

The customers are many individual patients, and insurers and TPAs pay about 55% of the bill, patients 44% and government schemes 1%. The insurers are the concentrated side and negotiate tariffs, but the run did not establish the split by named insurer. Doctors are the key supplier: 2,052 consultants on fixed, minimum plus variable or per service terms, free to leave on one to three months' notice.

Present demand comes from lifestyle disease and from insurance cover that rose from about 37% to 41% of Indians between CY21 and CY25, tracked by "Health insurer and TPA cashless empanelment and claim settlement" and "Health insurance penetration and premium trend". The three listed peers report the same pull, with in-patient volume at their existing hospitals up 27% to 30% in Q2 FY27.

Forward growth comes from new beds, because management says mature ARPOB at Thane and Pune tracks inflation and both run near 70% occupancy. The plan adds 500 beds at Pune South in CY28, 300 at Mira Road in CY29 and 400 at BKC in CY31, dated by "MMRDA BKC lease registration and MBMC Mira Road approvals", "NABH accreditation of Dombivli" and "Pune private bed supply". By count the markets are not short of beds: MMR has 33, Pune 30 and Indore 37.5 per 10,000 people against a global median of 29, and only Kalyan-Dombivli, at 10, is short. "NPPA and DoP onco-drug 30% margin cap" and "Peer micro-market occupancy and ARPOB" are the signals that could cut against growth.

The hospital services line has a regional moat of moderate depth: a Thane brand since 2007, NABH accreditation, owned land in dense Mumbai and Pune markets, and local scale near 70% occupancy. The Emerging Moat scan scores 13.6, MODEST, with two active categories, a land bank first mover position and an execution record, both moderate. It found no pricing or retention edge, and talent asymmetry (Category 21) and cannibalization barrier (Category 22) both score zero. Dombivli has no moat yet, because its local scale is unproven at about 35% occupancy. Pharmacy, cafeteria and the hotel have no moat.

Mature hospitals fund a costly bed build

FY26 revenue was Rs 1,500 Cr, EBITDA Rs 343 Cr at a 22.9% margin, and PAT Rs 194 Cr. Thane and Pune run near 70% occupancy and carry the group. Each occupied bed earned Rs 67,700 a day in FY26 and Rs 71,800 in the first half of FY27. Doctor fees take 24% of income and grew 19% against revenue growth of 15%. Mature margin held near 23% through the year. Group occupancy fell from 65.3% to 61.2% as new beds opened, and the hospital-wise split that the FY25 report printed is gone from the FY26 report.

The business is moving from three mature hospitals to a seven hospital network of about 2,900 beds. Dombivli opened in February 2026, ahead of the Q1 FY27 date management gave. Its EBITDA loss fell from Rs 9.5 Cr in Q1 FY27 to Rs 0.2 Cr in Q2 FY27. Two readings fit that print. Either the ramp runs ahead of plan, or seasonal medical admissions flattered Q2 and Q3 reverts toward Rs 2 to 3 Cr a month. Management could not size the seasonal part. The Dombivli EBITDA footnote in the Q3 FY27 deck separates the two. Only Pune South is under construction, and it alone adds about 62% to FY26 revenue capacity. The 177% figure counts the whole announced pipeline, and Mira Road and BKC have not started.

The backward screen grades the company GOOD. The verifier's recompute gives a core score of 68 out of 100 (the stage printed 71), because a promoter holding test was scored on 18 months, not three years. Weak free cash flow capped a GOOD+ result at GOOD. Profit has stalled under the build. PAT was flat at Rs 194 Cr in FY25 and FY26 while revenue grew 15%. Depreciation rose from Rs 57 Cr to Rs 88 Cr and interest from Rs 11 Cr to Rs 33 Cr. The squeeze deepened in the first half of FY27, when PAT fell 7.4% to Rs 94 Cr. ROCE fell from 20.5% in FY23 to 13.8% in FY26 on the screener basis, against 18.55% in the annual report's own definition.

Operating cash is steady and the build eats all of it. Operating cash flow held at about Rs 268 Cr in FY25 and FY26, while capex rose to Rs 502 Cr and free cash flow fell to minus Rs 234 Cr. Borrowings reached Rs 589 Cr at September 2026, or Rs 669 Cr with Rs 80 Cr of leases, from nil in March 2024. A Rs 500 Cr HDFC term loan was signed in October 2026. Trade receivables rose from Rs 75.5 Cr to Rs 118 Cr in six months, against income growth of 16%. Part of that rise is a new unbilled revenue line created by an accounting change that management never sized. No document in the corpus gives the September 2026 ageing, so the cash read stays open. On the other side, the share of receivables older than six months fell from 10.4% to 6.8% of the gross balance in FY26. Separately, 89% of the group's liquid assets sit in one issuer's commercial paper.

Management delivered its hard milestones and stayed silent where it hurts. The concall grade is B, at the low end. Dombivli opened early, the monthly loss band was beaten, and Pune South is on its CY28 schedule. On promises that are already due, the recount is 4 delivered, 2 partial and 0 missed. The Dombivli breakeven guidance held at 1.5 to 2 years on both recent calls, so no slip happened there. Mira Road softened from excavation by the end of CY26 to the next phase by the end of FY27. The funding story moved from internal accruals in May to a Rs 500 Cr term loan by October. In May the CEO said "we pretty much own all the real estate". In October the company announced a Rs 150 Cr purchase of the Pune South land it had leased. The CFO left after 12.5 months, and the call on his filing day did not mention it. The promoter read is CAUTION. Three Bombay High Court PILs on the Thane land were pending in 2023, and the run did not find their status today. A Rs 3.78 Cr purchase of a promoter company went through the pharmacy subsidiary, and fee flows to family and directors rose. The notes score 6 out of 10. They disclose no capital commitments and no capitalised interest, and the auditor found no audit trail on the billing software.

The peers confirm the operating story and contradict the funding story. At KIMS, Medanta and Yatharth new hospitals break even in 9 to 15 months, occupancy is reported on census beds, and Q1 and Q3 run weaker. All three peers show PAT growth 20 to 30 points below EBITDA growth in Q2 FY27, so Jupiter's lag is normal for a build cycle. The funding contrast cuts the other way. Jupiter allows debt up to 3 times EBITDA, while KIMS works at 1 to 2 times and Yatharth near 2 times, and Medanta holds net cash. KIMS saw FY26 PAT fall 41% in its own build and raised equity. KIMS also says NABH is not a gate for insurer empanelment, which weakens the Dombivli signal management points to. Medanta is adding beds in Indore and Mumbai, and KIMS already runs a hospital in Thane.

The market is large against the company, but the sizing rests on one 2023 source. Private tertiary care in MMR, Pune and Indore is worth about Rs 25,155 Cr in FY27, with a serviceable ceiling of Rs 18,363 Cr. Jupiter holds at least 9.4% of that ceiling, and the runway grades STRONG at the bottom of its band. The five year market share case gives revenue of Rs 3,768 Cr by FY31, a 20% annual growth rate. The cross check range runs from 12% to 23%. That case needs 101% of the bed capacity the plan provides at today's ARPOB, so it needs ARPOB growth near 6% a year and an on time Pune South and Mira Road.
