## Business Understanding Narrative

Supreme Petrochem imports styrene monomer and converts it into polystyrene (PS), expandable
polystyrene (EPS), specialty compounds, extruded boards (XPS) and, newly, ABS. It is India's
largest producer, over 50% combined PS and EPS share (CRISIL p.3). Revenue is volume times
realisation; profit is the spread, the "global delta", between styrene cost and product price. 85%
of raw material is imported (India Ratings p.4), so SPL is a price-taker on both legs.

What is changing is the mix. SPL is building ABS capacity (140,000 tonnes nameplate, Concall
p.13), compounds and XPS boards, and bought 80% of Xmold Polymers, a tier-2 compounder to auto and
appliance makers (AR p.44). Management: "we are now moving more towards the ABS compounds... we
see a lot of growth coming in there" (Concall p.6). ABS is claimed to carry a higher through-cycle
margin, made by continuous polymerisation rather than batch (India Ratings p.3).

Why now: Q1FY27 delivered the best margin quarter on record, 19.53% against a historical range of
3% to 21% (CRISIL p.3). But the CFO calls this margin "an aberration" (Concall p.8) from a supply
shock, not the mix shift; volume fell 24.5% the same quarter.

What must be true: ABS must run reliably after the December 2025 failure, and capex, guided at
both Rs 250-350 crore a year (CRISIL p.2) and Rs 900 crore for FY27 alone (Concall p.10), must
stay debt-free. What breaks it: a second ABS failure, a slide back to the 3-10% margin band before
2021, or Chinese oversupply eroding the spread that funds everything else.

## 2. Business model and archetype
Manufactured products were 77% of FY25 revenue, traded styrene resale the rest (CRISIL p.3). PS
and EPS were 71% of manufactured revenue, SPC/XPS 6%; value-added share rose from 29% (FY22) to
33% (FY25) (India Ratings p.3). Two plants: Amdoshi, Manali. Archetype: COMMODITY CONVERTER.
Engine is scale: largest installed PS (300,000 t) and EPS capacity in India, after the number-two
player shut down in FY21 (CRISIL p.3). Scale holds share through cycles; the base price tracks a
global delta management does not set (Concall p.7-8).

## 3. Competitive advantages
Scale and quality: grades "developed specially to counter low-priced imports" (CRISIL p.3), Three
Star Export House status to March 2028, EPS grades approved in Europe (Concall p.9). Long supplier
relationships give favourable credit terms. ABS claims a process edge: continuous mass
polymerisation, more consistent than batch, lower cost (India Ratings p.3), and Xmold gives a
captive tier-2 OEM base. Against this: "the base price of polystyrene... is all decided keeping in
view the global trends" (Concall p.7), and a temporary duty exemption "led to unnecessarily
increased imports" (p.3). Moat: present in scale, forming but unproven in ABS/compounds.

## 4. The promoters
Dual-promoter structure: Supreme Industries Limited 30.78%, R. Raheja Investments Private Limited
30.78%, combined 61.56%, plus small family holdings under 3% (AR p.150). M. P. Taparia (Supreme
Industries promoter-MD) chairs the board; Rajan B. Raheja is a non-executive promoter director

6 independent, 5 non-exec non-independent (mostly family), 1 whole-time director running
operations. No material related-party transactions with promoters disclosed FY25 (AR p.50).

## 5. Financial trajectory
FY24: revenue Rs 5,253 cr, adj PAT Rs 346 cr (6.6% margin), ROCE 24.16%, debt-equity 0.06x
(lease-only). FY25: revenue Rs 6,023 cr, up 15%; PAT Rs 390-391 cr, up 12.7%; volume up 9.4%;
utilisation 79%; ROCE 24.86%; debt-free, Rs 872 cr surplus (AR p.42-45; guidance). 1HFY26: revenue
fell ~18%, margin fell to 7.7% from 8.9%, EBITDA to Rs 195 cr from Rs 285 cr, on price correction
and subdued appliance demand; net debt briefly turned slightly positive, first since FY15. Q4FY26:
revenue Rs 1,587 cr +3%; EBITDA Rs 253 cr +75%, margin 15.9%; PAT Rs 168 cr; volume +5.4%;
debt-free, Rs 700 cr surplus. Q1FY27, the headline quarter: revenue Rs 1,693 cr +22%, driven
entirely by a raw-material price spike, not volume, which fell 24.5% to 70,842 MT. EBITDA
Rs 331 cr +188%, margin 19.53%; PAT Rs 236 cr, margin 13.96% (Concall p.3). Styrene-to-product
deltas widened to GPPS $250-275, HIPS ~$350, versus a normal $200 and $275-300.
Inflection: MARGIN-LED and SPREAD-DRIVEN, not mix-driven. The quarter with the biggest margin
jump also has the biggest volume fall, and management calls the margin an aberration, not
evidence of the ABS transition.

## 5A. FY27 forward read
FY26 actual: revenue Rs 5,338 cr, down 11% on lower styrene prices; PAT Rs 327 cr, down
16%; margin 6.13% (IP Q4FY26 p.19). Management guides 8 to 10% volume growth for FY27
(Q4FY26 call, guidance p.6) and refuses margin guidance (Concall Q1FY27 p.9); basis (a)
applies to volume, with realisation held at FY26.
- Primary (a): volume +9%, price flat. FY27E revenue Rs 5,820 cr. PAT at the FY26 margin:
  Rs 357 cr, +9%. Forward P/E 43x, P/S 2.6x.
- Alternate (b): Q1FY27 revenue grew 22% on price alone, volume fell 24.5% (Concall p.3).
  Revenue Rs 6,520 cr; PAT at Q1's 13.96% margin Rs 910 cr, +178%. Forward P/E 17x, P/S 2.3x.
- Separating observation: the styrene-to-polystyrene spread. Q1 ran at $250 to 350 a
  tonne against a normal $200 to 300 (Concall p.3). The CFO calls the quarter "an
  aberration" (Concall p.10). The alternate needs the wide spread to hold three more
  quarters; the primary needs volumes to recover after a 24.5% fall. Amendment 17
  binds any deep run: a converter's spread year is not a re-rating.

## 6. The transition and the quality ladder
Management's claim, quoted once: "we are now moving more towards the ABS compounds... has done
very well" (Concall p.6).
FROM R1/R2 boundary, COMMODITY PRICE-TAKER bordering COST-ADVANTAGED CONVERTER: margin has ranged
3% to 21% across cycles, decadal average near 9% (India Ratings p.4); scale gives an edge, but
volatility argues R1. Amendment 17 binds regardless.
TO R3 VALUE-ADDED/SPEC'D SUPPLIER, via ABS sold into spec'd auto and appliance applications
through Xmold, plus SPC and XPS. The mechanism, if real, is OEM-qualification stickiness absent
in commodity resin sales.
Rung-jump base rate: the ABS push has run roughly three years, with repeated slippage (targeted
Mar-2025, delayed to May-2025, commissioned Sep-2025, suspended Dec-2025), inside the
one-rung-per-2-to-3-year norm on pace, but the slippage and failure flag control risk.

## 7. Growth-trigger register
- ABS Phase 1 (70,000 MTA): commissioned Sep-2025, suspended Dec-2025 for equipment failure,
  restoring with Versalis (CRISIL p.2). Status: underway, troubled.
- ABS Phase 2 (70,000 MTA), terminal 140,000 MTPA, by FY28. Status: stated.
- EPS Phase-II, 85,000 to 115,000 TPA, commissioned 14-Apr-2026. Status: delivered.
- New XPS line, compounding expansion (to 80,000 MTA, mainly ABS), new PS 5th line (+80,000 TPA,
  due Dec-2028), styrene backward integration (to 80,000 TPA by June 2027) (Concall p.3-4,
  p.10-11). Status: mostly underway.
- Haryana greenfield complex, Rs 800-850 cr, clearances pending, pushed repeatedly since FY25.
  Status: stated.
- Xmold acquisition, 80% for Rs 31.42 cr (Apr-2025), remaining 20% by Mar-2027. Status: delivered.
  All capacities on board by March 2029 (Concall p.13).

## 8. Proof check
Fired: EPS Phase-II delivered on schedule, Xmold completed as planned, FY25 delivered 9.4% volume
growth and 24.86% ROCE, Q4FY26 showed a real pre-shock margin recovery to 15.9%.
Not fired: ABS, the product carrying the rung claim, slipped twice then failed mechanically. The
best margin quarter on record is not proof of the transition; management calls it "an aberration"
that "will get normalized" (Concall p.8). ABS and compound volumes are not disclosed separately,
so the mix claim cannot be checked.
Named proof point: whether ABS resumes stable production after the failure, at what utilisation
and margin, once the aberration passes. Not visible here.

## 9. Flags from the documents
- ABS equipment failure Dec-2025, three months after a commissioning already delayed 6-9 months.
- Management calls its own best-ever quarter's margin "an aberration" (Concall p.8): candid, but
  means the good news is not transition evidence.
- ABS/compound volumes bundled into "Styrenics" with no separate disclosure.
- Import data publication "made a crime" by a March notification (Concall p.4). A duty exemption
  separately eroded domestic share for months in 2026.
- 1HFY26 net debt briefly turned positive, first since FY15; reversed by FY26 year end.
- Capex guidance inconsistent: Rs 250-350 cr/year (CRISIL) versus Rs 900 cr FY27 alone (Concall).
- Both agencies cut outlook Positive to Stable within ten weeks of the Q1FY27 beat.
- No material related-party transactions with promoters disclosed FY25. Pledge NOT FOUND.

## 10. Independent check: the credit rating
FOUND, two live ratings, both cut in outlook shortly before this quarter's beat.
CRISIL: AA-/Stable long-term (cut from Positive), A1+ short-term, 27-Feb-2026, on "recent
moderation in SPL's operating profitability and unexpected delays in the commercial ramp-up of
its new mass ABS capacity," plus the equipment failure (CRISIL p.2). Steady-state margin expected
around 7%.
India Ratings: IND AA-/Stable (cut from Positive), 16-Dec-2025, on weaker 1HFY26 performance and
a six-month ABS delay, "deferring the increase in the proportion of value-added products" (p.2),
expecting FY26 EBITDA to fall before an ABS-led FY27 recovery.
Both agencies reached the same conclusion independently: direction intact, near-term execution
worse than expected. Neither saw the Q1FY27 windfall, which the company frames as noise.

## 11. Posture: Transition Decision Matrix
Proof gate: NOT FIRED cleanly. EPS and Xmold delivered; ABS, the product carrying the rung claim,
slipped twice then failed mechanically. The best headline quarter is a spread windfall the company
itself discounts.
Ugliness: mixed, leaning ARTIFACT-OF-CLIMB. Margin volatility and this quarter's spike read as
noise around a rising base in management's framing, and both agencies still model a structural
EBITDA step-up once ABS stabilises. An unrepaired failure keeps this unresolved.
Recognition gap: cannot be resolved here, no price data held. One arithmetic flag: Rs 15,220 cr
market cap against FY25 PAT of ~Rs 391 cr is roughly 39x trailing earnings, rich for a margin
range of 3% to 21%, pointing toward CLOSED, a flag for `/step1`, not a finding.
Proof not fired, ugliness leaning artifact, gap likely closed: maps toward PRICED NARRATIVE
(TRAP), clean optics over an unproven transition already priced, the cell the matrix calls most
seductive.

## 12. Verdict card
**WATCH.** Supreme Petrochem is a real market leader, debt-free, dual-blue-chip-promoted, rated
AA-/Stable by two agencies. The transition claim is partly delivered: EPS expansion and Xmold
landed on schedule. But ABS, the product carrying the rung claim, slipped twice then failed
mechanically, and this run's best quarter is one management calls an aberration, not transition
evidence. With a market cap far above the mandate and a rich implied multiple, this is not a
clean PROCEED. Not a PASS either: the franchise and disclosed optionality are too real to drop.
Load-bearing facts a deep run must verify first:
1. Live CMP and market cap, resolving the recognition gap this read could only estimate.
2. ABS Phase 1 status after the failure: production resumed, at what utilisation and margin.
3. Reconcile Rs 250-350 cr/year (CRISIL) versus Rs 900 cr FY27 (concall) capex, confirming
   internal-accrual funding.
4. Promoter pledge status, NOT FOUND here, and the current combined promoter percentage.
5. FY26 annual report and any investor presentation with segment ABS/compound volume.
What would change the view: a disclosed ABS utilisation and margin, sustained without a
supply-shock explanation, moves this toward PROCEED. A second ABS failure, or confirmation the
market fully prices the transition, moves it to PASS. No price given here.
