## PASS 3: PATTERN FINDINGS

Read note-by-note, Pass 1 and Pass 2 together already surfaced the filing's
two largest pattern-type issues (the standalone-vs-consolidated divergence,
and the Note 34.1.3 mislabelling). This pass adds one further synthesis and
one further genuinely new pattern observation; the rest is confirmation.

**P3-1 (synthesis, new framing) — Standalone FY20 PAT growth is a
three-mechanism presentational artifact, not organic earnings growth.**
Combining Pass 1 and Pass 2 findings into one line: standalone PAT rose
+17.0% (₹402.48cr vs ₹344.09cr, Note 37 p.149) even though (i) the
Associate's actual equity-method profit share fell 22.7% (₹100.38cr →
₹77.59cr, Consolidated P&L p.159) while the dividend booked standalone from
that Associate grew +100.1% and exceeded the equity-method profit share
(Note 25(b)(i) p.132); (ii) that dividend income is treated as tax-exempt,
the single largest driver of the FY20 tax-rate reconciliation at ₹(20.38)cr
(Note 20.1.3 p.127), larger than the Section 115BAA rate-switch effect
(₹(14.59)cr) Pass 1 flagged as primary; and (iii) the one-time Section 115BAA
regime-switch reversal of opening DTL further compressed the effective tax
rate to 19.7% from a normal ~35% (Note 20.1, Note 2, p.127). All three
mechanisms point the same direction — inflating standalone PAT in a year
when consolidated PBT fell 16.1% and consolidated PAT fell 4.0% (Consolidated
P&L p.159, verified directly against source this pass). This is the single
most important lens through which to read this filing's headline numbers.

**P3-2 (confirmed via direct source re-check) — Note 34.1.3 Level 3 fair
value reconciliation is genuinely mislabelled.** Verified directly against
p.141 of the PDF this pass: the table is headed "Unlisted shares
irrevocably designated as at FVTOCI" but its FY20 figures (opening
₹103.26cr, loss ₹(34.84)cr, closing ₹68.42cr) equal the combined
quoted+unquoted FVTOCI book (Note 6: ₹77.74cr+₹25.52cr=₹103.26cr opening;
₹21.98cr+₹46.44cr=₹68.42cr closing), not the unquoted-only book, which
actually rose from ₹25.52cr to ₹46.44cr over the same period. This is a
confirmed disclosure-quality defect, not a suspected one.

**P3-3 (confirmed) — Same JV impairment event reported at two different
amounts across statements with no cross-reference.** Standalone: ₹35.10cr
(Note 5.2 p.116, Note 31(s) p.134). Consolidated: ₹21.58cr (Consolidated
Note 31(s) p.195, Consolidated Cash Flow p.160). This reflects genuinely
different impairment-testing bases (Ind AS 36 cost-less-impairment
standalone vs Ind AS 28 equity-method testing consolidated) rather than an
error, but no note anywhere flags the divergence to a reader comparing the
two statements.

**P3-4 (new this pass) — Customer advances and finished-goods inventory
both spiked at the same COVID-lockdown year-end, unexplained.** Advances
from customers rose 169% (₹8.32cr → ₹22.39cr, Note 23 p.130) in the same
quarter that finished goods rose 8.4% (₹274.31cr → ₹297.34cr, Note 11
p.122) against a revenue decline (-6.5%). Both movements are plausibly
explained by the same COVID-19 dispatch disruption the Chairman's letter
describes (goods produced/invoiced but not shipped, advances collected
defensively ahead of lockdown), but the notes do not connect the two
disclosures or explain either individually. Read together rather than
separately (as Pass 1 and Pass 2 did), the combination is exactly the
pattern an investor would want ruled out as period-end revenue/channel
optics rather than assumed benign. Flagged as a new management question
below (not elevated to a red flag, since a plausible non-adverse explanation
exists and no other indicator — e.g., no unusual reversal of these balances
disclosed — corroborates a more adverse reading).

**P3-5 — No restatements found.** Ind AS 116 (Leases) was adopted via the
modified retrospective method with FY19 comparatives explicitly not
restated (Note 2.24 p.113) — a proper, disclosed non-restatement, not a
gap. Ind AS 115 was adopted in the prior year (FY19) via cumulative
catch-up with an "insignificant" effect (Note 2.5 p.106). No note discloses
any prior-period error correction or reclassification of comparatives
beyond ordinary presentation. **restatements_found: none.**

**P3-6 — Going concern language: NONE.** No explicit going-concern doubt
language appears anywhere in the standalone or consolidated notes. Note 38
(COVID-19, p.149) is a standard forward-uncertainty/estimation-sensitivity
disclosure ("expects the carrying amount of these assets will be
recovered"), not a going-concern qualification. The auditor's opinion
(Deloitte Haskins & Sells LLP) is clean and unmodified, standalone and
consolidated, with no Emphasis of Matter (p.96, p.157).

**P3-7 — Events after the reporting period: only the dividend proposal.**
Note 39 (p.149) confirms no significant adjusting events beyond the
₹5.50/share final dividend (₹84.12cr, Note 16(b) p.125), correctly treated
as non-adjusting. No other subsequent events identified across either pass.

PASS 3 otherwise yields no further material new findings beyond P3-1
through P3-7 above; the two prior passes' note-by-note and
standalone-vs-consolidated cross-reads already captured the filing's
principal contradictions and disclosure gaps.

### D. FIVE QUESTIONS FOR MANAGEMENT

1. Consolidated PBT fell 16.1% and consolidated PAT fell 4.0% in FY20 while standalone PAT rose 17.0%. Does management consider standalone or consolidated results the more representative measure of the group's underlying earnings power, and why did the dividend received from the Associate exceed that year's equity-method profit share?
2. What is the expected quantum or ceiling of the open-ended financial-support commitment to Finolex J-Power Systems (Note 32(b)(ii)), and at what point would continued support be reassessed as unrecoverable?
3. What is the status of the Bombay High Court proceedings on the four directors' (including two Independent Directors') reappointment, and what is the clawback exposure on the ₹18.56cr+ remuneration paid/payable pending that ruling?
4. What drove the 169% jump in advances from customers (Note 23) and the 8.4% rise in finished-goods inventory against a 6.5% revenue decline in the same COVID-affected quarter — can management confirm there was no acceleration of billing or advance collection to manage year-end reported figures?
5. Can management clarify the counterparty, tenure and rate for the new ₹50cr "Deposits with others" (Note 9), and correct the Note 34.1.3 Level 3 fair-value reconciliation table, which is labelled as unquoted-only but reports the combined quoted+unquoted FVTOCI movement?

### E. NOTES-BASED RED FLAGS

- Standalone PAT growth (+17.0%) is a three-mechanism presentational artifact (dividend exceeding equity-pickup, tax-exempt dividend treatment, one-time Section 115BAA rate-switch benefit) that masks a 16.1% consolidated PBT decline and a genuine fall in the Associate's own profitability — materially misleading if the standalone figure is read without the consolidated context.
- Electoral Bonds of ₹20.00cr, a new, undisclosed-recipient political donation exceeding the entire CSR spend — a governance/capital-allocation flag, not an accounting misstatement.
- Four directors' reappointments sub judice with continuing, unprovisioned remuneration payments pending a Court ruling.
- Note 34.1.3 Level 3 fair-value reconciliation table mislabelled — confirmed disclosure defect.
- Same JV impairment event reported at two different amounts (₹35.10cr standalone, ₹21.58cr consolidated) with no cross-reference between statements.
- Open-ended, unquantified financial-support commitment to a loss-making JV already carrying ₹135.73cr of cumulative impairment.

No evidence of revenue misstatement, channel stuffing (beyond the flagged-but-unresolved Note 23/Note 11 coincidence), or fictitious asset creation was found in either pass.

### F. ONE-LINE NOTES VERDICT

The notes reveal moderate accounting practices bordering on concerning
given presentation choices. Key concern: standalone PAT growth of 17.0%
is a three-mechanism presentational artifact masking a 16.1% consolidated
PBT decline and a genuine fall in the Associate's underlying
profitability. Key strength: a debt-free balance sheet, conservative PPE
useful lives, prudent and rising ECL provisioning, and an unmodified audit
opinion with no going-concern language. Overall accounting quality: 6/10.
