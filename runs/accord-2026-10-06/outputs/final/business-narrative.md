# ACCORD business narrative (phase 1)

Accord Transformer & Switchgear Ltd, BSE SME 544710. Run runs/accord-2026-10-06, dated 2026-10-06. This is a phase 1 file. The valuation paragraph is left out by design, because valuation is phase 3 work.

## Business understanding narrative

Accord makes transformers at two plants in Bhiwadi, Rajasthan, and transformers were 83% of FY26 sales. A transformer changes voltage so power from a grid line, solar inverter or wind turbine can be used. The range runs from small distribution units to 20 MVA power units, including solar and wind duty units. Compact substations, which pack a transformer, switchgear and panel into one unit, were another 11% of sales. A power network has no substitute for a transformer, so a utility or solar park builder cannot skip the purchase. Buyers are state distribution companies such as UGVCL, Torrent Power, and renewable builders such as Aditya Birla Renewables. Each sale is a separate purchase order won by tender or quote, so a buyer can switch at the next order. The top five customers were 74% of FY25 sales and 48% of the nine months to December 2025. Distribution demand comes through tenders, tracked as UGVCL/GUVNL DT tenders and POs and as RDSS DT sanctions and DISCOM tenders. Renewable demand is tracked as Aditya Birla Renewables and Sterling and Wilson awards and as MNRE monthly solar and wind capacity additions. India added 44.6 GW of solar and 6 GW of wind in FY26. CRISIL puts domestic transformer sales at about Rs 33,000 Cr in FY25, growing 10 to 11% a year. IEEMA transformer production and order-book data and IEEMA price variation indices check volume and metal cost each month. A claimed Rs 1,600 Cr NHEV programme has no filed order, and NHEV and Megha Engineering EV substation awards would test it. The Emerging Moat scan found weak evidence in five categories and nothing in talent or cannibalisation barrier (Categories 21 and 22). In transformers, vendor approvals are entry tickets that peers also hold, and the company lists limited brand recognition as a weakness. Substations and panels rest on partner terms: a Lucy Electric licence to September 2027 and a Schneider certification to December 2026. EBITDA margin is about half of Danish Power's, so no line has a moat and cost advantage remains a claim.

## Orders arrived, cash and margin still unproven

Accord listed on BSE SME on 2 March 2026. It sold Rs 70 Cr of transformers and substations in FY26, down 11% from Rs 79 Cr in FY25. Profit after tax was Rs 4.5 Cr. It is about one eighth the size of Danish Power by revenue. It earns about half of Danish Power's EBITDA margin.

The claimed change has two legs. Accord wants to move from tender distribution units, where it takes the price, to solar and wind duty units on a larger plant at a higher margin. Orders support the first leg. The order book was Rs 164 Cr in January 2026 and Rs 173 Cr at 30 September 2026, after Rs 77 Cr of new orders in the half. H1 FY27 revenue was Rs 53 Cr, up 90%, but the base half was only Rs 28 Cr. Two wind orders of about Rs 17 Cr and Rs 20 Cr, before GST, make up the Rs 37 Cr Aditya Birla Renewables figure. The margin leg is not filed. Management guides 13 to 15% EBITDA against 9.9% in FY26, and H1 FY27 margin is not public yet. On filed evidence, the proof gate has not fired. Land for a new plant cost Rs 10.7 Cr in July 2026. No machinery order and no capex amount for the planned 5,000 MVA plant is filed.

Accord scores 80 of 160 on Gate 0, the backward quality screen. The core score is 65 of 100, which on its own would read GOOD. One rule caps it at AVERAGE. Operating cash flow summed over FY23 to FY26 was negative, at minus 0.18 times profit. Return on capital ran 33% to 40% from FY23 to FY25 and fell to 13% in FY26. Unspent IPO cash explains most of that fall. Without it, FY26 return on capital is about 24%.

FY26 operating cash flow was Rs 8.7 Cr against profit of Rs 4.5 Cr. Rs 13.5 Cr of it came from collecting receivables built up in FY25. Without that release, operating cash flow was minus Rs 4.8 Cr. Across FY25 and FY26, operating cash flow was Rs 0.5 Cr against profit of Rs 10.4 Cr. The Rs 22.3 Cr cash balance is 91% unspent IPO money. Cash excluding all fixed deposits is Rs 7.4 Cr, below borrowings of Rs 8.8 Cr. In the March 2026 quarter, vendor advances rose to Rs 6.2 Cr with no named supplier. Rs 6.2 Cr of new stock in transit sat beside them. Inventory days rose from 83 to 146 on a cost basis while revenue fell. One reading says cash follows growth. Receivables built in the FY24 and FY25 growth years and came back when sales fell, a pattern Danish Power and Shilchar also showed. The other reading says the business needs more cash than it makes, and the March quarter cluster is year end positioning. The H1 FY27 cash flow statement, due in November 2026, separates the two.

Management delivered what it controls in the four months after its only call. The order book it described held, and the land was bought. Two promises missed. The Rs 125 Cr MVVNL and Rs 100 Cr Torrent bids have no reported outcome. The promised note on customer concentration never came. Five more promises are partial. The guidance has two forms that do not agree. Growth of 60 to 80% means Rs 112 to 126 Cr, while the rupee band is Rs 120 to 180 Cr. Capacity is stated as 900 MVA certified and 1,200 MVA claimed. On the call, management called FY26 a year of "steady growth and improved profitability". Revenue fell 11% and profit after tax fell 24%. The Rs 1,600 Cr NHEV programme has no filed order. The concall stage grades credibility C. Verifier B argues for D, because the C grade leaves out the NHEV claim and the FY26 misstatement. The H1 FY27 results decide between them. C holds if gross margin and the deferred Rs 31 Cr order match what management said about passing on metal costs and about billing.

The promoter check returns CAUTION: three clean items, six cautions and one red. The red item is the auditor change. The auditor resigned in August 2024, and the successor firm shares its address and email. The managing director's director number was disqualified from 2016 to 2021, yet his board seat dates from 2019. No order lifting the disqualification was traced. Rs 7 Cr of IPO money moved from machinery to building seven months after listing. Promoter pay rose 78% while profit before tax fell 24%. ESOP 2026 allows 5 lakh options, with the exercise price floor at the Rs 10 face value. The two largest order filings withhold the customer name. The promoter holds 62%. Pledge was nil at the prospectus date, and no shareholding pattern after listing was in the corpus. The 3 September 2026 board outcome that named Tipco in its subject line is Accord's own document, corrected the same day. Three checks could lift CAUTION to CONCERN: the auditor change filing, the bank trail for the land payment, and the first ESOP grant price.

Danish Power and Shilchar confirm that demand is real and that oil and metal costs jumped in 2026. They contradict Accord on build time. Both peers say a new plant takes 12 to 18 months to reach production. Accord says six months minimum. Danish has its price variation clause on about 30% of its order book. Shilchar recovered 50 to 60% of the cost rise on orders in hand. Accord claims it passes on all cost changes on every order longer than three months. Peer revenue per MVA of Rs 8 to 11 lakh puts Accord's plant at Rs 74 to 131 Cr at full use. That sits below the Rs 150 to 200 Cr ceiling management states. Peers booked 38% to 51% of a year's sales in the first half. Applied to Accord's Rs 53 Cr first half, that gives Rs 104 to 138 Cr for FY27. Rs 120 Cr fits that range. Rs 180 Cr needs a first half share below every peer. Shilchar's margin excluding other income fell from 33% to 16% in a year, so peer margins are not a fixed target. Voltamp has no transcripts and adds only data sheet numbers.

The market is not the limit. Accord's served market is about Rs 19,000 Cr, and Accord holds 0.3% of it. Capacity is the limit. The certified 900 MVA at FY25 revenue per MVA gives about Rs 150 Cr a year. At the nine month FY26 rate, the same plant gives about Rs 102 Cr. Rs 120 Cr in FY27 needs 90% use of certified capacity in the second half. Growth of 30 to 50% after FY27 needs the new plant, and its cost is not filed. Management's market figure is a global number, 11.7 times the conservative India market. The run classes the runway as MASSIVE on headroom and names capacity as the binder. H1 FY27 MVA made separates the two capacity readings, and the company has not disclosed it.
