## SECTION 1: THE BUSINESS MODEL IN PLAIN ENGLISH

### 1A One-line description
It makes colour powders and pastes (organic and inorganic pigments, pigment preparations, dyes) at three Indian plants, and sells them to paint, plastics and ink makers and, in a quarter of cases, to its own parent group's overseas companies (MD&A p.17; Note 40 pp.92-93).

### 1B Money flow, per revenue stream

| Stream | Input | What the company does | What it delivers | Who pays | How they pay |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Third-party goods (558.71, derived) | Chemical intermediates and packing 361.75 purchased (Note 25 p.88); power 46.71 (Note 30 p.88) | Makes pigments at Roha, Cuddalore, Nagda; some job-worked outside (24.23, Note 30) | Pigments and preparations for coatings, plastics, printing, special uses (MD&A p.18) | Paint, plastics, ink makers; "over 100 domestic customers" (MD&A p.17) | Credit; 89.1% of receivables not due (B02, Note 10); receivable days 83.9 (B02, derived) |
| 2 Group goods (191.40) | Same plants and inputs | Ships finished pigment to group companies abroad (Singapore 137.25, Shanghai 19.30, USA 20.99) and parent 10.15 (Note 40 pp.92-93) | Finished pigment for resale by group | Group entities | Group receivables 56.56 = 31.5% of trade receivables; about 107.9 days (B02, derived) |
| 3 Services (24.83) | Staff, lab and plant time | Services rendered (nature NOT FOUND, Note 23 p.87) | Not disclosed | Not disclosed | Not disclosed |
| 4 Other operating (5.69) | Export scheme, scrap, indenting | Incidental | Export incentives 3.78, commission 0.47, scrap 1.44 (Note 23 p.87) | Government and group | Receivable (incentives 2.00, Note on other assets) |

Parallel flow in the other direction: group sells to the company. Purchases of 159.15 = 32.0% of the 497.68 total (361.75 raw materials + 135.93 traded goods), largest being HLD1 99.62 and Gujarat MFG 40.95 (Note 40 p.93; Notes 25, 17 summary p.74, derived). Traded goods are bought and resold, so part of revenue is a trading margin.

### 1E Chai-stall uncle version
Think of a tea-powder blender with three small factories. A quarter of what it sells goes to the owner's own cousins' shops abroad. A third of what it buys comes from the same family. The owner decides where the big orders go. Outside customers buy about the same amount each year, Rs 589 Cr. The family cousins' orders went down, so the total fell. Cash in the till is large (Rs 235 Cr), yet its own suppliers are paid late.
