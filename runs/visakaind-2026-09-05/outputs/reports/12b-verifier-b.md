## PART 1: INDEPENDENT RED-FLAG LIST (from the raw transcripts)

### 1A. Main company (Visaka)

**V1. ATUM: a rolling deferral repeated across four consecutive calls.**
May-2022: "we closed about a volume of around 6 megawatts... This will reach
full capacity this year" (Q4 FY22, Vamsi Krishna, p.4), against a stated 30 MW
capacity. Aug-2022: walked back to "This year, we are trying to do almost
about 15 to 20 megawatts" (Q1 FY23, Vamsi Krishna, p.6). Nov-2022: asked
whether the Rs 60-70 crore full-year figure holds, "We may see a slight
decline in that... we have integrated around 50 Crores earlier" (Q2 FY23,
Vamsi Krishna, p.9). Aug-2023: "a good idea for how the ATUM is driving should
come within the next maybe 10 to 12 months to be realistic" (Q1 FY24, Vamsi
Krishna, p.9) — after Nov-2022 had already promised "another six months to a
year" (Q2 FY23, p.13). Four calls, four horizons, each one further out.

**V2. ATUM current-period revenue withheld against direct, repeated requests.**
Nov-2022, Dhananjay Mishra asks the contribution for the quarter; the answer
contains no number: "this quarter has been little slow in terms of overall
product outlay... Our booking order is quite strong so in that point of view
we are okay" (Q2 FY23, Vamsi Krishna, p.9). Divyanshi Maghdani, same call:
"we have been hearing since the past eight concalls... can we please get the
revenues or order book for it and the margins" (Q2 FY23, p.12). Aug-2023 gives
only a hypothetical: "if we do about 30 megawatt, we would be seeing anywhere
between Rs. 85 crores to Rs. 90 crores" (Q1 FY24, Vamsi Krishna, p.14).

**V3. Segment-margin disclosure requested and deferred in at least three
separate calls.** May-2022, Deep Gandhi asks for the board division's FY22
EBITDA margin and volume: "I do not have the detail with me, my team will
share that with you offline" (Q4 FY22, Vamsi Krishna, p.8). Aug-2022, the same
analyst asks for the quarter's segment EBITDA margin: "last quarter I will be
able to share with you offline" (Q1 FY23, Vamsi Krishna, p.11). Aug-2022,
Ankit asks for a Vnext / ATUM / asbestos / yarn breakup: "we are actually
working on that space, we will let at the right time... Point noted" (Q1 FY23,
Vamsi Krishna, p.8). Nov-2022, Rajat Setiya asks whether segment margins can
be reported: "We will share once it works out for us... Anyhow we can tell you
on one-on-one basis" (Q2 FY23, Shafiulla, p.16). Four requests, three calls,
four deferrals, zero disclosure.

**V4. Vnext margin: a gross-to-EBITDA basis conflation left uncorrected.**
Aug-2023, Dhananjay Mishra: "What kind of margin we are doing in Vnext."
Vamsi Krishna: "Margins have grown significantly. We can look at upwards of
15% gross." Dhananjay: "15% EBITDA margin on a yearly basis?" Vamsi Krishna:
"Yes." (Q1 FY24, p.8). Management stated a gross margin and then affirmed it
as an EBITDA margin two lines later. Later in the same call, to a different
analyst: "margins go up right from 8% to 10%, 12%, and now we are doing an
upwards of 14%. We are around the 15% mark" (Q1 FY24, Jasdeep Walia exchange,
p.14) — basis again unstated.

**V5. Same-call contradiction: 43% volume growth against 5-10% revenue growth.**
Opening remarks: "the Vnext has seen a tremendous growth in terms of volume of
almost 43% over the past year" (Q1 FY24, Vamsi Krishna, p.4). Q&A, same call:
"the growth rate from last year to this year has been around 5% to 10% for the
first quarter because we have seen an overall slowdown" (Q1 FY24, p.8). If both
describe the same period, realisation fell roughly 25-30%. Nobody reconciled
them. The same call also gives Vnext utilisation twice and differently: "about
85%" (p.4) and "around 88% to 90% in the first quarter" (p.6).

**V6. The Vnext growth track record was retroactively revised downward.**
Nov-2022: "the confidence comes from actually achieving it. Year-on-year we are
experiencing minimum of 35% plus in terms of growth" (Q2 FY23, Vamsi Krishna,
p.16). Aug-2023: "year-on-year growth we are looking at around 25% upwards.
That's the track that we have been having for over the last 2 to 3 years also"
(Q1 FY24, p.8). The claimed history changed, not just the forecast.

**V7. The FY30 target and the growth guidance are arithmetically incompatible,
and management conceded it without revising either.** Aug-2023: Vnext "would
likely touch around Rs. 450 crores by the end of this year 2024... targeting a
turnover of Rs. 1,000 crores by the year 2030" (Q1 FY24, p.5) — about 14% CAGR
— alongside "25% growth year on year is what we can expect" (p.8). Madhur
Rathi put the contradiction on the record: "why are we only guiding for
doubling our revenue in the next 5 to 7 years? Because, it will be a lower
target than what the growth we are seeing" (p.9). Management: "The projections
are based on various market conditions", then agreed the Rs 1,000 crore figure
"will be kind of a conservative number" — "Sure." (p.10). Both numbers stayed
in the deck. A downstream model can pick either and land 2x apart.

**V8. Volunteered negative on the profit engine, buried in the opening script.**
"The capacity utilization for Q1 FY24 stood at more than 100% for our roofing
businesses... we are expecting to do good volumes for the rest of the year,
though rural income is down at the moment" (Q1 FY24, Vamsi Krishna, p.4).
Same call, same segment: building products EBIT Rs 27 crore against Rs 53 crore
a year earlier (p.6). Volume at maximum, profit halved, end-market income
falling. That triple is the cleanest price-taker evidence in the corpus. No
analyst picked it up.

**V9. A structural cost reset classified as a one-off, twice in one call.**
"we are cognizant of the fact that margins have suffered due to raw material
cost but this is a one-off phenomenon" (Q2 FY23, Vamsi Krishna, p.5); "I would
say this is a one-off situation in terms of unstable high input cost that has
been taken into account for this quarter" (Q2 FY23, p.6). Nine months later the
same management quantified it as permanent-looking: "the input costs have taken
almost a 25% upward trend... And the offset that we could do in selling price
was hardly about 2%. It's almost 10x" (Q1 FY24, p.9). When the analyst supplied
the words "the new normal", management declined them and reverted to "as the
war situation settles down, we will see a normalization of the prices" (Q1 FY24,
p.11). Four years later the peer still calls fibre cost inflationary
(BirlaNu Q4 FY26, Akshat Seth, p.9-10).

**V10. The load-bearing 30% input-cost figure was destabilised by management in
the very next answer.** Nov-2022: "the input cost increase just on the asbestos
front has been close to 30% increase in terms of pricing" (Q2 FY23, p.6).
Dhananjay Mishra then asks whether the 12% and 30% are YoY or sequential.
"It is from Q1 to Q2." (p.8). Asked to confirm a 30% one-quarter jump, the
answer dissolves: "Not just in asbestos what I have indicated is in terms of
our (inaudible)... due to that you are able to see a sudden push also because
of volumes being low the impact is seen a little higher in terms of actual
numbers" (p.8). The figure was given, re-based, then partly retracted.

**V11. A full pass-through claim against a 4.4% segment EBIT.** "We have taken
almost the same amount if not more in terms of the benefit... almost 11% to 12%
positive" (Q2 FY23, Vamsi Krishna, p.6), against Subham Agarwal's unchallenged
"In this division building product we did EBIT margin of 4.4%" (p.6).

**V12. A distribution-growth claim falsified by the company's own later number.**
Nov-2022: "We have touched 2500 selling points like I mentioned we see that
number increase or double, triple every year and every half year that we go in
terms of distribution network" (Q2 FY23, Vamsi Krishna, p.17). Aug-2023: "for
the Vnext space, we have reached about 3,500 selling points" (Q1 FY24, p.14).
About +40% in fifteen months, against a claim of doubling or tripling every
half year. The claim was not merely optimistic; the company's own next data
point refutes it by roughly an order of magnitude.

**V13. Market share: 40% from management, 32% from an analyst, never reconciled.**
May-2022: "I can tell you that we are almost about 40% of the current market
share" (Q4 FY22, Vamsi Krishna, p.8). Aug-2023, Jasdeep Walia: "I understand
you have around 32% market share in this business." Management neither confirms
nor corrects, answering only "the second and third will be much behind us"

**V14. Promoter pledge: a large number, surfaced by a retail investor, then

"5.8% on the one promoter holding and 57% of another promoter holding is still

**V15. A promoter-relationship question answered with a self-contradiction.**
Aug-2022, Ankit: "there is a shareholder Vigilant Security Services, so wanted

**V16. Demerger: identical question, identical non-answer, twelve months apart,

Aug-2023: "We have been discussing that topic internally as well. We will get

**V17. Defensiveness under a direct strategy challenge.** Shambhavi: "none of

**V20. The FX answer: unit-ambiguous exposure, and the policy question unanswered.**
Sunny Wadhwa asks quarterly FX exposure and whether a hedging policy exists.
Shafiulla: "Almost 40 to 45 lakhs exposure is there towards import and it is

lakh of quarterly import exposure is not credible for a company whose imported
fibre cost had just moved 25-30% on a Rs 1,400 crore revenue base; the unit is
never stated. Asked again, "What is the FX hedging policy as on date?", the CFO
answers with a sensitivity, not a policy: "It will have impact of 2% to 3% on the

stated ">50%" goal, and against the 60:40 claim in the same breath.

**V22. Yarn: a flat denial of a trend the same call's own table shows.** Madhur

Vamsi Krishna: "I don't agree that the margins have been decreasing constantly.
It has been only this quarter" (Q1 FY24, p.10). The same call's segment numbers:
yarn revenue Rs 62 crore against Rs 88 crore, yarn EBIT Rs 8 crore against

consecutive declining prints, denied. Separately, Nov-2022: "we are consistently
selling higher than what we are producing so are we engaging in trading
activity" — "We are not doing any trading" with no alternative explanation for
the gap (Q2 FY23, p.8).

**V23. Export share: 25-30% asserted, 20-21% put on the record, unreconciled.**
"across all the four divisions, we do about 25% to 30% of our overall output"

cater to the exports market" (Q1 FY24, p.6). Anika Mittal, same call: "our
exports for board division have been 20% or 21 odd percent in the last 3 years?"
— answered with a discussion of country margins, never the number (p.6).

**V24. Headline framing leads with the flattering basis.** "The EBITDA including

gain are delivered in one breath, and "the previous quarter" labels the
year-ago comparator while the actual previous quarter is named second.

### 1B. Peer statements bearing on the main company (BirlaNu)

**P1. The peer puts long-run roofing demand growth at 2-3%; Visaka said 5-10%.**
"demand itself is by and large flat or if you take long-term averages, is
growing by 2% to 3%" (BirlaNu Q2 FY26, Akshat Seth, p.17). Visaka: "anywhere
between 5% and 10% in terms of the overall market and industry is what we can
observe every year" (Q2 FY23, p.9), later revised to "hardly maybe around 3% to
5%... in good times" (Q1 FY24, p.12). The peer's number sits below even
Visaka's revised floor and at a third of its original one.

**P2. The peer's four-year record contradicts the "one-off" framing.** Fibre and
cement input pressure is named as live in Aug-2025 (p.3), Nov-2025 (p.17) and
May-2026 (p.9-10), and roofing price erosion is called structural: "This is now
the second consecutive year where there is a year-on-year price drop... the
route to improve profitability has to come from better pricing in the market"
(BirlaNu Q1 FY26, Akshat Seth, p.20).

**P3. The self-declared roofing price leader claims a 400-600 bps profitability
lead over the whole competitor set.** "We continue to be at least 400 to 600
basis points better on profitability compared to any of our competitors"
(BirlaNu Q1 FY26, Akshat Seth, p.11). Visaka sits inside the cohort that
statement describes. This is the single most directly valuation-relevant peer
number in the four transcripts.

**P4. Boards industry growth is 10-14%; Visaka guided Vnext at 25-30% for years.**
"[the Board segment is] roughly anywhere between Rs. 1,500 crores to Rs. 2,000
crores industry segment but growing at close to 10% to 14% growth rate
year-on-year" (BirlaNu Q1 FY27, Akshat Seth, p.12).

**P5. The peer is adding boards capacity sized against Visaka's entire Vnext base.**
Two greenfield boards plants (Nellore, then Hyderabad/Telangana at Rs 167 crore):
"it will inject close to a revenue upside of about INR 300 crores to INR 350
crores and hopefully an EBITDA uplift north of INR 75 crores to INR 85 crores"
(BirlaNu Q1 FY27, Akshat Seth, p.12-13). Visaka's whole Vnext line was guided at
about Rs 450 crore for FY24. Visaka's own posture on competition was
dismissive: "Nothing to worry about particularly" (Q1 FY23, p.11) and "Not new
competitors; the existing competitors themselves are becoming more active"
(Q1 FY24, p.15).

**P6. The peer's record roofing quarter rests on an exogenous, reversible driver.**
"in 17%, the volume growth is nearly 10%. What also helped was our closest
competition has a substitute product, the steel sheets were at an elevated
price point" (BirlaNu Q1 FY27, Akshat Seth, p.13).

**P7. Time-currency.** The main-company record ends Aug-2023; the peer record
runs to Aug-2026. Every forward Visaka statement in the corpus is now three
years past its test date and untested by any later management commentary.

misses are real and specific, not because the reports are unsound.
