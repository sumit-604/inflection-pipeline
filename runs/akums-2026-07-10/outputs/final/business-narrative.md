India's largest CDMO priced above transition value

Akums is India's largest domestic pharmaceutical CDMO, making finished dosage forms under contract for branded drug companies, with contract manufacturing at 80% of sales and smaller domestic branded, API, export and trade generics lines around it. FY26 revenue was Rs 4,359 Cr, EBITDA Rs 522 Cr at a 12% margin, and reported PAT Rs 256 Cr on diluted EPS of Rs 16.67. The shares trade at Rs 702, about 42 times earnings. The company listed in August 2024 and the promoter family holds 75.26%.

Two engines are meant to move the mix up from thin CDMO margins. An exclusive European oral liquid supply contract runs to December 2032, worth about EUR 35m a year from FY28 and EUR 200m in total, with a EUR 100m advance already received. A Zambian government procurement JV adds USD 50m of India sourced supply across FY27 and FY28. CDMO segment margin rose from 10.4% to 14.4% across FY26 quarters. Both revenue engines are back loaded to FY28 and FY29 and neither has booked commercial revenue yet.

Gate 0 scored 79 of 160, an AVERAGE reading capped by a FY24 loss year, and combined with an Emerging Moat score of 26.3 it reads as a TURNAROUND setup rather than a broken business. Reported cash conversion looks strong at 4.61 times PAT but is flattered by a Rs 1,032 Cr customer advance equal to 23.7% of revenue. Stripping that advance, organic cash conversion is about 0.99 times PAT, adequate but not firing. ICRA rates the group AA stable with a strong liquidity call, net cash, and total debt at 0.2 times operating profit, so the cash question is growth induced, not structural.

Reported PAT fell 25.4% even as underlying operating PBT grew 22.4%, the gap driven by an effective tax rate of 33% against a 25.17% statutory rate on unrecognised deferred tax at loss making subsidiaries. Management headlined "Adjusted PAT +27.3%" with no reconciliation to the GAAP decline, and the annual report carries internal inconsistencies including an EBITDA margin quoted at 43% against the correct 12%. Credibility grades C. Management delivered the CDMO margin rise and the trade generics turnaround it promised, missed domestic branded formulation growth at 2.9% against a mid teens guide, and evaded questions on cash deployment and Schedule M enforcement across three consecutive calls.

Governance is the binding concern, and most of it only surfaced with the annual report. An Income Tax search in January 2025 carries a formal auditor Emphasis of Matter and a CARO fraud qualification at three group entities. The parent holds a Rs 331 Cr unsecured deposit in a subsidiary whose net worth is minus Rs 654 Cr, with no disclosed impairment test. A Rs 134 Cr block period tax demand is company disclosed from May 2026 with entity splits, but does not appear in the FY26 annual report, which states no demand was raised as of the reporting date and carries an auditor Emphasis of Matter instead. Set against this: zero promoter pledge, a credentialed independent board, DII ownership at 14.3%, and a blue chip anchor book. The most informed prior holder, Quadria Capital, fully exited at a 3 times return, and the external CEO for the CDMO business resigned after 11 months.

Peers confirm the API price cycle and a genuine sector capex wave, but they do not confirm Akums's headline CDMO surge. Cohance reported early single digit underlying CDMO growth for FY26, directly against the 25% variance Akums cited and could not fully explain. Windlas at 18 to 23% and Innovacap at 6 to 10% corroborate share shift only at lower magnitude. Market runway is GOOD, with a conservative domestic CDMO TAM of Rs 13,880 Cr, a three year serviceable opportunity of Rs 5,396 Cr, and revenue headroom of 3.34 times.

The earned destination multiple lands at 21.5 to 24.5 times on the additive track and 18.5 to 22.0 times on the required return track, a 12% divergence, with the more conservative required return track governing entry. Against a current 42.1 times, the Hurdle Ratio is 0.91 at base and 1.02 on the grade C capped bull case, both a STOP. Three year base fair value is Rs 635 on the additive track and Rs 559 on the required return track, both below the current price, and probability weighted expected return is minus 8.9% a year. The re-engagement zone is Rs 254 to 286 with a margin of safety price of Rs 229.

The decision is AVOID, over determined by an AVERAGE Gate 0, a promoter CONCERN, a Hurdle STOP, and an upside to downside ratio of 0.21. The devil's advocate review found AVOID survives on growth, moat, management and valuation, and reads it as understated rather than harsh. The unnamed European counterparty is a single point of failure, spanning 23.7% of revenue through the Rs 1,032 Cr advance, the entire cash quality story, and two of the moat legs. A drop into Rs 254 to 286 is only an entry if the latest quarter shows no thesis broken trigger fired.
