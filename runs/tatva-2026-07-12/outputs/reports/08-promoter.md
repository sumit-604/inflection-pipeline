### 3B. Capital allocation behaviour
**Flaggable finding.** FY2024-25 was a year of severe profit compression: standalone PAT fell
98.9%, from ₹293.48 million (FY24) to ₹3.27 million (FY25); basic/diluted EPS fell from ₹12.82 to
₹0.14 (−98.91%, Note 34); EBITDA (excl. other income) fell 60.5%. The Board proportionately cut the
recommended final dividend from ₹2/share (FY24) to ₹1/share (FY25) (Board's Report Item 4). Against
this backdrop, **remuneration to all three promoter-executive directors rose 27.89% year-on-year**
(each drawing ₹16,503,252 plus ₹621,792 PF contribution, total ₹17,125,044 in FY25 vs ~₹13.39
million each in FY24 employee-benefit-expense terms per Note 42), while the average salary increase
for non-managerial employees was only 3.36% (Annexure-D to Board's Report, p.63). The average
increase for managerial personnel overall was 24.12%. This is a direct instance of the "salary
rising through performance decline" pattern the framework flags under capital-allocation red flags,
even though the dividend was cut in parallel (so it is not "cash hoarding with high promoter
salary" — the two behaviours point in different directions and should both be weighed). No buyback,
no bonus shares, no sweat equity, and no ESOP scheme were issued/exist during the year. The FY24
QIP (₹2,000 million at ₹1,630/share, 29 Aug 2023) was fully utilised per the stated objects with no
diversion (Note 49) — no promoter warrants issued or lapsed. (Source: AR Board's Report p.50-51;
