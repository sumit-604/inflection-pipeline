### 1. 🔴 NEW — The ₹250 Lakh GAAT "professional fee" to Dr. Manika Khanna traces back to a FY24 capital
advance, not a fresh FY25 cash decision (Note 13, Note 42(b)/(c) Standalone p.~92/102–103; Note 13, Note
43(b)/(c) Consolidated p.~144/158–159)
Pass 1 (Rank 4) flagged the ₹250.00L "Professional Fees for Development of GAAT" paid to the Chairperson/MD
in FY25 as a new-in-year related-party payment capitalised into Intangible Assets Under Development. Re-reading
Note 13 (Other Assets) shows a line "Capital Advances to Related Parties" of exactly ₹250.00L, Non-Current, at
31 March 2024 — reducing to NIL at 31 March 2025. Cross-referencing Note 42(b) (Standalone RPT transactions):
Dr. Manika Khanna received an "Advance to Supplier" of ₹250.00L in FY24 (prior year column), and Note 42(c)
(balances outstanding) confirms a "Capital Advance" of ₹250.00L owed by Dr. Manika Khanna to the Company at
FY24 year-end, falling to NIL at FY25 year-end — the exact amount and exact counterparty as the FY25 "GAAT
Professional Fees." This pattern is identical in the Consolidated notes (Note 13, Note 43(b)/(c), p.~144/158).
The precise amount match (₹250.00L in, ₹250.00L settled) across two consecutive years' notes indicates the
FY25 GAAT capitalisation was not a new decision but the retrospective RE-CHARACTERISATION of an already-
outstanding FY24 capital advance to the Chairperson into a "professional fee," which was then capitalised as
an intangible asset. This is materially different from — and more concerning than — Pass 1's framing of a
single-year cash outflow: it shows a two-year-old related-party funding arrangement being converted into a
capitalised "development cost" with no contemporaneous FY24 disclosure of what the ₹250L capital advance was
actually for. 🔴 Strengthens Pass 1 Rank 4 substantially; should be treated as pre-planned related-party fund
transfer engineering ahead of the capitalisation and the IPO, not a one-off FY25 event.

### 2. 🔴 NEW — Consolidated Trade Payables ageing schedule (Note 22, Consolidated, p.~147) does not
reconcile to its own summary table/balance sheet figure — a SECOND uncaught ageing-note error, mirroring the
standalone Note 15 receivables mismatch already flagged in Pass 1
Note 22 (Consolidated) summary table states Total Trade Payables at 31 March 2024 = ₹76.75L (matching the
consolidated balance sheet). The Ageing for Trade Payables as at 31 March 2024 sub-table immediately below it,
however, totals ₹101.24L ("Others" bucket, Less than 1 Year). The gap is ₹24.49L. This ₹24.49L is not a random
figure: standalone Note 42(c) shows a FY24 balance of "Trade Payable — ₹24.49L" owed by the Holding Company to
its subsidiary Gaudium International Private Limited — an intercompany balance that should be ELIMINATED on
consolidation (101.24 − 24.49 = 76.75, an exact reconciliation). This strongly indicates the consolidated
ageing sub-schedule was populated from pre-elimination (standalone-plus-subsidiary) figures while the
headline consolidated balance-sheet total was correctly eliminated — the second instance across the audited
notes (after standalone Note 15's FY24 receivables ageing mismatch, Pass 1 Rank 5) of an ageing sub-schedule
failing to reconcile to its own parent note. This is now a PATTERN, not an isolated drafting slip: two
independent ageing tables in two different notes, in two different financial statement sets (standalone and
consolidated), both fail internal reconciliation in the same reporting year — a materiality-adjacent
audit-quality signal for the incoming (first-year) auditor.

### 3. 🟡 NEW — Goodwill amount is stated inconsistently between notes, in BOTH Standalone and Consolidated
sets (Note 7 vs Note 47 Standalone p.~90/107; Note 7 vs Note 48 Consolidated p.~145/~160)
Note 7 (Goodwill movement table) states Goodwill of ₹13.82L, static across FY23/FY24/FY25, in both the
Standalone and Consolidated financial statements. Note 47 (Standalone) / Note 48 (Consolidated) — the Business
Combination narrative note describing the same M/s Gaudium Bawa IVF acquisition — states: "resulted in a
goodwill of ₹13.08 Lakhs for the Company." The ₹0.74L discrepancy (₹13.82L vs ₹13.08L) is immaterial in
absolute quantum but appears identically in BOTH the standalone and consolidated note sets, indicating a
systemic (not one-off) inconsistency between the asset-movement note and its own narrative note for the same
underlying transaction — reinforcing the internal-consistency concerns already raised in Pass 1 for Note 15
(receivables) and Note 44/48 (leverage narrative).

### 4. 🟡 NEW — Leverage deterioration is a THREE-YEAR swing from net cash to net debt, not framed as such by
management (Note 44 Standalone p.~104; Note 45 Consolidated p.~156)
Pass 1 (Rank finding under Note 44 discussion) captured the FY24→FY25 net-debt deterioration (0.22x→0.31x
standalone; 0.21x→0.29x consolidated). Re-reading Note 44/45 in full reveals an explicit disclosure of the
1 April 2023 opening position that was not previously extracted: "As of April 1, 2023, the Company's total
borrowings including lease liabilities amounted to ₹1,154.50 Lakhs [Consolidated: ₹1,154.51L], while cash and
cash equivalents stood at ₹1,478.63 Lakhs [Consolidated: ₹1,505.71L], resulting in a net NEGATIVE debt position
of ₹324.13 Lakhs [Consolidated: ₹351.20L]." This means the company held NET CASH (negative net debt) at the
Ind AS transition date, and the subsequent trajectory is: FY23 net cash −₹324.13L (standalone) → FY24 net debt
₹575.62L → FY25 net debt ₹1,388.07L — a full swing of ~₹1,712L over the two-year comparative window, not a
one-year event as Pass 1's framing (drawn from the FY24→FY25 ratio movement alone) implied. This is a longer
and larger deleveraging-to-releveraging story than previously captured, and coincides with the same period as
the non-core investments (Notes 10–11) and receivables build-up (Note 15).

### 5. 🟡 NEW — The KMP-linked advance flagged generically in Note 13 is specifically identified, by
cross-reference only, as an advance to the CFO (Note 13 footnote vs Note 42(c)/43(c) balance table, Standalone
p.~92 & Consolidated p.~144, cross-ref p.~103/159)
Note 13's footnote states: "# Includes Closing balance of Advance given to KMP of Rs. 4.75 Lakh as at 31st
March 2025," embedded inside the generic "Advances to Employees" line (₹13.84L total). Cross-referencing Note
42(c) (Standalone) / Note 43(c) (Consolidated) identifies the counterparty by name: Mr. Rakesh Kumar Sharma
(CFO, appointed 3 Oct 2024) — "Advance ₹4.75L" balance outstanding at FY25 year-end, with zero comparative in
FY24 (consistent with his October 2024 appointment). This is a disclosure-location observation: the RPT nature
and identity of this advance is only discoverable by cross-referencing two separate notes; Note 13 itself does
not name the KMP or the nature of the advance beyond a generic parenthetical. Immaterial in quantum but
relevant for RPT completeness assessment given the other, larger RPT items already flagged in Pass 1.

### 6. 🟡 NEW — CONSOLIDATED-ONLY: a separate, smaller intangible-under-development project (ERP Software,
₹13.42L) was carried for multiple years and then WRITTEN OFF in FY25 for delay — a useful contrast to the
continued GAAT capitalisation (Note 9, Consolidated, p.~144)
The Consolidated Note 9 ageing tables show a "Projects in progress" balance of ₹13.42L present at both 1 April
2023 (aged 2–3 years even at opening, implying origination in FY21-22 or earlier) and 31 March 2024 (aged
1–2 years), with a footnote: "**Intangible Assets under development is ERP Software. Asset has been
derecognised in current year due to delay in implementation." This item does NOT appear in the Standalone
Note 9 (it is subsidiary-level only) and was not captured in Pass 1, which reviewed Standalone Note 9 in depth
but did not separately extract the Consolidated Note 9 delta. This establishes that the Group DOES have a
precedent of writing off stalled/delayed capitalised development projects when they fail to progress — which
sharpens the Pass 1 Rank 4 concern about GAAT (₹738.32L, continuing to be capitalised, no comparable
milestone-failure or feasibility-gate disclosure) by showing the accounting policy is capable of being applied
conservatively for one project (ERP software, non-related-party) while a much larger, related-party-linked
project (GAAT) shows no equivalent scrutiny trail in the notes.

### 9. 🟡 NEW — Borrowing facility mechanics: lender named, term loan final maturity disclosed, and the
Overdraft Facility is additionally secured by a floating charge over Trade Receivables (Note 18 Standalone
p.~97; Note 18 Consolidated p.~143)
Pass 1 flagged that Term Loan/Overdraft/Drop Down Facility borrowings are secured by promoter-directors'
personal properties but did not extract two additional mechanical details present in the note's narrative
paragraphs: (i) all three facilities are from a single named lender, HDFC Bank; (ii) the Term Loan is
repayable in EMIs through 07 May 2032 (a specific final maturity date, not previously captured); and (iii) the
Overdraft Facility (₹206.91L, current, FY25) is "secured by a floating charge against the Receivables" — in
addition to the directors' personal-property mortgage. This means the trade receivables book, whose collection
quality is independently and materially deteriorating per Note 15 (Pass 1 Rank 3), is itself pledged as bank
collateral for working-capital debt — a funding-risk linkage between the receivables quality concern and the
company's short-term credit facility that was not previously connected in Pass 1.
