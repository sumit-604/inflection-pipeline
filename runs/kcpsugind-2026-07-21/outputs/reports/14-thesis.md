## NARRATIVE (plain English)

KCP Sugar is not really a sugar company you buy for sugar. The sugar mill loses money and has lost more each year for three years; the cash flow just turned negative for the first time, burning about Rs 30 Cr a year. What sits inside the same listed shell is a Rs 332 Cr pile of shares and mutual funds, plus a genuinely good little engineering business (Eimco) that just won a Rs 257 Cr order. After the operator ruled that Eimco should be valued at 15x — an ordinary engineering multiple, well under the 25x cap for the sector — the parts add up to about Rs 33.88 a share, against a price of Rs 21.71. So on paper there is now a real gap: the market is paying about 36% below the re-rated asset value. But that gap only turns into a 25% annual return if you buy the discount, not the current price. From Rs 21.71 the base case compounds to just +16% over three years, because you are waiting for an asset value to be recognised, not for earnings to grow. Only the bull case clears 25%. That is why the answer is watchlist and buy-on-dips, not buy today. The place to act is roughly Rs 15.42 to Rs 17.35, and even there only a small starter, only if the cash isn't visibly leaking, the Eimco order has actually been filed, and Eimco's margin still supports the 15x. The two things that can quietly ruin this are the same book that makes it attractive being spent to plug sugar losses before the discount ever closes, and the Rs 257 Cr order — the reason for the re-rating — never showing up in a filing.
