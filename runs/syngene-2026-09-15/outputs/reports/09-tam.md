## SECTION 1: MARKET DEFINITION

### 1A. Precise boundaries

**Product scope (two engines, per COMPANY MEMORY first verification
priority):**
- Engine 1 — Research Services / discovery CRO: discovery chemistry,
  discovery biology and biotherapeutics, dedicated R&D centres (BMS,
  Amgen, Baxter), translational and clinical research (T&CR). Maps to the
  industry categories "Pre-Clinical CRO" (discovery) plus a small slice
  of "Clinical CRO" (T&CR only, not full multi-site trial management).
- Engine 2 — CDMO: small-molecule development and commercial API
  manufacturing (Mangalore), large-molecule biologics development and
  manufacturing (Unit 3 Bengaluru, Bayview Maryland), new modalities
  (ADC bioconjugation, peptides, oligonucleotides). Maps to "Small
  Molecule CDMO" + "Large Molecule CDMO."

**Geographic scope:** delivery from India (Bengaluru, Mangalore,
Hyderabad) plus one small US site (Bayview, Maryland, acquired from
Emergent BioSolutions Mar-2025). Customers are global (pharma, biotech,
animal health, nutrition, agrochemical, consumer goods innovators
headquartered mostly in the US/EU). This is an export-of-services /
inward-investment model, not a domestic-consumption model.

**Customer scope:** innovator pharma, biotech, animal health, nutrition,
agrochemical and consumer-goods companies that outsource R&D and/or
manufacturing. Excludes: generic API/formulation manufacturers serving
domestic Indian or generic-export markets (a much larger, differently
priced and differently regulated market that Syngene does not compete
in), and companies relying purely on in-house/captive R&D.

**Channel scope:** direct, long-term contracted relationships (dedicated
centres, FTE/FFS, milestone, batch/volume-based manufacturing contracts).
No distributor/channel-partner layer.

**Price segment:** premium/specialised technical service pricing
(regulatory-compliant, USFDA/EMA/UK-VMD/PMDA/Health Canada-approved
facilities), not the lowest-cost/commodity CMO tier.

**Explicit inclusions:** pre-clinical/discovery CRO services; dedicated
R&D centres; T&CR (small slice of clinical CRO); small-molecule CDMO
(development + manufacturing); large-molecule/biologics CDMO
(development + manufacturing); new-modality CDMO (ADC, peptides,
oligonucleotides).

**Explicit exclusions:** full-scope multi-site clinical trial management
(Phase 2/3 CRO at scale); generic API/formulation contract manufacturing
for off-patent, price-competitive markets; cell & gene therapy CDMO
(not a demonstrated Syngene capability); vaccines CDMO.

A wrong definition would make every downstream number useless here in a
specific way: the market-research industry publishes a "$17-25bn India
CDMO market" figure that mixes in generic API/formulation manufacturing
(Divi's, Laurus-type businesses) with the NCE-innovator CRDMO space
Syngene actually competes in. Section 2 uses the narrower, correctly
scoped figure and flags the broader one as a scope mismatch, not a
higher/lower estimate of the same thing.

### 1B. Management's own TAM claim

Management (via the Dec-2025 investor presentation, slide "Global CRDMO
market expected to grow at ~9% CAGR," sourced to Evaluate Pharma / Frost
& Sullivan / IMAP, and repeated with near-identical figures in the AR26
Business Review, p.181-190) shows:

- Overall global CRDMO market: USD 213bn (2024) -> USD 330bn (2029F),
  ~9% CAGR (deck p.7; AR26 components sum to the same number, see Section
  2 Method 1).
- Component breakdown: Pre-Clinical CRO USD 59bn -> 99bn (10% CAGR);
  Clinical CRO USD 26bn -> 41bn (11% CAGR, ~30% of the overall CRDMO
  market); Overall CDMO (small + large molecule) USD 129bn -> 190bn (8%
  CAGR).
- AR26 restates the CRO line as "USD 84.3bn (29% share of pharmaceutical
  R&D spend) in 2024 to USD 139.8bn (42% share) by 2029" and CDMO as
  "USD 128.8bn (33% share of pharmaceutical manufacturing) in 2024 to
  USD 190.2bn (39% share) by 2029" (AR26 p.28, "Operating Environment").
  These are the same underlying Frost & Sullivan figures expressed as a
  rising outsourcing-penetration rate on total pharma R&D/manufacturing
  spend, not an independent estimate (see Method 2).
- India-specific: management's Dec-2025 deck gives only an **Indian
  Clinical CRO** market figure — USD 1.9bn (2024) -> USD 3.4bn (2028),
  16% CAGR, "outpacing the global rate of 11%" (deck p.9) — a narrow
  slice of Engine 1 only. No India figure is shown for discovery CRO or
  for CDMO.

**Definition:** specific and dated (three-segment breakdown, named
source, base and forecast years stated).

**Credibility read: SPECIFIC** on definition and sourcing. But the
material finding is what management does **not** show: at no point in
the AR or the three investor decks read for this run does management
narrow the USD 213bn/330bn global figure down to what Syngene, an
India-plus-one-US-site delivery model with ~400 active clients, can
actually address. The only India-specific figure shown (Clinical CRO,
$1.9-3.4bn) covers a segment that is a minor part of Syngene's own
business. This is the textbook "TAM shown, SAM never shown" pattern the
framework treats as dishonest-adjacent — held here for the Section 2
triangulation and revisited in Section 5.

## SECTION 2: TAM ESTIMATION, MULTIPLE METHODS

### Method 1 — Top-down (global, Frost & Sullivan / Evaluate Pharma / IMAP
via Syngene's own AR26 and Dec-2025 deck)

Core scope (Engine 1 discovery/pre-clinical CRO + Engine 2 CDMO,
excluding full Clinical CRO since Syngene's T&CR footprint is a small
slice of that $26-41bn line, not the whole thing):

- 2024: USD 59bn (Pre-Clinical CRO) + USD 129bn (CDMO) = **USD 188bn**
  = Rs 18,04,800 cr (at Rs 96/USD).
- 2029F: USD 99bn + USD 190bn = **USD 289bn** = Rs 27,74,400 cr.
- Implied CAGR: (289/188)^(1/5)-1 ≈ **9.0%**.

Full scope (management's own headline, including Clinical CRO):

- 2024: **USD 213bn** = Rs 20,44,800 cr. 2029F: **USD 330bn** =
  Rs 31,68,000 cr. ~9% CAGR.

Source: 2025-12-17 investor presentation p.7 ("Global CRDMO market
expected to grow at ~9% CAGR"); cross-checked against AR26 p.185-190
("CRO market... USD ~84bn... to ~USD 140bn," "CDMO market... USD ~130bn
... to ~USD 190bn"). Both documents attribute the data to Frost &
Sullivan (AR) / Evaluate Pharma, Frost & Sullivan, IMAP (deck).
Confidence: **M** (single ultimate data provider behind both
publications; broad global scope, not company-specific). Staleness: not
stale — published with AR26 and the Dec-2025 deck, both within the
2-year window, though the underlying base year (2024) is itself the most
recent full year the provider had at publication, which is normal for
this kind of industry report.

### Method 2 — Bottom-up (outsourcing penetration on total pharma spend)

Addressable unit: one dollar of global pharmaceutical R&D spend or
manufacturing spend. Total units and penetration, from AR26 p.28:

- CRO: 29% of pharma R&D spend in 2024 rising to 42% by 2029. Back-solving
  total R&D spend: USD 84.3bn / 29% = **USD 290.7bn** (2024 total pharma
  R&D spend, implied); USD 139.8bn / 42% = USD 332.9bn (2029 implied).
- CDMO: 33% of pharma manufacturing spend in 2024 rising to 39% by 2029.
  Back-solving: USD 128.8bn / 33% = **USD 390.3bn** (2024 total pharma
  manufacturing spend, implied); USD 190.2bn / 39% = USD 487.7bn (2029
  implied).
- Combined 2024 CRO+CDMO revenue at these penetration rates: USD 84.3bn +
  USD 128.8bn = **USD 213.1bn** — this reproduces Method 1's full-scope
  headline almost exactly (USD 213bn).

This confirms internal consistency of Syngene's own disclosed figures,
but it is **not an independent method** — same ultimate Frost & Sullivan
source, restated via a penetration-rate lens rather than a market-size
lens. Kept here because it satisfies the "define the addressable unit,
total units, penetration" format and because the *rising penetration
rate itself* (CRO 29%->42%, CDMO 33%->39%) is a genuine, separately
useful growth-driver data point for Section 4A. Confidence: **M, marked
not independent of Method 1.**

### Method 4 — Import substitution

**NOT APPLICABLE in the classic sense.** Syngene is an outsourcing-*
capture* story (global demand routed to India-based delivery), not a
domestic-consumption-vs-import story. There is no "India consumes X,
produces Y domestically, imports the gap" framing here. The closest
analogue — India capturing outsourcing share that would otherwise go to
China or other geographies — is covered under Method 5.

### Method 5 — Global benchmark (India vs. China / global CRDMO share
migration)

- India's current share of the global CRDMO/outsourcing market: **2-3%**
  (BCG/IPSO report via IBEF, published 27-Feb-2025; India Rs 86,850 cr
  opportunity noted alongside this, but that specific Rs/USD figure did
  not internally reconcile with the stated 2-3% share math and is
  **not used**; the 2-3% share and the USD 22-25bn/2035 target figure
  do reconcile with each other and are used).
- Projected: India CRDMO to reach **USD 22-25bn by 2035**, 15% CAGR
  (same source), i.e. roughly 6-7x today's implied USD 3-3.5bn (2-3% of
  a ~USD 140-145bn base as scoped by that report).
- Benchmark comparator: China's CDMO market alone was USD 27.12bn in
  2023 (Mordor Intelligence), against a global CDMO market of ~USD 130bn
  the same period — a China share of roughly **20%** of global CDMO,
  itself the world's second-largest after the US. India's 2-3% share of
  a comparably-scoped global CRDMO market against China's ~20% CDMO-only
  share shows the size of the gap a "China+1" shift could theoretically
  close; BCG's own 15% CAGR/2035 projection embeds only a partial closing
  of that gap (India to ~4-4.5% of an extrapolated ~USD 500-550bn 2035
  global market, back-solved from the USD 22-25bn figure), not parity.
- Confidence: **L-M** — the India-specific numbers rest on one BCG/IPSO
  report; the China comparator rests on one Mordor Intelligence data
  point from 2023 (3 years old at run date — **directional only, not a
  headline number**, per the staleness rule for data 2-4 years old).

## SECTION 3: SAM & SOM

### 3A. SAM — five filters applied to the conservative global TAM
(Rs 18,04,800 cr / USD 188bn, 2024)

1. **Product fit filter:** starting from the full-scope headline (USD
   213bn) rather than the core figure to show the subtraction explicitly
   — remove full Clinical CRO (USD 26bn), since Syngene's T&CR is a small
   piece of that line, not the whole multi-site trial-management
   business. Remainder: USD 187bn (≈ the USD 188bn core figure, rounding).
2. **Geography filter:** of this ~USD 187-188bn, apply the
   independently-sourced India-delivery share rather than a mechanical
   percentage of the same top-down figure (the BCG-IPSO study and the
   Frost & Sullivan/Evaluate Pharma figure are not built on identical
   segment definitions, so multiplying one by a share derived from the
   other would manufacture false precision). Substitute BCG-IPSO's
   India CRDMO market value directly: **USD 3-3.5bn (Rs 28,800-33,600
   cr)**.
3. **Channel filter:** no material subtraction — both the global figure
   and the India-specific figure are already scoped to direct-contract
   CRDMO relationships, not a channel/distributor market.
4. **Customer filter:** no material subtraction — BCG-IPSO's India
   CRDMO figure is itself scoped to innovator-pharma/biotech NCE work,
   which matches Syngene's actual customer base (excludes the much
   larger domestic/generic API-CMO market covered by the ~USD 17.5bn
   "India CDMO market" figures found in general market research, which
   are **not used here** — flagged as a scope mismatch, not a competing
   estimate, per Section 1A).
5. **Capability filter:** no further subtraction — Syngene's actual
   service spectrum (discovery, dedicated centres, T&CR, small-molecule
   CDMO, biologics CDMO, new modalities) covers essentially the full
   definitional range of the India CRDMO figure used. The filter instead
   sets up the competitive-share question for SOM: Syngene is one of
   five-plus organised competitors for this pool (Method 3), not the
   only one.

**SAM = midpoint of BCG-IPSO's USD 3-3.5bn range = USD 3.25bn = Rs
31,200 cr** (2025 base year).

**SAM as % of (conservative) TAM:** Rs 31,200 cr / Rs 18,04,800 cr =
**1.73%.** A ~98% reduction from global TAM to SAM is expected and
appropriate for an India-plus-one-site delivery model inside a global
industry — this is the funnel doing its job, not an error.

### 3B. SOM at 3 and 5 years

**Current SAM share:** Syngene FY26 revenue Rs 3,739 cr ÷ SAM Rs 31,200
cr = **12.0%.** Syngene already holds a meaningful, top-tier share of
the India CRDMO opportunity as narrowly and defensibly scoped here —
consistent with its "16/14 of top-20 global pharma clients" claim
(AR26/CRISIL) and its position among the largest organised India CRDMO
players in Method 3.

**SAM growth used for the funnel:** 15% CAGR (BCG-IPSO, 2025-2035),
since this is the operative India-specific growth rate feeding SOM, not
the 9% global blended rate (see Section 5B for why both numbers are kept
distinct).

- SAM(3yr, ~FY29) = 31,200 x 1.15^3 = **Rs 47,451 cr**.
- SAM(5yr, ~FY31) = 31,200 x 1.15^5 = **Rs 62,754 cr**.

**Share-gain assumption.** The proof gate for the CDMO-refill thesis has
**not fired**: per B04/B07, no non-Zoetis CDMO customer is named anywhere
in the corpus, the emerging-moat score is MODEST (23, not EXPANSION), and
guidance credibility is graded C (B05). This rules out the "3-5pp
aggressive" share-gain bracket, which the framework reserves for cases
with *both* capacity *and* proven execution. Capacity exists (Unit 3,
Bayview, Mangalore, ADC suite); proven execution does not. Applying the
low end of the "1-2pp normal" bracket:

- 3yr: +1.0pp -> share 13.0%. **SOM(3yr) = 13.0% x 47,451 = Rs 6,169
  cr.**
- 5yr: +2.0pp -> share 14.0%. **SOM(5yr) = 14.0% x 62,754 = Rs 8,786
  cr.**

**Implied revenue CAGR (from FY26 Rs 3,739 cr):**
- 3yr: (6,169/3,739)^(1/3) - 1 = **18.2%**.
- 5yr: (8,786/3,739)^(1/5) - 1 = **18.6%**.

**Cross-check against management's own claim.** Management describes
"double-digit sustainable growth ... starting next fiscal [FY28]"
(Q1FY27 call, 30-Jul-2026, p.9), with FY27 itself guided to a
single-digit/mid-single-digit revenue **decline** (Q1FY27 call p.3;
CRISIL 11-Sep-2026). Taking CRISIL's mid-single-digit FY27 decline at
face value (~-5%), FY27 revenue would land near Rs 3,552 cr. To then
reach the SOM(3yr) figure of Rs 6,169 cr by FY29 (two years later)
requires a **31% CAGR across FY28-FY29 alone** — well above "double
digit" in the ordinary sense of the phrase. Two readings, one
observation separates them:
- Reading A: the SOM math above (15% market growth + modest 1-2pp share
  gain) is a reasonable mechanical result, and management's own
  "double-digit" language is the more conservative, hedged figure,
  understating what the market could support **if** the refill proves
  out — normal for guidance given late in a difficult year.
- Reading B: the SOM math is the optimistic side, because it assumes
  the market-share gain the proof gate has not yet delivered; the
  actually evidenced path is closer to management's own guided
  trajectory (single-digit decline this year, low-to-mid teens recovery
  next, "double-digit" meaning low-teens, not high-teens).
- The observation that separates them: whether a **named, non-Zoetis
  CDMO customer with disclosed revenue linkage** appears in FY27-FY28
  filings (B07's own optionality-register item). Until it does, Reading
  B is the more evidenced path; the conservatism this produces belongs
  in position size, not in silently lowering the SOM math itself.

### 3C. Capacity cross-check (B07 capex-embedded-growth, 34.5%)

B07's capex-embedded-growth figure (34.5% of current revenue) implies a
capacity ceiling, from capex already deployed or committed and captured
in that figure, of approximately:

Rs 3,739 cr x 1.345 = **Rs 5,029 cr.**

Comparing to the SOM figures above:

- 3yr: SOM Rs 6,169 cr **exceeds** the capacity ceiling by **~Rs 1,140
  cr**.
- 5yr: SOM Rs 8,786 cr **exceeds** the capacity ceiling by **~Rs 3,757
  cr**.

**The SOM is the optimistic side of this comparison, not the capex
plan.** Installed-plus-committed capacity, as captured in B07's own
figure, cannot physically produce the 3yr SOM revenue without either (a)
a faster-than-typical utilisation ramp on Unit 3/Bayview/Mangalore than
history suggests (Unit 3 alone took "almost 18-odd months" to capitalise,
per the Q4FY26 call), or (b) incremental capex beyond what B07 captured
— and Bayview's total committed capex is **NOT FOUND IN DOCUMENT**, so
this gap cannot be closed with evidence available in this run's corpus.
This is the same tension identified in 3B from a different angle: both
the share-gain assumption and the capacity base are stretched to hit the
SOM figure, which argues for treating Rs 6,169-8,786 cr as a ceiling
case, not a base case.

## SECTION 4: GROWTH DRIVERS, RISKS & STRUCTURE

### 4A. TAM growth drivers

| Driver | Impact | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Outsourcing penetration rising | High | CRO 29%->42% of pharma R&D spend, CDMO 33%->39% of manufacturing spend by 2029 (AR26 p.28) |
| Regulatory/geopolitical tailwind (China+1, BIOSECURE Act) | Medium-High, gradual | BIOSECURE Act US law Dec-2025; AR26 p.32-33 says "China+1 is accelerating... reinforced by... BIOSECURE Act momentum," India "positioned to benefit" — **but the same AR's outgoing-CEO letter separately says BIOSECURE "eliminated any short-term China+1 tailwinds"** (B07 flag, carried forward, unresolved this pass) |
| New modalities / technology enablement | High for CDMO | Large-molecule CDMO fastest-growing sub-segment (13% CAGR vs 7% for small molecule, AR26 p.184-190); ADCs, oligonucleotides, peptides, cell & gene named as high-growth (AR26 p.183) |
| Cost/geographic arbitrage | Medium | India cost advantage "~40-60% vs North America & Europe" (Dec-2025 deck p.9) |

| Advanced-modality share of pipelines | Medium | "Advanced modalities... over 40% of clinical pipelines," growing faster than small molecules (Q4FY26 call p.4-5; AR26) |

### 4B. TAM risks

| Risk | Monitoring signal |
|---|---|
| Pipeline rationalisation shrinking early discovery spend | AR26 p.33: sponsors "placing greater emphasis on acquiring or in-licensing later-stage assets," "discovery starts may slow" — directly on point for Syngene's own Research Services attrition |
| Funding-cycle cyclicality | Biotech VC funding trackers; 6-12 month lag to CRO revenue |
| Rising competition (China, Europe, captive R&D, boutique specialists) | CRISIL rationale 11-Sep-2026 names this explicitly as a rating weakness ("more CROs may enter the fray... constrain pricing flexibility") |
| BIOSECURE Act cuts the other way (US reshoring, not "friend-shoring" to India) | Watch whether US policy favours domestic US capacity (e.g. Bayview-type onshoring) over India-based delivery specifically — genuinely two-sided, not resolved in the corpus |
| AI reducing standardised-work outsourcing | AR26 frames AI as expanding the opportunity set (more candidates screened per budget), but the same technology could commoditise routine discovery chemistry faster than it creates new outsourced work — a real two-sided risk, not resolved either way in the corpus |
| Industry-wide capacity race creating overcapacity | B06 peer read: Anthem, Sai Life and Piramal are all committing large, overlapping advanced-modality capacity on FY27-FY28 timelines; none frames the synchronised timing as a sector risk — a structural over-build risk if demand does not arrive on the same timeline as the capacity |

### 4C. Market structure

- **Competitor count/concentration:** globally dominated by a handful of
  mega-CDMOs (Lonza, WuXi Biologics/AppTec, Samsung Biologics, Catalent);
  China alone ~USD 27.12bn CDMO revenue in 2023 (~20% of global CDMO,
  Mordor Intelligence). India's organised layer: Syngene, Piramal
  Pharma (CDMO segment), Aragen, Anthem, Sai Life — Method 3's top-5
  aggregate to Rs 15,111 cr (FY26), of which Piramal's CDMO segment
  (~Rs 4,878 cr, estimated) and Syngene (Rs 3,739 cr) are the two
  largest.
- **Organised vs. unorganised:** ~45-55% organised (top-5 listed/IPO-stage
  players) vs. ~45-55% unorganised/smaller/captive, per the Method 3/5
  cross-check — at the higher end of the framework's 30-60% India
  unorganised norm.
- **Consolidating or fragmenting:** consolidating. Aragen filed its DRHP
  in Aug-2026; Anthem listed in 2025; Cohance is itself an earlier
  aggregation of Suven and Cohance Fine Chem; Syngene's own Bayview
  acquisition (from Emergent BioSolutions, Mar-2025) is inorganic
  capacity addition. The industry is moving toward fewer, larger,
  better-capitalised platforms, not more numerous small entrants.
- **Price vs. differentiation competition:** genuinely two-speed. Advanced
  modalities and dedicated-centre relationships command differentiated
  pricing and stickiness (BMS extended to 2035); standardised/legacy
  discovery chemistry is commoditising on price — Syngene's own Q1FY27
  language ("drifted towards commoditized research services") is a
  company-specific instance of an industry-wide bifurcation, not proof
  the whole market is commoditising (B06: Anthem and Sai Life show no
  comparable pricing pressure in the same quarter).
- **Entries/exits, import share trend:** India's share of the global
  CRDMO pool is rising from ~2-3% (2025) toward an independently
  projected ~4-4.5% by 2035 (BCG-IPSO, back-solved); China's absolute
  CDMO revenue base (~USD 27bn, 2023) remains much larger than India's
  entire CRDMO market today, so the "import substitution from China"
  story is directionally real but still small in absolute terms.

  expands at the India-specific pace (catch-up share gain from China),

- **Company CAGR vs. TAM:** Syngene's own 2-year revenue CAGR (FY24 Rs

  global TAM CAGR and the 15% India SAM CAGR. **Syngene has been riding
  below the market, not gaining share, over the period the corpus
  covers.** The +1-2pp share-gain built into the SOM funnel above is
  therefore an assumption that the CDMO refill and research-services
  stabilisation actually happen going forward — it is not a
  continuation of the recent trend, which points the other way.
- **Years to saturate SAM:** not a binding constraint. At a 1-2pp/3yr
  share-gain pace against a SAM growing 15%/year, closing the remaining
  ~88pp of SAM share would take multiple decades — far outside the 3-5
  year hold horizon. The binding constraint on this thesis is proving
  the CDMO refill, not running out of addressable market.

On pure market-opportunity math (8.35x headroom, 15% India-specific
