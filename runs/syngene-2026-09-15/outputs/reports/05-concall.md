**Computed cross-check (not management-stated, arithmetic on filed figures):** FY26 revenue = Rs2,702cr (9M, Q3 FY26 call) + Rs1,037cr (Q4, Q4 FY26 call) = **Rs3,739cr**. Applying Deepak Jain's own "mid-single-digit decline" framing (≈4-6%) to that base gives an FY27 revenue target of roughly **Rs3,515-3,589cr**. Q1 FY27 delivered Rs736cr (verified above), leaving **≈Rs2,779-2,853cr required from Q2-Q4 FY27** — an average of ≈Rs926-951cr/quarter, ~26-29% above the Q1 run-rate. On margin, a full-year mid-20s (≈25%) target on that revenue base implies full-year EBITDA of ≈Rs888-897cr; Q1 delivered Rs91cr, leaving ≈Rs797-806cr required from the remaining three quarters on ≈Rs2,816cr of revenue — a required blended margin of **≈28-29%**, which independently corroborates the analyst's own on-call estimate of 27-30% (Bino Pathiparampil, Q1 FY27 call p.9). Management's supporting explanation (cost savings, Q4 seasonality) is directional, not itself quantified to this level.

**Pattern check:** Under Peter Bains (Q2-Q4 FY26 calls), the house style leaned external-blame — "single product," "geopolitical uncertainties," rupee volatility — with almost no proactive self-criticism; the closest exception is Peter's own concession in Q3 FY26 that Mangalore utilization has "not yet" met expectations, offered under direct analyst pressure (Manoj Bahety), not proactively. Under Kiran Mazumdar-Shaw and Siddharth Mittal (Q1 FY27 call), the tone flips sharply to self-critical framing ("we have drifted," "complacent," "took our eye off the ball") within the very first call of the new team. This is a genuine and unusual admission by Indian-listed-company standards, but it arrives precisely coincident with a management change that has every incentive to reset the narrative and blame the departed regime — the two readings (real strategic reset vs. convenient scapegoating of predecessors before the new team's own numbers are tested) cannot yet be separated from these three transcripts alone.

### 2D. What they are NOT saying

- No client count, name, or revenue figure for the "attrition of a few clients" in research services (Q1 FY27) that is co-blamed, with Zoetis, for a 16% YoY revenue decline.
- No firm operational date for Bayview across any of the four calls — only "on track," "this year," "later this year," repeated without a date, despite the site being acquired specifically to diversify away from the single-customer dependence that then materialized.
- No segment-level (CRO vs CDMO) growth-rate disclosure, ever — only mix percentages (67/33, 2/3-1/3, 41/59 in Q4, 78/22 in Q1 FY27), never the growth rate of each piece.
- No acknowledgment, in the Q2 FY26 or Q3 FY26 calls, that Peter Bains was serving as an "interim" CEO — the word appears only in Kiran Mazumdar-Shaw's Q4 FY26 closing remarks ("Peter has done an excellent job of being that interim CEO"), retroactively recharacterizing a role that was presented without qualification in the two prior earnings calls. Likely reason for the earlier silence: naming an interim arrangement publicly before a successor is named would itself have been a negative signal during the Librela crisis.
- No explicit reconciliation between Peter Bains's Q2 FY26 statement that China+1 is "not a factor in our consideration" (for clinical trials) and Siddharth Mittal's Q1 FY27 statement that the Biosecure Act is "a great advantage for us to shift molecules from China to India" (for CDMO) — different business lines, but the flat denial-to-embrace shift on the same geopolitical theme is never addressed.

## SECTION 3: COMPETITIVE INTELLIGENCE FROM CONCALLS

### 3A. What management says about competitors

- Q3 FY26: Manoj Bahety directly benchmarks Syngene's capacity-utilization pace against unnamed CDMO peers who are showing "significantly faster and much bigger numbers" — Peter Bains concedes Mangalore "has not yet" met expectations, a candid rather than defensive response (p.13).
- Q1 FY27: Siddharth Mittal positions Syngene's large-molecule CDMO breadth as matched only by "Chinese and Korean companies" in scale/quality, and cites the Biosecure Act (excluding certain Chinese CDMOs from US biotech contracts) as a competitive tailwind, "a great advantage for us to shift molecules from China to India" (p.13). Credibility check: directionally plausible given the named policy, but no client name or won-business evidence is offered — classify as an aspirational competitive claim pending verification.
- Q1 FY27: research-services attrition is attributed to unnamed "competitors who offer these services at a much lower cost" in "commoditized" work (Kiran Mazumdar-Shaw, p.6) — no competitor named, a category claim rather than a named-rival claim.

### 3B. Industry and market intelligence dropped in the calls

- "Advanced modalities [peptides, ADCs, oligonucleotides] now account for over 40% of clinical pipelines and are growing at a materially faster pace than traditional small molecules" — cited as an "industry estimate" with no source named (Peter Bains, Q4 FY26 call p.3). Flagged for peer verification.
- Biotech VC funding: characterized as "thawing"/"accelerating a little" in Q2-Q3 FY26 (Peter Bains); an analyst (Kunal Dhamesha, Q1 FY27) separately cites "VC funding in U.S. up by single digit year-to-date" — this is the analyst's claim, not management's, and management does not confirm or deny it in that exchange.
- Section 232 tariffs assessed by management as having "negligible" impact on Syngene, with continued RFP flow post-announcement (Peter Bains, Q4 FY26 p.8-9).
- Middle East conflict cost pass-through: management states "no disruptions to continuity of service," some cost spikes seen but not material, contract-by-contract pass-through, no blanket policy (Deepak Jain/Peter Bains, Q4 FY26 p.9-10).
- China+1 / Biosecure Act framing shift: explicitly denied as a factor for clinical trials in Q2 FY26 ("that's not a factor in our consideration at this stage" — Peter Bains, p.10) but claimed as a CDMO tailwind in Q1 FY27 (Siddharth Mittal, p.13) — different business lines, unreconciled in either call.

### 3D. Customer and order book signals

- BMS: extension to 2035, expanded scope across discovery-to-clinical lifecycle — the single clearest positive renewal signal in the period.
- Zoetis/Librela: the defining loss/decline of the period; quantified at "nearly $50 million" annual impact (Q1 FY27); the follow-on molecule Lenivia confirmed to have no Syngene role (Q4 FY26); Zoetis retains "delivery obligations" in H2 FY27 and is "not expected to be zero even in FY28."

- Unit 3/Stelis: multiple customers signed for clinical/development-stage large-molecule work; no large-volume commercial molecule yet as of Q1 FY27.
- Mangalore small-molecule CDMO: new commercial and clinical molecule "lock-ins" reported in Q1 FY27, unquantified.
