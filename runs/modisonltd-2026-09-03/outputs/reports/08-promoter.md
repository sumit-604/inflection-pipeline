## SECTION 3: BUSINESS CONDUCT & ETHICAL TRACK RECORD

**3A. RPT history — this is the load-bearing section for this company.**

- **Modison Copper Private Limited (MCPL)** is a >98% promoter-family-
  owned entity that is simultaneously Modison's **major raw-material
  supplier and a partial customer**, and is itself materially dependent
  on Modison as a customer. FY26 disclosed figures (VERIFIED, AR FY26 EGM
  notice, line 1591-1634):
  - FY26 actual RPT with MCPL: Rs 5,041.74 lakh (Rs 50.42 cr), 11.27% of
    Modison's FY26 consolidated turnover of Rs 70,957.70 lakh.
  - That same Rs 50.42 cr is **48.76% of MCPL's own FY26 turnover** (Rs
    16,406.88 lakh) — MCPL is nearly half dependent on Modison.
  - Forward ceiling sought and approved at the 43rd AGM: Rs 8,000 lakh
    (Rs 80 cr) for FY27, split Rs 6,500 lakh purchases/job-work and Rs
    1,500 lakh sales, under omnibus approval, till the 2027 AGM.
  - MCPL FY26 provisional financials: Net worth Rs 4,343.80 lakh,
    turnover Rs 16,406.88 lakh, PAT Rs 1,005.57 lakh (6.1% net margin).
  - Girdhari Lal Modi and Kumar Jay Modi are named as personally
    interested in MCPL (line 1469-1475); Rajkumar Mohanlal Modi
    "refrained from voting" on the MCPL resolution (line 1824), and
    Girdhari Lal Modi and Kumar Jay Modi were also excluded from voting
    on interested resolutions (line 1827) — **the company did follow the
    correct interested-director recusal procedure**, a governance
    positive on process, even though the substance (scale and mutual
    dependency of the relationship) remains the key watch item.
  - The AR's stated justification for the RPT is thin: "the transactions
    with MCPL is being conducted for past many years without any
    hindrances... beneficial to the Company" (line 1719-1722) — this is
    a **process/rationale disclosure, not an arm's-length pricing
    benchmark**; no independent pricing comparison or valuer opinion is
    quoted in the extract. This is a documentation gap the operator
    should weigh, not a proven mispricing.
  - No default by MCPL on any obligation in FY26 (line 1542-1548, "Nil").
- **M/s. Modison (Partnership Firm)** and **Dishah Innovative Solutions
  Private Limited**: both are disclosed related parties (rent paid,
  purchase of goods, service/maintenance charges per the FY26 RPT
  schedule) but the extracted transaction table is row/label-corrupted
  (line 9337-9416) and individual rupee amounts cannot be reliably
  attributed to each entity from this document. Recommend the operator
  pull the clean AR PDF table directly if RPT-by-entity sizing is needed
  beyond MCPL.
- **Modicon Private Limited**: RPT counterparty for "Sale of Goods/Other
  Operative Revenue" and "Sale of Plant & Machinery" (line 9412-9414);
  same table-corruption caveat applies; identity/ownership link to the
  Girdhari Lal Modi branch is not confirmed (see 1B).
- **Subsidiary divestment pattern**: two wholly-owned subsidiaries
  incorporated by Modison (Modison Hitech in Jul-2023, later renamed
  Modison Solartech) or long-held (Modison HV, incorporated 1982) were
  both divested within the past ~15 months — Solartech on 09-Jun-2025 and
  HV on 13-Aug-2026 — with HV going directly into the personal hands of
  the MD and JMD (Girdhari Lal Modi, Kumar Jay Modi) for Rs 27.68 lakh,
  a price that is close to the subsidiary's own book net worth (Rs 28.26
  lakh, immaterial 0.10% of group net worth). This reads as **group
  simplification of small/non-performing vehicles rather than value
  extraction on the numbers available**, but the pattern — company
  incubates a subsidiary, then sells it to the promoters themselves
  rather than to a third party or via a competitive process — is worth
  flagging for continued monitoring, especially if it recurs at larger
  scale.
- No loans-to-group-entity, guarantee-for-private-entity, or royalty
  payment to a promoter individual (as opposed to entity) was found in
  the legible portion of the AR.

**3B. Capital allocation behaviour.**
- No dilution: promoter holding has been flat to marginally rising for
  years (screener.in shareholding pattern, non-anchored aggregate); no
  preferential allotment or lapsed promoter warrant found.
- **Dividend paid against negative operating cash flow.** CARE Ratings
  (Dec-2025) states "Cash flow from operations remained negative and free
  cash and bank balance stood at modest levels in FY25" (CARE Dec-2025,
  line 133) in the context of an elongated, silver-price-driven working-
  capital cycle (gross current assets 161 days FY25 vs 158 days FY24;
  operating cycle 139 days vs 128 days, CARE Dec-2025 line 112-114). The
  AR shows a dividend deduction of Rs 1,460.25 lakh (Rs 14.60 cr) against
  Rs 1,135.75 lakh the prior year (AR FY26 line 2012-2013) — consistent
  with the context brief's Rs 14.60 cr figure. Paying a rising dividend in
  a year CARE describes as operating-cash-flow-negative, funded via
  increased working-capital bank borrowing (gearing rose to 0.34x FY25
  from 0.16x FY24, CARE Dec-2025 line 78-81), is a **capital-allocation
  conduct flag**: it prioritises the promoter-heavy dividend stream
  (promoters hold 52%+ of the payout) over balance-sheet conservatism in
  a year the company was simultaneously raising bank debt to fund
  inventory. This is judged CAUTION-level, not AVOID-level, because (a)
  the dividend is paid company-wide and pro-rata, not routed
  disproportionately to promoters via salary or a private vehicle, (b)
  the underlying driver (silver price spike inflating working capital)
  is an external, disclosed, and temporary factor rather than a hidden
  cash diversion, and (c) gearing at 0.34x remains moderate in absolute
  terms.
- No buyback found. No value-destructive related-party acquisition found
  beyond the subsidiary-divestment pattern in 3A (which is a divestment,
  not an acquisition).
- **Managerial remuneration.** MD Girdhari Lal Modi's remuneration ratio
  to median employee remuneration is **111.38x** (AR FY26 line 2991,
  Annexure C), with an 11.24% increase in FY26. The two JMDs each sit at
  ~69.8x median (line 2992-2997), each with <2% increase. Combined
  promoter-family executive remuneration context: the operator's brief
  states promoter-family EDs draw ~12.6% of PAT in remuneration (not
  independently recomputed here; AR does not disclose the absolute
  Rupee remuneration figures in the extracted Annexure C, only ratios and
  % changes — flagged as a documentation gap, the ratio-only disclosure
  is the statutory minimum but not sufficient on its own to fully audit
  the PAT-percentage claim). For calibration: median employee
  remuneration rose 12.25% in FY26 and non-managerial employee average
  salary rose 9.02%, while average managerial remuneration rose only
  5.59% (line 3009-3030) — **on this specific metric, managerial pay grew
  slower than both median and average employee pay in FY26**, a point in
  the promoters' favour even though the absolute MD-to-median ratio
  (111x) is high in absolute terms for a company this size. A ratio this
  high mainly reflects a low median wage base (288 permanent employees,
  a labour-intensive manufacturer) rather than an accelerating promoter
  payout; it should be read as CAUTION (worth watching, especially if the
  ratio widens further) rather than a proven extraction pattern.
