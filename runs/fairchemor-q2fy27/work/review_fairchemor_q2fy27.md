### 2. Sector intelligence
The company turns vegetable-oil refining leftovers (acid oil and deodoriser distillate) into fatty acids, dimer acid, isostearic acid and tocopherols (MGMT, deck p5-p6).
These leftovers are about 1.25% and 0.25% of oil processed (MGMT, deck fulltext L98-104). So feedstock supply follows India's edible-oil refining volume. It cannot grow faster than refining (INFERENCE).
India cut the import duty on crude soya oil from 16.5% to 11.0% (MGMT, deck R035 R036). Management says refiners will now process more soya oil than palm oil, so acid-oil supply "will improve substantially" (MGMT, call, ASR). The size of the gain is not stated.
The feedstock also has other buyers: soap makers and biodiesel makers (MGMT, call, ASR). An analyst on the call linked this to crude oil prices. So feedstock prices can move with crude oil and biodiesel demand (INFERENCE).
Selling prices track the cost of imported finished products (MGMT, deck R430). Management says Chinese dumping of dimer acid has gone down, but gave no reason (MGMT, call, ASR). A trade deal with the EU or UK could help exports but could also let cheaper imports in (INFERENCE, A3P-05).
End markets are paints and inks, soaps, animal feed, cosmetics, pharma and FMCG (MGMT, deck R347). Management says European cosmetic demand is down (MGMT, call, ASR). Demand-cycle data for these markets was not in the run (not disclosed).

### 3. Business-model intelligence
Fairchem earns a spread: the price of its acids minus the cost of oil leftovers. Gross margin was 23.07% this quarter against 20.32% a year ago (FILED, derived). Gross profit per kg sold rose 5.9% from June to September (INFERENCE from FILED lines and call tonnage).
Operating costs (staff, power, other) rose only 13.5% while sales rose 49.7% (FILED, derived). That operating leverage added about 4 points of margin (INFERENCE from FILED lines). It reverses if prices fall.
The company pays its suppliers at the factory gate on short terms (MGMT, deck fulltext L233-235). Supplier credit is about 8 days (FILED, derived). Management puts the cash cycle at about 100 days, longer for exports (MGMT, call, ASR). This half's derived 94 days is a low point, because inventory is at its lowest; without inventory, the cycle got 2.5 days longer (INFERENCE from FILED lines). The deck's own cycle chart rises every period, from 76 to 128 days (MGMT, deck p22). So when prices rise, stock is rebuilt or exports grow, the extra working capital lands on bank borrowings.
The business has one plant, one segment, and almost all sales are domestic (FILED, Note 3). It runs with almost no cash and funds itself on short-term bank lines (FILED: cash Rs 0.57 L; borrowings 68.65 Cr, all current).
Model drift this quarter: none in the product mix that the documents show. The capital pattern is the change to watch. Borrowings rose about 29.4 Cr in the half-year when the company spent 34 Cr on its buyback, so about 86% of the buyback was bank-funded. The filed totals show new liabilities paid for it (INFERENCE from FILED totals and deck figures, A3-13). The dividend was cut from Rs 7.5 to Rs 1.0 per share over the same years (FILED, derived; deck p23). Now comes a purchase of promoter land the company already uses (FILED; MGMT, call). Management says it will spend on capacity only after the new product proves out (MGMT, call, ASR).
The deck promises "low volatility in the cash flow generation" (MGMT). Its own cash-flow history swings from minus 16.8 Cr to plus 41.6 Cr in two years (MGMT, deck p20).
