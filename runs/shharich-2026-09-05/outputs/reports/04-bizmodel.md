## SECTION 1: THE BUSINESS MODEL IN PLAIN ENGLISH

### 1A. One-line description
Shree Hari Chemicals makes H-Acid, a chemical building block that other
factories turn into dye. It buys naphthalene and other industrial
chemicals, cooks them into H-Acid at one plant in Mahad, Maharashtra, and
sells that H-Acid to dye makers in India and abroad (AR FY26, p.5).

### 1B. Money flow chain, one line per revenue stream

**Stream 1: Manufactured H-Acid (and other manufactured chemicals)**
[Naphthalene, sulphuric acid, oleum, caustic soda, and other industrial
chemicals] -> [Company reacts these chemicals in a multi-step process at
its Mahad plant] -> [Delivers bulk H-Acid, a dye intermediate, direct to
buyer or through export agents] -> [Dye and textile-chemical
manufacturers in India and 4 export countries pay] -> [Payment against
invoice, standard B2B trade credit terms; FY26 receivables sit mostly
under 6 months, Note 35.17, AR FY26, p.135].
(AR FY26, p.5, p.119, p.123)

**Stream 2: Traded H-Acid (new in FY26, Rs 3,349.83 lakh)**
[Company buys already-made H-Acid from another producer] -> [Company adds
no manufacturing step, only resells] -> [Delivers the same bought-in
H-Acid to a buyer] -> [Buyer pays] -> [Standard trade credit]. This line
did not exist in FY25 (AR FY26, Note 27 p.119, Note 30 p.123). Note 35.2
labels this trading volume together with manufactured H-Acid under one
"H-Acid" line (Rs 17,826.28 lakh), which overstates organic manufactured
growth unless the trading line is separated out (AR FY26, Note 35.2
p.126; see B02 finding, flagged again in Section 3A below).

**Stream 3: By-products (Gypsum, Iron Sludge, Globular Salt)**
[Waste stream generated while making H-Acid] -> [Company packages the
gypsum/sludge/salt by-product] -> [Sells to the cement industry as a raw
material substitute] -> [Cement buyer pays] -> [Standard trade credit].
Rs 225.82 lakh in FY26, Rs 115.20 lakh in FY25 (AR FY26, Note 35.2
p.126; by-product-to-cement-industry claim, AR FY26, p.8).

**Stream 4: Other operating revenue (lifting charges, scrap sales,
subsidy, export benefit)**
Small ancillary lines, Rs 398.38 lakh combined in FY26 (Lifting Charges
Rs 219.28 lakh + Scrap Sales Rs 3.09 lakh + Subsidy Rs 14.02 lakh +
Export Benefit Rs 161.99 lakh), together 2.2% of revenue (AR FY26, Note
27, p.119/p.161).

The company discloses only ONE reporting segment: "the Company's business
activity falls within a single primary business segment 'H-Acid Chemicals
product'... the disclosure requirement of Ind AS 108 'Operating Segment'
are not applicable" (AR FY26, Note 35.4, p.127, repeated identically in
consolidated Note 35.4, p.166). There is no product-wise or geography-wise
segment profit disclosure beyond the revenue notes cited above.

### 1E. The chai-stall-uncle version

Think of this company like a small factory that makes one kind of raw
masala paste, not a finished snack. It buys basic chemicals, cooks them
into H-Acid, and sells that paste to other factories that turn it into
dye for clothes. It does not sell to you or me; it sells factory to
factory. Its price for the paste goes up and down with the world market,
the same way onion prices swing at the mandi, so some quarters it makes
good money and some quarters it barely breaks even (Screener Data_Sheet
quarterly PAT: Rs 0.02 cr in Mar-2025, a loss of Rs 1.67 cr in Jun-2025,
then Rs 5.92 cr in Jun-2026). This year it also started simply buying and
reselling some H-Acid it did not make itself, like a shopkeeper buying
sacks of grain and reselling them at a thin margin instead of growing the
grain (AR FY26, Note 27, p.119; Note 30, p.123).

### 2D. Industry lifecycle stage and this company's position

MD&A frames the Indian dyes/dye-intermediates industry as in a mature,
steady-growth phase: "expected to witness steady growth in 2026-27,
supported by demand from key end-use sectors such as textiles, printing
inks, plastics, industrial coatings and leather processing" (AR FY26,
MD&A p.76). Within that mature industry, the company positions itself as
an established, single-plant, single-product converter riding the
industry's overall demand growth and global H-Acid price cycle, not as a
category creator or a consolidator (no acquisition, no new product launch
disclosed in this AR beyond the SDPL capacity build discussed in Section

### 3D. Unit economics: the physics of the business

**Define one unit:** 1 metric tonne (MT) of H-Acid produced and sold.

| Element | Finding | Anchor |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue per unit | NOT FOUND. AR gives an installed capacity of 270 tonnes but no production volume, so revenue/tonne cannot be computed from this AR | AR FY26, p.5 (capacity only) |
| Cost per unit | NOT FOUND for the same reason; only aggregate raw material (Rs 9,275.48 lakh) and power (Rs 2,082.61 lakh) costs are disclosed, with no per-tonne breakdown | AR FY26, Note 29 p.119, Note 34 p.124 |
| Volume drivers | End-use textile/dye/leather demand growth (MD&A p.76); the pending SDPL capacity addition (Chairman's Message, p.4) | AR FY26, p.76, p.4 |
| Price drivers | Global H-Acid price, set by supply/demand balance and Chinese competitor pricing (MD&A names Chinese price competition explicitly, p.76); naphthalene and other input costs move the spread from the cost side | AR FY26, MD&A p.76 |
| Cost drivers | Raw material basket (naphthalene, sulphuric acid, oleum, nitric acid, soda ash, caustic soda flakes, cast iron powder, lime stone powder - Note 35.3, p.126) and power/fuel (Note 34, p.124); together c.79.5% of FY26 revenue | AR FY26, Note 35.3 p.126, Note 34 p.124 |
| Incremental margin and operating leverage | Cannot be computed precisely without volume data, but the FY26 print shows a directional signal: revenue rose 30.7% while EBITDA (per MD&A's own table) FELL from Rs 1,147.66 lakh to Rs 980.64 lakh (AR FY26, MD&A p.77), meaning incremental revenue in FY26 carried NEGATIVE incremental margin at the blended (trading-diluted) level. This is consistent with the trading line diluting margin and/or a genuine spread squeeze on the manufactured line; it cannot be separated further from this AR alone | AR FY26, MD&A p.77 |

**The physics, in plain terms:** this is a spread business. The company
does not earn from selling more units at a stable price; it earns from
the gap between what it pays for naphthalene-chain chemicals and what
it can charge for H-Acid, a gap set largely outside the company's
control by the global H-Acid market and Chinese competitor pricing.
Volume growth only helps if the spread holds; a volume increase during a
spread trough (as the FY26 EBITDA fall despite revenue growth suggests
may have partly occurred) can still shrink profit. This is precisely why
CLAUDE.md's v3.7 Amendment 17 bars feeding spot-year ROCE or WC trends
from a converter year like FY26 into Section 1B or FTTCP without first
separating spread from volume.
