Net cash compounder funding unproven storage bet

DDev Plastiks makes polymer compounds for cable and wire manufacturers. PVC, XLPE and Sioplas, HFFR, engineering grades and Antifab, sold on cost-plus B2B terms to cable makers in India and 55 export markets. FY26 revenue landed inside the Rs 2,850 to 2,950 Cr guided band, PAT was about Rs 202 Cr, and the EBITDA margin held near 11%. The company sells one tonne at a time, at roughly Rs 1.45 lakh of revenue and Rs 15,800 of EBITDA per tonne. It was carved out of Kkalpana Industries through an NCLT-approved demerger in 2022 and has run net cash ever since.

Two things are changing at once. Capacity is stepping up: the Bhiwadi XLPE line at 48,000 MTPA was commissioned in the last week of April 2026, and 5,000 MT of HFFR plus 10,000 MT of PVC came in by December 2025. Alongside it sits a new battery energy storage assembly vertical with zero operating history, revenue recognition starting in the second half of FY27. These are two different businesses. One is a proven cost-plus compounder, the other is a pre-revenue assembly bet, and they earn different margins on different capital.

Gate 0 reads GOOD, with a core score of 74 out of 100, a grand total of 86 out of 160, and three moats confirmed. The classification is capped at GOOD by a single deal-breaker: Block B, which measures cash conversion, scored 7 against a required 8.

Cash conversion is the watch item, and it is the reason for that cap. Working-capital days rose from 63 to 82 across FY24 to FY26, almost entirely on an FY26 inventory build. Cumulative CFO to PAT over the three years was 0.59 and FCF to PAT was 0.33; FY26 alone turned only 42% of PAT into operating cash. Finished-goods inventory rose 60% while revenue rose 7%. Against that, the balance sheet carries net cash, no term borrowings, and a capex programme funded from accruals rather than debt. Whether the inventory is stock awaiting dispatch from the new lines or the first sign of a sell-through slowdown is not yet provable from the record.

Management credibility grades B. The company delivered its capacity additions, roughly 30% export growth, and the 10 to 12% margin band it guided. It missed FY26 volume, printing 201,370 MT against 210,000 to 220,000 MT guided. BESS FY27 revenue guidance was cut twice within two quarters of launch, from Rs 300 to 500 Cr down to Rs 200 to 250 Cr, and Phase-1 capex was revised up from Rs 150 Cr to about Rs 200 Cr. A guided Adani and UltraTech PVC offtake was dropped silently after its window passed. An external shock was cited as the primary excuse in each of the three quarters reviewed.

Accounting quality scores 6 out of 10. There is one red flag: Note 52's lease-liability roll-forward carries a Rs 492 Lacs unreconciled gap, a disclosure-control weakness rather than a balance-sheet misstatement, sitting under an otherwise clean unqualified audit with no going-concern language and no promoter pledge. The promoter read is TRUSTWORTHY. Royalty paid to former parent Kkalpana fell 72.5% while purchases from the same entity rose 88.9% with no explanation given, Chairman and MD pay fell from Rs 93 lakh to Rs 87 lakh while PAT grew 9%, and the promoter group filed zero share encumbrance in April 2026. Two of the three independent directors also sit on Kkalpana's board, a governance-proximity item to watch given the related-party flows.

Peer corroboration is thin. Only Apcotex Industries is usable, and it has zero product overlap with DDev beyond shared crude-linked feedstock. Apcotex confirms the raw-material shock existed at roughly the magnitude DDev described, but its own 7-May-2026 call contradicts DDev's claim that prices normalized by April 2026, with the Strait of Hormuz still closed and coverage managed only to end-June. The net effect complicates the cost story rather than confirming it.

Runway reads STRONG. Core serviceable market of Rs 5,378 Cr over three years and Rs 7,666 Cr over five implies a revenue CAGR near 22% and 21%, against a current served share around 31% and 3.25 times revenue headroom on a market growing about 9% a year. The BESS opportunity is sized separately, anchored to CEA's 208 GWh FY30 storage build. Management's own "2.5 lakh tonne cable-compounding market, one-third share" claim is stale and internally impossible against its own 2.01 lakh tonne FY26 output, and carries zero weight in the sizing.
