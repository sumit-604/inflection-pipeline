## Rating Action

- Short Term Rating: Crisil A1 (Reaffirmed)

## Detailed Rationale (key points)
- Upgrade reflects significant improvement in business risk profile following the
  NPC (National Pipe Company, Saudi Arabia) acquisition completed 21-May-2026:
  scale +50-60%, OPBDIT margin to ~14-15% (12.3% FY26), +430,000 MT LSAW/HSAW
  Saudi capacity, bought at 1.5x EV/EBITDA, revenue/earnings accretive day one.
- Operating performance improved: OPBDIT margin 12.3% FY26 vs 8-10% in the past,
  on better export/value-added mix.
- NPC (Rs1,000cr / USD102m) funded USD70m debt + USD32m equity. Despite the debt,
  financial risk profile stays comfortable: gearing ~0.6-0.7x and TOL/TNW 2-2.2x
  in FY27 (vs 0.25x and 1.45x in FY26). Liquidity strong: NCA >Rs450cr vs
  repayment Rs240-250cr.
- Strengths partially offset by NPC integration risk, working-capital-intensive
  operations, and cyclicality/raw-material/forex volatility.

## Analytical Approach
Consolidated view of the Man group: MIIL + Man USA INC, Man Overseas Invest FZCO,
Merino Shelters Private Limited, Man Offshore and Drilling Limited, Man
International Steel Industries Company (MISIC), and Man Stainless Steel Tubes
Limited (financial fungibility; full consolidation). NOTE: Merino Shelters is
consolidated FULL here (rationale for consolidation: Managerial Linkages) —
relevant to the SEBI non-consolidation matter.

## Key Rating Drivers — Strengths
1. Improved business risk profile following NPC acquisition. NPC: 100% via MISIC
   for ~Rs1,000cr (USD102m), API-certified, DEBT FREE, revenue Rs2,000cr
   (USD211m) and OPBDIT margin 25% in CY25. Combined group capacity ~16,00,000
   MTPA. Direct entry into Saudi demand (Vision 2030); coating plant in Saudi by
   Mar-2027 makes MIIL a fully integrated in-Kingdom player. NPC on Saudi Aramco
   approved vendor list >2 decades, plus SWA, SWPC, WTTCO, KOC, Qatar Petroleum.
2. Established SAW-pipes position. Domestic industry consolidated (top 4 ~80% of
   capacity); MIIL one of the largest, >30 years, HSAW+LSAW since 1995; 175,000
   MT ERW plant (FY24); upcoming 20,000 MT Jammu stainless plant (expected
   completion Sep-2027, commissioning/ramp-up a monitorable). FY26 revenue
   Rs3,564cr, margin 12.3% (vs Rs3,507cr, 9.6% prior year); OPBDIT margin +~275bps
   FY26; order book ~Rs3,000cr (6-8 months).
3. Healthy financial risk profile. Adjusted networth Rs2,017cr FY26; gearing
   0.6-0.7x FY27 (0.25x at 31-Mar-2026); TOL/TNW peaks 2-2.2x FY27 (1.45x FY26);
   interest coverage ~3.1x FY26 improving to ~3.3-4x medium term. NCA >Rs450cr
   vs yearly obligation Rs230-240cr. Ability to manage incremental working
   capital while holding coverage ratios is crucial given moderate-to-high
   fund-based limit utilisation.

## Key Rating Drivers — Weaknesses
1. Cyclicality + raw-material/forex volatility. Majority revenue from oil & gas,
   rest water/irrigation. Fixed-price tenders with 2-4 month award lead time mean
   input-cost increases after tender application cannot be passed on. Forex losses
   incurred in FY23 and FY26; hedged, which mitigates.
2. Working-capital-intensive operations (VERBATIM): "Gross current assets have
   been around 200 days in the past, driven by debtors of over 100 days. However,
   in the past two years, inventory and debtor days have remained stretched. In
   Fiscal 2025, working capital was impacted due to several factors, including a
   delay in the execution of a Taiwan order, which led to inventory buildup. The
   execution of this order was completed in Q1 of Fiscal 2026. In Fiscal 2026,
   inventory and debtor days increased to approximately 180 and 128 days,
   respectively, due to the Middle East conflict, which hindered the delivery of
   ships to the United Arab Emirates. However, working capital levels are expected
   to normalize in the current fiscal year, as MIIL has received approval to
   deliver the shipment to the eastern port of UAE - Fujairah port. Also,
   concentration in receivables renders MIIL susceptible to steady increase in the
   working capital requirement, and hence, remains a key monitorable. As of March
   31, 2026, out of the total debtors of Rs 1,250 crores, Rs 109 crores remained
   under arbitration. According to the company, this amount has a high chance of
   recoverability"
3. Integration risks associated with the large NPC acquisition; realisation of
   synergies a key monitorable.

## Liquidity: Strong (VERBATIM)
"Liquidity is aided by moderate fund-based working capital lines utilization and
internal cash accruals; the company also avails need-based project-specific limits.
MIIL is expected to earn NCA of Rs 450 crores to Rs 580 crores against repayments
of Rs 230-250 crores each year in the next 3 fiscals. The working capital limit of
over Rs 2180 crore (fund based Rs 310 and non-fund-based limits of Rs 1870 crores)
were utilized ~77% on average over the 11 months through April 2026. Cash and cash
equivalents were around Rs 657 crores (unencumbered of ~Rs 300 crores) as on March
31, 2026. Crisil Ratings expects internal accrual, cash & cash equivalents, and
unutilized bank lines to be sufficient to meet repayment obligations as well as
incremental capex requirements."

## Outlook: Stable

## Rating Sensitivity Factors
- Upward: operating margin above 13-14%; product diversification; liquidity
  improvement via realisation of non-current/non-core assets.
- Downward: margins below 10%; scale/order-book reduction; financial-risk
  deterioration from higher-than-expected debt-funded capex or support to
  non-business entities; "Any liability due to SEBI audit observations impacting
  the business or financial risk profile of the company."
