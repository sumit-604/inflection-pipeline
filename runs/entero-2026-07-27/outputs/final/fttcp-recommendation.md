The determination leans STRUCTURAL on the rating agency's own view, not GROWTH-INDUCED, so it caps the disposition at PROCEED WITH CAVEATS and never clears to a BUY.

Rating agency verbatim (India Ratings, ratings.pdf page 3, Liquidity section, 2025-12-03): "Entero group's cash flow from operations has been negative since the first full year of its operations in FY19 due to the intense working capital requirement and modest yield nature of its business. The management expects the cash flow from operations to be positive by the end of FY26. The group's net working capital cycle on sales basis marginally elongated to 78 days in FY25 (FY24: 75 days) but remained in line with the peers in the pharmaceutical distribution segment. The cash flow from operations and free cash flow are likely to remain negative, in the agency's view, in the near term. However, the reduction in the goods and services tax rates to 5% in September 2025 from 12%, and management's cost-efficient strategies may turn the operating cashflows positive by end-FY26."

## Publish check

📤 PUBLISH CANDIDATE

Observation: Entero's organic outperformance over the Indian Pharmaceutical Market compressed from about 1.9x to about 1.4x across three consecutive quarters, and management attributes the compression to IPM itself accelerating rather than to any loss of share. The value-versus-volume distinction matters: a faster market can shrink an outperformance multiple even as absolute growth holds, and the two readings point to opposite conclusions about franchise strength. MedPlus, the nearest listed analogue, told analysts it does not track share at that granularity, so the claim sits unchecked.

Why worth publishing: it is a clean, teachable case of how an outperformance ratio can decay for a benign reason (a rising base) or a malign one (fading share), and why the distinction changes the investment read. It needs no price call and names no target.
