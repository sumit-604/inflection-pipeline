## Business Understanding Narrative

**What does it do, and how does it make money?** Merritronix assembles
electronics for defence and aerospace programmes in Hyderabad. It buys the
components, builds the circuit boards and boxes, tests them and ships them.
Turnkey manufacturing was 92.07% of FY26 revenue of Rs 155.90 crore (RHP
page 36). Aerospace and defence was 97.81% (RHP page 37). The money is a
conversion margin on material it buys: raw material was Rs 122.58 crore of
Rs 155.90 crore revenue in FY26 (deck page 29).

**What is changing?** The model moved from job work to turnkey. Job work and
trading fell from 9.29% of revenue in FY25 to 1.40% in FY26 (RHP page 36).
EBITDA margin rose from 7.82% in FY24 to 17.42% in FY26 (deck page 27). The
company credits "increased direct customer engagement" and "a higher
proportion of turnkey and obsolescence management programmes" (RHP page 160).

**Why now?** The IPO funds the next step. The RHP earmarks Rs 21.36 crore
for machinery, Rs 21.95 crore for working capital and Rs 12.72 crore for debt
repayment (RHP page 92, figures in Rs lakh). A new SMT line started in
January 2026 (deck page 11). Two manufacturing MoUs were signed in June 2026
(deck page 13).

**What must be true?** The 17% margin must be a durable spec position, not a
pass-through quirk of one large customer. Customer 5 holds Rs 50.57 crore of
the Rs 96.65 crore order book, 52% (RHP page 158).

**What breaks it?** Cash. Operating cash flow was negative Rs 23.38 crore in
FY26 against profit of Rs 16.10 crore (RHP page 57). A turnkey model that
cannot convert profit to cash needs new equity every year.

## 2. Business model and archetype

Four lines, FY26, Rs lakh (RHP page 36):

| Line | FY26 | Share | FY25 | Share |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Turnkey / build to print | 14,353.01 | 92.07% | 9,384.62 | 82.64% |
| Obsolescence engineering | 1,018.02 | 6.53% | 916.52 | 8.07% |
| Trading | 99.55 | 0.64% | 830.64 | 7.31% |
| Job work | 118.98 | 0.76% | 224.60 | 1.98% |

Obsolescence work is the niche: reverse engineering and form-fit-function
replacements for legacy defence platforms (deck page 22). It is 6.53% of
revenue.

Government entities bought 7.02% of FY26 revenue. Non-government buyers took
92.98% (RHP page 155). Telangana took 98.19% (RHP page 155). The buyers are
private defence primes and integrators in Hyderabad. The RHP names Apollo
Micro Systems, Bit Mapper Integration, Linkwell Telesystems, SCI-COM Software
and Sigma Advanced Systems (RHP page 153).

**Archetype: outsourcing partner (EMS).** Client concentration, wallet share,
capacity fill and contract stickiness are the variables. Build-to-spec
component maker is the second reading.

## 3. Competitive advantages

Forming, and narrow. Defence qualification is real friction. The company is
EN 9100 certified and an approved supplier to BEL and HAL (deck pages 7
and 10). Repeat customers were 86.08% in FY26, 68 names (RHP page 159).

Against that, the RHP rates customer bargaining power "High" (RHP page 149).
Top 10 customers took 89.36% of FY26 revenue (RHP page 276). A subcontractor
to a prime holds a qualification, not pricing power. The deck's margin story,
"Direct engagement with defence PSUs reduces intermediary dependence" (deck
page 25), sits beside a 7.02% government share. That claim does not match the
revenue split. Moat: forming in obsolescence, absent in build to print.

## 4. The promoters

The Dovari family runs it. Dovari Amarnath, IIT Madras, is Managing Director.
Dovari Yesudas is Chairman. Darsy Kethan Chandra is Executive Director and
CFO with "over 3 years of experience" (deck page 15). Promoters held 62.28%
at 30-Jun-2026 (deck page 31). Pledge NOT FOUND.

Related-party money moves both ways. In FY25 the company advanced Rs 5.55
crore to the MD and recovered Rs 5.71 crore (RHP page 59). A Rs 4.25 crore
capital advance to the MD sat on the FY24 balance sheet (RHP page 61). At
31-Mar-2026, **Rs 4.84 crore is outstanding as an advance to Sunrise Telecom,
a director's proprietorship**, which also sold the company Rs 2.29 crore of
goods in FY26 (RHP pages 59 and 60). Net worth before the IPO was Rs 53.13
crore (RHP page 55). The advance is 9% of it.

**Classification: margin-led, cash-negative.** Profit grows faster than
revenue. Cash goes the other way. The RHP blames growth: "Sustaining this
growth required proportionate expansio[n]" of working capital (RHP page 35).
That reading and a slow-paying customer reading both fit the numbers.

## 6. The transition and the quality ladder

FROM R2, a cost-competitive contract assembler doing job work. TO R3, a
spec'd supplier to defence programmes with obsolescence engineering and
system design. The mechanism is turnkey scope plus qualification.

Management's claim, quoted once: "Invest in R&D to evolve from component-level
work to complete system design and manufacturing" (deck page 25).

The margin moved. The cash did not. An R3 business earns spec rents in cash.
This one earns them in inventory. Record the ladder reading as **R2 moving
toward R3 on margin, unproven on cash.**

## 8. Proof check

No post-listing quarter or half-year exists in the corpus. The proof test
cannot run. The FY26 annual numbers prove the margin claim. They disprove
the cash claim.

The order book is a warning, not a proof. At 30-Jun-2026 it was Rs 98.06

## 11. Posture: the Transition Decision Matrix

- **Proof gate: CONTESTED.** FIRED on margin, 7.82% to 17.42% EBITDA in two
  years. NOT FIRED on cash conversion.
- **Ugliness: ARTIFACT-OF-CLIMB, provisionally.** Turnkey work means buying
  the bill of material, so inventory rises. The alternative reading is a
  concentrated buyer stretching payment. One observation separates them:
  receivable and inventory days in H1 FY27.
- **Recognition gap: CLOSED.** Price basis, not a corpus document: Bull AI
  company record, market capitalisation Rs 765.40 crore. On FY26 PAT of
  Rs 16.10 crore that is 47.5 times trailing. The R3 neighbourhood is about
  19 times.

**Posture: EARNINGS-ONLY if the proof gate holds; PRICED NARRATIVE (TRAP) if
it does not.** Returns ride EPS growth alone either way.

## 12. Verdict card

**WATCH.**

The margin inflection is real and filed. The cash, the concentration and the
related-party advances are also real and filed. The market already pays a
full multiple. A `/step1` now would work from a prospectus and nothing else.
Wait for the first post-listing result.

**Load-bearing facts a `/step1` must verify first**
1. Identity of Client 5 and its share of FY26 revenue.
2. H1 FY27 operating cash flow, inventory days and receivable days.
3. Settlement of the Rs 4.84 crore Sunrise Telecom advance.
4. Listing platform, issue price and reporting cadence.
5. Margin by line: obsolescence against turnkey.

**What would change the view**
- Positive operating cash flow in H1 FY27 with margin held moves this to
  PROCEED.
- A falling order book or a rise in related-party balances moves it to PASS.

No Role 1 valuation was run and no target price is set.
