## SECTION 1: MARKET DEFINITION

### 1A. Precise boundaries

- **Product scope**: unsecured personal loans (PL) and loan against
  property (LAP, secured). Excludes gold loans, business loans, education
  loans, auto loans, loan-against-mutual-funds — all named in management's
  own 5-year product roadmap (Concall_Aug_2026 p.15-16) as FUTURE, not
  current, product lines, and therefore excluded from this TAM.
- **Geographic scope**: India, pan-national in principle, but the served
  base skews to top-50 cities (63.38% of active customers, RHP p.183) and
  to internet/smartphone-reachable population (internet penetration 55%
  in 2025, RHP p.148).
- **Customer scope**: mass-market and mass-affluent households, defined
  by the 1Lattice/RHP income tiers as Rs 0.5-1.5 million annual household
  income (RHP p.153); Kissht's own disclosed customer base skews to the
  lower end of that band and below (67.65% of customers earn Rs 25,000-
  75,000/month = Rs 3-9 lakh/year, RHP p.183, i.e. straddling the "mass
  market" and "lower segment" tiers as 1Lattice defines them). Median
  CIBIL 746, average age 32 (RHP p.183).
- **Channel scope**: 100% digital PL sourcing; LAP through 101 branches
  across 8 states/UTs (press release p.2). This is the single most
  important boundary: it excludes the large share of PL+LAP originated
  through bank branches, DSA networks and traditional NBFC physical
  channels, which is most of the nationwide PL+LAP market.
- **Price segment**: small-ticket. Kissht's average PL ticket size was
  Rs 25,556.79 in 9M FY26 (RHP p.180, down from Rs 31,808.05 FY25 — note
  this is a DECLINE, consistent with the stated "high-quality, lower-
  yield customer" strategy shift, B04/05). LAP ticket size is disclosed
  inconsistently across three filed documents (up to Rs 30 lakh/15yr AR
  p.9; Rs 15 lakh/10yr IP1 p.11; Rs 20 lakh/10yr CRISIL) — B04
  FLAG-DISCLOSURE-INCONSISTENCY, unresolved in this container, carried
  forward as a caveat on the LAP portion of this sizing.
- **Explicit exclusions**: home loans, auto loans, gold loans, education
  loans, credit cards, microfinance, business loans, corporate/wholesale
  credit, and any lending to the "upper segment" (>Rs 1.5 million
  household income) that Kissht does not target and that 1Lattice itself
  says is "predominantly served by private and foreign banks" (RHP
  p.159).

## SECTION 2: TAM ESTIMATION, MULTIPLE METHODS

**TAM definition used below**: the full nationwide PL + LAP market
(all lenders — banks, HFCs, NBFCs, fintechs; all channels; all customer
income segments), NOT narrowed to mass-market or to digital origination.
This is the broadest boundary consistent with Kissht's PRODUCT scope
(1A); the mass-market and digital-channel narrowing happens at the SAM
step (Section 3), where it belongs.

### Method 1 — Top-down (1Lattice/RHP, issuer-sourced)

- India personal loan (PL) market: Rs 14.7 T (FY25) -> Rs 36.5-39.6 T
  (FY30P), ~18% CAGR FY20-25, ~20-22% CAGR FY25-30P (RHP p.163, AR p.32,
  identical figures in both documents).
- India LAP market: Rs 12.3 T (FY25) -> Rs 30.6-33.2 T (FY30P), 18% CAGR
  FY20-25, ~22% CAGR FY25-30P (RHP p.163/167, AR p.32).
- **TAM (PL+LAP) FY25 = Rs 14.7T + Rs 12.3T = Rs 27.0 T = Rs 27,00,000
  Cr.** FY30P (using low end of each guided range, conservative) =
  Rs 36.5T + Rs 30.6T = Rs 67.1 T = Rs 67,10,000 Cr; FY30P (midpoint of
  each range, realistic) = Rs 38.05T + Rs 31.9T = Rs 69.95 T ≈
  Rs 70,00,000 Cr.
- **No subtraction needed at this step** — TAM is already defined at the
  PL+LAP product level; the customer-segment and channel subtractions
  happen in SAM (Section 3), per the 1A boundary decision above.
- Confidence: **M** (issuer-commissioned source, but see Method 5
  independent cross-check below, which corroborates the PL leg).
  Staleness: 1Lattice report dated 9-Apr-2026, run date 19-Sep-2026 — 5
  months old, NOT STALE.

### Method 2 — Bottom-up (unit economics, partially derivative)

Addressable unit = one mass-market PL/LAP borrower. RHP discloses
mass-market population reaching 572 million by FY30P, with the
mass-market share of households rising from 31% (FY25) to 37% (FY30P)
(RHP p.146). Back-solving proportionally, FY25 mass-market population ≈
572M x (31%/37%) ≈ 479 million (ASSUMPTION: population share scales
proportionally with household share — not independently verified).

Dividing the RHP's own mass-market PL+LAP stock (Rs 11.6T FY25, Section
1B funnel) by this population gives an implied per-capita mass-market
PL+LAP exposure of **Rs 24,217 per person** (FY25) — strikingly close to
Kissht's own average PL ticket size of Rs 25,557-31,808 over the same
period (RHP p.180). This is not an independent number (it derives from
the same 1Lattice source), but it is an internal consistency check: it
implies roughly one outstanding PL/LAP-sized exposure per mass-market
adult on average, which is a plausible order of magnitude given India's
still-low retail credit penetration (household credit-to-GDP 45.56% vs
USA 68.0%, RHP p.159). Scaling forward to FY30P (Rs 33.4T / 572M) gives
Rs 58,392 per capita — a 2.4x rise, consistent with the RHP's own
observation that rising average ticket size (not just borrower count) is
a stated driver of mass-market credit growth (RHP p.160). **Confidence:
L** (derivative, not independent; shown for internal-consistency
purposes only, not counted separately in the triangulation table below).

Against this, the 1Lattice-modelled "New Age/Digital Lender" segment
size is Rs 60,000 Cr (Rs 0.6T) FY25 (Section 1B). The five NAMED peers

independent triangulation**: a bottom-up sum of five peers' own filed
AUM numbers lands within one-fifth of the top-down modelled segment
size, with the residual (~20%) being a plausible "other digital NBFC
lenders" share — well below the 30-60% unorganised-sector share typical

already the organised, RBI-regulated NBFC-ML space, not an informal
market. **Confidence: M-H** (five of five inputs independently
sourced per-peer, converges with the top-down figure). This method
validates SAM (Section 3), not TAM directly, since the "new-age
lender" bucket is narrower than the full PL+LAP TAM.

### Method 4 — Import substitution

**NOT APPLICABLE.** Domestic lending business; no import/export or
domestic-production dynamic exists for a credit product.

### Method 5 — Global benchmark (partially independent)

- India credit-to-GDP ~97% (Q3-2025) vs USA ~140%, China ~201% (RHP
  p.150-151). India household credit-to-GDP 45.56% (Q3-2025) vs USA
  68.0%, UK 73.9% (RHP p.159). If India's household credit-to-GDP closed
  half the gap to the USA level (a structural ceiling, no date attached),
  mass-market PL+LAP (currently Rs 11.6T) could scale by roughly
  (68.0/45.56 =) 1.49x on leverage alone, before accounting for
  population/income growth — implying a STRUCTURAL ceiling near Rs 17.3T
  for the mass-market PL+LAP slice, independent of the FY30P calendar
  date. This corroborates DIRECTION (large headroom) without adding a
  new absolute number; it is a sense-check, not a fourth independent
  estimate, since the underlying ratios are themselves RHP-sourced.
- **Genuinely independent cross-check (web search, this stage)**: four
  market-research aggregators (IMARC, Research and Markets, Ken Research,
  TechSci) size India's PERSONAL LOAN market (not PL+LAP; PL only) at
  USD 157-174 billion for 2025, i.e. **Rs 13.0-14.4 lakh crore** at
  ~Rs 83/USD — this converges within 2-13% of the RHP/1Lattice PL figure
  of Rs 14.7 lakh crore FY25 (RHP p.163). No independently-sourced LAP-
  only market-size estimate was found in this search round (see Search
  Log, skipped). **This is the strongest single piece of evidence that
  the issuer-commissioned 1Lattice PL number is not inflated relative to
  independent research**, though it does not verify the LAP leg or the
  more granular mass-market/digital-lender funnel, both of which remain
  1Lattice-only.

### 3B. SOM at 3 and 5 years

**Current SAM share**: Kissht's most recent AUM (Rs 8,001 Cr, Jun-26)
against the interpolated FY27 SAM (Rs 1,29,400 Cr, the closest fiscal
year to the current quarter) = **6.2%**.

Share-gain trajectory applied (per the standard rules): Kissht sits 5th
of 5 named new-age peers by AUM (smallest of the group) but grew AUM at
79.53% CAGR FY23-25 — the FASTEST of all listed AND new-age peers in the
RHP's own comparison table (RHP p.179-180) — and has continued at 73%
YoY (FY26) and 61% YoY (Q1 FY27). This is genuine demonstrated execution,
which under the framework's rules could justify an AGGRESSIVE (3-5pp)
share-gain case. Per rule 6 (conservative bias: take the lower estimate
when choosing), this report uses a MODERATE trajectory — 2pp gain at 3
years (top of the "normal" 1-2pp band, not the aggressive band) and 3pp
at 5 years (bottom of the "aggressive" 3-5pp band) — explicitly flagging
that Kissht's own historical growth rate would support a faster case,
left as upside for stage 11 to weigh, not as this report's base case.

| Horizon | SAM share | SAM level (Rs Cr) | SOM (AUM, Rs Cr) | Implied revenue (@ ~40% yield, Rs Cr) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Current (FY27) | 6.2% | 1,29,400 | 8,001 (actual) | 2,209 (actual FY26 total income) |

**Implied AUM/revenue CAGR from current AUM (Rs 8,001 Cr, Jun-26)**:
- 3-year: (33,620/8,001)^(1/3) - 1 = **61.4%**
- 5-year: (63,747/8,001)^(1/5) - 1 = **51.4%**

This is the formal handoff to stage 11 (`som_implied_revenue_cagr`). It
sits ABOVE management's stated FY27 guidance floor (>40% AUM growth,
Concall_Jun_2026 p.8-9) and BELOW Kissht's own delivered pace over the
last two years (73% FY26, 79.53% FY23-25 CAGR) — a middle path that
requires guidance to hold as a floor, not a ceiling, for several years
running, and does not require Kissht to accelerate beyond what it has
already shown.

**Tension worth naming explicitly**: Kissht's own AUM CAGR (79.5%
historical, 61-73% recent) already runs well ahead of the SAM's own
46.9% growth rate, meaning Kissht has been TAKING SHARE, not merely
riding the market, even faster than this report's conservative 2-3pp
share-gain assumption implies over a multi-year compounding view. The
SOM figures above are therefore a deliberately conservative read of
Kissht's own demonstrated trajectory, consistent with rule 6, and stage
11 should treat them as a floor case, not a central case.

### 3C. Capacity cross-check

No manufacturing capex applies (B07 note). For a lender, "capacity" is
capital (net worth) and leverage headroom, not physical plant.

- Net worth Jun-26: Rs 2,245 Cr (+107% YoY, press release p.2). CRAR
  40.2%, Tier-1 39.2% (vs 15% RBI minimum). D/E 0.91x.
- Steady-state on-book leverage target, management's own long-run
  guidance: 2.5-3.0x debt-to-equity (Concall_Jun_2026 p.22).
- Pending capital event: Rs 832.20 Cr preferential issue to 34 investors
  at Rs 314.11/share, board-approved 17-Sep-2026, subject to shareholder
  approval at an EGM on **14-Oct-2026** (announcement
  20260917-acc1d37a-*.txt p.1-4) — NOT YET FINAL as of this run date.

**Arithmetic (3-year SOM, ~FY30)**: on-book AUM share has been falling
(53.6% off-book Jun-26, up from ~46.4% off-book a year earlier per B04);
assume it continues to ~60% off-book by FY30 (management's own stated
direction, capital-light growth), giving on-book AUM of ~40% x
Rs 33,620 Cr = Rs 13,448 Cr. At 3.0x leverage, this needs Rs 4,483 Cr of
net worth. Starting from Rs 2,245 Cr (Jun-26) + Rs 832 Cr (if the
preferential clears) = Rs 3,077 Cr, compounding at the guided 19-21% RoE
band (use 20%) for ~3.5 years to FY30: Rs 3,077 Cr x 1.20^3.5 ≈
**Rs 5,998 Cr** — comfortably above the Rs 4,483 Cr required, even
without further capital raises beyond the pending preferential.

**Arithmetic (5-year SOM, ~FY32)**: assuming a further off-book mix
shift to ~65%, on-book AUM = 35% x Rs 63,747 Cr = Rs 22,311 Cr, requiring
Rs 7,437 Cr net worth at 3.0x leverage. Compounding the same Rs 3,077 Cr
base at 20% RoE for ~5.5 years: Rs 3,077 Cr x 1.20^5.5 ≈ **Rs 9,188 Cr**
— still above the Rs 7,437 Cr required.

**capacity_check: SUFFICIENT, but CONDITIONAL.** The math clears only if
(a) the Rs 832 Cr preferential clears its 14-Oct-2026 EGM (not yet
approved as of this run date), and (b) RoE holds near the guided 19-21%
band for several years. Both are open items flagged by stage 5 (LBF-4,
the raise rationale was never explained on any call; and the disclosed
Cost-to-Income deterioration from 45.54% to 56.64% FY24-26, B04
FLAG-BUSINESS-MODEL, works directly against the RoE assumption if the
trend continues rather than reversing as management claims it will).
If Cost-to-Income keeps worsening, the SOM side of this equation is the
optimistic one, not the capital-capacity side — capital appears
sufficient; earnings quality is the binding constraint to watch.

### 5B. Runway assessment

- **Revenue headroom**: SAM (interpolated FY27, Rs 1,29,400 Cr AUM) at a
  ~40% revenue/AUM yield (RHP's own peer-benchmarking formula, p.180) =
  Rs 51,760 Cr of revenue potential, against current FY26 total income
  of Rs 2,209 Cr = **23.4x** headroom on a revenue basis. On a direct
  AUM basis (SAM Rs 1,29,400 Cr / current AUM Rs 8,001 Cr) = **16.2x**.
- **TAM growth rate**: ~20% CAGR (conservative case, FY25-30P, computed
  from the PL+LAP totals in Section 2); realistic case ~22%.
- **SAM growth rate**: 46.9% CAGR (FY25-30P, the "new-age/digital
  lender" segment).
- **Company CAGR vs TAM/SAM**: Kissht's own AUM CAGR (79.53% FY23-25,
  73% FY26, 61% Q1 FY27) runs well ahead of BOTH the TAM (~20-22%) and
  the SAM (~47%) growth rates — Kissht is gaining share, not merely
  riding the market, and has been doing so faster than this report's own
  conservative SOM share-gain assumption (Section 3B).
- **Years to saturate SAM at current growth**: a mechanical extrapolation
  (company AUM growing ~60%, SAM growing ~47%, from FY25 bases) implies
  roughly 30+ years to full SAM capture — a number this report treats as
  a stylised sense-check only (both growth rates will certainly decelerate
  long before then), not a forecast. The direction it confirms is that
  SOM is nowhere near saturating SAM within any planning horizon relevant
  to a 3-5 year hold.

### 5C. Runway classification

**MASSIVE.** Revenue/AUM headroom of 16-23x against a SAM growing at
~47% CAGR, inside a TAM growing at ~20-22% CAGR, with the company itself
outgrowing both, and SOM projections capturing under 10% of SAM even at
the 5-year mark. The binding constraints on Kissht realising this runway
are NOT market-size constraints — they are the earnings-quality and
capital-approval conditionals named in Section 3C and the asset-quality
tripwire (GNPA direction) named by stage 5.

## ANALYST NOTE

The market-size story here is not the risk. TAM is large (Rs 26-27 lakh
Cr), SAM is growing fast (47% CAGR) off a small base (2.3% of TAM), and
Kissht has grown faster than both for three straight years. The genuine
independent cross-check found in this search round — four separate
market-research aggregators sizing India's personal loan market within
2-13% of the issuer-commissioned 1Lattice figure — meaningfully de-risks
the "is the TAM inflated" question on the PL leg specifically. What
remains unverified is the LAP leg and the granular mass-market/digital-
lender funnel, both 1Lattice-only, and the entire SOM capacity math in
Section 3C is conditional on two unresolved items already flagged by
stage 5: the pending Rs 832 Cr preferential clearing its 14-Oct-2026 EGM,
and the Cost-to-Income trend reversing rather than continuing its FY24-26
deterioration. Stage 11 should treat the 51-61% SOM-implied CAGR as a
believable middle case bounded by management's own >40% guidance floor
and the company's own 73-79% delivered pace, not as either the ceiling
or the floor.
