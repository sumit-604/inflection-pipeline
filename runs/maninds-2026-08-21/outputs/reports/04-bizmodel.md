## SECTION 1: THE BUSINESS MODEL IN PLAIN ENGLISH

### 1A. One-line description
MANINDS buys steel plate and coil, welds and coats it into large-diameter
line pipe, and sells it against confirmed purchase orders to oil-and-gas,
water and infrastructure buyers in India and 30+ export countries (AR p.3,
p.6); a real-estate monetisation of one non-core Mumbai land parcel and a
new stainless-steel line ride alongside the core pipe business (AR p.9,
p.247-248).

### 1B. Money flow chain, by revenue stream

**Stream 1: Steel line pipe (LSAW/HSAW/ERW), manufacturing and trading —
core business**
[Steel plate/coil, imported and domestic] → [MIIL cuts, welds (LSAW/HSAW/ERW
process), pressure-tests, and coats the pipe with 3LPE/FBE anti-corrosion
coating at Anjar, Gujarat and Pithampur, MP (AR p.4, p.6)] → [delivers
finished line pipe to oil & gas majors, EPC contractors, water utilities]
→ [payer: GAIL, IOCL, HPCL, BPCL, ONGC, Reliance, Adani, Shell, Kinder
Morgan, Kuwait Oil Company and others (AR p.3, p.6)] → [pays 0-60 days
from delivery per contract terms, revenue recognised at point of shipment/
delivery, not percentage-of-completion (AR p.203-204, Note 3.09)].

**Stream 2: Real estate monetisation (Merino Shelters, non-core, wholly
owned subsidiary)**
[6-acre land parcel opposite D.Y. Patil Stadium, Navi Mumbai (Inv. Pres.
p.16)] → [MIIL signed a Joint Development Agreement (JDA) with Paradise
Green-Spaces LLP; MIIL contributes land, developer builds] → [delivers
MIIL's 30% share of developed carpet area (~450,000 sq ft) plus an upfront
cash payment] → [payer: developer JDA partner and eventual flat/unit
buyers] → [MIIL received ₹700 million upfront in Q4FY25 (AR p.9) and

consolidated accounts already recognised ₹368.60 Cr (₹36,860.27 lakh) of
Real Estate segment revenue (AR p.247, Note 44A), well above the ₹70 Cr
cash actually received that year — a revenue-versus-cash timing gap worth
tracking].

**Stream 3 (nascent, not yet revenue-generating in FY25): Stainless-steel
seamless pipe, Jammu**
[Nickel/molybdenum alloy raw material] → [new 20,000-22,000 MTPA greenfield
line for SS mother pipe and pilgered pipe, targeting chemical, defence,
marine, nuclear, power and refinery buyers (AR p.8; Inv. Pres. p.16)] →
[capex incurred ₹350 Cr of a planned ₹600 Cr as of Q1FY27, production
targeted March 2027 (Inv. Pres. p.16)] → not yet a revenue stream in the
FY25 AR period.

**Stream 4 (post-AR period, not in FY25 numbers): National Pipe Company
(NPC), Saudi Arabia**
Acquired 21 May 2026 for ~₹960 Cr (US$102mn: US$70mn debt + US$32mn
equity), adding 430,000 MTPA of HSAW+LSAW capacity, an Aramco-approved
vendor status held since 2005, and a US$120mn opening order book (Inv.
Pres. p.9-12). This sits entirely outside the FY25 AR (which is the
primary source for this report) and is flagged as a forward structural
change, not evidenced in the audited FY25 numbers.

### 1E. The chai-stall-uncle version
Think of MIIL like a tailor who buys cloth and stitches suits only when a
customer places a firm order, not suits made to sell off a shelf. The
"cloth" here is steel plate and coil, and the "suit" is a large steel pipe
that carries oil, gas or water over long distances. The tailor's profit
comes mostly from the stitching charge (the conversion spread), because
the cloth itself is nearly three-quarters of the bill. Big buyers like
government oil companies place large, lumpy orders, so business comes in
bursts, not a steady drip. Alongside the tailoring, the family also owns
one old piece of land in Navi Mumbai that it is slowly cashing in with a
builder partner — useful extra money, but not the main shop. And now the
tailor has bought an established stitching shop in Saudi Arabia that
already has the right customer permits, instead of building a new one
from scratch.

### 2D. Industry lifecycle stage
Global large-diameter line pipe demand sits in a structurally growing
phase tied to energy security, water infrastructure and industrial
diversification programmes, not a mature/declining one: global oil & gas
capex is guided from $680B to $831B (2026-2031, 4.1% CAGR), GCC
desalination investment of $100B over five years (+37% capacity), and
India's Jal Jeevan Mission 2.0 outlay of ₹8.69 lakh Cr through Dec 2028
(Inv. Pres. p.14-15). Within this growing industry, MIIL is a mid-scale
established India-based producer that is now moving from a single-country
manufacturer to a two-country (India + Saudi, post-acquisition) platform,
i.e., an expansion/consolidation stage for the company even as the
end-market itself is in growth (Inv. Pres. p.7-11).

## SECTION 3: FINANCIAL METRICS THAT MATTER FOR THIS BUSINESS MODEL

### 3A. Ignore-these, track-these

| Commonly tracked ratio | Why misleading/irrelevant for MIIL |
|---|---|
| Headline gross margin % in isolation | Swings with steel input prices, not with operational efficiency; a margin move can be a raw-material price move, not a demand or execution signal |
| Trailing P/E on reported (blended) PAT | FY25 consolidated PAT includes a real-estate segment profit of ₹44.26 Cr from a one-off land monetisation (AR p.247, Note 44B) sitting alongside the steel segment's ₹211.91 Cr; blending the two overstates the recurring earnings power of the core pipe business |
| Same-period revenue growth rate (YoY, single quarter) | Order-book business recognises revenue on shipment; quarterly revenue swung from ₹774 Cr to ₹1,166 Cr across FY26 (Inv. Pres. p.18) purely on shipment timing, not underlying demand change |
| Dividend yield / payout ratio as a value signal | Company is in a debt-funded capacity-expansion phase (NPC ~₹960 Cr, Jammu ~₹600 Cr) — capital allocation priority is growth capex, not distribution |
| Standard receivable-days benchmark taken at face value | Stated payment terms are 0-60 days from delivery (AR p.203, Note 3.09), but actual standalone trade receivables turnover fell from 7.02x to 3.17x in one year (AR p.176) — the terms-on-paper and the actual collection experience have diverged; use the actual turnover, not the stated policy |
| Return on equity/return on assets without adjusting for the treasury equity portfolio | MIIL carries a sizeable quoted-equity investment book (multiple listed names, AR p.145-146) that is unrelated to the core pipe business and can distort ROE/ROA if not stripped out |

## SECTION 4: RISKS, VALUATION APPROACH & MONITORING

### 4A. Business-model-specific risks

| Category | Risk | First line item that would deteriorate (quarterly monitoring signal) |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue model | Order book fails to convert / bid pipeline win rate disappoints | Order inflow and order book (₹ Cr) reported at quarterly results falling sequentially without a matching revenue recognition |
| Margin | Steel input price rises faster than order pricing can absorb, or export freight/duty costs spike | EBITDA margin (%) sequential compression, and cost of materials consumed/revenue ratio rising |
| Balance sheet | Working capital build (inventory + receivables) outpaces revenue and is funded by rising short-term debt | Inventory turnover and trade receivables turnover (Note 40/41 style ratios) falling further, alongside short-term borrowings rising |
| Execution | NPC (Saudi), Jammu, or Dammam coating-plant integration/ramp slips versus the guided timelines (Dammam and Jammu both targeted March 2027) | Capex-incurred-versus-plan disclosure and stated production-start date slipping in subsequent investor presentations |
| Structural | Export-market disruption (Strait of Hormuz, trade protectionism) or loss of an approved-vendor status | FOB export earnings (₹ Cr) falling YoY in the Directors' Report foreign-exchange annexure, or export share of segment revenue dropping sharply |

### 4B. Valuation method applicability — handoff to Role 1

| Method | Applicability | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| EV/EBITDA | **PRIMARY** | Neutralises the capital-structure shift from the debt-funded NPC acquisition and rising working-capital debt; standard cross-cycle metric for a capital/commodity-linked manufacturer whose PAT is thin and volatile relative to EBITDA (FY25 PAT margin 4.3% consol vs EBITDA margin 10.1%, Inv. ### 4C. Quarterly monitoring checklist (10-15 items)

1. Order book (₹ Cr) — good: flat-to-rising sequentially; trouble: falling two quarters running
2. Bid pipeline (₹ Cr) and disclosed win-rate commentary, if any — good: pipeline growing with cited conversions; trouble: pipeline static or undisclosed win rate
3. Revenue from operations, standalone and consolidated — good: growth consistent with order-book conversion; trouble: sharp unexplained miss vs guided order execution
4. EBITDA margin (%) — good: stable-to-rising; trouble: sequential compression >200bps
5. Cost of materials consumed / revenue — good: stable or falling; trouble: rising alongside falling EBITDA margin (spread squeeze, not pass-through)
6. Inventory turnover (x) — good: stable or improving; trouble: further deterioration from the FY25 level (4.37x standalone)
7. Trade receivables turnover (x) — good: stable or improving toward the stated 0-60 day terms; trouble: further deterioration from 3.17x standalone
8. Debt-equity ratio — good: stable near 0.25-0.28x or declining; trouble: rising materially without a matching EBITDA increase
9. Export share of steel-segment revenue — good: stable or explained shifts; trouble: sharp, unexplained swing
10. FOB export earnings vs foreign exchange outgo — good: gap narrowing or explained by capex-linked imports; trouble: persistent unexplained widening
11. NPC integration milestones (contract retention, Aramco status continuity, order-book conversion) — good: on track per guidance; trouble: slippage or customer attrition disclosed
12. Jammu and Dammam capex-incurred vs plan, and stated production-start date — good: on track for March 2027; trouble: date pushed out
13. Merino Shelters JDA cash realisation (₹80-120 Cr/yr guided from FY28) — good: on schedule; trouble: developer delay or dispute disclosed
14. SEBI/MCA compounding matters (Notes 46-48) — good: resolved/no new notices; trouble: new regulatory action
15. Related-party and treasury equity portfolio size — good: stable, disclosed; trouble: material unexplained growth

### 4D. Highest-value questions for management

1. What is current capacity utilisation at Anjar and Pithampur individually, and what utilisation is assumed for NPC's 430,000 MTPA by FY27/28? Reassures: utilisation trending above 75-80%. Worries: utilisation stagnant below 60% while capex keeps rising.
