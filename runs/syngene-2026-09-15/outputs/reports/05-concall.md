**"No surprises yet" test (corrected item 7):** Deepak Jain told analysts on the Q2 FY26 call (06-Nov-2025) that the CDMO impact was "no surprises yet" and that the mid-single-digit guide reflected a "one-off inventory correction," with underlying growth "robust and strong" (p.7-8, p.12-13). Eleven weeks later, on the Q3 FY26 call (23-Jan-2026), guidance was cut from growth to a 3-5% decline, described as "more than expected impact" (p.9). Either the deterioration between early November and mid-January was genuinely unforeseeable at the time of the Q2 FY26 call, or the "no surprises" framing was already optimistic when it was said. The transcripts cannot resolve which reading is correct, but the 11-week gap between reassurance and cut is itself evidence against taking management's real-time reassurances at face value.

**Zoetis-absence attribution test (corrected item 2):** the Q4 FY26 "broadly flat" FY27 guide (30-Apr-2026) already built in "almost no Librela" for Q1-Q2 FY27 (Deepak Jain, p.6). Management's own explanation conflates a known factor with a new one.

**Pattern check:** Under Peter Bains (Q2-Q4 FY26 calls), the house style leaned external-blame — "single product," "geopolitical uncertainties," rupee volatility — with almost no proactive self-criticism; the closest exception is his own concession in Q3 FY26 that Mangalore utilization has "not yet" met expectations, offered under direct analyst pressure (Manoj Bahety), not proactively. Under Kiran Mazumdar-Shaw and Siddharth Mittal (Q1 FY27 call), the tone flips sharply to self-critical framing ("we have drifted," "complacent," "took our eye off the ball") within the very first call of the new team — genuine and unusual by Indian-listed-company standards, but arriving precisely coincident with a management change that has every incentive to reset the narrative, and containing two of its own internal contradictions (Bayview conversion vs. lock-in; FY28 growth aim vs. refusal to guide medium-term) in that same call.
