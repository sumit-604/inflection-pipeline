# INDNIPPON business narrative (Phase 1 lite, run 2026-09-10)

## Business understanding narrative

India Nippon Electricals makes the electrical parts that start a two wheeler's engine and run its electronics. The core product is the flywheel magneto and ignition system, which the company says it leads in India at about 2 million magnetos a quarter, and it now adds integrated starter generators (ISG), controllers, sensors and DC to DC converters. Each part is designed to one vehicle platform, qualified by the maker, and fitted on the assembly line, so a buyer cannot swap it out mid model without new testing and approval. Motorcycles and scooters take 85% of sales, three wheelers 6% and general purpose engines 9%, while replacement spares through 95 dealers bring in about 11% and exports about 8%. The customers are a few large vehicle makers: TVS Motor, Hero MotoCorp and Bajaj Auto are named, and two customers alone took 71% of FY26 revenue. They buy against platform nominations, pay on 45 to 90 day terms, and settle steel, aluminium and copper cost moves through a rate recovery formula. Demand today is two wheeler production, which the TVS Motor Company, Bajaj Auto and Hero MotoCorp volume signals and the SIAM monthly two wheeler data track directly. SIAM reports domestic two wheeler sales up 36% in FY26, a jump the annual report ties to the GST 2.0 rate cut, so GST Council and CBIC GST 2.0 rate slab stability is a live demand signal too. Demand should grow if more of each vehicle's value comes from electronics the company makes, but the company does not disclose any product revenue split, so the run could not measure that shift. Electric two wheeler adoption, tracked by the Vahan and FADA electric two wheeler registration data, raises spend per vehicle, yet peers say that spend goes to product lines built for electric vehicles, not to legacy ignition parts. The BorgWarner EFI ECU licensing status is the named route into fuel injection electronics, and it rests on investor deck claims the annual report does not back. China MOFCOM rare earth magnet export licensing status sits under both the magneto business and the newer motor lines, so it is a demand risk as well as a supply risk. The OEM ignition line carries a moderate qualification lock in moat, but two customer concentration caps how much that lock in is worth. The electronics line carries a moderate patent moat, with 29 patents granted, but no revenue is tied to it yet. Exports score a moderate China plus one benefit, and the aftermarket and general purpose engine lines show no moat the run could establish. The scan found no talent asymmetry and only a weak cannibalisation barrier around the TVS relationship, so no new moat is forming at the forward scan's threshold.

## Ignition leader growing, cash and margins unproven

India Nippon Electricals is a 41 year old TVS group company that sells ignition parts to Indian motorcycle and scooter makers from plants in Hosur, Puducherry and Rewari. FY26 revenue was Rs 1,068 Cr, up 27%, and reported profit was Rs 111 Cr, of which Rs 15 Cr before tax was a one time land compensation gain. The balance sheet carries no debt and a Rs 532 Cr investment book, 65% of net worth. Rs 264 Cr of that book is a stake in Lucas TVS, a sister company whose managing director also runs India Nippon.

The transition claim is a move from one ignition part to a fuller electronics system per vehicle. The evidence for it is real but thin. The company completed ISG validation and won new ISG and DC to DC converter business in Q1 FY27, and exports grew 162% to Rs 87 Cr. Against that, R&D spend was flat at about Rs 29 Cr in a year sales grew 27%, R&D capex fell 42%, and three product promises from the FY25 annual report were dropped or slipped. The drive assist controllers vanished from the record, the 3 kW motor controller is still in development, and the EFI licence turned into an in house build that no investor deck mentions.

The backward score is average. Gate 0 scores the company 54 out of 100 on the core test, corrected down from 56 after a verifier found one return on capital band misapplied. The low score has two causes. Cash generation scores 5 out of 20. Reported return on capital sits near 15% because the investment book swells the capital base, while the run's own calculation for the factory business alone puts it at 22% to 31% over FY24 to FY26. The margin story is in dispute. The investor decks show EBITDA margin rising from 11.27% to 11.44% in FY26. A verifier rebuilt the audited results filing and found 11.11% to 11.10%, flat to down, because the deck counts foreign exchange gains as operating income. That claim is not yet checked by the numbers verifier, and until it is, no FY26 margin is safe to use.

Cash is the sharpest concern. Operating cash flow fell to Rs 40 Cr against Rs 111 Cr of profit, 0.36 times, from 0.60 times in FY25. Receivables and inventory absorbed Rs 54 Cr. Receivables up to six months overdue rose from Rs 17 Cr to Rs 43 Cr, and the overdue share of the book doubled to 22%. The company booked no credit loss provision and added a new paragraph defending that choice in the same year. The growth reading has support: raw material stock rose 41% in a year the company says it built magnet inventory on purpose, and finished goods fell 9%. The structural reading has support too: the receivable from the parent, Lucas Indian Service, stretched from about 80 to about 108 days, and the one peer asked directly said its own receivable days had not changed. The filings do not split overdue receivables by customer, so the two readings cannot yet be separated.

What management says and what it files do not always match. The chairman's letter says working capital days fell from 42 to 40. The company's own decks show them rising from 40 to 42, and a calculation from the audited statements gives 51. The decks show return on capital of 35%, while the audited ratio note shows 17%, and no deck defines the 35%. The annual report says customer dependence is falling, yet two customer share rose from 67% in FY24 to 71% in FY26. The company holds no earnings calls, so no analyst has ever pressed management on any of this. The run grades credibility C. A verifier would grade it D. Both grades stand until the operator rules. On governance, the promoter verdict is CAUTION, set by the Lucas TVS interlock and an unexplained Rs 31 Cr apprentice stipend paid to a group trust. That verdict rests on a partial search, and the promoter pledge position is still unknown.

Peers confirm the demand and complicate the rest. Varroc, Pricol and Minda Corp all report two wheeler growth of 22% to 23% in the June 2026 quarter, above the 20% industry figure India Nippon uses for its 35.5% growth claim. In FY26 as a whole the industry grew 36% and India Nippon grew 27%, so the company lagged its own benchmark. All three peers quantify how fast they recover magnet and metal costs, one quarter to six months. They also put rupee values on content per vehicle and name each capex project. India Nippon does none of this. Varroc's statement that its receivable days did not change removes the easy industry wide excuse for India Nippon's overdue build.

The market opportunity looks narrow on today's products. The run sizes the Indian two and three wheeler electrics market India Nippon can serve at about Rs 1,122 Cr. The company already holds about 87% of it. That leaves room for revenue to grow only 1.14 times, a limited runway, and implies about 5% growth a year. That sits far below the 27% and 35.5% the company just delivered. Either the served market is wider than the run could measure, through new makers, electric vehicle parts and exports, or FY26 was a one time lift from the GST cut. The electric vehicle content per vehicle number that would settle this was not found, because web search failed for the whole market sizing stage.
