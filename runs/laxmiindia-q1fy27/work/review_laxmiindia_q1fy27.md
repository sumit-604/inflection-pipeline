**Six mandatory diagnostics:**
1. **Did NII grow YoY?** Yes, +38.97% (33.86 → 47.06). This outpaces interest expense (+15.48%), the signature of a re-priced, better-funded book: COB fell 67bps (11.33% → 10.66%) while yield rose (21.77% → 21.89%), lifting spread to 11.01% and NIM to 11.36%. AUM grew +27.9% and own book +31.7%, so NII growth (39%) ran ahead of book growth — genuine spread expansion, not just volume. No company guidance base to compare (new name); management's own medium-term target is ~30% AUM CAGR (Slide 19).
2. **Did the operating margin expand YoY?** Yes. Cost-to-Income 60.7% (Q1 FY26) → 53.9% (Q1 FY27) = -6.8pp; PPOP margin on NII+OI rose correspondingly. Operating leverage is real: opex grew +33.85% vs total income +50.73%.
3. **Did core operating PBT grow YoY?** Yes, +71.6% (12.77 → 21.91). Because there are no exceptional items (res VII = V), core operating PBT tracks reported PBT almost exactly. **Headline growth is real, not treasury-driven** — but see diagnostic 6 on Other Income.
4. **What drove the gap between core-PBT growth and reported-PAT growth?** Very small gap (71.6% core PBT vs 70.2% PAT incl OCI). PAT growth is fractionally slower because (a) tax rose +78.49% (ETR 23.42% → 24.37%, a 95bps ETR step-up) and (b) OCI swung slightly more negative (-0.12 → -0.14). Each delta is minor; PAT growth is operationally supported.
5. **Are finance costs scaling faster than the book?** No — finance costs +15.48% against AUM +27.9% and own book +31.7%. Finance cost is scaling **slower** than assets, because the post-IPO equity raise (net worth +79.81%) de-levered the book (D/E 2.87 → 3.10, still well below FY26's 4.13). This is the under-leverage story (F6-a): cheap equity is funding growth, which flatters COB and NIM now but caps ROE.
6. **Is Other Income concentration changing?** Yes, and it warrants flagging. Other/Fee income jumped +184.29% (2.98 → 8.48) and now equals 15.3% of net total income (8.48/55.54) vs 8.1% a year ago. This did **not** drive the PAT beat (core PBT grew 71.6% on its own), but the source and recurrence of the 8.48 Cr is undisclosed and is a management question (Q… fee/co-lending income mix). Stripping Other Income back to the prior 2.98 Cr, PBT would still be ~16.40 Cr, up ~29% YoY — the operating story survives the strip.

## PLAIN-LANGUAGE BRIEF (mandatory final narrative section)

### 1. Summary narrative

Laxmi India Finance grew net interest income 39% to Rs 47.06 Cr this quarter versus Rs 33.86 Cr a year ago, and profit after tax rose 70% to Rs 16.43 Cr from Rs 9.65 Cr. Assets under management reached Rs 1,721.74 Cr, up 28% year on year, and the own loan book grew 32% to Rs 1,626.90 Cr. The lender earned a 3.45% return on assets and a 13.86% return on net worth, both healthy for a small NBFC. Cost of borrowing fell 67 basis points to 10.66%, spreads widened to 11.01%, and the cost-to-income ratio improved to 54% from 61%. So the core engine ran well and the profit growth is real, not a one-off.

Two things temper the quarter. First, bad loans rose: gross NPA went from 1.28% to 2.08% and net NPA from 0.67% to 0.93%, and provisions nearly doubled. Last year the company sold Rs 27.93 Cr of stressed loans to an asset reconstruction company, which flattered the headline; this quarter there was no such sale, so the NPA rise is the book showing its own hand. Second, the balance sheet is under-levered after the August 2025 IPO (debt-to-equity 3.10 versus 4.13 a year earlier), which means return on equity is capped near management's own 13.5-14.0% target rather than climbing. Sequentially the quarter stepped down from a seasonally strong Q4, which is normal for this industry.

The filing also carries housekeeping flags: earnings per share is printed as Rs 3.07 in one table and Rs 3.17 in another, the auditor's cover certificate calls a limited review an "audit," and the co-lending note contradicts itself. None is fatal, but together they say the reporting controls are still maturing. This is a new name with no prior thesis, no Decision Status, and no valuation set here. The recommendation is narrow: do not take a position, do flag the name for promotion into the full pipeline, and resolve the open questions at the Annual Report and the 16 September 2026 AGM. Decision Status stays unset.

### 2. Sector intelligence

Laxmi India is a small NBFC in India's secured MSME, vehicle and mortgage lending space, regulated by the RBI and classified in the Middle Layer (NBFC-ML) (this quarter's filing, Note 4). The structural tailwind is credit demand from under-banked, semi-urban and rural borrowers in Tier 2 and Tier 3 towns, where 37% of the company's customers are first-time borrowers (this quarter's presentation, Slide 34). The sector's swing factor is asset quality through the cycle: a rising gross NPA (1.28% to 2.08%) and provisions doubling (this quarter's presentation, Slide 12) are the classic small-NBFC pressure point, especially where an ARC sale masked the prior year. The funding cycle is currently favourable for well-capitalised NBFCs, visible here as a 67 basis-point fall in cost of borrowing and a rating upgrade from Acuité to "A / Stable" (this quarter's presentation, Slide 6). There is no prior Notion or peer work for this new name, so sector positioning against listed peers (for example Five-Star, SBFC, or vehicle-finance NBFCs) is not yet built and would need external verification before any relative call.

### 3. Business-model intelligence

The company lends through 184 branches across six states, anchored in Rajasthan (92 branches), with a hub-and-spoke, relationship-manager-led model where the branch sources, underwrites and collects (this quarter's presentation, Slides 22-24). It makes money on spread: it borrows at 10.66% and lends at a 21.89% portfolio yield, keeping an 11.01% spread and an 11.36% net interest margin (this quarter's presentation, Slide 9). Roughly 28% of the portfolio is funded by net worth and internal accruals (this quarter's presentation, Slide 25), which is why margins look strong but return on equity is capped while the book is under-levered. The model is drifting in one important way: a company built on secured MSME and vehicle loans is now scaling an unsecured Personal Loan vertical (ticket up to Rs 200 lakh) and a wholesale on-lending book, both started from nil a couple of years ago (this quarter's presentation, Slides 35-36). Unsecured growth lifts yield but raises future credit cost, so the mix shift is the model risk to watch. Two capital-light levers, co-lending and loan assignment, are set up on paper but ran at zero this quarter (this quarter's filing, Notes 8.1-8.4 and 11). No prior peer benchmark exists for unit economics; the return figures here are unaudited management numbers.

### 4. Competition intelligence

Where Laxmi India wins: a dense branch-embedded model in under-served semi-urban markets that competitors find costly to replicate, a diversified funding base of 47-plus named lenders across PSU banks, private banks, small finance banks and NBFCs (this quarter's presentation, Slides 43-45), strong capitalisation at 25.32% CRAR after the IPO, and a fresh rating upgrade to "A" (this quarter's presentation, Slides 6 and 11). Where it is structurally weaker: it is sub-scale at Rs 1,722 Cr AUM against larger secured-MSME and vehicle-finance NBFCs, its return on equity (13.86%) trails higher-return peers partly because it is over-capitalised for now, and its asset quality is deteriorating just as it pushes into unsecured lending where established consumer-credit players have deeper data and collections. The competitive risk to watch is margin and credit-cost pressure if larger, cheaper-funded lenders enter its semi-urban strongholds while its own unsecured book seasons. All competitive claims here, including "zero repayment delays since inception" to its lenders (this quarter's presentation, Slide 20), are management assertions from this quarter's documents; there is no prior Notion or peer work to corroborate them, and they would need external verification (rating rationales, peer disclosures) before being relied on.
