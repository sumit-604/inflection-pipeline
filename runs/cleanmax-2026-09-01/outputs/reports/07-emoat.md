### FAMILY B — SUPPLY CHAIN

**B1 backward integration and infrastructure security — STRONG, 📄.**
CleanMax secures grid evacuation capacity years ahead of contracted demand, the renewable-IPP equivalent of backward integration/RM security. As of 30-Jun-2026: Evacuation Available = 3,424 MW (STU) + 677 MW (CTU) = 4,101 MW; Evacuation Applied (not yet firm) = a further 2,668 MW (STU) + 1,745 MW (CTU) = 4,413 MW. Combined evacuation pipeline of ~8.5 GW against a contracted portfolio of 6.8 GW (📄 Inv. Pres. p.35). Land: >=80% secured for the FY27 pipeline as of Feb-2026, structurally never 100% at any point given fragmented farmer landholdings (~4 acres/farmer), but the reported trajectory (70-80%→100% by Sep-2026) is management's own commitment (🎙️ Mar-2026 call p.16-17; this milestone dropped from later calls per B05, a genuine follow-through gap). CTU connectivity in particular is a multi-year, capacity-constrained national-grid resource — a genuine structural lock-in candidate, not merely operational discipline.

**B2 qualification lock-in (adapted: customer-equity structural lock-in) — STRONG, 📄.**
The Group Captive model requires the customer to invest a minimum 26% equity stake in the project SPV and consume 51%+ of its power, per Electricity Act rules (📄 Inv. Pres. glossary p.36). CleanMax runs "nearly 100 SPVs with client equity in them" (🎙️ Kuldeep Jain, Aug-2026 call p.16) and 44% of operational RE Power Sales capacity by MW sits in the STU-Group Captive structure (📄 Inv. Pres. p.33). Once a customer has committed equity to a specific SPV, unwinding is costly and slow — this is a genuine, contractually documented switching-cost mechanism, arguably stronger than a certification-based lock-in because it ties customer capital directly to the asset. Related-party transaction resolutions for named Group Captive SPVs (Ajanta, Terra, Vayu, Ahhope, Astria, BIAL, Como, Eliora — AR pp.2-8) confirm the structure is executed at scale, not a slide-deck description.

**B3 supply chain network effect** — NO EVIDENCE FOUND. CleanMax is not a platform connecting fragmented suppliers/buyers.

**E1 geographic first-mover — MODERATE, 📄.**

## SECTION 4: REGULATORY & POLICY TAILWINDS (Category R1)

**4A. Regulatory approvals in pipeline**: CTU/STU evacuation approvals (see B1) are the binding regulatory approval in this business; ~4.4 GW is "applied for, not yet firm" as of Jun-2026 (📄 Inv. Pres. p.35). No competitor-specific approval-holder data found (input gap, consistent with B01's noted peer-data gaps).

**4B. Government policy tailwinds**:
- GST on renewable energy devices cut from 12% to 5% in September 2025, lowering landed developer/consumer costs (📄 AR p.141). Industry-wide, shared by all competitors.
- PLI-supported domestic solar module manufacturing capacity reached ~172 GW by March 2026 (📄 AR p.141). Industry-wide.
- ALMM2 (Approved List of Module Manufacturers, cell stage) deferred to 31-Dec-2026, permitting continued use of imported-cell domestic modules at a ~Rs60 lakh/MW cost advantage over compliant cells for projects commissioned before the deadline (📄 Aug-2026 call, p.9). Industry-wide policy, but CleanMax's brownfield STU sites in Maharashtra and Karnataka give it a faster-than-peers ability to capture the window (🎙️, same call) — an execution-speed edge on a shared benefit, not exclusive access.
- Green Energy Corridor Phase-I completed in seven states, easing clean-power evacuation broadly (📄 AR p.141). Industry-wide.

**4C. Regulatory moat assessment**: The policy tailwinds named above (GST, PLI, ALMM2 timing, Green Corridor) are real but shared across the C&I renewable sector — they are not exclusive to CleanMax and do not by themselves constitute a moat. The genuinely differentiated regulatory element is CleanMax's demonstrated capability to structure and operate the Group Captive framework under Electricity Act rules at scale (~100 SPVs, 26% customer equity + 51% consumption rule, B1/B2 above) — this is a company-specific execution capability layered on top of an industry-wide regulatory structure, not a policy exclusivity. Sustainability of the shared tailwinds is time-bound (ALMM2 window closes 31-Dec-2026); sustainability of the company-specific structuring capability is durable as long as the Electricity Act framework persists. Net: MODERATE, weighted down by the non-exclusive nature of most of the disclosed tailwinds.
