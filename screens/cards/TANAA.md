## Business Understanding Narrative

**What does it do, and how does it make money?** Not what the sector label
suggests. The screen list describes "manufacture of parts and components for
aviation, airfield services and MRO". The company's own Management Discussion
and Analysis says something much simpler: "The Company earns its revenue by
providing technical support and leasing services. The Company has leased hangar
space and buildings to a Maintenance, Repair and Overhaul (MRO) organization.
The Company also generates income from the use of infrastructure facilities
including runway and hangars" (Reg. 34(1) Annual Report, FY2025, Q4, page 28).

Taneja owns an airfield at Belagondapalli in Tamil Nadu, with a runway and
hangars, and rents it out. The identical sentence appears in the FY23 annual
report (Reg. 34(1) Annual Report, FY2023, Q4, page 19). This is a landlord with
an airstrip, not an aerospace manufacturer.

**What is changing?** On the evidence, nothing. FY26 revenue from operations was
Rs 40.16 crore against Rs 40.62 crore in FY25, down 1.1% (Financial Results,
FY2026, Q4, page 15).

**Why now?** There is no "now" in this corpus. No new contract, no new tenant,
no capacity addition and no order is disclosed in any document read.

**What must be true for the thesis to work?** There is no thesis to test. A
transition thesis requires a claimed climb, and the company claims none.

**What breaks it?** Tenant concentration. One MRO organisation leases the
hangars. Its departure would remove the revenue line.

## 2. Business model and archetype

One asset, rented. Revenue is rent plus infrastructure usage charges plus
technical support fees. The company "operates in a single business segment of
aerospace and aviation. Accordingly, no further segment disclosures are
required" (Financial Results, FY2026, Q4, page 16), so the split between rent
and services is not published.

FY26 consolidated, Rs lakh (Financial Results, FY2026, Q4, page 15):

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue from operations | 1,154.06 | 1,238.07 | 4,015.97 | 4,061.69 |
| Other income | 116.89 | 53.67 | 273.61 | 152.92 |
| Total income | 1,270.95 | 1,291.74 | 4,289.57 | 4,214.61 |
| Cost of materials consumed | 37.40 | 19.65 | 59.02 | 19.65 |
| Employee benefits | 190.04 | 155.45 | 537.18 | 409.56 |
| Finance costs | 15.97 | 10.37 | 52.48 | 42.35 |
| Depreciation | 82.39 | — | 332.13 | 351.53 |

Cost of materials consumed of Rs 59.02 lakh against revenue of Rs 4,015.97 lakh
is 1.5%. **A manufacturer consumes materials. This company does not.** That
single ratio settles what the business is.

**Archetype: licence/scarcity business.** An airfield with a runway is a
scarce, regulated asset that cannot be replicated nearby. Licence validity,
regulated access, capacity cap and entry-barrier durability are the variables,
not order book or design wins.

## 3. Competitive advantages

Present, narrow, and structural rather than earned. A private airfield with a
licensed runway in Tamil Nadu is genuinely scarce: aviation approvals, land and
airspace clearance make replication slow and expensive. Any MRO needing hangar
space near that catchment has few alternatives.

That is the whole moat. There is no technology, no brand, no process and no
customer capture beyond the physical asset. Note also that scarcity cuts both
ways here: the asset is scarce, but so are its potential tenants. A landlord
with one irreplaceable building and three possible tenants has less pricing
power than the scarcity suggests.

The FY25 MD&A talks at length about Indian aviation's growth, rising middle
class and MRO demand, then states plainly that the company earns from technical
support and leasing. **The industry narrative and the revenue model are not
connected in the document.**

## 4. The promoters

Clear and disclosed. The promoter is **Asscher Enterprises Limited, formerly
Indian Seamless Enterprises Limited**, which held 50.75% and rose to 51.85% by
open-market purchase; with persons acting in concert the group held 52.32%
(Disclosures under Reg. 29(2) of SEBI SAST, FY2024, Q4, pages 2 and 4). Shares
in the nature of encumbrance: nil. **No pledge.** A promoter buying in the open
market and lifting its stake is a positive signal and is recorded as one.

Persons acting in concert are Salil Taneja, Alka Mehta, Laurus Tradecon Private
Limited and Vishkul Enterprises Private Limited. The ultimate holding company
was Vishkul Enterprises Private Limited (Reg. 34(1) Annual Report, FY2022, Q4,
page 69).

Related-party breadth is the concern. Entities under common control have
included ISMT Limited (until 10 March 2022), Laurus Tradecon, **TAAL Enterprises
Limited**, **TAAL Tech India Private Limited**, First Airways Inc. and Knox
Investments Private Limited, alongside subsidiary Katra Auto Engineering Private
Limited. Rakesh Duda has been Managing Director since 16 May 2022.

A landlord company inside a group containing several related aviation and
engineering entities raises an obvious question this corpus does not answer:
**who leases the hangars, and is the tenant related?** The MRO tenant is never
named in any document read. That is the single most important unanswered
question on this card.

Share count has been static at 2,49,30,736 shares of Rs 5 since at least FY21
(Reg. 34(1) Annual Report, FY2023, Q4, page 101). No dilution. That is rare in
this run and it counts in the company's favour.

## 12. Verdict card

**PASS. Do not run `/step1`.**

The reason is not that the company is bad. It owns a scarce asset, has no
pledge, has never diluted, has a promoter buying in the open market and reported
no audit qualification. Those are good facts.

The reason is that it is not a transition candidate and cannot be valued by this
framework's operating machinery. Materials at 1.5% of revenue and a disclosed
leasing model put it at the R0 to R1 boundary, where the ladder itself directs
SOTP or NAV treatment rather than a destination PE. There is no claimed climb,
no proof gate, no forward trigger, and the most recent quarter shrank.

If the operator wants this name, it is a **real-asset or NAV case**, valued on
the airfield and land rather than on earnings, and it should be routed that way
rather than through `/step1`.

Load-bearing facts a deep run would verify first, should the operator override:
1. **Who the MRO tenant is, the lease term, and whether the tenant is related.**
   Everything else is secondary to this.
2. **A legible FY26 cash flow statement.** The one in this corpus does not
   reconcile through the reader.
3. **The three unread "Award of Order / Receipt of Order" filings** of July
   2024, December 2024 and March 2025.
4. **The land and airfield carrying value** against market value, which is the
   NAV case.
5. **FY26 profit after tax**, which is not in this corpus.

What would change the view. A named, unrelated, long-term MRO tenant with an
escalating lease, plus disclosure of a second revenue line with actual materials
consumption. That would establish the operating engine the R0 rung requires
before any transition can be discussed.

No price. No position.
