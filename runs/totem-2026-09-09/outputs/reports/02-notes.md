let a reader choose between them. GREEN/AMBER — feeds the LBF-2 verdict below as one data point, not a
resolution.

## CONFLICT 1 — underlying employee-cost growth ex-Labour-Codes: RULING FOR PASS 2 (~1.2%, not 8.6%)

Source figures, both from Note 24 (Employee Benefits Expense, PDF p.101-102) and Note 39 (Labour Codes
past-service cost, PDF p.126-127):
- FY26 employee benefits expense: Rs 50.44 cr. FY25: Rs 44.03 cr. Total increase = Rs 6.41 cr.
- Labour Codes one-time charge, entirely incremental to FY26 (Note 39): Rs 5.90 cr.
- Underlying increase ex-Labour-Codes = Rs 6.41 cr - Rs 5.90 cr = **Rs 0.51 cr**.
- Rs 0.51 cr / Rs 44.03 cr FY25 base = **1.16%, rounds to ~1.2%**.

Cross-check the other way: Rs 50.44 cr - Rs 5.90 cr = Rs 44.54 cr adjusted FY26 base; (Rs 44.54 cr - Rs 44.03
cr) / Rs 44.03 cr = 1.16%. Same answer both ways. Pass 1's "~8.6%" does not reconcile under either method;
the arithmetic was stated incorrectly in Pass 1. **RULING: underlying employee-cost growth ex-Labour-Codes
was ~1.2% (Rs 0.51 cr on a Rs 44.03 cr base), not ~8.6%.** This is carried forward as the sole figure; the
8.6% figure is retired. The ~1.2% growth against 7.9% revenue growth (Note 21) is a materially more positive
operating-leverage signal than "roughly tracking revenue" — employee cost barely moved while revenue grew.
Note 31 Section I (PDF p.113) shows gratuity-scheme active members fell 14.8% (481 to 410) while average
monthly salary per active member roughly doubled (~Rs 17,100 to ~Rs 36,400, this analyst's own division of
the disclosed aggregate/headcount figures) — a plausible mechanism (fewer, higher-paid staff) but NOT FOUND
IN DOCUMENT: any management explanation for either the headcount fall or the pay jump, and no standalone
total-headcount figure exists elsewhere in the notes to confirm the "active members" reading is like-for-like
across the Labour Codes recompute. This is now a five-questions item (see below), not a settled fact.

## CONFLICT 2 — receivables trend: STATED ONCE, ON THE COMPANY'S OWN AVERAGE-BALANCE BASIS

Three bases exist in the notes; they are reconciled here into one statement:
1. Point-in-time (Pass 1's calc, not a disclosed ratio): closing net receivables / revenue x 365 = ~52 days
   FY25 to ~44 days FY26 (Note 5 closing balances, Note 21 revenue).
2. Company's own average-balance ratio (Note 33.2, PDF p.117-118, the disclosed metric): Trade Receivables
   turnover 7.45x (FY25) to 7.94x (FY26), i.e. **~49 days to ~46 days**. Same direction, smaller magnitude.
3. Note 36 (Offsetting, PDF p.125): the receivables balance Note 5 reports as "gross" is already net of a
   rebate/discount liability offset against customers. True gross-gross receivables **fell 3.4%** (Rs 38.36
   cr to Rs 37.04 cr) while the offsetting rebate/discount liability **grew 41.6%** (Rs 3.32 cr to Rs 4.71
   cr) over the same year.

**RULING: state the trend on the company's own average-balance basis (Note 33.2), the only one of the three
that is a disclosed ratio rather than an analyst reconstruction: receivable days improved modestly, ~49 to
~46 days, FY25 to FY26.** The point-in-time figure (52 to 44) overstates the improvement and is not carried
forward as the headline number. The Note 36 rebate/discount growth is carried as a qualifier, not a
contradiction: part of the improvement in the net, cash-relevant receivables figure is a larger customer
rebate/discount accrual sitting on the liability side, not purely faster collection. Net: receivables
collection quality improved, modestly, and did not drive the FY26 cash-conversion-cycle deterioration; that
deterioration is entirely an inventory story (LBF-2), confirmed once more by Note 33.2's Current Ratio
falling 1.95 to 1.84 even as receivables turnover improved.

## LBF-2. THE FY26 INVENTORY BUILD — FINAL VERDICT: the accounts do NOT let anyone distinguish ramp stock
from dead stock.
Confirmed across all three passes: inventory Rs 31.93 cr to Rs 56.42 cr (+76.7%, Note 8, PDF p.92); CFO Rs
51.32 cr to Rs 27.55 cr (Cash Flow Statement, PDF p.71), with the inventory swing (Rs (24.49) cr use of cash
vs Rs 6.54 cr source prior year) the single largest driver. No write-down, no ageing schedule, no
obsolescence-provision movement is disclosed anywhere in the 44 notes, despite the company's own policy
(Note 2B(v)) naming inventory obsolescence a critical judgement area, and despite full ageing/movement tables
being given for the comparable working-capital lines: trade receivables (Note 5, full ageing + ECL
roll-forward) and trade payables (Note 19, full ageing). Note 33.2's own ratio table shows inventory turnover
falling 2.27x to 1.83x (-19.35%) and marks it "No Major Change" in the variance-explanation column — the
company's own commentary soft-pedals a swing it is required to explain. Pass 3 adds one data point that
cuts the other way in emphasis, not in substance: the auditor's sole Key Audit Matter is revenue recognition,
not inventory (P3-2), which is evidence the audit found no matter rising to KAM significance, but is not
itself an inventory-quality disclosure and does not substitute for one. Net working-capital ratios (current
ratio 1.95 to 1.84, net capital turnover, Note 33.2) show the build was funded largely through MSME and other
trade payables growing (Note 19, MSME payables Rs 7.41 cr to Rs 14.32 cr, +93.3%), not primarily through cash
drawdown — a financing-mechanism fact, not a stock-quality fact.
**VERDICT: NOT FOUND IN DOCUMENT — the notes give no basis to separate ramp stock from dead stock.** The
exact disclosure that would settle it: an inventory ageing schedule and NRV write-down/obsolescence-provision
movement table, broken out by category (raw materials/WIP/finished goods) and ideally by product line
(carbide vs HSS), of the same form the company already gives for trade receivables (Note 5) and trade
payables (Note 19). Absent that, this is a genuine open question for management, not a red flag on its own.

## LBF-3. THE PROMOTER PLEDGE — FINAL VERDICT: not disclosed in the Notes; must come from the SHP filing.
A full-text search of Notes 1-44 for "pledg*" returns only company-asset-pledged-as-security language (Note
3 footnote, Note 37 — company assets pledged to lenders, not promoter shares). The Notes to Financial
Statements carry no shareholder-pledge disclosure of any kind; Schedule III does not require it there. The
evidence has to come from the quarterly Shareholding Pattern (SHP) filings already staged in this run's
corpus (work/text/shareholding__SHP_30Jun2026.txt, __SHP_31Mar2026.txt, __SHP_30Jun2025.txt), which are
outside this stage's remit (Notes only). Separately and cleanly resolved from the Notes: the operating
company itself shows no loans, ICDs, or advances to the promoter/group, and no guarantee given for any
promoter/group borrowing (Note 40(v)(a)-(b), explicit negative disclosure, both years); the only material
promoter cash flow is dividend, Rs 19.05 cr FY26 (Note 30). Note 37's newly-quantified collateral release
(pledged company assets fell 74.9%, Rs 115.12 cr to Rs 28.91 cr) and the still-unfiled RoC charge-satisfaction
paperwork (Note 41(i)) are the operating company's own governance items, unrelated to a promoter share pledge.
**VERDICT: promoter pledge status CANNOT be settled from the Notes; route to the SHP filings.** Operating
company's own related-party conduct is clean (GREEN); disclosure completeness on the pledge question is a
watch item at the corpus level, not a Notes-stage finding to resolve further.
