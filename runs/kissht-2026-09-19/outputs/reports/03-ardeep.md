### 4B. MISSING RISKS
1. **Parent guarantee capacity / single-point-of-failure funding risk.** Nowhere in
   the Risk Management section (MD&A Section 5) or the "Balanced liability base"
   pillar (front matter) does the AR name the fact that 99.8% of the subsidiary's NCD
   book and most of its term loans rest on one parent guarantee already exceeding the
   parent's own net worth (B02 Finding 1/4; independently confirmed this pass via
   CARO Annexure A). This is the single largest quantified red flag in the entire
   document and it has no corresponding line in the risk factors at all. **Likely
   reason for omission: naming it would require quantifying the guarantee-to-net-worth
   ratio in the risk section itself, which the Company evidently prefers to leave
   inside Note 32/Note 36 where a casual reader will not find it.**
2. **FIU-IND "High Risk Financial Institution" classification / regulatory history.**
   The RHP (dated 25-Apr-2026, filed four months before this AR) disclosed that Si
   Creva was "previously classified as a 'High Risk Financial Institution' by
   FIU-IND due to certain non-compliances under the Prevention of Money Laundering
   Act, 2002... for which a show cause notice was issued" (RHP p.3476-3494), later
   resolved. **This AR (signed 27-May-2026, less than a month after listing) makes
   NO mention of this history anywhere** — not in the risk section, not in Boards'
   Report item 36 ("no significant or material orders... impacting going concern"),
   not in the notes. Boards' Report item 36's statement is arguably technically true
   (the matter was resolved before the AR's reporting period even if it dates from
   an earlier compliance failure), but a first-year listed-company AR silently
   dropping a regulatory history it disclosed to IPO investors four months earlier is
   a disclosure-consistency gap worth naming. **No evidence of an Enforcement
   Directorate notice was found in the AR, the RHP, or the 26 BSE announcements in
   this container — the "ED notice" referenced in company context (B00 LBF-4) is NOT
   FOUND in any document available to this analysis; only the FIU-IND/PMLA
   show-cause-notice history (RHP) is evidenced, and that was resolved pre-RHP.**
3. **FLDG/off-balance-sheet cost escalation.** B02 Finding 3 (FLDG cost more than
   tripled group-wide, outpacing revenue growth) has no corresponding risk-factor
   line despite off-book AUM being 50% of total AUM (KPI table, AR p.12) — a business
   line half the size of the whole book, with a rapidly rising associated cost, and no
   risk-factor acknowledgement.
4. **Write-off policy / DPD trigger disclosure gap.** The MD&A's own asset-quality
   narrative (Section 4, "Analysis of Financial Performance") attributes the GNPA
   improvement to "the effectiveness of our underwriting framework, portfolio
   monitoring capabilities and robust collection infrastructure" (AR p.65) without
   any acknowledgement that the ratio's stability also reflects a rising write-off
   rate removing defaulted assets from the denominator (B02 Finding 2). This is not a
   missing risk so much as an attribution gap — covered in 4C and 6A below.

### 6E. Quiet Abandonment Check (mandatory)
**Finding 1 — Silent drop, HIGH materiality.** The "Balanced liability base" pillar

guarantee already exceeds the parent's own net worth by 128 percentage points
(228% of net worth). The pillar names counterparty count and instrument-type mix as
its diversification evidence but never mentions the guarantee-concentration fact
that sits directly underneath that same funding — the one dimension of concentration
that actually matters (credit-enhancement provider, not lender counterparty) is the
one dimension never disclosed in the pillar built to reassure readers about exactly
this risk. **Classification: silent drop.** **Materiality: HIGH — this goes to
whether the "balanced liability base" claim is true in the sense that matters for
solvency, not just in the sense of counterparty headcount.**

**Finding 2 — Implicit retraction, MEDIUM materiality.** The CEO's message and MD&A
attribute the GNPA improvement to underwriting/collections effectiveness (Section
6A above). The notes (operational section, in the backward-reading sense — the least
PR-managed part of the document) show the same improvement is substantially explained
by a rising write-off rate outrunning new defaults. The opening narrative does not
acknowledge the write-off mechanic at all. **Classification: implicit retraction**
(opening says "quality improved because of X," operational detail says "the ratio
improved because of Y," with no acknowledgement of Y). **Materiality: MEDIUM** — it
does not invalidate the growth story, but it does mean the specific causal claim in
the CEO letter is not fully supported by the Company's own audited notes.

**No third quiet-abandonment candidate met the evidence bar** in this pass; the LAP
new-business-line disclosure gap (B02 Finding 12) is better classified as a Phase 4B
missing-risk/disclosure item than a quiet abandonment, since the front matter does
not make an LAP-specific claim that the operational sections then withdraw — LAP is
consistently described as small and growing throughout.
