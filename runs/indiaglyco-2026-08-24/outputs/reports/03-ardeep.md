**CFO quality checks**:
1. **CFO/PAT of 2.72x is a quality flag in the opposite direction from
   the pipeline's usual <0.7x screen, but a flag nonetheless.** The
   entire excess of CFO over PAT is explained by a one-time working
   capital release: (Increase)/Decrease in Inventories contributed
   +₹28,081.05 Lakh (a 24% drawdown of the FY25 closing inventory base
   of ₹1,16,879.91 Lakh) and Trade & Other Receivables contributed
   +₹6,962.07 Lakh, partially offset by a payables reduction of
   −₹20,369.97 Lakh. **This inventory rundown is very unlikely to repeat
   at this scale next year and should not be extrapolated as a
   sustainable CFO run-rate.** This is a genuine, anchored
   quality-of-cash-flow issue (Board's Report/MD&A give no explanation
   for the large inventory reduction beyond generic "optimised
   procurement" language elsewhere in the MD&A). 🟡
2. **Interest classification**: Finance Costs (₹16,821.00 Lakh paid) are
   classified entirely under Financing Activities, not Operating — the
   more conservative of the two permitted Ind AS 7 choices (some
   companies classify interest paid under Operating to inflate apparent
   CFO from core operations). This is a positive quality signal. 🟢
3. **Capex vs depreciation of 5.3x (FY26) and 6.6x (FY25) confirms a
   genuinely heavy, multi-year capex cycle**, consistent with the
   Board's Report narrative of distillery, New Value-Added Chemical
   Products, and Bio-Fuel Ethanol Plant capex "now completed." **FCF
   is negative in both years** despite very strong CFO, because capex
   still exceeds even the CFO-inflated-by-inventory-drawdown figure.
   Genuine capex intensity, not an aggressive-capitalisation quality
   problem per se (see 2A cross-reference on interest capitalisation
   rate gap), but it means the Company has not yet reached a
   cash-generative post-capex phase on a standalone FCF basis. 🟡
4. **Cash pile remains thin in absolute terms** (₹3,106.47 Lakh closing,
   ~0.5% of total assets) despite the ₹466.99cr equity raise — because
   the raise was largely directed to working capital (₹350.25cr of
   ₹466.99cr) and term-loan repayment (₹116.74cr), not cash build.
   Consistent with the Board's Report's own stated use of proceeds.

## 6E. Quiet Abandonment Check (mandatory)

**Finding: implicit retraction, FX hedging.**

- **Opening claim** (Corporate Governance Report, section V(o),
  [[page 65]], filed alongside the front-matter governance disclosures
  read early in the document): "The Company has a sizeable forex
  exposure and in order to mitigate the fluctuations in exchange rate,
  the Company hedges its import liabilities and export by adopting
  appropriate measures. The Company has adopted Forex Risk Management
  Policy in terms of applicable laws."
- **Operational section where it should have shown up, but instead
  contradicts it**: Note 45(i)(a) standalone / Note 42(i)(a) consolidated
  (FX sensitivity disclosure, triple-passed in B02 rank 1): outstanding
  hedge notional collapsed 92.2% (USD 52.86mn to USD 3.73mn) in the same
  year, while the unhedged export/import exposure roughly doubled
  (export debtors net of packing credit +62.1%, payables +103.7%), with
  the FX PBT sensitivity per 1% USD move rising from ₹155.46 Lakh to
  ₹295.69 Lakh as a direct, quantified consequence.
- **Classification**: **(a) implicit retraction** — the front-matter
  claim asserts an ongoing hedging practice in the present tense
  ("the Company hedges...") with no qualification, while the operational
  note shows that practice was almost entirely dismantled during the
  same fiscal year, with zero acknowledgement anywhere in the document
  that the claim and the note describe two different realities. This is
  not a case of the claim being generically true in the past and merely
  silent on the present (which would be a silent drop); the claim is
  phrased as a current, ongoing state of affairs and is falsified by the
  Company's own numbers filed in the same Annual Report.
- **Materiality**: **changes the thesis.** This is not framework noise.
  It is the single largest quantified accounting-quality Red Flag in
  this document (B02 rank 1), and the fact that it sits directly beside
  an affirmative governance-report claim to the contrary — rather than
  being silently omitted — raises the materiality further: a reader who
  reviewed only the governance section (a common shortcut for investors
  screening a large AR) would come away with an incorrect, reassuring
  impression of the Company's FX risk posture that the Notes directly
  contradict a few hundred pages later.

No other candidate abandonment reached this level of quotable,
side-by-side contradiction. Two weaker candidates considered and
rejected: (1) the Bio-Fuel Outlook framing ("well-positioned for
sustained growth") versus the already-disclosed 22% ESY allocation
decline for the current supply year — this is a **hedged retreat**
candidate (the decline is disclosed, just not connected to the Outlook
framing), materiality low-to-medium, not elevated to a standalone
finding here because the underlying number is fully disclosed elsewhere
in the same Board's Report (Finance/segment section), not silently
dropped; (2) the Rating Watch (Developing Implications) mention — a
**silent drop** candidate (mentioned once, never revisited), materiality
low given it reflects the already-fully-disclosed demerger rather than a
new undisclosed problem.

### Phase 6 summary

🔴 **Red Flag**, carried directly from the 6E finding. The document's

both silent on specifics (no MD&A risk mention) and affirmatively
misleading (Corporate Governance Report claim).**

## GARP (full reasoning, per operator mandate)

**PASS, with a structural caveat that governs everything else.**

Top 3 reasons:
1. **Real earnings growth with improving returns**: consol PAT +26.8%,
   standalone-normalised (ex one-off dividend) PAT growth ≈+38-40%, ROE
   10%→13%, ROCE 10%→12%, net profit ratio 5%→7%, active deleveraging
   (D/E 0.96x→0.62x) — a genuinely improving quality profile on a
   trailing basis.
2. **The single most important fact for a GARP read is structural, not
   financial**: the consolidated FY26 numbers analysed in this entire
   report are a **last full pre-split picture**. From 1 September 2026
   (appointed date 1 April 2026), the entity a continuing shareholder in
   "India Glycols Limited" will actually hold is the **residual
   chemicals + industrial gases business** — i.e., predominantly the
   BSPC segment, which **declined 10.4% this year and whose largest
   sub-component (Bio-based Glycols) declined 30% on realisation, not
   volume**. The Potable Spirits business (+7-14% growth, the largest
   revenue segment in absolute terms) and Bio-Fuel (+41% growth, but
   with a disclosed 22% forward allocation cut already known) are being
   demerged into IGL Spirits Ltd, a currently near-dormant shell (FY26
   loss ₹0.22 Lakh). **None of the trailing consolidated growth-and-
   quality metrics in this report are a clean base for valuing the
   post-demerger residual entity.** A GARP framework applied naively to
   the consolidated numbers would be pricing growth (Spirits, Bio-Fuel)
   that a continuing shareholder will not retain, against a base
   business (BSPC) that just shrank.
3. **Fair value CAGR question is unresolvable from this document alone**
   (per Amendment 19 discipline): what matters for entry-zone
   interpretation is how fast BSPC's *own* fair value can plausibly grow
   post-demerger, independent of the Spirits/Bio-Fuel growth this report
   documents. That number is NOT FOUND IN THIS DOCUMENT and must come
   from the Section 1B valuation stage working off a demerger-adjusted
   earnings base, not the consolidated FY26 P&L.

## Turnaround (full reasoning, per operator mandate)

**WATCHLIST.**

Top 3 reasons:
1. The BSPC segment's decline is **price/realisation-driven, not
   demand-driven** — Bio-based Glycols volume grew +16.0% (27,736→32,170
   MT) while value fell 29.9%, and MD&A's own account attributes this to
   global MEG oversupply and China competitive pricing, with a
   late-FY26 turn ("escalated conflicts in West Asia and Middle East...
   led to a hike in the prices & availability constraint") that could
   plausibly mark a cyclical trough rather than a structural share loss.
   This is the classic shape of a turnaround setup: volume/market-share
   intact, pricing depressed by an industry-wide cycle.
2. Counter-evidence against calling this a clean turnaround yet:
   Performance Chemicals (+37.5%) and Industrial Gases (−20.7%) moved in
   opposite directions within the same BSPC segment, so the "recovery"
   thesis is not yet broad-based across all of BSPC's sub-lines; and
   management's MD&A Outlook offers only generic optimism ("well-
   positioned to capitalise") without a quantified recovery timeline or
   margin-bridge for BSPC specifically.
3. The demerger timing compounds the turnaround-timing risk: if BSPC
   pricing does not recover before or shortly after the 1 September 2026
   listing, the newly-independent "India Glycols" entity will begin its
   standalone public life on a down-cycle base, with no Potable Spirits/
   Bio-Fuel earnings to cushion the transition period.
