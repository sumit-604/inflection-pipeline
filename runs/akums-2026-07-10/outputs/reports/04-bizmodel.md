## SECTION 1: THE BUSINESS MODEL IN PLAIN ENGLISH

### 1A. One-line description

Akums runs 11 large pharmaceutical factories that mostly make medicines **for other companies** under contract (tablets, capsules, syrups, injectables), while also selling a smaller slice of medicines under its own brand names and selling the raw drug chemicals (API) that go into tablets.

### 1B. The money flow chain, for each revenue stream

**CDMO (Contract Development & Manufacturing) — 80.0% of FY26 revenue (Inv. Pres. slide 11)**
[Input: APIs, excipients, packaging material] → [Akums develops the formulation and manufactures the finished dosage form — tablet, capsule, liquid, injectable — in its own GMP-certified plants, to the client's specification] → [delivers finished, packaged medicine to the client pharma company] → [the client pharma company (a domestic or international branded/generic marketer) pays] → [paid per batch/contract on delivery, under multi-year supply agreements — e.g., the 200 Mn Euro European CDMO contract with supplies starting FY28 (Inv. Pres. slide 8)].

**Domestic Branded Formulation (Akumentis brand) — 10.2% of FY26 revenue (Inv. Pres. slide 11)**
[Input: Akums's own formulations] → [Akums markets these under its own brand names through a field force to doctors, chemists and hospitals] → [delivers branded medicine into the retail/hospital pharmacy channel via stockists] → [chemists/stockists pay, ultimately funded by patients] → [paid on the standard India Pharma Market (IPM) distributor credit cycle].

**API (Active Pharmaceutical Ingredient) — 4.2% of FY26 revenue (Inv. Pres. slide 11)**
[Input: basic chemicals and intermediates] → [Akums (via its Parabolic Drugs acquisition, Inv. Pres. slide 4) chemically synthesizes bulk drug ingredients] → [delivers bulk API to formulators, including third parties and potentially Akums's own CDMO business] → [formulator-buyers pay] → [priced on global commodity API cycles — "API prices remained soft though initial signs of stabilization" in FY26, Inv. Pres. slide 7].

**International Branded Formulation — 3.3% of FY26 revenue (Inv. Pres. slide 11)**
[Input: Akums's own formulations, registered for export markets] → [Akums manufactures and exports under its own brand or via JV partners, e.g. the Zambia JV with the Government of the Republic of Zambia] → [delivers to overseas distributors / JV entity] → [overseas distributor or JV pays] → [paid under export contracts / JV supply commitments — USD 25 million per annum from Akums to the Zambia JV for FY27 and FY28, Inv. Pres. slide 8].

**Trade Generics — 2.3% of FY26 revenue (Inv. Pres. slide 11)**
[Input: Akums's own unbranded generic formulations] → [Akums sells unbranded generics in bulk into the trade/wholesale channel] → [delivers to wholesalers/distributors] → [wholesalers pay] → [paid on trade credit; historically loss-making, "turned EBITDA positive" only in Q4 FY26, Inv. Pres. slide 7].

### 1E. The chai-stall-uncle version

Think of Akums as a giant, extremely skilled halwai kitchen that other sweet shops hire to make their sweets, because building their own kitchen would be too slow and too expensive. Most of Akums's business — 80 paise of every rupee it earns — is exactly this: another pharma company sends the recipe, Akums makes the tablets, syrups or injections in its own factories, and the client puts its own brand on the box and sells it. On the side, Akums also runs its own small sweet shop (its own-brand medicines, about 10% of revenue), sells raw sugar to other halwais (the API business, about 4%), and does a bit of both abroad (international formulation and trade generics, about 6% combined). The contract-kitchen (CDMO) business is steady because clients keep coming back year after year, but each batch isn't hugely profitable — this is why Akums is now chasing bigger export contracts and its own brands, which fetch a much better price per batch.

### 2D. Industry lifecycle stage

## SECTION 3: FINANCIAL METRICS THAT MATTER FOR THIS BUSINESS MODEL

### 3A. Ignore-these, track-these

| Commonly tracked ratio | Why it is misleading/irrelevant here |
|---|---|
| Reported P/E (standalone) | PAT is distorted by exceptional items and a one-time FY25 deferred-tax impact of Rs 106 Cr (Inv. Pres. slide 23 definitions); use Adj PAT / Adj EPS instead |
| Net profit margin | Distorted by a sharply rising finance-cost line (+172% YoY FY26, Inv. Pres. slide 12) tied to capex financing, not core operating performance; EBITDA margin is the cleaner read |
| Consolidated revenue growth alone | Masks divergent segment trends — CDMO grew 8.6% while API fell 15.9% and Trade Generics fell 13.2% in FY26 (Inv. Pres. slide 19); always read growth at the segment level |
| Reported ROE | Distorted by fair-value changes and exceptional items; use Adj ROE (Adj PAT / Total Equity, Inv. Pres. slide 23) instead |
| Gross margin trend without mix context | API price cycles swing consolidated gross margin independent of operating execution (39.5%→43.0% Q4 YoY, Inv. Pres. slide 12) |

### 3D. Unit economics — the physics of the business

- **One unit**: Akums itself reports capacity in "Cr units" of dosage form (tablet/capsule/vial-equivalent) manufactured (Inv. Pres. slide 5); this is the natural unit for this business.
- **Revenue per unit**: NOT FOUND, check annual report / concall — the presentation discloses aggregate capacity (~5,059 Cr units) and aggregate revenue (Rs 4,359 Cr FY26) but no disclosed per-unit realization.
- **Cost per unit**: NOT FOUND, check annual report / concall — same gap; only aggregate COGS (Rs 2,514 Cr FY26) is disclosed.
- **Volume drivers**: capacity utilization (44% FY26, room to scale — Inv. Pres. slide 7); new facility ramp-up (all lines of the new injectable facility now operational — Inv. Pres. slide 7); new product lines (Oncology and steroid lines going live in domestic CDMO in FY27 — Inv. Pres. slide 20).
- **Price drivers**: product mix (management notes "price variance impacted as base portfolio grew faster than niche portfolio," Inv. Pres. slide 16); export vs domestic mix (export margins structurally better per management, Inv. Pres. slide 20); API price cycle.
- **Cost drivers**: raw materials/API (COGS ~57.7% of revenue, Inv. Pres. slide 12); employee costs (Rs 754 Cr, 17.3% of revenue, scale of multi-site GMP workforce, Inv. Pres. slide 12); power and fuel (cited as the reason "other expenses" rose FY26, Inv. Pres. slide 17).
- **Incremental margin / operating leverage**: FY26 Adj EBITDA margin expanded 80bps YoY (11.2%→12.0%) on only 5.8% revenue growth, driven mainly by gross-margin improvement (+1.4pp) and, in Q4 specifically, positive operating leverage on employee and other expenses (+0.5pp combined, Inv. Pres. slide 12, 16, 17). With utilization still at only 44% (Inv. Pres. slide 7), each incremental point of utilization should carry disproportionately high drop-through to EBITDA — this is the central operating-leverage thesis for the stock, though the exact incremental-margin ratio is NOT FOUND, check annual report / concall.
