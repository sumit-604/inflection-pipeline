## SECTION 1: MARKET DEFINITION

### 1A. Precise boundaries

**Product scope (IN):** EPC construction and HAM-concession development of national and
state highways, expressways, elevated corridors, bridges, flyovers, tunnels, and
metro/rail civil structures; plus O&M service contracts on completed road assets.
Contract mechanisms: EPC lump-sum/item-rate, HAM annuity-concession (SPV equity +
government annuity), O&M fee. This matches B04's disclosed revenue split: EPC 57.86%,
HAM 41.68%, O&M 0.46% of FY26 revenue (B04-bizmodel.yaml; AR sheet 32, printed p.60).

**Product scope (OUT):** Renewables (solar, BESS, wind) EPC and power transmission &
distribution EPC. Both carry Letters of Award/PPAs (cumulative Rs 39,365m solar/BESS,
Rs 4,068m T&D — Investor_Presentation_1.pdf p.17) but show **0.0% completion on every
line as of 30-Jun-2026** (Investor_Presentation_1.pdf pp.22-23). Per B04's explicit
flag (FLAG-PRERISK-RENEWABLES), these are not yet a revenue stream and are excluded
from core TAM/SAM/SOM; sized qualitatively only, as an expansion lever, in Section 5D.
International operations (Ceigall Global Pte. Ltd., Singapore; Ceigall EPC Global
Contracting LLC, UAE) are also excluded — zero disclosed international revenue, B07
classifies this an optionality with a 24-month+ window.

**Geographic scope:** India. Ceigall holds execution capability across 13 states as of
30-Jun-2026 (Investor_Presentation_1.pdf p.10), out of India's ~29 states/UTs.

**Customer scope:** NHAI, MoRTH, state PWDs and road development corporations (e.g.
MPRDCL, Bihar PWD, Punjab PWD, IRCON), and metro rail corporations (UPMRC, JMRC).

**Channel scope:** Competitive public tender (L1 lowest-cost or techno-commercial
bid). No retail/B2C channel; not price-segmented in the consumer sense — segmentation
runs by project ticket size and structural complexity.

**Explicit exclusions restated:** renewables/T&D EPC, international operations, and
the undisclosed post-COD annuity slice of HAM revenue (B04 flags this split is not
disclosed, so it cannot be separately sized here and is left inside the HAM
construction-phase estimate).

### 1B. Management's own TAM claim

Ramneek Sehgal, Chairman & Managing Director, on the FY26 NHAI awarding-pace
question (Concall_May_2026_Transcript.txt, p.9):

> "...the pipeline of NHAI is close to INR2 lakh crores."

No stated time horizon (annual flow vs cumulative multi-year backlog), no segment
breakdown, and no independent citation — when pressed for the actual FY26 awarding
figure in value terms, management deflected: "these are the figures which NHAI has
put on their own website" (same page). No renewables/T&D-specific market-size claim
appears anywhere across the four transcripts or the investor presentation.

**Credibility read: BROAD.** The figure is directionally plausible in scale (an
independent web search found NHAI's active construction pipeline at Rs 7.72 lakh
crore across 27,597 km — see Section 2 — and NHAI's own FY26 capex was reported near
Rs 2.44 lakh crore), but "close to INR2 lakh crores" mixes an undefined time horizon
against a market that dozens of qualified EPC/HAM contractors compete for, not a
number specific to Ceigall's addressable slice. Held for the ratio test in Section 2.

## SECTION 2: TAM ESTIMATION, MULTIPLE METHODS

### Method 1 — Top-down (government budget line)

Start: MoRTH total budget allocation, FY2026-27 (BE) = **Rs 3,09,375 crore**, up 8%
YoY from FY2025-26 (RE) Rs 2,87,142 crore (AR sheet 31, printed p.59, "Road Sector
Budget Allocations (Updated – FY 2026-27)", cited to prsindia.org budget analysis;
FRESH).

Subtract: the ministry total includes debt servicing, land-acquisition compensation,
administration and safety/maintenance grants that never become an EPC/HAM contract
awarded to a company like Ceigall. NHAI's own allocation is Rs 1,87,293 crore (61% of
the MoRTH total, +10% YoY). Within NHAI's allocation, the line item that maps
directly to constructible contract value — **"Roads & Bridges"** — is:

- FY2025-26 (RE): Rs 1,16,337 crore
- FY2026-27 (BE): **Rs 1,21,999 crore** (+5% YoY)
(AR sheet 31, printed p.59)

This Rs 1,21,999 crore is the closest available single-line proxy for the annual
NATIONAL-HIGHWAY EPC/HAM contract-value pool. It excludes state-highway and
metro-rail work, which Ceigall also bids (Bihar/Punjab PWD, MPRDCL, UPMRC, JMRC are
all counterparties in its own order book) — no consolidated all-India state-highway
capex figure was found in the corpus or by search (**NOT FOUND**), so this method's
output is a floor, not the full addressable pool.

**Cross-check (higher, "actual spend" figure):** independent web search found NHAI's
FY26 capital expenditure reported at **Rs 2,44,000 crore** in connection with the
5,313 km construction achievement (BusinessWorld, "NHAI Builds 5,313 km Of Highways
In FY26, Exceeds Target"; corroborated by IBEF's republication of the same NHAI data,
Apr-2026). This differs from the AR's FY27 budget-ESTIMATE line because it reflects
actual FY26 spend, not next year's budget estimate.

Per the CONSERVATIVE-BIAS rule, Method 1's headline TAM takes the lower figure
(**Rs 1,21,999 crore**), carrying the higher actual-spend figure (Rs 2,44,000 crore)
as the "realistic" alternative. Confidence: **M**. Staleness: fresh (FY26/FY27 data).

### Method 2 — Bottom-up (lane-km unit economics)

Addressable unit: 1 lane-km of national highway/expressway/structure constructed.

Total units: NHAI completed **5,313 km** of highway construction in FY26, beating its
revised 4,640 km target by 15% (AR sheet 28, printed p.52-53; independently
corroborated by BusinessWorld/IBEF, FRESH). This is highway LENGTH, not lane-km. The
AR states the length of four-lane-and-above highways has grown 2.6x, from 18,371 km
(2014) to 48,568 km today (AR sheet 28), implying a modern-highway average in the
4-6 lane band; no disclosed average-lanes-per-km figure exists for the specific FY26
5,313 km (**NOT FOUND**). Applying a conservative 4-lane assumption (the minimum
access-controlled spec the AR itself uses as the modern baseline):

5,313 km x 4 lanes = **~21,252 lane-km** constructed nationally in FY26.

Revenue per unit: B04's illustrative total-project-cost-per-lane-km range is
Rs 131 million - Rs 197 million, depending on structure type (Investor_Presentation_1.pdf
pp.20-22, as compiled in B04-bizmodel.yaml). Taking the conservative low end:

21,252 lane-km x Rs 13.1 crore/lane-km = **~Rs 2,78,401 crore**

This is a single-year (FY26), national-highway-only bottom-up TAM. It excludes
metro, standalone bridges/flyovers, state highways and tunnels priced above this
range, so it likely overstates the true national-highway-only figure somewhat (by
assuming every km is priced at the low end) while understating the true ALL-SCOPE
figure (by excluding metro/state-highway spend entirely). Confidence: **L** (compounds
two unconfirmed assumptions: 4-lane average and low-end per-lane-km cost).

Method 1 (Rs 1.22-2.44 lakh crore) and Method 2 (~Rs 2.78 lakh crore) triangulate
into the same order of magnitude (roughly Rs 1.2-2.8 lakh crore) for the
national-highway-only annual TAM, despite using entirely different inputs
(budget-line vs unit economics) — a reasonable cross-check.

### Method 4 — Import substitution: NOT APPLICABLE

This is a domestic government-infrastructure-SERVICES market. India does not import
road-construction services; imported inputs (e.g. tunnel-boring machinery) are a
minor cost line, not a market-sizing axis for the EPC/HAM service itself.

### Method 5 — Global benchmark: NOT MEANINGFULLY APPLICABLE

India's road-per-capita or road-per-GDP gap to China/global averages is directionally
informative of a large multi-decade opportunity, but does not sharpen a
company-specific TAM bounded by INDIAN GOVERNMENT BUDGET ALLOCATIONS (a fixed annual
fiscal ceiling), unlike a consumer-demand-elastic market. Not computed as a headline
number; noted for completeness only.

## SECTION 3: SAM & SOM

### 3A. SAM — five filters applied to conservative TAM (Rs 1,21,999 cr)

All five filters below are ANALYST JUDGMENT applied to a sourced TAM base (as SAM/SOM
are defined to be), not independently sourced percentages; each is shown with its
reasoning.

| Filter | Cut | Retained | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Product fit | -5% | 95% | Ceigall's product scope (highway/expressway/bridge/tunnel/elevated-corridor EPC + HAM) covers nearly all of the Roads & Bridges line; excludes large-diameter TBM megaprojects beyond current fleet scale and pure BOT-toll bids (no disclosed toll-operating track record). |
| Geography | -50% | 47.5% | Ceigall operates in 13 of ~29 states/UTs (Inv. Pres. p.10) against a national budget line spanning all states; no state-wise NH-km breakdown exists to compute an exact proportion (**NOT FOUND**), so a count-based ~45-50% proxy is used. |
| Channel | 0% | 47.5% | Same open-tender channel as all qualified bidders; no exclusive access disclosed. |
| Customer | 0% | 47.5% | NHAI/MoRTH/state PWD/metro corporations are all customers Ceigall already serves. |
| Capability (bonding/net worth) | -30% | 33.25% | NHAI/state tenders gate on net worth, past-performance and bank-guarantee capacity scaling with ticket size. Ceigall's FY26 equity base (~Rs 2,098 cr per Aug-2026 call) and already-high working-capital intensity (Contract Assets + RUSCA ~71% of FY26 revenue, B04) cap how many large-ticket packages it can credibly carry simultaneously. |

**SAM = Rs 1,21,999 cr x 33.25% ≈ Rs 40,565 crore (33.3% of TAM).**

Current share of SAM = Rs 4,022 cr (FY26 revenue) ÷ Rs 40,565 cr = **9.9%**.

### 3B. SOM at 3 and 5 years

Share-gain rule set: 1-2pp/3yr is "normal"; 3-5pp is "aggressive" (requires capacity +
execution evidence); >5pp requires a competitor exit/acquisition.

Two facts pull in opposite directions on which band to use:
- FY26 order inflow hit Rs 11,332 cr against Rs 5,000 cr guidance, in the SAME year
  NHAI's own new awards fell ~22% YoY (peer evidence, B06-peers.yaml) — a real,
  large beat.
- But ~35% of that FY26 inflow came from renewables (May-2026 call, p.3), which sits
  OUTSIDE this TAM's scope entirely, and capex_embedded_growth_pct = 0 (B07) means
  there is no capex-funded capacity expansion behind the remaining highway-scope
  growth — only rented equipment and working capital, both already flagged as
  stretched (B04).

Given the countervailing signals, and per the CONSERVATIVE-BIAS instruction, the
"normal" 1-2pp band is used, not "aggressive":

- 3yr share: 9.9% + 1.5pp = **11.4%** of SAM
- 5yr share: 9.9% + 2.5pp = **12.4%** of SAM

SAM itself grows with the underlying TAM. Using Method 1's Roads & Bridges YoY growth
(~5%) as the TAM/SAM growth rate:

- SAM(yr3) = Rs 40,565 cr x (1.05)^3 ≈ Rs 46,960 cr
- SAM(yr5) = Rs 40,565 cr x (1.05)^5 ≈ Rs 51,780 cr

**SOM(3yr) = 11.4% x Rs 46,960 cr ≈ Rs 5,353 crore**
**SOM(5yr) = 12.4% x Rs 51,780 cr ≈ Rs 6,421 crore**

Implied revenue CAGR from FY26 base (Rs 4,022 cr):
- Yr3: (5,353 / 4,022)^(1/3) - 1 = **~10.0%**
- Yr5: (6,421 / 4,022)^(1/5) - 1 = **~9.8%**

**This is the load-bearing finding of this stage.** The SOM-implied revenue CAGR
(~10%) sits BELOW management's own FY27 guidance of a minimum 15% revenue growth
(May-2026 and Aug-2026 calls) and far below the strategy's 25% CAGR hurdle. Three
non-exclusive explanations: (a) the TAM/SAM used here is a conservative floor that
excludes state-highway/metro spend Ceigall also captures; (b) continued outsized
share gains beyond the "normal" band (as FY26 already showed) would close the gap,
but that is not the conservative case; (c) a meaningful share of forward growth is
expected from the renewables/T&D vertical, which is outside this TAM's scope by
design (zero revenue as of 30-Jun-2026) and cannot be credited here.

### 3C. Capacity cross-check

B07's capex_embedded_growth_pct = **0**. Per the injected note, Ceigall is
asset-light: equipment is rented rather than owned, and its own capital is deployed
into HAM SPV equity, not into owned plant. Physical execution capacity therefore
scales through rented-equipment availability (elastic, not a hard capex ceiling) and
in-house design/engineering headcount (100+ per Inv. Pres. p.27), not through a
company-funded capacity buildout.

The real constraint the corpus evidences is FINANCING capacity, not physical
capacity: B04 already flags Contract Assets + RUSCA at ~71% of FY26 revenue with nil
impairment, rising Debt/Equity, and an Interest Service Coverage Ratio (3.31x FY26,
down from 3.55x FY25, AR sheet 32) trending toward its own "red flag below 2x"
threshold. Ceigall's current order book (Rs 18,554 cr, 4.8x book-to-bill) already
covers roughly 4-5 years of execution at the FY26 revenue run-rate WITHOUT any fresh
wins — meaning the SOM figures above (Rs 5,353-6,421 cr/yr) require CONTINUED order
INFLOW at a pace the current soft-award NHAI environment does not obviously support,
not simply a run-down of the existing backlog.

**Capacity check: sufficient on a physical/capex basis (no capex bottleneck, rented
model) but financing capacity is the binding, evidenced constraint — the SOM path is
the optimistic side of the ledger if working-capital and bonding capacity do not
scale with the assumed order-inflow pace.**

## SECTION 5: SUMMARY & RUNWAY

### 5A. Funnel

```
TAM (conservative, national-highway EPC/HAM, FY27 BE)   Rs 1,21,999 cr
   -> product fit (-5%), geography (-50%), channel (0%),
      customer (0%), capability (-30%)
SAM (33.3% of TAM)                                       Rs   40,565 cr
   -> current share ~9.9%; +1.5pp (3yr) / +2.5pp (5yr), "normal" band
SOM 3-year                                                Rs    5,353 cr  (11.4% of SAM@yr3)
SOM 5-year                                                Rs    6,421 cr  (12.4% of SAM@yr5)
```

### 5B. Runway assessment

- **Revenue headroom** = SAM ÷ current revenue = Rs 40,565 cr ÷ Rs 4,022 cr = **10.1x**
- **TAM growth rate** ≈ **5%/yr** (Roads & Bridges budget-line growth, FY26RE->FY27BE)
- **Company CAGR vs TAM:** Ceigall's FY24-26 consolidated revenue CAGR =
  (Rs 4,022 cr / Rs 3,029 cr)^(1/2) - 1 = **~15.2%**, roughly 3x the ~5% TAM growth
  rate — Ceigall is GAINING SHARE, not merely riding the market.
- **Years to saturate SAM** at current trajectories (Ceigall growing ~15%/yr, SAM
  growing ~5%/yr): solving (1.15/1.05)^n = 10.1 gives **n ≈ 25 years** — a long
  notional runway, though a 25-year linear extrapolation of both growth rates is
  illustrative, not a forecast.

### 5C. Runway classification: **GOOD**

Revenue headroom is large (~10x SAM/revenue) and the underlying government capex
pipeline is multi-decade (NIP 2.0, only 20% complete). But TAM itself grows only
modestly (~5%/yr) and the SOM-implied revenue CAGR (~10%) falls short of both
management's own 15% guidance and the strategy's 25% hurdle under the conservative
share-gain assumption used here. That shortfall is the disqualifying reason this is
GOOD rather than STRONG/MASSIVE: real headroom exists, but this market, at a
"normal" 1-2pp share-gain pace, does not organically clear the growth bar this
thesis needs.

### 5D. SAM expansion levers actually being pursued

- **Renewables & T&D (solar, BESS, substations):** order book already carries
  Rs 3,937 cr (Rs 39,365m) solar/BESS LOA/PPA value and Rs 407 cr (Rs 4,068m) T&D
  value (Inv. Pres. p.17), 0% executed. India's 500 GW 2030 non-fossil target vs
  274.68 GW achieved (AR sheet 13) implies a large incremental build-out this
  decade, but no disclosed all-in EPC Rs/MW figure exists in this corpus to convert
  that gap into a defensible Rs-crore SAM addition (**NOT FOUND**) — left
  qualitative and unquantified, consistent with B04's instruction not to model
  pre-revenue verticals as revenue.
- **Geographic expansion:** entry into Delhi, Arunachal Pradesh and Andhra Pradesh
  in FY26 (Inv. Pres. p.11) directly relaxes the 13/29-state geography filter used
  in 3A over time; no separately anchored Rs-crore addition beyond what the current
  order book already reflects.
- **International (Singapore, UAE):** explicitly pre-revenue (B07 optionality
  register, 24m+ window); not sized.
- **HAM capital recycling:** Rs 1.52 lakh crore monetised sector-wide (AR sheet 11)
  is a funding-cycle lever that could relax Ceigall's OWN capability-filter
  constraint (Section 3A) by freeing equity capital for new HAM bids — it does not
  create new government demand, so it is not sized as new TAM.

### 5E. Final output card

- Conservative TAM: **Rs 1,21,999 cr** | Realistic TAM: **Rs 2,44,000 cr**
- SAM: **Rs 40,565 cr** (33.3% of conservative TAM)
- SOM 3yr / 5yr: **Rs 5,353 cr / Rs 6,421 cr**
- SOM-implied revenue CAGR: **~10.0% (3yr) / ~9.8% (5yr)**
- Runway classification: **GOOD**

**Valuation implication line:** At approximately **10% revenue CAGR** implied by
SOM, against a consolidated EBITDA margin trajectory that has been DECLINING (17.1%
FY24 -> 15.1% FY25 -> 14.6% FY26, Inv. Pres. p.36), the earnings growth embedded here
tracks close to 10% CAGR or below (margin compression would push PAT growth under
revenue growth), which **does not support** a growth-premium valuation multiple, and
falls short of both management's own minimum-15% FY27 revenue guidance and the
strategy's 25% CAGR hurdle. Current CMP/P/E was **NOT FOUND** in this stage's inputs
(no price data supplied); Stage 11 should apply this ~10% SOM-implied CAGR directly
against the traded multiple at valuation time to complete this sentence.
