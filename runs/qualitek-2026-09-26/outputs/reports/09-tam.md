# Stage 9 — TAM / SAM / SOM Market Sizing: Qualitek Labs Ltd (QUALITEK)
Run date: 2026-09-26 | Model: claude-sonnet-5 | All figures ₹ Cr unless stated. USD/INR = 96 (Federal Reserve H.10, 21-Sep-2026; used for every $ conversion below, flagged where used).

## SECTION 1: MARKET DEFINITION

### 1A. Precise boundaries
- **Product scope**: third-party, accredited (NABL for testing / NABCB Type A for inspection) product-compliance testing, homologation, inspection and certification services, sold on a fee-per-assignment or purchase-order basis. Excludes: in-house/captive testing done by manufacturers themselves, calibration-only services, and non-accredited/informal testing.
- **Geographic scope**: India only for the headline TAM (Qualitek has no material overseas revenue today; FY31 ambition of ≥30% overseas revenue is a SAM-expansion lever, not part of the current addressable base — Section 5D).
- **Customer scope**: manufacturers and processors in transport/defence/mechanical/E&E (Qualitek FY26 mix 40-45%), food & feed processors and exporters (25-30%), and pharma/life-sciences companies (15-20%) (FY26 deck, inputs/presentation/20260521-...txt). Government/PSU counterparties (e.g. IOCL) sit inside these verticals as customers, not a separate scope.
- **Channel scope**: outsourced third-party labs (own labs + government PPP-operated labs), not manufacturer in-house labs.
- **Price segment**: mid-market accredited testing; excludes premium global-scope certification (e.g. full USFDA drug-approval testing suites, aerospace/nuclear-grade testing) where Qualitek holds no accreditation.
- **Explicit exclusions**: calibration-only market, medical/clinical diagnostics (Metropolis/Krsnaa-type), construction/infrastructure inspection (a market segment the sourced reports show as the single largest by application — DRHP p.128, "infrastructure segment dominated the market"), and oil & gas/mining/textile-specific TIC verticals beyond the one named IOCL fuel-testing work order.

### 1B. Management's own TAM claim
Qualitek states **no quantified industry TAM, market-size, or CAGR figure anywhere in the AR, MD&A, or investor decks** (confirmed independently in this stage's read of the FY26 AR MD&A and both decks, and consistent with B05-concall's finding: "no quantified industry growth rate ever cited anywhere in its filings," peer_questions row 4). The only industry-level statement is qualitative: "long-term fundamentals of the Indian TIC industry remain favourable... supported by India's expanding manufacturing base" (DRHP, p.[123-131] paraphrase area) and a similar unquantified line in the FY26 AR. **Credibility read: N/A — no claim exists to grade.** This is itself notable: a company setting a Rs 500 Cr FY31 revenue ambition (FY26 AR MD&A) never states what fraction of any addressable market that would represent.

The one number in the DRHP that *looks* like a TAM claim — "the Indian TIC market size was valued at USD 26 million in 2021 and is projected to reach USD 47 million by 2030... CAGR of 6.9%" (DRHP p.[153], citing straitsresearch.com) — is **not credible as stated** and is **not management's own claim** (it is a third-party report Qualitek's banker inserted into the "Industry Overview" chapter, standard SME-IPO boilerplate, not a company assertion). Re-checked live: the same source firm (Straits Research) currently publishes the India TIC market at **USD 31.76 billion (2024) rising to USD 61.90 billion (2033) at a 6.9% CAGR** — the identical CAGR, confirming the DRHP text is a **unit transcription error (million should read billion)**, not a genuine $26-47mn market claim. Flagged STALE (2023 source) and ERROR; excluded from the headline TAM and not treated as a management claim.

### Method 4 — Import substitution
**Not applicable.** TIC is a domestically-delivered accredited service; there is no import/domestic-production/consumption split to construct (a small, indirect exception is that some Indian exporters currently send products abroad for certain foreign-standard certifications — e.g., US/EU-specific marks — which is a modest upside SAM-expansion lever, not an import-substitution TAM component; carried into Section 5D).

### Method 5 — Global benchmark
- Global TIC market: ~USD 265.6 bn (2025, mid-estimate across three sources found: USD 262.97 bn to USD 417.8 bn range; wide divergence noted, not resolved further given time budget).
- China TIC market: USD 39.58 bn (2025) → **Rs 3,79,968 Cr**, on a population base (~1.41 bn) close to India's (~1.45 bn).
- If India's TIC market matched China's on a per-capita basis today, India's market would be **roughly Rs 3.8 lakh Cr — 3-5x the highest of the four India-specific top-down estimates** (Straits' own Rs 3,04,896 Cr). This is a **directional growth-driver signal** (India is at an early stage of TIC-market development relative to a comparable-population, more industrialised peer), not a current-year TAM input; it is not triangulated into the headline number because it measures potential catch-up, not the market as it exists today.

## SECTION 3: SAM & SOM

### 3A. SAM — five filters applied to realistic TAM (Rs 1,22,880 Cr)
1. **Product/application fit** (transport-defence-mechanical-E&E + food & feed + pharma verticals only, vs the full TIC market where infrastructure/construction "dominated the market" per DRHP and other applications — oil & gas, mining, textiles, general consumer/retail, environmental-only — make up the balance): **0.40**. Basis: DRHP names infrastructure and automotive as the two largest applications by CAGR ranking; Qualitek's named verticals are a plausible ~40% slice of total applications, no filed India-specific revenue-share-by-application table exists (NOT FOUND at the precision of an exact %; this is a bounded estimate, shown as such).
→ 1,22,880 × 0.40 = **49,152**
2. **Channel/sourcing fit** (outsourced-to-third-party-lab spend only, since DRHP states "the in-house segment is expected to dominate the market"): **0.50**.
→ 49,152 × 0.50 = **24,576**
3. **Geography fit**: TAM is already India-scoped and Qualitek is pan-India; **1.00** (no cut; overseas is an expansion lever, Section 5D, not a current cut).
4. **Customer/accreditation fit** (Qualitek's specific NABL/NABCB scope does not cover every accreditation class in its own verticals — e.g., full USFDA drug-approval-grade pharma testing, aerospace/defence-grade specs beyond its current scope): **0.85**.
→ 24,576 × 0.85 = **20,890**
5. **Capability/capacity fit** (16 labs — 10 owned + 6 government PPP — against a national accredited-lab base in the low thousands; a modest cut for network breadth Qualitek cannot yet physically reach): **0.90**.
→ 20,890 × 0.90 = **18,801 ≈ Rs 18,800 Cr**

**SAM = Rs 18,800 Cr = 15.3% of realistic TAM.**

### 3B. SOM at 3 and 5 years
- **Current SAM share**: Rs 124.52 Cr / Rs 18,800 Cr = **0.66%**.
- Share-gain rule applied: **"normal" band (1-2pp over the horizon)**, not the aggressive 3-5pp band, because Qualitek's disclosed capacity/cash-conversion constraints (CFO/EBITDA 0.46, FCF -Rs 31 Cr, debtor days 124-150 — B00 LBF2) argue against assuming aggressive, unconstrained share capture even though the company has demonstrated fast M&A-led growth.
- **3-year**: SAM grows at the Mordor 6.46% CAGR to Rs 22,682 Cr (18,800 × 1.0646³). Share gain +0.80pp → 1.46% share.
  **SOM 3yr = 22,682 × 0.0146 = Rs 331 Cr.**
  Implied CAGR from FY26 revenue Rs 124.52 Cr: (331/124.52)^(1/3) − 1 = **38.6%**.
- **5-year**: SAM grows to Rs 25,700 Cr (18,800 × 1.0646⁵). Share gain +1.30pp → 1.96% share.
  **SOM 5yr = 25,700 × 0.0196 = Rs 504 Cr.**
  Implied CAGR: (504/124.52)^(1/5) − 1 = **32.3%**.
- **Cross-check against management's own ambition**: management's Rs 500 Cr FY31 (5-year) revenue target (FY26 AR MD&A) sits almost exactly at this stage's independently-modelled SOM 5yr of Rs 504 Cr, and the FY27 single-year guide of +35-40% revenue growth (B05-concall) brackets the modelled 3yr CAGR of 38.6%. This convergence, reached from a completely different starting point (market-share-gain arithmetic vs management's internal plan), is a meaningful cross-check that neither figure is wildly implausible — though both share the same underlying assumption that continued M&A, not organic growth, funds most of it (Section 3C).

### 3C. Capacity cross-check
Qualitek runs 16 labs (10 owned + 6 government PPP) generating Rs 124.52 Cr — **~Rs 7.8 Cr revenue/lab** (average, heavily blended across vertical and lab size; no per-lab disclosure exists to refine this). At the same average revenue/lab, the SOM 3yr figure of Rs 331 Cr would require **~42 labs — roughly 2.6x the current network**, and SOM 5yr (Rs 504 Cr) would require **~65 labs — roughly 4x**.
FY26 capex was Rs 51.3 Cr and the FY27 plan is Rs 63 Cr (B07 injected figure) — Rs 114.3 Cr cumulative over two years. No cost-per-new-lab figure is disclosed anywhere in the corpus (B04 input_gaps: "no lab capacity-utilisation percentage disclosed"), so the exact ₹ Cr capacity gap **cannot be quantified precisely — NOT FOUND**. Directionally: building ~26-49 additional labs organically from a Rs 114.3 Cr two-year capex budget is implausible at any realistic per-lab cost the corpus can support (Qualitek's own 6 most recent labs, per the H1 FY26 MD&A, were built for a combined annualised revenue potential of Rs 120 Cr — implying organic lab-building alone, at the demonstrated pace, gets the company only partway to SOM 3yr, let alone SOM 5yr).
**Read: the SOM path is the optimistic side of this comparison.** It is achievable only if Qualitek continues acquiring labs/entities at roughly the pace already demonstrated (ITCPL, QTIPL, LabOps Global, plus the pending Hyderabad target — four to five entities in ~26 months per B00 LBF1/B05). The capex plan alone (organic-only) is the conservative, capacity-constrained side.

## SECTION 4: GROWTH DRIVERS, RISKS & STRUCTURE

### 4A. TAM growth drivers
- **Regulatory tailwind** (strong, evidenced): BIS mandatory Quality Control Orders and FSSAI licensing/safety mandates are named repeatedly as the primary demand driver in every industry source searched; each new QCO notification mechanically creates new mandatory-testing demand.
- **Formalisation** (strong, evidenced): DRHP names the in-house segment as currently dominant; government policy push toward accredited third-party testing (a stated driver in the sourced reports) is the single largest addressable-market-expansion lever for outsourced players like Qualitek.
- **Per-capita catch-up** (directional, Method 5): India's TIC market is a fraction of China's on a comparable population base — a multi-decade runway, not a near-term one.
- **New applications** (moderate, evidenced): EV/battery testing, cybersecurity-linked testing ("technology will play an increasingly important role," DRHP p.[91]) are named growth vectors not yet reflected in Qualitek's current revenue mix.
- **Geographic expansion** (moderate, management-stated): FY31 ambition of ≥30% overseas revenue (FY26 AR MD&A) — unquantified, no overseas revenue base disclosed today.
- **Demographics/manufacturing base** (moderate, evidenced): DRHP and AR both cite India's expanding manufacturing base and PLI-linked capex as a driver context, consistent with the general macro picture (not TIC-specific data).
- Premiumisation and import substitution: **not material/not applicable** to this service category (Section 2, Method 4).

### 4B. TAM risks
- **Disruption via insourcing**: the DRHP's own note that "in-house segment is expected to dominate the market, registering the highest CAGR" is a structural risk to the outsourced-TIC addressable pool — large OEMs building captive labs shrinks Qualitek's SAM over time. Monitor: any named large-customer captive-lab announcement.
- **Regulatory headwind (reversal risk)**: DRHP's own "Market Restraints" section names "relaxation of certification norms offered to SME's by some Government Authorities" as a market-growth constraint. Monitor: any BIS/FSSAI relaxation notification for SME manufacturers.
- **Cyclical downturn**: new-product testing volume tracks the manufacturing capex/new-launch cycle; a manufacturing slowdown (IIP deceleration) reduces testing volume with a lag. Monitor: IIP manufacturing sub-index trend (Section 6).
- **Import competition / substitution**: low relevance (service is delivered locally); a longer-horizon risk is AI/automated compliance-checking tools reducing the need for physical third-party testing in some categories — speculative, no evidence found either way.
- **Saturation**: not a near-term risk given the revenue-headroom multiple computed in Section 5B.
- **Environmental restriction**: net driver, not risk, per the sourced reports (environmental testing demand is rising).

### 4C. Market structure
- **Concentration**: fragmented. The named organised global players (SGS, Intertek, TÜV Rheinland, Bureau Veritas, DNV, Eurofins, DEKRA) each hold a low single-digit-percent share of even the conservative Rs 75,840 Cr TAM estimate (the four with a found revenue figure sum to ~Rs 1,966 Cr, each under 3% of the conservative TAM individually).
- **Organised vs unorganised**: DRHP's own text implies unorganised/in-house dominates; no precise India-TIC-specific % split was found in this search pass (flagged gap).
- **Consolidating or fragmenting**: consolidating at the margin via bolt-on M&A — Qualitek's own strategy (four acquisitions in ~26 months, B00 LBF1) is itself evidence of this pattern, not merely a market observation.
- **Price vs differentiation competition**: differentiation-led in Qualitek's higher-accreditation segments (NABCB Type A, USFDA-linked pharma lab); price-competitive in commodity BIS-mandated testing.
- **Entries/exits**: no named entries or exits found in this search pass beyond Qualitek's own acquisitive expansion.
- **Import share trend**: not applicable (Section 2, Method 4).

## SECTION 5: SUMMARY & RUNWAY

### 5A. Funnel
```
TAM (India, all TIC segments, realistic)         Rs 1,22,880 Cr
  → SAM (5 filters applied, 15.3% of TAM)        Rs    18,800 Cr
    → SOM 3yr (1.46% of grown SAM)               Rs       331 Cr
    → SOM 5yr (1.96% of grown SAM)                Rs       504 Cr
      → Current revenue (FY26, 0.66% of SAM)      Rs    124.52 Cr
```

### 5B. Runway assessment
- **Revenue headroom** = SAM ÷ current revenue = 18,800 / 124.52 = **151x**.
- **TAM growth rate**: 6.46% CAGR (Mordor, 2025-2030, the realistic anchor's own forecast).
- **Company CAGR vs TAM**: FY26 consolidated revenue grew +77% YoY (B00 LBF1) — vastly outpacing the 6.46% market CAGR. This is **share-gaining, not market-riding growth**, and per B04/B05 is predominantly acquisition-driven rather than organic (flagged, not re-litigated here).
- **Years to saturate SAM** at a sustained ~30-40% CAGR (the SOM-implied range): roughly **15-19 years** (ln(151)/ln(1.30) ≈ 19.1 yrs; ln(151)/ln(1.40) ≈ 14.9 yrs). Even after the 5-year SOM target is hit, the great majority of SAM remains unaddressed.

### 5C. Runway classification
**MASSIVE.** A 151x revenue-to-SAM headroom multiple, a market growing steadily (not shrinking or saturating), and a company currently capturing well under 1% of its own realistically-filtered addressable market together place this at the top of the standard runway matrix. This classification is about the size of the opportunity, not about whether the company can execute against it (Section 3C already names the execution/capacity constraint).

### 5D. SAM expansion levers actually being pursued
1. **Overseas revenue** (FY31 ambition ≥30% of revenue, FY26 AR MD&A) — expands the geography filter beyond India-only TAM. Potential addition: **NOT FOUND** (no overseas TAM figure disclosed or found; management gives no quantified target market).
2. **New inspection/audit/certification vertical** (NABCB Type A accreditation obtained Jun-2026) — expands the product/service-type filter within the existing customer base. Potential addition: **NOT FOUND** (zero revenue disclosed for this line to date, B04 revenue_streams).
3. **QTIPL (digital/technology solutions) and LabOps Global (lab-operations outsourcing)** — both expand the addressable revenue-stream definition beyond core lab testing. Potential addition: **NOT FOUND** (both zero-revenue-disclosed lines per B04).
4. **Continued bolt-on M&A into Pharma/Food/E&E** funded by the Rs 63 Cr FY27 capex plan (B05 trigger 4) — expands product/customer-fit filter incrementally with each named acquisition.
All four levers are **directionally real but currently unquantified** in the corpus; none should be added to the headline SAM/SOM numbers above without a disclosed revenue or market-size figure specific to each.
