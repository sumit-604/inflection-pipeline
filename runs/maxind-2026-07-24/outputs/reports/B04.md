## SECTION 1: THE BUSINESS MODEL IN PLAIN ENGLISH

### 1A. One-line description
Max India Ltd is a holding company that owns 100% of three operating
businesses under the "Antara" brand — a real-estate-plus-annuity senior
residences developer, an occupancy-based assisted-care operator, and a D2C
senior-care consumer products company — bundled with a treasury/business
investments book (AR Note 39, p.287; Inv. Pres. slide 6).

### 1E. The chai-stall-uncle version
Imagine three shops run by the same family under one signboard: one shop sells
retirement flats to old people who want safety railings and a clubhouse
(and gets a small cut for managing similar flats built by a bigger builder
next door); one shop rents out hospital-like rooms by the day to old people
who need daily nursing care (like a paying-guest home with a nurse on call);
and one shop sells walking sticks, knee braces and vitamin tablets online to
old people everywhere in India. All three shops serve the same customer —
India's fast-growing population of seniors — but one shop makes money from
selling a flat once, one shop makes money from how full its rooms are each
night, and one shop makes money from how many packets it ships and how
cheaply it can get someone to click "buy". Right now the family (Max India)
is still building all three shops, so all three are losing money, but the
building next door is being paid for mostly with money the shopkeeper
collected from customers in advance.

### 2D. Industry lifecycle stage
Early growth / emerging stage. Organised senior-living penetration is
1.3-1.5% of the addressable market vs 6-7%+ in mature markets (AR p.33; Inv.
Pres. slide 6), and the company itself frames FY25 as "Antara 3.0's intensive
execution year" of "accelerated geographic expansion" (AR p.42) — i.e. the
company is still in capacity build-out, not harvest. All three verticals are
consolidated-EBITDA-loss-making (FY25 EBITDA loss Rs99.2 Cr, AR p.31), which
is itself consistent with an early-stage industry position rather than a
mature, cash-harvesting one.

### 3D. Unit economics — this is a sum-of-parts business with THREE distinct "units"

occupancy-based service and a consumer product would misstate the physics of
each.

### 4B. Valuation method applicability table
**NOTE FOR PIPELINE:** this is a formal handoff to the Role 1 valuation stage.
Standard trailing/forward P/E is explicitly **NOT** the primary method for
this name — the group is consolidated-EBITDA-loss-making (FY25 EBITDA loss
Rs99.2 Cr, AR p.31), and a single blended earnings multiple would obscure
three fundamentally different unit economics.

| Method | Applicability | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Sum-of-the-parts (SOTP) | **PRIMARY** | The only method that respects the fact that Residences (real-estate/DM-fee annuity), Assisted Care (occupancy-ramp services) and AGEasy (D2C consumer products) have different WC intensity, pricing power and correct valuation logic; treasury/cash valued separately at book |
| Asset-based / invested-capital cross-check | **SECONDARY** | Company itself discloses segment invested capital as of Mar-26: Senior Living ~Rs490 Cr, Assisted Care ~Rs484 Cr, plus Rs58 Cr other assets and Rs58 Cr cash (Inv. Pres. slide 36) — a useful floor/sanity check against the SOTP build, not a substitute for it |
| Forward EV/Sales (segment-level, not blended) | **TERTIARY** | Usable only once each vertical's revenue run-rate stabilises and only applied per-segment (e.g., AGEasy against D2C/consumer-wellness peers, Care Homes against occupancy-stage senior-care peers) — a blended consolidated EV/Sales figure would still mix three different margin profiles and should be avoided |
| Trailing/forward consolidated P/E | **NOT APPLICABLE** | Consolidated entity is loss-making; even if/when it turns profitable, a single P/E would blend the annuity-like Residences DM/maintenance income with the still-scaling, higher-risk Care Homes and AGEasy businesses |
| Dividend discount model | **NOT APPLICABLE** | No dividend paid or plausible during the current investment phase |
| Blended consolidated EV/EBITDA | **NOT APPLICABLE** | Consolidated EBITDA is negative and, even once positive, would blend a real-estate-annuity margin profile with occupancy-ramp and consumer-products margin profiles — apply EV/EBITDA only within each vertical once each is individually EBITDA-positive |
| Replacement cost (real estate) | Usable as a secondary check specifically for the Residences vertical's land/development-in-progress, not for the group as a whole | Not separately disclosed at a granular enough level in the documents provided in this run to build independently — treat as a stage-11 exercise using the segment invested-capital figures as a starting point |

Cycle stage that matters for valuation: **early growth/investment phase**
(see 2D) — valuation should be anchored to the company's own disclosed
milestones (Noida Phase 1 possession/handover, E361 sell-through, Care Home
occupancy ramp toward the 65-70% "mature" band, AGEasy path to the guided
Q4FY27 EBITDA breakeven), not to current trailing profitability.
