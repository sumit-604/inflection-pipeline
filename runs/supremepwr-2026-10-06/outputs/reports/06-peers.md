## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

**2A Demand.** Peers agree demand is firm. Shilchar order visibility about Rs 800 Cr for FY27 (Q4 FY26, p.3). Danish book over Rs 500 Cr (H2 FY26, p.4). Both still missed their own revenue guides. Shilchar guided Rs 750 Cr for FY26 (Q4 FY25 p.11, Q2 FY26 p.3) and reported Rs 652 Cr (Q4 FY26 p.2). Its FY27 target fell from Rs 850-900 Cr to Rs 800 Cr (Q4 FY26, analyst Garvit Goyal, p.9). Its Q1 FY27 revenue was Rs 134.60 Cr (p.2), about 60-65% utilisation (p.11). Danish cut its FY26 guide from about Rs 600 Cr to Rs 500-550 Cr and reported Rs 521 Cr consolidated (H1 FY26 p.15; H2 FY26 p.4). Causes were external: monsoon, West Asia shipping, late commissioning. The peers support SPEL's "demand is fine, delivery is slow" framing. They also show guide cuts are normal here, so SPEL's guidance cascade (B05 grade C) is not unique, but SPEL's three-quarter pattern of landing at the floor is not peer-tested.

**2B Pricing and inputs.** Oil about doubled from February to May 2026 and other inputs rose 10-25% (Shilchar Q4 FY26, p.7-8). Danish new orders carry 5-18% price rises (H2 FY26, p.14). Pass-through lagged: Shilchar passed 50-60% on older orders (Q1 FY27, p.5). Domestic prices showed "no pressure" (Shilchar Q1 FY27, p.9). Brand premium is small: "1%, 2%, 3% maximum" (Shilchar Q4 FY25, p.18). Peers carry real fixed-price exposure. SPEL's 80-85% PVC story is the exception and drives its margin claim.

**2C Capex cycle.** An industry-wide capacity race, not a lone expander. Shilchar goes from 7,500 to 14,000 MVA by April 2027 (Q2 FY26 p.3; capex Rs 90 Cr then about Rs 120 Cr, Q4 FY26 p.3) and bought 4.5 more acres (Q1 FY27 p.6). Danish reached about 11,000 MVA in 2026, bought land and may add 80-100% more (H2 FY26 p.2, p.11). An analyst notes top-10 players adding about 50% in three years (Shilchar Q4 FY25, p.7). Waaree entered by acquisition (Danish H1 FY26 p.36). Both peers deny oversupply for 3-4 years (Shilchar Q2 FY26 p.8) or "four or five years" (Danish H1 FY26 p.37). SPEL's Kannur plant is a small addition inside this wave. Peers fund from accruals. SPEL used term debt (B05), which gives it less room if the cycle turns.

**2D Competitive mentions.** No peer names SPEL. Shilchar names Raychem, Danish, Transformer and Rectifier (Q4 FY25 p.17). Danish names Shilchar, Wilson Power, Crompton, CG, Atlanta Electric (H1 FY26 p.10) and Waaree. SPEL is outside the peer competitive set, which fits a different segment (power and distribution transformers for utilities).

**2E Risks peers raise that SPEL does not.**
- West Asia shipping disruption and 3-5 times container cost cutting dispatches (Shilchar Q1 FY27 p.3).
- Fixed-price orders hit by an input spike, with customers asked to share the cost (Danish H2 FY26 p.14; Shilchar Q4 FY26 p.15).
- Product concentration: Danish IDT at 65-70% and wants it capped (H1 FY26 p.14-15). Shilchar single-product exposure raised by an analyst (Q2 FY26 p.8).
- New capacity commissioned late: Danish phase 1 about four months late, FY26 guide cut (H1 FY26 p.15).
- Entry-segment margin concessions for 220 kV (Danish H1 FY26 p.17; Shilchar Q1 FY27 p.6).
- 220 kV bushing shortage and long lead (Shilchar Q4 FY26 p.13).
- Monsoon timing and H2-heavy revenue (Danish H1 FY26 p.4).
- Government discom payment delays (Danish H1 FY26 p.29).
- Utilisation disclosed on dispatch, not production, which confuses reads (Shilchar Q4 FY26 p.13).
- New entrants by acquisition (Danish H1 FY26 p.36).

## PART 4: TRIANGULATION SUMMARY

- Claims verified: 2 of 13 verdict lines (Q1a, Q4). Partially verified: 8. Contradicted: 3. Unverifiable as a whole line: 0. Three sub-points are NOT FOUND: Kannur-type ramp path of 20/35/50, the 220 kV prototype cost and CPRI time, wage ramp per head.
- Claims contradicted, priority items for synthesis: Q1b (12-24 month lead time), Q3a (80-85% PVC as a common level), Q6 (1-2 point MVA margin gain).
- Most consequential contradiction. Danish reports about 30% of its Rs 500 Cr book on price variation and Shilchar reports "some" (Q3a). SPEL reports 80-85%. SPEL's margin stability rests on that figure. If it is wrong, Q1 FY27 and Q2 FY27 margins carry the oil and copper spike peers already absorbed.
- Strongest confirmation. CRGO and general material supply is free-flowing at both peers, and the 220 kV entry barrier (cost, testing, audits, trial orders) is described the same way by both (Q4, Q5). Both peers also back a 5-7 year demand run (Q1a).
- Overall. The peer set supports the demand story and the plant ramp base rate. It complicates the margin story: peers say margin follows customer quality and mix, not MVA size, and entry into 220 kV costs margin first. It undercuts the lead time and PVC coverage claims as industry-wide facts. Peer data does not test SPEL's government exposure, receivable days or Danya Electric. The peers are IDT-led, so mapping is partial.
