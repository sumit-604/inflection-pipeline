## Business Understanding Narrative

**What does it do, and how does it make money?** Since the scheme, the company runs only Daurala Sugar Works: sugar, alcohol and co-generated power at one site (AR26, p.76). Capacity is 12,500 tonnes of cane a day, a 215 KLPD distillery and 94 MW of co-generation (CARE-26, p.1). It reports one segment, "Sugar (including distillery)" (FY26 res, p.8). FY26 revenue was Rs 1,160.12 crore and profit Rs 41.61 crore (AR26, p.29). The money is the spread between a state-set cane price and sugar, ethanol and power prices, plus mill recovery of 10.39% (AR26, p.31).

**What is changing?** The perimeter. A composite scheme merged Lily Commercial Pvt Ltd, the promoters' holding company, into the company. It then demerged the Chemical undertaking into DCM Shriram Fine Chemicals Ltd (DSFCL) and the Rayon undertaking, with Engineering Projects, into DCM Shriram International Ltd (DSIL) (AR26, p.29). The NCLT sanctioned it on 21-Nov-2025 (Sanction, p.1). It took effect on 17-Dec-2025, with an appointed date of 1-Apr-2023 (Eff, p.1). Holders received one DSFCL and one DSIL share per share on the record date of 26-Dec-2025 (CoA, p.2). The investment in DCM Hyundai went to DSIL and Daurala Foods went to DSFCL; the company now has no subsidiary (AR26, pp.31-32).

**What left and what stayed.** In FY25 the old company earned segment results of Rs 77.09 crore in sugar, Rs 108.30 crore in chemicals and Rs 39.29 crore in rayon (AR25, p.118). The listed entity kept 34% of the profit pool [INFERENCE: derived]. Restated FY25 profit is Rs 30.18 crore against Rs 100.30 crore as first published (FY26 res, p.9). Tax-cost guidance gives the listed entity 42.66% of a holder's cost, DSFCL 25.22% and DSIL 32.12% (CoA, p.3).

**Why now?** FY26 profit rose 38% on revenue up 7.4% (FY26 res, p.7). The stock trades between Rs 31.9 and Rs 66.3, and both exchanges queried price and volume moves in October 2026 (MD; Surv-1, p.1; Surv-2, p.1). The company replied that only "the general perception about the sugar industry" applied (Surv-2, p.1).

**What must be true?** The 2026-27 crushing season must repeat or beat FY26 recovery and spread. Cane price rises must be offset by sugar or ethanol prices. The unnamed buyer that indemnifies Rs 371.11 crore of GST provisions must stay solvent (AR26, p.144).

**What breaks it?** A season like FY24, when this sugar business earned Rs 1.76 crore (CARE-26, p.4). A higher UP cane price with no sugar MSP change. A Supreme Court GST loss with a failed indemnity.

## 2. Business model and archetype

Product mix by line is not filed after the scheme. CARE puts FY25 sugar and co-generation at about 85% of revenue and distillery at about 15% (CARE-26, p.2). Sugar production was 21.11 lakh quintals from 203.15 lakh quintals of cane in FY26 (AR26, p.31). Distillery output rose 7% to 25,172 KL (AR26, p.31). The mill crushes on a C-heavy molasses basis (AR26, p.31).

Power and steam go to DSFCL's chemical units next door, about Rs 40 crore a year at grid rates, under clause 6.8.1 of the scheme (AR26, p.36). FY26 sales to DSFCL were Rs 35.94 crore (AR26, p.137).

**Archetype: Commodity converter** (Amendment 17 binds). Spread is cane price against sugar, ethanol and power realisation; utilisation is crush days and recovery; cycle position is the sugar season. A regulated-price layer sits over it: the state sets cane price (SAP) and the centre sets sugar MSP, export quotas and ethanol prices (AR26, p.77).

## 3. Competitive advantages

**Cost position: partial.** Recovery of 10-11% from irrigated western UP cane (CARE-26, p.1). Plant runs fully on agro-fuels (AR26, p.80). **Pricing power: absent.** Management names the "mismatch between cane prices" and sugar realisations as a weakness (AR26, p.77). **Scale: single mill**, a weakness CARE names (CARE-26, p.3). **Captive demand: present but small.** The DSFCL power and steam contract is about 3% of revenue [INFERENCE: derived from AR26, pp.29, 36].

**Verdict: no moat. A mid-recovery UP mill with an integrated distillery.**

## 10. Independent check: the credit rating

CARE downgraded the long-term rating from A+ to A-, Stable, on 01-Apr-2026, and the short-term rating from A1+ to A2+ (Rate, p.1). The reason is the scheme: "narrower scale of operations and reduced benefits of diversification" (CARE-26, p.1). The watch had been negative since the scheme was proposed (CARE-25, p.1). CARE credits promoter track record, integration and recoveries. It flags cyclicality, single-mill risk, working-capital intensity and regulation (CARE-26, pp.1-3). FY25 net debt to PBILDT was 5.14x (CARE-26, p.2). Downgrade triggers: gearing above 1.4x or net debt to PBILDT above 5x (CARE-26, p.1). CARE expects cash accruals of Rs 60-70 crore in FY26-FY27 (CARE-26, p.3).
