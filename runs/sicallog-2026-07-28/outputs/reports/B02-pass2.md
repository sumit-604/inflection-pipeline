NEW FINDING 1 -- Cash losses widened 45.6% YoY, contradicting the "narrowing loss" reading of the P&L
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
🔴 The Statutory Auditor's Annexure A (CARO) to the standalone Auditor's Report, clause (xvii), separately discloses actual **cash losses** of **Rs 30.37 Cr (FY25) vs Rs 20.87 Cr (FY24), +45.6% YoY** (Rs 3,037 lakh vs Rs 2,087 lakh) (Annexure A to Standalone Auditor's Report, cl. xvii, p.79). This is a CARO-mandated disclosure directly cross-referenced by the notes' loss trend and is materially relevant to the FLAG-CASH question: Pass 1's Note 24 finding showed the reported/accounting loss narrowing modestly year-on-year aided by exceptional-item provision reversals (Rs 9.67 Cr FY25 vs Rs 14.38 Cr FY24). The CARO cash-loss figure shows the opposite direction on a cash basis: actual cash burn accelerated by nearly half again, even as the P&L narrative (helped by non-cash provision reversals) looked flat-to-improving. Reading only the P&L, an investor would miss that the underlying cash outflow is getting worse, not better.

NEW FINDING 2 -- Rs 100 Cr guarantee and Rs 8.43 Cr interest-free advance both identified by name: Sical Multimodal and Rail Transport Ltd (SMART), with a related-party funds pass-through
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
🔴 Pass 1 flagged the Rs 100.00 Cr standalone guarantee (Note 25) as generic "other bodies corporate." CARO Annexure A, clause iii(a), names the counterparty explicitly: **Corporate Guarantee to Sical Multimodal and Rail Transport Limited ("SMART", a step-down subsidiary)**, Rs 25.00 Cr given during the year, cumulative balance outstanding Rs 100.00 Cr (Annexure A, cl. iii(a), p.78) -- i.e. the entire guarantee balance flagged in Pass 1 as ~355% of standalone net worth is concentrated in one step-down subsidiary.
🔴 Same clause discloses an **advance to SMART of Rs 5.50 Cr during the year, balance outstanding Rs 8.43 Cr** (matches Pass 1's Note 28(vii) related-party-loan figure of Rs 8.54 Cr, now identified by name) -- **repayable on demand or with no stipulated repayment terms whatsoever**. The auditor states explicitly: "as the repayment terms are not stipulated, we are unable to comment on whether it is overdue for a period of more than 90 days" (cl. iii(c)-(e), p.78).
🔴 CARO clause ix(e) reveals a **fund pass-through**: the standalone Company itself took Rs 5.50 Cr of funds FROM its "Holding Company" during the year specifically "to meet the obligation of Subsidiary" SMART (cl. ix(e), p.80). Read together with the item above, this describes a channel of Rs 5.50 Cr flowing from the Pristine-group holding company, through Sical Logistics Limited, down to SMART -- the parent is functioning as a conduit for holding-company-to-subsidiary financing rather than funding the advance from its own operating cash. This related-party fund-flow architecture is not visible anywhere in the Note 28 RPT tables as presented and only surfaces via the CARO cross-reference.

NEW FINDING 3 -- Standalone has not filed quarterly returns/statements with banks since CIRP initiation; contrasts with the subsidiary-level HDFC facility, which is compliant
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
🟡 CARO Annexure A, clause ii(b): "the Company has not been sanctioned working capital limits in excess of Rs Five crores... on the basis of security of current assets during the year. In respect of loans sanctioned during earlier years, **the quarterly returns or statements have not been filed by the Company with the banks from the date of initiation of CIRP**" (Annexure A, cl. ii(b), p.78; cross-refers to standalone Note 14.1). This is a live, ongoing compliance gap (not merely historical CIRP-era language) that Pass 1 did not surface because it sits in the audit Annexure rather than the notes proper, even though the notes cross-reference it.
🟡 By contrast, at the consolidated level the subsidiary-level HDFC Bank working-capital facility (secured by hypothecation of current assets including book-debts) is explicitly stated to be current on this exact obligation: "the quarterly statements submitted to the banks are in line with the books of accounts" (Consol Note 13.1(iii), p.171) -- a direct compliance-discipline divergence between the loss-making, legacy-debt-carrying parent and its operating subsidiary.

NEW FINDING 4 -- The Rs 67.48 Cr equipment lease carries an annual cash-payment commitment equal to roughly a quarter to a third of standalone revenue
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
🔴 Pass 1 identified the Rs 67.48 Cr ROU addition and flagged its probable link to Pristine Malwa via the AGM Notice, but did not quantify the cash-flow consequence. Note 29(b)-(c) (standalone, p.125) discloses the full lease-liability movement and maturity profile: opening balance nil, additions Rs 67.48 Cr, finance cost Rs 0.56 Cr, payments Rs 1.43 Cr, **closing lease liability Rs 66.61 Cr**, of which **Rs 11.04 Cr falls due within one year and Rs 55.57 Cr in one-to-five years** (nothing beyond five years). Against FY25 standalone revenue from operations of just Rs 40.22 Cr (Note 17), the committed annual lease cash outflow (~Rs 11-14 Cr/year once fully phased, per the AGM Notice's separately-flagged Rs 24.95 Cr/year renewal cap) represents a material and growing claim on a shrinking standalone revenue base, over and above the KLPL/Pristine interest burden already flagged in Pass 1.

NEW FINDING 5 -- ECL allowance for the "Unbilled" receivables bucket has been frozen at an identical rupee amount for two straight years, at both standalone and consolidated level, despite gross unbilled receivables growing ~45-49%
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
🔴 Standalone ageing table (Note 31, p.126): Unbilled bucket -- gross Rs 41.65 Cr (FY25) vs Rs 28.70 Cr (FY24), **+45.2%**, while the loss allowance for this bucket is Rs 28.58 Cr in BOTH years (unchanged to the lakh: Rs 2,858 lakh both years), driving the weighted-average loss rate for this bucket down mechanically from 100% (FY24) to 69% (FY25).
🔴 The identical pattern recurs at consolidated level (Note 31, p.187-188): Unbilled gross Rs 46.63 Cr (FY25) vs Rs 31.23 Cr (FY24), **+49.3%**, with the SAME Rs 28.58 Cr allowance held flat both years, pulling the weighted loss rate down from 91.51% to 61.29%.
This is not explained anywhere in the document. Because the allowance for this specific bucket did not grow at all while the underlying gross balance grew by roughly Rs 13-15 Cr in each scope, the improvement in the bucket's headline loss rate is an artefact of a static, unrefreshed provision rather than a genuine credit-quality improvement -- the opposite conclusion an investor might draw from the declining percentage. This sits underneath the overall 88.7% (standalone) / 74.7% (consolidated) allowance ratios Pass 1 already flagged as declining, and helps explain the mechanism: new unbilled revenue is being added without matching incremental ECL, while the "more than 3 years" legacy bucket (100% provided, static) continues to anchor the headline ratio. A direct management question on the FY25 ECL matrix recalibration for the unbilled category is warranted.
🟡 Partially offsetting: the "Less than 1 year" bucket moved the other way -- standalone loss rate 0% (FY24) to 29% (FY25); consolidated 0% (FY24) to 5.21% (FY25) -- i.e. freshly-aged receivables are being provided MORE conservatively even as unbilled receivables are provided less so. The net effect across buckets is a mixed, non-uniform recalibration rather than a single-direction easing, which is itself worth naming as inconsistent ECL-matrix discipline.

NEW FINDING 6 -- Full CIRP resolution-plan settlement waterfall: Rs 1,720.57 Cr of "securities premium" was generated directly from extinguishing admitted liabilities via equity issuance, bypassing the P&L entirely
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
🟡 Note 35 (standalone, p.127-129), "Implementation of the approved resolution plan," discloses the full historical mechanics behind the current capital structure (background context carried forward each year, not an FY25 transaction, but never surfaced by Pass 1's Note 11 equity discussion):
- Total liabilities admitted by the RP and approved under the NCLT order: Rs 2,102.87 Cr, of which Rs 426.32 Cr was provided for and Rs 1,676.55 Cr assigned to the Resolution Applicant (Pristine Malwa).
- Resolution Applicant consideration for taking on this assigned liability load: Rs 65.00 Cr (upfront infusion) + Re 1 (nominal consideration for the balance).
- 6,19,86,626 equity shares issued to the RA at face value Rs 10 = Rs 61.99 Cr share capital, generating **Rs 1,720.57 Cr of securities premium** recognised directly in reserves as a consequence of the liability-for-equity exchange (Note 35, p.129).
- Total payout waterfall to stakeholders under the plan: CIRP costs + operational creditors Rs 10.68 Cr, financial creditors (upfront + deferred, at 8% p.a. on deferred tranches with a moratorium) Rs 470.14 Cr, cash/CCE transferred to financial creditors Rs 41.01 Cr -- total settlement Rs 521.83 Cr.
This Rs 1,720.57 Cr direct-to-reserves credit is the single largest number in the entire document. It is why the standalone entity's cumulative equity base can absorb a Rs 44.14 Cr FY25 comprehensive loss and still show only a Rs 28.14 Cr closing net worth rather than a much larger negative figure outright -- the base itself was rebuilt from a debt-to-equity conversion, not from retained operating profit. This context materially reframes how thin the buffer against further losses now is: the resolution-plan equity credit is a one-time, non-repeatable capital event, and the current run-rate of losses (Rs 44-45 Cr/year at standalone level per Pass 1) will exhaust the remaining Rs 28.14 Cr buffer within well under one year at the current pace if unchanged.

NEW FINDING 7 -- Borrowings cash-flow reconciliation shows large GROSS repayment activity alongside the RPT-driven additions, nuancing Pass 1's "borrowings surged" framing
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
🟡 The Ind AS 7 financing-activities reconciliation note (Standalone Note 36, p.129-130; Consol Note 40, p.194) shows: standalone borrowings opening Rs 409.52 Cr, **additions Rs 130.00 Cr, repayments Rs (126.68) Cr**, non-cash interest accrual Rs 5.41 Cr, closing Rs 418.25 Cr. Consolidated: opening Rs 496.97 Cr, additions Rs 161.00 Cr, repayments Rs (131.79) Cr, closing Rs 531.59 Cr.
This means gross new borrowing (Rs 130 Cr standalone, almost entirely the KLPL related-party loan Pass 1 flagged) was accompanied by an almost equally large gross repayment of Rs 126.68 Cr during the same year -- consistent with the Rs 105 Cr financial-creditor settlement-account deposit Pass 1 already noted from Note 12.1(f). The NET cash effect of the year's borrowing activity was closer to Rs 3.32 Cr (standalone), even though the outstanding related-party balance rose by roughly Rs 130 Cr. This is consistent with the new KLPL facility substituting for, rather than adding on top of, other (likely bank/FI resolution-plan) debt repayment -- a refinancing-via-related-party read rather than a pure balance-sheet-growth read. It does not eliminate the RPT-fairness concern Pass 1 raised (a 12.5% related-party facility used to fund resolution-plan settlement obligations still concentrates dependency and interest cost with the promoter group), but it refines the "debt surged" framing to "debt was substituted with related-party debt at a specific, disclosed rate."

NEW FINDING 8 -- 13+ immovable properties carried on the books with title deeds still in the names of decades-old predecessor/amalgamated entities
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
🟡 Note 2(b) (standalone, p.96-100) lists title-deed exceptions for land and buildings in Madurai, Tuticorin, Trichy, Chennai (multiple sites), Kolkata (multiple sites) and Mumbai -- all still registered in the names of entities merged into Sical decades ago (South India Corporation [Agencies] Ltd, Tube Suppliers Ltd / Tubes and Malleables Ltd, South India Steel and Sugars Ltd, ACT India) because the name changes were never registered with the relevant land/registering authorities, in some cases dating to a 2006 corporate name change. Every line states "No" under "whether in dispute," so this is not an active legal contest, but it is a chain-of-title gap across a meaningful number of the Company's immovable assets that would need to be cured before any of these specific properties could be sold, mortgaged afresh, or contributed to a joint venture -- a latent monetisation constraint not mentioned in Pass 1's PPE coverage.

No gain/loss on the vehicle deletions, or the counterparty to the disposal, is disclosed.
