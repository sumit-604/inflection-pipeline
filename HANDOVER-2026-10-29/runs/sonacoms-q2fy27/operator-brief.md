### 1. NARRATIVE

1. Sona makes driveline parts for car and two-wheeler makers. Its products are gears, differential assemblies, starter motors and electric traction motors. It also makes railway parts and sensors.
2. Group sales grew 35% on a year ago, to Rs 1,536 Cr. Owners' profit grew 29%, to Rs 223 Cr. Both are records.
3. Profit grew slower than sales. The operating margin fell to 23.2%, from 25.4% a year ago. Without a foreign-exchange gain, it is 22.5%.
4. The cause is mix and input cost. Lower-margin motors grew fastest, and steel, copper and freight cost more. Materials now take 51% of sales, up from 48%.
5. The parent company's own margin fell 3.4 points. The group margin held only because profit in the subsidiaries rose 3.6 times.
6. Cash looks strong on paper: operating cash was 1.48 times profit. But Rs 279 Cr of it came from paying suppliers later. Without that, cash was 0.78 times profit, worse than 1.00 a year ago.
7. Short-term debt doubled to Rs 462 Cr. Cash in hand also rose. The filing does not say why the company borrowed.
8. On the call, management admitted that the promised margin recovery did not come. The robotics production start promised for this quarter was not mentioned, despite five questions on robotics. The radar product came one quarter late.
9. DENSO buys 49% of the electric-motor business at a business value of Rs 1,750 Cr. Sona's 49% share is Rs 857.5 Cr before tax. The first close is due by end-December, maybe January (call). The unit's profit is not disclosed, so nobody can tell yet whether the price is fair.
10. That price is about 2.2 times yearly sales of the two- and three-wheeler EV segment, the nearest filed proxy for the unit. Sona itself trades near 10 times sales. So the deal either sells a thin-margin unit at a fair price, or sells 49% of the fastest-growing business cheaply.
11. The auditors signed their review reports about 33 minutes before the board met. This happened in Q1 too.
12. No thesis-breaking rule fired. Watch the Q3 FY27 operating margin in late January 2027. At 24% or above, with supplier payables flat, the margin floor holds. Below 23%, or 22.5% without forex, the lower margin is the new normal.
