## OPERATOR OVERRIDES

### Override 1 — Operational ROCE basis for Pillar 1

- Draft determination: Pillar 1 ROCE used the statutory ROCE (~8.8% FY26), giving a 60/40 blended ROCE of ~10.9% and a Pillar 1 base of ~13.0x.
- Operator ruling: use operational ROCE only; strip the idle QIP capital from capital employed.
- Operator's stated reasoning (verbatim): "for calculating ROCE, we should calculate only operational ROCE. We should remove this idle QIP cash."
- Effect carried to phase 3: FY26 operational ROCE is about 12% (EBIT 167.66 / operational capital employed ~1,348 Cr, after removing residual cash 183.58, CWIP 256.68, and capex advances 108.43; EBIT already excludes the idle-cash interest income). Blended operational ROCE ~13.6%; Pillar 1 base ~14.3x. Do NOT use statutory 8.8% and do NOT use management's adjusted 20.7%. This also hardens the TEMPORARILY DEPRESSED (not DECLINING) reading, since operational ROCE was low-teens throughout FY22 to FY24.

### Override 2 — Strategic Premium given, set to +3x

- Draft determination: Strategic Premium +2x (conservative end of the "strong franchise, limited competition, documented pricing power" tier). An interim mis-read briefly set it to +0x and was corrected the same session.
- Operator ruling: give the Strategic Premium and raise it; set to +3x (analyst declined the +4 to +6x monopoly tier absent a documented licence).
- Operator's stated reasoning (verbatim): "strategic premium should be given to it. It has got such a strong relation with strategic players." And on the level: "why is the strategic premium just 2? ... such strong relations, there are a lot of them. Don't they have a kind of monopoly?"
- Analyst response carried to phase 3: set to +3x, the middle of the "strong franchise with limited competition and documented pricing power" tier (+2 to +4x), on the qualification lock-in into marquee OEMs (GE Vernova, Siemens Energy, Mitsubishi, Baker Hughes, Rolls Royce, Pratt & Whitney) and the 37% EBITDA margin as documented pricing power. NOT placed in the rare-licence / regulatory-monopoly tier (+4 to +6x): Azad competes globally with Howmet and Precision Castparts, OEMs dual-source, the sole-qualified-Indian-supplier claim is company-informed and unverified by the peer stage, and 81% three-OEM concentration signals customer power rather than supplier monopoly. Top of the defensible range is +4x if the operator weights limited-competition scarcity to the maximum; +5 to +6x requires a documented exclusive licence or regulatory barrier, none found in this run.
