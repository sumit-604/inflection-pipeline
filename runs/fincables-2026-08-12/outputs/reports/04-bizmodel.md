## SECTION 1: THE BUSINESS MODEL IN PLAIN ENGLISH

### 1A. One-line description
Finolex Cables is a backward-integrated Indian manufacturer of electrical
and communication wires/cables that also sells a smaller, growing basket of
branded consumer electrical goods (fans, water heaters, switchgear, lighting)
through the same distribution network (AR p.02; Inv. Pres. slide 13-14).

### 1B. Money flow chain, by revenue stream

**Electrical Wires & Cables (core, ~87% of segment revenue, FY26)**
Copper cathode + PVC/polymer compound (self-made) → drawn, insulated and
extruded into wire/cable at 5 plants (Roorkee, Urse, Pimpri, Verna, Ponda)
→ delivered as house wire, power/control cable, auto/battery cable, solar
cable → sold to distributors/dealers/retailers and institutional/OEM/
government buyers (real estate, construction, auto, power utilities) → paid
mostly cash-and-carry at retail, agreed credit terms for institutional/OEM
(AR p.06, p.35, p.41; Inv. Pres. slide 5).

**Communication Cables (~8% of segment revenue, FY26)**
Optic fibre preform/glass + copper → drawn into optic fibre cable, LAN,
coaxial, CCTV, speaker and legacy jelly-filled telephone cable (JFTC) →
delivered to telecom operators, ISPs, PSUs (e.g. BSNL) and system
integrators → paid per contract/tender terms, exposed to public-sector
payment cycles (AR p.37-38; Inv. Pres. slide 6).

**FMEG / Others — fans, water heaters, switchgear, lighting, switches,
accessories (~4% of segment revenue, FY26)**
Bought-out/manufactured components → assembled/branded under the Finolex
name → sold via a parallel two-tier consumer distribution network and 8+
"Finolex House" exclusive stores plus e-commerce → paid retail cash terms
(AR p.10, p.12, p.39; Inv. Pres. slide 7, 13-14).

**EHV Cables (equity-accounted JV, not consolidated revenue)**
Finolex J-Power Systems Corp. (Sumitomo Group, Japan JV) manufactures
extra-high-voltage cables up to 500kV — India's first private-sector
capability at this voltage — but this sits below the revenue line as an
"Investment in Associate and Joint Venture," Rs 262.76 cr as at 31 Mar 2026
vs Rs 174.56 cr a year earlier (AR p.02, p.35; Inv. Pres. slide 9).

### 1E. The chai-stall-uncle version
Think of Finolex Cables as a very serious electrician's supply shop that,
unlike most shops, grows its own wheat and bakes its own bread — it buys raw
copper, melts and draws it into rods itself, wraps it in plastic insulation
it also makes, and only then sells the finished wire. Because copper is the
main ingredient and its price bounces around like vegetable prices in
monsoon, the shop's total bill (revenue) can jump or fall a lot even when it
is selling the same number of wires. It reaches customers through thousands
of small distributor and retailer shops across India, mostly on a
cash-and-carry basis, so it is rarely left waiting for payment. On the side,
it also makes and brands fans, water heaters, switches and lights, the way a
grocery store might add its own branded snacks next to the sacks of rice —
this is the newer, smaller bet on becoming a full electrical-goods brand. The
core wire-and-cable business is steady and needed wherever construction,
power, or telecom infrastructure happens; the FMEG side is still finding its
feet and its margins have been under some pressure this year from regulation
and price competition.

### 4B. Valuation method applicability — handoff to Stage 11

| Method | Applicability | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| EV/EBITDA | **PRIMARY** | Normalises for the (irrelevant, since debt-free) capital structure and for copper-pass-through noise in revenue/PAT; standard comparable basis for cable manufacturers |
| P/E (cash-adjusted) | **SECONDARY** cross-check | Must strip out the ~Rs 2,600+ cr non-operating treasury/investment book and the JV/associate stake (Inv. Pres. slide 9) to avoid distorted comparisons to leaner-balance-sheet peers |
| SOTP / asset-based (core operations + treasury investments + JV stakes at book/fair value) | **TERTIARY** | Relevant given the unusually large non-operating investment book and the equity-accounted EHV cables JV (Finolex J-Power Systems) sitting outside consolidated operating revenue |
| DCF | Not applicable | Copper-linked, cyclical cash flows make long-run projection unreliable without normalized margin assumptions; better used as a sensitivity check than a primary anchor |
| Dividend discount model | Not applicable | Not a yield-focused security; payout ratio ~20.9% (FY20, AR p.19), current FY26 payout NOT FOUND |
| Standalone P/B | Not applicable | Book value inflated by the large non-operating treasury cash/investments, distorting a book-based multiple unless adjusted |
| Replacement cost / asset value | Not applicable | Backward-integration assets have some replacement-cost relevance but this is not primarily an asset-arbitrage story |

**Cycle stage that matters for valuation:** the business is currently in a
volume-led upcycle (Electrical Cable revenue +22% YoY YTD FY26) but segment
margin sits well below the level the FY20 annual report describes for that
era (~10.3% now vs 15-16% then). Valuation should use a normalized/
through-cycle EBIT margin bracketing these two data points, not the peak
single-quarter or the FY20 pre-pandemic figure alone, and Stage 11 should
seek to understand and reconcile the margin gap before anchoring on either
extreme.

### 4D. Highest-value questions for management

1. Electrical Cable segment EBIT margin has moved from ~15-16% (FY20) to
   ~10.3% (FY26) — what is driving this, and is it structural (competitive
   intensity, BIS-driven cost) or cyclical (RM pass-through timing lag)?
   Reassuring: a quantified bridge showing the gap is temporary/RM-timing
   related. Worrying: vague attribution to "competitive market" with no
   stated path back toward historical margins.
2. Q4FY26 EBITDA grew only 7% YoY against 22% revenue growth — is this
   copper pass-through inflating revenue, or are fixed costs/opex growing
   faster than sales? Reassuring: clear volume growth plus a one-off
   RM-timing explanation. Worrying: admission that employee/other costs are
   structurally outpacing growth.
3. What is the current organised wires & cables market share (last
   disclosed was 22% in FY20) and how has competitive intensity evolved?
   Reassuring: share stable or gained. Worrying: management unable or
   unwilling to quantify.
4. The company carries roughly Rs 2,600+ crore of investments/cash against
   ~Rs 5,100 crore of equity — what is the capital allocation plan, and how
   is it governed given the FY20-era promoter-family board dispute?
   Reassuring: a clear, board-approved policy with independent oversight of
   related-party matters. Worrying: no clear plan or opacity around
   related-party use of funds.
5. Communication Cables segment revenue declined 1% YTD FY26 despite the
   OFC/5G/data-centre narrative being framed as a tailwind — what is
   holding this back, and what is the roadmap to capture the fibre
   transition? Reassuring: order-book visibility tied to specific
   contracts. Worrying: continued reliance on narrative without bookings
   evidence.
6. BIS norm revisions hurt fan sales this year — how is the FMEG portfolio
   being repositioned against established branded competitors, and what is
   the medium-term segment margin target? Reassuring: a specific product
   and margin roadmap. Worrying: repeated "channel expansion continues"
   language without margin progress (Others EBIT margin actually declined
   YoY).
7. What is the company's current credit rating, and has it changed since
   the FY20 CRISIL AA+/stable, A1+ rating? Reassuring: maintained or
   upgraded. Worrying: downgrade or rating withdrawal without explanation.
