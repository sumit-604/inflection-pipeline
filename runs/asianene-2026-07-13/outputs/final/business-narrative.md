Real oilfield turnaround, valuation still says avoid

Rs791 Cr consolidated sales in FY26, up 70% on the year, is what Asian Energy Services shows after buying Kuiper Group, a Middle East offshore services firm, in September 2025. The company runs Indian oilfield and upstream energy services: seismic, operations and maintenance, integrated field development, and crude production through the Indrora participatory interest, alongside a mineral and coal handling business. Standalone sales were Rs492 Cr, up only 6% organically once Kuiper is stripped out. FY26 EBITDA was Rs99 Cr at a 12.5% margin, down from 15.6% the year before, and net profit was Rs51 Cr. The promoter is Oilmax Energy, the managing director is Kapil Garg, and the shares trade at Rs340 for a market value of Rs1,654 Cr.

Rs305 Cr to Rs465 Cr to Rs791 Cr is the three year sales path from FY24 to FY26, a real recovery from a business that had collapsed and lost money in FY23. Two structural moves define the change. Kuiper is already consolidating and nearly doubled reported size. The promoter's private company Oilmax is being reverse merged into the listed entity, pending the NCLT and guided for September or October 2026, which would add roughly 70 million barrels of reserves. The forward moat read is strengthening and the combined verdict is turnaround.

37 out of 100 on the backward looking Gate 0 core score classifies the company AVOID, but that number looks backward and carries a data hole. It is driven by the FY23 revenue collapse to Rs110 Cr with a Rs44 Cr loss, an earlier FY17 loss year, and a shareholding block that scored zero only because promoter holding data was missing from the Gate 0 inputs. The FY24 to FY26 window is a fully anchored recovery. AVOID here does not cap the decision.

Negative operating cash flow in both FY24 at minus Rs44 Cr and FY25 at minus Rs33 Cr sat against rising reported profit, all four working capital ratios worsened at once, unbilled contract assets grew 234% against 52% revenue growth, and borrowings rose roughly 6.7 times into FY26. The FY25 cash build was funded by a Rs157 Cr equity raise, not by operations. FY26 is the counterweight: operating cash flow turned positive at plus Rs53 Cr and CFO to PAT printed 1.02 times during the growth ramp. That turn reads as a growth effect in a percentage of completion business rather than a structural drain, but half the standalone revenue and more than half the receivables sit inside related party JVs, so the read is not yet settled. The two year cumulative CFO to PAT is only 0.21 times on that single positive print.

Grade C credibility comes from a delivery record of 5 promises kept, 2 partial, and 4 missed, with the misses blamed on outside events. FY26 standalone guidance of Rs650 to Rs700 Cr was reaffirmed and then missed, blamed first on monsoon and then on the West Asia conflict. Field production dates at Duarmara, Tiphuk and Amguri slipped in every one of the three calls, with Duarmara ending with no committable date. The order book fell from over Rs2,000 Cr to Rs1,750 Cr even as FY27 growth guidance was raised to 30 to 40%. An analyst challenge on the roughly 50 times valuation of the Oilmax merger went unanswered across all three calls.

Both peers contradict the monsoon excuse. Deep Industries grew revenue 69% in the same quarter with no weather mention anywhere, at a 46.6% EBITDA margin. The West Asia disruption claim is partly corroborated by one peer only. The net effect of the peer read is that it complicates the company's account rather than clearing it. Good runway sits under the story with 14.8 times revenue headroom, but management's $100 billion by 2030 framing reads inflated at 41.8 times the independent market estimate because that figure is sector capex commitment, not services demand the company can capture.

Half of standalone revenue, about 50.8%, is related party, concentrated in newly formed majority owned JVs, and the Oilmax reverse merger is flagged rich by outside commentary at roughly 53 times FY25 earnings. The professional CEO was replaced by the promoter as managing director in 2023, MD remuneration is disclosed at both Rs277 Lakh and Rs157 Lakh in the same annual report, and the auditor notes recovery steps were not taken on a fully impaired related party loan. The promoter also cut his own stake from 60.75% in March 2026 to 56.16% in May 2026, with public holding up to 41.87%, ahead of the related party merger listing. On the other side, the pledge is zero, the board carries credentialed former PSU heads, and the public shareholders passed the merger vote 99.99% in favour. The promoter verdict is CONCERN, not a deal breaker.

Rs356 base fair value on the additive track and Rs210 on the more conservative reverse rate track sit against Rs340 today, so the valuation decision is AVOID. This re-run gave the price every legitimate benefit. The undiscovered alpha uplift now applies, because the full shareholding pattern confirms FII 1.18% plus DII 0.75% is 1.93%, under the 3% institutional test, with all three qualifiers met. A full duration premium now applies too, because the Vedanta contract runs 4.75 years and the Mahanadi contract 7 years, together about 76% of the standalone book at 3.56 times order cover. Those two corrections roughly doubled the destination multiple to 19.1 times on the additive four pillar build (range 17.5 to 20.0) and lifted the reverse rate method to 10.7 times (range 10.0 to 11.5); on a 44% divergence the more conservative reverse rate method governs the entry. ROCE enters STAGNANT at 13.71%, the cash multiplier is 0.85 times for growth induced conversion, the Emerging Moat scores 28, and the sector cap is 20 times. The Hurdle check still returns STOP at a base ratio of 1.10 and a bull ratio of 1.24, and the probability weighted three year return is minus 1.9%. The entry range is Rs86 to Rs108 with a margin of safety price of Rs86.

The reason the higher multiple does not rescue the price is arithmetic. At Rs340 the stock trades near 30 times earnings against a disciplined destination of about 19 times, and even the absolute sector ceiling is 20 times, so the multiple sits above every price the framework can justify. Even if earnings grow 20 to 25% a year for three years the stock delivers roughly flat to negative returns, because the multiple has to fall faster than earnings rise. The corrections lifted expected return from minus 12.1% to minus 1.9%, but none of that closes the 30 versus 20 gap.

The devil's advocate review keeps the overall thesis alive: growth, moat and management trust all survive, and only valuation safety weakened. That objection is now largely resolved on magnitude. The entry zone rose from Rs75 to Rs93 up to Rs86 to Rs108, and the base additive fair value of about Rs356 sits right beside the devil's own sum of the parts of about Rs290 to Rs320. The AVOID is now confirmed robust rather than punitive, because it holds on a fully un-stacked valuation. The AVOID direction stays triple bound by the Gate 0 AVOID, the promoter CONCERN, and the Hurdle STOP. The pivotal unverified fact is the Oilmax merger accounting basis: if it pools at book with audited accretion, ROCE flips to recovering and the dilution ladder flips to accretion in one event.

The operator reads the same facts as a bull case, seeing roughly 50% upside on valuation alone and not overpaying at Rs340 because the standalone business plus Kuiper roughly cover today's price, with the Oilmax merger and the exploration book as the largely unpriced upside and Duarmara production as the medium term high gear. That upside rests on forward EBITDA and sum of the parts plus merger accretion, not on a low current multiple, since the shares already trade near 30 times earnings against a 20 times sector cap. The framework AVOID and the operator bull case diverge on the entry price, and both are on the record here.
