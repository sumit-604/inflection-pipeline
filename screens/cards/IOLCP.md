# IOLCP — IOL Chemicals and Pharmaceuticals Ltd

Shallow read, 2026-09-16. Framework: screens/SHALLOW_ANALYSIS_FRAMEWORK.md v1.0.
Corpus: screens/corpus/IOLCP/. BSE 524164, NSE IOLCP, ISIN INE485C01029.

## Business Understanding Narrative

**What it does, and how it makes money.** IOL makes bulk drug ingredients and industrial
solvents at one integrated site in Barnala, Punjab. Two segments. Pharmaceuticals is 60
percent of segment revenue and chemicals is 40 percent (Q1FY27 deck, p.12). The pharma
business was built on one molecule. IOL is the world's largest ibuprofen producer with
about 35 percent global share and 12,000-plus MT of capacity, fully backward integrated
(Q2FY23 deck, p.4). Backward integration is where the margin comes from. IOL makes its own
intermediates, so it sits low on the cost curve and earns a spread over input cost rather
than a price premium. The chemicals segment makes ethyl acetate at 1,20,000 MTPA, India's
largest single-location plant, plus acetic anhydride and Triacetin.

**What is changing.** IOL is moving revenue from ibuprofen into other APIs that carry
regulatory approvals for rich markets. Non-ibuprofen has gone from 18 percent to 37 percent
of the pharmaceuticals segment over six years (Q1FY27 deck, p.31). In rupees, non-ibuprofen
drugs went from Rs 35 cr in FY18 to Rs 431 cr in FY24 (FY24 deck, p.29). The portfolio now
carries 16 USFDA DMFs and 19 EDQM CEPs (AR FY2025, p.53). Paracetamol at 10,800 MTPA is the
current driver, and management names it as the main source of the Q1 FY27 jump.

**Why now.** Five years of flat revenue just ended. Revenue from operations ran Rs 1,967 cr,
2,184, 2,217, 2,133, 2,079 across FY21 to FY25, then Rs 2,319.1 cr in FY26. Margin turned
with it. EBITDA margin went 12.4 percent in FY26 and 14.6 percent in Q1 FY27, against 10.8
percent in FY25. The new capacity is filling and the mix is shifting at the same time.

**What must be true.** Paracetamol utilisation must go from 55 percent to the guided 70 to
75 percent. Non-ibuprofen must reach roughly half of the API segment, which management
places in FY29. The chemicals leg must keep the margin it found in Q1 FY27. And ibuprofen
prices must not fall further.

**What breaks it.** A fresh ibuprofen price war breaks the base. The chemicals segment
sliding back to a 3 percent margin breaks the blended number. And the Rs 1,200 to 1,400 cr
greenfield, if it lands ahead of demand, turns a cash-generating business into a
capex-absorbing one.

## 2. Business model and archetype

Margin sits in the pharma segment and the chemicals segment drags it. FY26 segment EBIT:
pharmaceuticals Rs 172.3 cr on Rs 1,396.3 cr revenue, which is 12.3 percent; chemicals
Rs 30.2 cr on Rs 922.8 cr, which is 3.3 percent. Q1 FY27 improved both: pharma 14.7 percent
and chemicals 6.0 percent. The economic engine is capacity utilisation on a backward
integrated site, sold into a mix that is gradually getting better.

**Archetype: commodity converter.** This is the clean fit. The drivers are the spread of
output price over input price, utilisation, cycle position and cost-curve rank. All four
appear in the documents. Ibuprofen export prices run above USD 10 per kg and domestic
around USD 9 to 9.25 (Q3FY25 call, p.10). Utilisation is quoted constantly. Cost-curve rank
is the stated strength. **Section 1B v3.7 Amendment 17 binds this name.** Spot-year ROCE and
rupee-denominated working-capital trends must not feed Section 1B or FTTCP. Any later
valuation must denominate on a through-cycle basis.

Sector cap row: contested. Pharma and CDMO at 38x flatters a bulk API converter. Specialty
chemicals at 35x flatters the solvents leg. Neither row describes this business. **Operator
ruling needed before Stage 11.**

## 3. Competitive advantages

Cost, not price. IOL has no pricing power in ibuprofen and says so in effect: it holds 35
percent of a roughly 35,000 tonne global market and runs at 75 to 80 percent utilisation
while prices correct. What it does have is real. Backward integration it names as a core
strength across every annual report. Regulatory filings that take years to obtain: 16 USFDA
DMFs, 19 CEPs, ANVISA approvals, China DMFs active on Metformin and Fenofibrate. Those
filings are switching costs, because a formulator who qualifies a supplier does not requalify
cheaply. Customer diversification is genuine: the top customer was 3.72 percent of sales in
FY24 (AR FY2024, p.160).

**Moat: forming, in one segment only.** The regulatory filings are building spec-in
economics in non-ibuprofen. Ibuprofen and solvents remain price-takers. That is precisely
the transition being claimed.

## 6. The transition and the quality ladder

Management's claim, quoted once: "The non-Ibuprofen share in the pharmaceuticals segment has
increased from 18% to 37% over the past six years, with a strong trajectory to further drive
revenue and margin expansion over the next 2-3 years" (Q1FY27 deck, p.31).

- **FROM: R2 cost-advantaged converter.** Margin from cost position, not price. Mid-teens
  returns. That is IOL today, and Amendment 17 caps converter slices.
- **TO: R3 value-added and spec'd supplier.** Regulatory filings give partial pricing power
  through qualification and switching costs.

The mechanism is filings plus capacity. A CEP or a USFDA DMF converts a commodity molecule
into a qualified one, and a qualified supplier is harder to swap. The rung-jump base rate is
one rung per two to three years. **This is a single-rung claim on a documented six-year
trend, which is the shape the framework expects.** It is not a multi-rung leap and it does
not raise a red flag on that count.

The claim is partial. Management's own FY29 target is non-ibuprofen at 50 to 55 percent of
the API segment. Even delivered, ibuprofen and the 40 percent chemicals leg stay at R2. So
the honest ladder read is a blended migration, not a clean R2 to R3 step.

## 8. Proof check

The last two quarters move the way the triggers claim, and they move harder than guided.

Q4 FY26: revenue Rs 619.5 cr, up 17.4 percent, EBITDA margin up 251 basis points to 15.2
percent. Q1 FY27: revenue Rs 756.3 cr, up 37.1 percent year on year and 22.1 percent
sequentially; EBITDA margin 14.6 percent; PAT up 89.9 percent to Rs 64.5 cr. The pharma
segment grew about 42 percent year on year. Segment EBIT margins rose in both segments at
once: pharma to 14.7 percent, chemicals to 6.0 percent from 3.3 percent for FY26.

An analyst put the right question on the Q1 FY27 call: the margin is already ahead of the
full-year guidance range. The CFO named "higher capacity utilization, better product mix,
operational efficiencies, and also stronger non-ibu segment API demand" plus "some
improvements in the finished product prices" (p.6).

**Named proof point: chemicals segment EBIT margin at 6.0 percent in Q1 FY27 against 3.3
percent for FY26, alongside pharma EBIT at 14.7 percent.** Both legs improved together, so
the gain is not one segment borrowing from the other.

**Proof gate: FIRED, partially.** One qualification, and it is the weak spot. Management
declined to split the pharma growth between volume and realisation (p.6). Until that split
is known, part of the Q1 FY27 jump may be price, and price in a converter mean-reverts.

## 9. Flags from the documents

- **Product concentration is the company's own named risk.** "Dependence on key products
  like Ibuprofen makes it vulnerable to market fluctuations and regulatory changes"
  (AR FY2025, p.54). Repeated in the FY24 SCOT as dependence on bulk drugs.
- **A competitor entered the core.** SMS Pharma launched ibuprofen about eighteen months
  before the Q3 FY25 call, and management links domestic price correction to it.
- **Global demand grows 3 to 4 percent a year** for ibuprofen. The core does not grow.
- **The chemicals leg is structurally thin.** 3.3 percent segment EBIT in FY26 on 40
  percent of revenue, and 1.8 to 2.2 percent in individual FY26 quarters.
- **FY22 earnings have not been regained.** PAT was Rs 166 cr in FY22 and Rs 137.7 cr in

- **A large greenfield is coming.** Rs 1,200 to 1,400 cr over four to five years, against
  FY26 operating cash of Rs 214.4 cr.
- Capex for FY26 is stated as Rs 164 cr in the deck and "around INR160 crores" on the call.

- No related-party or auditor-emphasis item surfaced. No litigation surfaced. No dilution.

## 11. Posture: the Transition Decision Matrix

- **Proof gate: FIRED, partially.** Margin and mix moved together for two quarters, ahead
  of guidance. The volume-versus-price split of the pharma jump is not disclosed.
- **Ugliness: MIXED, and the two readings must both be stated.** Reading one, ARTIFACT OF
  CLIMB: five flat revenue years were capacity built ahead of fill, with paracetamol at 55
  percent utilisation and ibuprofen prices at a cyclical trough. Reading two, STRUCTURAL
  FEATURE: the chemicals segment is 40 percent of revenue at a 3.3 percent margin and will
  dilute the blend permanently. **The one observation that separates them:** whether the
  chemicals segment holds EBIT above 6 percent for two more quarters while paracetamol
  utilisation reaches 70 to 75 percent. If both hold, the optic was an artifact. If
  chemicals falls back toward 3 percent, it is structural.
- **Recognition gap: INDETERMINATE at shallow depth.** Against the Bull AI market cap of
  Rs 5,267 cr, undated, FY26 PAT of Rs 137.7 cr is roughly 38 times, while annualised Q1
  FY27 PAT is roughly 20 times. That spread is the whole question and shallow cannot close
  it. Stage 11 resolves it. No valuation is performed here and no fair value is stated.

**Posture: provisionally RE-RATING LIVE, falling to EARNINGS-ONLY if the gap reads closed.**
Proof fired and the ugliness reads artifact on the current evidence, which puts this in the
constructive half of the matrix. Which cell it lands in depends entirely on the gap, and the
gap is not resolvable without Stage 11. Recorded as indeterminate rather than guessed.

## 12. Verdict card

**PROCEED to /step1.** This is the one name of the three read today that earns a full run.

The case in four lines. The transition is documented over six years, not narrated. It is a
single-rung claim, which matches the base rate. The balance sheet funds it without dilution
or debt. And the last two quarters delivered margin ahead of guidance in both segments at
once.

**Load-bearing facts to verify first in the deep run.**

1. **The volume-versus-realisation split of the Q1 FY27 pharma growth.** Management declined
   to give it. Pharma grew about 42 percent year on year. If most of that is realisation, a
   converter's price gain will mean-revert and the proof gate weakens. Derive it from
   volumes in the FY26 annual report and the quarterly filings.
2. **Chemicals segment EBIT durability.** 6.0 percent in Q1 FY27 against 3.3 percent for
   FY26 and 1.8 percent in one FY26 quarter. This is the observation that decides whether
   the margin optic is an artifact of the climb or a structural feature.
3. **Paracetamol utilisation against the 70 to 75 percent guide.** 55 percent at FY26 on
   10,800 MTPA. This is the trigger carrying the near-term mix shift.
4. **ROCE on a through-cycle basis, and the sector cap row.** ROCE is NOT FOUND for FY25 and
   FY26. Amendment 17 binds, so the deep run must denominate through-cycle. The cap row
   needs an operator ruling: neither Pharma and CDMO at 38x nor Specialty chemicals at 35x
   describes a bulk-API converter with a 40 percent solvents leg.

**What would change the view.** Two quarters of chemicals EBIT back near 3 percent would
move this to WATCH, because the blended margin story would then be structural. An ibuprofen
price break below the domestic USD 9 floor would do the same to the base. On the other side,
the volume split coming back volume-heavy would strengthen the proof gate materially.

**Nothing here is a price, a target or a position.** The shallow verdict decides only
whether the name earns a full run.
