# Stage 6 — Peer Concall Verification: United Foodbrands Ltd (UFBL)

Run date: 2026-08-05
Peers triangulated: Restaurant Brands Asia (RBA — Burger King India/Popeyes), Sapphire Foods
(KFC/Pizza Hut India, Sri Lanka), Speciality Restaurants (Mainland China, Asia Kitchen, Oh! Calcutta
and adjacent brands), Westlife Foodworld (McDonald's West & South India). 15 transcripts read in full
(14 earnings calls + 1 Sapphire/Devyani merger-announcement call).

Note on comparability: none of the four peers operate a buffet-format casual dining chain. RBA and
Westlife are QSR (burger); Sapphire is QSR (chicken/pizza); Speciality is fine/casual Asian dining.
Evidence is treated as adjacent-segment corroboration, not a like-for-like match, per protocol.

## PART 1: CLAIM-BY-CLAIM VERIFICATION

### Claim 1 — SSSG recovery timing: did peers turn positive ~2 quarters before UFBL?

**Claim (verbatim from B05 handoff):** "Did listed peers turn SSSG positive roughly two quarters
before UFBL (around Q4 FY25/Q1 FY26), as an analyst asserted on the Q2 FY26 call?"

**Verdict: CONTRADICTED**

**Peer evidence:**
- Sapphire (KFC): SSSG was still only +1% for the Oct-Dec 2025 quarter (Q3 FY26), turning
  meaningfully positive only in Jan 2026 and reaching +5% only in Apr-Jun 2026 (Q1 FY27).
  Pizza Hut SSSG was -12% in Oct-Dec 2025, not turning positive (+1%) until Apr-Jun 2026, "after
  five quarters" of decline. (SAPPHIRE, Q3 FY26 call, Feb-2026, p.3; SAPPHIRE, Q1 FY27 call,
  Jul-2026, p.2-4)
- Westlife (McDonald's): SSSG was -3% for Oct-Dec 2025 (Q3 FY26); guest counts only turned
  "flat to positive" in Nov-Dec 2025 and SSSG itself turned positive only in Jan 2026. The first
  full positive-SSSG quarter was Jan-Mar 2026 (Q4 FY26, +1.5%). (WESTLIFE, Q3 FY26 call,
  Feb-2026, p.2-3; WESTLIFE, Q4 FY26 call, May-2026, p.3-4; WESTLIFE, Q1 FY27 call, Jul-2026, p.7:
  "October, November, December was the first quarter where we got positive same-store sales
  growth on the guest count front")
- RBA (Burger King): not a comparable case for this claim — RBA's dine-in SSSG had already been
  positive for "10 consecutive weak quarters" as of Oct-2025 and "11th consecutive quarter" as of
  Feb-2026, i.e. it never had a negative-to-positive turn in this window. (RBA, Q2 FY26 call,
  Oct-2025, p.2; RBA, Q3 FY26 call, Feb-2026, p.2)

**Peers silent:** None literally silent, but RBA's case is structurally inapplicable (continuously
positive, no turn event to date).

**Net read:** Per B05, UFBL's own SSSG went 8.2% (Q3 FY26, i.e. Oct-Dec 2025) to 14.4% (Q4 FY26,
Jan-Mar 2026). On the peer evidence gathered, Sapphire's KFC and Westlife's McDonald's were both
still negative or barely positive in the same Oct-Dec 2025 quarter, turning positive only in
Jan-2026 — concurrent with, or after, UFBL's already-strong recovery, not two quarters before it.
The specific analyst assertion made on UFBL's own Q2 FY26 call is not supported by the peer record.
This complicates the "catching up to an already-recovering sector" framing; the peer data instead
suggests UFBL's recovery timing was, if anything, ahead of two of the three comparable peers
checked.

### Claim 2 — GST rate cut cited as demand tailwind in H2 CY2025

**Claim (verbatim):** "Did peers cite the GST rate reduction as a demand tailwind in H2 CY2025?"

**Verdict: VERIFIED**

**Peer evidence:**
- RBA: "these [GST benefits] came in at the tail end of the quarter... we passed on that GST
  benefit... directly to the consumer" and "we have seen a very substantial benefit in October."
  (RBA, Q2 FY26 call, Oct-2025, p.11)
- Westlife: "we have also passed on the benefit of the GST rate cut to our customers through a
  price reduction of around 80 to 100 basis points... we are optimistic that this historic
  government initiative will augur well for consumer sentiment and spending in the mid to long
  term." (WESTLIFE, Q2 FY26 call, Nov-2025, p.3)
- Speciality: "the kind of euphoria which has been built up because of the GST rationalization...
  we are working hard towards it so that at least this growth [10-15% guidance]... we would be
  lower to that number [without it]." (SPECIALITY, Q2 FY26 call, Nov-2025, p.5)

**Peers silent:** Sapphire does not explicitly cite the GST cut as a demand driver in the
transcripts reviewed (its SSSG narrative centres on its own INR99 Krisper Meal launch in Dec-2025).

**Net read:** Three of four checked peers (RBA, Westlife, Speciality) explicitly and independently
credit the GST rationalisation as a real, quantified tailwind (RBA: pass-through evident in
October; Westlife: 80-100bps pass-through; Speciality: explicit "euphoria" driving H2 guidance).
This corroborates that UFBL's H2 FY26 demand environment benefited from a genuine sector-wide
regulatory tailwind, not solely company execution — though it does not by itself explain the
much larger magnitude of UFBL's own SSSG move (8.2% → 14.4%) relative to peers.

### Claim 3 — Middle East/GCC input-cost inflation comparability

**Claim (verbatim):** "Have peers with Middle East/GCC exposure reported comparable input-cost
inflation or currency pressure in FY26?"

**Verdict: UNVERIFIABLE (direct comparison); adjacent macro corroboration found**

**Peer evidence:** Neither RBA (India/Indonesia) nor Sapphire (India/Sri Lanka) operates
company-owned restaurants in the Gulf. Speciality's Dubai/UAE presence is an asset-light master
franchise ("there's no capex which is required... it's just that our management, our menu
reengineering... obviously at their cost"), so it carries no comparable margin-drag exposure.
(SPECIALITY, Q3 FY26 call, Feb-2026, p.5)

Adjacent, indirect evidence is nonetheless informative: Sapphire's Sri Lanka business specifically
cited "cost of utilities and fuel increasing because of the geopolitical crisis in the Middle
East" (SAPPHIRE, Q1 FY27 call, Jul-2026, p.4). Separately, both RBA and Speciality (May-2026 calls)
and Westlife (May-2026 and Jul-2026 calls) report a domestic Indian LPG/PNG/gas-cost and supply
crisis linked to global/"geopolitical" tension, causing store-level operational disruption and
material cost inflation across food, packaging and utility lines in Apr-Jun 2026. (RBA, Q4 FY26
call, May-2026, p.15; SPECIALITY, Q4 FY26 call, May-2026, p.7-8; WESTLIFE, Q4 FY26 call, May-2026,
p.14; WESTLIFE, Q1 FY27 call, Jul-2026, p.9, 13-14: "there is of course lot of pressure which is
there on the suppliers too with the geopolitical situation" / "April, May were probably some of
the worst months in terms of geopolitical impact on inflation")

**Peers silent:** RBA and Sapphire are silent on any direct GCC-based restaurant cost exposure
(neither has any).

**Net read:** No peer offers a like-for-like comparison to UFBL's Bahrain/Dubai company-operated
restaurant cost base, so the claim itself cannot be verified directly — UNVERIFIABLE by protocol.
However, the broader pattern across three peers (RBA, Speciality, Westlife) confirms that a
genuine, sector-wide, Middle East-linked energy/input cost shock hit Indian F&B operators broadly
in Mar-Jun 2026 (a different mechanism — domestic LPG/fuel/packaging pass-through rather than GCC
local costs — but the same root cause). This lends indirect credibility to UFBL's framing of
Middle East disruption as a real macro phenomenon rather than a pure execution failure, even though
the specific claim (comparable GCC restaurant cost inflation) is not directly testable from this
peer set.

### Claim 4 — Competitive intensity in casual dining/buffet-adjacent formats: flat or rising?

**Claim (verbatim):** "Do peer concalls describe competitive intensity in casual dining/buffet-
adjacent formats as flat, or rising?"

**Verdict: CONTRADICTED (peers describe it as rising, not flat)**

**Peer evidence:**
- RBA: an analyst directly asked about "increase in aggression by the competition... from value
  launches perspective, from faster deliveries perspective" in Feb-2026; management did not deny
  it, only reiterated its own strategy. In the same call, another analyst noted "McDonald's has
  become a bit aggressive on their value combo. They've slashed their prices down from INR119 to
  INR99." (RBA, Q3 FY26 call, Feb-2026, p.12, 14)
- Westlife: an analyst noted "other cuisine QSRs, like, say, Domino's or even Chinese QSR players
  have also increased their value proposition. They are also coming with INR99 lunch menu, INR99
  combos" in Feb-2026; separately, an analyst asked whether "competition from some of the newer
  players, specifically Third Wave, Blue Tokai" has eroded McCafé's dayparts advantage — management
  agreed competition is intensifying industry-wide even while framing it positively.
  (WESTLIFE, Q3 FY26 call, Feb-2026, p.18-19)
- Speciality: an analyst asked how the company is "responding to increasing competition from new
  age F&B brand and cloud kitchen players" (Nov-2025); by May-2026, a different analyst flagged that
  "one of our competitors is getting Panda Express into India" and that "Chinese Wok has raised a
  decent amount of funding" — both new-entrant signals in the closest comparable segment (Asian
  casual dining) to UFBL's own space. Management downplayed direct threat but did not dispute rising
  entrant activity. (SPECIALITY, Q2 FY26 call, Nov-2025, p.5; SPECIALITY, Q4 FY26 call, May-2026,
  p.3-4)

**Peers silent:** Sapphire's only direct comment narrows the picture rather than broadening it —
on aggregator-platform discounting specifically, management said "we haven't heard about any
heightened competition on these two aggregators, at least for the last few quarters." (SAPPHIRE,
Q1 FY27 call, Jul-2026, p.7) This is a narrower, delivery-channel-specific observation and does not
speak to dine-in/store-level competitive intensity.

**Net read:** Across three of the four peers, competitive intensity is explicitly described as
rising — new entrants, aggressive value-price launches, and faster-delivery arms races — during
the same period UFBL's management asserted unchanged competitive intensity across three
consecutive calls (per B05). This is a direct, well-evidenced contradiction of that framing, and
should be treated as a priority item for synthesis: UFBL's margin-recovery narrative rests partly
on an "unchanged competitive intensity" premise that the peer set does not support.

### Claim 5 — Volume-led, no-price-hike growth vs price increases across peers

**Claim (verbatim):** "Are peers pursuing volume-led, no-price-hike growth in FY26, or taking
price increases?"

**Verdict: PARTIALLY VERIFIED (sector is split, not uniform)**

**Peer evidence — volume-led, no price hike:**
- RBA: repeated across every call — "it's not on the top of APC or increase in average check or
  increase in pricing. It is basically traffic coming in." (RBA, Q2 FY26 call, Oct-2025, p.12)
- Westlife: "growth must be volume-led while remaining margin protected" (five-pillar framework,
  Q3 FY26 call, Feb-2026, p.3); confirmed again in Jul-2026: "we haven't done any price increase
  yet... we typically do 50% of the inflation to 3% broadly... but we haven't taken anything yet."
  (WESTLIFE, Q1 FY27 call, Jul-2026, p.20-21)

**Peer evidence — price increases taken:**
- Sapphire: explicit 2-3% price hikes taken in two instalments (April and June 2026) for both KFC
  and Pizza Hut, alongside discount reduction. (SAPPHIRE, Q1 FY27 call, Jul-2026, p.3-4, 10-11)
- Speciality: "we've just taken a 4% price rise" during FY26 in response to input-cost headwinds.
  (SPECIALITY, Q4 FY26 call, May-2026, p.6)

**Peers silent:** None.

**Net read:** The peer set splits 2-2. RBA and Westlife (the two burger-QSR peers) mirror UFBL's
volume-led, no-price-hike framing almost verbatim. Sapphire and Speciality took explicit price
increases in the same window. UFBL's framing is a genuine, defensible strategic choice shared by
half the peer set, not a sector-wide norm — the claim should be read as "one recognisable school of
thought in the sector," not as evidence the whole industry avoided pricing.

### Claim 6 — Store-level capex and payback assumptions

**Claim (verbatim):** "What store-level capex and payback assumptions are peers disclosing for
their own expansion?"

**Verdict: PARTIALLY VERIFIED (one peer discloses in detail; the QSR peers do not)**

**Peer evidence:**
- Speciality discloses granular unit economics: capex of roughly INR12,000-13,000/sq ft on
  2,500-3,000 sq ft formats (~INR3-4 crore per restaurant), annual revenue of INR8-9 crore per
  restaurant, restaurant-level EBITDA of ~22% pre-rent / ~15% post-rent, and stated breakeven
  windows that varied across calls (3-6 months in Aug-2025 and Nov-2025 calls; "we look at
  breakeven between 4 to 6 months" restated in May-2026). New format (Siciliana Bangalore): ~INR4
  crore capex against ~INR6-7 crore turnover. (SPECIALITY, Q1 FY26 call, Aug-2025, p.3-4;
  SPECIALITY, Q2 FY26 call, Nov-2025, p.2, 11-12; SPECIALITY, Q4 FY26 call, May-2026, p.9-10)
- Sapphire discloses profitability/payback logic without rupee figures: smaller-city KFC stores
  "work similarly" to Tier-1 stores on payback despite ~20% lower ADS, because operating costs are
  proportionately lower; new-store openings continue to be governed by internal "strike rate"
  metrics rather than a fixed rupee capex disclosure. (SAPPHIRE, Q1 FY27 call, Jul-2026, p.7-8)
- RBA and Westlife do not disclose store-level rupee capex or payback periods in the transcripts
  reviewed; both instead disclose blended targets (RBA: FY29 gross-margin target; Westlife: Vision
  2027 margin band and INR200-250 crore annual capex budget referenced only in the Nov-2025 call).

**Peers silent:** RBA and Westlife on specific rupee capex-per-store and payback-period figures.

**Net read:** Only Speciality offers a benchmarkable capex/payback figure set, and it is drawn from
a structurally different, smaller-format, non-buffet business, limiting its use as a direct
comparator for UFBL's larger-format buffet economics. The claim is therefore only partially
answerable from this peer set; a genuine like-for-like capex benchmark for UFBL's expansion pace
is not available from RBA, Sapphire, or Westlife disclosures.

### Claim 7 — Rising back-end/corporate overhead costs across peers

**Claim (verbatim):** "Are peers also reporting rising back-end/corporate overhead costs as a
percentage of revenue?"

**Verdict: CONTRADICTED (peer evidence shows declining or stable overhead ratios, not rising)**

**Peer evidence:**
- RBA: actively cutting Indonesia corporate overhead — "we have reduced already 25% of the
  corporate overheads. That's about 15 crores... [targeting] a further 10%... That will have
  reduced the total corporate head by 35%." (RBA, Q1 FY26 call, Aug-2025, p.4-5); confirmed
  continuing in Q2 FY26 call (Oct-2025, p.4).
- Speciality: "corporate costs, etcetera... used to be 6%, 7% is now at 4%," explicitly attributed
  to amortising fixed corporate cost over a growing restaurant base. (SPECIALITY, Q4 FY26 call,
  May-2026, p.14-15)
- Westlife: G&A "sharp decline" quarter-on-quarter in Q1 FY27, guided to "range of 6% to 6.5%"
  going forward — stable-to-falling, not rising. (WESTLIFE, Q1 FY27 call, Jul-2026, p.17)

**Peers silent:** Sapphire does not explicitly discuss corporate/back-end overhead trend as a
percentage of revenue in the transcripts reviewed.

**Net read:** All three peers with explicit disclosure show falling or stable overhead ratios —
the opposite direction of UFBL's own reported step-up (6.1% to 7.1% of revenue, per B05). This
undercuts a "sector-wide SG&A inflation" explanation for UFBL's back-end cost increase and points
toward a company-specific driver (most plausibly the deliberate investment/catch-up spend UFBL's
own management has cited, per B05's promise-delivery notes) rather than an industry-wide cost
pressure.

## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

**2A Demand environment.** The peer set collectively paints a materially weaker H2 CY2025 demand
picture than a simple "category-wide recovery" narrative would suggest. RBA's own dine-in traffic
stayed positive throughout, but management explicitly stated in Feb-2026 that "at [the] industry
level, there has been a negative sales reporting" for the prior 3-4 quarters. Sapphire's KFC and
Pizza Hut SSSG were negative through most of 2025. Westlife's SSSG was negative through Dec-2025
and its own brand-track data showed the broader informal-eating-out ("IEO") market "remained pretty
flattish" through Oct-Dec-2025 — management explicitly attributed its own eventual recovery to
company-specific value-platform execution, not a market uplift. Speciality's SSG hovered near flat
for most of FY26, jumping only in April-2026. Taken together, this complicates rather than confirms
a "category-wide recovery" framing for UFBL's own sharp Q3-Q4 FY26 SSSG acceleration: the peer
record instead shows a staggered, execution-driven recovery pattern across brands, with UFBL's
magnitude (8.2% → 14.4%) well ahead of any peer's comparable move in the same window.

**2B Pricing and input costs.** Gross margins expanded across the peer set for most of FY26 (RBA
to ~70%, Westlife to near-record highs, Speciality steadily up on "favourable inflation" and
efficiency, Sapphire's KFC gross margin recovering via discount discipline) — a broadly favourable
margin backdrop that UFBL's own margin trajectory (65.5% actual vs 68.5% a year earlier, per B05)
diverges from rather than tracks. This divergence is notable: while peers were expanding gross
margin through most of FY26, UFBL's margin compressed over the same period, suggesting UFBL's own
mix-shift and Middle East cost pressures were more severe or more idiosyncratic than what the
domestic-only peers experienced — at least until the Apr-Jun 2026 LPG/energy shock hit everyone
simultaneously (see 2C/risks below).

**2C Capex cycle.** This is an industry-wide capacity build-out, not a lone UFBL expansion. RBA
(60-80 stores/year toward ~800 by FY29), Sapphire's KFC (60-80/year, reaffirmed even after a soft
patch), Westlife (60-plus/year toward 580-630 by Dec-2027, accelerated from a prior 45-50/year
guide), and Speciality (accelerating from 8-10/year to a planned 32 new outlets in FY27 across
formats) are all expanding simultaneously. UFBL's guided 40 new restaurants/year toward 400-425 by
FY30 (per B05) sits inside this broader industry-wide capacity race rather than standing apart from
it — the relevant read-through for stage 11/13 is "industry-wide capacity race," not "lone
expander."

**2D Competitive mentions.** One direct, named mention of UFBL was found: on Speciality's Q4 FY26
call (May-2026), analyst Madhur Rathi benchmarked Speciality's own weak SSG against UFBL directly
— "if we see our other peers, they might not be like-to-like now, for example, this Barbeque
Nation, they did 14% SSG in fourth quarter. So any reason why we are lagging on this parameter?"
(SPECIALITY, Q4 FY26 call, May-2026, p.4) This independently corroborates the magnitude of UFBL's
own reported Q4 FY26 SSSG figure (~14.4% per B05) from an external, non-UFBL source, and confirms
UFBL's recovery is visible and used as a benchmark within the peer investor community. No peer
commented on UFBL's competitive strategy or market share.

**2E Risks peers discuss that UFBL's own concalls (per B05) do not appear to raise.**
- **Domestic LPG/PNG/gas supply crisis (Mar-Jun 2026):** Three peers (RBA, Speciality, Westlife)
  describe a material operational and cost disruption from a domestic Indian cooking-fuel supply
  crisis tied to global/geopolitical tension, forcing capex on electric broilers/induction cooking
  and, for Westlife, leaving "less than 10% of restaurants operating on limited menu" as of
  Q4 FY26. B05's flags do not mention this risk for UFBL's India operations (only Middle East
  input-cost inflation for its GCC restaurants is flagged) — worth checking whether UFBL's India
  kitchens carry comparable gas-dependency exposure.
- **India wage code implementation:** RBA took a one-time INR2.3 crore hit from wage code
  implementation in Q3 FY26 (RBA, Q3 FY26 call, Feb-2026, p.6); Westlife's labour line was also
  affected by minimum-wage changes. This is a sector-wide, near-term cost item not flagged in
  B05's UFBL risk list.
- **Aggregator-platform relationship volatility:** Both RBA and Westlife flag unpredictable,
  partner-specific swings in third-party aggregator performance as a distinct, actively-managed
  risk. UFBL's ~90% captive-channel dine-in mix (per B05) likely reduces this exposure, but it is
  worth noting as a sector risk UFBL's own concalls do not surface.

## PART 4: TRIANGULATION SUMMARY

- **Claims verified:** 1 of 7 (GST tailwind).
- **Claims partially verified:** 2 of 7 (volume-led/no-price-hike framing; store-level capex/payback
  benchmarks).
- **Claims contradicted:** 3 of 7 (SSSG-timing-vs-peers assertion; competitive-intensity-flat
  claim; back-end/corporate-overhead-rising-sector-wide claim) — **these are priority items for
  synthesis.**
- **Claims unverifiable:** 1 of 7 (direct Middle East/GCC comparable cost inflation — no peer
  carries comparable exposure).

**The single most consequential contradiction:** the "unchanged competitive intensity" premise.
Three of four peers (RBA, Westlife, Speciality) independently and explicitly describe rising
competitive intensity — new entrants, value-price wars, faster-delivery arms races — in the exact
window UFBL asserted flat competitive intensity across three consecutive calls (per B05). Because
UFBL's own margin-recovery narrative rests partly on that flat-competition premise, this
contradiction should be weighted heavily in synthesis and treated as undermining, not merely
qualifying, the margin thesis.

**The single strongest independent confirmation:** the GST rate-cut tailwind. Three independent
peers (RBA, Westlife, Speciality) each quantify a genuine, government-driven demand/margin benefit
in the same H2 CY2025 window UFBL's own SSSG recovery began, giving UFBL's recovery a real,
external tailwind component — though the tailwind's modest disclosed magnitude (Westlife: 80-100bps
price pass-through) does not by itself explain the much larger scale of UFBL's own SSSG move.

**Overall assessment (symmetric):** The peer set both supports and complicates UFBL's narrative in
roughly equal measure, but tilts toward complicating it on the two dimensions that matter most for
the margin-recovery thesis. It supports UFBL by (a) confirming a genuine GST tailwind existed
sector-wide, (b) showing UFBL's SSSG-recovery timing was, if anything, ahead of rather than behind
peers (undercutting a "mere catch-up" reading and crediting company-specific execution), and (c)
placing UFBL's aggressive store-expansion plan inside a genuine industry-wide capacity build-out
rather than an outlier move. It complicates or undercuts UFBL's narrative by (a) directly
contradicting the "unchanged competitive intensity" premise underlying the margin story, (b)
showing peer back-end/overhead costs falling rather than rising — weakening the "sector-wide SG&A
inflation" explanation for UFBL's own cost step-up and pointing toward a company-specific driver
instead, and (c) showing that peers' own gross margins expanded through most of FY26 while UFBL's
compressed, a divergence UFBL's own concalls do not fully reconcile. On net: **complicates.**

## PART 5: CROSS-PEER HYPOTHESIS

**Hypothesis:** The QSR/casual-dining sector's demand-side recovery in FY26 was idiosyncratic and
staggered — each brand's SSSG inflection was driven by its own value-platform relaunch timing
(Sapphire's INR99 Krisper Meal in Dec-2025, Westlife's INR99 everyday-value rollout starting
Dec-2025 in the West and Mar-2026 in the South, Speciality's store renovations from
Andheri-West-onward) rather than a single synchronized macro trigger — but the cost side became a
synchronized, sector-wide shock for the first time in this cycle via the Mar-Jun 2026
LPG/gas/fuel/packaging inflation event, which hit RBA, Speciality and Westlife simultaneously
regardless of where each brand was in its own demand-recovery cycle. This implies gross-margin
trajectories across RBA, Sapphire, Westlife, Speciality and UFBL should converge or compress
together in Q1-Q2 FY27 (Apr-Sep 2026) even as their SSSG trajectories remain dispersed by
company-specific execution timing rather than a shared tailwind. This is testable: track whether
the five companies' quarterly gross-margin deltas narrow toward each other over the next two
quarters while their SSSG deltas do not.

This hypothesis is not stated by any single peer — each discusses its own demand recovery as
self-caused and its own cost shock as exogenous, but none frames the sector-wide pattern of
"dispersed demand drivers converging into a shared cost shock" as a single dynamic.
