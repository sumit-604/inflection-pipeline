Let me begin with our financial performance for the quarter on a year -on-year basis. Our total 
income for the quarter two is increased by approximately 20% year -on-year to INR967 crores,

was primarily supported by an approximately 11% increase in goods transport volumes from 
976000 tons to 1,079,000 tons, along with a 9% improvement in freight realization per ton from 
INR8,079 to INR8,840. 
 The improvement in realization reflects the positive impact of our freight rate hike and 
rationalization initiatives. Our continued focus on securing remunerative business and 
maintaining pricing discipline has enabled us to improve revenue quality while retaining and 
expanding our customer base. Our EBITDA for the quarter increased to INR226 crores from 
INR158 crores in quarter two, registering an impressive growth of 43%.

Correspondingly, the EBITDA margin expanded to 23.3% from 19.7% in the corresponding

 The operating environment during  this quarter remained challenging, particularly due to 
geopolitical developments, crude oil price volatility  and the resulting increase in fuel 
procurement cost. Fuel continues to be one of our major operating expenses during the period. 
The cost per litre increased significantly from approximately INR 85- INR 97, putting pressure 
on our operating cost structure. The increase also affected the benefits we traditionally derive 
from bulk fuel procurement. 
However, through timely and calibrated freight rate  adjustments, we were able to pass on a 
substantial part of these cost increases to our customers while maintaining healthy volume 
growth. At the same time, our continued focus on cost management and operating leverage 
helped us to contain the impact of in flation across most of the operating expenses, including 
employee costs. 
 Employee costs declined from 18% to 16.48% of total income, in spite of increase in absolute 
terms from INR147 crores to INR159 crores. Administrative expenses declined from 2.28% to  
1.43%, largely reflecting lower legal and professional expenses incurred in the last year. Other 
operating expenses also declined from 10.48% to 10.06% and we also maintained better control 
over toll and hamali expenses as a proportion of income, notwiths tanding the increase in toll 
plaza, higher toll rates effective from April 1 
and increased transportation volumes as well.  
There were some moderate increase in vehicle running expenses, primarily associated with 
higher driver incentives, along with mode st increase in driver salaries. Overall, these 
improvements in expenses ratio demonstrate our ability to manage costs effectively, improve 
asset utilization and protect operating margins in a challenging cost environment. 
 On the business front, our primar y focus continues to be the expansion of our goods 
transportation business through a wider branch network and deeper penetration into existing and 
new markets. During this quarter, we added 51 branches, taking the total number of new 
branches added during the first half to 67 numbers. This expansion has strengthened our 
geographical reach and enabled us to access new customer segments and business opportunities.  
We have also seen encouraging traction in customer acquisition alongside onboarding new 
customers. We have successfully regained business and tonnage from certain customers who 
had earlier discontinued services following our freight rate rationalization and strategic 
withdrawal from low-margin contracts. 
This is an important development because it demonstrates that our disciplined pricing approach, 
along with unmatched service quality, is helping us improve profitability without compromising 
our ability to retain and attract customers.

 As you are aware, VRL has a strong presence in the B2B less-than-truckload segment, supported 
by a diversified customer base of over 10 lakh customers across multiple industries. One of the 
key strengths of our business model is our collection mechanism. A substantial majority of our 
LTL business operates through paid and to-pay segments, under which the freight is collected 
either at the time of bookings or upon delivery of the consignment. 
This substantially reduces our dependence on extended customer credit and supports efficient 
working capital management. Consequently, our trade receivable cycle remains very healthy at 
approximately hardly around 10 - 11 days. Our diversified customer base, relatively low 
customer concentration  and extensive branch network and owned asset operating model 
continue to provide us with a competitive edge over other operators in the industry. 
 Similarly, the sequential performance is also on a growth trend and both Q1 and Q2 put together, 
the H1 results are also commendable numbers. An important highlight of the first half has been 
our strong cash generation. The cash generated from operations before working capital changes 
is around INR415 crores, compared with around INR314 crores in the last first half year. Net 
cash generated from operating activities increased to INR363 crores from INR334 crores. 
We have strategically deployed these internally generated funds towards business expansion, 
infrastructure development and strengthening our balance sheet. Our capital expenditure for the 
first half stood at around INR158 crores, comprising around INR76 crores in quarter one and 
INR82 crores in quarter two. The expenditure was primarily towards the addition of commercial 
vehicles and investment in land and building facilities at a strategically important location, 
including Vijayawada, Nagpur, Lucknow and Raipur. 
 These investments are intended to strengthen our operational infrastructure, support future 
network expansion and facilitate long-term consolidation of our logistics hubs. Despite these 
investments, we have successfully reduced our n et debt to INR337 crores as of September 30, 
compared with INR440 crores as on March 31, 2026. Our net debt -to-EBITDA ratio has 
consequently improved to 0.3 x from 0.4x at the end of FY 2026. Our own asset strategy 
continues to remain an important differentiator for VRL. 
As of the end of H1 FY27, our fleet comprised 5,941 vehicles. We continue to follow a 
disciplined fleet rationalization program involving the replacement and scrapping of older 
vehicles while improving the utilization of our existing owned fleet. Approximately 76% of our 
total fleet is debt-free, while around 11% is fully depreciated and continues to operate in optimal 
condition.
