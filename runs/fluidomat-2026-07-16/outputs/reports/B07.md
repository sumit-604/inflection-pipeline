**2C. Growth embedded in capex — arithmetic shown**

Two methods, both shown per instruction, with the mechanical one flagged as an overstatement:

*Method A — capacity-unit basis (preferred, more defensible):*
- Current capacity ≈ 1,500 units/yr generating FY26 revenue from operations of ₹72.46 Cr (Audited Results, 31-Mar-2026, p.3).
- Implied average realisation ≈ ₹72.46cr / 1,500 units ≈ ₹4.83 lakh/unit.
- New capacity: +2,000 units/yr.
- Implied incremental revenue at full utilisation and unchanged mix ≈ 2,000 × ₹4.83 lakh ≈ ₹96.6 Cr.
- As % of current revenue (₹72.46cr): **+133%** (mechanically identical to the unit-capacity growth %, since ASP is held constant — a simplifying assumption that likely understates true upside if "higher-range" couplings carry higher ASP, per capex slide language "Higher-range coupling units," Inv. Pres. slide 22).

*Method B — historical fixed-asset-turnover (FAT) basis, exactly as the framework specifies:*
- Net fixed assets (PP&E + CWIP + intangibles): FY25 = ₹1,373.44 lakh; FY26 = ₹1,479.15 lakh (Balance Sheets, 31-Mar-2025 and 31-Mar-2026).
- FAT FY25 = ₹7,218.29 lakh / ₹1,373.44 lakh ≈ 5.26x; FAT FY26 = ₹7,246.14 lakh / ₹1,479.15 lakh ≈ 4.90x; average ≈ 5.08x.
- ₹35 Cr (₹3,500 lakh) capex × 5.08x ≈ ₹17,780 lakh (₹177.8 Cr) implied incremental revenue ≈ **+245%** of current revenue.
- 🔍 ANALYST INFERENCE: Method B is almost certainly an overstatement. Fluidomat's FAT is unusually high because its existing base (older, mostly-depreciated plant) already produces ₹72cr of revenue on a small net-asset base; multiplying a lumpy new capex tranche by that ratio double-counts the productivity of assets not actually being replaced. Method A (capacity-unit based, +133%) is used for the YAML `capex_embedded_growth_pct` field as the more credible figure.

Full reasoning against the two "watch" labels:

- **Not HIGH POTENTIAL:** HIGH POTENTIAL requires an AVERAGE (or better) backward score combined with a genuinely accelerating forward moat picture (STRENGTHENING/EXPANSION-grade, evidence quality weighted 📄-heavy). Gate 0's backward score is AVERAGE, not weak/declining in the sense that would define a turnaround situation, even though the working-capital trend within it is deteriorating — the deterioration is a headwind noted inside an otherwise-average business, not a broken business being repaired.
- **Result: AVERAGE.** The emerging-moat scan surfaces real, capital-backed developments (capex, subsidy eligibility, government project pipeline) but they are early-stage, only Moderate-strength, and are set against a backward trend (receivables up, cash conversion down) that argues against, rather than for, an upgrade.
