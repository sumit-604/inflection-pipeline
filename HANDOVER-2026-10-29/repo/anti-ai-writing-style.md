# ANTI AI WRITING STYLE

House style for X posts, WhatsApp messages, narrative summaries, and any reader facing prose.

## THE CORE PRINCIPLE

The brain does not want to think. Thinking burns energy. The reader wants to believe.

The job of the writer is to make believing easy by removing every obstacle the reader's mind might use as an excuse to stop. Dashes are obstacles. Drama is an obstacle. Complicated sentences are obstacles. Adjectives the reader has to verify are obstacles. Hard words are obstacles.

Simple, direct, numbers led, fragment tolerant, dash free, drama free. That is the bar.

## THE READING LEVEL RULE (RULE ZERO)

Public posts must be readable by a 15 year old. That is the ceiling, not the floor.

The reader is not a finance professional. The reader is a normal person scrolling on a phone. The post must work for that reader. If it does not, it does not earn the share, the follow, or the comment.

Plain English words beat technical words whenever the plain word does the same job.

- "Italica is not a stranger" beats "Italica is a related party".
- "The family's real control is 65.5 percent, not 60 percent" beats "Effective promoter group economic interest is 65.5 percent, not 59.84 percent".
- "Aeroflex builds the product. Italica resells it. The extra profit on the resale stays with the family, not with Aeroflex shareholders" beats "The listed entity carries the capex and the first leg margin. The second leg margin sits with the promoter group."

When a technical term is the only honest word for the concept, use it once and explain what it does in one short sentence next to it. RPT, ROCE, CFO, ECL, KAM, CARO, Notice of AGM, Note 36, debtor days, capex, working capital all need a plain English gloss the first time they appear in a public post.

Test by reading the post out loud at normal speed. If you have to slow down, simplify, or stop and reread any line, that line is too complex. Rewrite it until it flows in one pass. If a 15 year old would stop and ask "what does that mean", that line fails.

This rule overrides the Peer to peer voice rule for any post meant for the public timeline. The peer to peer voice rule still applies to internal research notes and posts explicitly addressed to a professional audience. For public posts, simplicity wins every time.

## WHAT TO AVOID

### 1. Dashes and hyphens

Never use em dashes or en dashes as punctuation inside sentences. Use commas, full stops, or restructure into two sentences.

The same rule extends to hyphens inside compound modifiers. "450-bed hospital" becomes "450 bed hospital" or "hospital with 450 beds". "Super-specialty" becomes "super specialty". "Tier-2 city" becomes "Tier 2 city". "Single-location concentration" becomes "single location concentration". "300-bed expansion" becomes "300 bed expansion".

A hyphen is still a dash mark on the page. The reader's eye catches it the same way. If the rule is no dash marks, then no hyphens either.

The only exception is hyphens that are part of a proper noun, ticker symbol, or a filename. Those stay because they are names, not punctuation.

### 2. Dramatic qualifiers

Never use: elite, robust, strong, outstanding, stellar, world class, masterclass, fascinating, remarkable, exceptional, phenomenal, mesmerizing, compelling, intriguing, transformative.

These add nothing. The numbers above the sentence already said it. If the numbers did not say it, the adjective will not save them.

### 3. Landing lines

Do not end a section with a triumphant summary sentence. "This is what an elite cash machine looks like in audited filings" is exactly the line that signals AI authorship. The numbers in the section already did the work. Stop writing.

### 4. Virtue signal headings

Section headings should describe what is in the section. Nothing more. Do not advertise tone or stance through the heading.

Wrong: "Risks, the honest read"
Right: "Risks"

Calling a section "the honest read" implies the rest of the post is dishonest. It is also a virtue claim. The reader does not need the writer to declare honesty. Honesty shows up in the numbers and the symmetric treatment of bull and bear. It does not need a label.

Same rule applies to headings like "The real story", "The truth about", "What management will not tell you", "The honest assessment". All of these are dramatic frames that the body of the section then has to live up to. Drop the frame. Just write the section.

### 5. Arrow notation

Arrows are mechanical shorthand. They are easier than writing the sentence out. Avoid them in published posts. Prefer the plain summary.

Avoid: "EBITDA margin Q2 to Q3 to Q4: 29.0% to 31.1% to 33.0%."
Prefer: "EBITDA margin rose every quarter through FY26, from 29 percent in Q2 to 33 percent in Q4."

Avoid: "FY21 ₹105 Cr to FY26 ₹311 Cr to 3 year CAGR 25%."
Prefer: "Revenue grew from ₹105 crore in FY21 to ₹311 crore in FY26. Compound growth of 25 percent per year."

Use arrows only when a true four or more point sequence cannot be replaced by a sentence. Even then, follow with a one line plain summary.

### 6. Bullet as default

Do not bullet spam. If a paragraph reads fine as a paragraph, leave it as a paragraph. Use bullets only for true lists where each item is parallel and short.

### 7. AI tell vocabulary

Never use these verbs and phrases: delve, dive in, unpack, explore, navigate, journey, leverage as a verb, comprehensive, multifaceted, holistic, paradigm, at the end of the day, it is worth noting, it is important to note, in essence, fundamentally, ultimately, that said, indeed.

### 8. Hedge phrases

Either it is or it is not. Drop "in many ways", "broadly speaking", "more or less", "to a large extent", "by and large".

### 9. Long or fancy words when a short one will do

This is the Reading Level Rule applied at word level. If a 15 year old would not use the word in normal conversation, the post probably should not either, unless it is a technical term that has been glossed.

- "purchase" becomes "buy"
- "utilise" becomes "use"
- "facilitate" becomes "help" or "let"
- "additional" becomes "more"
- "approximately" becomes "about"
- "subsequently" becomes "then" or "later"
- "demonstrate" becomes "show"
- "indicate" becomes "show" or "say"
- "sufficient" becomes "enough"
- "commence" becomes "start"
- "terminate" becomes "end" or "stop"
- "implement" becomes "do" or "put in place"
- "endeavour" becomes "try"

Long Latin and Greek root words signal a writer who is showing off, or a machine. Neither wins on a public timeline.

## HASHTAGS

Hashtags are allowed. Use them sparingly and only when they carry signal.

Good uses:

- The company or ticker the post is about, at the top or bottom. One tag. `#QualityPower`, `#Aeroflex`.
- A widely followed theme tag when the post is about that theme. `#Nifty`, `#BankNifty`.

What to avoid:

- More than two hashtags per post. Three or more starts to read as SEO spam and hurts trust.
- Hashtags in the middle of sentences. Break the flow. Keep them at the top or bottom.
- Generic marketing tags (`#investing`, `#stockmarket`, `#trader`). They pull in wrong audience and add no signal.
- Hashtag stacks at the end of the post. One or two, not a wall.

The rule is not "never". The rule is "sparingly, and only when the tag helps a real reader find the post."

## WHAT TO DO

Numbers carry meaning. Adjectives do not.

Wrong: "Margins improved significantly to a strong level."
Right: "EBITDA margin rose from 24 percent to 33 percent over three quarters."

The reader sees 24 to 33 and knows the move is large. The word "strong" is redundant noise.

Short sentences. Fragments are fine.

"One hospital, one city." is a complete thought. Do not fluff it into "The company operates a single hospital located in a single Tier 2 city."

Plain prose over compressed shorthand

Better: "Free cash flow swung from minus 6 crore in FY25 to plus 62 crore in FY26 as capex collapsed 74 percent."
Worse: "FCF +62 Cr FY26 vs -6 Cr FY25, capex -74%."

The plain version reads like a human said it. The compressed version is faster but signals machine.

Symmetric treatment of bulls and bears

The risk section uses the same calm voice and the same numbers first discipline as the bull section. No softening on the risks. No drama on the upside. The symmetry is what makes the reader trust the writer. The reader who senses asymmetric treatment stops trusting the post.

Peer to peer voice (internal notes and professional audiences only)

For internal research notes and posts that are clearly addressed to a professional audience, assume the reader is competent. CFO/PAT, CWIP, debtor days, ROCE, J curve, IND AA, BMT, OPD/IPD go in without translation. Do not slow down for the back of the room. The audience that needs translation will read someone else.

Applies to internal notes and professional posts only. Public posts follow the Reading Level Rule above.

One thought per sentence

If a sentence has two ideas, break it. The reader handles two short sentences faster than one compound sentence.

Explain the structure with everyday pictures

When a structure is hard, draw a picture in words.

- "Aeroflex builds the product. Italica resells it. The resale profit stays with the family." beats any sentence that uses the words "first leg" and "second leg".
- "The factory is paid for by the listed company. The extra money earned on the resale goes to the family company." beats any sentence that uses the word "margin" twice.

The picture sticks. The jargon does not.

## FORMAT CONVENTIONS FOR X LONG FORM

Headings

Unicode mathematical sans serif bold. Renders bold natively on X, LinkedIn, WhatsApp, Telegram, without markdown.

Example heading style: 𝗧𝗵𝗲 𝗯𝘂𝘀𝗶𝗻𝗲𝘀𝘀

Section numbering

Unicode bold digits: 𝟭, 𝟮, 𝟯, 𝟰, 𝟱.

Structure

A typical post has a 4 to 5 word headline at the top, then 4 or 5 numbered sections, then a Risks block, then a Verdict or Conclusion block.

Each section opens with a one line heading and runs 3 to 5 short prose lines. Numbers in every section.

Length

Around 3,500 to 4,000 characters for X long form premium. A non premium variant tightens to about 1,800. A thread variant splits across 4 tweets each under 280.

Verdict or Conclusion

Non directional. No buy or sell. No price targets in the published version.

State the binary gate and what would confirm or deny the thesis.

Example: "Q1 FY27 in August 2026 is the master gate. EBITDA margin above 32 percent, occupancy above 80 percent, IPD volumes above 5,000. Those are the green signals."

Sign off

Optional. If a sign off is used, keep it short and personal. A single line is enough. Do not force a sign off on every post.

## FORMAT CONVENTIONS FOR SHORT POSTS

Short posts are single paragraph or 4 to 5 point posts meant to make one point well. They follow every rule above, including the Reading Level Rule. They drop the Risks block and the Verdict block.

A short post has one job. Pick the one thing the reader should walk away knowing. Build the post around that thing. Cut everything else.

## THE MASS MIND PRINCIPLE (THE WHY)

Simplicity is not a stylistic preference. It is a strategic choice about how readers actually consume content.

The mass mind reads to believe, not to evaluate. Every line that makes the reader stop and process is a line that loses readers. Adjectives ask the reader to evaluate. Dashes ask the reader to figure out the relationship between two ideas. Compressed shorthand asks the reader to expand mental abbreviations. Drama asks the reader to decide if they trust the writer. Hard words ask the reader to translate.

Each of those asks costs energy. The brain that does not want to spend energy clicks away.

The style described in this document removes those asks. Numbers carry the claim. Short sentences carry the thought. Plain words carry the meaning. The reader does not need to translate, evaluate, or trust. The reader just reads.

That is why this style works on X. And that is why it converts to followers.

## QUICK CHECKLIST BEFORE PUBLISHING

- Reading level test: Read the post out loud at normal speed. Did you stop or slow down on any line? Rewrite that line.
- 15 year old test: Could a 15 year old who is not in finance follow every sentence? If no, simplify.
- Technical term gloss: Is every technical term explained in plain English the first time it appears in the post?
- Long words: Any "purchase", "utilise", "facilitate", "subsequently", "demonstrate", "approximately"? Swap for the short word.
- Dashes: Any em dashes, en dashes, or hyphens in compound modifiers? Strip them.
- Virtue signal headings: Any "the honest read", "the real story"? Just name the section plainly.
- Dramatic qualifiers: Any of the banned adjectives? Cut and let numbers speak.
- Landing lines: Any line summarising what the section just said? Delete it.
- Arrows: Replaceable by a plain summary? Replace.
- Sentence length: Any sentence longer than 25 words? Break it.
- Symmetry: Same calm voice in the risks as in the bulls? If not, rewrite the risks until the cadence matches.
- AI tells: Delve, unpack, explore, leverage, comprehensive, multifaceted? Remove.
- Hashtags: More than two? Cut. Generic marketing tags? Cut. In the middle of a sentence? Move to top or bottom.

If every box is checked, post.
