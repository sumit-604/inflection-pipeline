## DIRECT ANSWER TO THE OPERATOR
The entry zone moves up only marginally, from about Rs 1,497-1,796 to about Rs 1,542-1,789
(roughly +3% at the low end, flat at the high end), because refreshed FY31 base PAT rises
only from Rs 420 Cr to Rs 440 Cr. Even if the 80% guide is credited in full (which
conservative discipline forbids on one back-half-dependent, soft-base-flattered quarter
with INDETERMINATE cash and a scale-driven, not gross-margin-driven, margin beat), the
entry zone reaches only about Rs 1,694. The zone barely moves because the raised guidance
mostly pulls forward growth the prior model already expected MTAR to reach by FY31; the
FY31 destination earnings, which are the only thing that can lift the entry price, rise
only about 5%. The other lever, a higher exit PE, is unavailable: 40x is the
peer-validated ceiling, and the newly evidenced FY26 ROCE of 17.2% feeds the Pillar 1
formula to 16.1x versus the 19x the prior rode on a 22% destination assumption, so the
evidence pressures the multiple down, not up. At CMP about Rs 5,200 the Hurdle Ratio is
1.22 on base earnings and 1.53 on bull earnings, both below the 1.953 pass line, so the
25% hurdle is infeasible even on the bull case, and the probability-weighted 5-year
expected return is about -2.7%. CMP Rs 5,200 enters NO zone: it is about 2.9x the top of
the entry zone and about 2.2x the AVOID line, so the call is AVOID-on-valuation. The stock
is cheaper than it was at Rs 7,210 (expected return improved from -8.7% to -2.7%), but
"cheaper" is not "in the zone." Entry price is a valuation output set by FY31 destination
earnings and the exit PE. It does not move because the market price fell.

One-line thesis: MTAR at Rs 5,200 stays AVOID-on-valuation because FY31 base EPS grows
Rs 30.6 to Rs 143 at a carried 40x destination PE gives Rs 4,707 base FV and a Rs 1,542
to 1,789 entry zone, so CMP sits ~2.9x above entry; the raised 80% guide lifts the zone
only ~3% (pull-forward, not a higher destination), and evidenced ROCE 17.2% (formula
16.1x) argues the 40x is already generous. Key risk: back-half delivery and H1 cash.
