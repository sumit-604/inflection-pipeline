# BUSINESS UNDERSTANDING NARRATIVE

India Glycols makes four physical things: bio based glycols and glycol ethers, potable spirits, fuel grade ethanol, and plant based pharma ingredients. The glycols and glycol ethers are industrial building blocks; the company converts molasses and ethylene oxide into MEG, DEG, TEG and heavy glycols that go into antifreeze, polyester fibre, paints and personal care. It is the only Indian maker of ethylene based glycol ethers on Sulzer Chemtech technology, so some buyers cannot swap it out quickly. The potable spirits line sells IMFL, country liquor and extra neutral alcohol, and it leads in Uttar Pradesh and Uttarakhand under state excise licences. The bio fuel line supplies fuel grade ethanol to oil marketing companies under the national blending programme, and Ennature makes plant APIs, nutraceuticals and nicotine with a global position in the Thiocolchicoside API. Customers differ by line: brand owners and institutions buy the spirits under a 15 year Bacardi tie, an expanded Amrut distribution arrangement, and CSD and paramilitary canteen approvals; oil marketing companies take the ethanol; chemical and consumer formulators and the Clariant JV take the glycols and specialty chemicals. Spirits buyers qualify a distiller once and then stay, so the Bacardi, Amrut and CSD approvals lock in volume, while ethanol offtake is set by government tender rather than by any single end buyer.

Present demand reads through named external signals. OMC ethanol tender and allocation results by supply year set bio fuel volume and price; UP and Uttarakhand excise revenue bulletins proxy demand in the core spirits states; Brent crude oil price sets how competitive the EO and bio MEG lines are; the China glycol ether and MEG feedstock and export price index drives glycol ether export margin; Clariant IGL Specialty Chemicals JV disclosures feed the chemicals outlook; and EU and US brand owner sustainability procurement commitments pull premium priced bio MEG. Forward growth rests on the E20 to E25/E100 blending policy notification, the NSU bio amine platform qualifying at named buyers BASF, Unilever and Mibelle, and CSD and paramilitary brand approvals widening the spirits channel. Two of the company's own tailwinds move the wrong way: EBP ethanol allocation fell about 22% year on year even as national blending held at 20%, and BSPC chemicals revenue fell about 26% across three years while its India market grew about 6.7% a year.

Competitive advantage sits mostly in spirits. Qualification lock in with Bacardi, Amrut and CSD scored Strong, and captive ENA backward integration scored Moderate. The chemicals line has a narrow and eroding edge: the Sulzer glycol ether position and a six year process innovation margin trend are Moderate, but the "only maker in India" scarcity claims are self reported and unverified, and no peer in the set makes glycols so no independent check exists. The bio fuel line has no moat; it is a government quota taker whose volume and price are set by tender. Ennature rests on a single product Thiocolchicoside position with concentration risk, so its edge is real but thin.

# Three way demerger with every part expensive

India Glycols is a four business group splitting into three separately listed companies on 1 September 2026. A continuing holder keeps residual India Glycols, the chemicals and industrial gases plus a 49% Clariant JV stake. The holder also receives IGL Spirits, one share for one, and Ennature Bio Pharma, one share for every three. One blended multiple cannot price this, so the whole run values it as a sum of the parts.

FY26 net revenue was Rs 4,211 Cr on a net of excise basis, EBITDA Rs 654 Cr, and consolidated PAT Rs 293 Cr. Gate 0 scored the group AVERAGE, 44 of 100 core after a verifier correction, zero moats confirmed at the scoring bar, mostly for lack of peer data rather than proven absence. Cash conversion over ten years is strong at 2.06 times CFO to PAT, but free cash flow was negative in both FY25 and FY26 as a Bio MEG and EO capex build ran ahead of it, and the current ratio sits below 1.0 on both bases. Revenue growth over nine years is unremarkable at 6.0%; the 27% PAT growth reads high only because FY17 was a depressed base.

Management delivers the core financial guidance but goes silent where it hurts. It graded B on credibility, delivered debt reduction and the spirits pace it guided, and kept aspiration separate from projection. It also missed the NSU doubling twice, never once mentioned an FX hedge book that collapsed 92% while unhedged exposure roughly doubled, never named the 22% ethanol allocation cut against an upbeat bio fuel story, and deflected a twice repeated segment debt breakup question. The promoter side is clean: TRUSTWORTHY, zero pledge, and a Rs 467 Cr preferential raise deployed to debt reduction and confirmed by the rating agency.

Peers complicate the story rather than confirm it. Two sugar and ethanol peers and two spirits peers corroborate industry wide excess ethanol capacity and the shift to grain majority feedstock. They contradict the UP market volume framing, where Globus puts UP near a crore cases a month against the range IGL implied, though a verifier notes the gap reconciles as an IMFL only versus total market scope mismatch. No peer confirms the 22% allocation magnitude, and no peer makes glycols, so the bio glycol realisation decline cannot be cross checked at all.

The market opportunity is MODERATE, not a land grab. The residual chemicals entity has a realistic TAM near Rs 63,000 Cr and a five year SOM near Rs 1,870 Cr, a 16.6 times revenue headroom on paper. The company has been losing share in a growing market, which is why the runway class is MODERATE rather than EXPANSION grade. Management's Rs 2,500 Cr four to five year revenue aspiration implies about 16.7% CAGR, roughly double the SOM implied 8.4%.

Valuation concludes AVOID on valuation. Sum of the parts base fair value is Rs 722 to 838 per original share against CMP Rs 1,191, so the parts are worth 30% to 39% below the traded price. Both destination PE tracks are carried per entity: residual India Glycols at an additive 14.5 times, RRM 11.5 times, under a 30 times chemicals cap; IGL Spirits at RRM 16.0 times, additive 19.5 times, pillar base 19.5 times under a non binding 40 times consumer cap; Ennature a provisional 12 times under a 25 times pharma cap. Every entity fails the 25% hurdle at CMP, a STOP on each. Fair value itself compounds only 8.5% a year, so the return classifies DISCOUNT CLOSER, not compounding; the entry zone Rs 370 to 429 is where the maths works, and reaching it needs a 64% to 69% de rating. Ennature carries INSUFFICIENT CONVICTION until a guar free quarter clears its base, and every per entity figure is a labelled estimate pending filed standalone opening balance sheets.

The thesis and the devil's advocate land on the same side. Role 2 set WATCHLIST at a Small size and an entry of Rs 380 to 429, since narrowed to Rs 370 to 429. Role 3 graded the thesis WEAKENED BUT ALIVE: valuation safety survives, but growth, moat and trust all weakened. The sharpest counters are that the JV stake sale which deleverages the residual entity also removes about half the JV earnings that are its main asset; that the converter's 13% through cycle ROCE rides an EO price crude tailwind management itself credits to the war, never shown at median input prices; that 74% of the sum of the parts value sits on a government set spirits margin with no pricing power; and that the entry zone is reachable only in states that would themselves withdraw it.
