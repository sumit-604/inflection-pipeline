### 19.1 SUMMARY NARRATIVE

Revenue rose 23.7% to ₹281.75 Cr and operating EBITDA rose 34.3% to ₹63.88 Cr, lifting the operating margin 180 basis points to 22.7% (R L582-593), and the earnings call confirmed every one of those filed numbers to the rupee (C l.10/12). ### 19.2 SECTOR INTELLIGENCE

Dialysis is a chronic, recurring, life-sustaining service: a patient needs roughly three sessions a week, cannot travel far for each, and cannot survive long without treatment, which produces sticky annuity revenue. *(Provenance: this-quarter concall, C l.8; corroborates the Notion structural, non-cyclical sector read.)* The demand driver is the diabetes and hypertension burden feeding chronic kidney disease (C l.8), and the load-bearing regulatory variable is reimbursement — the business is built around government and insurance schemes, with 76.5% of guests on public insurance (P L1542, prior deck). India is early in the cycle: penetration is low, and the organised (pure-play network) share of dialysis is only ~20-22%, up from 0% sixteen years ago, with ~78-79% of capacity still run in-house by hospitals (Vikram, C l.38/110). *(Provenance: this-quarter concall; consistent with Notion ~7% penetration thesis.)* The structural tailwind is the shift from unorganised to organised provision: hospitals lose money on in-house dialysis (it is "not even the 10th profit driver," under 1% of hospital revenue) because dialysis needs massive scale and 100% focus to earn margins, so hospitals should rationally outsource — a conversion management says "will pay out over the next several years" (C l.8/35/38). *(Provenance: this-quarter concall.)* Globally, dialysis is done in standalone clinics almost everywhere except India and Indonesia, and India's regulatory ecosystem is only now "coming to terms" with standalone clinics — a slow 5-10 year regulatory shift that, if it accelerates, is a second structural tailwind (Vikram, C l.110). The named global sector reference points are the two listed majors, Fresenius Medical Care and DaVita (C l.117). No sector headwind surfaced beyond the inherent reimbursement dependence and lumpy, once-a-decade price resets (CGHS +35% after ~10-11 years; Philippines +55-60% after ~10 years, C l.40/56).

### 19.3 BUSINESS-MODEL INTELLIGENCE

NephroPlus runs an asset-light, build-own-operate dialysis network, mostly inside partner hospitals (captive and PPP) with a smaller standalone base (25 standalone clinics in India, C l.110); the hospital provides space and utilities, NephroPlus provides machines, staff and protocols and takes the dialysis revenue, freeing the hospital's bandwidth and capex (C l.37). *(Provenance: this-quarter concall; extends the Notion asset-light model note.)* The economic engine is volume × price: guests +13% to 38,262, treatments +13.3% to 10.3 lakh, revenue per treatment +9.2% to ₹2,733 (C l.12). Four cost levers create the margin: global procurement of consumables (COGS ~22% of cost, improved 175 bps this quarter) including a new lever of **contract manufacturing of low-complexity consumables**; an in-house training academy (NIDA) for renal nurses; an in-house biomedical team for machine maintenance; and a lean India-built operating overhead (C l.27/35). The defining model fact this quarter is the **geographic margin structure**: India RPT is only $20-23 while Kazakhstan is ~$75 and the group runs the India cost model in higher-price markets, so the India parent earns ~14% operating EBITDA margin against the consolidated ~22.7% — the higher-price international clinics carry the margin (C l.27/71; Steps 1c/2b, A3 F2). *(Provenance: this-quarter filing + concall.)* The model is a fixed-capacity, not a same-store, business: a machine does only three 4.5-hour cycles a day, so mature clinics at ~85% utilisation grow mainly by adding capacity, and network utilisation is 74% (C l.75/120). The drift to watch: headline profit is presented adjusted (removing the Saudi drag), a one-time depreciation benefit flattered this quarter's margin, and the contract-manufacturing move is the first hint of a step away from pure asset-light — the honest gauge of whether international expansion is accretive is the gap between reported and adjusted profit as Saudi and Kazakhstan scale. *(Provenance: this-quarter concall, F1.1/F6.7/F16.1.)*

### 19.4 COMPETITION INTELLIGENCE

NephroPlus is India's and Asia's largest dialysis network by treatment volume at 550 clinics across five countries, with organised-market leadership and a moat management frames as "massive scale + 100% focus" — the company was EBITDA-negative for 11 years and PAT-negative for 13 years, which is itself the barrier to entry (Vikram, C l.8/35). *(Provenance: this-quarter concall; aligns with Notion market-leader / moat-expansion positioning, EM 46/100.)* Where it wins: scale-led procurement, an in-house biomedical team that removes the manufacturer information-asymmetry on repairs, a training academy that de-bottlenecks nurse supply, a 75%+ tier-2/3 footprint hard to replicate, and deep hospital relationships (300+ private hospital partners, C l.35). In the Philippines it is #2 of ~900 clinics (the largest chain has 58, NephroPlus 51) in a fragmented mom-and-pop market where scale and brand are decisive (C l.106); in Uzbekistan it is the **only** private operator, serving ~1,800 of ~9,000 patients — a large white space in a market it already leads (C l.106). Where it is structurally weaker / the risk to watch: internationally it competes against the deep-balance-sheet global majors **Fresenius and DaVita** in the high-price markets it is now entering, and its Saudi entry is a **51:49 JV with a local listed partner (Tibbiyah)** structured around a **binary, government-tender market** — "you win one of the four clusters, you have a business; if you don't win, you pack the bags and leave" (Vikram, C l.81), with the loss run-rate and win probability undisclosed. Public-payer dependence means competition is partly a tendering game, so a lost Saudi MoH tender is a lost market — which is exactly why the pending tender is the key competitive as well as growth variable. No peer dialysis-chain concall reported within the ±4-week window, so no direct peer-narrative cross-check was available this quarter. *(Provenance: this-quarter concall for positioning; peer/sector framing reconciled with Notion memory.)*
