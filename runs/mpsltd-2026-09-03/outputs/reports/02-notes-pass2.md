## NEW FINDINGS

### 1. Title deeds not in the Company's name — larger and older than a routine footnote
(Note 3.1.1 standalone, p.180; Note 3.2 standalone, p.181; mirrored at consolidated Note 3.2, p.263)
Schedule III mandatory disclosure, absent from Pass 1 entirely. Four property items, net carrying
value at 31-Mar-26:
- PP&E freehold land: 400.00 — title in the name of an erstwhile MPS Interactive/HASCO
  Associates partnership structure ("HMG Ambassador Building"), held by the Company since
  09-Feb-2000, arising from a 2004 Karnataka scheme/partnership conversion (Companies Act 1956,
  Part IX).
- PP&E building: 697.79 — same erstwhile-partnership title, held since 09-Feb-2000.
- Investment property freehold land: 4.36 — title in the name of Brigade Marketing Company
  Private Limited, an entity merged into Macmillan India (now MPS Limited) in 2001 under a
  Karnataka High Court-approved scheme (Companies Act 1956, ss.391-394).
- Investment property building: 84.18 — same Brigade Marketing entity, held since 31-Dec-1993.
Combined ≈1,186.33 lacs, roughly 48% of the Company's total PP&E + investment property net book
value, sits on title deeds never formally re-registered in the Company's own name across THREE
DECADES of subsequent corporate mergers. No current legal dispute is disclosed, but the
administrative gap has persisted since 1993/2000 through multiple court-approved schemes without
resolution. 🟡 Watch — legitimate management question: why was title never transferred, and is
there any residual risk on eventual sale, financing, or the pending ADI BPO amalgamation.

### 2. Investment property fair value gap widened, never touches the P&L
(Note 3.2 standalone, p.181; consolidated Note 3.2, p.263)
Fair value (independent registered valuer, market approach) rose from 3,881.03 (FY25) to
4,683.91 (FY26), +20.7%, against a cost-model carrying value of only 88.54-88.55. An
unrecognised ≈803 lacs of asset value appreciation sits off-balance-sheet under the cost model —
legitimate accounting treatment, but a hidden-asset-value data point Pass 1 did not surface.
🟢 Clean treatment / informational.

### 3. Standalone "Rent" expense line up ~16x, unexplained
(Note 27 standalone, p.201; consolidated Note 27, p.283)
Standalone Rent (within Other Expenses, distinct from the ROU/short-term-lease exemption lines
in Note 33) rose from 11.91 (FY25) to 187.86 (FY26), +1,477.8%. Consolidated Rent rose from
149.22 to 363.28, +143.5%. No narrative anywhere in the notes explains the jump; it is not the
same line as the short-term-lease expense (1,078.49 standalone, separately disclosed in Note
33(iv)). NOT FOUND IN DOCUMENT: reason for the increase. 🔴 Red flag / management question.

### 4. Consolidated Royalty expense up ~20x while standalone Royalty is flat-to-down
(Note 27 consolidated, p.283, vs Note 27 standalone, p.201)
Consolidated Royalty expense: 30.15 (FY25) → 626.16 (FY26), +1,977%. Standalone Royalty expense
over the same period: 71.40 → 66.67 (a slight decline). Because the two known intra-group
royalty flows (MPS Limited ↔ Semantico Limited, disclosed in the RPT note) are small and
roughly offsetting, this implies a large NEW third-party royalty cost has appeared at a
subsidiary — plausibly tied to the newly acquired Unbound Medicine or AJE content-licensing
lines — with no narrative anywhere in the notes. 🔴 Red flag / management question.

### 5. Gratuity plan-asset composition data is stale, for the second year running
(Note 32(b) standalone, p.206; consolidated Note 31(b), p.286)
"The above composition of plan assets are based on details received for 31 March 2025; details
for 31 March 2026 are awaited from Life Insurance Corporation of India." This same caveat
structure recurs year to year, indicating a persistent administrative lag between MPS and its
insurer on a disclosure that should be current at each reporting date. 🟡 Watch.

### 6. Current Ratio collapse — the single largest ratio movement in the filing, missed in Pass 1
(Note 51 standalone, p.226; Note 50 consolidated, p.309)
Pass 1 flagged only the Trade Receivable Turnover Ratio move (-4.46% standalone), which the
company itself labels "not applicable" under its 25%-variation threshold. The notes disclose
two much larger, THRESHOLD-BREACHING movements that Pass 1 did not report at all:
- Current Ratio: standalone 4.67x → 2.53x, -45.82% (management remark: "decrease is on account
  of external borrowings and increased gratuity and leave encashment liability due to change in
  labour code"); consolidated 1.97x → 1.45x, -26.40%.
- Standalone Net Capital Turnover Ratio: 2.51x → 3.37x, +34.26% (same causes).
Both exceed the company's own 25% disclosure trigger and carry explanatory remarks in the
notes — this is a materially weaker standalone liquidity position post-acquisition-debt than
Pass 1's ratio coverage conveyed. 🔴 Escalate.

### 7. Forward currency book: notional up ~72-83%, and a first-ever net unrealised LOSS position
(Note 35(iii) standalone forward covers, p.213; Note 18 standalone, p.198; Note 16 standalone
DTA schedule, p.196; mirrored at consolidated Note 34, p.293 and Note 19, p.280)
- USD forward-sell notional: FC 47.00 lacs (INR 4,052.43) → FC 81.00 lacs (INR 7,431.53),
  +72.4% standalone (consolidated book unchanged, same contracts).
- "Unrealised loss payable on forward covers" appears for the FIRST TIME: 358.56 (FY26) vs Nil
  (FY25), sitting in Other financial liabilities.
- Simultaneously a DTA line "Unrealised MTM gain receivables on forward covers" of 90.22 appears
  in the same year (Note 16), implying a gross book with both winning and losing legs, not a
  single simple hedge.
- Net USD statement-of-financial-position exposure grew 8,170.29 → 10,394.94 lacs, +27.2% (Note
  35, p.212), with a 1% USD move now swinging P&L by ≈104-114 lacs at either level.
Pass 1 covered only the MPSi-stake derivative asset; the currency-hedge book itself, larger and
directionally mixed, was not surfaced. 🟡 Watch — rising USD concentration plus a swing to a net
hedging loss in the same year revenue growth quality is already in question (see Pass 1 Finding
#2).

### 8. Trade receivable "significant increase in credit risk" bucket — Pass 1 mis-anchored the
number; the real trend is worse than stated
(Note 11 consolidated, p.270-271)
Pass 1 reported "totals 256.54 (FY26)... entirely zero at standalone level." Re-reading the
actual ageing tables: 256.54 is the FY25 comparative; the FY26 figure is 338.65 (unbilled/not

not merely "existed" in one year — a materially worse deterioration signal than Pass 1's framing
conveyed, on top of the already-flagged 35.4% ECL-allowance growth. 🔴 Correct and elevate Pass
1 Finding #3.

### 9. Consolidated Auditor's Report carries THREE Key Audit Matters, not one
(Auditor's Report pp.228-231; Pass 1 cited only the standalone report's sole KAM)
The consolidated audit identifies: (i) Revenue Recognition (same judgement area as standalone);
(ii) Business Combination — the Unbound Medicine acquisition (USD 16.50m consideration),
flagged for the subjectivity of intangible-asset valuation and purchase price allocation; (iii)
Impairment assessment of Goodwill — the auditor states goodwill of INR 37,632.09 lacs represents
40% of the Group's total assets, with an actual FY26 impairment charge of 1,292.54 already
recorded against the Liberate Group CGU. This materially reinforces Pass 1's Finding #1 with the
auditor's own materiality framing that goodwill is 40% of the balance sheet. 🔴 Elevate.

### 10. Meaningful reliance on other auditors for newly acquired overseas entities
(Auditor's Report, Other Matter paras 15-16, pp.234-235)
Four subsidiaries not audited by Walker Chandiok & Co LLP — combined total assets 5,776.74
lacs, total revenues 11,072.60 lacs (≈14.4% of consolidated revenue), net cash outflows 210.05
lacs — audited by other auditors whose reports were relied upon (all located outside India, with
management-performed GAAP-to-Ind AS conversion reviewed by the principal auditor). Separately,
MPS Employee Welfare Trust and one overseas branch (combined assets 1,839.46 lacs, revenues
797.67 lacs) were audited by yet another (branch) auditor with a further AS-to-Ind AS
conversion. Not a red flag in itself, but a meaningful slice of a newly acquisitive group's
numbers is second-hand to the principal auditor. 🟡 Watch.

### 11. AJE Group purchase price allocation materially revised, ~13 months post-acquisition
(Note 40(b) consolidated, p.303; corroborated in the Note 5 goodwill roll-forward, p.264-265)
The AJE Group (acquired 29-Feb-2024) PPA was revised via a "measurement period adjustment"
recorded in the FY25 comparative column of the goodwill roll-forward: goodwill cut from a
provisional 10,165.50 to a final 7,299.40, a reduction of 2,866.10 (-28.2%), reallocated chiefly
to a newly recognised Deferred Tax Asset of 2,360.06 (from zero) and a full reversal of an
initially recognised Deferred Tax Liability of 688.91 to nil, plus a 321.90 write-down of
identified intangibles. This finalisation sits at or just past Ind AS 103's 12-month
measurement-period boundary from the acquisition date, and represents a large retrospective
revision of a previously reported figure. 🔴 Red flag / management question on timing.

### 12. Unbound Medicine acquisition is extremely goodwill-heavy; pro-forma margin implies a
much lower run-rate than the stub period showed
(Note 40(a) consolidated, p.301-302)
Of the total 14,039.93 purchase consideration, 12,386.36 (88.2%) sits in goodwill against net
identifiable assets of only 1,653.57. Pro forma disclosure: had the deal closed 1-Apr-2025, FY26
Group revenue would have been 6,713.02 higher and profit 594.30 higher — an implied 8.85% margin
on the incremental pro-forma revenue, materially below the actual 10-Feb-to-31-Mar-26 stub-period
margin of 14.5% (171.42 PAT / 1,182.92 revenue). The stub period may not be representative of a
full-year run rate. 🟡 Watch, not a red flag — but a caution against extrapolating the stub
period.

### 13. Liberate Group liability write-back — the numbers and cross-references do not reconcile
across notes
(Note 19(b) standalone/consolidated equivalent, p.280; Note 33(h)/34(h) fair-value roll-forward,
p.292; Note 28(f), p.283)
- Note 19(b) narrative: "the liability of INR 1,324.79 lacs was written back under 'Exceptional
  Items (net)' for the year ended 31 March 2026. Amount of INR 1,572.68 lacs shown as
  Exceptional Items (net) for the year ended 31 March 2025."
- The Level 3 fair-value roll-forward (Note 33(h)) shows, for the SAME Liberate 35%-stake
  liability, a "Liability written back during the year" of 1,572.68 in the FY26 movement
  column, cross-referenced to "note 28(c)" — which is actually the Goodwill Impairment note, not
  the write-back note.
- A separately labelled "Contingent consideration" liability (Note 33(h) FY25 column) shows a
  591.07 write-back apparently occurring in FY25, even though Note 28(f) (the FY26 Exceptional
  Items note) describes this same 591.07 figure as a FY26 write-back for "the remaining amount
  for purchase of 65% stake of Liberate Group."
A reader cannot cleanly trace which rupee figure and which fiscal year attaches to which
sub-event from the notes as drafted. This is not merely a rounding quirk; two different amounts
(1,324.79 vs 1,572.68) are both described as "the" write-back for the same liability in the same
year, and a wrong note cross-reference compounds the confusion. This sits directly inside the
mechanism Pass 1 flagged in Finding #4/#5 (exceptional items positive both years, driven by
write-backs) and provides a concrete, traceable example of why that pattern is hard to verify
from the filing alone. 🔴 Red flag — elevate; strong candidate for a management question.

### 20. ESOP Trust / Treasury Shares mechanism — not previously described
(Note 13(i)(g) consolidated, p.273; cross-referenced at standalone Note 30, p.204)
The MPS Employee Welfare Trust ("ESOP Trust") purchases MPS shares from the secondary market and
holds them as treasury shares for issuance on option exercise, rather than the Company issuing
fresh shares. Treasury shares held: 1,45,107 (opening FY26) → 1,39,884 (closing FY26), with

EPS-note treasury-share adjustment Pass 1 noted only as a number, not as a described structure.
🟢 Clean/routine, informational — relevant context for why dilution is managed via secondary
purchase rather than new issuance.

## PASS 2 NEW FINDINGS SUMMARY

Twenty new items surfaced on the second pass, none of which were manufactured to appear
thorough — each traces to a specific note, page, and number. The most consequential corrections
to the Pass 1 record are:
1. The Current Ratio collapse (-45.82% standalone / -26.40% consolidated) is the single largest
   ratio movement in the entire filing and was absent from Pass 1's ratio coverage (Finding #6).
2. The consolidated Trade Receivable "significant increase in credit risk" bucket is UP 32.0%
   YoY (256.54 → 338.65), not a same-year-only figure as Pass 1 stated — the deterioration Pass 1
   flagged is worse and more established than reported (Finding #8).
3. Two unexplained cost-line spikes — Rent +1,478% standalone, Royalty expense +1,977%
   consolidated — have no narrative anywhere in the notes (Findings #3, #4).
4. The Liberate Group liability write-back cannot be reconciled across Notes 19(b), 28(f), and
   33(h): two different rupee amounts (1,324.79 vs 1,572.68) are both described as "the"
   write-back for the same liability in the same year, with a mis-directed cross-reference
   (Finding #13). This gives Pass 1's "recurring positive exceptional items" concern (Finding
   #4/#5) a concrete, traceable example of poor internal consistency.
5. Consolidated employee costs fell 2.4% while the Group added two acquisitions' headcount,
   while standalone employee costs rose 25.4% — a cost-side echo of the revenue-recharge pattern
   Pass 1 already flagged, now visible on both sides of the P&L (Finding #15, #16).
6. The consolidated Auditor's Report carries three Key Audit Matters (revenue recognition,
   business combination, goodwill impairment — goodwill framed by the auditor as 40% of total
   Group assets), not the single KAM Pass 1 cited from the standalone report only (Finding #9).
7. A material, ~13-month-post-acquisition PPA revision cut AJE Group goodwill by 28.2% (Finding
   #11) — a genuine restatement of a prior year's reported figures that Pass 1 did not surface.

These findings should be carried into Pass 3 for pattern-checking (especially Finding #13's
cross-note numbers, and the Rent/Royalty spikes) and folded into the consolidated Top 15 and Red
Flags sections.
