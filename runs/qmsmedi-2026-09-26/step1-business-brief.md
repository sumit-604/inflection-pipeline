## The engine the thesis would rest on

A mix shift from device trading to pharma-commercialisation services. The
bull reading: services (camps, PSPs, Saarathi) are sticky, pharma-budget
funded and asset light, so services at 40%+ of revenue lift margin and cash
conversion, and the own brand adds product margin. The bear reading: services
revenue is bought through acquisition, FY26 margin fell as services grew, and
the product book is a working-capital-heavy trading business funded by rising
short-term debt. The observation that separates them: segment EBIT margin
(services vs products) and consolidated CFO/EBITDA in FY27, after Saarathi is
fully consolidated.
