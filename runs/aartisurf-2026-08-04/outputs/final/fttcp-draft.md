## THE CATALYST STORY IN PLAIN WORDS

There is exactly one real catalyst in this company and everything hangs off it.

**The capex.** Aarti Surfactants is spending about Rs 85 Cr over two years across Pithampur and Silvassa, roughly Rs 60 Cr of it borrowed, with the main Pithampur expansion due to finish in December 2026. This is documented in CARE's December 2025 rating note, not merely claimed by management, and the audited balance sheet corroborates it: capital work in progress went from Rs 14.65 Cr to Rs 41.03 Cr and capex spend went from Rs 19.98 Cr to Rs 45.10 Cr. What would confirm it firing: commissioning announced on schedule, then capital work in progress converting into productive assets and revenue in FY28. What would kill it: a cost overrun, a delay past mid 2027, or commissioning into a market where the extra volume only earns 5% margins.

**What could fix margin.** Operating leverage from the new capacity is the honest answer, and it is a FY28 story, not a FY27 one. Beyond that there is nothing documented: no pricing action with a date, no hedging programme, no named cost reduction target, no backward integration. Q1 FY27 at 7.49% is an outcome, not an action, and one quarter does not establish a trend against peers who are describing fresh input cost pressure in the very same quarter.

**What could fix cash: NONE FOUND.** This is a real finding, not an empty cell. Across the annual report, both results filings and the rating note there is no working capital programme, no factoring or discounting facility, no debtor day target and no payment term renegotiation. Because the company holds no earnings calls, there is not even a management claim to discount. The FY26 cash strength came from releasing working capital and cannot be repeated by the same route.

**What could fix ROCE.** Only margin, and only then. The capacity spend makes ROCE worse first. There is no idle capital to unwind, no asset sale disclosed, no subsidiary unlocking. The one subsidiary, Aarti HPC, holds a land parcel at Pithampur with no meaningful operations and its net assets of Rs 4.17 Cr already sit below the Rs 5.75 Cr the parent paid, with no impairment recognised.

**What the peers say, which matters because it cuts against the bull case.** Rossari is rephasing its own capex and targeting a debt free balance sheet within 18 months. Galaxy has kept growth capex paused since August 2025. That leaves Aarti Surfactants as the only one of the three adding debt funded capacity into this cycle. Rossari also discloses that no single customer is above 2% of its sales, against 71% in two customers here.

### THE EARNINGS BASIS QUESTION, your call

The valuation needs one basis and the answer changes the picture materially here.

- **One year forward PE**, applying the multiple to forward EPS. Fits a business mid capex whose current earnings are depressed by a margin trough. But it credits the margin recovery that this very draft scores as STAGNANT and genuinely uncertain, so it risks paying for the same unproven recovery twice, once in the earnings and once in the multiple.
- **Trailing PE**, applying the multiple to trailing EPS. Fits the conservative read and avoids the double credit. But FY26 trailing EPS of Rs 14.99 is itself depressed and flattered in the comparison by FY25 one time gains, so trailing overstates how expensive the stock is relative to normalised earnings.
