## The picture in one paragraph

Friday was the first down day of the week, and it was a mild one. The Nifty fell about half a percent and closed near 25,150. Only two of 35 indices rose. A little over a third of all stocks rose on the day, and the rest fell. Still, 75 stocks rose four and a half percent or more, against 59 that fell that hard, so the buyers kept a small edge at the extremes. Turnover eased again to about 450 crore. The short trend gave back the most: 118 stocks slipped under their 20 day line. The count above the 50 day line fell to 1,262, back to Wednesday's level and still above half the market. The count above the 200 day line fell by only 13, to 1,132. Realty, the best index on Thursday, was the worst on Friday. Autos, metals and banks also fell. New yearly highs rose to 68, the most since 23 September. The fear gauge fell again, to 15.5, on a day when prices fell. The repair paused on light volume, and the long clock held almost all of its ground.

## Where the market stands

Each stock in the record has three trend lines: 20 days, 50 days and 200 days. A stock above a line is in an uptrend on that clock. On Friday 1,597 stocks stood above the short line, a little over two in three. On Thursday it was 1,715. 1,262 stood above the middle line, a little over half. 1,132 stood above the long line, a little under half. The short trend still belongs to the buyers by two to one. The middle trend belongs to them by a thinner margin than Thursday. The long trend is still with the sellers, by 87 stocks.

The year gives these counts their scale. On 23 March the long count hit a low of 287. On 1 April came a great thrust day, when 1,129 stocks rose hard. The long count climbed through the summer and peaked at 1,373 on 26 August. It slid through September, and 23 September was the last day with more stocks above the long line than below it. The floor broke in October. On 21 October 414 stocks fell hard in one day. Two days later only 606 stocks held the long line and only 188 held the short line. Friday's long count sits more than 500 stocks above that low.

The bounce is now three weeks old, and Friday was its second down day. The first came on 6 November, with 151 hard fallers. Friday brought 59. That is the most since 6 November, and it is small beside October. Wednesday was the wide day of the week, with 187 hard risers. Thursday held it. Friday gave back part of Thursday and none of Wednesday on the middle and long clocks.

The damage on Friday was shallow and wide. About one stock in eight traded four percent below its prior close at some point, twice Thursday's share. About a third of those closed down there, so most recovered by the close. About one stock in seven traded four percent above its prior close, and fewer than four in ten of those kept the gain. Four stocks in ten stayed inside a narrow three percent range, the same as Thursday. Turnover came to about 450 crore, down from about 490 on Thursday and about 570 on Wednesday. A year ago the same session saw about the same turnover. The sellers came on quiet volume, not heavy volume.

The top of the market grew while the middle slipped. 68 stocks made a new yearly high, up from 62 and the most since 23 September. New yearly lows doubled from seven to 14, which is still very few. The stocks within fifteen percent of a yearly low again outnumber the stocks within fifteen percent of a yearly high, one in five against a little under one in five. On Thursday the two were level. Fewer than one stock in a hundred is down ten percent over five days, the lowest share in a month.

Fear fell on a down day. The India VIX measures what traders pay for protection against big Nifty swings. It fell almost seven percent on Friday and closed at 15.5. It is down almost sixteen percent on the week. It sits about eight percent above its thirty week line, against fifteen percent on Thursday. A fear gauge that falls while prices fall says traders did not rush to buy protection. Gold funds are down about eight percent over the month. The Nifty is up about eleven percent over the same month.

## Thirty points

### For the trader

**1. Friday was a mild down day, and the first of the week.** Only two of 35 indices rose. A little over a third of stocks rose. Yet 75 stocks rose hard against 59 that fell hard. A down day where the hard risers still outnumber the hard fallers is a pause, not a break.

**2. The volume fell with the price.** Turnover eased to about 450 crore, from about 490 on Thursday and about 570 on Wednesday. Fewer than five stocks traded on heavy volume for every ten on light volume. Selling on light volume is the ordinary kind. Selling on heavy volume would be the kind to respect.

**3. The thrust test is still open.** The earlier briefs asked for 200 or more hard risers on turnover above about 610 crore. Wednesday came closest with 187 on about 570. Friday brought 75 on about 450. The bounce has run three weeks without a thrust day to confirm it.

**4. The swing vote gave back Thursday and kept Wednesday.** 1,262 stocks hold the 50 day line, down 73 from Thursday. That is level with Wednesday. Half the market is about 1,175, so the cushion is about 85 stocks. One more day like Friday would bring the count to the edge.

**5. The short count came off its stretch.** 1,597 stocks stand above the 20 day line, down 118. Thursday's brief called the count stretched and said a dip would be ordinary. The dip came the next day. A count under about 1,280, the 6 November level, would say something more than a dip.

**6. Most intraday falls recovered.** About one stock in eight traded four percent down at some point. About a third of those closed there. The rest came back. Buyers used weakness on Friday.

**7. Gains still fade by the close.** Fewer than four in ten stocks that traded four percent up kept that gain. That share has stayed in a narrow band all week. Sellers still use strength to leave. Half or more holding would mark a real change in who controls the close.

**8. Small companies fell less than large ones.** The Nifty fell about six tenths of one percent. The small company 250 index fell about three tenths. The mid select index rose a little. On a down day the smaller end held up better for a second session.

**9. Realty showed the index trap in two days.** The realty index rose about four and a half percent on Thursday and fell about one and a half on Friday. About one realty stock in four holds the forty week line. Thursday's brief warned that an index ahead of its members can trap a late buyer. The members have to catch up before the index move can be trusted.

**10. Government banks fell less than private banks.** The PSU bank index fell about half a percent and the private bank index about three quarters. The PSU index is still up almost eight percent on the week. A leader that gives back so little on a down day keeps its lead.

**11. The auto index fell a second day while parts makers rose.** The index is down about two percent over two sessions. Automotive Axles, Fiem and Sharda Motor rose eight to ten percent on Friday. Autos hold the second largest share of the momentum screens. The split between makers of cars and makers of parts ran a second session.

**12. Castings and forgings ran on a down day.** Alicon Castech traded about 80 times its normal volume. MM Forgings sits on eight screens. Kennametal rose about fourteen percent. A small group that rises together against the tape is worth a place on the watch list.

**13. Metals and commodities joined IT on the losing side of the week.** Both indices fell about one percent on Friday and are now down on the week. IT is down almost three percent. The gap between government banks and IT is more than ten percent on the week. It is the widest pair on the sheet for a second night.

**14. The short side stays penalised.** Only two stocks are down twenty percent over five days, against 15 up that much. New yearly lows came to 14. Fewer than one stock in a hundred is down ten percent over five days. A day with more than 300 hard fallers would change this.

**15. Fresh momentum is leaving the daily clock for the weekly one.** Daily crossings fell to 91 from 125. Weekly crossings rose to 97 from 85, on a down day. Weekly signals in large companies last longer than daily ones in small companies. The fast money is thinning and the slow list is growing.

## What would change this read

The read is an early uptrend that paused on Friday, led by banks, healthcare and the makers of parts and forgings. It turns worse if a day of 300 or more hard fallers returns and goes unanswered. A 50 day count back under half the market, about 1,175, would undo the week's main gain, and Friday left a cushion of only about 85 stocks. A short count back under about 1,280, the 6 November level, would say the bounce is losing its grip. A long count back under about 1,050 would erase the week's progress. A long count under about 606, the 23 October low, would erase the whole bounce. New yearly lows above about 100 would say sellers have returned. A VIX close back above about 18 would say fear has taken charge again. A sharp fall in banks on heavy volume would remove the group now carrying the market.

The cautious read fails if the long clock clears half and the fear gauge keeps falling. The first test is the long count above about 1,175, half the market. Friday closed at 1,132. The second test is a wide up day, with 200 or more hard risers, on turnover above about 610 crore. Friday brought 75 on about 450. The third test is a VIX close under 15. Friday closed at 15.5. The fourth test is gains held into the close. About half of the stocks that trade four percent up should keep it. Friday held fewer than four in ten. Two of these in the same week, with the 50 day count still above half, would confirm the uptrend on every clock.
