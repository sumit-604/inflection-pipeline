## Business Understanding Narrative

Forbes Precision Tools makes the things that cut metal. Taps, dies, drills and related
precision cutting tools, sold under the Totem brand, made at a single plant at MIDC Waluj
in Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar (search extracts, Financial Results FY2026 Q4 p.9). It is one
business and the company says so in every filing: "The operating segment of the Company is
identified to be, 'Precision cutting tools and related components'" (search extracts, and
repeated verbatim in four filings). There is no second segment, no subsidiary and no joint
venture (search extracts, AGM p.22).

It exists as a listed company because of a demerger. The precision tool business of Forbes
& Company Limited was "transferred to and vested in the company from the appointed date of
April 1, 2023" (search extracts, AGM p.49). Shapoorji Pallonji and Company Private Limited
is the promoter and held 72.56% with no encumbrance at the date of the SAST disclosure held
in the corpus (search extracts, Reg. 29(2) disclosure p.2). The company held its third AGM
in 2025.

Money comes from selling consumable tooling to engineering workshops and manufacturers.
That is a repeat-purchase business with a brand that has been in the Indian market for
decades. In FY2026 it earned Rs 251.0 crore of revenue and Rs 39.5 crore of profit before
tax, with a derived EBITDA of about Rs 52.9 crore or 21.1% of revenue (addendum, p.5).
Those are good margins for a component maker.

What is changing: the balance sheet, not the profit. Revenue grew 7.9% in FY2026 while
profit after tax was flat at Rs 28.8 crore against Rs 28.7 crore, and earnings per share
moved from Rs 5.57 to Rs 5.58 (addendum, p.5). Meanwhile inventories rose 77%, from
Rs 31.9 crore to Rs 56.4 crore, which took cash from operations down from Rs 51.3 crore to
Rs 27.6 crore (addendum, p.6, p.7). The company spent Rs 18.4 crore on plant in FY2026
after Rs 28.3 crore in FY2025, and paid a Rs 25.3 crore dividend funded partly by selling
Rs 45.2 crore of mutual funds (addendum, p.7).

What must be true for a thesis to exist here: the inventory build must be pre-positioned
demand rather than unsold stock, and the Rs 46.7 crore of capex across two years must
produce revenue growth above 8%.

What breaks it: it may already have. Two years of heavy investment have produced flat
profit, and the dividend now exceeds free cash flow.

## 2. Business model and archetype
Archetype: build-to-spec component maker, at the consumable end. Revenue is units of
tooling times price. Taps and dies wear out and get replaced, so the demand is repeat and
tracks industrial activity rather than a capex cycle.
The cost structure, from FY2026: materials net of inventory movement were Rs 8,080 lakh or
32.2% of revenue; employee costs Rs 5,044 lakh or 20.1%; other expenses Rs 6,683 lakh or
26.6%; depreciation Rs 1,551 lakh; finance costs Rs 166 lakh (addendum p.5). So gross
margin is roughly 68%, and the business is labour and overhead heavy rather than material
heavy. That is the signature of a precision manufacturer with skilled
operators, and it explains why attrition matters here.
The one economic engine is brand plus tool room capability. Totem is an established Indian
cutting tool name, and the barrier is metallurgy, grinding accuracy and a distribution
reach into workshops. None of that is quantified in the corpus.

## 3. Competitive advantages
Probable but undocumented, which is the honest position.
A 21% EBITDA margin and a 68% gross margin on a consumable tool say the company is not
price-taking (derived from the FY2026 profit and loss). A commodity toolmaker competing
with Chinese imports would not hold those. So there is pricing power, and the most likely
source is the Totem brand and a workshop distribution network built over decades.
The Shapoorji Pallonji parentage gives balance sheet credibility and, in principle,
customer access.
Against that, everything a shallow read would want is absent. No market share, no export
share, no customer list, no capacity, no comparison with Addison, Miranda or the imported
competition. And there is one documented operational weakness: attrition at 17.24%, "higher
than normal, some of it at critical positions, due to which, there were some delays in some
the planned operations of the company" (search extracts, AGM p.22). In a business whose
advantage is skilled grinding, losing critical people is the advantage leaking.
Moat: inferred from margin, not evidenced in the corpus.

## 4. The promoters
Shapoorji Pallonji and Company Private Limited, holding 3,74,37,172 shares, 72.56% of
voting capital, with nil encumbrance at the date of the disclosure held (search extracts,
Reg. 29(2) disclosure p.2).
That disclosure is itself the flag. A Regulation 29(2) filing is made when a promoter's
holding changes materially, and Bull AI's availability map lists five such disclosures plus
one Reg. 29(1) filing across FY2025 and FY2026. The rows giving the direction and size of
the transaction were cut off in the retrieved snippet. So the corpus establishes that the
promoter has been transacting in the company's shares repeatedly, and does not establish
whether it was buying or selling. That must be settled before anything else on this name.
Board and governance signals from the corpus are clean: an unmodified audit opinion for
FY2026 from Sharp & Tannan Associates, no qualification in the FY2025 auditors' or
secretarial auditors' reports, cost auditors reappointed, a labour settlement running to
the end of 2028, and no subsidiaries or joint ventures to obscure the picture (search
extracts, Outcome of Board Meeting p.9; AGM p.3, p.22).
On the numbers, now confirmed rather than derived: paid-up capital of Rs 5,159 lakh at a
Rs 10 face value is 5.159 crore shares, and the FY2026 cash flow records a dividend paid of
Rs 2,531 lakh (addendum p.5, p.7). That is Rs 25.3 crore against FY2026 profit after tax of
Rs 28.8 crore, an 88% payout, and against derived free cash flow of about Rs 9.2 crore. The
shortfall was funded by liquidating mutual fund investments. A promoter holding 72.56% took
about Rs 18.4 crore of that dividend. It is not improper, and it is worth naming: a company
paying out 88% of earnings while inventories rise 77% is choosing distribution over working
capital.

## 5. Financial trajectory
FY2026 audited, year ended 31 March 2026 (Rs lakh, addendum p.5): revenue from operations
25,101 against 23,266, up 7.9%. Profit before tax 3,950 against 4,088, down 3.4%. Profit
after tax 2,877 against 2,875, up 0.1%. Earnings per share Rs 5.58 against Rs 5.57.
Derived EBITDA: Rs 5,294 lakh, 21.1% of revenue, against Rs 5,113 lakh and 22.0% in FY2025.
Cost lines tell where the profit went. Materials net of inventory movement were 32.2% of
revenue against 34.3%, so input costs improved. Employee costs rose 14.6% to 20.1% of
revenue from 18.9%. Other expenses rose 15.9% to 26.6% from 24.8%. Depreciation rose
Rs 201 lakh and finance costs Rs 53 lakh. Overheads and people, not materials, absorbed the
revenue growth.
Quarterly through FY2026: revenue 5,241, 6,327, 6,437 and 7,096 lakh, so the year built
steadily. Profit before tax was 508, then a derived 1,104, then 804, then 1,533. Q4FY26
profit before tax of 1,533 against 1,438 a year earlier is up 6.6% on revenue up 7.6%, so
the year ended in line rather than ahead.
Balance sheet at 31 March 2026 (addendum p.6): total assets Rs 25,133 lakh, total equity
Rs 16,858 lakh. Total borrowings Rs 1,487 lakh against cash, bank and current investments of
Rs 2,951 lakh, so the company is in a small net cash position of about Rs 1,464 lakh.
Property, plant and equipment Rs 10,064 lakh, barely moved from Rs 9,946 lakh, with capital
work in progress falling from Rs 820 lakh to Rs 356 lakh.
The standout line is inventories: Rs 5,642 lakh against Rs 3,193 lakh, up 76.7%, or
Rs 24.5 crore of cash tied up in a year on Rs 251 crore of revenue. Trade receivables
actually fell, from Rs 3,304 lakh to Rs 3,020 lakh, so this is not a receivables problem.
Cash flow (addendum p.7) makes the consequence plain. Operating profit before working
capital was Rs 5,482 lakh against Rs 5,302 lakh, essentially unchanged. But working capital
absorbed Rs 1,562 lakh where it released Rs 1,040 lakh the year before, and after tax of
Rs 1,165 lakh, net cash from operating activities fell from Rs 5,133 lakh to Rs 2,755 lakh.
Capital expenditure was Rs 1,839 lakh in FY2026 after Rs 2,826 lakh in FY2025, Rs 46.7
crore across two years on a plant carrying Rs 100 crore of gross block.
Derived free cash flow for FY2026 is therefore about Rs 916 lakh, operating cash of 2,755
less capex of 1,839. The dividend paid was Rs 2,531 lakh. The gap was met by selling
Rs 4,523 lakh of mutual funds against Rs 2,450 lakh bought, a net Rs 2,073 lakh released
from the investment portfolio.
Derived returns: return on equity is 2,877 on average equity of Rs 16,741 lakh, about 17.2%.
Return on capital employed, taking profit before tax plus finance costs over average equity
plus borrowings, is about 4,116 on Rs 18,483 lakh, roughly 22.3% pre-tax. Those are
respectable and they are falling, because profit is flat while equity accretes.
Inflection classification: **none, and a mild deterioration.** Revenue grew 7.9%, profit was
flat, margin fell 90 basis points, operating cash halved and the dividend exceeded free cash
flow.

## 8. Proof check
The FY2026 accounts settle the question this card was previously unable to answer, and the
answer is negative.
What fired: revenue grew 7.9% to Rs 251.0 crore, the year built quarter by quarter from
Rs 52.4 crore to Rs 71.0 crore, materials fell as a share of revenue, the audit opinion was
unmodified, the balance sheet moved to a small net cash position, and receivables fell while
revenue rose. The labour settlement to 2028 removes a real risk. None of that is a business
in trouble.
What has not fired: earnings. Profit after tax was Rs 28.77 crore against Rs 28.75 crore,
and earnings per share Rs 5.58 against Rs 5.57. A year of 7.9% revenue growth and Rs 18.4
crore of capital expenditure produced two lakh rupees of extra profit. EBITDA margin fell
from 22.0% to 21.1%, employee costs rose 120 basis points as a share of revenue and other
expenses 180 basis points. Operating cash flow halved.
Named proof point, and it is now a specific one: whether the Rs 24.5 crore inventory build
converts into FY2027 revenue growth materially above 8%. If it does, the build was
pre-positioning and this card understates the company. If FY2027 revenue grows at the same
mid single digits with inventory still elevated, the build was unsold stock and the cash
flow deterioration is structural.
Second proof point: what the Rs 46.7 crore of two-year capital expenditure was for. Property,
plant and equipment barely moved, from Rs 99.5 crore to Rs 100.6 crore, and capital work in
progress fell. So the spending was largely replacement of a depreciating asset base, not
expansion. That is consistent with a business holding its position rather than growing it.

## 11. Posture: Transition Decision Matrix
The matrix requires three state variables from a signed mental model. On this corpus, the
first cannot be set at all.
- Proof gate: **NOT APPLICABLE**, because no transition has been claimed. There is no gate
  to fire. This is a different condition from a gate that has failed to fire, and the card
  records it as such.
- Ugliness: with the FY2026 accounts held, there is now something to classify. Flat profit
  on 7.9% revenue growth, a 77% inventory build, halved operating cash flow and a dividend
  exceeding free cash flow read as a **STRUCTURAL-FEATURE** rather than an artifact of a
  climb, because there is no climb being attempted. Nothing in the corpus proposes that
  these numbers are the temporary cost of anything.
- Recognition gap: INDETERMINATE. No price is held. A BSE-only, uncovered, recently
  demerged name could plausibly carry an open gap purely from neglect, which remains the
  one genuinely interesting possibility on this card.
With no claimed transition and a structural reading of the deterioration, the matrix's
nearest cell is VALUE-TRAP RISK, which classifies as DEEP WATCH or AVOID unless the
classification is disproven. State the disproof condition precisely: it is disproven if
FY2027 revenue grows well above 8% as the inventory unwinds, or if the promoter's SAST
filings turn out to be purchases. Both are checkable within a year.

## 12. Verdict card
**PASS for this mandate.** With the FY2026 audited accounts now in hand, the earlier
uncertainty on this card is resolved, and it resolves against the name. Forbes Precision
Tools is a sound niche manufacturer with a real brand, a 68% gross margin, a 21% EBITDA
margin, a clean unmodified audit, a small net cash balance sheet and a labour settlement
running to 2028. It is not in difficulty.
It is also not going anywhere on the evidence. FY2026 revenue grew 7.9% and profit after
tax grew by two lakh rupees. Inventories rose 77%, operating cash flow halved, and the
dividend of Rs 25.3 crore exceeded free cash flow of about Rs 9 crore, funded by selling
investments. Rs 46.7 crore of capital expenditure over two years left property, plant and
equipment Rs 1.2 crore higher, so it was replacement rather than expansion. The company
publishes no capacity, no order position, no export share, no capex plan and no outlook,
holds no earnings call, and uses its management discussion to describe the Indian economy.
Under a mandate that hunts a documented rung climb the market has not repriced, there is no
climb here to assess and no evidence one is being attempted.
Load-bearing facts to settle before this is dismissed permanently:
1. The direction and size of the Shapoorji Pallonji SAST transactions. Five Reg. 29(2)
   filings in two years with the direction unknown is now the single most important open
   item. A promoter buying and a promoter selling are opposite signals, and on a 72.56%
   holding it is the only signal the company gives.
2. What the Rs 24.5 crore inventory build is. Pre-positioned demand or unsold stock decides
   whether FY2026 was an investment year or a bad one.
3. What the Rs 46.7 crore of two-year capital expenditure bought, given property, plant and
   equipment barely moved.
4. Capacity, utilisation, export share and customer concentration, from the FY2025 or
   FY2026 annual report body.
5. The credit rating rationale, for capacity and working capital.
6. The price. A BSE-only, uncovered, recently demerged name is where a neglect discount
   actually lives, and this card cannot see it.
What would change the view: FY2027 revenue growth in the mid teens as the inventory
unwinds, or disclosure of a real expansion behind the capex, or evidence the promoter has
been buying, would each move this to WATCH, and two together to PROCEED. Absent those it
belongs in a neglect or value screen rather than the transition pipeline. No price here.
