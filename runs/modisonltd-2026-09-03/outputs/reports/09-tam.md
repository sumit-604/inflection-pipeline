## SECTION 1: MARKET DEFINITION

### 1A. Precise Boundaries

**Product scope (IN):** Electrical contacts and contact materials — silver, silver-alloy, copper, copper-brass, and copper-tungsten contacts, brazing assemblies, HV "crowns," and vacuum-interrupter components (brazing washers, finger contacts) — used as a COMPONENT inside low-voltage (LV), medium-voltage (MV) and high-voltage (HV) switchgear, circuit breakers, contactors and relays for power transmission and distribution (T&D), industrial and select infrastructure applications.

**Product scope (OUT):** (a) Finished switchgear/circuit-breaker assemblies themselves (Modison sells the contact component into an OEM's assembly, not the assembly) — that is the much larger adjacent "switchgear market," used here only as a bottom-up cross-check, never as the TAM itself. (b) Automotive-grade and consumer-electronics-grade electrical contacts (micro-switches, connectors, relays for appliances/EVs' low-power circuits) — different customer base, different price point, no evidence Modison serves this segment. (c) Bullion/silver-refining-as-a-product revenue — Modison's refinery is a backward-integrated INPUT to its own contact manufacturing, not a separately sold product line per the B04 revenue-stream breakdown (two streams only: domestic and export contacts/materials).

**Geographic scope:** Primary — India (domestic T&D, industrial, infrastructure demand). Secondary — export markets (20+ countries, ~12% of FY26 revenue, concentrated with GE/Siemens/ABB group entities in Europe/Japan and other geographies). TAM figures below are built India-first; the global figure is shown for context and to size the export opportunity separately (Section 5D), not blended into the primary funnel, to avoid double-counting.

**Customer scope:** OEM switchgear/circuit-breaker/contactor/vacuum-interrupter manufacturers (GE, Siemens, ABB, L&T, BHEL and similar) who buy contacts as an input; end-use sectors named in the AR: power/electrical T&D, infrastructure, pharma, glass, defence, railways, bullion (ancillary).

**Channel scope:** Direct B2B supply to OEMs under long-standing design-in/type-approval relationships (a "build-to-spec" moat per B04). Excludes distributor/retail/spares-replacement channel, which the AR and AGM transcript do not describe Modison as serving.

**Price segment:** Precision-metallurgy, engineered contact materials for utility/industrial-grade switchgear (mid-to-high specification), not commodity low-cost contacts for mass-market appliances.

**Explicit exclusions restated:** automotive contacts, consumer-electronics contacts, finished switchgear/breaker revenue, silver bullion trading, Modison Copper Ltd's non-Modison-Ltd external sales (separate unlisted entity, ~75% of its own book sold to third parties incl. railways — a related but distinct revenue pool, not folded into Modison Ltd's TAM here).

### 5B. Runway Assessment

- **Revenue headroom = SAM ÷ current revenue = ₹2,920 Cr ÷ ₹710.33 Cr = 4.11x**
- **TAM growth rate ≈ 5.0%/yr** (blended across sourced CAGRs, Section 3B)
- **Company CAGR vs TAM:** FY26 revenue grew 44.89% YoY (B04) — vastly outgrowing the ~5% TAM growth rate, i.e. Modison is currently GAINING SHARE, not merely riding the market. Caution: a meaningful share of that 47% headline growth is silver-price-driven pass-through, not volume (B04 flags this explicitly; volume-only growth is smaller, though not separately quantified — NOT FOUND).
- **Years to saturate SAM:** at the SOM-implied sustainable CAGR of ~9.2-9.5% (a more realistic forward run-rate than FY26's commodity-inflated 47%), and with SAM itself growing ~5%/yr, the effective closing-gap rate is only ~4-4.5pp/yr against a 4.11x headroom — full SAM saturation would take on the order of **two-plus decades** at that pace. This is a GOOD, not MASSIVE, runway: real headroom exists, but the pace at which Modison can plausibly close it (per the share-gain rules) is slow relative to the size of the gap.

### 5C. Runway Classification

No numeric threshold matrix was supplied in the source instructions beyond the five-label set; thresholds are stated here for transparency:
- MASSIVE: headroom >20x AND TAM growth >15%
- STRONG: headroom 10-20x, or TAM growth 10-15% with headroom >5x
- GOOD: headroom 3-10x AND TAM growth 5-10%
- MODERATE: headroom 1.5-3x and/or TAM growth 2-5%
- LIMITED: headroom <1.5x or TAM growth <2%

With headroom 4.11x and TAM growth ~5.0%, Modison lands in **GOOD**.
