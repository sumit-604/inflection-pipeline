# Fortress moat cannot outrun 121x price

Azad Engineering forges and machines precision components for turbines: airfoils, blades and 3D rotating parts that sit in the hot, high stress core of energy, aerospace, defence and oilfield equipment. Rakesh Chopdar founded it in Hyderabad in 1983. The customers are Tier 1 OEMs: GE Vernova, Mitsubishi Heavy Industries, Siemens Energy, Arabelle and EDF, BHEL, Baker Hughes, Rolls Royce and Pratt & Whitney. FY26 revenue was Rs 590 Cr, PAT Rs 132 Cr, EBITDA margin 37%, and the shares trade at Rs 2,480, about 121 times earnings. Energy and oil and gas are 81% of sales and the top five customers 51%.

The transition is a capacity build. The company is turning a roughly Rs 700 Cr FY25 QIP, on top of its December 2023 IPO, into eight dedicated single customer lean factories, four commissioned by Q4 FY26 and four more guided to about November 2026, to serialise a named order book of about Rs 6,500 Cr, roughly eleven times revenue, on contracts running four to eight years. Revenue has compounded 30% a year since FY20 and margins sit at a peak. The bet is that serial production converts this backlog into cash and lifts returns.

The moat numbers are strong; the cash and return numbers are not. Gate 0 scores a backward AVOID, core 38 of 100, dragged by two blocks tied entirely to the post raise capex ramp, on a FORTRESS backward moat with 6 of 12 tests confirmed. FY26 operating cash flow was minus Rs 123 Cr against positive PAT of Rs 132 Cr, cumulative operating cash flow over seven years is 0.04 times cumulative profit, the balance sheet flipped from net cash to net debt of Rs 280 Cr, and the working capital cycle stretched to 344 days from 287. CARE rates the company CARE A Stable, calls the elongation a growth effect that should normalise once serial production begins, and adds that collection days are likely to stay stretched.

Management delivers on what it guides and goes quiet on what it cannot. Revenue guidance of 25% to 30% was met at 30%, margin guidance of 33% to 35% was beaten at 37%, and the Pratt & Whitney and Mitsubishi hot section contracts were signed. Against that, the working capital target was missed and quietly loosened from 140 to 150 days out to 160 to 170 days and pushed to FY27, the exact operating cash flow figure was deflected every quarter, and an indigenous defence engine was promised a couple of months away across three straight calls. The credibility grade is B.

Peers corroborate the setup, not the exclusivity. MTAR, PTC Industries and Unimech all run elongated working capital during comparable ramps and all build capacity ahead of utilisation, so Azad's cash cycle and capex intensity are sector normal rather than an outlier. MTAR itself swung from negative operating cash flow to plus Rs 197 Cr and cut working capital days from 260 to 172 once its ramp matured, which is the pattern Azad needs to repeat. No peer could verify the claim that Azad is the only qualified Indian supplier of gas and nuclear turbine airfoils, and none confirmed a share gain against Howmet or Precision Castparts.

The runway is wide and not the constraint. The serviceable market is about Rs 15,230 Cr against Azad's roughly 3.9% share, giving headroom of about 26 times and a MASSIVE runway class. The revenue CAGR implied by the three year opportunity is 31%, close to what the company already delivers, so demand is not what caps the thesis. The five year opportunity of about Rs 2,232 Cr does need capex beyond what is committed today.

Price is what caps it. The four pillar destination PE lands near 21 times on both derivations, a required return track at 20.6 times and an additive track at 21.9 times, built on operational ROCE of 13.6%, a growth induced cash multiplier of 0.90, an emerging moat score of 26 and a plus 3 franchise premium. Base year three fair value is about Rs 720, which from Rs 2,480 is a negative 34% CAGR. The hurdle ratio is 0.32 at base and still fails on the bull case, so the verdict is STOP and the decision is AVOID on valuation. A 25% return needs entry between Rs 295 and Rs 368, about 85% below the current price and below the December 2023 IPO level.

The devil's advocate leaves the business alive and the price indefensible. The bear case is WEAKENED BUT ALIVE, with growth triggers, moat and management trust each only dented, but valuation safety rated DESTROYED. The sharpest counter is that CARE's own words, that receivables are likely to stay stretched, argue the cash drag is part structural rather than purely growth, which would pull the cash multiplier toward 0.65 and the destination PE toward 16 to 18 times. A positive operating cash flow number sourced from factoring rather than operations would read as a turn without being one. The call rests on one print: Q1 FY27 consolidated operating cash flow in August 2026, negative with working capital at or above 344 days breaks the thesis.
