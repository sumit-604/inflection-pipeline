### 1C. Trigger evolution across the three quarters

- **Capacity stabilization narrative** — UNCHANGED across all three calls. FY26 = stabilize, FY27 = stable ops, FY28 = max utilization was stated identically in Q2, Q3 and Q4 FY26 calls. This consistency is itself notable (positive for credibility) but also means the "step-up" the market wants has not yet been offered any earlier than originally guided.
- **Revenue growth guidance — narrowed, not raised.** Q2 FY26 call: "25% to 30%." Q3 FY26 call: "25% plus" (upper bound quietly dropped even as H1 was tracking above 30%). Q4 FY26 call: "approximately 25%+" reaffirmed even after FY26 closed at ~30.2% actual and even as CEO explicitly conceded to Amit Dixit (Goldman Sachs) that "25% plus... appears... conservative." This is a genuine, repeated, self-acknowledged conservative-guidance pattern — WEAKENING in specificity even as delivery strengthens.
- **EBITDA margin guidance similarly narrowed downward in stated language** (36% "sustain" in Q2 FY26 call → "33%–35%" explicit long-term band in Q3 FY26 call) even as actual quarterly prints ran at 36–38.6%. Analysts (Mulesh Savla, Q3; Amit Dixit, Q4) explicitly challenged this as too conservative; management acknowledged the pattern but did not raise the guided band.
- **GTRE/ATGG defence engine — timeline STRENGTHENING IN LANGUAGE BUT SLIPPING IN FACT.** "Couple of months" (Q2 FY26 call, Nov 2025) → "couple of months," ~70–75% progress (Q3 FY26 call, Feb 2026) → "years got to months, now it has come to weeks" (Q4 FY26 call, May 2026). Over 6+ months, the promised delivery window has continuously reset without ever landing; the qualitative language ("weeks") sounds like progress but no firm date has ever been given.
- **Working-capital normalization — WEAKENING, target quietly loosened.** Promised for "H2" in the Q2 FY26 call. Given specific day-count targets in the Q3 FY26 call (H2 FY26: 140–150 days). By the Q4 FY26 call — which covers the very H2 that was supposed to show the improvement — the target has both slipped (still described as "in execution," not achieved) and been loosened (restated language implies 160–170 days, not 140–150), and normalization is now listed as an FY27 priority, a full year beyond the original H2 FY26 promise.
- **Order book — STATIC despite new contract wins, unexplained.** INR6,500cr+ stated identically in the Q3 FY26 call and the Q4 FY26 call, despite the intervening period including the new MHI single-source 8-year hot-section Nozzle Vane contract and continued Safran/Pratt & Whitney progression. Management's explanation (net of INR600cr delivered in FY26, and some contracts' values undisclosed) is plausible but was not proactively reconciled — a topic that deserved a clearer bridge and did not get one.
- **NEW triggers appearing only in Q4 FY26 call**: nuclear turbine airfoils via EDF/Arabelle Solutions (described as an existing multi-year relationship, disclosed for the first time only when an analyst asked about the "nuclear theme"); explicit Energy-segment mix target of 55–60% in 5 years; the missile/drone/UAV framing of the GTRE engine program (first raised by an analyst in the Q3 FY26 call, expanded on by management). None of these were flagged in the Q2 FY26 call — worth asking why the nuclear relationship, in particular, was never volunteered earlier.
- **Dropped without follow-up**: the "forging plant" (mentioned as partially operational in the Q2 FY26 call) is never mentioned again in Q3 or Q4 FY26 calls. The "center of excellence" investment (Q2 FY26 call) likewise disappears from Q3/Q4 commentary. The AMCA / indigenous domestic-engine-ecosystem discussion (detailed in the Q2 FY26 call) is not substantively revisited in Q3 or Q4, replaced by broader, vaguer "engine ecosystem" language. The "single crystal blades" capability gap flagged by an analyst in Q2 FY26 call ("not yet") is never revisited.
- **Saudi Arabia / Baker Hughes MOU** — timeline "shifted" per Q4 FY26 call due to "the current situation" (an analyst's phrase referencing Middle East geopolitical tension, which management did not dispute). This is the one trigger that moved backward, not just slower-than-hoped.

### 2B. Excuse pattern analysis

- Working-capital miss: attributed to inherent "complexity" of simultaneous plant ramp-up ("this is a very complex project management exercise") — an internal, largely honest admission of execution difficulty rather than externally blamed. No explicit "we got this wrong," but also no attempt to blame customers, suppliers or macro conditions.
- GTRE/ATGG engine miss: partially externally attributed to the joint Azad/GTRE (DRDO) development structure — plausible given it is genuinely a co-development program, not pure deflection, but management repeats the same "couple of months" framing three calls running without ever explicitly resetting expectations or acknowledging the prior miss.
- Saudi Arabia delay: openly externally attributed to "the current situation" (geopolitical), a framing management did not contest when an analyst raised it — a legitimate external factor, disclosed rather than hidden.
- Conservative revenue/margin guidance that is then beaten: management explicitly conceded (Mulesh Savla, Q3 FY26 call; Amit Dixit, Q4 FY26 call) that its own guidance is deliberately conservative — this is closer to honest-admission than deflection, and is a mild positive credibility signal (they are not over-promising on the metrics they control most directly).
- Management consistently credits the team ("hats off to my team," "beautiful planning," repeated across all 3 calls) for successes, which is standard promotional framing rather than a deflection pattern per se.
- Pattern check: management does raise some hard topics proactively (working-capital targets, stabilization framing, conservative-guidance acknowledgment) without being pushed, but the actual operating cash flow (OCF) shortfall implied by the injected context (negative FY26 OCF against positive PAT) is never volunteered or explicitly quantified in any of the three calls, even when directly asked about "cash flow generation" (Manish Ostwal, Q4 FY26 call) — the answer redirected to inventory-to-revenue conversion language rather than an OCF figure.

**Classification: balanced.** Genuine external factors are cited for the two clearest misses (DRDO joint timeline, Saudi geopolitics) rather than blanket external-blame; internal execution complexity is openly admitted for the working-capital miss. The weakness is less deflection than persistent over-optimism on timelines and an unwillingness to state OCF numbers directly.

### 2D. What they are NOT saying

- **Operating cash flow.** The injected context flags negative FY26 operating cash flow against positive PAT — a first-order fact for the cash-conversion determination — yet management never states an OCF figure or explicitly reconciles PAT-to-OCF in any of the three calls, even when directly asked about "sustainable cash flow" (Manish Ostwal, Q4 FY26 call). The answer redirected to inventory conversion and WC-day targets rather than confirming or quantifying the OCF gap. This is the single most consequential silence in the three transcripts given the strategy's cash-conversion test.
- **Customer concentration.** With Energy & Oil and Gas (dominated by MHI, GE and Siemens) contributing ~81% of FY26 revenue, management never states a specific top-1/top-3/top-5 customer revenue percentage across any of the three calls, despite repeated wallet-share discussion.
- **Leverage trajectory / ceiling.** Gross borrowings (INR457cr) and net borrowings (INR272cr) are disclosed once (Q4 FY26 call) but with no forward guidance on a target leverage ceiling or debt/EBITDA band as capex continues.
- **Unit economics / payback on the ~INR700cr QIP program.** Asset-turn ranges (1.7x–2x) are given but no per-plant ROIC, payback period, or IRR figure is offered despite this being the single largest capital allocation decision in the company's history.
- **Precise inventory/receivable day counts for the reporting quarter itself.** Analysts asked for this twice (Q3 and Q4 FY26 calls) and were given only forward targets, never a hard current number.
- **Order-book reconciliation.** The static INR6,500cr+ figure across the Q3 and Q4 FY26 calls, despite new contract signings in between, was never proactively bridged by management (e.g., "gross additions of X, less Y delivered, less Z undisclosed value = net INR6,500cr").
