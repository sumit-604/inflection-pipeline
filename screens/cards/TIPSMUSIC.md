## Business Understanding Narrative

**What does it do, and how does it make money?** Tips owns a music catalogue of over 38,000 songs
in 25-plus languages (IP-Q2, p.11; p.4). It licenses that catalogue to YouTube, Spotify, JioSaavn,
Meta, Apple and Amazon, and to broadcasters, brands and venues. Digital platforms paid 73% of
revenue in H1 FY27 (IP-Q2, p.4). Old songs carry the business: 85% of revenue comes from content
older than three years (CC-Jul26, p.12). The 1990s Bollywood soundtracks (Khalnayak, Pardes, Taal)
are the core asset (PR-Q2, p.3). It expenses 100% of content cost in the quarter of release (IP-Q2, p.4).

**What is changing?** The price per stream, management says. Indian labels earn 4 to 10 paise per
stream; global labels earn 50 to 90 paise (AR26, p.5). The gap is subscription. Paid subscription
is 10% to 15% of Tips' digital revenue and grows 30% to 50% a year (CC-Apr26, pp.7-8; CC-Jul26, p.4).
Spotify targets 15 crore paying users in India, from about 1.4 crore (AR26, p.5).

**Why now?** Revenue growth accelerated. FY26 revenue rose 20.87% to Rs 375.51 crore and profit
after tax rose 30.13% to Rs 216.75 crore (AR26, p.35). Q2 FY27 revenue rose 35% to Rs 120.06 crore
(R-2026-09, p.2). Spotify and YouTube raised subscription prices late in 2025 (CC-Jul26, p.6).

**What must be true?** Platforms must move Indian listeners behind a paywall, and that money must
reach labels pro rata. Short-video platforms must move from fixed fees to revenue share. Tips'
catalogue must keep its share of listening as old songs age.

**What breaks it?** Concentration of buyers. A few platforms set the payout terms. The
short-video deals are lump sums Tips cannot convert to revenue share (CC-Oct25, p.7). One platform
exit (Resso) cost about USD 2 million a year (CC-Oct25, p.6). A second break is the price: the
market already pays 41.8 times trailing earnings (MKT).

## 2. Business model and archetype

One segment, no subsidiary, 94 employees (R-2026-09, p.3, notes 6-7; IP-Q2, p.4).

The engine is a fixed-cost catalogue earning variable licence fees. Content cost was Rs 59.24
crore in FY26, 15.8% of revenue, against Rs 71.00 crore and 22.9% in FY25 (R-2026-03, p.2; sum of
acquisition, royalty and promotion lines). Employee cost was Rs 20.33 crore. Little else sits
between revenue and profit. FY26 operating EBITDA was Rs 275.8 crore, a 73.4% margin, computed from
R-2026-03, p.2.

Content sources split in thirds: outside producers, Tips Films (a promoter company), and in-house
non-film music (CC-Jul26, p.12).

**Archetype: licence/scarcity business.** Precisely: the "licence" is copyright, held for 60
years from production (`credit-ratings/02-CARE.pdf`, p.1). Renewal risk sits on the platform
contracts, not the copyright: Warner is locked to 2028 and YouTube Shorts, due June 2026, is still
in negotiation (CC-Oct25, p.10; CC-Jul26, p.8). There is no regulated price. The price is a share of each platform's pool. Spotify pays labels 55% of its revenue, split
by consumption (CC-Oct25, p.11).

## 3. Competitive advantages

**Moat: present in the asset, absent in the price.** The catalogue is scarce. Nobody can re-record
the 1990s. Viral reuse keeps old songs earning: "Deewana Mujhe Kar Gaya" did 3 billion Instagram
views in Q4 FY26 (CC-Apr26, p.3). Platforms need every major catalogue to compete, so Tips is a
must-carry supplier.

Pricing power is weak. Tips does not set its per-stream rate. Management called yields under
"overall pressure" after platform shutdowns (CC-Oct25, p.5). Short-video deals pay fixed fees
because the platforms set the model (CC-Oct25, p.7). Digital licensing was 58% of Indian music
revenue in 2025 (AR26, p.35), and that pool has few buyers.

## 4. The promoters

The Taurani family holds 64.32% at September 2026 (MKT). Kumar Taurani holds 15.80% and Ramesh
Taurani 15.76% (AR26, p.25). Each promoter declared no encumbrance in FY26 (`filings/team_sandeshc_11052026110731_Disclsoure.pdf`, pp.1-6 read, 11 pages).
Ramesh Taurani draws nil remuneration (AR26, p.33). Kumar Taurani drew Rs 1.93 crore (AR26, p.157).
The buyback excluded the promoters (AR26, p.22).

Skin in the game is high. The company pays out the prior year's profit each year (CC-Jul26, p.13). FY26 dividends were
Rs 166.18 crore (AR26, p.36). H1 FY27 payout was Rs 127.1 crore, including the Rs 44.44 crore
buyback (IP-Q2, p.6; PR-Q2, p.2).

The related-party channel is step 9's first flag. The CEO, Hari Nair, left on 30-Apr-2026 and had
not been replaced by 22-Jul-2026 (AR26, p.37; CC-Jul26, p.10).

Revenue grew at a 29.0% CAGR over FY22-FY26 (IP-Q2, p.7). H1 FY27 revenue rose 28%. H1 operating
EBITDA margin fell from 70.1% to 61.0% because content cost nearly doubled (IP-Q2, p.17). H1 PAT

Return on equity was 92% in FY26 (IP-Q2, p.16). Read it with care. The catalogue is not on the
balance sheet because content is expensed, and full payout keeps equity at Rs 324.2 crore
(IP-Q2, p.18). The ratio measures the accounting policy as much as the business.

Debt is nil; cash and investments were Rs 311 crore (IP-Q2, p.4). Tax was 25.3% in Q2 FY27
(R-2026-09, p.2).

Cash conversion is weakening at the margin. FY26 operating cash flow was Rs 197.0 crore against
PAT of Rs 216.7 crore. H1 FY27 was Rs 82.6 crore against Rs 109.4 crore (IP-Q2, p.19). Receivables
rose from Rs 34.3 crore to Rs 48.2 crore in six months, 41%, against 28% revenue growth (IP-Q2,
p.18). Advances from customers, mainly from Warner and other licensees (CC-Oct25, p.13), fell from
Rs 95.89 crore to Rs 72.17 crore in FY26 (AR26, p.142). Other current liabilities, which hold
them, fell to Rs 50.4 crore by September 2026 (IP-Q2, p.18). Cash received in advance is now
being earned out.

**Classification: earnings-led.** Revenue growth comes from consumption of an existing asset.
Margins move quarter to quarter with release timing, not with a structural shift.

## 6. The transition and the quality ladder

**No rung migration. FROM R5 TO R5.**

Tips meets two R5 tests: structural scarcity and ROCE above 30%. It fails the third, pricing
power. Two precisions keep the label honest.

First, R5 here means scarcity, not price-setting. Tips owns an asset nobody can copy. The platform
fixes the rent: a pool share per stream. On pricing power alone it sits nearer R4, a share-of-wallet
supplier.

Second, the 92% ROE overstates the rung. A label that capitalised its catalogue would show a far
larger equity base and a lower return. The headline return proves less than it seems.

The claimed transition is a change in yield inside R5. Ad-funded streams at 4 to 10 paise move
toward subscription streams that pay several times more. Management's claim, quoted once: "It is
likely that India will be amongst the top three music markets in the world by volume as well as
value, over the next few years" (AR26, p.5).

The ladder therefore names no re-rating engine.

## 8. Proof check

Revenue proof is clear. Year-on-year revenue growth was 21% in Q3 FY26, 32% in Q4 FY26, 21% in
Q1 FY27 and 35% in Q2 FY27 (IP-Q2, p.13). The CFO said Q4 held "no one-off" (CC-Apr26, p.4).

Profit proof lags. H1 PAT grew 10.5%. The 20% PAT guide needs H2 PAT of Rs 150.65 crore against
Rs 117.72 crore in H2 FY26 (R-2026-03, p.2), a 28% rise. The content budget leaves Rs 19-29 crore
for H2 against Rs 22.5 crore last year (IP-Q2, p.13), so the H2 margin can hold if revenue does.

**The mechanism proof is absent.** No filed number splits revenue by platform, by subscription
and advertising, or by volume and price. Management declined each split (CC-Oct25, p.12;
CC-Apr26, p.7; CC-Apr26, p.18). Management credits growth to catalogue virality on Instagram
(CC-Apr26, p.11). That is real, and it is not the subscription thesis. Annual YouTube views fell
from 228.3 billion in FY25 to 210.4 billion in FY26 (IP-Q2, p.11).

## 9. Flags from the documents

- **Tips Films related-party content channel.** Tips Films is a listed promoter company. Kumar and
  Ramesh Taurani hold 20.28% and 20.24% of it (AR26, p.21). It lost Rs 15.85 crore on Rs 158.27
  crore turnover in FY26 (AR26, p.21). Tips Music bought Rs 17.26 crore of its music rights in FY26,
  up from Rs 9.68 crore (AR26, p.158). It also held a Rs 15.53 crore advance to Tips Films at 31-Mar-2026
  (AR26, p.138). Related-party purchases rose from 13.63% to 26.42% of total purchases (AR26, p.80).
  Shareholders approved a Rs 60 crore omnibus limit to the 31st AGM (AR26, p.20; AGM results,
  p.15). Each deal carries an external valuer's report (AR26, p.19). On the Q1 FY27 call, the

- **Disclosure mismatch.** Form AOC-2 shows "Amount paid as advances: NIL" for Tips Films (AR26,

- **Platform concentration, undisclosed.** International billing was Rs 257.49 crore, 68.6% of FY26

## 10. Independent check: the credit rating

**The ratings are old and say little about today.** No rating exists after January 2022. CARE
withdrew its CARE BBB+ (Credit Watch) at the company's request on 14-Jan-2022, after a no-dues
certificate (`credit-ratings/01-CARE.pdf`, p.1). The company has no debt, so it needs no rating.

The last live view, CARE on 19-May-2021, named strengths: promoter experience, stable licence fees
and strong liquidity. It named weaknesses: moderate scale, a catalogue concentrated in 1990s and
2000s music, content acquisition risk, format obsolescence and piracy (`credit-ratings/02-CARE.pdf`,
pp.1-2). CARE's upgrade triggers were music revenue above Rs 150 crore and ROCE above 35%
(p.1). Both are long met.

Footnote: CRISIL (April 2019) and ICRA (August 2018) had classed the then film-and-music company
"issuer not cooperating" (`credit-ratings/04-CRISIL.html`). Today's filings win.

## 11. Posture: the Transition Decision Matrix

- **Proof gate: FIRED on growth, NOT FIRED on the yield mechanism.** Four quarters of 21-35%
  revenue growth are filed. No filed number shows subscription yield doing the work.
- **Ugliness: ARTIFACT-OF-CLIMB.** The H1 margin drop comes from expensing film content in the
  release quarter. Q2 recovered to 70.5% (IP-Q2, p.17).
- **Recognition gap: CLOSED.** Market capitalisation Rs 9,498 crore and trailing PE 41.8x (MKT).
  The business already sits at the TO rung. 41.8x sits well above the R5 neighbourhood of about
  24x; only a strategic premium bridges the difference.

**Posture: EARNINGS-ONLY.** The quality is real and priced. Returns ride EPS growth. Guided EPS
growth of 20% sits below the 25% CAGR target before any multiple change.

## 12. Verdict card

**WATCH.**

A good business at a full price, with no rung to climb. The strategy hunts an unpriced climb;
here the climb is done and priced. What remains is a yield thesis the company's disclosure does
not let anyone test.

**Load-bearing facts a `/step1` would verify first**
1. Platform concentration: the share of revenue from YouTube and from Warner, and the Warner
   minimum-guarantee terms and the 2028 renewal.
2. The Tips Films channel: FY27 spend against the Rs 60 crore limit, the valuer reports, and the
   Rs 15.53 crore advance.
3. H2 FY27 PAT growth of 28% or more, and the YouTube Shorts renewal terms.

**What would change the view**
- A filed revenue-share deal for short-form video, or a subscription split that shows yield rising.
  That would fire the mechanism proof.
- A price that reopens the gap against guided growth.
- Toward PASS: related-party purchases above the Rs 60 crore limit, or growth in receivables that
  keeps outrunning revenue.

No Role 1 valuation was run and no target price is set. This verdict decides only whether the
name earns a full `/step1`, and it is not a position.
