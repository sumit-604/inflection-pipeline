## NEW FINDING 1 — 🔴 Managerial remuneration paid in excess of the 11% statutory cap on net profits (Section 197(16))

The Independent Auditor's Report contains a distinct paragraph, separate from and not covered anywhere in the CARO annexure or the notes narrative, under "matter to be included in Auditors' Report under section 197(16) of the Act":

> "the Company has paid Managerial Remuneration in excess of eleven percent of the net profits of the company for current year in pursuance of the special resolution passed by the company in its 33rd and 34th Annual General Meetings and in accordance with the proviso to item (A) in Section II under Part II of Schedule V read with section 197 of Companies Act, 2013."
(Auditors' Report on Financial Statements, "Report on Other Legal and Regulatory Requirements" para 3(i), p.39)

This confirms that the company's combined managerial remuneration (Managing Director + Joint Managing Director) exceeded the default 11%-of-net-profit ceiling under Section 197/Schedule V of the Companies Act, and was paid only because shareholders passed special resolutions at the 33rd AGM (FY2021-22) and 34th AGM (FY2022-23) invoking the Schedule V Part II Section II proviso (which permits above-cap or minimum remuneration when profits are inadequate, subject to shareholder approval). This is a standing arrangement, not a one-off.

For scale (illustrative only — NOT a restatement of the Section 198 computation the statutory cap is based on, which differs from accounting PAT): combined cash remuneration to the two Managing Directors (Purushotham ₹212.43L + Swapnil ₹141.51L = ₹353.94L, Note 39, p.81) is close in magnitude to the company's entire FY25 net profit after tax of ₹356.29L (P&L, p.47). Adding Ms. Smruthi's director remuneration (₹59.35L, up 55.1% YoY) brings total family remuneration to ₹413.29L, above FY25 PAT.

This is a material governance/quality-of-earnings item: a statutory finding, formally reported by the auditor, that promoter-family remuneration structurally exceeds the normal profit-linked ceiling and requires recurring shareholder authorization to remain compliant. It was not captured in Pass 1's RPT section (which flagged the remuneration quantum in revenue terms — 3.5% of revenue — but not this specific statutory breach and its Schedule V waiver mechanism). Directly relevant to a related-party-fairness assessment.

🔴 — feeds accounting-quality / RPT-fairness dimension.

## NEW FINDING 2 — 🟡 Two near-identically named related entities not cross-referenced: an LLP shareholder vs. a proprietorship RPT vendor

Note 15(e), Promoter's Shareholding (p.69), lists **"Smruthi Chemicals and Intermediates Limited LLP"** as a promoter-group shareholder holding 2,46,000 shares (2.15% of equity), unchanged FY24→FY25.

Note 39(a), List of Related Parties (p.81), separately lists **"Smruthi Chemicals & Intermediates (Proprietor Mrs. Eaga Vaishnavi)"** — a sole proprietorship of the MD's wife — as the counterparty for job-work charges paid (₹148.95L), job-work charges received (₹3.47L), and raw material sales (₹29.81L, Note 39(b)/(c), p.81–82).

These are two distinct legal forms (an LLP vs. a proprietorship) carrying nearly identical trade names, and the annual report does not cross-reference or clarify whether they are related, successor entities, or entirely separate operations both connected to the promoter family. Pass 1 covered only the proprietorship RPT vendor and did not identify the separate LLP shareholder entity. This is worth a direct question to management: is the LLP shareholder the same underlying business as the proprietorship vendor (e.g., a conversion in progress), or two unconnected entities that happen to share a name — either way, the lack of cross-reference is a disclosure-clarity gap.

🟡 — disclosure transparency.

## NEW FINDING 3 — 🟡 MD's wife is the second-largest shareholder (15.27%) while also being the RPT vendor's proprietor

Note 15(d), Shareholders holding >5% (p.69): Mrs. Eaga Vaishnavi holds 17,48,120 shares = **15.27%** of the company — the second-largest holding after Mr. Purushotham Eaga himself (41.23%), and larger than the Joint Managing Director Mr. Swapnil Eaga's own holding (5.24%).

Pass 1 discussed the job-work fee flows to "Smruthi Chemicals & Intermediates (MD's wife's proprietorship)" and the receivable growth from that entity but did not connect this to her standing as the company's second-largest individual shareholder in her own name. This sharpens the concentration picture already flagged in Pass 1 (RPT #8): the same family member is simultaneously a top-2 equity holder and the counterparty on a growing RPT job-work/receivable relationship (fees +15.4% YoY to ₹148.95L; receivable +160.8% YoY to ₹29.81L, both anchored in Pass 1).

🟡 — RPT fairness / ownership concentration context.

## NEW FINDING 4 — 🟡 Internal pay ratio: MD's remuneration is 63.55x median employee remuneration

Directors' Report, Annexure III item 1 (p.23): ratio of each director's remuneration to median employee remuneration — Mr. E. Purushotham (MD): **63.55x**; Mr. E. Swapnil (JMD & CFO): 42.37x; Ms. E. Smruthi (Non-Executive, promoter family): 20.95x. Median employee remuneration itself rose 9.77% during the year (Annexure III item 3, p.23) against a 267-person workforce.

This specific multiple was not cited in Pass 1 and quantifies the internal pay disparity underlying the broader "family remuneration is significant and growing" finding already flagged (Pass 1 RPT #8). Useful concrete number for a governance-quality discussion alongside New Finding 1.

🟡 — governance/RPT context, supporting existing flag.

## NEW FINDING 5 — 🟢/🟡 Annexure III shows "nil" remuneration increase for both Managing Directors, apparently inconsistent with the RPT note's actual YoY change

Directors' Report, Annexure III item 2 (p.23), "% increase in remuneration... in the financial year": Mr. E. Purushotham "--" and Mr. E. Swapnil "--" (i.e., no increase disclosed).

However, Note 39(b), RPT table (p.81), shows actual remuneration paid: Purushotham ₹212.43L (FY24: ₹201.60L, **+5.4%**) and Swapnil ₹141.51L (FY24: ₹142.72L, -0.8%). The "--" in Annexure III likely reflects that the contracted base salary (₹180L/₹120L p.a. per the AGM resolutions) was unchanged, while the RPT figures include perquisites/PF that did move. The two disclosures are not reconciled anywhere in the report, which is a minor internal-consistency gap worth noting (not large enough to independently rate as a red flag, but relevant alongside New Finding 1).

🟢/🟡 — disclosure consistency, minor.
