### The six mandatory diagnostics (standalone anchor; consolidated identical on operating lines)

1. Did revenue grow YoY? Yes, +23.76% (732.60 -> 906.64). No prior guidance on file to benchmark against (first-look).
2. Did operating EBITDA margin expand/contract? Contracted. Q1 FY27 21.07% vs Q1 FY26 23.19% = −212 bps, despite the Rs 79.03 cr GST Compensatory Cess expense disappearing YoY. The cess removal was more than offset by cost inflation elsewhere (see diagnostic 4).
3. Did core operating PBT ex-Other Income grow YoY? Only +2.51% (147.40 -> 151.10), against +23.76% revenue. This is the headline: top-line growth did NOT convert to operating profit growth. Reported PBT +1.14% and PAT −0.68% are both flat-to-down. The growth is real in volume/revenue terms but did not reach the bottom line this quarter.
4. What drove the gap between +23.76% revenue and −0.68% PAT? Expense walk YoY (Rs cr): Loading of lignite/overburden +145.88 (290.90 -> 436.78, +50.15%); Other Expenses +47.21 (96.86 -> 144.07); Royalties +27.17 (59.53 -> 86.70); GST cess −79.03 (79.03 -> nil); Depreciation +11.33; Finance +6.11; Employee +5.70; Inventory swing +5.97. Net expense +170.34 against revenue +174.04. The dominant swing is the +145.88 cr loading/overburden line: cost of mined output rose in step with (or ahead of) the mining ramp, absorbing the cess benefit. Tax rose (ETR 26.95% -> 28.27%). Result: PAT flat-to-down.
5. Are D&A and finance costs scaling faster than revenue? Yes. Depreciation +51.50% and finance costs +1,222% (off Rs 0.50 cr) vs revenue +23.76%. This is an early capex-absorption signal: assets and borrowing are being added ahead of the profit they will eventually carry (corroborated by Mining segment assets +39.77% YoY, Step-segment below). ROCE compresses until volumes/prices catch up.
6. Is Other Income concentration changing? No. Other Income Rs 76.15 cr is flat YoY (−1.47%) and 33.51% of PBT — material but stable. Headline PAT is NOT being masked by treasury timing this quarter; the miss is genuinely operational (loading-cost inflation), which is the more concerning read.
