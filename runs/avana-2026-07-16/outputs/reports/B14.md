## NARRATIVE (plain English)

AVANA is a genuinely fast-growing, high-operating-ROCE power-equipment maker that listed on the NSE SME board six months ago, and that is the whole problem. FY26 numbers are good: Rs 84 Cr revenue, Rs 11.72 Cr profit, roughly 40% ROCE once you strip out the idle IPO cash, and a net-cash balance sheet. The operator's own valuation correction is right that a 40% ROCE grower should not be handed a sub-20x multiple on trailing earnings, which is why the valuation runs on forward earnings at a 20.9x destination multiple and lands a base target of Rs 322, about a 30% three-year return with roughly seven-to-one reward-to-risk.

But almost nothing here is confirmed on a primary document. There is no annual report, no results PDF, no concall, no rating, no shareholding filing. The growth story rests on one factory (KIADB) that has slipped its commissioning deadline by about six years, and that same factory is doing double duty in the math — it justifies both the high forward ROCE and the revenue ramp, which is why it is flagged SHARED CATALYST. The cash-conversion quality is INDETERMINATE because no one can see the receivables ageing, and the working-capital cycle is heavy at roughly 245 days. Gate 0 is AVERAGE, the emerging-moat scan finds essentially nothing yet, and the promoter carries a CAUTION for a short track record and disclosure gaps.

So the verdict is WATCHLIST, provisional on filings, sized Small only if it ever clears the gates, entered only in the Rs 145-165 zone and only when the quarterly checklist shows no broken trigger. The single most important thing to watch is whether the KIADB facility actually commissions. If it does and the first real filings confirm the receivables and the run rate, this becomes a live BUY candidate. If it slips again, the thesis is broken on its own terms.
