# Listed grid equipment micro cap expanding capacity

Avana Electrosystems is a Bengaluru maker of customised control and relay panels rated 11kV to 220kV, numerical protection relays, electromechanical relays and accessories, and substation automation panels, sold mostly to private power players and to state tender buyers. The model is asset light: major fabrication is outsourced and the company does assembly, wiring, relay fixing, testing, and dispatch, per an operator relayed digest of the RHP and four NSE filings dated 17 July 2026. FY26 revenue was Rs 84 Cr, PAT was Rs 11.7 Cr, the balance sheet carried net cash of about Rs 28 Cr, and the shares trade near Rs 146 against a Rs 331 Cr market cap.

What is changing is a plant move, not just the January 2026 listing. The company is relocating its two existing units into one integrated manufacturing unit on KIADB land at Avverahalli near Bengaluru, and the relayed RHP puts capacity up 150% on both lines, protection relays 70,000 to 175,000 units and control and relay panels 600 to 1,500 units, with built up area rising from about 12,500 to 58,286 sq ft. The expansion is space driven rather than machinery driven, needing more floor area and bought in components rather than new machines, and commercial production was targeted for mid May 2026 with no filing in hand confirming it started. The FY26 capital picture matches the raise: equity share capital jumped about 28.7 times to Rs 22.7 Cr, reserves rose to Rs 36 Cr, and financing brought in Rs 19.8 Cr. Use of proceeds is now partly known, with Rs 8.6 Cr earmarked for working capital and the rest tied to the integrated facility, though the primary use of proceeds document is still not in the run folder.

The revenue record shows a real inflection off a weak base. Sales moved from Rs 22.6 Cr in FY20 down to Rs 15 Cr in FY21 and Rs 20.4 Cr in FY22, then up to Rs 53 Cr, Rs 61.5 Cr, and Rs 84 Cr across FY24 to FY26. PAT tracked the climb, Rs 4 Cr to Rs 8.3 Cr to Rs 11.7 Cr over the last three years, and the seven year sales CAGR is about 24.5%. Gate 0 scored the company AVERAGE, because the sub scale FY21 and FY22 years drag the through cycle return and profitability blocks even as the recent two years look strong. Order book and customer count read as genuine positives: pending orders were Rs 52.24 Cr as of 30 November 2025 in the relayed RHP, about 0.62 times FY26 revenue, customers served rose from 275 in FY23 to 367 in FY25, and exports to a Kuwait customer began in FY26 at about Rs 14.2 lakh in H1. None of this is yet anchored to a primary filing.

Cash conversion is the sharpened weak spot. The relayed RHP puts the H1 FY26 net working capital cycle near 245 days with high inventory days, which says the working capital intensity is structural and real rather than only a screener artifact, and the IPO raised Rs 8.6 Cr specifically to fund it. Operating cash flow did improve, from minus Rs 0.3 Cr in FY23 to Rs 7.95 Cr in FY26, and the full window operating cash to profit ratio is 0.62 times. The determination stays INDETERMINATE because the two items that would settle structural versus growth, the receivables ageing schedule and a rating rationale, are still absent.

The promoter read is CAUTION, not clean and not alarming. There is no SEBI, criminal, or tax action against the company or its four promoters, no pledge found, and an unmodified FY26 audit. Against that, the listed track record is six months old, the Company Secretary and Compliance Officer resigned effective 31 March 2026 and was replaced from 10 April 2026, the KIADB commercial production deadline stands at 22 May 2026 with project implementation due by October 2026 and explicit land loss risk if missed, the promoter group disclosure is incomplete on a father in law who qualifies under SEBI rules, and a 23 June 2026 NSE reply on proceeds utilisation adds a mild governance item. The market opportunity behind all this is real and roomy: the domestic addressable market for medium and high voltage control and relay panels, protection relays, and substation automation sits at roughly Rs 1,100 Cr to Rs 1,700 Cr, with a serviceable slice near Rs 800 Cr and about 9.5 times revenue headroom, though the share path implies a 14% to 15% revenue CAGR against the 24.5% delivered.

Valuation lands at WATCHLIST, provisional on primary filings, entry Rs 145 to Rs 165, margin of safety price Rs 132. The exit multiple runs on two tracks. The governing additive track builds a four pillar destination price to earnings of 20.9 times, from an operating ROCE near 40% ex idle cash giving a 26.1 times base, a cash multiplier of 0.80 times for the unresolved conversion, zero growth premium, and the 25 times sector cap. The conservative RRM floor track lands 18.3 times. Applied to a base FY30 EPS of Rs 14.82, plus Rs 12 of net cash per share, the base fair value is Rs 322, bear Rs 202, bull Rs 372, and the probability weighted three year return is about 22.7%. The Hurdle Ratio is CONDITIONAL: it passes at 2.12 on the forward price to earnings method the operator authorised, and fails at 1.63 on a trailing exit basis, because at Rs 146 the stock already trades near 28 times FY26 earnings.

That last point is where the devil's advocate lands hard: valuation safety rated DESTROYED, the thesis overall WEAKENED BUT ALIVE. The 25% clearance exists only on the forward priced frame. Priced in trailing terms the same 20.9 times destination is 27.2 times at the FY29 exit, above the 25 times absolute sector cap, so the base case assumes no multiple compression over the hold, and honest trailing math gives a 19.6% return, a fail. The 40% ROCE base rests on stripping about Rs 22 Cr of cash that the RHP itself earmarks for working capital and KIADB capex, so that cash is arguably claimed three times over, as non operating for the ROCE, as returnable at Rs 12 a share, and as the money that funds the factory. And the shared catalyst is circular: one KIADB slip collapses the ROCE base and reverts revenue toward the 15% SAM path at the same time, and the 22 May 2026 deadline has already passed unconfirmed after a roughly six year slip. The devil's own recommendation is to harden entry below about Rs 120 to Rs 130, which would put CMP above the buy zone.

The binding constraint is still evidence, not the business. The FY26 audited results were approved on 21 May 2026 but the numbers are not in hand, and the RHP and the four NSE filings reach this run only as an operator relayed digest, a lead and cross check layer, not the primary PDFs. Stages 2 and 3 stayed skipped because no annual report or filing exists in the folder to deep read, the manifest sector tag of Pharma or CDMO is a confirmed collection error to be ignored, and the whole valuation resets the moment the first primary filing arrives.
