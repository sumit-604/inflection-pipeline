## Business Understanding Narrative

Fratelli makes and sells wine, and now a wine-based ready-to-drink (RTD) product.
It grows grapes on its own vineyards, makes wine, and sells across premium, super
premium, and luxury tiers, led by J'NOON and Sette (IP FY26 Q4 p.14). More than
70% of bottle sales are premium and above (IP FY26 Q4 p.14). It is the domestic
luxury-wine leader, with a claimed >50% share of that niche and ~30% of the
broader premium market (Concall FY26 Q4 p.8). Gross margin is high, 77-80%, because
it owns the grapes and prices at the top end (IP FY26 Q4 p.24). In February 2025 it
launched Shotgun, a wine RTD, which sold ~100,000 cases in year one and holds ~6%
share of a small but fast-growing category (IP FY26 Q4 p.13).

What is changing is a bet on premiumisation and new formats converting the high
gross margin into real operating profit. Today there is almost none: FY26 EBITDA
was Rs 1.06 cr on Rs 184 cr revenue, and PAT was minus Rs 16 cr (IP FY26 Q4 p.24).
Revenue actually peaked at Rs 216 cr in FY24 and fell to Rs 182-184 cr in FY25-26
(IP FY26 Q4 p.24). Vision 2030 targets Rs 500+ cr revenue at 20%+ EBITDA margin
(IP FY26 Q4 p.20).

Why now: the RTD launch and a premiumisation push are meant to restart growth, with
FY27 guided at 30% and net-net breakeven (Concall FY26 Q4 p.12).

What must be true: high gross margin must convert to EBITDA as brand spend (8-9% of
sales) scales, and Shotgun must keep growing.

What breaks it: brand spend stays high without operating leverage, revenue stays
stuck near Rs 180-240 cr, and Vision 2030 slips.

## 2. Business model and archetype
Premium wine and RTD maker, vineyard-backed. Revenue is cases times price across
premium, super premium, luxury, plus the Shotgun RTD (IP FY26 Q4 p.14). Archetype:
brand/franchise consumer, aspiring to R4-R5, but currently pre-profit. The economic
engine is a very high gross margin (77-80%) that has not yet dropped through to
EBITDA because brand and trade spend absorb it (Concall FY26 Q3 p.8). Own-vineyard
sourcing gives cost control and quality but ties up capital.

## 3. Competitive advantages
Real in a narrow niche. Fratelli leads domestic luxury wine (>50% share) with
recognised labels (J'NOON, Sette) and is the wine-in-a-can leader at >90% share
(Concall FY26 Q4 p.8; guidance). Brand and premium positioning give some pricing
power, visible in the 78-80% gross margin. But the Indian wine market is small and
alco-bev is heavily state-regulated, and scale economics are unproven. Moat:
forming, brand-based, in a small pond.

## 5. Financial trajectory
Revenue Rs 144 (FY22), Rs 197 (FY23), Rs 216 (FY24), Rs 182 (FY25), Rs 184 (FY26)
(IP FY26 Q4 p.24). Gross margin rose steadily to 78-79%. But EBITDA collapsed from
Rs 29 cr (FY24) to Rs 0.23 cr (FY25) to Rs 1.06 cr (FY26), and PAT went from +Rs 9
cr to -Rs 13 cr to -Rs 16 cr. So this is not a flat business, it is a business
whose revenue fell from its FY24 peak and whose profit turned to losses while it
spent on brand building and new products (IP FY26 Q4 p.21). Capex is light, ~Rs 10
cr in FY26 (guidance). The inflection the company wants is a margin recovery, not
yet visible in the numbers.

## 6. The transition and the quality ladder
FROM R4-ish on brand but pre-profit in practice, TO a R4-R5 branded consumer with
20%+ EBITDA margin by FY30 (IP FY26 Q4 p.20). That is a large margin leap from
~0.6% today, and the base-rate caution applies: a jump of several rungs in
profitability is rare and needs proof. Management's claim, quoted once: "Vision
2030: Targeting Rs 500+ crore revenue with 20%+ margin" (IP FY26 Q4 p.20). The
proof gate for the margin transition has not fired.

## 8. Proof check
The last quarters do not yet prove the margin thesis. FY26 revenue was flat and
EBITDA was Rs 1 cr; the company only reached operating breakeven (IP FY26 Q4 p.24).
Shotgun's 100,000 cases and export growth are real early wins (IP FY26 Q4 p.13),
but the core proof point, high gross margin converting to double-digit EBITDA, has
not fired. FY27 is guided as the breakeven year at Rs 240 cr (Concall FY26 Q4
p.12). Until then this is a narrative.

## 9. Flags from the documents
- Two straight years of losses (FY25 -Rs 13 cr, FY26 -Rs 16 cr) and revenue below
  the FY24 peak (IP FY26 Q4 p.24).
- The whole thesis is operating leverage that has not appeared; brand spend 8-9%
  plus high trade promotions absorb the gross margin (Concall FY26 Q3 p.8).
- A hospitality diversification (Rs 70-80 cr, up to Rs 100 cr) adds capex and a new
  business risk while the core is still pre-profit (IP FY26 Q4 p.17, p.20).
- BSE-only listing: thin liquidity, lighter coverage.
- Alco-bev is state-regulated, distribution is state-by-state.
Not seen: promoter holding/pledge, related-party detail, debt, auditor notes.

## 11. Posture: Transition Decision Matrix
- Proof gate: NOT fired (no margin conversion yet).
- Ugliness: an artifact of the climb (brand-investment drag), on management's telling.
- Recognition gap: hard to read; there is no PE (losses), and the stock trades on
  ~3x sales for a story.
Proof-not-fired plus artifact ugliness plus an open-ish gap maps to RESEARCH /
WATCH: a narrative until the proof gate fires. It is the seductive kind, because
the gross margin and the brand are genuinely good; the risk is paying for Vision
2030 before the margin shows up.

## 12. Verdict card
**WATCH; optional spear if you want premium alco-bev exposure.** Fratelli is the
right size and has a genuine premium brand with 78-80% gross margin, but it is
pre-profit, its revenue is below the FY24 peak, and Vision 2030 is a big unproven
margin leap. Load-bearing facts to verify first: (1) whether gross margin converts
to EBITDA as FY27 revenue scales toward Rs 240 cr, (2) Shotgun's real sell-through
and profitability, (3) promoter holding, related-party terms, and debt, (4) the
hospitality capital commitment. Read it against Sula Vineyards as the listed peer.
What would change the view: two quarters showing operating leverage (EBITDA margin
climbing toward 10%) would move it to PROCEED. Until then it stays a watch, not a
position. No price here.
