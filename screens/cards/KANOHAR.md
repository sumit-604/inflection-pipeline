## Business Understanding Narrative

**What does it do, and how does it make money?** Kanohar makes power
transformers at Gangol and sells them mostly to state power utilities. It also
builds substations and transmission lines as an EPC contractor. Power
transformers were 62.17% of H1 FY26 revenue (DRHP, page 380). EPC was 23.97%
(page 44, full page). Money comes from built-to-order units priced by tender or
purchase order. Twenty-five of its 31 contracts carry a price variation clause
for copper, steel and oil; six are fixed price (page 42, full page).

**What is changing, the transition?** The mix and the margin. EPC fell from
48.48% of revenue in FY23 to 14.66% in FY25 (page 44, full page). Transformers
rose. Gross margin went from 23.60% in FY23 to 32.51% in FY25 and 43.16% in H1
FY26 (page 46, full page). EBITDA margin went from 9.34% to 20.73% to 25.81%.
The company is moving from a low-margin contractor to a specified transformer
maker, including railway units (traction and Scott) and shunt reactors.

**Why now?** Order inflow. The order book was Rs 1,635.56 crore at 30 September
2025, against Rs 861.47 crore six months earlier and Rs 522.38 crore in FY23
(page 45, full page). That is 3.6 times FY25 revenue (derived: 1,635.56 /
450.61). Transformers are 83.42% of the book.

**What must be true for the thesis to work?** Three things. The book must
convert at the H1 FY26 margin, not the FY23 one. Working capital must not
swallow the profit: net working capital days rose from 64 in FY23 to 197 in H1
FY26 (page 46, full page). And the market cap must be right. The Bull AI record
shows Rs 7,187 crore, which is about 110 times FY25 PAT.

**What breaks it?** Concentration and price. The top ten customers were 97.04%
of H1 FY26 revenue; the largest alone was 36.33% (page 41, full page). 95.56%
of the order book is government (page 45, full page). One state utility slowing
payment or ordering breaks the year. At the recorded market cap, even a
perfect execution year leaves the multiple far above the earnings.

## 3. Competitive advantages

Forming, not proven. The DRHP claims short-circuit testing capability (page
380) and over 40 years in transformers (page 379). The evidence of pricing
power is the margin jump. The evidence against it is in the company's own risk
factor: competitors have "wider product portfolios, larger sales and service
networks, greater brand recognition" (page 385). Utilities buy by tender, so
switching cost is low. Capacity is not the moat: utilisation was 29.70% in FY25
and 15.53% in H1 FY26 on 19,200 MVA (page 39). Moat status: forming, on
railway and reactor approvals; not yet visible in any number that tender
competition cannot erase.

## 6. The transition and the quality ladder

FROM **R2** (cost-advantaged converter: tender-priced transformers and EPC,
EBITDA 9% to 11% in FY23 to FY24) TO **R3** (spec'd supplier: railway traction
and Scott units, shunt reactors, short-circuit tested designs; EBITDA above
20%). One rung, inside the base rate. Management's claim, once: the company
"manufacture[s] five different types of transformers with customized technical
specifications" (page 379).

Mechanism: capex of Rs 66.74 crore at Gangol for capacity, backward integration
and automation, plus Rs 130.00 crore of working capital from the fresh issue
(page 24, full page). No orders for that capex had been placed at filing (page
53).

## 8. Proof check

The last two periods move one way on margin and the other way on revenue. H1
FY26 EBITDA margin was 25.81% against 20.73% in FY25. H1 FY26 revenue was Rs
165.58 crore, or 36.7% of FY25 in half a year (derived). The order book doubled
in the same six months. Proof point named: **FY26 audited revenue above Rs 450

## 9. Flags from the documents

- **Customer concentration.** Top ten customers were 97.04% of H1 FY26 revenue;
  customer 1 was 36.33% (page 41, full page). Punjab was also exactly 36.33% of
  H1 revenue by state (page 41), so customer 1 is most likely a Punjab utility.
- **State churn.** Bihar was 29.21% of FY23 revenue and 1.33% in FY25. Gujarat
  was nil in FY24 and 14.86% in FY25 (page 41, full page). The customer base
  rotates with tenders.
- **Supplier concentration.** Top three suppliers were 59.27% of H1 FY26 cost
  of goods sold (page 42, full page; derived 26.42 + 17.36 + 15.49). APAR
  Industries is named among the top ten (page 43).
- **Working capital.** Net working capital days 197 in H1 FY26 against 64 in
  FY23 (page 46, full page). The IPO earmarks Rs 130 crore to fund it.
- **Litigation.** By the company: 3 tax and 2 civil matters, Rs 8.71 crore.
  Against: 2 tax and 2 civil, Rs 13.11 crore (page 26, full page). Detail pages
  returned empty. Past disputes with customers over arrears are disclosed (page
  42, full page).
- **Fixed-price exposure.** 6 of 31 contracts carry no price variation (page
  42, full page).
- **Capacity utilisation** 15.53% in H1 FY26 (page 39), yet the IPO funds more
  capacity.
