## PART 1: CLAIM-BY-CLAIM VERIFICATION

### Q1. Consolidated operating cash flow trend and management tone ("not a problem" vs negative CFO)

**Claim (B05):** Ceigall says cash flow and equity funding are "not a problem" / "amazing" on every
call while consolidated CFO was negative in FY25 (Rs -515.6cr) and FY26 (Rs -91.3cr), never
reconciled.

**Verdict: CONTRADICTED** (the claim being tested is whether this framing is sector-wide)

**Peer evidence:**
- No peer transcript contains a blanket qualitative reassurance phrase equivalent to "cash flow is not
  a problem" or "amazing." Searches for "free cash flow," "cash rich," "comfortable cash," "liquidity
  position" and "cash flow generation" returned zero hits across all 12 transcripts.
- Where leverage or funding gaps show up, each peer names the specific cause and the specific fix, not
  a general reassurance. HGINFRA, Q4 FY26, in prepared remarks: "To bridge the temporary gap, funding
  gap and to maintain the project momentum, the company secured additional working capital for
  temporarily increasing overall leverage" (HGINFRA-Concall_May_2026_Transcript.txt, **p.5**), naming
  the cause (delayed solar-plant debt disbursement) and the expected resolution quarter. When pressed
  later in the same call, management is even more specific about the mechanism: "the bank has recalled
  certain loans because the timeline of these solar projects... were not extended" (p.13).
- KNRCON discloses consolidated debt rising quarter to quarter with a number each time (Rs 1,847cr
  Mar-25 -> Rs 2,018cr Jun-25 -> Rs 2,443cr Dec-25) and ties it to named receivable delays (irrigation
  debtors, HAM debtors) rather than a cash-flow-is-fine framing (KNRCON-Concall_Aug_2025_Transcript.txt,
  p.5; KNRCON-Concall_Feb_2026_Transcript.txt, p.6).
- When analysts press on working-capital or debt movements, all three peers answer with hard
  debtor/payable/unbilled numbers by project or client (see Q6 and Q10 below), not qualitative comfort
  language.

**Peers silent:** None; all three peers discuss cash/debt/working capital in every call, but none uses
Ceigall's reassurance framing.

**Net read:** The sector norm observed here is granular, named-driver disclosure of debt and
working-capital movements, not blanket qualitative reassurance. Ceigall's "not a problem" / "amazing"
framing, unreconciled against a negative consolidated CFO, is not matched by any peer and reads as a
company-specific communication choice rather than sector-standard tone.

### Q2. NHAI award pace (km and Rs value) when asked directly

**Claim (B05):** Ceigall deflects to the NHAI website on this repeatable question across multiple
calls; tests whether that is unusual or standard reticence.

**Verdict: PARTIALLY VERIFIED** (mixed: 2 of 3 peers disclose unprompted; 1 of 3 deflects like Ceigall)

**Peer evidence:**

  remarks every quarter, unprompted by any analyst question:
  - KNRCON, Q3/9M FY26: "road project award during 9 months of FY '26 remained muted, with the
    Ministry of Road Transport and Highways and NHAI awarding 1,448 km and 712 km of the project
    respectively" (KNRCON-Concall_Feb_2026_Transcript.txt, p.3).
  - KNRCON, Q4/FY26 full year: "During FY '26, NHAI awarded 3,100 kilometres of project, lower by
    approximately 22% year-on-year and below the initial target of 7,500 kilometres"
    (KNRCON-Concall_Jun_2026_Transcript.txt, p.3).
  - PNCINFRA, Q2 FY26: "NHAI has set an ambitious target of 6,300 km length of new projects award for
    FY26" (PNCINFRA-Concall_Nov_2025_Transcript.txt, p.4).
  - PNCINFRA, Q3 FY26: "NHAI awarding only 377 km of new road projects in Q3 FY26, compared to around
    504 km in Q3 FY25" (PNCINFRA-Concall_Feb_2026_Transcript.txt, **p.4**).

**Peers silent:** None on the topic generally, but HGINFRA is effectively "silent" on the precise
number when asked directly, matching Ceigall's pattern.

**Net read:** Two of three peers show that proactive, precise NHAI/MoRTH award disclosure is achievable
and is in fact standard practice for them every quarter without being asked. One peer (HGINFRA) shares
Ceigall's deflection when the question is put directly. Ceigall's deflection is not universal sector
behaviour, but it is not unique either — it sits with the weaker third of this three-peer set on this

### Q4. HAM equity-commitment reconciliation quarter to quarter

**Claim (B05):** Ceigall's HAM/renewable/T&D equity-commitment totals are quoted on different,
unreconciled bases every call; tests whether this is a company-specific disclosure problem.

**Verdict: PARTIALLY VERIFIED** (2 of 3 peers reconcile cleanly with explicit scope explanations; 1 of 3
shows a smaller, less-explained drift)

**Peer evidence — clean reconciliation:**
- KNRCON's HAM equity figures reconcile arithmetically quarter to quarter on a stated "revised" basis:
  Aug-25 call — "already invested INR676 crores out of INR990 crores of the revised equity
  requirement... additional requirement of INR314 crores... INR185 crores in FY26 and INR129 crores in
  FY27" (KNRCON-Concall_Aug_2025_Transcript.txt, p.4); Nov-25 call — "already invested INR698 crores
  out of the INR991 crores revised equity requirement... additional equity requirement of INR292
  crores... INR175 crores and INR117 crores for FY26 and '27" (KNRCON-Concall_Nov_2025_Transcript.txt,
  p.4). The Rs 22cr rise in cumulative infusion (676 to 698) and the matching Rs 22cr fall in the
  outstanding requirement (314 to 292) tie out exactly.
- PNCINFRA reconciles even through an explicit scope change: Nov-25 call — total requirement Rs
  1,744cr, invested Rs 1,081cr (PNCINFRA-Concall_Nov_2025_Transcript.txt, p.5); Feb-26 call — same Rs
  1,744cr total, invested Rs 1,110cr, remaining Rs 634cr, arithmetic exact
  (PNCINFRA-Concall_Feb_2026_Transcript.txt, **p.5**); May-26 call — total requirement revised down to
  Rs 1,623cr "excluding 2 HAM projects, for which LOAs are yet to be received," invested Rs 1,081cr,
  remaining Rs 542cr, arithmetic exact and the reason for the total changing is stated explicitly
  (PNCINFRA-Concall_May_2026_Transcript.txt, **p.7**).

**Peer evidence — unexplained drift (partial echo of Ceigall's problem):**
- HGINFRA's "total equity requirement for 11 HAM projects" figure itself rises every quarter on the
  same declared project count, without an explicit reconciliation of why the total climbs: Aug-25 Rs
  1,664cr -> Nov-25 Rs 1,709cr -> Feb-26 Rs 1,750cr -> May-26 Rs 1,903cr (HGINFRA-
  Concall_Aug_2025_Transcript.txt, p.6; -Nov_2025, p.6-7; -Feb_2026, **p.5**; -May_2026, p.6). The
  cumulative-infused figure climbs consistently in step (Rs 997cr -> Rs 1,165cr -> Rs 1,242cr -> Rs
  1,210cr, the last a stated typo-corrected "as of March '26" restatement), so the mechanics are
  traceable, but no call explains why the total requirement itself keeps rising on an unchanged project
  count.

**Peers silent:** None; all three quantify HAM equity requirements every call.

**Net read:** Clean, scope-explained reconciliation is achievable and is what two of three peers do.
HGINFRA shows a milder, unexplained version of the same problem Ceigall has (a rising total on a flat
project count, never reconciled). This makes Ceigall's version of the problem more severe in degree —
its swings are described in B05 as larger and mixed across HAM, solar and T&D scopes together — but not
unique in kind; the failure mode exists elsewhere in the peer set too.

### Q5. Proactive disclosure of fraud, IFC qualifications, auditor resignations, leadership churn

**Claim (B05):** Ceigall stays silent across three calls on the FY26 procurement fraud, the qualified
IFC opinion, the NHAI SPV termination, an auditor resignation, and a CEO/WTD change; tests whether peer

**Verdict: CONTRADICTED** (re-ruled from UNVERIFIABLE in Run 1 — a real, testable benchmark exists in
this peer set)

Run 1 found no comparable governance/compliance event in the peer set and ruled this question
unverifiable. A closer audit finds that comparable events are present. The re-ruled verdict is
CONTRADICTED: this peer set's norm is proactive or, at minimum, non-evasive disclosure of adverse
events, which Ceigall's total three-call silence on comparable (arguably more severe) items does not
match.

**Peer evidence — proactive, unprompted disclosure in prepared remarks:**

  the matter and we'll continue to update the investors through our disclosure on stock exchange upon

  raised it first, named the agency, and gave a forward commitment to keep disclosing.

**Net read:** Two of three peers (HGINFRA, KNRCON) volunteer governance/compliance-adjacent trouble

### Q6. Working-capital-day trends (receivables, unbilled revenue, payables)

**Claim (B05):** Tests whether Ceigall's working-capital stretch (payable days 79 to 138,
unbilled-revenue days 94 to 133) is sector-wide or company-specific, and ties it to the FY24
Atmanirbhar-scheme withdrawal.

**Verdict: PARTIALLY VERIFIED**

**Peer evidence:**
- No peer transcript mentions "Atmanirbhar" at all — the specific causal link Ceigall's calls imply is
  not corroborated or contradicted by any peer; that piece is unverifiable.
- Working-capital stretch itself is visibly sector-wide and can be severe: KNRCON's standalone
  working-capital days swung from 93 days (Mar-25) to 169 days (Jun-25) — nearly double — before easing
  to 82 days (Dec-25) (KNRCON-Concall_Aug_2025_Transcript.txt, p.5; KNRCON-Concall_Feb_2026
  _Transcript.txt, p.6). Management explained the spike with a named cause (irrigation debtors ~Rs
  800cr and HAM debtors ~Rs 1,200cr pending from state governments, KNRCON-Concall_Aug_2025
  _Transcript.txt, **p.7**) and the subsequent recovery.
- PNCINFRA's net working-capital days sit around 110 (Q1 FY27, PNCINFRA-Concall_Aug_2026_
  Transcript.txt, p.9) with management naming the specific driver each quarter (a Rs 741cr Jal Jeevan
  Mission pending realisation, a Rs 416cr Andhra Pradesh irrigation invoice) and citing named
  improvements when a state releases payment.
- The pattern across peers is: working-capital stretch happens, is large in magnitude at times
  (KNRCON nearly doubling in one quarter), but each instance is tied to a specific named
  counterparty/cause and each is followed, within one to two quarters, by improvement or an explicit
  explanation of the expected reversal trigger.

**Peers silent:** None on working capital generally.

**Net read:** The direction (working-capital stretch under government-counterparty delay) is
sector-wide and can be sharper than Ceigall's numbers in a single quarter (KNRCON's spike). What is not
observed in this peer set is Ceigall's pattern of one-directional, multi-quarter worsening (79->138
payable days, 94->133 unbilled days) without a named reversal catalyst on its own calls. Peers show
volatility with recovery and attribution; Ceigall (per B05) shows sustained drift without either.

### Q9. Escalation cost pass-through ("fully compensated by the authority") — NEW TEST

**Claim:** Ceigall claimed on its May-2026 call that escalation cost is fully compensated by the

**Net read:** All three peers describe the same-period escalation-cost mechanism as partial and

## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

**2A. Demand environment.** All three peers describe FY26 as a below-peak, "muted" or "subdued"
NHAI/MoRTH award year, continuing into Q1 FY27: KNRCON's full-year FY26 NHAI award total of 3,100 km was
22% below the prior year and well under the initial 7,500 km target (KNRCON-Concall_Jun_2026
_Transcript.txt, p.3); PNCINFRA's Q1 FY27 update still shows only 107 km awarded
(PNCINFRA-Concall_Aug_2026_Transcript.txt, p.3). All three attribute the slowdown to land acquisition
and cabinet-approval delays, not to a demand-side problem — the pipeline (NHAI's invited bids
"aggregating to 1.5 trillion" per KNRCON, p.3 of the Feb-2026 call) is described as large and improving.
This is consensus across the peer set, not a split.

Set against this, Ceigall's own FY26 order inflow (Rs 11,332cr, beating its own Rs 5,000cr guide 2.3x,
per B05) grew sharply in a year the primary NHAI award market was at a multi-year low. This run adds a
direct peer counter-example that sharpens why that beat needs its own explanation: HGINFRA, in the same
period, guided FY26 order inflow of roughly Rs 10,000cr and secured only about Rs 1,300cr — an 87% miss,
disclosed unprompted in its own prepared remarks: "We have started the last year with expected new order

mix (state/non-NHAI, T&D/renewables LOAs), or simply better bidding — but it raises the bar for what
"beating guidance in a soft-award year" should be expected to explain, and it is exactly the kind of
guidance-miss candour Ceigall's own calls do not offer in either direction.

**2B. Pricing and input costs.** All three peers explicitly name the same driver for elevated input

## PART 5: CROSS-PEER HYPOTHESIS

Run 1's hypothesis held that disclosure granularity in this peer set is a single company-level trait
running consistently across unrelated line items, with HGINFRA cast as the uniformly lower-granularity
peer (evidenced only by its NHAI-km deflection and its unexplained HAM-equity drift). This run's new
evidence complicates that hypothesis: HGINFRA is also the peer that gives the single most granular
rupet-level WC/debtor breakdown in the entire corpus (Q10, p.12), the cleanest quantified segment-margin
split (Q8, p.14), and the most proactive, unprompted disclosure of adverse events anywhere in the peer
set (Q5: a live CBI search, an 87% guidance miss, a loan recall). HGINFRA cannot be a uniformly
low-granularity discloser and also be the peer setting the high-water mark on three separate axes. Run
1's hypothesis is retired.

**Revised hypothesis:** The reticence this peer set does show is narrowly confined to a single kind of
claim — a precise, forward-looking number the peer cannot control (an exact NHAI award figure for a
future period; a monetization price still under live negotiation) — while candour is close to universal
on a different kind of claim: a known, already-occurred fact, however adverse (a CBI search, a lender
loan recall, a guidance miss, a show-cause notice, a stuck debtor). The axis this peer set organizes its
reticence around is FORECAST UNCERTAINTY versus KNOWN FACT, not FLATTERING versus DAMAGING. Ceigall's
silence runs on the opposite axis: it discloses and beats its own forward guidance (order inflow,
margin, revenue growth — all committed, specific, and delivered per B05/05-concall) while staying silent
on known, already-filed, adverse facts (fraud, IFC qualification, SPV termination, auditor resignation,
leadership churn). This is testable: if the hypothesis holds, a fourth road-EPC peer added to this
corpus should show the same split — precise on past adverse facts, hedged only on uncontrollable future
numbers — and Ceigall, if it behaves consistently with its own pattern, should continue delivering
precise forward guidance while remaining silent on any new adverse fact that arises, rather than
becoming vague on both axes or precise on both. This is inferable only from comparing behaviour across
the whole peer set on both kinds of claims together; no single peer transcript states this distinction,
and no single transcript of Ceigall's states it either — it emerges only from the combined pattern
across all sixteen transcripts examined in this and the parent stage.
