## PASS 3 NEW FINDINGS (pattern check)

Six items emerged from cross-referencing the notes against the Independent Auditors' Reports (standalone and consolidated) — these were outside Pass 1/2's notes-only scope but directly corroborate, and materially escalate, findings those passes already flagged from the notes alone. Also two "no new finding" confirmations (restatements, going concern) that close out open pattern-check items Pass 1/2 left pending.

1. **Auditors placed a formal "Emphasis of Matter — Search and Seizure" paragraph in BOTH the standalone and consolidated Independent Auditor's Reports**, each explicitly drawing attention to the same note Pass 1/2 already flagged as an unresolved tail risk (standalone note 50(c); consolidated note 52(d)) (Auditor's Report — Standalone, para 4, p.14366-14394; Auditor's Report — Consolidated, para 4, p.19656-19684). The EOM text confirms: search covered "offices and manufacturing units of the Company/Group and its subsidiaries, and the residences of selected key managerial personnel" (15-21 Jan 2025, Section 132); post-year-end the Company/Group has received show-cause notice(s) under Section 158BC and is "in the process of submitting its response"; auditors state "pending conclusion of the ongoing search and seizure proceedings, the impact of this matter on the [standalone/consolidated] financial statements... [is not yet determinable]" (text truncates at page break but the EOM mechanism itself is the material fact). **This elevates Pass 1 Finding #8 / Pass 2 refinement #8 from a notes-level disclosure to a matter the statutory auditors judged significant enough to require formal EOM treatment in their opinion** — a materially higher severity signal than an internal note alone conveys. 🔴 Red Flag — escalates Pass 1 #8/Pass 2 #8.

2. **CARO Clause (xi) — the fraud-reporting clause — is qualified/adverse for THREE entities in the consolidated Auditor's Report**: the Holding Company (Akums Drugs and Pharmaceuticals Ltd), Pure & Cure Healthcare Pvt Ltd, and Plenteous Pharmaceuticals Ltd (Consolidated Auditor's Report, "Report on Other Legal and Regulatory Requirements," table at p.19989-19999). This confirms the Section 143(12) fraud reporting Pass 2 identified from Note 55(d) was not confined to Head Office as the note's own language might suggest in isolation — it independently qualifies the audit opinion at **two additional subsidiaries by name** (Pure & Cure, the single largest subsidiary by segment contribution per Pass 1 Section 6; and Plenteous, already flagged in Pass 1 as one of the negative-net-assets loss-making subsidiaries). The fraud footprint is broader across the group structure than the ₹9.54M consolidated quantum alone conveys. 🔴 Red Flag — escalates Pass 1 #5/Pass 2 #7.

3. **The audit-trail (edit-log) database-level gap Pass 1 flagged from Note 52(a) is independently confirmed as a formal auditor finding under Rule 11(g)** of the Companies (Audit and Auditors) Rules, both in the standalone Auditor's Report (p.14653, 14699, 14738-14753) and the consolidated Auditor's Report (p.20006, 20064, 20126-20150): "The audit trail feature was not enabled at the database level for accounting software to log any direct data changes" — auditor language, not merely the Company's own note. This is boilerplate-adjacent (audit-trail Rule 11(g) qualifications are common across Indian listcos this cycle) but is a genuine, named audit-report qualification, not just a soft disclosure. 🟡 Watch — corroborates Pass 1 #10 with auditor-level confirmation.

4. **No restatements of prior-year comparative figures were found anywhere in the document** on a full-text pattern check (grepped "restated/reclassified/regroup" across both standalone and consolidated note sets). The only "reclassified" hits relate to (a) standard Ind AS foreign-currency-translation boilerplate, (b) the FY25 (not FY26) ₹13,615.12M put-option-liability reclassification into equity Pass 1 already identified as a prior-year, not current-year, event, and (c) routine OCI "items that will/will not be reclassified to P&L" line headers. **Confirms Pass 1's tentative "no restatements identified" note as a settled finding**, not merely provisional.

5. **Going-concern language check closed out clean**: only standard "accrual and going concern basis" boilerplate appears in the accounting policies (Note 1.2, Consolidated, p.302, per Pass 1); the standalone Auditor's Report's CARO commentary on going concern (p.15025-15043) affirmatively states "nothing has come to our attention, which causes us to believe that any material uncertainty exists as on the date of the audit report indicating that Company is not capable of meeting its liabilities existing at the date of balance sheet... within a period of one year," with the standard caveat that this is "not an assurance as to the future viability of the company." No Emphasis of Matter, qualification, or doubt language tied to going concern anywhere in either Auditor's Report. **NONE.**

6. **Minor note-to-note tax figure inconsistency (Pass 2 Finding #12) is confirmed as a genuine, if immaterial, numbers-do-not-tie pattern**: Note 46's segment reconciliation states consolidated tax expense of ₹1,257.04M while Note 44(a)'s DTA reconciliation (the note Pass 1 anchored its effective-tax-rate finding to) totals ₹1,260.94M for the same P&L line — a ₹3.90M (0.3%) variance between two notes that should tie to the single reported tax expense figure. Immaterial in size but included per Pass 3's explicit mandate to surface numbers that do not match between notes.

No further contradictions, deliberately-thin-disclosure patterns, or material post-balance-sheet events beyond those already surfaced in Pass 1 (dividend recommendation, VDA wage arrears already booked as an adjusting event, tax SCNs) were identified on this pattern re-read.

## D. FIVE QUESTIONS FOR MANAGEMENT

1. What specifically drove Akums Healthcare Ltd's loss to widen to -₹623.86M and its net worth to -₹653.87M in FY26, and why did the parent increase its unsecured inter-corporate deposit to this subsidiary to ₹330.68 Cr in the same year — what recoverability assessment or impairment testing was performed on this related-party receivable?
2. Who is the counterparty and what are the commercial terms of the "long-term agreement" generating the ₹1,032.31 Cr advance-from-customer contract liability (23.7% of FY26 revenue), and why is neither the counterparty nor the specific product/facility involved named anywhere in the notes?
3. Why does Akumentis Healthcare's standalone exceptional item of ₹630.48M not appear as a separate line in the Group's consolidated Exceptional Items note (Note 33), and what is the composition of the ₹700.58M "Intra group eliminations" contribution to consolidated profit (27.3% of consolidated PAT) in Note 49?
4. Given the formal Emphasis of Matter in both the standalone and consolidated Auditor's Reports on the Section 132 search and seizure, what is the Company's estimated range of exposure (if any can be quantified) and expected resolution timeline for the Section 158BC block-assessment proceedings, and how does the "no undisclosed income" self-assessment already filed reconcile with the post-year-end show-cause notices received?
5. What remediation and control enhancements followed the procurement fraud reported under Section 143(12) — which resulted in CARO Clause (xi) qualifications at the Holding Company, Pure & Cure Healthcare, and Plenteous Pharmaceuticals specifically — and why was the accounting-software audit-trail feature not enabled at the database level for the entirety of FY26?

## E. NOTES-BASED RED FLAGS

- **Related-party credit-risk concentration**: increasing unsecured ICD (₹330.68 Cr) extended by the parent to a wholly-owned subsidiary (Akums Healthcare Ltd) whose net worth deteriorated ~27x in one year to -₹653.87M, with no disclosed impairment assessment (Note 34B/49, 53).
- **Disclosure opacity on a large, structurally significant liability**: ₹1,032.31 Cr advance-from-customer contract liability (23.7% of revenue) with an unnamed counterparty and an imputed ₹77.61 Cr financing-component finance-cost charge (Note 30, 42(C)).
- **Earnings-quality distortion**: ~46% of reported PBT growth traced to offsetting treasury-interest and imputed-financing-cost movements rather than core operations; two consecutive years of opposite-signed "exceptional" items further complicate YoY comparability (Notes 26/27/30/33).
- **Inconsistent exceptional-item presentation**: a subsidiary's ₹630.48M exceptional charge flows into consolidated PAT via equity pickup but is not separately disclosed in the Group's own consolidated exceptional-items note (Note 33 vs Note 34B/49).
- **Formal auditor escalations**: Emphasis of Matter on the tax search-and-seizure matter (both audit reports); Section 143(12) fraud reporting with CARO Clause (xi) qualifications at three named entities (Holding Co, Pure & Cure, Plenteous); Rule 11(g) audit-trail qualification (both audit reports).
- **Unexplained large swing in a consolidation-mechanics line**: ₹700.58M "Intra group eliminations" contribution to consolidated PAT (27.3%), up ~13x YoY, with no explanatory note (Note 49).

No indicators of outright earnings manipulation (e.g., channel stuffing, fictitious revenue, off-balance-sheet vehicles) were found; the pattern is better characterised as disclosure opacity and related-party credit-risk concentration on a small number of high-materiality items, layered on an otherwise conservative core accounting policy set, with formal statutory-audit escalations that raise the severity above a typical notes-only read.

## F. ONE-LINE NOTES VERDICT

The notes reveal moderate to concerning accounting practices. Key concern: an increasing unsecured related-party loan to a subsidiary with collapsing net worth, sitting alongside an opaque ₹1,032 Cr customer-advance liability and formal auditor escalations (Emphasis of Matter on the tax search, Section 143(12) fraud reporting with CARO qualifications at three entities). Key strength: a structurally net-cash, near-zero-debt balance sheet with an honest R&D expense/capitalisation split and no restatements or going-concern issues. Overall accounting quality: 5/10.
