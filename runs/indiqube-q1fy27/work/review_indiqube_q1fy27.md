### 2. SECTOR INTELLIGENCE

The Indian managed and flexible workspace market is in a demand up-cycle led by Global Capability Centres and large domestic enterprises taking long, whole-floor space rather than hot desks [Notion prior work]. IndiQube's own mix reflects it: GCC clients contribute 53% of revenue and enterprises/unicorns dominate over startups, with 855 clients pan-India and 41% of revenue from multi-center clients [this quarter, deck L536-538]. The structural tailwind is that occupiers increasingly prefer an operator to carry the fit-out capital and operations while they take managed seats, which suits a lease-arbitrage model. The cyclical risk is the commercial real estate rent cycle: the operator signs 15 to 20 year landlord leases (lock-in around 38 months) against client contracts that are shorter, so a soft leasing patch lengthens the gap between paying rent and yielding rent. This quarter that gap shows as 7.84 Mn sqft of rent-paying area against 6.74 Mn sqft of rent-yielding area, an 86% occupancy on the rent-paying base [this quarter, deck L410-412]. Regulation shapes the optics more than the economics: Ind AS 116 forces the full lease onto the balance sheet and the P&L, which is why every listed flex operator in India shows a statutory loss while arguing an IGAAP cash profit. The monitoring-agency regime (CRISIL, Reg 32) that produced one of these documents is itself a sector-specific control on how freshly raised IPO cash is spent, and it is the document that exposed the ₹340.6 Cr still parked and the four new discretionary objects [this quarter, monitoring L458, L357-362].

### 3. BUSINESS-MODEL INTELLIGENCE

The company makes money three ways. It leases buildings from landlords, fits them out at about ₹1,650 per sqft, and sublets managed seats to enterprises at a revenue-to-rent ratio of 2.21 [this quarter, deck L483, L491]. It sells value-added services (facility management, design and build, sustainability) that reached ₹72 Cr and 17% of operating revenue [this quarter, deck L612, L630]. And it recovers fit-out capital across a roughly three year cycle in which client lock-in, landlord lock-in and payback are meant to align [this quarter, deck L468-499]. The unit economics the model rests on: a center breaks even at 55% to 60% occupancy, reaches steady state at 85% to 90%, and management claims fit-out capex is fully recovered inside the lock-in [this quarter, deck L466-492]. The single largest cash cost, landlord rent, is ₹190 Cr this quarter and does not appear in the Ind AS EBITDA margin of 61%, which is why that number is misleading and why the honest cash read is Adjusted Cash EBIT of ₹75 Cr, up 44% [this quarter, deck L351, L727]. Model drift to watch this quarter: the profit growth leaned on one-time VAS revenue that jumped from ₹7 Cr to ₹39 Cr [this quarter, deck L617-623], and the lock-in figure is stated inconsistently as three years, 3.5 years and 38 months across the same deck [this quarter, deck L468/L708/L869], which matters because the whole payback claim depends on it. The IPO cash that was meant to fund new centers is being partly redirected toward broad, undescribed real estate objects [this quarter, monitoring L357-362], a shift in how the model is being financed.

### 4. COMPETITION INTELLIGENCE

IndiQube competes with Awfis (listed), Smartworks, EFC and WeWork India in Indian managed workspace [Notion prior work]. Where it wins: a large-format, long-stay enterprise and GCC book rather than short-tenure freelancer desks, 137 centers and 10.61 Mn sqft of area under management concentrated in Bangalore (76 centers, 6.68 Mn sqft) with a hub-and-spoke supply strategy [this quarter, deck L379, L573]. It presents net cash of ₹66 Cr post-IPO, which is a stronger balance sheet than debt-funded peers on the management ex-lease basis [this quarter, deck L196]. Where it is structurally weaker: the model carries full landlord lease risk on 15 to 20 year terms, so a demand slowdown hurts more than an asset-light broker, and the statutory loss makes the equity story harder to tell than a peer that reports IGAAP-style profits directly. The competitive risk to watch is supply discipline. Every operator is racing to lock future supply, and IndiQube has 2.77 Mn sqft of LOI pipeline converting over 12 to 18 months on top of the current book [this quarter, deck L409]. If peers over-build in the same Bangalore and Tier II micromarkets, occupancy and pricing compress across the sector at once.
