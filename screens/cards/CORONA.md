## Business Understanding Narrative

Corona Remedies sells branded medicine to Indian doctors. It does not discover
molecules and it does not chase the United States. It builds brands in known
chemistry, and it pays a field force to put those brands in front of
prescribers. Revenue is the number of medical representatives, times how
productive each one is, times how long a patient stays on the drug. Gufic
Biosciences appears as a contract manufacturer (doc 6JrGfR p.22), so some
production sits outside the company's own plants.

What changed is the kind of prescription. In FY2013 the mix was 70 acute to 30
chronic. In FY2026 it was 28 acute to 72 chronic (doc b5rkW7 p.13). Chronic
therapies were 71.9% of FY26 revenue and 72.3% of Q4FY26 revenue (doc ltHusO
p.5). That is the whole thesis in one line. An acute prescription is a cough
syrup bought once. A chronic prescription is a cardiac or diabetes drug refilled
every month for years. The second is an annuity. The first is a sale.

Why now is the harder question, and the honest answer is that the climb already
happened. It ran over thirteen years and it finished before the company listed.
The forward story is smaller and more ordinary: more field force, a few
acquisitions, and a first attempt at exports. Management targets 15% organic
revenue growth plus 1.5% to 2% inorganic, so about 17% revenue growth and 20%
PAT growth in FY27 (doc MUyBwX p.7). It adds 200 to 250 medical representatives
a year, 6% to 8% of the force (doc MUyBwX p.9). It bought Wokadine from Dr
Reddy's with internal accruals, entering a povidone iodine market it sizes at
about Rs 648 cr, where the brand ranks second (doc ltHusO p.5).

What must be true for the thesis to work is that 20% PAT growth is enough. The
chronic shift is banked. The market can see it. Nothing in the reachable record
suggests a second re-rating is coming.

What breaks it is the multiple. FY26 adjusted PAT was Rs 199.4 cr (doc ltHusO
p.5). Market cap is Rs 12,761 cr. The market already pays a large number for
each rupee of that profit. If growth runs at 20% and the multiple does not rise,
the return is 20%. The operator's hurdle is 25%.

## 2. Business model and archetype

Corona sells formulations into the Indian pharmaceutical market and keeps 90%
plus of revenue at home. International was about 3% and management wants high
single digits in three to four years (doc MUyBwX p.15).

Archetype: brand and franchise consumer. Pricing comes from the prescription
habit, distribution comes from the field force, and the gross margin comes from
formulation rather than chemistry.

The one economic engine is prescriber capture in chronic therapy. A doctor who
starts a patient on a chronic brand rarely switches. The field force wins the
first script and the disease keeps paying for it. FY26 EBITDA margin was 20.9%
and PAT margin 14.2% (doc ltHusO p.5). Those margins say the brands hold price.

Manufacturing sits at Bhayla in Gujarat. Capacity rose by 400 Mn units in
December 2025 to 1.65 Bn units a year (doc ltHusO p.5).

## 3. Competitive advantages

Present, and of the ordinary Indian pharma kind rather than the rare kind.

The moat is the field force plus the brand. Neither is unique. Every listed
Indian formulations company owns some version of both. What separates them is
productivity per representative, and management makes that the explicit lever:
grow market share by "improving MR productivity and expanding prescriber base"
(doc ltHusO p.25).

The chronic mix is the real advantage. Chronic revenue repeats without being
resold. At 72% of revenue the base is unusually sticky for a company this size.

Switching cost is the patient, not the doctor. A stable cardiac patient is not
moved to a rival brand without reason. That is a genuine barrier and it is
earned one prescription at a time.

Cost advantage: none evidenced. Scarcity: none. The EAEU-GMP certificate at
Bhayla, granted January 2026, is a regulatory key rather than a moat (doc
ltHusO p.5).

Verdict: moat present, not wide, and normal for the sector.

## 6. The transition and the quality ladder

FROM R3, value-added supplier. TO R4, franchise and share of wallet leader.

The mechanism is prescription annuity. Moving the book from 30% chronic to 72%
chronic converts one-off sales into repeat revenue, which raises both the margin
and its durability. FY26 EBITDA at 20.9% and PAT at 14.2% sit in R4 territory.

Management's claim, quoted once: "Shifted Portfolio Mix from 70:30 to 28:72
(Acute:Chronic) from FY 2013 to FY 2026" (doc b5rkW7 p.13).

Read the rung-jump base rate against this. One rung per two to three years is
normal. This company took thirteen years to move one rung. That is slow, which
means it is real. Nothing here looks like a leap that needs explaining away.

The problem for an investor is timing. The climb is finished, not starting.

## 12. Verdict card

**WATCH.** Do not spend a full `/step1` at this price.

This is a good business that finished its climb before the operator could buy
it. The chronic transition is delivered, the margins are real, insiders are
buying, and none of that is the problem. The problem is that the market already
knows. On management's own guidance the name compounds at 20%, and the strategy
needs 25%. Without a re-rating there is no gap to close, and the recognition
gap is where the alpha in this framework lives.

  would say the guidance is a floor rather than a target.
- Evidence that international revenue is converting faster than the 3% to high
  single digit path, which would add a growth engine the current price does not
  reflect.
- Reported PAT close to adjusted PAT, which would make the earnings base cleaner
  than it currently reads.

No valuation, no target price and no position is set here. The verdict decides
