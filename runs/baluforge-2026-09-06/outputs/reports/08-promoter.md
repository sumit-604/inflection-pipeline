### 1D. History before the current company — the shell-company origin (VERIFIED, web, 2 independent sources)

The listed vehicle "Balu Forge Industries Limited" is **not** an IPO company. Corporate history (CIN L29100MH1989PLC255933, confirmed AR-anchored at Annual Report FY2025, p.42 and p.66):
- Incorporated March 1989 as **Bell Agromachina Limited** (agricultural machinery).
- Renamed **Amaze Entertech Limited** on 16-Nov-2016 (entertainment technology) — a business-model pivot with no operating continuity, the classic pattern of a "vehicle" company. 📰 (goodreturns.in company-name-change record; corroborated by BSE's own company page and MarketScreener corporate-action record, cross-checked, 2 independent sources).
- On 3-Aug-2020, Jaspalsingh Prehladsingh Chandock (then proprietor of "M/s. Balu India," his sole-proprietorship forging business) launched an open offer to acquire a 26% stake in the then-dormant Amaze Entertech Limited from its existing promoters Shivputra G. Bellad, Prashant Shivputra Bellad and others, for approximately Rs 20 crore (INR 200 million). 📰 (MarketScreener corporate-action record; SEBI-format Letter of Offer hosted at systematixgroup.in, both independently found).
- Renamed **Balu Forge Industries Limited** on 30-Sep-2020.
- Business Succession Agreement dated 3-Aug-2021: the company acquired the operating forging business of M/s. Balu India (Chandock's proprietorship) — this is how the current operating business entered the listed shell. 📰 (company-history summary corroborated across two independent aggregator sources).

**Assessment (flag, prominent):** this is precisely the pattern this stage's priority list anticipated. There was no prospectus because there was no IPO — control of a twice-renamed, near-dormant micro-cap shell (a company that had gone from agri-machinery to "entertainment tech" with no disclosed operating business in between) was acquired via open offer, and the promoter's own private forging business was then injected into it. This explains the corpus's absent-prospectus gap structurally, not just as a filing omission. It is not, by itself, proof of wrongdoing — reverse mergers into listed shells are a recognized (if scrutinized) route to listing in India — but the combination of a dormant-shell entry route with the Section 1C/2C findings below raises the bar for independent verification of the operating numbers. No evidence was found (nor refuted) that Amaze Entertech / Bell Agromachina was ever named on SEBI's 2017 list of 331 suspected shell companies under the Graded Surveillance Measure; this is marked NOT FOUND, not cleared.
