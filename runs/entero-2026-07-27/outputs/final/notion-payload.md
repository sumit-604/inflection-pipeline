## BUSINESS UNDERSTANDING NARRATIVE (read first, before the verdict card)

Entero buys pharmaceutical and healthcare products from about 3,300
manufacturers and resells them in smaller lots to retail pharmacies and
hospitals across India. Traded goods are 99.22% of FY26 revenue; a
manufacturer marketing-support fee is 0.50%, the only other stream, and it
sits well under 10% of sales. The physical service is bulk-breaking, storage
across 136 warehouses in 21 states, and last-mile delivery on 30 to 60 day
credit. A pharmacy cannot stock from 3,300 factories directly, and a factory
cannot reach every chemist, so the middleman step is hard to skip. Since FY26
the same network also moves MedTech goods, IVD analysers, cardiology devices
and consumables, which carry a fatter margin than pills. The customers are
about 105,300 retail pharmacies, 3,600 hospitals, sub-wholesalers and
diagnostic labs. They reorder often but multi-source across distributors, so
switching costs are low, and no single-customer concentration figure is
disclosed. Present demand rides Indian Pharmaceutical Market consumption, read
directly through IQVIA and AIOCD IPM MAT growth; hospital MedTech demand tracks
hospital-chain bed-count and capex, read through Apollo, Max and Fortis
releases, while Apollo HealthCo and Keimed disclosures mark the organised-sector
consolidation pace, and Sun Pharma, Cipla and Alkem commentary corroborates the
IPM read. Demand should grow because organised distributors held 5-10% of the
market in FY23 and are guided toward 20-30% by FY28, a formalisation shift the
GST e-way bill pharma data can proxy, while MedTech mix is guided from about
15% toward 20% over two to three years; NPPA and DPCO price notifications on
NLEM SKUs cut the other way and can compress the spread. The supply-chain
network is the stated core moat and scored Moderate, but its own reach metrics
fell 28-36% in one quarter and stayed unreconciled, so the moat reads weaker
than claimed. Guidance-delivery execution and the working-capital improvement
trajectory are the other active edges, and being an industry-consolidation
beneficiary is the strongest tailwind, though it is sector-wide, not
company-specific. There is no moat on the core pharma trade spread, an 8-15%
band set by the manufacturer-retailer chain and not by Entero. No structural
barrier stops a well-capitalised rival replicating the roll-up while organised
share is still only 8-10%, so the cannibalisation-barrier test scored zero. The
four digital platforms, Entero Direct, Teqtic, HealthEdge and the Enteropreneur
Programme, disclose zero adoption metrics, so their lock-in is unproven.
