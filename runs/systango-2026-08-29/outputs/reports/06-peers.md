## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

### 2A. Demand environment
Split by sub-segment, not uniform. InfoBeans and Ksolves (ERP/AI/cloud/data
services) both report currently healthy-to-strong demand in FY26: InfoBeans
added ~200 headcount in Q4FY26 which management called "the fulfillment of
demand" rather than anticipation (May-2026 call), and reported "good demand
from all directions" (Nov-2025 call). Ksolves states "demand across our core
services offering remains healthy, particularly in ERP, cloud, data
engineering, AI, Salesforce" (Jan-2026 call), though Q4FY26 sequential growth
slowed to 1.7% specifically because war-related conditions delayed UAE
customer purchase-order releases (May-2026 call) — a geopolitical friction,
not a demand-quality problem. OnwardTec (engineering services, automotive-
heavy) reports a tougher backdrop: one analyst characterized FY26 as "a tough
year like FY'26... for the industry" in which "Onward has outperformed almost
everyone" (Oct-2025 call), and OnwardTec confirms its own FY25 EBITDA was
"low single-digit" before recovering in FY26. Its transportation/auto vertical
de-grew 1% due to "the overall slowdown in the automotive sector" and OEM
project cancellations in Europe (May-2026, Jul-2026 calls). Net: demand
softness in this peer set concentrates in automotive/mechanical engineering,
not in digital/AI/ERP services — closer to Systango's own archetype, the
peer evidence leans toward current health rather than distress, though this
is FY26 data, not FY23/H1FY24.

### 2B. Pricing and input costs
No peer reports pricing erosion. Ksolves states explicitly: "we have not seen
pricing pressure from clients. Instead clients are focused on improving
output and accelerating... programs with AI rather than negotiating lower
rates" (Jan-2026 call). OnwardTec reports revenue-per-employee rising from
$8-9 lakhs to ~$22 lakhs over four years, attributing gains to value-chain
movement and offshore-mix shift rather than price cuts, and states flatly
"AI is affecting IT companies and Onward Technologies is not an IT company"
(Jul-2026 call) — i.e., no AI-driven price compression seen in its segment.
InfoBeans gives no explicit pricing commentary beyond stable margin targets.
None of this maps directly onto Systango's margin claims, but it establishes
that pricing power was not visibly eroding across this peer set in the
current era.

### 2C. Capex/investment cycle
Not a uniform capacity race; each peer is investing or retreating for
different reasons. InfoBeans is expanding (headcount, AI tooling, an IT-park
investment funded from twelve years of accumulated rent savings, May-2026
call). Ksolves is retreating from a diversification bet: after spending
roughly Rs5-6cr on events/marketing and ~Rs2cr on its DFM data-product over
FY26, management concluded "we could not generate revenue from that" and
committed to "focus only on services, not on the product" for FY27 (May-2026
call) — a defensive pullback from product diversification, not expansion.
OnwardTec is doing both: opening a fourth design centre in Pune (Aug-2026)
and expanding its Chennai AI lab, while simultaneously completing its first-
ever share buyback (Jul-2026 call), signalling confidence in cash generation
alongside continued delivery-capacity investment. Call: this is a MIXED,
segment-specific picture — not an industry-wide capacity race and not a
uniform retreat. Ksolves' retreat from a failed product bet is the most
directly relevant data point for Systango, whose own diversification
attempts (blockchain) followed a similar arc.

## PART 5: CROSS-PEER HYPOTHESIS

Hypothesis: Indian micro-cap IT/digital-engineering peers appear to follow
a recurring roughly 2-3-year "sprint, consolidate, retreat-to-core" cycle
that is structural to the segment's balance-sheet depth, not a
company-specific strategic choice, even though each peer frames its own
version as a one-off decision.

The same shape appears independently in all three peers, each triggered by
a different cause and on a slightly different clock: InfoBeans doubled
revenue FY21-23 (pandemic-era digital-spend boom), invested in a blockchain
diversification FY23-24, found insufficient traction, exited, and
stagnated at ~Rs400cr for two years before a FY26 breakout funded by
re-focusing on core AI/ERP delivery. Ksolves ran high-30s-percent margins
for years, then in FY26 spent heavily on ESOPs, events, branding, and a
data-product diversification (DFM), admitted the product "could not
generate revenue," and is reverting to "focus only on services" for FY27,
with margins guided back toward the historical 25-30% band. OnwardTec exited
Tier-1/Tier-2 automotive client relationships roughly 18 months ago, ran
"low single-digit" EBITDA in FY25 during the reset, then delivered a
record 13.2% margin in FY26 by concentrating on its strategic top-25
accounts rather than broadening its client base.

In each case: rapid growth or high margin, funded partly by a
diversification or client-broadening bet -> the bet fails to pay off or
the client base proves too broad to service profitably -> an 18-24 month
margin/growth reset while the company writes off or winds back the bet ->
recovery driven specifically by retreating to a narrower, better-understood
core rather than by the diversification succeeding. No single peer states
this as a pattern; each presents its own cycle as a considered strategic
choice. The combined read suggests Indian micro-cap IT/digital-engineering
companies structurally lack the capital and management depth to run core
growth and meaningful diversification at the same time, forcing a repeated
retreat-to-core rhythm rather than sustained multi-line expansion.

Testable implication for Systango: if its blockchain retreat follows the
same shape (bet, insufficient traction, exit, reset, core-focused
recovery) on a similar 18-24 month clock, that argues the retreat is
segment-structural rather than a company-specific execution failure — a
materially different read for the transition thesis than if the blockchain
bet is treated as evidence of one-off poor capital allocation. Also
testable: whether any of these three peers' CURRENT new bets (Ksolves'
Agentic-AI DFM 2.0 relaunch, OnwardTec's digital/AI vertical build-out,
InfoBeans' Insane SDD product) survive past 18-24 months before hitting the
same retreat-and-refocus point, or whether one of them breaks the pattern.
