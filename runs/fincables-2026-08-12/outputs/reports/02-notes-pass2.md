## A. THE SINGLE MOST IMPORTANT MISS: CONSOLIDATED FINANCIALS TELL A DIFFERENT
## STORY THAN STANDALONE — PROFIT GROWTH REVERSES INTO A DECLINE

Pass 1 explicitly flagged that the Consolidated Notes (p.163-212) were not
walked given the entity has no subsidiaries (only one Associate and two
Joint Ventures under equity method). That was the single biggest gap. Having
now read the Consolidated Balance Sheet, Statement of P&L, Cash Flow
Statement and Notes 18-33, the gap turns out to hide the most consequential
finding in the entire filing.

🔴 **Consolidated Profit Before Tax fell 16.1% YoY (₹512.05cr FY20 vs
₹610.22cr FY19)** — nearly three times the standalone PBT decline of -5.9%
already flagged in Pass 1's top finding. **Consolidated Profit for the Year
fell 4.0% YoY (₹391.00cr FY20 vs ₹407.47cr FY19)**, and **Consolidated
Basic/Diluted EPS fell 4.0% (₹25.57 FY20 vs ₹26.64 FY19)** (Consolidated
Statement of P&L, p.159). This is the *opposite direction* of the standalone
headline: standalone PAT rose +17.0% (₹402.48cr vs ₹344.09cr) and standalone
EPS rose +17.0% (₹26.32 vs ₹22.50) (Note 37, p.149, already in Pass 1).

**Mechanism**: standalone financials recognise the Associate (Finolex
Industries Ltd, 32.39% stake) only through dividends received — ₹80.39cr in
FY20, up +100.1% YoY (Note 25(b)(i), p.132, Pass 1 Finding #5). Consolidated
financials instead recognise FINCABLES' actual equity-method share of the
Associate's and JVs' profit: **"Share of net profit of an Associate and
Joint Ventures accounted for using the Equity Method" fell 22.7% YoY, from
₹100.38cr in FY19 to ₹77.59cr in FY20** (Consolidated Statement of P&L,
p.159). In other words, the Associate's own underlying profitability
declined in FY20, but FINCABLES paid out (and standalone-booked) a dividend
*larger* than that year's equity-method profit share — flattering the
standalone P&L twice over (once via the dividend timing/size, once via the
Section 115BAA tax-rate-switch benefit already flagged in Pass 1). An
investor reading only the standalone numbers — which are what get quoted as
"the company's results" in most contexts — would conclude earnings grew
strongly in a difficult year; the consolidated numbers, which better reflect
FINCABLES' true ~32% economic interest in Finolex Industries, show earnings
actually contracted.

**Mechanical cross-check (ties out cleanly)**: Consolidated Other Income is
₹91.50cr (FY20) vs standalone Other Income of ₹171.89cr — exactly the
₹80.39cr Associate dividend, which is eliminated on consolidation and
replaced by the equity pick-up line (171.89 − 80.39 = 91.50, matches
Consolidated Note 25 exactly, p.193). This confirms the mechanism is real,
not a reading error.

🟡 **Consolidated tax reconciliation, new line not present standalone**:
"Effect of Tax on undistributed profit" ₹22.20cr (FY19: ₹14.98cr)
(Consolidated Note 20.1.3, p.188) — this is the deferred tax charge
associated with point B below, recognised only at the consolidated level.

## B. NEW: DEFERRED TAX LIABILITY ON UNDISTRIBUTED ASSOCIATE PROFITS
## (CONSOLIDATED ONLY, LARGE, FORWARD-LOOKING)

🟡 Consolidated Note 20.2, note 3 (p.189): "During the year, the Company has
assessed the recognition of a deferred tax liability on the undistributed
profits of an associate due to abolition of dividend distribution tax and
taxability of dividend in the hands of recipient. Based on such assessment,
the Company has recognised additional deferred tax liability of Rs. 19.69
crores on undistributed profits of the associate as at 31st March 2020."
The cumulative balance of this specific line, "Undistributed profit of
associate," is **₹109.93cr at FY20 close (FY19: ₹87.73cr)** (Consolidated
Note 20.2.2, p.189) — this single item is the primary reason Consolidated
net DTL (₹145.99cr) runs more than 4x standalone net DTL (₹36.06cr, Note
20.2, p.128, Pass 1).

This is genuinely new information with two-sided implications: (i) it is
conservative, forward-looking accounting — the Company is proactively
providing for the future tax cost of eventually extracting the Associate's
accumulated undistributed profits via dividend, following the FY20 Finance
Act's abolition of Dividend Distribution Tax and shift to taxing dividends
in recipients' hands; (ii) it is a direct, quantified caveat to Pass 1's
"hidden value" finding on the Finolex Industries stake (book cost ₹151.85cr
vs market value ₹1,570.32cr, Note 5) — a portion of that market-value gap
carries a latent, now partially-provided tax liability if realised through
dividends rather than a sale.

## D. NEW: LEVEL 3 FAIR VALUE RECONCILIATION MISLABELLING — CONFIRMED, NOT
## JUST SUSPECTED

Pass 1 flagged Note 34.1.3 as a possible labelling inconsistency worth Pass
2/3 verification. On closer re-read this pass, the inconsistency is
confirmed and sharper than Pass 1's hedge suggested: the reconciliation
table (Note 34.1.3, p.141) is explicitly headed **"Unlisted shares
irrevocably designated as at FVTOCI"** (i.e., it purports to be the Level 3
/ unquoted-only roll-forward) for both FY20 and FY19, but its actual figures
— Opening ₹103.26cr, Total loss in OCI ₹(34.84)cr, Closing ₹68.42cr for
FY20 — are the TOTAL FVTOCI equity book (quoted + unquoted combined: per
Note 6, FY19 close = ₹77.74cr quoted + ₹25.52cr unquoted = ₹103.26cr; FY20
close = ₹21.98cr quoted + ₹46.44cr unquoted = ₹68.42cr). The true Level
3/unquoted-only movement, confirmed both by Note 6 and by the separate Fair
Value Hierarchy table on the same page (Level 3 total ₹46.44cr FY20 vs
₹25.52cr FY19), actually **rose** by ₹20.92cr — the opposite direction from
what the mislabelled reconciliation table shows (a ₹34.84cr loss). This is a
genuine disclosure-quality defect in the standalone Note 34, not merely a
"worth checking" item.

🟡 **Related, new**: the same underlying JV impairment event is reported at
two different amounts depending which statement is read. Standalone Note
31(s) / Note 5.2 (p.134/p.116) shows FJPS impairment of ₹35.10cr for FY20.

an investor citing "the JV impairment charge" gets a different number
(₹35.10cr vs ₹21.58cr) depending on which financial statement is the
source, without any note cross-referencing this to the reader.

## F. NEW / REFINED: THE FY19 "OTHER EXPENSES" BASE WAS ARTIFICIALLY LOW —
## THE +24.4% YOY JUMP IS PARTLY A BASE-EFFECT ARTIFACT

Pass 1 flagged Other Expenses rising +24.4% YoY (₹274.52cr vs ₹220.65cr,
Note 31, p.134) driven by Electoral Bonds and rising impairment. Missed on
Pass 1: line (f) of the same note, "**Provision Other-Duties/Taxes**," shows
a **credit of ₹(22.16)cr in FY19 and NIL in FY20** (Note 31(f), p.134) — a
one-time prior-year reversal that suppressed the FY19 comparison base.
This is independently cross-referenced in the Segment Reporting footnote:
"*Exclude reversal of Provision other-Duties/Taxes of Rs. Nil (Previous
year Rs.22.16 crores)" (Note 36, capex/depreciation table footnote, p.148).
Adjusting FY19 Other Expenses for this one-time credit (₹220.65cr + ₹22.16cr
≈ ₹242.81cr "normalised" base), the FY20 figure of ₹274.52cr represents
growth of roughly +13.1%, materially less dramatic than the headline
+24.4%. The Electoral Bonds line (₹20.00cr, new) and the rising Impairment
of Financial Assets (₹35.10cr vs ₹18.17cr) remain real, material items in
their own right — this finding does not remove either — but it corrects the
denominator an investor would otherwise use to judge how much of the jump
is "new spending" versus "an unusually low prior-year base."

## G. NEW: GRATUITY ACTUARIAL LOSS IS MOSTLY "EXPERIENCE," NOT DISCOUNT RATE

Pass 1 attributed the FY20 actuarial loss on the gratuity obligation (₹3.51cr,
Note 33, p.136) to the fall in discount rate (7.47% → 6.82%). The note's own
attribution table, read in full this pass, shows otherwise: **only ₹1.33cr
of the ₹3.51cr loss (38%) is "Due to change in Financial Assumptions"**
(which includes the discount rate); **the larger ₹2.18cr (62%) is "Due to
change in Experience"** (Note 33, p.136) — meaning most of this year's
obligation growth reflects real deviations between what actually happened
(salary increases, exits, etc.) and what was assumed, not simply the
mechanical effect of falling G-sec yields. This is a more persistent-pattern
concern than a pure rate-driven revaluation, since experience losses do not
automatically reverse if discount rates recover. Also new: the actuarial
report's forward estimate — "**expected employer's contribution in next
year is Rs. 3.79 crores (previous year Rs. 3.38 crores)**" (Note 33, p.137)
— a specific forward cash-flow figure Pass 1 did not capture.

## H. NEW: THE CSR SHORTFALL CATCH-UP IS A TWO-YEAR-RUNNING PATTERN

Pass 1 flagged FY20's CSR spend of ₹12.27cr (against a ₹8.17cr requirement)
as including a ₹4.10cr catch-up for "earlier years" and suggested this
"may" reflect an FY19 shortfall. Confirmed this pass: **FY19 also carried a
catch-up spend of ₹1.25cr "in respect of earlier years,"** on top of that
year's own ₹8.19cr current-year spend against a ₹7.43cr requirement (Note
31, note 2, "Details of CSR expenditure," p.135; identical table repeated
in Consolidated Note, p.196). The pattern recurred in both years shown, not
just FY20 — evidence of a structural, not one-off, tendency to run slightly
behind the current year's Section 135 obligation and true it up a year
later. Worth a direct management question on why this keeps recurring
rather than treating it as a single-year explanation.
