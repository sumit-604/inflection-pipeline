## The picture in one paragraph

Friday was a quiet repair day after Thursday's sell-off. Big risers beat big
fallers 108 to 24, and about half the market closed up. The index barely moved.
Volume was the thinnest in more than a week, so buyers came back without force.
The long-term floor gained only ten names and still sits under half, with 1,102
stocks above the two-hundred-day line and 1,166 below. The large and midcap
lists above the day's average price more than doubled, so the big end came back
into the day. Midcaps closed under their trend line for a second session, while
small and micro indices stay well above theirs. Sugar stocks, jewellers, small
pharma and pipe makers moved as groups. Fear eased. The repair that began after
the 15 September washout is still alive, still unproven, and ended the week a
little weaker than it began.

under their lines too, and midcap select lost more than three percent on the
week, the worst of any index. The smallcap 250 sits nearly four percent above
its line. The smallcap 50 sits about six percent above. The microcap index sits
about seven percent above. The bottom of the market still carries the trend. The
middle has given ground two days running.

The story of the last two weeks runs like this. On 15 September, 345 stocks fell
four and a half percent in one session. That was the washout. Only 475 stocks
held their twenty-day line that day. The next day was calm. Then buyers came for
five straight sessions, with between 103 and 167 big risers each day. Wednesday
23 September was the best day of the repair on the long lines. Thursday reversed
it, with 85 big fallers against 53 big risers. Friday turned back up, with 108
big risers against 24 big fallers. Buyers won four of the five sessions this
week. They won without a single day of real force.

A thrust day has still not come. In this record, a thrust day means 300 or more
stocks jumping four and a half percent in one session. It is the mark that a
low has been proven by buyers in a hurry. The best day since the washout had
167. Friday had 108. Eight sessions after the washout, the low rests on a steady
climb and not on a surge. A steady climb can hold. It is easier to undo.

The long line barely moved. On Friday 1,102 stocks sat above their
two-hundred-day line and 1,166 below. That is ten more than Thursday. On
Wednesday the count had reached 1,184 and crossed above half for one day. Before
the washout, on 7 September, the count stood at 1,254. On washout day it fell to
1,035. The market has won back about a third of that loss and holds it. A little
under half of all stocks sit in a long uptrend.

The short lines recovered part of Thursday's loss. About 820 stocks sit above
their twenty-day line, up from about 780. About 890 sit above their fifty-day

700. Both sit below Wednesday's highs of about 1,060 and 1,030. All three lines
ended the week a little lower than the Friday before. The repair made ground

Inside the day, the sellers lost their grip again. About one stock in seven

Volume was the story's weak spot. Total turnover fell below its level of a year

Stocks are coiling. A market coils before it picks a direction, and the volume

Fear eased. The India VIX, the market's fear gauge, fell about four percent to

## What would change this read

The constructive read, that the repair from the washout is intact, fails if the
fifty-day count falls back under about 700, its washout-day reading. It fails if
the two-hundred-day count falls under about 1,035, the washout-day level. It fails
if a second washout arrives, 300 or more stocks falling four and a half percent in
one session. It also fails if the count of stocks down twenty percent in five days
keeps climbing past about ten while the VIX rises above its thirty-week average.
That would show a real down tail forming under a calm surface.

The cautious read, that the repair is stalling and the middle is breaking, fails
if the two-hundred-day count climbs back above about 1,150 and stays there. It
fails if the midcap 150 index closes back above its thirty-week line. It fails if
the large-cap list above the day's average price holds above about 60 for several
sessions. It also fails if a thrust day arrives, 300 or more stocks jumping four
and a half percent in one session, on volume above its year-ago level. That would
give the washout low the proof it still lacks.
