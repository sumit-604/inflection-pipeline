## 1. Related Party Transactions — a distinct FY23 rent stream to the promoter individually, missed from Pass 1's RPT table (Annexure Y §B, p.187)

Pass 1's related-party rent table showed ABJ Rub Metalico rent of ₹24.50L (FY25) / ₹16.50L (FY24) / ₹0.00 (FY23) and treated ABJ as the sole related-party landlord. The RHP's actual line-by-line rent table (Annexure Y §B, p.187) shows a **second, separate rent line**:

| Party | FY25 | FY24 | FY23 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mufaddal Najmuddin Deesawala (CMD, individually) | 0.00 | 0.00 | 12.00 (0.16% of revenue) |
| ABJ Rubber Metalico | 24.50 | 16.50 | 0.00 |

The company paid rent of ₹12.00 Lakhs directly to its CMD/Promoter Mufaddal Najmuddin Deesawala in FY23, and this stream stopped exactly as the ABJ Rub Metalico rent stream started in FY24. 🟡 Watch — this is either the same underlying premises with the landlord entity/invoicing changed from the individual promoter to a promoter-controlled enterprise, or two separate promoter-linked leases. Neither the FY23 property rented from Mufaddal individually nor the ABJ premises (already flagged by Pass 1 as unidentified) are reconciled to the Immovable Properties table (Business section, p.116-117). The related-party rent exposure to promoters/promoter entities is therefore continuous across all three restated years, not just FY24-25 as Pass 1's table implied.

## 2. Group Company disclosure directly contradicts the RHP's own stated criterion (Our Group Company section, p.219, cross-ref Annexure Y)

The "Our Group Company" section (p.219) states a company is disclosed as a Group Company if it meets **either**: (a) it is a company "with which there were related party transactions (in accordance with AS-18), as disclosed in the Restated Financial Statements"; **or** (b) it is in the Promoter Group AND has transactions exceeding 10% of revenue. The section then concludes: "there are no company/entity falling under definition of SEBI (ICDR) Regulations, 2018 which are to be identified as group company/entity."

ABJ Rub Metalico is disclosed with related party transactions in the Restated Financial Statements (Annexure Y, p.187-188) — sales of ₹418.32L/₹1,336.68L/₹1,230.21L across FY25/24/23, i.e. up to 16.61% of revenue — which on its face satisfies criterion (a) exactly as worded. 🔴 Red Flag — the Group Company conclusion appears to directly contradict the RHP's own stated test. This is either a drafting error in the Group Company section or an undisclosed/unexplained narrower interpretation of "Group Company" that is not reconciled anywhere in the document. Given ABJ was already flagged by Pass 1 as the single largest, non-arm's-length-certified related party (Section 2, Section 5), its total omission from Group Company disclosure compounds that concern.

## 3. Ownership/control of ABJ Rub Metalico — the single largest related party — is never named (Annexure Y §A, p.187)

Pass 1 flagged the bidirectional trading and rent exposure to ABJ Rub Metalico as a red flag but did not note that **the RHP never discloses which individual controls or owns ABJ Rub Metalico**. It is described only generically throughout as an "Enterprise under significant influence of KMP or their relatives," alongside Deesawala Rubber Industries, Indian Rubber Co and Ace Commercial Equipments — with no promoter/relative name attached to any of the four. 🟡 Watch — for an entity that supplied 16.61% of FY23 revenue and remains a bidirectional trading counterparty with rent payments and no arm's-length certification (Pass 1, Section 2), the absence of a named controlling party is a disclosure gap that compounds the RPT-fairness concern already raised.

## 4. Employee Benefit Expenses — contract labour cost has grown 5.7x in two years while formal EPF/ESI headcount fell (Annexure T, p.184; cross-ref Business section p.115 and Annexure AE §Employees Provident Fund/ESIC data)

Within "Employee Benefit Expenses" (which Pass 1 discussed only at the aggregate level — 18.32% growth FY25, 77.91% growth FY24), the sub-line "Contractor Worker Expenses" shows:

| | FY23 | FY24 | FY25 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Contractor Worker Expenses | 163.42 | 678.53 (+315%) | 927.39 (+37%) |
| Salary to Staff | 154.36 | 160.94 | 186.43 |
| EPF-registered employees | 81 | 37 | 39 |
| ESI-registered employees | 74 | 35 | 29 |

🔴 Red Flag — contract labour cost rose 5.7x over two years (versus 27% revenue growth over the same period) while the number of employees formally registered for EPF and ESI **fell** from 81/74 to 39/29. This pattern — rising reliance on contract labour cost alongside a shrinking formally-registered payroll — is not explained anywhere in the notes and raises two distinct concerns: (i) potential understatement of statutory employee-benefit obligations if contract labour is functionally performing employee roles, and (ii) a labour-cost structure shift that could carry contingent liability risk under the Contract Labour (Regulation & Abolition) Act if any "sham contracting" challenge arose. This is a materially different framing from Pass 1's generic observation that "salary and wages" grew.

## 5. Restated Cash Flow Statement — FY23 "Sale of Fixed Assets" of ₹571.23L appears to be a non-cash CWIP-to-Building capitalisation, not an actual asset sale (Annexure III p.159, cross-ref Annexure G p.177)

The Restated Cash Flow Statement (Annexure III) shows, under investing activities, "Sale of Fixed Assets including of CWIP" of ₹571.23 Lakhs in FY23 — the single largest "sale" figure in any of the three years, dwarfing FY24's real vehicle-disposal figure of ₹98.41 Lakhs (which correctly nets to the ₹72.46L profit on sale of motor car already flagged by Pass 1). Cross-checking Annexure G (PP&E rollforward, p.177): FY23 Capital Work-in-Progress fell from an opening balance of ₹561.77L to a closing balance of ₹0.00 (deduction of ₹571.23L), and Building gross block rose in the same year from ₹124.83L to ₹696.06L (addition of ₹571.23L) — an exact match. 🔴 Red Flag — this indicates the ₹571.23L "sale of fixed assets" cash inflow in the FY23 investing activities is in substance the **non-cash capitalisation of completed CWIP into the Building account**, not a third-party disposal generating cash. Presenting a non-cash internal transfer as a cash "sale" inflow in the Restated Cash Flow Statement is a notes-vs-notes inconsistency that Pass 1 did not identify (Pass 1 accepted the cash flow figures at face value). This does not appear to change the net cash flow from investing activities (the offsetting Purchase of Fixed Assets line would net out), but the gross presentation is potentially misleading if read in isolation, and should be verified with management/auditor before relying on the FY23 investing cash flow breakdown.

## 6. Non-agricultural land purchased for ~₹2 Crore in Jan 2023 remains "not put to use" 2.5+ years later, and its sole disclosed function is loan collateral (Annexure I p.180, cross-ref Annexure B(A) Note 4 p.173, and Business section Immovable Properties table)

Two parcels of non-agricultural vacant land at Sikindlapur, Shivampet, Medak (Survey Nos. 288/1 and 288/2, each ~1.125 acres) were purchased by the Company in January 2023 for ₹1,00,00,000 each (₹2 Crore combined) from unrelated third-party vendors. The Immovable Properties table in the Business section lists both parcels explicitly as "**Land not put to Use**." Annexure B(A) Note 4 confirms these same two survey numbers are pledged as **exclusive collateral charge for the Aditya Birla Finance working capital/capital expenditure facility** (₹1,000 Lakhs, 11.50% p.a.). 🟡 Watch — the Company has held ~₹2 Crore of idle land for over two and a half years with no disclosed operational plan, and its only stated function to date is as loan security. This ties capital that could otherwise fund working capital or capex, and is not discussed anywhere in the notes despite the buildup of finished-goods inventory and stretched receivables that Pass 1 already flagged as consuming cash.

## 7. Unexplained, constant ₹(12.77) Lakh adjustment to opening reserves as of April 1, 2022, carried identically through all three restated years (Annexure V, "Reconciliation of Reserves & Surplus," p.165)

The restatement reconciliation shows a line "Add/(Less):- Cumulative adjustments made in Opening Reserves as on 01-04-2022" of exactly **₹(12.77) Lakhs in FY25, FY24 and FY23 alike**. Every other adjustment line in Annexure V is explained by a specific named cause (gratuity restatement, deferred tax recalculation, prior period income tax, prepaid expense adjustment, depreciation correction, fixed asset write-back). This ₹12.77 Lakh opening-balance adjustment is the only line with **no narrative explanation anywhere in Annexure IV or V**. 🟡 Watch — because it is dated to the opening balance sheet (April 1, 2022), it necessarily originates in a period *before* the earliest restated year (FY23) and its cause is untraceable from the disclosures provided.

## 8. FY23 "Fixed Assets Written Off" of ₹63.57 Lakhs (Annexure W, p.185) has no matching deduction in the Annexure G PP&E rollforward for FY23 (p.177)

Annexure W (Other Expenses — Miscellaneous Expenses) shows a standalone FY23 line "Fixed Assets Written Off — 63.57" (nil in FY24 and FY25). This is distinct from, and much larger than, the ₹1.40 Lakh "Fixed Assets written off write back" restatement adjustment Pass 1 already noted (Annexure V). Cross-checking the FY23 PP&E rollforward (Annexure G): no asset category shows a "deduction during the year" in FY23 (all deduction columns read nil), meaning the ₹63.57 Lakh charged to the P&L as a fixed-asset write-off in FY23 **cannot be traced to any specific asset class in the PP&E note**. 🟡 Watch — either the written-off item was fully depreciated/off-balance-sheet already (e.g., old software, minor tools not separately tracked) or the two notes are simply not cross-referenced; either way, the nature and justification of a ₹63.57 Lakh write-off (18% of FY23 PAT) is not identifiable from the notes as drafted.

## 9. Auditor resignation followed the FY23 audit sign-off by only seven days (Independent Auditor's Examination Report, p.153-154, cross-ref General Information p.56)

Pass 1 flagged the September 12, 2023 resignation of the previous auditor (M/s Sharma & Sastry) as a "mid-year auditor resignation just ahead of the IPO preparation period." This pass adds the precise, sharper fact: the Auditor's Examination Report on the Restated Financial Statements states that the **FY23 standalone financial statements were audited by the outgoing auditor M/s Sharma & Sastry under a report dated September 5, 2023** — meaning the previous auditor signed off on the FY23 numbers and then resigned just **seven days later** (September 12, 2023), citing "paucity of time." 🟡 Watch — a resignation this close on the heels of signing the year's audit report (rather than, say, at the start of the following audit cycle) is a timing pattern investors should specifically ask about, beyond the general observation that a change occurred.

Comparing each director's remuneration-payable closing balance to their annual remuneration for the same year:

| Director | FY23 remuneration / unpaid at YE | FY25 remuneration / unpaid at YE |
|---|---|---|
| Mufaddal Najmuddin Deesawala | 24.00 / 0.00 (0%) | 20.40 / 1.30 (6.4%) |
| Sakina Mufaddal Deesawala | 24.00 / 0.00 (0%) | 30.00 / 1.88 (6.3%) |

The pattern (fully paid in FY24, in arrears in FY23 and FY25) is not explained in the notes and is worth a specific question to management, particularly alongside the working-capital stress already evidenced by the promoter short-term loans, MSME payable stretching and receivables slowdown Pass 1 identified.

## 11. Minor items noted for completeness (individually immaterial, listed for traceability)

- **GST appeal fee does not reconcile to the disclosed pre-deposit.** Annexure O (Other Current Assets, p.182) shows "GST Appeal Fees — 12.15" (FY25 only), while the Litigation section (p.208) separately quantifies the appeal pre-deposit for the sole GST appeal on record (against the ₹89.51L demand) as "10% of the disputed tax i.e., 8,13,742/-" (₹8.14 Lakhs). The ₹4.01 Lakh gap between the two disclosures is not explained. 🟡 Watch.
- **"Loss in Chit Funds" of ₹3.68 Lakhs, FY24 only** (Annexure W, p.185-186) — an unusual disclosure for a manufacturing company, with no counterparty, purpose, or continuing exposure described. 🟡 Watch.
- **Voluntary donations of ₹23.00 Lakhs (FY23, 6.6% of that year's PAT) and ₹11.95 Lakhs (FY24)** recorded in Other Expenses (Annexure W) with no named recipient or purpose, and nil in FY25. Read together with Pass 1's finding that CSR-applicability status was never addressed anywhere in the notes, the scale of FY23's voluntary donation (larger than many years' worth of CSR spend would be, if applicable) is worth a specific management question. 🟡 Watch.
- **Audit fee escalated roughly 10x** from ₹0.40 Lakhs (FY24) to ₹4.00 Lakhs (FY25) (Annexure AE §3, p.191) — largely explainable by IPO-related restatement and peer-review audit work, but the scale of increase is notable and undiscussed. 🟢 Clean (expected in IPO year) but flagged for completeness.
- **"Other Borrowing Cost" within Finance Cost swung sharply and unexplained**: ₹41.51L (FY23) → ₹5.05L (FY24, -88%) → ₹40.14L (FY25, +695%) (Annexure U, p.185). The FY25 rebound is plausibly linked to the multiple new facilities disclosed by Pass 1, but the FY23-to-FY24 collapse has no stated cause. 🟡 Watch.
- **Long-term Fixed Deposits (>12 months) declined ~41% over two years** — ₹248.68L (FY23) → ₹199.70L (FY24) → ₹147.36L (FY25) (Annexure I, p.180) — during the same period that Guarantees given on behalf of the Company rose from Nil to ₹154.39L (Pass 1, Section 3). The notes do not state whether these FDs serve as margin money against the guarantees, so whether guarantee cover is increasingly "naked" cannot be confirmed from the disclosures as drafted. 🟡 Watch.
- **A new, undocumented "Other Loans and Advances" line of ₹54.19 Lakhs appears for the first time in FY25** (Annexure H, Long-Term Loans and Advances, p.180), with no counterparty or purpose disclosed, and no equivalent line in FY24 or FY23. 🟡 Watch.
- **Capital Advances grew to ₹402.68 Lakhs by FY25** (from ₹60.00L in FY23) (Annexure H, p.180), funded before and independent of the IPO. This is consistent with Pass 1's point that no formal capital-commitment note exists to reconcile this against contracted purchase orders, but adds the observation that a substantial capital advance has already been committed using non-IPO funds even though, per the Objects of the Issue disclosures, orders for the specific IPO-funded conveyor-belt machinery had not yet been placed as of the RHP date. 🟡 Watch.
- **Clerical note-numbering duplication**: the letter "Annexure V" is used twice for two entirely different notes — once for "Material Adjustments to the Restated Financial Statements" (p.165-166) and again for "Restated Statement of Depreciation & Amortisation" (p.185). Purely a drafting/labelling issue with no substantive impact, noted only for completeness. 🟢 Clean (cosmetic).
