## Business Understanding Narrative

Laxmi Organic runs two chemical businesses. Essentials makes acetyl intermediates,
chiefly ethyl acetate and acetic anhydride, from acetic acid and ethanol. It is a
scale-and-spread business: buy feedstock, convert it, sell the difference. Specialty
makes diketene derivatives, batch products sold into pharma, agrochemicals, pigments,
printing and coatings, where Laxmi is the world's number three producer (Concall p.7). In
Q1FY27 Essentials was Rs 7,265 Mn of revenue and Specialty Rs 2,418 Mn, so Essentials is
75% of the top line (Concall p.6).

What is changing is a large capex at Dahej that is now finishing. Phase 1 is capitalised
and running, backed by a multi-year contract with a large global MNC. Phase 2, 85% of
whose capex capitalises in Q2FY27, doubles the diketene derivatives capability (Concall
p.6, p.7). Dahej is 60% specialty and 40% essentials by dedication (Concall p.7). Two
more projects sit behind it: project Vaayu with a partner, mechanically complete in early
Q3FY27 with revenue in FY28, and electrochemical fluorination, where the company says it
is India's first and still the leader (Concall p.10).

Why now: the capex cycle is ending. Term debt has peaked near Rs 6,100 Mn at about 0.3x
net debt to equity, FY27 capex drops to Rs 125 to 150 crore, and debt repayment starts
next year (Concall p.5, p.6, p.12).

What must be true: the doubled diketene capacity must qualify at customers in Q3FY27 and
ramp from Q4FY27 into FY28, and specialty margin must return toward the 20% to 25% the
company used to earn (Concall p.6, p.11, p.12).

What breaks it: reading this quarter as the transition. It is not. EBITDA rose 272% year
on year because ethyl acetate spreads went above their 12-year average during the West
Asia crisis (Concall p.5, p.8). Management says Essentials over the cycle earns
mid-single-digit EBITDA and printed 11% to 12% (Concall p.7).

## 2. Business model and archetype
Two archetypes in one company, and they must not be blended.
Essentials is a commodity converter. Revenue is tonnage times price; profit is the spread
between acetic acid or ethanol in and ethyl acetate out. Management names the variables
exactly as the library does: "economies of scales and cost positions" and cycle position
(Concall p.10). It publishes no spread itself but points analysts at the 12-year average
ethyl acetate spread of $215 to $220 (Concall p.8).
Specialty is a value-added supplier. Batch processes, multipurpose reactors, campaign
products, seasonality, and customer qualification cycles (Concall p.7). Revenue rests on
being specified into a customer's product.
The one economic engine of the group today is scale in Essentials paying for the
specialty build. "upcycle benefits larger players who certainly have economies of scale"
(Concall p.5).

## 3. Competitive advantages
**Customer concentration halved, and this is the strongest advantage evidence in the
corpus.** Revenue from the top 10 customers was 20% in FY26, down from 34% in FY23 (sweep
file s.4). A commodity converter that has cut its dependence on its largest buyers by half
in three years has done something real, whatever the spread is doing.
Different in each business, and stronger than the current mix suggests.
In Specialty the advantage is structural: number three globally in diketene derivatives,
with Site 2 at Mahad fully utilised before Dahej was built (Concall p.7). Full
utilisation forced the company to drop the bottom slice of its portfolio, and Dahej lets
it bring back products "which our customers, by the way, still need" (Concall p.11). That
is a real scarcity position. Electrochemical fluorination adds a technology platform
where the company claims first-mover leadership in India (Concall p.10).
In Essentials the advantage is cost position and scale, not price. A world-scale ethyl
acetate plant is now commissioned at Lote (Concall p.10). That wins share through a
cycle; it does not create pricing power. The proof is in management's own answer: when
feedstocks fell about 25%, "customers will seek for some accounting of that in your
prices" (Concall p.11). Even the specialty book gave back price on deflation.
Moat: present and durable in diketene derivatives, absent in Essentials.

## 5. Financial trajectory
Q1FY27 revenue Rs 9,683 Mn, up 40% year on year and 32% sequentially. EBITDA Rs 1,143 Mn,
up 272% year on year and 113% sequentially (Concall p.5). Derived EBITDA margin 11.8%.
Segments: Essentials Rs 7,265 Mn, up 50% year on year and 39% sequentially; Specialty
Rs 2,418 Mn, up 17% and 13% (Concall p.6).
Volume growth at the enterprise level was about 10%; the rest of the 40% came from price
and mix (Concall p.9). So roughly three quarters of the revenue growth, and almost all
of the EBITDA growth, is price.
Balance sheet, as described on the call: term debt peaked at about Rs 6,100 Mn, net debt
to equity about 0.3x (Concall p.5). FY27 capex Rs 125 to 150 crore, all in. Incremental
depreciation of Rs 7 to 7.5 crore a quarter from Q2FY27 as Dahej Phase 2 capitalises.
Debt repayment starts FY28 and runs five years (Concall p.6, p.12). Net working capital
rose in the quarter to secure raw material availability (Concall p.5).
**PAT is now FOUND.** Q1FY27 consolidated PAT is Rs 677.16 million on basic and diluted
EPS of Rs 2.44, and FY26 consolidated PAT is Rs 793.62 million on EPS of Rs 2.87 (board
meeting filing FY2027 Q1, p.9). ROCE, ROE and cash flow remain NOT FOUND.
**The FY28 plan gives the yardstick, and two years in the company is behind it.** Since FY25
the company has said, unchanged across three presentations: "On Consolidated basis the
company plans to achieve RoCE: 20%, Revenue: ~2x and EBITDA: ~2.7x by FY 28", with Essentials
at 8-12% EBITDA margin and Specialties at 20-25%, on Rs 11,000 million of capex (sweep file
s.1).
Progress, arithmetic shown. FY25 revenue was Rs 29,854 million, up 5%, on EBITDA of Rs 2,796
million. FY26 revenue was Rs 28,467 million, which management calls a 6% degrowth. FY26
EBITDA derives to Rs 1,713.89 million (PBT 879.71 plus finance 220.88 plus depreciation
766.35 less other income 153.05), **down 38.7% on FY25**. The FY24 base the plan was set
against implies about Rs 28,432 million of revenue. So **FY26 revenue is flat on FY24 after
two of the plan's four years, against a target to double, and FY26 EBITDA is below the FY24
base against a target to triple** (sweep file s.2).
Read Q1FY27 against that. **Q1FY27 EBITDA of Rs 1,143 million is 67% of the whole of FY26,
in one quarter.** That single ratio states how far outside the run-rate this quarter sits.
One more caution on the PAT line: the holding company moved to a 25.17% tax rate in Q1FY27
after reverting to 34.94% in Q4FY26, and the filing states the tax expense "is not
comparable" with either comparative (board filing p.10, note 5). Part of the PAT jump is a
tax-regime change.
Inflection classification: **one-off, cycle-driven.** This is not an earnings-led
transition and not yet a margin-led one. Essentials printed 11% to 12% EBITDA against a
mid-single-digit through-cycle expectation stated by management, on 75% of revenue, in a
quarter when acetic acid rose about 200% from a March base and ethyl acetate spreads ran
above their 12-year average (Concall p.4, p.7, p.8). Q3FY26 was the mirror image, a quarter
when "margins were under quite a lot of pressure" (Concall p.8).

## 6. The transition and the quality ladder
Management's claim, quoted once: "with these expansions globally, we become the number
three producer for the diketene derivatives. So we wanted to expand our portfolio and
penetration into our existing and new customers. So that has been the thesis" (Concall
p.7).
FROM R2, cost-advantaged converter. Essentials is the majority of revenue, earns a spread,
and is explicitly steered on cycle position and cost curve rank.
TO R3, value-added specialty supplier. Diketene derivatives with a number-three global
position, multi-year MNC contracts, customer qualification gates and 20% to 25% historic
margins is an R3 business, arguably reaching toward R4 on the scarcity of the global
position.
This is a single-rung claim over roughly three years, FY27 qualification to FY29 focus
(Concall p.6). That respects the one-rung-per-2-to-3-years base rate.
The honest caveat: even after Dahej, the mix does not flip. Dahej is 60% specialty and
40% essentials, and specialty is 25% of revenue today. The company is adding a rung, not
leaving one. `/step1` should value it as two businesses, not one blended converter.

## 8. Proof check
The last quarter does not prove the transition. It proves the cycle.
What fired: revenue up 40% and EBITDA up 272%. Essentials volume grew double digits and
the world-scale ethyl acetate plant is commissioned and selling (Concall p.6, p.10). Term
debt peaked, which is a real balance-sheet milestone. Dahej Phase 1 is capitalised and
running.
What has not fired: everything the transition depends on. Specialty grew 17% against
Essentials at 50%, so the mix moved away from the destination this quarter. Specialty
margin is below its 20% to 25% history and management says it will stay there in the
short term (Concall p.12). Dahej Phase 2 has not been qualified at a customer. Project
Vaayu has no revenue until FY28. Electrochemical fluorination has no named partner and
no quantified contribution.
Named proof point: Q3FY27 customer qualification at Dahej Phase 2, then the Q4FY27 ramp.
Second proof point: specialty EBITDA margin printing above the current level with
Essentials margin normalising down. If specialty margin does not lift as Essentials
reverts, group EBITDA falls and the transition is still ahead.

## 9. Flags from the documents
- **The headline quarter is a spread, not a transition.** Essentials at 11% to 12% EBITDA
  against a stated through-cycle mid-single-digit expectation, on 75% of revenue (Concall
  p.7). Roughly 30% of the 40% revenue growth is price, not volume (Concall p.9).
- Management is already flagging the reversal: West Asia 2.0 in Q2, a South China typhoon,
  "too early to say how quarter 2 will pan out" (Concall p.8).
- Specialty lost a product worth 10% of its revenue to phaseout, with no replacement named
  (Concall p.11).
- Specialty pricing gave back feedstock deflation to customers, about 25% (Concall p.11).
  That is weak pricing power in the segment the whole thesis rests on.
- Net working capital rose in the quarter for raw material security (Concall p.5). No
  number given.
- Depreciation steps up Rs 7 to 7.5 crore a quarter from Q2FY27 before Phase 2 revenue
  arrives in Q4FY27 (Concall p.12). Two to three quarters of cost without sales.
- No PAT was disclosed on the call. Revenue and EBITDA only.
- Feedstock volatility is extreme: acetic acid up about 200% from a March base, methanol
  more (Concall p.4).
- Customer buying behaviour has fragmented into overstocking, just-in-time and deferment
  (Concall p.4), which makes near-term volume unreadable.
- A new CFO took office this quarter (Concall p.3). Neutral, but it resets the comparison
  base for disclosure practice.
Not seen: promoter identity and holding, board, pledge, related-party detail, auditor
opinion, contingent liabilities, segment EBITDA in rupees, capacity utilisation numbers.

## 11. Posture: Transition Decision Matrix
- Proof gate: NOT FIRED. The capex is nearly done, which is a milestone, not proof. The
  specialty ramp has not qualified at a customer and specialty margin has not recovered.
- Ugliness: this name's problem is the inverse of ugliness. The optics are currently
  flattering, not ugly: a 272% EBITDA quarter driven by a spread that management says will
  revert. Read against the matrix, the honest classification of the underlying condition
  is ARTIFACT-OF-CLIMB (specialty margin depressed by a product phaseout and feedstock
  deflation while new capacity was built), but it is hidden behind a good-looking print.
- Recognition gap: INDETERMINATE, and specifically dangerous. No price is held. The risk
  the matrix warns about is that the market capitalises a peak-spread quarter, closing the
  gap on earnings that are not repeatable.
Proof NOT FIRED plus ARTIFACT ugliness plus an unresolved gap maps to RESEARCH / WATCH.
But note the specific trap this name is closest to: PRICED NARRATIVE. If the share price
has moved on a cycle quarter while the specialty transition is still two to six quarters
away, the downside is double, because the spread reverts and the multiple compresses.
Establish the price before anything else.

## 12. Verdict card
**PROCEED to `/step1`, with one condition stated up front: normalise Q1FY27 before any
other work.** Laxmi is a genuine R2-to-R3 candidate. It is the world's number three in
diketene derivatives, its Mahad specialty site was fully utilised before Dahej, the
doubling capacity capitalises this quarter, the capex cycle is over, term debt has peaked
at 0.3x net debt to equity, and two further platforms (project Vaayu, electrochemical
fluorination) sit behind. The proof gate has a near date, Q3FY27 qualification and Q4FY27
ramp, which makes a deep run well timed rather than early.
The condition is that this quarter must not be annualised. Management said plainly that
Essentials earns mid-single-digit EBITDA over the cycle and printed 11% to 12%. Any model
built on Rs 1,143 Mn of quarterly EBITDA is modelling a spread.
Load-bearing facts a deep run must verify first:
1. Mid-cycle Essentials EBITDA margin, and the ethyl acetate spread history against the
   $215 to $220 twelve-year average. Restate Q1FY27 at mid-cycle before anything else.
2. Promoter identity, holding, pledge and related-party dealings. The corpus holds none
   of it. This is the biggest hole on the card.
3. Specialty segment EBITDA in rupees, and the size of the phased-out product against the
   current specialty base.
4. Dahej total capex, the returns assumption at sanction, and the Phase 2 capitalised
   asset against the promised diketene doubling.
5. Balance sheet and cash flow: the Rs 6,100 Mn term debt, the FY28 repayment schedule,
   and the working capital that "increased a bit".
6. The multi-year MNC contract behind Dahej Phase 1: counterparty, tenor, volume.
What would change the view: two quarters in which specialty revenue and margin rise while
Essentials margin normalises down would confirm the climb and make this a strong PROCEED.
A Q3 or Q4 in which Essentials margin reverts to mid-single digits and specialty has not
moved would put the transition back a full year, and the name back to WATCH. No price
here.
