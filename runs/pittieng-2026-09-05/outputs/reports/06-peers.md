## PART 1: CLAIM-BY-CLAIM VERIFICATION

### Claim 1
**Claim:** Is BIS-certified electrical steel from Korean and Japanese mills now reliably available, resolving the import-supply uncertainty Pitti cited through FY26?

**Verdict:** CONTRADICTED (with strong product/geography mapping caveats)

**Peer evidence:**
- VILAS is the closest product analog but sources CRGO (transformer grade) via BIS-gated imports mainly from China, with Japan/Korea/Germany/Russia/Poland/Brazil as a diversified basket, not a single Korea+Japan tie-up. Across four calls VILAS shows the BIS-import situation as persistently volatile, not settled.
- Nov-2024 call: government "put up a ban... on the BIS license of the Chinese mills but they have started the Korean mills or BIS again which was kept on the hold" and the company had "recently placed an order for the Poland mill... one Brazilian mill... tie-up a few more mills" (VILAS, H1 FY25 call, Nilesh Patel, PDF p.10) — supply was being patched together from many countries, not resolved by Korea/Japan alone.
- Nov-2025 call (the call closest in time to Pitti's own claim window): "The recent opening of a BIS of Chinese mill into the Indian market has brought a major shift, helping reduce import dependency and improve local availability, while intensifying price competition among domestic players, which is putting some pressure on margins. Over the past few months, CRGO prices have declined by nearly 15-20% from their previous highs" (VILAS, H1 FY26 call, opening remarks, PDF p.5). Supply loosened because of CHINESE mills reopening, not Korea/Japan, and the effect was margin pressure from oversupply, the opposite problem to the one Pitti describes.
- May-2026 call (same month as Pitti's Q4 FY26 call): "There are only two mills has got the BIS approval. Their BIS approval was open for one year in last June. So this June one mill's BIS has been is going to be finished. And July there is one more mill whose BIS is going to be finished. It may be renewed, but we don't know exactly at present will it be renewed or not" (VILAS, FY26 call, Nilesh Patel, PDF p.7). VILAS still imports 75% of raw material, sourced 25% from local mills (same page). Government is also weighing an anti-dumping duty on CRGO imports that "if it will be there, then CRGO price will increase" (PDF p.14, and reiterated PDF p.16) — a live policy risk that would tighten, not ease, supply economics.

**Peers silent:** RKFORGE and SANSERA do not discuss electrical steel, BIS certification, or Korea/Japan steel sourcing at all (different raw material base: forging billet/aluminum/steel bar, not electrical lamination steel).

**Net read:** The one relevant peer shows an import-supply situation defined by rolling uncertainty (BIS licenses with fixed one-year terms, renewal outcomes unknown even one month before expiry, a live anti-dumping-duty threat) rather than a "resolved" state. The specific claim (Korea/Japan tie-ups) is not tested directly — VILAS's own volatility centres on Chinese mills — but the closest analog available shows the underlying BIS-gated import market as still structurally unpredictable through the exact window Pitti was claiming resolution. Weight this cautiously: VILAS makes CRGO (transformer grade) at SME scale; Pitti's problem is mainly CRNGO (motor grade) at a much larger scale, so this is an adjacent-market read, not a direct test of Pitti's specific supplier relationships.

### Claim 2
**Claim:** Do peers report the same India-US tariff relief (Section 232 reciprocal cut 50% to 18%) and the same customer-acquisition tailwind Pitti describes?

**Verdict:** VERIFIED

**Peer evidence:**
- SANSERA directly corroborates the specific 18% figure: "we really don't know yet whether our component falls under 0% or under 18%. I mean 0% would really be a huge potential" (SANSERA, Q3 FY26 call, B.R. Preetham, PDF p.7). The same call's opening remarks: "Following the interim U.S.-India trade deal and EU FTA, we expect a positive impact on both current exports and new opportunities" (PDF p.2).
- SANSERA's Q1 FY27 call confirms the tailwind has converted into actual customer behaviour, not just sentiment: "we are seeing increased momentum from the customers to start sourcing more from Sansera, India, without really waiting for us to start our operations in the U.S. We can see that trend as a stop-gap arrangement, and that is also contributing to increased exports" (SANSERA, Q1 FY27 call, Hari Krishnan, PDF p.7).
- RKFORGE corroborates the direction (tariff relief improving the export environment and re-accelerating customer decisions) though its specific tariff number differs, because it is a different tariff code (automotive components at 25%, not the steel-content Section 232 mechanism Pitti describes): Q2 FY26 call describes tariff confusion up to 50% resolving to a clarified 25% automotive rate by mid-September, after which "we have seen, again, the pullback in terms of shipments have started happening" (RKFORGE, Q2 FY26 call, Naresh Jalan, PDF p.5). By Q1 FY27: "the operating environment has become considerably more stable, providing greater visibility and confidence across our export markets" and export growth guidance of "almost 20% plus" for the year (RKFORGE, Q1 FY27 call, PDF p.3, p.9).

**Peers silent:** VILAS not relevant to US tariffs (its exports are 1-4% of revenue, mostly Gulf/Europe, not the US).

**Net read:** Two independent peers corroborate the direction (tariff relief is real and easing through FY26 into FY27) and the magnitude for SANSERA specifically matches Pitti's own cited 18% figure. RKFORGE's magnitude differs (25% autos vs 18% steel-content) because it sits under a different tariff schedule, which is expected and does not weaken the corroboration of the broader claim. Both peers also independently confirm the customer-acquisition tailwind materialising (SANSERA: India-sourcing preference building even ahead of a US plant decision; RKFORGE: new North American and European customer wins alongside existing-customer recovery).

### Claim 3
**Claim:** Is domestic railway capex actually moderating, even as Pitti's named railway customers (Wabtec, Alstom, Progress Rail, Siemens Mobility) keep growing internationally?

**Verdict:** CONTRADICTED

**Peer evidence:**
- RKFORGE's domestic-Indian-Railways business strengthens sharply across every one of the four calls in the window, the opposite of "moderating": "the Railway segment is gaining healthy traction with our products being integrated into the bogey assemblies. Our newly launched vertical supplying castings to railways has made a promising start" plus "we have bagged orders worth Rs. 200 crores" in castings for railways alone in one quarter (RKFORGE, Q2 FY26 call, PDF p.4).
- Q3 FY26 call: "Indian Railways has started showing a demand worth Rs. 2,000 crores itself for the forthcoming year" and "railway is going to form a strong pillar of growth for us and our order book from railways is extremely strong" (RKFORGE, Q3 FY26 call, Milesh Gandhi / Naresh Jalan, PDF p.5-6).
- Q4 FY26 call gives the clearest hard number: "the share of business from railways grown to 7.5% of revenue in this year against 4.6% a year ago" (RKFORGE, Q4 FY26 call, opening remarks, PDF p.4) — the share nearly doubled in one year.
- Q1 FY27 call: an 80,000-wheel confirmed order from Indian Railways plus a 25,000-wheel JV-partner obligation, trial production underway, "we are already working with our export customers... where we see a lot of traction in terms of wheel requirements" (RKFORGE, Q1 FY27 call, PDF p.6-7).

**Peers silent:** SANSERA and VILAS do not serve Indian Railways and offer no read on this claim.

**Net read:** The one peer with direct exposure to domestic Indian Railways capex shows the opposite of moderation: order inflow, revenue share, and forward guidance all point to acceleration (share nearly doubling year on year, a named Rs 2,000 Cr annual railway opportunity, a wheel-manufacturing JV ramping toward 40,000+ units). Weight the mapping carefully: RKFORGE's railway exposure is domestic wheels, axles, bogies and castings sold largely to or for Indian Railways itself, while Pitti's railway revenue is electrical laminations sold to global rolling-stock OEMs (Wabtec, Alstom, Progress Rail, Siemens Mobility) who may serve domestic or export programmes. The two are adjacent, not identical, segments of the same railway ecosystem. But on the specific macro question (is Indian Railways capex slowing), RKFORGE is the more current and specific data point, and it says no. This reframes Pitti's declining railway revenue share (31.9% to 33% to 28%) as more likely reflecting company-specific mix dilution or share loss inside named accounts than a real end-market slowdown, since the underlying end market that both companies ultimately serve is demonstrably not moderating.

### Claim 4
**Claim:** Do peer forged/cast component makers report the same casting and machining capacity tightness Pitti calls "the biggest bottleneck" (not the order book)?

**Verdict:** PARTIALLY VERIFIED (peer set genuinely splits)

**Peer evidence:**
- SANSERA corroborates a capacity-tightness narrative closely resembling Pitti's, concentrated in ADS (aerospace/defence/semicon) and flagged as coming in forging generally: "forging could be a very, very -- there could be a shortage as we see because last year, we have produced close to 120 million components... we are already seeing that this needs to be 40% increase has to happen in the next couple of years. So we have already on that program" (SANSERA, Q4 FY26 call, B.R. Preetham, PDF p.9). SANSERA is racing to add ADS capacity every quarter (new hangar, special-process facility, 10-15 acres more land, a build-to-suit facility) specifically because it cannot make orders fast enough: "we will run out of space" (Q2 FY26 call, PDF p.7) and by Q1 FY27, "the biggest deterrent" to conversion speed is "availability of the mother machines where the lead time is a big deterrent... sometimes 7, 8, 9 months long" (implicit machine-lead-time bottleneck, SANSERA commentary across Q3 FY26/Q4 FY26 calls).
- RKFORGE shows the opposite picture: substantial idle capacity being deliberately NOT rushed into. Cold forging utilization was 40% in Q2/Q3 FY26, targeted to reach only 80-85% by FY27-end; casting utilization guided to 85-90% (not 100%); and management explicitly throttles back capex once the current wave is absorbed: "this year we are looking at more of consolidation... we are not looking at capex of more than Rs. 300 to Rs. 400 crores maximum to the tune of Rs. 400 crores" (RKFORGE, Q4 FY26 call, Naresh Jalan, PDF p.13). On the existing net block, management itself estimates room to nearly 2.5x current sales before any further capex is needed: "if you look at asset turn of 2.5, it will be somewhere around Rs. 9,000 crores of sales on this if we have a 100% capacity utilization" (RKFORGE, Q1 FY27 call, Lalit Khetan, PDF p.19).

**Peers silent:** VILAS operates at a much smaller scale (12,000 to 36,000 MT capacity) and describes its own capacity constraints as self-imposed and deliberate ("we always focus that it will be slow and steady"), not an industry-wide shortage claim comparable to Pitti's.

**Net read:** The claim only partly holds. SANSERA's evidence supports Pitti's framing in the specific high-growth, high-technology segments (ADS/aerospace-semicon, and forward-looking general forging), where genuine industry-wide capacity tightness driven by long machine lead times is real and openly discussed. RKFORGE's evidence complicates it: a large forged/cast peer just finished a multi-front capex wave and is now consciously moderating further capex because existing capacity, once ramped, comfortably outpaces near-term demand. Pitti's "biggest bottleneck" framing looks more like a company-specific capital-discipline choice (echoing RKFORGE's post-wave caution) than proof of an industry-wide shortage, except in the SANSERA-type high-tech segments Pitti does not itself occupy.

### Claim 6
**Claim:** Do peers report the same working-capital/inventory build feeding a net-debt increase across FY26, and are they unwinding it faster than Pitti's missed promise?

**Verdict:** CONTRADICTED

**Peer evidence:**
- VILAS shows the same underlying trigger (raw-material stocking against BIS/import uncertainty) feeding a working-capital build, but WITHOUT it becoming a net-debt problem: "our working capital side... that number has been going up year-over-year... receivables will definitely increase... around INR15 crores to INR20 crores... working capital day cycle will be close to 95-100 days end-to-end" (VILAS, FY26 call, Aman V exchange, PDF p.7), and VILAS states across every one of its four calls that it remains net-debt free, funding the buildup from IPO proceeds and internal cash, taking only small term loans (Rs 15-20 Cr) for a new product line.
- RKFORGE shows a genuine net-debt run-up of similar shape to Pitti's (debt "shot up as on September '25 to, I think, more than Rs. 2,500 crores" on capex plus a working-capital and creditor swing, RKFORGE, Q2 FY26 call, Lalit Khetan, PDF p.9) but, unlike Pitti, delivers the promised unwind on schedule and ahead of some markers: "debt as on date is about Rs. 2,250 crores... we have achieved already Rs. 350 crores of debt reduction in this quarter" (Q3 FY26 call, PDF p.6), and by Q1 FY27: "last quarter, if you remember INR1,990 crores was the net debt, and it is Rs. 1,900 crores of net debt this quarter" with explicit further working-capital-day targets ("debtor days by at least five to 10 days, inventory days by another five days, and increase the creditors' days by another 10 days," Bharat Shah exchange, RKFORGE Q1 FY27 call, PDF p.21) and operating cash flow "after working capital at about Rs. 840 crores last year and probably is highest in the history of the company" (same call, PDF p.21).
- SANSERA shows no comparable pattern at all: it stays net-debt free throughout (cash position ~INR 3,972 million at FY26-end) and its only working-capital pressure is growth-driven (the ADS segment carries a structurally longer 170-180 day cycle versus 80 days for Auto), not a raw-material/BIS-driven story: "you would have noticed the pressure on the operating cash during the current year. We are down from 12% last year to 11% this year. We believe that this is the initial capital buildup in this segment" (SANSERA, Q4 FY26 call, Vikas Goel, PDF p.16).

**Peers silent:** None; all three peers speak directly to this question.

**Net read:** The claim does not hold as an "industry-wide, company-agnostic" defence for Pitti. Only VILAS shares Pitti's specific trigger (raw-material stocking against import/BIS uncertainty), and even VILAS kept it off the balance sheet by staying net-debt free. RKFORGE shares a comparably sized net-debt run-up (for a different underlying reason: a multi-front capex wave, not raw-material stocking) but is unwinding it faster and on a more disciplined, quantified schedule than Pitti has managed against its own Rs 300 Cr inventory promise. SANSERA shows neither the build nor the debt. Every available peer comparison makes Pitti's slower, missed unwind look more like company-specific execution lag than a shared industry phenomenon.

## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

**2A. Demand environment.** All three peers describe a strongly supportive FY26-27 domestic environment (post-GST-cut consumption bounce, strong two-wheeler/PV/CV volumes, capex-led industrial demand), consistent with Pitti's own framing. RKFORGE and SANSERA both independently describe recovering, then strengthening, US/export demand through FY27 after a rough FY26 tariff-shock period. The one clear split from Pitti's own narrative: Pitti's railway revenue share is falling even as its named international customer roster grows, while RKFORGE's comparable (domestic-facing) railway business is accelerating sharply. That divergence is a genuine, evidenced tension worth carrying into synthesis (also Claim 3 above).

**2B. Pricing and input costs.** RKFORGE describes steel as a clean one-quarter-lag pass-through, but energy/gas and shipping costs are NOT fully pass-through and are actively compressing margin through FY26-27 (West Asia conflict spillover); this closely mirrors Pitti's own "Black Swan" energy-cost framing in Q4 FY26 but suggests the issue is a recurring, multi-quarter risk industry-wide, not a one-off. SANSERA shows broadly stable gross margins with unresolved customer negotiations to pass on aluminum/consumable/labour inflation. VILAS is structurally different: its CRGO margin is explicitly NOT hedged or contractually reset like Pitti's quarterly mechanism; it is opportunistic, rising and falling directly with spot supply/demand tightness ("if you ask about maximum margin, I cannot judge... it purely depends on the supply and demand"). This is a useful contrast: Pitti's own stated mechanism (quarterly price resets matched to quarterly raw-material resets, removing the need for commodity hedging) is a materially more insulated structure than the smaller electrical-steel convertor peer operates under, which somewhat supports Pitti's own claim of margin protection on the commodity side specifically (as distinct from the energy-cost exposure both share).

**2C. Capex cycle.** Not a uniform industry-wide capacity race. SANSERA is a clear, confident capacity racer, continuously adding ADS/aerospace-semicon space funded from strong self-generated cash flow, staying low-leverage throughout. RKFORGE just completed an aggressive multi-front capex wave (casting, aluminium forging, Mexico, wheel JV) funded partly by debt, saw its ROCE and margins compress through the ramp, and is now deliberately pausing/digesting rather than committing to new capex until utilization catches up. VILAS just completed its own 3x capacity expansion and is adding only small, incremental follow-on projects (copper conductors, bushings JV), funded mostly from IPO proceeds and cash, staying debt-light. Pitti's Rs 290 Cr greenfield casting/machining commitment, funded partly through net debt and layered on top of an unresolved working-capital story, sits closer to RKFORGE's debt-funded, margin-compressing playbook than to SANSERA's or VILAS's self-funded, discipline-preserving one.

**2D. Competitive mentions.** No peer transcript names Pitti Engineering or gives an unmistakable description of it. This is an informative silence given genuine but partial product overlap (RKFORGE and SANSERA are auto/rail forged-component makers, not motor-lamination or integrated-assembly suppliers; VILAS is a much smaller, differently-scaled electrical-steel convertor); none treats Pitti as a direct competitor worth naming.

**2E. Risks peers discuss that Pitti does not.**
- Energy/gas cost as a recurring, structural (not one-off) risk tied to the West Asia conflict, requiring ongoing customer force-majeure negotiations (RKFORGE, Q4 FY26 and Q1 FY27 calls); shipping costs and transit times up 15-20% (RKFORGE, Q4 FY26 call, PDF p.13).
- New Labour Code compliance cost, taken as an exceptional charge (RKFORGE, Q3 FY26 call, "exceptional provisioning of gratuity and leave on account of new Labour Code of Rs. 10.43 crores," PDF p.4).
- Industry-wide skilled-labour shortage and attrition risk, named explicitly and repeatedly as a growth constraint (SANSERA, H1 FY26 call, "there is a huge shortage of skilled manpower, both white and blue collared for the aerospace manufacturing industry, both in US and Europe," PDF p.11; and Q4 FY26 call on attrition pressure across its own plants, PDF p.16).
- Single/concentrated-customer dependency named openly as a risk by a peer (SANSERA repeatedly frames its largest ADS semicon customer and its Boeing exposure as concentration risks it manages proactively), a topic Pitti's own three calls never raise despite comparable concentration (2 customers = 25.2% of consolidated revenue per B02).
- A government anti-dumping duty on CRGO imports under active consideration (VILAS, FY26 call, PDF p.14, p.16), a specific, dated policy risk to the same BIS-gated import channel Pitti also depends on, never named on any Pitti call.
- Named, dated BIS-license-renewal risk (specific June/July 2026 expiry) for the Chinese mills that currently anchor VILAS's cheapest import supply (VILAS, FY26 call, PDF p.7) — a level of dated specificity about the same underlying import-BIS mechanism that Pitti's own commentary never provides, despite Pitti's own inventory/net-debt thesis resting on a version of the same mechanism.

## PART 4: TRIANGULATION SUMMARY

- Claims verified: 1 of 6 (Claim 2, tariff relief and customer-acquisition tailwind).
- Claims contradicted: 3 of 6 (Claim 1, BIS Korea/Japan resolution; Claim 3, railway capex moderation; Claim 6, industry-wide WC/debt excuse). These are priority items for synthesis.
- Claims partially verified: 1 of 6 (Claim 4, capacity bottleneck — genuine peer split).
- Claims unverifiable: 1 of 6 (Claim 5, data-centre genset demand — no peer serves this specific product).

**Single most consequential contradiction:** Claim 3. Pitti's own concall frames domestic railway capex as having "moderated to some extent," offered as the explanation for a declining railway revenue share even as its named international customer roster grows. RKFORGE, a peer with direct exposure to the same end market, shows the opposite: railway revenue share nearly doubling (4.6% to 7.5%) and a named Rs 2,000 Cr annual Indian Railways demand opportunity. This reframes Pitti's declining railway share as more likely a company-specific mix or share-loss issue than genuine end-market softness, a materially different read for the transition thesis's railway pillar.

**Strongest independent confirmation:** Claim 2. SANSERA independently and specifically cites the 18% tariff figure Pitti also uses, across two consecutive calls, and confirms the customer-acquisition tailwind has converted into actual increased India-sourcing behaviour by mid-2026, not just sentiment. RKFORGE corroborates the same direction from a different tariff schedule. This is Pitti's best-supported claim of the six tested.

**Overall assessment:** The peer set is genuinely mixed, not uniformly supportive or uniformly hostile. It clearly supports the sector-wide reality of India-US tariff relief as a real, converting customer-acquisition tailwind (Claim 2), which is good news for the thesis's China Plus One pillar. It complicates the capacity-bottleneck framing, since one peer confirms industry-wide tightness in high-tech segments while another shows a large forged/cast peer sitting on deliberately under-utilised capacity. It undercuts three of the more credibility-sensitive claims: the "resolved" framing of the BIS import-supply story (peer evidence shows continued, dated volatility), the "moderating domestic railway capex" explanation for declining railway share (a peer shows the opposite), and any implicit defence that Pitti's missed working-capital/net-debt unwind is an industry-wide, not company-specific, problem (every peer comparison makes Pitti look relatively slower or more exposed). On balance, the peer evidence complicates the main company's narrative more than it supports it, concentrated exactly on the claims where Pitti's own credibility grade (C, per B05) was already weakest.

## PART 5: CROSS-PEER HYPOTHESIS

**Hypothesis:** Among Indian precision-engineering component makers riding the same genuine capex supercycle (railways, defence/aerospace, semicon/AI, China Plus One reshoring), the peers that fund expansion from internally generated cash (SANSERA, VILAS — both staying net-debt free or near it through their respective build-outs) preserve margin and ROCE through the capacity ramp, while the peer that funded parallel, multi-front expansion partly through debt (RKFORGE) saw ROCE and margins compress sharply during the buildout (RKFORGE's own management describes FY26 ROCE of "12% to 15%... just coming back to our old levels," Q1 FY27 call, PDF p.9, versus a stated aspiration of 19-20%+ historically) before recovering only as utilization caught up. Pitti's own analyst-flagged ROCE decline from approximately 19% to 15% over three years, alongside a debt-funded Rs 290 Cr greenfield commitment layered on an already-elevated, unresolved working-capital position, places it on the RKFORGE side of this pattern rather than the SANSERA/VILAS side. This is testable: if the hypothesis holds, Pitti's ROCE should continue to lag until the Rs 290 Cr facility's utilization (targeted 1.2x asset turn) catches up post-Q1 FY30 commissioning, mirroring RKFORGE's own multi-year ROCE recovery path (12-15% in FY26 targeted to 20% only by FY28) rather than resolving quickly. No single peer states this financing-discipline-versus-ROCE-durability pattern explicitly; it only emerges from placing all three balance sheets side by side against their respective capex cycles.
