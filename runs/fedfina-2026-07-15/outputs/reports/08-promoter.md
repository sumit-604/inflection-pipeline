**Assessment:** separating the signal from the noise matters here. The two
independent-director exits (Krishnamurthy, Shah) were scheduled 5-year
statutory term completions under the Companies Act — not distress signals.
The nominee-director changes (Khajuria→Dugar, Juneja's resignation) track a
change of individual at Federal Bank and a PE fund's exit respectively — also
not distress signals in themselves. But the **executive layer** — MD & CEO,
CRO (twice), COO (vacant), two CBOs, plus a new Chairman and a new Company
Secretary, essentially the entire senior operating team — turned over inside
about 18 months. Each replacement to date has been a credible external or
internal hire with checkable credentials, and Parvez Mulla's own pedigree is
strong. The one open gap is the still-vacant COO seat. This concentration of
genuine executive churn, even where individually explained, is a real
key-person and execution-continuity risk during a period when the company is
also digesting a new CEO's strategy — this is the basis for the 🔴 rating
on this dimension below.

named gaps rather than resolved successions. Overall key-person risk is
elevated in the near term (12-24 months) given the breadth of concurrent
change, moderated by the credibility of hires made to date and by Federal
Bank's continued majority ownership and funding backstop.
