## SECTION 1: THE BUSINESS MODEL IN PLAIN ENGLISH

### 1A. One-line description
AMAGI rents out a cloud-based "control room and ad-sales desk" to TV networks, streaming apps and
advertisers, so they can broadcast, distribute and monetize video without owning studio hardware —
charging a mix of per-channel subscription fees, usage fees, and a cut of the advertising revenue
it helps generate (Pros. p.232, 255-256).

### 1B. Money-flow chain for each revenue stream

**Stream 1 — Cloud Modernization (18.71% of FY25 revenue, Pros. p.236):**
[Broadcaster's live/linear feed sitting on aging on-premise hardware] → [Amagi CLOUDPORT/STUDIO/
CLIP/DYNAMIC ingest, schedule, produce and play the channel out from the cloud instead of a
physical control room] → [Delivers a ready-to-air channel signal, replacing capex-heavy hardware]
→ [Content provider/broadcaster pays] → [Fixed fee per channel per month (CLOUDPORT), event-based
fee (DYNAMIC), usage-based fee on hours streamed (CLIP/STUDIO), or monthly/hourly managed-services
fee] (Pros. p.253-255, 259).

**Stream 2 — Streaming Unification (57.14% of FY25 revenue, largest division, Pros. p.236):**
[Content provider's fragmented live/linear/on-demand assets, needing to reach many streaming
formats] → [Amagi PLANNER schedules; Amagi ON DEMAND/CLOUDPORT prep and unify delivery across
SVOD/AVOD/FAST to 350+ distributors in 40+ countries] → [Delivers a single, multi-platform-ready
content/channel feed] → [Content provider pays] → [Monthly fee scaled by number of scheduling
users/automation complexity (PLANNER); volume-of-content fee (ON DEMAND); fixed fee per channel
(CLOUDPORT)] (Pros. p.253-256, 14680).

**Stream 3 — Monetization & Marketplace (24.15% of FY25 revenue, Pros. p.236):**
[Distributor/content-owner ad inventory + advertiser demand] → [Amagi THUNDERSTORM inserts
targeted ads server-side; Amagi ADS PLUS matches CTV inventory to demand-side buyers; Amagi
CONNECT syndicates content across FAST/OTT platforms] → [Delivers monetized ad impressions to
viewers and yield/analytics to customers] → [Content owners AND advertisers both pay] → [Tiered
usage-based billing on ad impressions (THUNDERSTORM); revenue-share/inventory-share fees from both
sides (ADS PLUS); fixed listing fee + variable distribution fee (CONNECT); monthly access fee
(ANALYTICS)] (Pros. p.255-256).

### 1E. The chai-stall-uncle version
Imagine every TV channel used to need its own giant satellite truck and control room just to go on
air — expensive, and one truck per channel. Amagi is like a shared, rentable "cloud control room"
that any TV network or streaming app can plug into instead of buying its own truck; it charges rent
per channel, or by usage. It also runs a matchmaking counter between advertisers and channels,
taking a cut every time an ad plays to a viewer. The more channels and apps plug in, the more
valuable that shared control room becomes to advertisers looking for eyeballs, and the more
advertisers who show up, the more channels want to plug in too — a virtuous cycle Amagi calls its
"flywheel" (Pros. p.243-244). Because it owns almost no physical equipment itself (its own "trucks"
sit at ₹158 million against ₹13.5 billion of total assets), nearly every extra rupee of rent it
collects falls straight to profit once the software is built (Pros. p.97).

industry that itself is still in the early-penetration phase of a structural (not cyclical) shift

### 3D. Unit economics — the physics of the business

- **Define one unit:** one channel delivered/managed (the KPI the company itself tracks as
  "number of deliveries," and the unit Amagi CLOUDPORT's core pricing is denominated in — "fixed
  fee per channel per month") (Pros. p.255, 13908-13912).
- **Revenue per unit:** NOT FOUND — pricing is bespoke/negotiated per customer and not disclosed
  as a per-channel rupee figure (Pros. p.255 states pricing is "bespoke ... across our solutions").
- **Cost per unit:** NOT FOUND — cloud infrastructure cost is disclosed only in aggregate
  (technology and cloud infrastructure costs = 28.59% of revenue, FY25) not per-channel (Pros.
  p.59).
- **Volume drivers:** number of channels/deliveries (+46.08% CAGR FY23-FY25), number of
  distributors (+26.68% CAGR), ad impressions monetized, hours of content processed (Pros. p.242).
- **Price drivers:** cross-sell/up-sell across the three divisions ("Expand" strategy), shift
  toward platform-based standardized pricing tiers, ad CPM/fill-rate improvements via zero-slate
  and AI-driven yield optimization (Pros. p.249, 254).
- **Cost drivers:** cloud infrastructure (hyperscaler committed spend, ~27-29% of revenue) and
  people cost (employee benefits expense, ~55-60% of revenue) are the two dominant cost drivers;
  R&D fell from 32.84% to 25.97% of revenue FY24→FY25 and S&M fell from 44.07% to 29.79%,
  demonstrating leverage as the customer base scales (Pros. p.24966-24973).
- **Incremental margin / operating leverage:** demonstrated and improving — gross margin rose
  from 64.73% (FY23) to 69.60% (H1FY26); combined opex ratio fell from 85.47% (FY23) to 67.30%
  (FY25) and to 61.34% (H1FY26 vs 73.14% in H1FY25) (Pros. p.240, 13812-13813). Each incremental
  revenue dollar is now carrying materially lower S&M and R&D intensity than two years ago — the
  core evidence that this is a genuine SaaS-style operating-leverage story, now crossing into
  positive Adjusted EBITDA (FY25) and positive PAT (FY26, first year) (Inv. Pres. slide 3).

9. Top-10 / top-1 customer concentration — good: stable, <35%/<12%; trouble: rising or a top-10 non-renewal
10. Customer churn among top-10 — good: zero (as on record); trouble: any churn event

12. R&D headcount as % of total workforce — good: maintained ~50-55%; trouble: sharp cuts (product-investment signal)

7. **Q:** What is the expected trajectory of Adjusted OCF/FCF conversion relative to Adjusted EBITDA over the next 2-3 years, given the receivables trend?
   **Reassures:** a committed path to 50%+ EBITDA-to-cash conversion.
