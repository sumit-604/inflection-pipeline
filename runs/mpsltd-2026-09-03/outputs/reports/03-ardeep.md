## 2B. RPT map — extension
The Board's Report (Item 26, p.45) and Form AOC-2 (Annexure-C, p.61) both state: **"NONE; during
the reporting period, there was no material contract or arrangement" with any related party**,
and all RPTs were "in the ordinary course of business and on an arm's length basis." This sits
directly beside the 761% surge in intercompany "rendering of services" billed to MPS North
America LLC (a wholly-owned subsidiary, ₹434.24 lacs → ₹3,737.81 lacs, B02 Finding 2). This is
not a compliance breach — transactions between a holding company and a wholly-owned
subsidiary in the ordinary course do not typically trigger Section 188/Regulation 23 "material
RPT" disclosure thresholds — but the magnitude change (a 761% one-year jump) sitting next to a
blanket "no material RPT" characterization is a disclosure-adequacy question worth an analyst
follow-up, not a governance violation. RPT as % of standalone revenue: the recharge alone
(₹3,737.81 lacs) = 8.53% of standalone revenue from operations (₹43,825.57 lacs) in FY26, up
from 1.24% in FY25 (₹434.24 lacs / ₹35,133.52 lacs) — a genuine step-change in RPT dependence
at the standalone level.

## 2H. Exceptional items pattern — NEW finding from this pass
The MD&A's Financial Review (p.29) explains the year's net-positive exceptional items line
(+₹764.24 lacs consolidated) entirely in terms of the two costs management wants credit for
"taking cleanly through the P&L": the labour-code gratuity/leave provisioning impact and the AJE

labeling defect (Finding 4) — B02 found the NOTES imprecisely label the write-back; this finding
is that the MD&A's NARRATIVE about the exceptional-items line omits the two largest,
Corporate-Learning-related components entirely while claiming credit for transparency on the
two smaller, unrelated ones. Carried forward into Phase 6E as the central Quiet Abandonment
finding of this report.
