## Business Understanding Narrative

**What does it do, and how does it make money?** STL Networks, which trades
under the brand Invenia, is the Global Services business demerged from Sterlite
Technologies with effect from 31 March 2025. It builds and integrates fibre
networks: network services, system integration, IT infrastructure, data-centre
setup and network and security operations centres. Revenue is project execution
for telecom operators and enterprises, billed as work is delivered. Its
consolidated accounts include three subsidiaries, Sterlite Technologies UK
Ventures, STL UK Holdco and Clearcomm Group (Updates, FY2027, Q2, page 12).

**What is changing?** The company is shrinking and losing money. Consolidated
revenue fell from Rs 1,179.72 crore in FY25 to Rs 958.96 crore in FY26, down
18.7%. The consolidated loss before exceptional items and tax was Rs 106.15
crore in FY26 (Financial Results, FY2026, Q4, page 17).

**Why now?** There is no positive "now". Q1 FY27 consolidated revenue was Rs
176.15 crore against Rs 189.95 crore a year earlier, down 7.3%, with a loss
before exceptional items and tax of Rs 27.25 crore against Rs 25.36 crore
(Outcome of Board Meeting, FY2027, Q1, page 11). The loss widened.

**What must be true for the thesis to work?** Finance costs must fall or EBITDA
must roughly treble. In FY26, consolidated EBITDA was Rs 47.85 crore and finance
costs were Rs 116.73 crore. **Interest is 2.4 times operating profit.** No
operational improvement short of a transformation closes that gap.

**What breaks it?** It is already broken. The question on this card is not
whether the business inflects. It is whether the balance sheet survives long
enough for the business to matter.

**Archetype: order-book business (EPC/system integration).** Order inflow,
book-to-bill, execution pace, working capital and margin on backlog bind. The
order book is not disclosed.

## 4. The promoters

**Promoter holding percentage: NOT FOUND.** Looked for in the preferential-issue
notice and the SAST disclosures. Bull AI indexes no shareholding pattern.

What is disclosed matters more than the percentage.

**The promoters are putting in Rs 108 crore.** A promoter preferential issue of
warrants was proposed, with "the contribution being made by the Allottees will
be Rs. 108,00,00,000 (Rupees One Hundred Eight Crores only)" (Preferential
Issue, FY2027, Q1, page 17). The company undertakes that none of the company,
its directors or promoters is a wilful defaulter, fraudulent borrower or
fugitive economic offender, and that the shares have been listed more than 90
days so no price re-computation applies (Preferential Issue, FY2027, Q1, pages
17 and 18).

Read against the loss, this is the single most important fact on the card. A
promoter injecting Rs 108 crore into a company burning Rs 106 crore a year
before tax is either conviction or life support. Note the instrument: warrants,
not shares. Warrants are typically 25% paid upfront, so the immediate cash may
be nearer Rs 27 crore, with the rest optional over 18 months. **The promoter has
bought an option to fund, not a commitment to fund.** That distinction must be
resolved in any deep run.

Institutional holders are leaving. Bandhan Mutual Fund sold 2,78,655 shares on
2 January 2026, taking its holding from 5.3635% to 3.3509% (Disclosures under
Reg. 29(2) of SEBI SAST, FY2026, Q4, pages 1 and 3). Total voting capital was Rs
97.58 crore in shares of Rs 2 each, so about 48.79 crore shares.

Four points from the table.

Revenue is lumpy and declining. Q3 FY26 at Rs 335.14 crore was almost double Q4
at Rs 203.08 crore. Project businesses are lumpy, but the annual line fell 18.7%.

Finance costs are relentless and rising: roughly Rs 29 crore to Rs 36 crore
every quarter regardless of revenue. Q1 FY27 finance cost of Rs 35.69 crore
exceeded EBITDA of Rs 9.47 crore by 3.8 times.

Q4 FY26 carried a Rs 27.27 crore depreciation and impairment charge against Rs
2.88 crore the prior quarter, plus a Rs 15.25 crore impairment on financial and
contract assets. The year was cleaned up at the end.

The receivable is frozen. Contract assets and trade receivables at 30 June 2026
include **Rs 155.74 crore and Rs 151.41 crore respectively, unchanged to the
rupee from 31 March 2026** (Updates, FY2027, Q2, page 12; Outcome of Board
Meeting, FY2027, Q1, page 12). Rs 307.15 crore of receivables did not move in a
quarter. The note describes them as "representing receivables from customers
based on the terms and con..." and the text is truncated in the reader, so the
explanation itself was not read. **An identical balance across two reporting
dates on a disputed or specially-termed receivable of Rs 307 crore, against
annual revenue of Rs 959 crore, is the largest single risk on this card.**

**Cash conversion: INDETERMINATE**, and worse than indeterminate. No cash flow
statement is in this corpus, and the frozen receivable suggests a collection
problem the accounts have not yet resolved.

**Inflection classification: negative. Revenue-declining, loss-widening.**

## 8. Proof check

**There is no claim to prove and the numbers move against any possible claim.**

The last two quarters: Q4 FY26 revenue Rs 203.08 crore with EBITDA margin of
3.3% and a Rs 49.56 crore pre-tax loss; Q1 FY27 revenue Rs 176.15 crore with
EBITDA margin of 5.4% and a Rs 27.25 crore pre-tax loss. Margin improved
slightly, revenue fell 13.3% sequentially and 7.3% year on year, and the loss
continued.

The only thing that improved is that Q1 FY27's loss is smaller than Q4 FY26's,
and Q4 carried Rs 42.52 crore of one-off impairments. Adjusting for those, Q4's
underlying loss was about Rs 7 crore and Q1 FY27's was Rs 27.25 crore. **On a
like-for-like basis the loss got worse, not better.**

## 12. Verdict card

**PASS. Do not run `/step1`.**

A loss-making project business with falling revenue, interest at 2.4 times
EBITDA, Rs 307 crore of receivables that did not move in a quarter, no disclosed
order book, no guidance and no earnings call is not a transition-alpha
candidate. It is a balance-sheet restructuring, and the framework has no
machinery for that.

One fact deserves to be stated fairly against the verdict: the promoters have
proposed to put in Rs 108 crore, and they are the party with the most
information. That is a genuine counter-signal and it is why this card ends at
PASS rather than at a recommendation to ignore the name permanently.

Load-bearing facts a deep run would verify first, should the operator override:
1. **The order book**, at any date. Without it nothing forward can be said.
2. **The Rs 307.15 crore of frozen contract assets and receivables.** Read note 3
   in full. Identical balances across two dates need an explanation.
3. **Whether the Rs 108 crore promoter warrant issue was subscribed**, how much
   was paid upfront, and the conversion schedule.
4. **The full FY26 audit report**, specifically whether any going-concern
   emphasis or material-uncertainty paragraph exists.
5. **The UK subsidiaries.** Standalone EBITDA margin is 10.3% and consolidated
   is 5.0%. What the overseas businesses cost, and whether they can be exited.
6. **The CGST demand amount** from Haldia Commissionerate.

What would change the view. Two consecutive quarters of revenue growth with
EBITDA above finance costs, plus the receivable moving. That is a long way from
here.

No price. No position.

The framework's third prohibition applies directly: **do not compute a forward
price-to-earnings ratio for a loss-making company. Write NOT APPLICABLE and name
the gate to profitability instead.**

**The gate to profitability, named.** For FY26, consolidated EBITDA was Rs 47.85
crore, depreciation Rs 37.27 crore and finance costs Rs 116.73 crore. To reach
breakeven before tax the company must close a gap of Rs 106.15 crore. Three
routes exist and each can be sized:

- **EBITDA route.** EBITDA must rise from Rs 47.85 crore to about Rs 154 crore,
  a 3.2-fold increase, with everything else held. At the FY26 EBITDA margin of
  5.0% that needs revenue of roughly Rs 3,080 crore against Rs 958.96 crore
  delivered, which is not plausible.
- **Margin route.** Holding revenue at Rs 958.96 crore, the consolidated EBITDA
  margin must rise from 5.0% to about 16.1%. The standalone entity already earns
  10.3%, so the arithmetic points at the overseas subsidiaries: exiting or
  fixing the businesses that consume roughly Rs 45 crore of EBITDA a year closes
  about 42% of the gap on its own.
- **Balance-sheet route.** Finance costs of Rs 116.73 crore must fall. The Rs
  108 crore promoter warrant issue, if fully subscribed and applied to debt,
  would cut interest by roughly Rs 11 crore to Rs 13 crore a year at typical
  rates, closing about 11% of the gap.

**No single route closes it. Two of the three together might.** The realistic
path is the overseas fix plus the equity injection plus a revenue recovery, and
none of the three is evidenced as underway in this corpus.

Bull AI holds zero management guidance records for this company, so there is no
company view on any of this. That absence is recorded as a finding.

Market capitalisation is the Bull AI company record at 22 September 2026, a
service record, not a filed document. It values the company at 2.3 times FY26
revenue while that revenue is falling and unprofitable.

**The warning this step exists to carry.** The framework warns that a run-rate
built on falling revenue screens well and misleads. Here there is no screen
result to mislead with, and that is the honest outcome. Read this step with
steps 5, 9 and 11, never instead of them.
