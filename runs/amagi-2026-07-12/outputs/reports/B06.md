### Claim 2 — Ad impressions growth (62% YoY) vs. Monetization revenue growth (36% YoY), "modest CPM pressure offset by volume"

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Claim | AMAGI states ad impressions grew 62% YoY vs. Monetization segment revenue growth of only 36% YoY, attributed to "modest CPM pressure offset by volume." Do ad-tech-adjacent peers describe similar CPM/volume divergence? |
| Verdict | **PARTIALLY VERIFIED** |
| Peer evidence | Affle (Q2 FY26 call, Nov 03 2025): CEO Anuj Khanna Sohum, responding to a question about large DSPs reporting lower CPMs, corroborates the underlying mechanic AMAGI describes — "there's a significantly larger number of consumers on connected devices and the amount of time people are spending on connected devices leads to a huge volume of supply of impressions… it is not a surprise that supply is increasing… the commodity or the raw material of our industry, the impressions, will become cheaper." This directly matches AMAGI's implied dynamic of impression/supply volume outpacing monetization. However, Affle differentiates itself on the outcome: "I don't remember any quarter where our CPCU pricing would have seen any kind of a dip. RateGain (both Nov-2025 and Feb-2026 calls) does not discuss CPM or impression-level economics at all; its "transactional volume" commentary (e.g., "almost a 5% growth in our transactional volume in our distribution business," Nov-2025 call) is driven by OTA churn dynamics, not CPM/volume divergence, and is not on-point. |
| Peers silent | RateGain never addresses CPM/impression pricing dynamics in either call reviewed. |
| Net read | Affle corroborates the *direction* of AMAGI's underlying claim — impression/supply volume genuinely is outpacing advertiser budget growth industry-wide — but Affle's own experience contradicts the *inevitability* framing: a well-differentiated platform (Affle's CPCU) reports never having conceded price. This partially verifies the mechanic while softening AMAGI's implicit "this is just how it is" framing; magnitude (62%/36% split specifically) is not testable against any peer number. |

## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

### 2A — Demand environment

The peer set shows a mixed-to-cautious picture across the Oct-2025–May-2026
window, not an unambiguous boom. Newgen reports "muted," then "slightly
recovering," then explicitly "AI-led uncertainty"-driven deferral of large
enterprise deals through its Q1–Q3 FY26 calls (Jul-2025 through Jan-2026),
with license conversion consistently the weak link even as logo-win counts
hold up. Tata Elxsi shows a volatile, segment-by-segment recovery:
transportation strengthens through the year (OEM mix now 77% of that
vertical by Q4 FY26), but healthcare repeatedly disappoints on delayed deal
closures (flagged as "bottomed" in the Jan-2026 call, then missed again in
the Apr-2026 call), and management's FY27 growth aspiration is explicitly
cut mid-stream from "double-digit" to "higher single digit" by the final
transcript, citing "geopolitical situation." Affle and RateGain both report
resilient double-digit-plus growth throughout, but even these two flag
"temporary softness" (Affle, Q4 FY26 call) and organic deferral into January
(RateGain, Q3 FY26 call) tied to the same Jan–Mar 2026 geopolitical window.
No peer in this set describes an unambiguous, industry-wide demand
acceleration matching AMAGI's framing of sustained 25-30% constant-currency
growth — the closest peers describe caution, deferral, or segment-specific
volatility in the same period.

### 2B — Pricing and input costs

Not a close comparable for most of the set — Newgen, RateGain and Tata Elxsi
are services/subscription businesses with no raw-material COGS line
analogous to AMAGI's gross margin structure. Affle is the nearest analog and
insists its per-conversion price (CPCU) has never declined even as impression
supply/volume growth outpaces advertiser budget growth — a partial
corroboration of AMAGI's implied "volume outpacing monetization" dynamic
(see Claim 2), but Affle frames this as evidence of its own pricing power
rather than a shared industry pressure. Tata Elxsi's primary cost-side
commentary is wage inflation (repeated wage-hike basis-point call-outs each
quarter), not pricing power or input costs. RateGain does not address unit
economics/pricing pressure directly, focusing instead on EBITDA-margin
guidance and reinvestment trade-offs.

### 2C — Capex cycle

Split, not a uniform pattern. Affle (self-described "investment" in
data/inventory cost for vertical expansion) and RateGain (Sojern
acquisition, aggressive GTM headcount build, $5-6m invested in sales teams
over 9-12 months) are actively expanding. Newgen ("we have not added
manpower compared to last year… roughly around 100 people less than last
year," Oct-2025 call) and Tata Elxsi (explicitly holding hiring until
utilization reaches 80%+ before adding headcount "in larger numbers," across
all three calls) are in a cautious, capacity-holding posture pending demand
confirmation. This is a **lone-expander-vs-cautious-peer-set split**, not an
industry-wide capacity race — two of four peers are expanding, two are
holding — which argues against reading AMAGI's own investment cadence as
automatically validated or contradicted by sector-wide momentum.

### 2D — Competitive mentions of the main company

No transcript across all 12 reviewed mentions AMAGI by name or by
unmistakable description (no reference to Amagi's cloud playout, FAST/CTV ad
insertion, or streaming-SaaS positioning was found in any Affle, Newgen,
RateGain, or Tata Elxsi call). Tata Elxsi's Media & Communication segment
discusses its own in-house OTT platform ("TEPlay," powering MBC Now in Saudi
Arabia) — an adjacent media-tech capability — but this is not a mention of
AMAGI or a comparison to it. `peer_mentions_of_company` is empty.

### 2E — Risks peers discuss that AMAGI does not

- **AI-driven decision deferral (demand-side risk):** Newgen (Q3 FY26 call,
  Jan 20 2026, CEO Virender Jeet) describes enterprise customers holding off
  on large commitments specifically because of uncertainty about how AI will
  reshape requirements — "customers are also having a re-evaluation in terms
  of how is AI going to impact that area of the business… they are slightly
  unsure about taking large order bets." This is a demand-side risk to any
  AI/agentic product roadmap that AMAGI's own concall does not address for
  its own NEWSPULSE/agentic monetization thesis.
- **M&A integration drag on reported earnings:** RateGain (Q3 FY26 call, Feb
  16 2026, CFO Rohan Mittal) shows reported PAT declining YoY despite EBITDA
  growth, driven by acquisition amortization, deal costs and finance costs
  from the Sojern acquisition — a caution that headline profit metrics can
  mislead investors during M&A integration. AMAGI has ~INR1,664cr of
  undeployed cash and a stated (but guardrail-free, per B05) M&A intent, with
  no comparable caution offered in its own disclosures about how a deal
  would affect reported earnings during integration.
- **Client-side operational risk cascading to vendor revenue:** Tata Elxsi
  (Q2 FY26 call, Oct 09 2025, CEO Manoj Raghavan) discloses a cybersecurity
  incident at a top automotive client that directly delayed projects and
  suppressed constant-currency growth for a full quarter — a third-party
  operational-risk transmission channel that AMAGI's own concalls do not
  address for its own customer base.
- **Fixed-price/outcome-based contract execution risk:** Tata Elxsi (Q4 FY26
  call, Apr 21 2026, Manoj Raghavan) flags that a shift toward fixed-price,
  outcome-based contracts is "a double-edged sword" — "if you do not execute…
  correctly, then it could also lead to revenue leakages and profitability
  dip." Relevant if AMAGI's own contract/monetization mix shifts toward
  outcome-linked SaaS or managed-services pricing; not discussed in AMAGI's
  own transcripts.

## PART 4: TRIANGULATION SUMMARY

- **Claims verified:** 1 of 5 (Claim 4 — 3-year guidance deflection is
  standard sector practice, not an AMAGI-specific problem).
- **Claims partially verified:** 3 of 5 (Claim 2 — CPM/volume mechanic;
  Claim 3 — absolute competitive claims are style-driven, not universal;
  Claim 5 — concentration-disclosure sequencing looks worse than a peer
  comparator).
- **Claims unverifiable:** 1 of 5 (Claim 1 — TAM/SAR framing; no peer
  discloses comparable figures either way).
- **Claims contradicted:** 0 of 5 (these would be priority items for
  synthesis; none found).

**Single most consequential finding (not a formal contradiction):** Affle's
repeated assertion that its own per-conversion pricing "has never seen any
kind of a dip" (Q2 FY26 call, Nov 2025) undercuts the framing — shared by
AMAGI — that modest monetization/CPM pressure from rising volume is simply
an unavoidable industry dynamic. A well-differentiated platform in an
adjacent ad-tech segment reports having avoided exactly the outcome AMAGI
concedes, which should raise (not resolve) the question of whether AMAGI's
"modest CPM pressure" is structural or addressable.

**Single strongest independent confirmation:** Newgen's and Tata Elxsi's
near-identical, repeated, verbatim-similar refusals to give forward
multi-year guidance ("we have never given any guidance… commenting on
guidance may not be the right thing to do" — Newgen, Jul-2025; "we don't
want to give any indicator at this point in time, we don't do that" — Tata
Elxsi, Jan-2026) directly corroborate that AMAGI's identical two-quarter
deflection on 3-year revenue/margin guidance is sector-standard behavior.
This should lower, not raise, the severity of B05's "low-medium" guidance
red flag in synthesis.

**Overall assessment:** The peer set **complicates** more than it either
supports or undercuts AMAGI's narrative. It resolves one flag favorably
(guidance deflection is normal, verified against two of three named peers)
but does not corroborate AMAGI's most distinctive promotional claims — the
quantified $16.9bn TAM/$1.9bn SAR framing has no peer analog to check it
against, and the CEO's "no competition" claim is shown to be a recognizable
founder-CEO rhetorical style (matched almost verbatim by RateGain) rather
than a claim any peer's evidence can validate as true. On customer
concentration, the nearest peer comparator (RateGain) discloses
revenue-impacting customer events with the number attached immediately,
making AMAGI's initial understatement-then-later-reveal of the top-5
renegotiation's margin cost look more like a company-specific disclosure lag
than sector practice. None of the five claims were outright contradicted,
but none of AMAGI's most promotional framings were independently validated
either — the peer evidence is genuinely mixed, symmetric treatment
warranted.
