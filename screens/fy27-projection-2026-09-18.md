## Is Q1 repeatable? The story behind each number

This is the part that decides whether the table above is worth anything.

### KISSHT — repeatable and improving, with the largest tail risk in the set
Q1 PAT Rs 95 crore, up 59% year on year and 16% sequentially.
**Why it repeats.** Profit tracks assets. AUM is Rs 8,001 crore, up 61% year on year and 13%
sequentially, and a loan book does not un-grow in a quarter. Return on average AUM has been 5.3%,
5.0%, 5.05% across three quarters, so the unit economics are stable, not lucky.
**Why it improves.** Three cost lines are falling exactly as the CEO said they would, measured as a
share of average AUM: funding 5.2% to 4.3%, operating expenses 20.0% to 18.0%, impairment 8.8% to
6.8%. Capital adequacy is 40.19%, roughly twice what the book needs, so there is funded headroom.
**Why it could worsen.** Yield fell 5.2 points of AUM year on year while costs fell 4.9, so the
return has been flat, not rising. Impairment at 6.8% of AUM is exactly the size of pre-tax profit at
6.8%, so a credit cycle that lifts losses by half halves the profit.
**On provisioning, I was wrong and the company was right.** I flagged that Stage 3 coverage fell 653
basis points while impairment cost fell, and said the corpus could not separate better assets from
lighter provisioning. Deck page 31 separates them, and I had not read it. Stage 2 ECL coverage rose
from 59.1% to 80.4% over the same five quarters, the management overlay rose from Rs 122 crore to
Rs 136 crore, and combined provision coverage plus overlay on Stage 2 and Stage 3 assets rose from
137% to 150%. Total provisioning went up, not down. The Stage 3 coverage fall is a mix effect as the
Stage 3 stock shrank from 3.6% to 2.2% of the book.
**And the underwriting evidence is better than I credited.** First-EMI-to-DPD-90, the cleanest read on
new origination quality, fell from 1.6% to 0.7% and held there, and page 29 states that series is for
**overall AUM**, not just on-book. So the off-book half is covered on that metric, on bounce rate and
on collection efficiency.
**What genuinely stays unverified.** Stage 3 and net NPA for the off-book 46.4% specifically, the
rupee FLDG exposure outstanding, and vintage or static-pool loss curves by cohort.
**Verdict: the most mechanically supported growth in the set, and better evidenced than I first
recorded.**

### OMNI — repeatable, and two fifths of the step is accounting
Q1 PAT Rs 29.73 crore, up 468% year on year and 1.4% sequentially. **Ignore the 468%.** Q1 FY26 was
Rs 5.23 crore, a collapsed quarter; the sequential comparison against Q4 FY26's Rs 29.33 crore is the
honest one.
**Why it repeats.** The order book is Rs 3,055 crore against FY26 revenue of Rs 511 crore, roughly six
years of backlog, and revenue grew 61.5% year on year. Capacity rose 20.7% in the quarter.
**Why it is flattered.** FY26 depreciation was Rs 481.6 million, about Rs 120 million a quarter. Q1
FY27 was Rs 62.7 million after the change from written-down value to straight line. That is roughly
Rs 57 million a quarter of pre-tax benefit, about Rs 4.3 crore after tax, or Rs 17 crore a year.
Against a projected FY27 increase of Rs 39.6 crore, **about 43% of the growth is the accounting
change, not operations.** The change is permanent, so reported FY27 keeps it; underlying growth is
nearer +28% than +50%.
**Why it could worsen.** Working capital was 294 days at FY26 and 233 annualised in Q1. Return on
capital employed fell to 11.7%. Energy is 77.5% of the order book, and oilfield capital spending is
one of the most cyclical end markets there is.
**Verdict: repeatable, and read +28% rather than +50% as the operating number.**

### MOTISONS — profit repeats, per share it roughly halves
Q1 PAT Rs 11.05 crore, up 37.6%. Q1 is the weakest quarter; Q2 and Q3 together were Rs 47.38 crore of
FY26's Rs 63.71 crore.
**Why it repeats.** The Rs 150 crore QIP put Rs 129.37 crore into inventory inside the June quarter.
In jewellery, inventory is selling capacity, and it lands before the festive quarter.
**The catch nobody should miss.** The QIP issued 13.57 crore shares on 100.18 crore existing, 13.5%
dilution. FY26 earnings per share was Rs 0.65. The base FY27 profit of Rs 87.7 crore on 113.75 crore
shares is **Rs 0.77 a share, up 18.6%, not 37.6%.** Half the headline growth is absorbed by the shares
that funded it.
**Why the driver is unverifiable.** FY26 revenue grew 5.8% while profit grew 47%. That gap is as
consistent with gold revaluation as with operating gain, and the company discloses no volume, no store
count, no same-store sales and no hedging policy. It has held no earnings call in 27 months.
**Verdict: profit growth plausible, per-share growth half of it, and the cause unknowable from
filings.**

### SHAREINDIA — the least repeatable quarter in the set, and management says so
Q1 PAT Rs 124.4 crore, up 47.4% year on year and 114% sequentially. EBITDA margin 44.77%, up 16.7
points from Q4 FY26's 28.11%.
**Why it probably does not repeat.** Broking and trading EBIT went from Rs 1,065 million to Rs 1,885
million on revenue up 7.4%. A 16.7-point margin swing inside one segment in one quarter, in a quarter
management calls volatile, is most consistent with proprietary trading gains. Prop is about half of
profit on management's verbal account and no filing splits it.
**The strongest evidence is management's own guidance.** They guided "around 20% growth during the
current financial year" against a Q1 that grew 47%. Management is telling you Q1 does not annualise.
That is why this row uses the GUIDANCE basis and not the run-rate; the run-rate would give 8.6x and a
number nobody should use.
**What is genuinely compounding.** Margin trading book up 47.8% across five quarters, institutional
clients 144 to 212, PMS at Rs 150 crore from nothing in one quarter, mutual fund customers 12,663 to
19,632. Those are annuity lines and they are real.
**Why it could worsen.** Average daily turnover fell from Rs 109 billion to Rs 90 billion
sequentially. NBFC clients fell 21% year on year. SEBI derivative rules and RBI prop-desk funding
norms are still tightening.
**Verdict: cheapest in the table and the quarter behind it is the softest.**

### KROSS — repeatable, and three capacity events land mid-year
Q1 PAT Rs 13.3 crore, up 24.4%, on revenue up 32.3% and EBITDA up 39.5%.
**Why it repeats.** Demand is from Tata Motors and Ashok Leyland order flow. Axle volumes rose 34%,
tractor and agri about 40%.
**Why it could improve in Q3 and Q4.** Extrusion commercial production started August 2026, the
high-pressure moulding line doubles casting capacity in September 2026, and robotic forging lands the
same month. All three sit inside FY27's second half.
**Why it could worsen.** Management declined to claim market share and said the 34% axle growth was
partly "a spillover of the very strong Q4 of FY26". Depreciation rose 46.7% year on year with Rs 167
crore of seamless tube yet to capitalise, which is why profit growth of 24.4% already lags EBITDA
growth of 39.5%. Q1 EBITDA margin of 12.2% is below FY26's 13.1%. India Ratings forecasts 13% to
13.5%, meaning an outside professional expects no margin expansion at all.
**Verdict: the steadiest base in the set, with the lowest ceiling.**

### RAMRAT — improving, and the year-on-year number misleads badly
Q1 PAT Rs 35.2 crore, up 121% year on year but **down 10% sequentially** from Q4 FY26's Rs 39.2 crore.
**Why the 121% misleads.** FY26's quarterly profits were Rs 15.9, 21.9, 31.6 and 39.2 crore. That is a
ramp as the Bhiwadi copper tube plant commissioned, not seasonality. Comparing a ramped quarter to a
pre-ramp quarter overstates. The run-rate, Rs 35 to 39 crore a quarter, is the honest basis, which is
why this row is projected on run-rate and not on the year-on-year rate.
**Why it improves.** Copper tubes went from 14% to 26% of revenue in five quarters. Bhiwadi is 24,000
tonnes a year against 7,200 before it. CARE puts tube economics at Rs 37,000 to Rs 52,000 per tonne
against Rs 30,000 to Rs 35,000 for wire. A countervailing duty protects the domestic tube market.
**Why it could worsen.** EBITDA margin fell from 5.3% to 4.8% sequentially. The cash conversion cycle
roughly doubled to about 334 days. Gearing is rising and a Rs 700 crore board-approved capex programme
could restart once Bhiwadi stabilises.
**Verdict: improving, and never annualise its year-on-year growth rate.**

### ALGOQUANT — not projectable, and the range is the answer
Q1 PAT Rs 16.52 crore, up 263%. Net gain on fair value changes was 88.9% of revenue.
**Why no projection is honest.** A proprietary trading desk's quarter is a result, not a run-rate.
Nothing filed describes the strategies, the risk limits, the value at risk or any drawdown history.
Q4 FY26's reported Rs 15.88 crore included a Rs 7.06 crore one-off deferred-tax MAT credit; clean, the
FY26 quarters were Rs 4.55, 6.32, 5.99 and 8.82 crore, totalling Rs 25.68 crore. Q1 FY27 at Rs 16.52
crore is 1.9 times the best clean quarter of the prior year.
**The one client line went backwards.** Fees and commission income fell 65% sequentially and 6% year
on year in the first full quarter after both broking and depository licences transferred.
**The range.** If the desk repeats Q1, FY27 is Rs 66.1 crore and the multiple is 27.8x. If it reverts
to FY26's clean level, FY27 is Rs 25.7 crore and the multiple is 71.5x. A 2.6 times spread is not a
forecast.
**Verdict: NOT PROJECTABLE. Do not put a growth number on this one.**

conversion rate**, which is the ORDER-BOOK tier of Amendment 26.1 and harder evidence than a run-rate.
What is genuinely absent is a profit line, so the margin is assumed and labelled.

**Confidence: lowest of the eight.** The revenue base is an unaudited business update that the company

## Answering the question directly

**High growth at a low forward multiple, on this evidence: KISSHT.** Base growth +52% at 14.2x, the
best combination in the set, and the growth rests on the most mechanical driver here, a loan book
compounding at a stable 5.05% return on assets. The price of that is the fattest tail: 92.3% of the
book is unsecured, impairment equals pre-tax profit in size, and half the book's asset quality is
undisclosed.

**Cheapest is SHAREINDIA at 10.4x**, and it is cheap for a reason the table cannot show. The quarter
behind it is the one management implicitly disowns by guiding to a third of its growth rate, and half
the earnings come from a trading desk no filing will let you see.

**Two to discount, for opposite reasons.** Omnitech's +50% is 43% accounting. Ram Ratna's +121%
year-on-year headline is a ramp artefact, and its real number is +30%.

**Do not read this table as a ranking to buy from.** Growth per turn of multiple is a screen, not a
verdict. On earnings quality the two top scorers now separate: Kissht's provisioning and underwriting
hold up on evidence I had not read, while Share India's prop-versus-client split is genuinely absent
from every filing and remains the open question on this table. None of the eight has passed the spear gate, none has a `companies/<TICKER>.md`, and no
Amendment 20 peer table exists because that requires live web this session does not have.
