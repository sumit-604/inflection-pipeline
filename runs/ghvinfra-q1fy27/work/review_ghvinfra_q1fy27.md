   EBITDA margin **expanded +245 bps** (Q1 FY27 12.83% vs Q1 FY26 10.38%, L96).

   margin is ND** — the honest core-margin comparison cannot be made.

3. **Did core operating PBT (ex-Other Income) grow YoY?** **ND — not computable.**
   Other Income is undisclosed, so the single cleanest test of operational health
   cannot be run. This is a first-order gap, not a pass.

4. **What drove the gap between EBITDA growth and PAT growth?** The mechanical
   decomposition (A3-02 / F8): EBITDA margin rose +245 bps yet PAT margin FELL

   revenue (+321 bps)** — i.e., below-EBITDA charges (depreciation + finance

   split between depreciation and finance cost is **ND** — but a finance-cost

   AVOID driver (FY25 CFO -Rs 55.63 Cr). This is the central analytical signal of
   the quarter.

5. **Are D&A and finance costs scaling faster than revenue?** Directionally
   **yes** — the EBITDA->PBT gap tripled in Rs terms (2.03 -> 12.52) and rose
   +321 bps as a share of revenue while revenue grew 171.67%, so the combined
   below-EBITDA charge grew even faster than the top line. Whether that is
   depreciation (capacity ramp) or finance cost (leverage) is **ND** and is the
   key management question (Q2 below).

6. **Is Other Income concentration changing?** **ND** — Other Income is not
   disclosed in either period. Because reported EBITDA may embed Other Income,
   the +245 bps "margin expansion" cannot be cleaned of treasury/other-income
   timing. Flag: the headline margin story is unverifiable.

## 1. SUMMARY NARRATIVE

GHV Infra put out a three-page press release for the June 2026 quarter, not a
full audited results filing. On the numbers it did share, standalone revenue rose
to Rs 218.59 crore from Rs 80.46 crore a year earlier, up about 172 percent, and
reported operating profit (EBITDA) rose to Rs 28.05 crore from Rs 8.35 crore.
Profit after tax rose to Rs 11.25 crore from Rs 4.72 crore. That all looks strong
at first glance. The catch is in the details. Even though the EBITDA margin went
up by 2.45 percentage points, the net-profit margin actually went DOWN, from
5.86 percent to 5.15 percent, a fall of 0.71 points. The reason is that the costs
sitting below EBITDA (interest on debt and depreciation) grew much faster than
sales, eating the whole margin gain and more, and the tax rate crept up to about
27.6 percent, above the normal statutory rate. The company did not disclose how
much of that below-EBITDA cost is interest versus depreciation, which is exactly
the number that would tell us whether this growth is being funded by borrowing.
The release also shows only standalone figures, not consolidated, which matters
because the group results are where payments to the parent company would show up.
It gives a diluted EPS of Rs 1.48 with no prior-year comparison and no share
count, which is a common way to avoid showing a share base that is not
comparable after a reverse merger. On governance, the company added three new
independent directors, including a retired banker who currently advises another
bank on corporate credit, which is worth a second look. For the thesis, nothing
here changes the picture: the company is on our AVOID list (permanently excluded),
and none of the three conditions that would let us reconsider it (independent
customers, the CBI case being closed, or two straight years of positive cash
flow) is addressed or advanced by this document. The honest verdict is that a
press release cannot verify a quarter, so the evidence is insufficient for a full
numbers check, and the company stays AVOID.
