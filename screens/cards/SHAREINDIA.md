## Business Understanding Narrative

Share India is a broker that makes about half its profit trading its own money. The other half
comes from clients. Founded 1994, run from Noida, it now holds 225 branches and franchises across
19 states, 48,061 broking clients and 212 institutional clients (IP p.3). Around it sit twelve
subsidiaries: an RBI-registered NBFC, an insurance broker, a merchant bank, two GIFT City entities,
an algo platform called uTrade, and a high-frequency trading firm called Silverleaf (IP p.8).
Revenue reaches Rs 14,703 million in FY26, of which broking and trading is Rs 13,878 million, or
93.2% (IP p.26, p.21). The conglomerate is wide and very shallow.

The money arrives three ways. Clients pay brokerage on turnover. The proprietary desk takes
arbitrage and market-making spreads with the firm's own capital. And the firm lends, through margin
trading facility at Rs 4,655 million and an NBFC book at Rs 2,682 million (IP p.22, p.23). Asked to
split prop from client, management answered: "when we say 50 to 50% approximately has contributed
the prop, it is in terms of the profitability" (Concall p.12). Volumes run 60% client, 40% prop
(Concall p.12). Eight years ago prop was 85% to 90% (Concall p.13).

What is changing is the mix, and the direction of travel is away from prop. Management calls it "A
Structural Shift in Business Quality", from transaction-dependent revenue to "Platform +
subscription + interest-led revenue" (IP p.15). Abhinav Gupta put the same claim plainly: "we have
been able to change the basic drivers from primarily being a transaction-based business to being
more of a lending-based business" (Concall p.10). The MTF book is the vehicle. It grew 47.8% year on
year (IP p.19), and the two-year target is Rs 1,000 crore from Rs 470 crore (Concall p.5).

Why now: the regulator forced the question. SEBI tightened derivative rules and the RBI restricted
prop-desk funding (Concall p.2). The old engine got harder to run. The firm raised equity, lifted
net worth from Rs 17,468 million in FY24 to Rs 26,350 million in FY26 (IP p.28), and is spending it
on branches, MTF, PMS, AIF and wealth.

What must be true: the lending and fee book must grow fast enough to replace prop profit before
prop profit shrinks. Four new lines all started inside the last four quarters.

What breaks it: the firm pays for all of this with capital, and capital has not earned its keep
yet. Return on equity fell from 24.4% in FY24 to 12.3% in FY26, as profit fell and equity rose 52%.
Revenue has not grown in three years. One very good quarter does not reverse that.

## 2. Business model and archetype
Share India sells access and lends against it. The broking platform takes a cut of turnover, the
prop desk takes a spread, and the lending books take a rate. Average daily turnover ran INR 90
billion in Q1-FY27 against INR 109 billion in Q4-FY26 and INR 88 billion a year before (IP p.22):
turnover is flat and volatile, so the transaction line cannot compound on its own. The lending
lines can. MTF AUM rose from INR 3,150 million to INR 4,655 million across five quarters (IP p.22),
and finance cost rose from INR 900 million in FY24 to INR 1,312 million in FY26 (IP p.26), which is
what a balance sheet business costs.

Archetype: Platform/network is the primary fit. Active users, take rate on transaction, retention
and network density are the right dials, and management uses them. A material Lender slice sits
inside it, and the lender's dials apply to that slice: AUM growth, NIM at 4.44%, GNPA at 4.05% and
NNPA at 2.26% (IP p.10). Declare both. A card that reads this as a single archetype will mis-read
the margin bridge.

## 3. Competitive advantages
Thin, and thinner than the deck claims. Brokerage is a price-taker business; the deck's own words
are "transparent pricing and competitive brokerage structure". The real candidates are three. Net
worth of Rs 2,760 crore lets the firm meet raised capital requirements that smaller brokers cannot:
"Not many small brokers are facing difficulties to meet the additional capital requirement, whereas
we have planned everything in advance" (Concall p.9). That is a scale advantage, and in a
tightening regime it is the most durable one here. Second, the technology stack, Algowire for
low-latency execution, Silverleaf for HFT protocol, uTrade for retail algo (IP p.12). Third, an MTF
presence in tier-3 cities where "brokers... have very limited options" (Concall p.7). Verdict:
forming, not present. None of the three has yet shown up as pricing power in the numbers.

Annualised, Q1 profit puts return on equity near 18%.

Inflection classification: MARGIN-LED, single quarter, source not separable. EBITDA margin moved
16.7 points from Q4-FY26's 28.11% to Q1-FY27's 44.77% on 7.7% sequential revenue growth (IP p.25).
Broking and trading EBIT rose from INR 1,065 million to INR 1,885 million in one quarter on revenue
up 7.4% (IP p.21). A swing that size inside one segment, in a quarter management describes as
volatile, is most consistent with proprietary trading gains. The corpus cannot confirm that,
because no filing splits prop from client. Cash conversion is INDETERMINATE: no cash flow statement
is held.

## 6. The transition and the quality ladder
FROM R1 COMMODITY PRICE-TAKER. Transaction broking has no pricing power, earns a cyclical return
around the cost of capital, and swings with volumes. FY24 to FY26 is exactly that shape.
TO R3 VALUE-ADDED / SPEC'D SUPPLIER. The claim is a platform with proprietary IP, subscription
income and an interest-earning book, where the client stays because the platform holds the
position, the algo and the margin loan.
Mechanism: replace turnover-linked revenue with the MTF spread, PMS and AIF fees, subscription
income from uTrade, and institutional flow. Management's claim, quoted once: "we have been able to
change the basic drivers from primarily being a transaction-based business to being more of a
lending-based business" (Concall p.10).
Ladder note: this is a one-rung claim, R1 to R3 is two. The rung-jump base rate is one rung per two
to three years, so the honest reading is R1 to R2 now, with R3 conditional on the fee and
subscription lines becoming material. Neither is material yet.

## 8. Proof check
Mixed, and the mix matters more than the direction. Four things moved the way the triggers claim,
across five quarters and not one: MTF AUM INR 3,150 to 4,655 million; institutional clients 144 to
212; mutual fund customers 12,663 to 19,632; NBFC ROA 0.48% to 1.71% and GNPA 4.82% to 4.05% (IP
p.22, p.23, p.24). Those are annuity metrics compounding, and they are the strongest evidence on
this card.

Three things move against it. Average daily turnover fell from INR 109 billion to INR 90 billion
sequentially (IP p.22). NBFC clients fell from 47,447 to 37,538 and branches from 80 to 68 across
five quarters, while AUM stayed flat (IP p.23): that is a book being shrunk and cleaned, not a
diversification engine growing. And broking clients grew 3.8% year on year, from 46,301 to 48,061,
which is not a retail franchise scaling.

The proof point, named: the mix has not moved. Broking and trading was 93.2% of FY26 segment
revenue and 94.5% of Q1-FY27 segment revenue (IP p.21, p.26). Every new line together is under 6%.
Prop is still about half of profit. Verdict: the proof gate has NOT FIRED. One quarter of earnings
inflection, against twelve quarters of flat revenue, with the mix unchanged, is not proof.

## 9. Flags from the documents
- **Auditor cannot vouch MTF repayment.** CARO, FY25: "In respect of loans granted under Margin
  Trade Facility ('MTF'), schedule of repayment of principal and payment of interest have not been
  stipulated. In the absence of stipulation of repayment terms, we are unable to comment on the
  regularity of repayment of principal and payment of interest" (AR FY25 p.124). MTF is the stated
  growth engine, so the one book the thesis rests on is the one the auditor will not certify as
  regular. Surfaced, not investigated.
- **The deck's own revenue split is mislabelled.** Deck p.5 prints NBFC 0.82%, Insurance 3.23%,
  Merchant Banking 0.37%, Technology 1.11%. Computed from the segment table on p.21, NBFC is 3.23%,
  Insurance 0.38%, Merchant Banking 1.11% and Technology 0.82%. The five values are right and the
  labels are swapped in two pairs. An error, not a misstatement, but it is on the summary page a
  reader trusts.
- **Prop share is disclosed only verbally.** No filing splits proprietary from client profit. The
  roughly 50% figure exists solely as an answer on a call (Concall p.12).
- **Contingent liabilities are large.** Guarantees of Rs 1,725.75 crore at 31 March 2024, of which
  Rs 1,200.86 crore is bank guarantees to NSE Clearing for margin (AR FY24 p.166). Normal for a
  broker at this size, and it still sits against net worth of that order.
- **Promoter holding is below half**, at 48.62% (IP p.29).
- **Related-party and pledge disclosure absent from the corpus.** Not a finding; a gap.
- **The transcript is misdated** "July 27, 2027" in the filed document (Concall p.2).

## 10. Independent check: the credit rating
PARTIAL. Management states the commercial paper programme carries "the highest short-term rating of
Crisil A1+" (Concall p.3). An A1+ is the top short-term grade and says the agency sees near-term
liquidity as very strong, which is consistent with Rs 26,070 million of bank balances and 0.25x
leverage (IP p.27, p.28). No rationale document exists in Bull AI's inventory for this company, and
CRISIL's site is unreachable from this session, so the agency's view on sustainability, on the
transition, and on the MTF book itself is NOT FOUND. This is one conclusion without its reasoning.
It does not stand in for the rating step the framework asks for.

## 11. Posture: the Transition Decision Matrix
- **Proof gate: NOT FIRED.** Step 8. The annuity metrics compound, the mix does not move, and the
  earnings inflection is one quarter old.
- **Ugliness: ARTIFACT-OF-CLIMB**, with a caveat. Flat revenue and halved return on equity are
  mostly explained by a regulatory shock to the F&O market and by equity raised faster than it could
  be deployed (IP p.28, Concall p.2, p.9). Both are climb costs. The caveat is the NBFC: a book
  losing 21% of its clients over five quarters looks structural, not transitional, and it is one of
  the lines the diversification claim rests on.
- **Recognition gap: OPEN, and narrowing fast.** At the deck's own 30 June figures the market paid
  Rs 2,942 crore for Rs 324 crore of FY26 profit (IP p.26, p.29). Bull AI's identity record puts
  market cap at Rs 4,293 crore on 2026-09-17, about 46% higher, with no new results filed in
  between. The re-rating has begun on one quarter. Treat the gap as open but no longer wide, and
  verify the price live before acting.

**Posture: RESEARCH / WATCH.** Proof not fired, ugliness an artifact, gap open. The framework's
reading: narrative until the proof gate fires. Not a trade; watch the gate.

## 12. Verdict card

**PROCEED to `/step1`.**

The posture says watch. The verdict says the name earns a full run anyway, and the two are not in
conflict: the posture governs position, the verdict governs research spend. This one is worth the
spend because the question is decidable. A firm with Rs 2,760 crore of net worth, 0.25x leverage,
an A1+ short-term rating and eleven dated triggers is not a story stock; it is a company whose
transition can be checked against filings quarter by quarter. Four of the eleven triggers have
already delivered. The bear case is equally concrete: three flat years, halved return on equity,
94.5% revenue concentration in a segment the regulator is actively reshaping, and half the profit
coming from a proprietary desk that no filing will let you see.

**Load-bearing facts a deep run must verify first:**
1. The proprietary versus client split of profit, for FY24 through Q1-FY27. It exists only as a
   verbal "50 to 50%" (Concall p.12). Half the earnings base is unverified. Nothing else on this
   card matters more.
2. What drove Q1-FY27's 16.7-point sequential margin jump (IP p.25). If it is prop trading gains,
   the inflection is not a transition.
3. The MTF book's quality: ageing, concentration, loan-to-value, and what the auditor's inability
   to comment on repayment regularity means in practice (AR FY25 p.124).
4. Whether the NBFC is being deliberately shrunk or is losing customers, and what management intends
   for it (IP p.23).
5. The FY26 audited accounts and cash flow statement, which the corpus does not hold.
6. The live price. The stock appears to have moved about 46% since the quarter these numbers
   describe.

**What would change the view.** To WATCH: a Q2-FY27 that gives back the margin, or a filed split
showing prop above 60% of profit. To a stronger PROCEED: two more quarters where broking and trading
falls below 90% of segment revenue while absolute profit holds, plus MTF past Rs 700 crore and PMS
past Rs 250 crore. That combination would fire the proof gate.
