### PART A — THE FROM STATE

**A1. Archetype.** Both lines fit the Outsourcing partner (CDMO/EMS/IT
services) archetype from the CLAUDE.md library: client concentration,
wallet share, capacity fill, contract stickiness, price per unit (B04).
- Research Services (Discovery + Dedicated Centres + Translational/
  Clinical Research), 61% of FY26 revenue (B04).
- CDMO (Small Molecule + Large Molecule manufacturing), 39% of FY26
  revenue (B04).

**A2. The simple analogy.** Picture a lab and a factory for hire. Global
drug and animal-health companies rent Syngene's scientists by the year and
its plants by the batch, instead of building their own. Syngene runs the
people and the machines; the client owns the molecule and keeps the
upside from it. About three rupees in five today come from renting
scientists. The other two come from renting regulated factory capacity at
Mangalore, Bengaluru, and Baltimore (B04, B09). About 400 companies buy
from Syngene, including 16 of the top 20 global drugmakers by the annual
report's own count, or 14 by CRISIL's count (B04, AR26).

## SECTION 3: BUSINESS UNDERSTANDING NARRATIVE

*(Drafted per prompts/13-synthesis-pipeline.md, BUSINESS UNDERSTANDING
NARRATIVE section, the shared five-question spec. Source: B03/B04/B06/
B07/B09. This is the Halt 1 draft; Stage 13's copy is the version of
record thereafter.)*

Syngene sells two things a global drug or animal-health company would
otherwise have to build itself: rented scientists and rented regulated
factory capacity (B04). Research Services, 61% of FY26 revenue, covers
discovery chemistry and biology, dedicated research centres run for a
single client, and translational and clinical work; a client cannot
easily replace this without years to rebuild the same regulatory approval
and scientific depth Syngene already holds (B04). CDMO, 39% of FY26
revenue, produces small-molecule drug material at Mangalore and biologics
at Unit 3 Bengaluru and Bayview, Maryland; once a drug's manufacturing
process is validated at a specific site under USFDA, EMA, or other
approval, moving it elsewhere is slow and costly, which is why dedicated
centres and validated manufacturing lines carry real switching costs
(B04, B07). The customers are about 400 global pharma, biotech, and
animal-health companies, including 16 of the top 20 pharma companies by
the annual report's count or 14 by CRISIL's (B04). Buying behaviour
splits sharply by line: dedicated-centre and CDMO clients sign long,
sticky contracts (Bristol-Myers Squibb to 2035, Baxter to March 2028),
while open discovery work is bought more transactionally and has proven
price-sensitive enough that clients have moved it to lower-cost rivals
(B04, B07). Two unnamed customers made 35.6% (standalone) to 37.2%
(consolidated) of FY26 revenue; the largest, named only in concall
transcripts, is the animal-health company Zoetis, whose Librela biologics
offtake has stopped (B04). Present demand for open discovery work follows
global biotech venture-funding levels; demand for the largest research
account follows Bristol-Myers Squibb's own R&D spend and pipeline
guidance; demand for the manufacturing line built around Zoetis now
depends on Zoetis's own Librela sales and inventory drawdown, and Zoetis
has said it holds inventory for the next couple of years (B09 downstream
candidates, B05). Demand should grow, if it does, on three named external
signals: BIOSECURE Act implementation status, which can shift Chinese-
sourced manufacturing orders toward Indian suppliers; the global
patent-cliff calendar, which pushes originator drug companies to
outsource manufacturing before losing exclusivity; and US FDA facility
inspection and approval status for Bayview, which gates whether that
plant can sell into the US market at all (B09 downstream candidates).
Peer capacity utilisation and new-contract wins at Aragen, Anthem, Sai
Life, and Piramal will show whether the industry's shared capacity race
is meeting real demand or creating overcapacity (B09, B06). On
competitive advantage: dedicated research centres hold Syngene's one
working moat, built on multi-year co-location switching costs, credited
once in the emerging-moat scan (B07); open discovery work carries no
moat and management's own words call it commoditised (B04, B07). CDMO
holds a real regulatory-licence barrier — USFDA, EMA, UK VMD, and PMDA
facility approvals are genuinely hard to replicate — but that evidence is
entirely supply-side: no named new customer and no disclosed utilisation
exists to show the capacity converts into revenue (B07). The scan found
no talent-asymmetry evidence and no cannibalisation barrier in any line,
and the total emerging-moat score is 19, classified MODEST (B07).

## SECTION 5: PLAIN-LANGUAGE SUMMARY

1. Syngene rents lab scientists and factory capacity to global drug and
   animal-health companies instead of selling its own products (B04).

   biology, dedicated research centres for single clients, and
   translational/clinical work (B04).
3. CDMO made 39% of FY26 sales: contract manufacturing of small-molecule
   drug material at Mangalore and biologics at Unit 3 Bengaluru and
   Bayview, Maryland (B04, B09).

   drugmakers by the annual report's count, or 14 by CRISIL's count (B04,
   AR26).
5. Dedicated-centre clients stay for years because regulatory approvals
   attach to the site: Bristol-Myers Squibb runs to 2035, Baxter to March
   2028 (B04, B07).
6. Two unnamed customers made 37% of FY26 revenue; the largest was the
   animal-health company Zoetis, whose Librela biologics order has
   stopped (B02, B04).
7. Demand for research work follows global biotech venture funding;
   demand for the Zoetis-anchored manufacturing line follows Zoetis's own
   Librela sales and inventory drawdown (B09, B05).
8. Growth from here needs new manufacturing customers to fill Bayview and
   Unit 3, since no other named customer exists yet at either site (B07,
   gate-recommendation.md).
9. A second demand problem surfaced only in the July 2026 call: Research
   Services is losing clients to lower-price competitors, on top of the
   Zoetis loss (B05).
10. Dedicated research centres hold Syngene's one working moat, built on
    years of co-location and switching cost; open discovery work has no
    moat (B04, B07).
11. Manufacturing holds real regulatory approvals, a genuine barrier to
    new entrants, but that strength is supply-side only: no named new
    customer and no disclosed utilisation exists at Unit 3 or Bayview
    (B07).
12. The mental model to test: can new manufacturing customers replace
    Zoetis and lift the company back up the quality ladder, while
    research services stops losing clients on price (Section 2; company
    memory).
13. The fragility read is FRAGILE: several of the variables the bull case
    needs, especially plant utilisation, are never disclosed as a number,
    and one variable, a named non-Zoetis manufacturing customer, can
    alone cap next year's growth (Section 4c).
14. The corpus could not establish Zoetis's peak revenue share, any

    plants built for it (B02, B03).
15. The two biggest open questions: will a named non-Zoetis customer sign
