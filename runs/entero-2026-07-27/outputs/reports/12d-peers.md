## PART 2: FINDINGS

**Finding 1 — MAJOR — RPTECH May-2026, Q5 evidence chain, speaker misattribution.**
B06 Part 1 Q5 states: "RPTECH separately, and independently, corroborates the
underlying logic without matching the exact mechanism: LIT (lifestyle/IT
essentials) is 'a better gross margin product' than PES (RPTECH May-2026 call,
Rajesh Goenka)." In the actual transcript (p.7), this sentence is spoken by the
ANALYST Madhur Rathi as the premise of his question ("Right. And sir, LIT is a
better gross margin product. So if you could help us understand...") — Rajesh
Goenka never says it. This matters because B06's argument rests on "independent
corroboration" from RPTECH management; an analyst's assumption carries far less
evidentiary weight than a management admission. Kapal Pansari's separate
"solution-driven economics... potential to earn higher margins" quote (p.8) is
genuinely his own and still supports the general PARTIALLY VERIFIED direction of
Q5, so the overall verdict likely survives, but the specific "LIT margin premium"
data point B06 leans on is not RPTECH's own claim.

**Finding 2 — MAJOR — RPTECH Nov-2025 mislabeled CITED-ONLY; Part 1 and Part 3 of B06 are internally inconsistent.**
B06's Part 3 coverage map lists RPTECH Nov-2025 as "CITED-ONLY... consistent
with, but does not add new decisive evidence to, any Part 1 verdict." This is
inaccurate on two counts:
(a) B06's own Part 1 Q1 net-read explicitly cites this same call for a
substantive, decisive data point: "10-12% annual market cited, RPTECH Nov-2025
call opening remarks" — verified verbatim on p.2 ("The Indian IT hardware
market continues to grow at 10% to 12% annually"). This is the exact figure
that drives B06's "false-friend risk" finding (RPTECH's PC-market growth range
coincidentally overlapping Entero's claimed IPM range). That is decisive
evidence, not "no new decisive evidence."
(b) The Nov-2025 call also contains "10,000-plus customers in 700-plus towns
and cities of India" (p.4) — the identical network-reach baseline metric B06's
Q8 evidence table says was "repeated... in every call from Feb-2026 through
May-2026." It in fact appears one quarter earlier, in Nov-2025, strengthening
(not weakening) Q8's central finding that RPTECH's reach metrics did not
contract over the window Entero's did — but B06's table undercounts how far
back this steady baseline extends and the Q8 evidence text never cites the
Nov-2025 instance.
Net effect: the underlying analysis in Part 1 is not compromised (if anything
it is slightly under-credited), but Part 3's coverage map — the artifact an
operator or downstream reader is most likely to trust at a glance — misstates
how this call was actually used. This is a documentation self-consistency
defect, not a missed opportunity to strengthen the thesis.

**Finding 3 — MINOR — REDINGTON Feb-2026, Q5 quote blends analyst premise with management response.**
B06 quotes: "'On the Software business side, we do close to 6% gross
margins... the PAT percentage compared to the other business verticals is
higher' (REDINGTON Feb-2026 call, response to Madhur Rathi)." The first clause
("we do close to 6% gross margins") is Madhur Rathi's stated premise in his
question, not S.V. Krishnan's disclosure; Krishnan's actual reply is "you are
right, the gross margin is high. Even the PAT percentage compared to the other
business verticals is higher." Management confirms the direction and magnitude
("you are right"), so the substance is fair, but presenting it as one
continuous management quote overstates how much of the 6% figure was
volunteered by RPTECH versus supplied by the analyst and merely confirmed.

**Finding 4 — MINOR — MEDPLUS Feb-2026, imprecise anchor on "10.5%" SSSG.**
B06 (Part 3 and its cross-reference in Part 1/Part 4) states MEDPLUS SSSG was
"10.5% (Feb-2026 call)." The transcript pages read do not contain this exact
figure spoken by management; only the analyst's paraphrase ("that 10% plus
SSSG") appears in the visible dialogue. The direction (accelerating growth,
attributed to incentive-structure change) is independently confirmed verbatim,
so the qualitative finding stands, but the precise number is not verifiable
from the transcript text as anchored.

**Finding 5 — MINOR — RPTECH Feb-2026, Micron risk overstated as "quantified."**
B06 Part 2E states RPTECH "explicitly quantifies and discusses the business
impact of a principal (Micron) declaring a 'crucial' consumer product line
end-of-life, naming the expected revenue hit and mitigation plan." The
transcript response (Rajesh Goenka, p.10) is qualitative only — "crucial
definitely will be a dent," "we will have a hit," backup plans referenced but
not detailed — with no revenue-hit number ever given. The topic, speaker, and
directional risk disclosure are all confirmed real; "quantifies... naming the
expected revenue hit" overstates the precision actually on record.

No CRITICAL findings. No SUBSTANTIVE-labeled peer was found to lack a real,
locatable citation. No UNVERIFIABLE verdict was found to rest on a fabricated
or upgraded-from-silence peer statement.

## PART 4: SUMMARY

12 of 12 peer transcripts were re-read against B06's coverage map. Every
SUBSTANTIVE-labeled peer-quarter has a real, locatable citation in its
transcript — B06 did not fabricate peer evidence or invent a citation. The
weaknesses found are precision and attribution defects, not fabrication:
one MAJOR speaker misattribution (an RPTECH analyst's premise credited to
RPTECH management, Finding 1), one MAJOR internal inconsistency between B06's
own Part 1 (which substantively used the RPTECH Nov-2025 call) and Part 3
(which labels that same call CITED-ONLY with "no new decisive evidence,"
Finding 2), and three MINOR anchor-precision issues (Findings 3-5). None of
these change any UNVERIFIABLE/PARTIALLY VERIFIED verdict's direction; Finding
1 modestly weakens, and Finding 2 modestly understates, the evidence base for
Q5 and Q8 respectively — both already conservatively graded PARTIALLY VERIFIED
rather than VERIFIED.

On the industry-mismatch question specifically: B06's framing that MEDPLUS
(pharmacy retail) and REDINGTON/RPTECH (tech distribution) are adjacent, not
identical, analogues to Entero's B2B pharma wholesale business is fair and
consistently applied. The six UNVERIFIABLE verdicts on pharma-specific claims
(IPM acceleration, GLP-1 share, Amazon exit, organised-retail counts) are the
honest consequence of that mismatch, not analyst under-effort — in each case
the audit confirmed the peers are in fact silent on the specific Entero claim
being tested, not merely under-mined for evidence that exists. The two
PARTIALLY VERIFIED calls (margin-mix direction; reach-contraction not being
industry-wide) are exactly the two questions where the peer set's
distribution-economics mechanics (not pharma-specific facts) are genuinely
transferable, which is the correct place for B06 to find real signal.
