## Q1. Is cutting-tool demand from automotive/aerospace/defence/railway customers accelerating
through FY25-FY26, or is TOTEM's 7.8-7.9% FY26 growth company-specific?

**Verdict: PARTIALLY VERIFIED**

Peer evidence (concall, structural): Kennametal's Mar-2024 call (the newest, still 2.5yr stale) describes
a genuine India manufacturing capex upcycle: "domestic market is really good... domestic growth on
CNC machines was 46%" (Vijaykrishnan Venkatesan, KENNAMET Mar-2024, p.5-6), aerospace named as
"our best performing and the fastest growing segment for Kennametal India... on a small base"
(Vijaykrishnan, KENNAMET Mar-2024, p.7-8), and auto dependence explicitly falling: "auto dependence
probably now is probably less than 50% from what it used to be [~65%]... the other sectors have grown
faster" (Vijaykrishnan, KENNAMET Mar-2024, p.14-15) — railways named a multi-decade strength
(Vijaykrishnan, KENNAMET Mar-2023, p.23). This corroborates the DIRECTION TOTEM's customer list
implies (aerospace/defence/railway outgrowing auto) but the evidence is dated Mar-2024 and cannot
speak to the specific FY25-FY26 magnitude TOTEM reports.

Peer evidence (concall, Wendt, inside the window): Wendt's FY25 AGM reports its OWN domestic sales
+7% ("higher demand from user industries like auto, auto ancillaries, bearing steel, cutting tools,
resellers", Bhagya Chandra Rao, WENDT AGM, p.3) — modestly BELOW, not above, TOTEM's 7.8-7.9%
domestic growth. This does not corroborate an industry-wide acceleration; if anything it is a mild
complication.

Peer evidence (CSV, not a concall citation, but the only same-quarter data available): Kennametal's
FY26 (Jun-2026 year end) sales grew 29.1% YoY (Rs1,170.3cr to Rs1,510.7cr), with the final quarter
(Apr-Jun 2026, the same calendar quarter as TOTEM's Q1 FY27) up 47.7% YoY (Rs323.4cr to Rs477.6cr).

(Rs52.17cr to Rs71.28cr). TOTEM's own Q1 FY27 print is +28.9%. Three names accelerating in the exact
same quarter is a striking coincidence and the strongest evidence in this corpus that TOTEM's ramp
rides a real, concurrent industry cycle. But Birlaprec (CSV only, no concall) shows the SAME quarter at
only +1.4% YoY (Rs59.62cr to Rs60.45cr) — a genuine complication, not smoothed over.

**Peers silent:** No peer (concall) speaks to the specific FY25-FY26 window's magnitude; Kennametal's
relevant commentary predates it by up to two years.

**Net read:** Structural direction (aerospace/defence/railway outgrowing auto) is corroborated by one

## Q2. What is the HSS-versus-carbide demand and pricing mix trend industry-wide?

**Verdict: PARTIALLY VERIFIED (complicating, not confirming)**

Kennametal explicitly excludes HSS from its own addressable-market framing: "I'm only taking tungsten,
only addressable market... not high-speed steel, not the taps and all that" (Vijaykrishnan, KENNAMET
Mar-2023, p.6-7) — Kennametal does not compete meaningfully in HSS at all, which limits how much its
commentary can say about HSS-vs-carbide SHARE directly. But the same call describes a structural
migration relevant to the question: "over the years... people have been migrating to CNC machines...
[the] single point tool is actually coming down because a lot of people are going for automation"
(Vijaykrishnan, KENNAMET Mar-2023, p.11). Single-point tooling is the classic HSS-adjacent application;
CNC/automation is carbide-adjacent. Read plainly, this is a peer describing a multi-decade structural
shift AWAY from the tooling category associated with HSS and TOWARD carbide/CNC.

This is a genuine complication for TOTEM, not comfortable confirmation: TOTEM's one CLEAN,
delivered growth trigger is the HSS Drills capacity ramp (B05 priority-1 trigger); its unproven,
aspirational trigger is Carbide Taps. If the sector-wide migration is toward carbide, TOTEM's proven
growth story may be riding the SHRINKING side of the mix, while its unproven story sits on the growing
side. Stated plainly per the run's evidenced-not-safe instruction, not softened.

**Peers silent:** Neither peer gives a quantified HSS-vs-carbide mix-shift number for FY25-26 or names
a pricing differential between the two.

**Net read:** One peer's structural framing (not a magnitude, not current-period) complicates rather
than supports TOTEM's proven Drills/HSS growth trigger; it does not resolve the question, but it is the
opposite of reassuring and should be carried forward as a named risk, not dropped for lack of a clean
number.

## Q5. What capex-to-margin payback timeline do peers describe for their own capacity expansions?

**Verdict: PARTIALLY VERIFIED**

Kennametal gives explicit structural numbers across three calls: "20% of the business is on Capex for
us, 80% is on consumables... we get a drag impact [because] a capacity expansion happens with a face
lag of 12 to 15 months. Once the machines are installed and the capacity expansion [occurs]..."
(Vijaykrishnan, KENNAMET Jun-2023, p.5-6) — describing the lag between CUSTOMER capex and
Kennametal's own consumable-tooling demand, the closest available analogue to TOTEM's own
capacity-to-volume claim. Separately, on OWN capacity: "lead time of any machine capacity, machine
installation or ordering to execution would be around 10 months maximum" (Suresh Reddy, KENNAMET
May-2023, p.13), and the Mar-2024 call describes roughly a four-quarter (~12-month) realised lag
between the Nov-2022 new-plant completion and "a sequential improvement in our PBT" becoming
visible (Vijaykrishnan, KENNAMET Mar-2024, p.5-6).

Read against TOTEM: Drills CNC capacity was delivered within FY26 (per B05/AR); the margin jump
appears in Q1 FY27, roughly within Kennametal's 10-15 month structural band, though on the fast side
of it if the capacity additions were completed mid-to-late FY26 rather than at FY26's start.

Wendt gives a genuinely complicating counter-data-point rather than confirmation: it made its largest-
ever capex (Rs58.29cr, up from Rs11.15cr) in FY25, explicitly "critical for future growth" (Bhagya
Chandra Rao, WENDT AGM, p.4) — and the very next reported quarter (Q1 FY26) shows its MACHINE
TOOLS segment DOWN 18% YoY, with total PBT down 34% ("decrease in profit is due to lower order...
and amortisation of Wendt brand", Ninad Gadgil, WENDT AGM, p.19). Capex does not guarantee an
immediate or even near-term payback for this peer; the payback can visibly lag or reverse in the short
run.

**Peers silent:** Neither peer states an explicit, formal "capex-to-margin payback period" as a stated
policy number; both are qualitative/structural.

**Net read:** One peer (Kennametal) gives a usable structural benchmark (roughly 10-15 months);
another (Wendt) shows that a large capacity investment can produce a WORSE near-term result before
any payback shows, which tempers, rather than confirms, a clean read of TOTEM's Q1 FY27 margin
jump as an obviously on-schedule capex payoff.

## Q6. How much of steel/carbide raw-material cost inflation could peers pass through to customers in
2023-2025? (check_peers: KENNAMETAL INDIA only)

**Verdict: PARTIALLY VERIFIED**

Kennametal states repeatedly, across the corpus window (2023-2024, stale relative to TOTEM's FY26),
that it has structural pricing power and uses it: "Sanjay, You're absolutely right. If you look at our input
raw material cost, the price increases, yes, we did transfer that price increase because we are well
positioned as a technology player in the market" (Vijaykrishnan, KENNAMET Mar-2023, p.10). By
Mar-2024, with tungsten prices actually FALLING 8-9%, the same management describes symmetric,
negotiated (not list-price) pass-through: "we don't make a list price change" but "wherever it is the
standard... customers may come up and ask for additional discounts... we may also have to offer
discounts" (Suresh Reddy/Vijaykrishnan, KENNAMET Mar-2024, p.19-20). This is a peer describing
consistent, structural pass-through CAPABILITY across a full commodity cycle — a materially more
confident posture than TOTEM's own weakening language ("successfully passed on", FY24 AR, to
"trying to pass on as much... as possible", FY26 AR, per B05).

The temporal mismatch is real: Kennametal's commentary is 2023-early 2024, before TOTEM's FY26
"abnormal" commodity spike (Apr-2025 to Mar-2026); no peer concall exists inside that specific window.
CSV cross-check (not a citation): Kennametal's own reported raw-material-cost-to-sales ratio (as filed,
unadjusted for the separate change-in-inventory line) rose from 55.4% (FY25, Jun-2025) to 67.4% (FY26,
Jun-2026) even as PBT margin IMPROVED (12.0% to 17.6%) — consistent with a peer that, unlike TOTEM,
kept overall profitability rising through a material cost spike, though the ratio comparison itself carries
a methodology caveat (screener's "Change in Inventory" convention is not verified against Kennametal's
own segment disclosures in this corpus).

**Peers silent:** No Kennametal call falls inside TOTEM's specific FY26 spike window.

**Net read:** Structural evidence (peer historically has and exercises real pass-through power) sits
beside TOTEM's own documented weakening of the identical claim. This does not prove TOTEM's FY26
experience was avoidable, industry-wide, or company-specific — the periods do not overlap — but it
is evidence the sector CAN sustain pass-through with sufficient pricing power, which sharpens rather
than dulls the question of why TOTEM's own language softened.

## 2C Capex cycle: lone expander or capacity race?

CAPACITY RACE, not a lone expander. Kennametal was mid-cycle on a major footprint expansion
through 2023 (new insert plant, "physical infrastructure is capable of taking double the capacity",
Vijaykrishnan, KENNAMET Mar-2023, p.5), moving to a harvest/modular phase by Mar-2024 ("we don't
see as of now any new or a major capex that is required", Suresh Reddy, KENNAMET Mar-2024, p.17).
Wendt made its largest-ever capex in FY25 (Rs58.29cr vs Rs11.15cr prior year, plus a separate
Rs35.08cr brand acquisition), explicitly framed as "critical for future growth" (Bhagya Chandra Rao,
WENDT AGM, p.4). TOTEM's own Drills/Taps/Austempering capacity additions (B05, FY24-26) sit inside
the SAME multi-year window as both peers' capex programmes. This is an industry-wide capacity
investment cycle across the whole peer set examined, not TOTEM alone racing ahead of a cautious
peer group — a materially different read than the B05/B04 upstream reports could establish on their
own (they had no peer capex benchmark at all).

- **China/single-market export concentration risk**: Kennametal's exports were dominated by a single

- **Transparent one-time-cost reconciliation**: Wendt explicitly separates and quantifies one-time costs

**Hypothesis: the FY26-Q1FY27 revenue acceleration visible across this peer set is not a uniform,
industry-wide demand event lifting every cutting-tool/precision-tooling maker equally — it is
concentrated among the names that had RECENTLY completed a capacity or capability investment,
while the one comparator without a matching recent capex program in this window (Birlaprec) largely
missed it.**

The pattern, combined across sources no single peer states: Kennametal completed a major new
insert-plant expansion (announced Nov-2022, transition finished by mid-2023) and by Mar-2024 was
already describing sequential PBT improvement from "cost absorption" (Vijaykrishnan, KENNAMET
Mar-2024, p.5-6); its FY26 (Jun-2026) revenue then accelerated to +29.1% with the final quarter at
+47.7%. Wendt made its largest-ever capex (Rs58.29cr, +420% YoY) in FY25 specifically for "capability
building in fast-growing products and new product capacity enhancement" (Bhagya Chandra Rao,
WENDT AGM, p.4); after an initial quarter of capex-related margin drag (Q1 FY26 machine tools -18%,
PBT -34%), its Q1 FY27 (same calendar quarter as TOTEM's) accelerated to +36.6%. TOTEM itself
completed its HSS Drills CNC capacity addition (+~350,000 units) inside FY26 (B05, Annexure IV) and
