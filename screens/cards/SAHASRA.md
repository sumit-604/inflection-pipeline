## Business Understanding Narrative

**What does it do, and how does it make money?** Sahasra assembles printed
circuit boards for other brands. It also packs memory chips and sells finished
memory products. It runs four lines: EMS (contract electronics manufacturing),
memory solutions, computer and IT hardware, and semiconductor packaging (Deck
p13). EMS was 66.0% of FY26 revenue, memory 18.9%, semiconductor 11.3% and IT
hardware 3.8% (Deck p7). The money is a conversion margin on customer-owned
designs. 140 customers buy; the top five take 55.6% (Deck p5).

**What is changing?** Two things. The customer base moved from export to home.
Exports were 55.8% of revenue in FY26 (Deck p20); management calls domestic and
export "almost equal" (Call p3). The second change is the bigger one. A
subsidiary, Sahasra Semiconductors, packs NAND flash in a clean-room plant at
Bhiwadi. Management claims "Aiming to establish Sahasra as an Original Design
Manufacturer (ODM) in IT hardware and memory domain" (Deck p6).

**Why now?** A memory shortage. Management says large memory makers moved to
enterprise products and left a gap in consumer memory (Call p18). Semiconductor
revenue grew 210% in H2 FY26 to Rs 13.1 crore (Deck p8).

**What must be true?** The semiconductor plant must fill. It ran at 6.1% of a
Rs 250 crore peak in FY26 (Deck p18). Management guides Rs 50 crore for FY27
and cash break-even at that level (Call p9). The core EMS plant at Bhiwadi also
must climb from 22.5% utilisation (Deck p18) to above 40% (Call p16).

**What breaks it?** Earnings quality. FY26 consolidated profit before tax was
Rs 13.3 crore. Other income was Rs 9.6 crore of that (Deck p36). Operating cash
flow was negative Rs 10.1 crore (Deck p38). A memory price reversal would expose
a business that has not yet shown operating profit at scale.

## 2. Business model and archetype

High mix, medium volume contract manufacturing. Eight SMT lines: four at Noida
for export and four at Bhiwadi for domestic (Call p13). Noida did Rs 78.7 crore
at 78.7% utilisation; Bhiwadi did Rs 44.9 crore at 22.5%; the semiconductor unit
did Rs 15.3 crore at 6.1% (Deck p18). Consolidated gross margin was 31.7% in
FY26 against 30.4% in FY25 (Deck p30).

The margin comes from the standalone EMS business. Standalone EBITDA was
Rs 18.8 crore at 15.2% (Deck p33). Consolidated EBITDA was Rs 18.2 crore at
13.1% (Deck p36). The subsidiary therefore lost money at EBITDA level in FY26.

**Archetype: outsourcing partner (EMS).** Client concentration, wallet share,
capacity fill and price per unit are the variables. The semiconductor slice
behaves like a build-to-spec component maker.

## 3. Competitive advantages

Forming, not present. Management claims "we being perhaps the only player doing
memory packaging" in India (Call p18). That is an issuer claim. Retention is 90%
(Deck p12). Customers "do not sign beyond one year" (Call p17). Switching cost is
modest. Scarcity in memory packaging lasts only as long as the shortage.

## 4. The promoters

The Manwani family. Amrit Manwani is Chairman and MD; Varun Manwani is a
non-executive director; Arunima Manwani is an executive director (Deck p15).
Promoter holding is **69.9%** before the merger and **74.1%** after it, on a
fully diluted basis at 31-Dec-2025 (Deck p21). Pledge NOT FOUND.

The merger folds three unlisted promoter companies into the listed one: SEPL at
226 SESL shares per 100, ITPL at 5 per 100, SSSDPL at 55 per 100 (Deck p21).
Management expects it "by the end of the financial year or earlier", effective
1-Apr-2026 (Call p8, p9). Related-party dependence is heavy; see step 9.

## 6. The transition and the quality ladder

FROM R1/R2, an export EMS assembler earning a conversion margin. TO R3, a
specified memory packager and ODM with in-house substrate design. Mechanism:
memory packaging moves the company from assembling someone else's board to
owning a packaged part that a customer qualifies.

Management's claim, quoted once: "Aiming to establish Sahasra as an Original
Design Manufacturer (ODM) in IT hardware and memory domain with in-house
substrate design capabilities" (Deck p6).

Two readings. **Climb:** qualifications convert and packaging earns above EMS.
**Cycle:** the gain is a memory shortage that let management "pass on the same
price increases" (Call p5); it reverses with the cycle. The observation that
separates them: semiconductor EBITDA sign in H1 FY27, with memory prices
disclosed.

## 8. Proof check

The triggers claim scale and a semiconductor ramp. H2 FY26 moved that way.
Revenue rose 58% to Rs 78.1 crore; semiconductor revenue tripled to Rs 13.1
crore; EBITDA turned from negative Rs 0.6 crore to Rs 7.6 crore (Deck p8, p36).
Management said April and May 2026 were "on course" for the Rs 50 crore
semiconductor year (Call p9).

**Name the gap.** No half-year after 31-Mar-2026 is reported. The proof is one
half-year of positive EBITDA. Cash did not follow: operating cash flow was
negative in both FY25 and FY26 (Deck p38). Management itself says "certain
strategic initiatives progress at a slower pace than originally anticipated"
(Call p3).

## 9. Flags from the documents

- **Other income is 72% of consolidated PBT** (Rs 9.6 of 13.3 crore, Deck p36).
  Core operating PBT is about Rs 3.7 crore. Flag, load-bearing.
- **Negative operating cash flow two years running** (Deck p38). Working
  capital absorbed Rs 31.6 crore in FY26.
- **Related-party lending.** Standalone lent Rs 20.0 crore to a related party
  in FY26, Rs 6.0 crore in FY25 and Rs 11.3 crore in FY24 (Deck p35). Loans to
  the semiconductor subsidiary stood at Rs 30.55 crore at 31-Dec-2025 (Interim
  p22). A sole proprietorship of Amrit Lal Manwani owed Rs 1.39 crore in trade
  receivables (Interim p22). AGM seeks Rs 19 crore of related-party trade for
  FY27 (AGM p101).
- **Merger with promoter companies** lifts promoter holding from 69.9% to
  74.1% (Deck p21). Valuation fairness is untested here.
- **Qualified opinion on the merger valuation accounts.** Labour-code
  liability "amount unascertained" and an unvalued Rs 11 lakh investment
  (Interim p27). Small in size; it sits on the accounts used to set the swap.
- **Top-five concentration 55.6%**, up from 37.9% in one year (Deck p5).
- **FY24 pre-IPO margins never repeated.** Standalone EBITDA 34.9% and ROCE
  80.7% in FY24 fell to 11.2% and 5.9% in FY25 (Deck p28, p29).

## 12. Verdict card

**WATCH.** Hard watch, no `/step1` yet.

The reason is the proof gate, not the story. The guidance is specific. The
FY26 profit is not operating profit. The next half-year settles it.

**Load-bearing facts to verify first**
1. H1 FY27 semiconductor revenue and EBITDA sign. Management guided Rs 50 crore
   for the year.
2. Other income split: subsidy amortisation, FD interest, other.
3. Operating cash flow and inventory days in H1 FY27.
4. Merger swap valuation basis and post-issue share count.
5. Related-party loan balances and the Rs 19 crore RPT approval.

**What would change the view**
- H1 FY27 consolidated PBT excluding other income above Rs 8 crore, with
  positive operating cash flow, moves this to PROCEED.
- A memory price fall that turns semiconductor EBITDA negative again moves it
  to PASS.

No Role 1 valuation was run. No target price is set. This verdict decides only
whether the name earns a full `/step1`. It is not a position.
