## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

**2A — Demand environment.** NOCIL's own volume growth across the aligned quarters is modest: full FY26 volume growth of 3% (a first-half decline of 5% offset by a GST 2.0-driven second-half recovery of 12%), Q1 FY27 up 9% YoY. NOCIL also quantifies underlying rubber-chemicals industry penetration growth at roughly 2.8% CAGR over 2022-25 (down from 5.7% CAGR in 2013-18) — i.e., the sector NOCIL sits in is growing in the low single digits, not 30-40%. FINEORG's demand commentary is more constructive (domestic "strong," most global markets "recovering" outside Europe) with EBITDA margins of 20-26%, the healthiest of the three peers. CAMLINFINE's demand story is dominated by the geopolitical conflict, with management repeatedly walking back near-term guidance ("we may end up with the same turnover"). None of the three describe anything close to a 30-40% sector-wide demand boom. This does not contradict Yasho's number directly (Yasho's growth claim rests on company-specific share gains and new capacity/molecules, not a sector tailwind), but it means the peer set offers zero corroboration that the underlying market itself is growing anywhere near that fast — the growth, if real, has to be almost entirely share-taking and capacity-driven, which raises the bar on execution risk rather than lowering it.

**2B — Pricing and input costs.** This is the most consequential unprompted finding. All three peers, independently and repeatedly, describe the same Middle East/geopolitical conflict driving severe petrochemical feedstock inflation (see Q3 evidence) and, for NOCIL and CAMLINFINE specifically, compressing EBITDA margins to the single digits (NOCIL: 6.4-11.2% across the four aligned quarters; CAMLINFINE: 2-14%, trending down and repeatedly guided lower) in the identical window Yasho reports margin expanding from 17.4% to 24.2%. Both peers describe a lag in passing RM cost increases through to customers (a quarter or more), and neither claims to be ahead of the cost curve. FINEORG, the one peer with margins in Yasho's range (20-26%), is a genuinely different business (branded specialty oleochemicals with long-term contracts and stated pricing power) rather than a commodity/converter analog. Read together, the peer set's uniform message is: petrochemical-derivative raw-material makers in India, in this exact window, are under margin pressure, not margin expansion. That does not prove Yasho's number is wrong — Yasho may genuinely have superior mix, hedging, or contract structure — but it is the single sharpest divergence between the main company's narrative and its peer set, and it deserves to be tested against Yasho's own RM cost-basket detail at the next print rather than accepted on the "10-12% margin premium on new products" mechanism explanation alone.

**2C — Capex cycle.** Not a lone-expander situation and not a uniform industry-wide capacity race either. NOCIL (Rs.130cr + Rs.250cr Dahej brownfield, "growth capex") and FINEORG (Rs.700-750cr SEZ plant plus a planned US plant) are both actively expanding through this window. CAMLINFINE is doing the opposite — shutting down its diphenol plant on economics, explicitly reducing cash burn, and describing itself as capital-constrained ("we are looking at some kind of support from the market on debt... to tide over this conflict situation"). So two of three peers are expanding brownfield/greenfield capacity in the same window Yasho is, which is mild corroboration that this is a genuine capacity-investment phase in Indian specialty/commodity chemicals rather than an isolated Yasho story — but the CAMLINFINE counter-example is a reminder that capex commitments in this sector are not universally rewarded; distress-driven capacity idling is happening in parallel.

**2D — Competitive mentions of the main company.** None. A full-text search of all 11 transcripts for "Yasho" returns zero matches. No peer analyst or management team references Yasho by name or unmistakable description. Expected, given the sub-segment mismatch (rubber chemicals / aroma-antioxidants / oleochemical additives vs. lube & fuel additives) — these are adjacent-chemistry peers, not direct competitors, and their analyst bases are unlikely to be tracking a name of Yasho's size in a different niche.

**2E — Risks peers discuss that Yasho does not.** (1) The Middle East/geopolitical conflict as a named, severe, ongoing driver of RM and freight cost spikes — discussed explicitly and repeatedly by all three peers, but characterised by Yasho (per company memory and B05) only as a mild, bounded "10-15% since pre-war" inflation, which the peer magnitude data suggests may be understated. (2) DGTR anti-dumping-duty recommendations stuck pending Finance Ministry sign-off for months (NOCIL) — a specific regulatory-lag risk not mentioned in Yasho's own China/competition framing. (3) Korean import dumping as a second, distinct Asian competitive threat beyond China, in some cases undercutting Chinese pricing itself (NOCIL) — Yasho's B05 competitive framing is China-only. (4) Trade/dealer financing dependency compressing margins (CAMLINFINE uses dealer and trade finance to bridge working-capital gaps, which itself erodes gross margin by 1-2pp) — a distinct margin risk mechanism not present in Yasho's disclosures. (5) The two-sided nature of an FTA — NOCIL flags that FTA tariff cuts can just as easily strip a domestic producer's existing duty protection on an imported intermediate as they can open an export market, "impacting profitability" — a risk absent from Yasho's purely tailwind framing of the EU FTA.

## PART 5: CROSS-PEER HYPOTHESIS

Across NOCIL, CAMLINFINE, and FINEORG, the same Middle East/geopolitical conflict (roughly Nov 2025 onward, intensifying through H1 2026) is described by every peer as the dominant swing factor in the period, and in every case where a peer quantifies its effect, the direction is the same: petrochemical-derivative feedstock costs up sharply (aniline +70-73%, phenol +76%, caustic roughly doubled), freight costs elevated and unpredictable, and margin realisation lagging cost increases by at least a quarter. No peer in the set claims pricing power sufficient to get ahead of this cost curve; NOCIL and CAMLINFINE's margins compressed to single digits as a direct result. This is a sector-wide input-cost shock, not an idiosyncratic event at any one peer.

The testable hypothesis this pattern implies, which no single peer states: an Indian specialty/commodity chemical maker sourcing petrochemical-derivative raw materials in this exact window should show margin compression, not margin expansion, unless it has a structurally different RM contract book (multi-quarter fixed-price agreements, captive backward integration, or genuinely differentiated product mix insulating it from spot petrochemical pricing) than the rest of the sector. Yasho's claimed 17.4%→24.2% margin move in the identical window is therefore not just an aggressive number against its own multi-year band — it is an outlier against the entire adjacent peer set's lived experience of the same macro shock. This is testable directly: the next quarter's print should show whether Yasho's RM cost basket (and specifically its aniline/phenol-type petrochemical intermediates, if any overlap exists) moved with the sector or against it, and whether Yasho can name the specific contract or mix mechanism that decouples it from what NOCIL, CAMLINFINE, and FINEORG all describe as an unavoidable, sector-wide cost shock.
