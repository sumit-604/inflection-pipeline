B06 handles both structural gaps correctly and honestly:

- **Coal/mining peer absence**: flagged up front in the report's opening caveat, repeated in the
  `input_gaps` YAML block, and correctly drives the UNVERIFIABLE verdicts on Q1, Q2, and Q7 (the
  three claims that are entirely dependent on a coal/OB-removal peer that does not exist in this set).
  B06 does not attempt to backfill this gap with an inappropriate analog presented as direct evidence
  — where it does reach for an analog (GDL's "bad business" refusal, for Q2), it explicitly labels it
  as an analog only and declines to upgrade the verdict on that basis.
- **GDL staleness (~5 years)**: flagged up front and in every individual verdict that touches GDL
  data ("~5 years stale," "never used for current-period magnitude"). The peer coverage map explicitly
  tags both GDL rows as "SUBSTANTIVE — structural/directional only, ~5 years stale, never used for
  current-period magnitude" rather than silently blending 2021 GDL figures with FY25-26 magnitude
  claims. This is the correct, conservative treatment and is applied consistently across Q3, Q5, Q6,
  and the Part 2/2E cross-reads.
- **Chennai/South Indian CFS operator absence**: also correctly flagged (Q4) and drives that
  UNVERIFIABLE verdict; B06 correctly notes GDL's CFS commentary is entirely North India (Punjab
  Conware) and therefore cannot triangulate a Southern-region, NICDC-sourced market-share claim.

## 4. Findings

| # | Severity | Location in B06.md | Finding |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | MAJOR | Q6 verdict table (Part 1) and Part 4 "Single most consequential contradiction" | B06 states GDL's CFS EBITDA/TEU "had fallen back toward pre-COVID levels (Rs 2,500-2,700/TEU vs a desired Rs 2,900-3,000/TEU)." The GDL Oct-2021 transcript's actual stated recovery target (Ishaan Gupta, in direct response to the analyst's Rs2,500-2,700 framing) is "Rs. 2,700/2,800," not Rs 2,900-3,000. The Rs 2,900-3,000 figure instead appears in the earlier (April/May-2021) GDL call as an *already-achieved* EBITDA/TEU level at that time (Samvid Gupta: "margins have stabilized it is about 2,900 to 3,000 per TEU EBITDA"), describing a different period. B06 conflates the two calls' figures into a single, inaccurate "desired" range. The underlying qualitative point (fuel-cost pass-through failing in the CFS segment, used as the report's flagship contradiction given Sical's terminals segment is 36% of FY26 revenue) is genuinely supported by the transcript and correctly quoted elsewhere — only this specific numeric range is wrong. |
| 2 | MINOR | Part 2B (Pricing and input costs) | The quote "let go a few customers" (illustrating Express cost-discipline-driven margin improvement) is attributed jointly to "Aug-2025, Feb-2026 calls." It is verified verbatim only in the Aug-2025 transcript (Ravi Jakhar). The Feb-2026 transcript supports the same general pattern (Express EBITDA improved on "yield improvement and cost control measures" despite revenue declining slightly YoY) but does not contain this specific phrase — a similar customer-shedding phrase ("weeded out a few non-profitable customers") instead appears in the May-2026 transcript, which B06 does not cite here. Imprecise span-attribution of a real quote, not a fabrication. |
| 3 | MINOR (unused_but_relevant) | Part 2C (Capex cycle) | GDL's Oct-2021 call discloses explicit ROCE targets for new capital-intensive infrastructure investment — "all new capacities that we are adding are somewhere around 17%-18% ROCE" (Sunil Nair) and "upwards of 21%" blended including BTS/lease projects (Ishaan Gupta). This is directly relevant context for assessing return expectations on Sical's own capex-heavy SECL/MMLP order book, which B06's 2C section discusses as a "lone-expander" capex-cycle divergence — but this ROCE benchmark from the same GDL transcript is not cited anywhere in B06, an industry-context miss rather than a claim-relevant omission. |

No CRITICAL findings. No fabricated or misattributed peer quotation was found — every directly
quoted passage in B06 that this audit checked against source was genuinely present in the cited
transcript and correctly speaker-attributed, with the two exceptions above being a numeric-precision
error (Finding 1) and a citation-span imprecision (Finding 2), not invented content.
