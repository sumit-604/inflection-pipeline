# Eimco Elecon Q2 FY27: the story in plain words

Source: the committed review (work/review_eimcoeleco_q2fy27.md), the A5 audit (work/audit_eimcoeleco_q2fy27.md) and work/notion-thesis.md. No agent was re-run. All money is in Rs crore.

**Earlier analysis:** first look, no earlier analysis on record. There is no Notion page, no company file, no thesis, no Decision Status and no tripwires to compare against.

1. **What the company does.** Eimco Elecon makes heavy machines that work underground in coal and metal mines. These include loaders, continuous miner sets and chair lifts that carry miners. It also makes piling rigs for construction, and it has started to sell battery vehicles. About 1,065 of its machines are in use today, and they need spare parts. The spares business is the steady part of the company.

2. **What happened this quarter.** Sales were about 57 crore, up 71% from a year ago. That sounds strong, but the same quarter last year was very weak. Against the June quarter, sales fell 27%. This company always sells more in the second half of the year. One quarter alone tells you little.

3. **What improved.** Profit from operations rose a lot against last year. The operating margin rose from about 16% to about 19%. The order book is 133 crore, three times what it was a year ago. If those orders are firm, they cover about six months of sales.

4. **Why the improvement is less than it looks.** The rise in profit starts from a bad quarter. The fair test is June against June, and there profit grew about 15%. Profit over the last twelve months is still below the year before last. Also, almost all of the margin gain comes from one cost line. The company paid its distributors much less as a share of sales than before, and nobody explains why. If that payment goes back to normal, the margin falls back to about 13% to 15%.

5. **What got worse.** Gross margin, which is what is left after the cost of materials, fell a lot against last year. Against the June quarter it actually rose. The review reads this as a shift toward more machines and fewer spares. The auditor pushed back: mix explains only part of the fall. The rest may be lower prices, higher input costs or a different machine mix, and the filing cannot tell which. Profit after tax grew less than operating profit, because income from investments fell.

6. **Cash: the weakest point.** In six months the company booked about 23 crore of profit, but only about 2 crore came in as cash. Even that 2 crore depends on one line of money it owes to others. Without it, cash from operations was negative. Customers owe the company more each month: unpaid bills rose by a quarter in six months. Over the last four years, only about half of profit turned into cash. This looks like a habit, not a one time problem.

7. **The balance sheet.** The company holds about 213 crore of investments, more than a third of everything it owns. Almost all of it is long term, and the company does not say what it holds. The short term, easy to sell part shrank, and the company borrowed for the first time in years. Most of that shrink was investments moved from short term to long term, not cash spent. So the borrowing may show a cash need, or it may be a choice to borrow rather than sell. Plant and equipment also jumped by about 47 crore with no note on what was bought.

8. **What management said and promised.** Management says it targets an operating margin of 18% to 20%, with no date. That target sits below what the company earned two years ago. It says the order book gives "strong visibility" for the months ahead. It says it will keep investing in its capabilities, with no amount given. It launched new products, but gave no orders or sales for them.

9. **Forensic red flags in plain words.** The chief financial officer leaves on 30 October and the company names no successor. His letter is dated 5 September, but the company told the market 38 days later. The 133 crore is called an "order book" in one place and an "open order pipeline" in another, and those are not the same thing. The press release says margins improved, while profit margin after tax actually fell. Some ratios on the slides do not match the balance sheet. None of these proves wrongdoing. Together they say the disclosure is loose.

10. **Open items the auditor flagged at close.** Three points were still not fixed when the work closed. First, the claim that the margin fall is only about mix is too strong, as point 5 explains. Second, the CFO letter went to an unnamed executive director, and the board has two. We do not know who held it for those 38 days. Third, the review should say both reasons the liquid cash shrank, as point 7 does. None of these changes the overall verdict.

11. **What this means for the thesis.** There is no thesis yet. This is a first look. The verdict is "proceed with flags": the numbers are filed cleanly and the auditor raised no concern, but cash, the unexplained cost line, the missing asset note and the CFO exit all need answers. Nothing here forces a decision. The real question is whether to start a full intake on this name.

12. **What to watch next quarter.** Watch the December quarter results, due by mid February. One number matters most: operating profit, which should reach about 10 crore if the business is truly back. Also watch four things. Does depreciation jump once the new asset starts to count? Does the distributor payment stay low, and does the company explain it? Is a new CFO named? Do the 133 crore of orders turn into sales and cash, or into more unpaid bills?
