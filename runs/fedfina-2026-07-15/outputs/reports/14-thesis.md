## NARRATIVE (plain English)

Fedbank Financial is a real turnaround inside a business that is genuinely getting better: credit cost has printed inside guidance for three straight quarters, direct-assignment income has been cut to almost nothing, gold branches are being built on schedule, and a credible new CEO sits behind a Federal Bank parent that keeps the funding cheap and the rating high. The trouble is entirely the price. At Rs 164 the market pays 2.09 times book and about 18 times earnings for a company earning about 12.6% on equity, which is roughly what it costs the company to raise that equity. When a lender earns about its cost of capital it creates almost no economic value, and the honest multiple for that is around one times book, not two. The four-pillar work says the multiple this business earns is 14 to 16 times, below where it already trades, so the most likely path over three years is the multiple drifting down while earnings grind up, leaving a slightly negative return. The whole constructive case rests on one number nobody has seen yet: the Q4 FY26 credit cost. If it stays at or below 1.1% the recovery holds; if it breaks, both the asset-quality read and the return recovery weaken together. This is a watch, not a buy. The stock becomes interesting only near Rs 68 to 77, and only if that credit-cost print confirms first.
