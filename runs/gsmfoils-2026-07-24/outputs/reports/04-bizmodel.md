# STAGE 4: BUSINESS MODEL DECODER — GSM Foils Ltd (GSMFOILS)
Run date: 2026-07-24 | Model: Sonnet 5

**Manifest sector correction (upfront):** The collector-fed sector row "Pharma / CDMO" is a
mislabel. GSM Foils does not manufacture, formulate, or contract-develop any drug — it
manufactures the **aluminium foil packaging** (blister lidding foil, strip/pouch foil, and,
from FY27, aluminium ROPP screw caps) that pharma companies buy to seal their tablets and
capsules. This is a **specialty packaging / foil-converting** business, one supply-chain
layer removed from pharma manufacturing. Correct comparable universe: CGRAPHICS, ESTER,
HUHTAMAKI, TCPLPACK — flexible/rigid packaging converters, not pharma/CDMO names. This
correction drives the valuation-method recommendation in Section 4B and must propagate to
Stage 9 (peer set) and Stage 11 (exit multiple selection).

Primary source: FY25 Annual Report (AR), the only audited full-year filing provided.
Secondary source: Investor Presentation, Q1 FY27 (Inv. Pres.), dated July 2026 — carries
FY26 and Q1 FY27 numbers not yet available in an audited AR. Tertiary, non-anchored lead
only: third-party IPO Note (May-2024), used for pre-IPO business description and peer
cross-check, never for anchored figures per pipeline input priority.

## SECTION 1: THE BUSINESS MODEL IN PLAIN ENGLISH

### 1A. One-line description
GSM Foils buys aluminium foil stock, coats/laminates/prints/slits it into pharma-grade
blister and strip packaging foil (and, from mid-2026, aluminium screw caps), and sells it to
Indian pharmaceutical formulators who use it as the sealed layer that protects tablets and
capsules (AR p.1; Inv. Pres. slide 4, 7).

### 1B. Money flow chain, by revenue stream

| Stream | Input | What GSM Foils does | What it delivers | Who pays | How they pay |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Blister foils (20/25 micron) | Bare aluminium foil stock, nitrocellulose coating, vinyl solution (AR p.1; Inv. Pres. slide 8) | Coats, poly-laminates, prints, slits to customer spec | Roll of pharma-grade blister lidding foil | Pharmaceutical formulators (domestic) | Credit terms — trade receivables of Rs.3,376.6 lakh at FY25-end vs Rs.723.5 lakh at FY24-end (AR Note 4.3, p.~90), implying lengthening customer credit as the book scales |

| Aluminium ROPP caps (new, FY27) | Aluminium coil, threading/roll-on tooling via MoU with AAPL Solutions (Inv. Pres. slide 13) | Precision roll-on-pilfer-proof cap manufacture at a third facility, commercial production started June 2026 | Tamper-evident caps for pharma syrups, and non-pharma (alcobev, edible oil) | Pharma + non-pharma bottlers | Not yet disclosed — too new for a trade-credit pattern to be visible |

The FY25 AR does not break the first two streams apart: the company reports a single

split (Blister 68.05%, Strip/Pharma 31.95% for the nine months to Dec-23) is a non-anchored,
dated lead only — treat as directional, not as an FY25 anchor.

### 1E. The chai-stall-uncle version
Think of the shiny foil wrapper on a strip of tablets you pop out with your thumb — someone
has to make that foil. GSM Foils buys plain aluminium foil, then coats it, laminates it, and
prints it so it seals tightly and protects the medicine from moisture and light, the same way
foil keeps your parathas from going soggy. They sell rolls of this foil to pharma companies,
who wrap it around their tablets. It is not a pharma company itself — it is one supplier deep
in the packaging chain, closer to a specialised commodity-converting workshop than a drugmaker.
It has recently added a second product — aluminium screw caps for syrup bottles — the same
idea, different shape.

### 2D. Industry lifecycle stage
The Indian pharma-packaging-foil sub-sector is in a **growth phase**, riding structural
tailwinds — India supplies ~20% of world generics by volume, pharma exports at $30.47 billion
in FY25 (+9.4% YoY), and USFDA/WHO-GMP barrier-packaging mandates (Inv. Pres. slide 19-20).
GSM Foils itself is a **sub-scale, high-growth entrant riding that wave**: revenue grew from
Rs.4,083 lakh (FY24) to Rs.13,380 lakh (FY25) to Rs.25,815 lakh (FY26) (Inv. Pres. slide 27,
28) — a two-year revenue CAGR of roughly 151%, off a small base, while simultaneously adding
two new facilities (Ahmedabad, ROPP Caps unit) and diversifying beyond its original two
products. This is a company still building scale and product breadth, not one defending an
established position.

### 2E. Key industry drivers

| Driver | Direction | Impact on GSM Foils |
|---|---|---|
| India pharma exports growth (+9.4% YoY, FY25) | Positive | Demand tailwind for barrier packaging, though GSM Foils itself reported zero foreign exchange earnings in FY25 (AR Annexure III, p.49) — it is not yet capturing export demand directly |
| Regulatory push for barrier-grade packaging (USFDA/WHO-GMP) | Positive | Structural volume support for aluminium foil over cheaper substitutes |
| Anti-dumping duty risk on foil-stock imports from China | Negative | AR MD&A names this explicitly as a threat that "could increase raw material costs, impacting margins" (AR p.53) |

| India aluminium demand growth (6-8% projected annually) | Positive | Broad industry tailwind, not company-specific |
| Global aluminium foil market growth (5.4% CAGR to 2035) | Modestly positive | Slow-growing global backdrop; India-specific pharma-packaging growth is the more relevant driver for this company |

## SECTION 3: FINANCIAL METRICS THAT MATTER FOR THIS BUSINESS MODEL

### 3A. Ignore-these-track-these

| Commonly tracked ratio | Why misleading/irrelevant here |
|---|---|
| Gross margin, viewed in isolation, year-on-year | Gross margin has been declining every period shown — 19.0% (FY24) to 16.4% (FY25) to 15.1% (FY26) to 14.6% (Q1 FY27) (Inv. Pres. slide 26, 28) — but this largely reflects commodity (aluminium) cost pass-through mechanics, not eroding value-add. Read alongside EBITDA margin, which is *expanding* over the same window (6.6% to 11.4% to 11.5% to 11.9%), before drawing a margin-erosion conclusion |
| ROE, as a standalone signal | ROE of 30.9% (FY25) and 26.6% (FY26) (Inv. Pres. slide 30) is flattered by a very small pre-IPO equity base (Total Equity Rs.1,103.3 lakh at Mar-24) recently topped up by a fresh issue; treat as mechanically elevated, not proof of superior capital efficiency, until multi-year post-IPO equity base normalises |
| Fixed-asset turnover / gross block turnover as a manufacturing-efficiency signal | Net PPE was only Rs.211.9 lakh at Mar-25 and Rs.508.9 lakh at Mar-26 against Rs.13,380 lakh and Rs.25,815 lakh of revenue respectively (AR Note 3.3, p.~87; Inv. | Working-capital days, standalone, without the OCF cross-check | The reported improvement in working-capital days (99 to 71 to 84, FY24-FY26, Inv. ### 4B. Valuation method applicability — formal handoff to Stage 9 / Stage 11

| Method | Applicability | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| EV/EBITDA | Normalises across the company's rapidly changing capital structure (fresh equity issuance, rising short-term debt) and captures true operating profitability independent of depreciation-policy quirks from a still-small, newly capitalised fixed-asset base. Standard method for the packaging/converter comp set (CGRAPHICS, ESTER, HUHTAMAKI, TCPLPACK) | **PRIMARY** |

| P/B (Price-to-Book) | NOT APPLICABLE — asset-light disclosed balance sheet (net PPE of just Rs.508.9 lakh at Mar-26) means book value is not tied to the company's true earning power; book value is also freshly distorted by IPO share-premium accounting |

**Cycle stage that matters for valuation:** early scale-up / capacity-ramp stage, not a
steady-state margin year. Any multiple applied should be tested against normalised (not
peak-transition) EBITDA once Ahmedabad and ROPP utilisation stabilise, and the size/liquidity
gap versus the CGRAPHICS/ESTER/HUHTAMAKI/TCPLPACK comp set should be reflected as a discount,
not ignored.

### 4D. Highest-value questions for management

1. **What is the FY25/FY26 revenue split between Blister Foils, Strip/Pharma Foils, and ROPP Caps?**
   Reassures: a granular, stable-to-diversifying mix with rising ROPP contribution.
   Worries: inability or unwillingness to disclose, implying weak internal MIS/segment tracking.

2. **What is customer concentration — top 5/top 10 customers as % of FY25 and FY26 revenue?**
