## SECTION 3: THE 20-CATEGORY SCAN

### FAMILY A — PRODUCT & TECHNOLOGY

**A1 — rare manufacturing capability**: NO EVIDENCE FOUND. Not applicable — AMAGI has no manufacturing footprint; it is a cloud-native software business built on third-party (AWS) infrastructure.

**A2 — patent and IP pipeline**: AMAGI holds 22 patents globally, of which 10 are granted (2 registered in India + 5 in the US at the Company level, plus additional grants at subsidiary level bringing the group total to 10) and 11 applications pending (5 India + 6 US) (Prospectus PDF p.5, p.267, p.430). Filings align topically with the product roadmap (content preparation/playout automation, ad insertion/monetization, cloud broadcast infrastructure). No licensing revenue, no named lab/university partnerships, no litigation-tested defensibility disclosed. Evidence: 📄 documented (grant/filing counts) but thin strategic weight. **Strength: Weak.**

**A3 — process innovation**: NO EVIDENCE FOUND at the specific yield/unit-cost level this category asks for. Gross margin did improve from 64.73% (FY23) to 69.33% (FY25) and operating expense ratio fell from 85.47% to 67.30% of revenue over the same window (Prospectus PDF p.239-240), but this is a scale/opex-leverage story, not a disclosed automation-driven unit-cost or yield improvement, and crediting it here would double-count the same evidence already used under F2 below. Not scored as an independent moat category to avoid crediting one improvement through two mechanisms.

**A4 — product platform / modular architecture**: 📄 Strong. A named, expanding product suite — Amagi NOW, CLOUDPORT, STUDIO, CONNECT, ADS PLUS, THUNDERSTORM, PLANNER, INTELLIGENCE, and now NEWSPULSE — spans the full "glass-to-glass" value chain (Prospectus PDF p.247-254). Documented launch cadence: NAB Show Product of the Year for Amagi NOW (2023) and Amagi DYNAMIC (2024); first Indian company to win a Technology & Engineering Emmy (2024, for manifest-based FAST playout); IDC MarketScape Leader (2024) (Prospectus PDF p.245, p.249). Two bolt-on acquisitions (Tellyo Nov-2023 for live production/social clipping, Argoid.AI Dec-2024 for AI/ML scheduling) were integrated into the platform rather than run standalone (Prospectus PDF p.251, p.253). Management states customers who begin with one solution "often expand across our stack" (Prospectus PDF p.248) — 🎙️ pattern claim, but backed by the hard NRR number below (C1). **Strength: Strong.**

### FAMILY B — SUPPLY CHAIN

**B1 — backward integration and RM security**: NO EVIDENCE FOUND. Not applicable — no raw-material supply chain.

**B2 — qualification lock-in**: NO EVIDENCE FOUND. Not applicable — no IATF/AS9100/NADCAP/cGMP-type industrial qualification regime; AMAGI's closest analogue (customer switching cost) is scored under C1/D2 instead, not duplicated here.

**B3 — supply chain network effect**: NO EVIDENCE FOUND as a standalone category. AMAGI does describe itself explicitly as connecting "fragmented supply & demand" (400+ content owners, platforms) (Inv. Pres. p.10), which is the identical evidence used for D2 below. To avoid crediting the same network-effect fact through two categories (B3 and D2), this is scored once, under D2 (the better-fitting digital-platform category), and B3 is left at zero here by design.

### FAMILY C — CUSTOMER

**C1 — customer ecosystem / embedded relationships**: 📄 Strong. Standard customer contract term is three years with automatic renewal provisions (Prospectus PDF p.67). Average relationship duration as of Sep 30, 2025: 5.25 years (largest customer), 4.16 years (top 5), 4.00 years (top 10) (Prospectus PDF p.68). Zero churn among the ten largest customers by revenue across the six months ended Sep 30, 2025/2024 and the last three financial years (Prospectus PDF p.67, p.251). NRR has held above 120% for three consecutive years: 122% (FY24), 127% (FY25), 126% (FY26) (B05; Inv. Pres. p.3, p.13) — this is the hardest, most directly moat-relevant number in the whole scan: existing customers are spending materially more each year without new-logo growth being required to sustain it. **Strength: Strong.**

**C2 — customer concentration improving**: NO EVIDENCE FOUND — **and the actual trend runs the opposite direction**. Largest-customer revenue share: 6.88% (FY23) → 6.93% (FY24) → 11.41% (FY25) → 14.06% (H1 FY26, annualised run-rate). Top-5 share: 25.94% → 23.76% → 23.65% → 30.94%. Top-10 share: 37.64% → 33.21% → 33.74% → 40.19% (Prospectus PDF p.67, table). One named customer (a "leading American electronics and media company") alone was 14.06% of H1 FY26 revenue, up from 6.36% in FY23 (Prospectus PDF p.67-68). This is a genuine and worsening concentration risk, not a moat, and is carried into Section 6B risks and the combined-assessment discussion rather than scored positively here.

### FAMILY D — DATA & DIGITAL

**D1 — proprietary data asset**: Mixed, primarily 🎙️. Management explicitly frames this as "our second biggest moat": ~10 years of accumulated customer content, viewership telemetry and "money flow through the whole pipes" (Q4 FY26 call, line 300-304 — a direct, self-interested characterisation that should be discounted per the pipeline's skepticism rule). Some underlying facts are 📄 documented: 876,000 cumulative content-hours processed (+51% YoY), 9,425 channel deliveries, 42.4bn ad impressions monetised (Inv. Pres. p.6-7, p.10), and the Argoid.AI acquisition explicitly built an ML scheduler trained on "historical viewership data, audience behavior, and content performance metrics" (Prospectus PDF p.253). No disclosed model-performance metric, no disclosed dataset scale in absolute terms, no replicability assessment. **Strength: Moderate**, weighted down for evidence quality (0.7x) because the "moat" framing itself is management's own characterisation rather than an independently verifiable claim.

**D2 — digital platform**: 📄 Strong. Explicitly described three-sided marketplace (content providers / distributors / advertisers) with a stated "flywheel effect": 400+ content providers, 350+ distributors, 75+ advertisers across 40+ countries as of Sep 30, 2025 (Prospectus PDF p.240, p.250); growing to 407 distributors (+24% YoY) by FY26 year-end (Inv. Pres. p.6). Digital revenue is effectively 100% of revenue (pure SaaS). Lock-in is explicitly claimed and partially evidenced: "the resulting increase in advertising revenue can be reinvested... creating a cycle of growth" and integration "increases the cost and complexity of disengaging from our platform" (Prospectus PDF p.242-243, p.13777). **Strength: Strong.**

Broader intended expansion (Australia, Canada, Japan, Southeast Asia) is 🎙️ aspirational only, with no capital or timeline committed (Prospectus PDF p.255). **Strength: Weak.**

**E2 — China+1 beneficiary**: NO EVIDENCE FOUND. Not applicable — no export manufacturing or supply-chain-shift dynamic; AMAGI's revenue concentration is in the US (73.23% H1 FY26) and Europe/UK (17.27%) (Prospectus PDF p.255), the opposite of a China+1 diversification story.

### FAMILY F — TALENT & ORGANISATIONAL

**F1 — talent density**: Weak, 📄 for the headcount fact, thin beyond it. As of Sep 30, 2025, AMAGI had 547 R&D engineers, 55.48% of its 986-person total workforce, based in Bengaluru, the US, Croatia and Poland (Prospectus PDF p.249, p.251). ESOP expense is falling as a share of revenue (from ~12% two years prior to ~7% in FY26) (Inv. Pres. p.14) — this indicates dilution discipline improving, not necessarily talent-retention strength. No PhD/specialist count, no attrition/retention data, and an investor request for a headcount-by-function KPI breakdown was accepted in the Q3 FY26 call and then not delivered by Q4 FY26 (B05 promise_delivery row, "missed"). **Strength: Weak.**

**F2 — execution moat**: 📄 Moderate. Revenue per employee grew from ₹8.82mn (FY23) to ₹10.71mn (FY24) to ₹13.15mn (FY25), a 22.10% CAGR (Prospectus PDF p.245-246) — a genuine, hard, improving productivity trend. Cross-referencing the injected B05 promise-delivery record: 3 of 7 tracked promises delivered, 2 partial, 2 missed, credibility grade B, with the single specific testable number given (steady-state adj. EBITDA margin ~10%, Q3 FY26 call) landing almost exactly the following quarter (FY26 actual 10.3%) (B05). NEWSPULSE moved from "in trials" (Q3 FY26 call framing) to "first paying customer" within one quarter (Q4 FY26 call) — a fast, credible ramp on at least this one data point. Offsetting: two investor commitments silently dropped (headcount-by-function KPI, Labour Code run-rate clarity) and a client-renegotiation cost understated on first mention then revealed the following quarter (B05 red_flags). **Strength: Moderate** — real execution evidence, but resting on only two quarters of public track record.

**HIGH POTENTIAL.**
