## SECTION 3: THE BULL CASE (Why the business is not junk)

Stated in full and honestly, because the AVOID is on price and governance, not on operations.

**3A. Quality Evidence.** ROCE 24.0% FY26, SUSTAINED and rising from 20.79% FY25 (FTTCP; the FY24 31.48% is a pre-IPO artifact). ROE 22.21%. EBITDA margin 24.05%, PAT margin 15.23% (B10). Net cash Rs8.16 Cr, pledge 0%, current ratio 2.6x (B01). These are real. The single quality hole is cash conversion: cumulative CFO/PAT -0.13x across FY24-26, FY26 CFO Rs0.47 Cr against PAT Rs39.16 Cr, FCF -Rs3.89 Cr (B10). Gate 0 Block B scored 0/20 on this alone.

**3B. Growth Triggers (ranked by conviction, B05):**
1. Greenfield Vadodara Mechatronics facility — 6 SMT lines, Rs500 Cr incremental capacity, FY27. Conviction M. Confirms: SMT lines shipping on schedule. Kills: delay past FY27-Q4 or cost overrun. (No audited total project cost disclosed — B07.)
2. AIC/ICS US acquisition margin ramp — 11% toward 18-20% EBITDA. Conviction M. Confirms: consolidated EBITDA trending 18-20%+ within a year. Kills: AIC margins stuck in low double digits.
3. Working-capital / cash-conversion normalization. Conviction L. Confirms: positive OCF, receivables days fall. Kills: continued negative CFO. **This is the thesis-critical one.**
4. ODM model expansion (US infra / $8bn customer group). Conviction M.
5. PSU/defence pilot-to-production (RDSO approval, H1-Q3 FY27). Conviction L-M. This is the named primary catalyst; it pays +0x in the exit multiple because EM is 23 and no order value is documented (B11).

**3C. Moat Assessment.** Strongest forward categories are B2 Qualification lock-in (Strong, documented: AS9100D, IATF 16949, ISO 13485, CDSCO) and H2 Strategic partnership via the AIC acquisition (Strong, documented). EM 23 overall = MODEST, below the 25 gate that would pay any Pillar 3 moat-formation premium. Moat is forming, not formed; direction is mildly expanding but unproven at scale (B07, B04).

**3D. Management Quality.** Promoter verdict CONCERN (B08). Credibility grade C / Mixed (B05): revenue, order-book and certification promises were delivered or beaten across all three calls, but two transparency commitments (reclassification press release, quarterly reporting) went unmet from H2 FY25 through H2 FY26, the H2 FY25 working-capital reassurance did not hold (FY26 consolidated OCF -Rs40 Cr), and the "no fundraising need" stance reversed via a warrant/preferential issue disclosed only when an analyst raised it. ## NARRATIVE (plain English)

Aimtron is a real, fast-growing electronics contract manufacturer with premium returns on capital and a certification moat that is forming. It is not a bad business. It is a badly priced one, wrapped in a governance problem. At Rs1,390 the market pays 75 times earnings for a company whose earned exit multiple — after crediting its 24% ROCE, docking it for cash that does not flow, and gating its emerging moat at a below-threshold 23 — is 9 to 13 times. No plausible earnings path closes that gap in three years; the probability-weighted expectation is a 42% annual loss. On top of the price, the chairman runs the company's largest related-party customer, roughly a third of revenue flows to non-consolidated promoter entities, and the board sought a Rs120 Cr related-party ceiling (three-quarters of turnover) without an outside valuation. Either the price or the governance alone would be enough to stand aside. Both together make it a clean AVOID.

The whole question is deferred to one number. If the H1 FY27 consolidated operating cash flow comes in solidly positive with receivables falling below 120 days, the cash drain was growth-induced and Aimtron becomes a business worth valuing seriously — but only at a price near Rs129-161, which is nowhere near today. If that print is negative a third time, the cash leak is structural and the AVOID is permanent regardless of price. Watch the print, watch the related-party ceiling, and do nothing until both the price and the cash verdict move.
