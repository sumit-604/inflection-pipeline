## Business Understanding Narrative

**What does it do, and how does it make money?** Zota is no longer mainly a
drug maker. It runs Davaindia, a retail generic pharmacy chain that sells only
private-label medicines, and that chain is now 83% of revenue (Investor
Presentation Q1 FY27, page 11). There were 1,856 company-owned stores and 970
franchise-operated stores at 30 June 2026, so 2,826 in total. The company still
exports generic formulations from a plant at Sachin SEZ, with 325 dossiers
registered and 261 pending across 30-plus countries, but that business is 3% of
revenue (Investor Presentation Q1 FY27, page 39). Domestic branded sales are
10% and the Everyday Herbal group is 4%.

The money is meant to come from making the medicine and selling it in its own
shop. The company keeps the manufacturer's margin and the retailer's margin.
Gross margin was 61.96% in Q1 FY27 (Investor Presentation Q1 FY27, page 45).

**What is changing?** Scale, very fast. Davaindia revenue went from Rs 186.21
crore in FY25 to Rs 417.41 crore in FY26, up 124%. Group revenue went from
Rs 292.97 crore to Rs 538.66 crore. Gross margin went from 53.13% to 60.28%
(Investor Presentation Q4 FY26, page 44).

**Why now?** The company says profitability is near. On the Q1 FY27 call
management discussed "achieving EBITDA positivity over the next two to three
quarters" and said margin pressure from input costs would last "one or two
quarters" (Earnings Call Q1 FY27, pages 9 and 16).

**What must be true for the thesis to work?** Store economics must carry the
overhead. Gross margin is already 62%. Employee cost was 35.2% of revenue in
Q1 FY27 and other expenses 33.4%. Together they exceed the gross margin. The
chain must either raise revenue per store or stop adding cost.

**What breaks it?** The lease. Right-of-use assets were Rs 224.36 crore at
31 March 2026 against Rs 125.38 crore a year earlier (Investor Presentation
Q4 FY26, page 45). Under Ind AS 116 store rent sits below EBITDA, in
depreciation and finance cost. Group depreciation was Rs 82.45 crore in FY26.
EBITDA therefore flatters this business badly, and management said so on the
call: "under EBITDA, rent gets adjusted through" (Earnings Call Q1 FY27,
page 16). Profit before tax is the honest line, and it is deeply negative.

## 1. Corpus ledger

## 2. Business model and archetype

Four verticals, one declared segment. The Q1 FY27 filing says the company "has
concluded that it is engaged in a single operating segment" (Q1 FY27 results,
page 8). It then consolidates five wholly-owned subsidiaries and further
subsidiaries: Zota Healthcare Lanka, DavaIndia Health Mart, Zota Nex Tech,
Curexis Ventures and KMHP Ventures. The company also acquired Globotask IT
Consultancy and is rolling out two more brands, UGO Generic and SKIA (Earnings
Call Q1 FY27, pages 4 and 9).

Revenue by vertical, Rs lakh (Investor Presentation Q1 FY27, page 45):

| Vertical | FY25 | FY26 | Q1 FY27 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Davaindia retail | 18,621.42 | 41,741.47 | 14,342.33 |
| Domestic | 6,341.50 | 6,794.29 | 1,790.67 |
| Everyday Herbal Group | 1,144.22 | 1,846.33 | 649.42 |
| Export (SEZ) | 3,190.34 | 3,471.09 | 573.51 |

Read that table twice. Everything except Davaindia is flat. Domestic grew 7% in
a year. Exports grew 9%. The company is one business now.

**Archetype: brand and franchise consumer retail, with a captive manufacturer
attached.** It is not the build-to-spec or converter archetype the pipeline
usually meets. Volume growth, distribution reach, gross margin and advertising
efficiency are the variables.

## 3. Competitive advantages

Forming, unproven. The advantage claimed is vertical integration: 100% private
label, sold at 30% to 90% below branded equivalents, through a chain the
company controls (Investor Presentation Q1 FY27, page 11). If it works, the
moat is density plus brand recall in a category where the customer repeats.

Nothing in the corpus proves it works. There is no revenue per store, no store
payback, no closure rate and no same-store growth in any document held. A
retail chain that does not publish same-store metrics is asking to be judged on
store count. Moat: absent, with a plausible route.

the shops made.

## 6. The transition and the quality ladder

This is an archetype change, not a rung climb. Zota is leaving R1, a small
generic manufacturer selling into tenders and exports, and claiming R4, a
franchise and share-of-wallet leader in retail pharmacy. That crosses two rungs
and changes the business model at the same time.

The framework's base rate is one rung per two to three years, and says a
claimed multi-rung leap requires extraordinary proof and is itself a red flag.
Record it as a red flag.

Management's claim, quoted once: Davaindia is "one of the fastest growing
retail generic pharmacy chains in India" (Investor Presentation Q1 FY27,
page 11). That is a growth claim. It is not a returns claim, and the company
makes no returns claim in any document held.

## 8. Proof check

The triggers claim that scale brings profit. The last two quarters say the
opposite. Q4 FY26 EBITDA was positive Rs 11.91 crore. Q1 FY27 EBITDA was
negative Rs 9.55 crore. Profit before tax went from negative Rs 20.42 crore to
negative Rs 43.65 crore. Revenue rose 6% sequentially over the same two
quarters.

Management gives two reasons: input cost inflation, and marketing. On
marketing the numbers are specific. Advertising and marketing across all of
FY26 was about Rs 18 crore. Q1 FY27 alone was about Rs 15 to 17 crore
(Earnings Call Q1 FY27, page 6). Other expenses rose to 33% of revenue from
about 21%.

**Name the absence.** The proof point for a retail thesis is same-store
revenue and store-level contribution. Neither appears in any document held.
Without them a reader cannot tell a chain that is investing ahead of a curve
from a chain whose unit economics do not work.

## 9. Flags from the documents

- EBITDA is the wrong metric for this company and management said so. Under
  Ind AS 116 store rent sits in depreciation and finance cost. FY26
  depreciation alone was Rs 82.45 crore against EBITDA of Rs 25.98 crore.
  Read profit before tax. Flag.
- Profit before tax loss is widening quarter on quarter. Flag.
- A single declared operating segment across a retail chain, a domestic
  branded business, an export SEZ plant and a herbal group. Flag the
  disclosure choice.
- Investments on the balance sheet rose from Rs 54.35 crore to Rs 263.88
  crore while the operating business lost money. Flag: money was raised and
  parked, not deployed into the stores.
- Subsidiary and venture sprawl. Five consolidated subsidiaries plus Globotask,
  UGO Generic and SKIA, several described as "initial rollout phase". Flag
  focus.
- Roughly Rs 464 crore of new equity in FY26, of which the Rs 509 warrant
  conversions explain a minority. The balance is NOT FOUND. Flag.
- The exception-item charge of Rs 5.99 crore in Q4 FY26 carries no explanation
  in the documents held. Flag.

## 11. Posture: the Transition Decision Matrix

- **Proof gate: NOT FIRED.** Group EBITDA turned negative in the most recent
  quarter, and profit before tax is worsening. Management's own gate, EBITDA
  positivity, is set two to three quarters out.
- **Ugliness: contested.** On management's account it is ARTIFACT-OF-CLIMB:
  store additions, a brand ambassador and input-cost inflation. On the filed
  numbers it reads STRUCTURAL-FEATURE: operating costs exceed a 62% gross
  margin, and the lease base grows with the store count. **The observation that
  separates them is store-level contribution, which the company does not
  publish.**
- **Recognition gap: CLOSED.** Price basis, not a corpus document: Bull AI
  company record, market capitalisation Rs 3,963.70 crore. Paid-up capital is
  3,46,32,587 shares of Rs 10 (Issue of Securities, page 3). FY26 revenue was
  Rs 538.66 crore and the group lost Rs 73.84 crore before tax. The market
  already pays a full growth-retail price for a business that has not shown a
  profit.

**Posture: PRICED NARRATIVE, the trap cell.** Proof not fired, the ugliness
argued away, the destination already in the price. The framework names this the
most seductive cell because the downside is double: the transition fails and
the multiple compresses together.

## 12. Verdict card

**WATCH. No position, and no `/step1` yet.**

This is not a PASS. The gross margin is real, the private-label model is
coherent, and the store base is large enough to matter. It is not a PROCEED
either, because the one number that would settle the case does not exist in
public form, the loss is widening, and the price already assumes the win.

The framework's rule for this cell is explicit: no position until the proof
gate fires and the price re-opens the gap. Both conditions, not either.

**The gate, stated so it can be checked**
Group profit before tax loss must narrow for two consecutive quarters while
revenue grows. EBITDA positivity alone does not count, because rent sits below
EBITDA in this company's accounts.

**Load-bearing facts a later run must verify first**
1. Revenue per store, store-level contribution and payback, split COCO against
   FOFO.
2. Store closure rate. A chain adding 200 stores a quarter can hide closures.
3. The full FY26 equity raise: who subscribed, at what price, and where the
   Rs 263.88 crore of investments sits.
4. Promoter holding and pledge.
5. Lease commitments and the maturity profile behind the Rs 224.36 crore of
   right-of-use assets.
6. Whether the Everyday Herbal, export and domestic verticals are being
   starved to fund the chain. All three are flat.

**What would change the view**
- Two quarters of narrowing pre-tax loss on growing revenue moves this to
  PROCEED.
- Disclosure of same-store metrics, whatever they show, moves this forward
  because it makes the thesis testable.
- A third quarter of widening loss makes the ugliness structural and the
  verdict PASS.

No Role 1 valuation was run and no target price is set. This verdict decides only whether the
name earns a full `/step1`, and it is not a position.
