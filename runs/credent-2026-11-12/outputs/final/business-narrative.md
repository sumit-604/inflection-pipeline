# CREDENT: business narrative (phase 1, evidence stages only)

Credent Connect N Care Ltd. Run 2026-11-12. Phase 1 lite: no valuation has run, the Mental Model is not signed, and this file carries no price view. Units: the filings report in INR Lakhs and the presentations in INR Mn; figures below are converted to ₹ Cr and rounded for reading (100 Lakhs = ₹1 Cr; 10 Mn = ₹1 Cr).

## BUSINESS UNDERSTANDING NARRATIVE

Credent sells three services to diagnostic labs and hospitals. Its phlebotomists draw blood at a patient's home or at a client's centre, and this healthcare services line made up 43% of FY26 revenue of ₹214 Cr. Its riders and vans carry those samples to the lab inside a 2 to 8 degree cold chain and a time window, and this logistics line made up 25%. Its operations and supply chain line posts lab technicians and field staff inside client labs, and it made up another 25%. A lab needs this work done because a sample that arrives late or warm is a failed test, but the lab can hire the same people itself, and Credent's contracts are terminable on notice. The buyers are a small group of organised diagnostic chains, pharma and IVD companies: the top customer took 15.76% of FY26 revenue and the top ten took 81.76%, out of more than 280 customers. On the results call management named Dr Lal, Metropolis, Apollo, Thyrocare, Neuberg, Lupin, Torrent and LifeCell as clients, but no filing names a customer, and the RHP records no top ten customer lost in the three years before the IPO. Present demand comes from chain test volumes and the shift to home collection, so "Thyrocare, Dr Lal, Metropolis quarterly volumes and collection-cost line" is the first outside signal, and "Credent top-10 customer share and top-1 customer" is the second. Forward demand rests on the diagnostics market growing about 11% a year, on the "Organised share of diagnostics and diagnostic market growth", and on "NABL accreditation and diagnostic lab regulation", which could push chains toward specialised sample handlers. The newest driver is "Wockhardt and Thyrocare prepaid On-Demand wallet onboarding", a pay per order product with no revenue disclosed yet. "Labour code and minimum wage changes" sit on the cost side of the same demand, because deployed people carry about 68% of revenue. The run did not establish what share of a chain's collection and logistics spend is bought from outside vendors, and that share decides how large the real market is. No business line holds a proven moat. Healthcare services and the stationed staff line are labour supply with weak pricing power, and the emerging moat scan found that a rival must destroy nothing to compete, because a large chain can insource collection at its own pace. Logistics has the best claim to an advantage through density, with 2,530 associated labs and more than 19,000 pincodes, but route level economics are not disclosed and density was scored as a claim. Thyrocare, a named customer, runs its own fleet of about 2,000 to 2,100 phlebotomists, which shows the largest buyers can do this work in house. The Alltrak tracking software was bought in February 2026 and has no disclosed adoption, and the scan scored the whole business 10.5 with no category above weak.

Fast growth, slow cash, and contested claims

Credent listed on NSE Emerge on 20 August 2026 and raised ₹93.9 Cr gross in its IPO. It reports half yearly, so the corpus holds one listed result (H1 FY27), one call and one annual report. At FY26 year end it ran 6,338 people, of which 4,052 were on payroll and 2,286 were contract staff. It bills per collection, per pickup or per stationed head, and the price per unit is not disclosed anywhere in the filings. Fixed and intangible assets are light, at 11.5% of total assets, so growth is bound by hiring and by working capital, not by plant.

The company changed shape in FY26, and it changed by acquisition. In FY25 it was mostly a logistics business, with logistics at 65% of restated revenue. In FY26 it bought three companies from the promoter side for a combined ₹11.5 Cr: Credent Healthcare from Ashok Kumar Sharma and Dimple Sharma, Credent Team from Karan and Dimple Sharma, and Alltrak from the COO and the CMD for ₹1 Lakh. Credent Healthcare alone now brings 43% of group revenue. Entities bought in the year supplied 41% of FY26 group profit. Alltrak booked ₹1.7 Cr of its ₹2.0 Cr full year profit in the 34 days after Credent took control. That window has two readings: lumpy software billing after the change of owner, or income placed inside the consolidated period. Alltrak's monthly revenue for April to September 2026 would separate them, and it is not disclosed. Management calls its 45% revenue growth plan to FY30 organic. The annual report says the FY26 step up came largely from consolidating the three subsidiaries. Standalone revenue rose 70%, or 20% once the operations and supply chain line is set aside.

The backward scorecard reads AVERAGE at 93 of 160, with the core at 72 of 100 and the moat block at 21 of 60. Two rules cap it: cumulative operating cash flow is below zero against three years of profit, and the cash block scored 1 of 20. Only three years of history exist, and FY24 and FY25 are standalone figures while FY26 is consolidated. FY26 ROCE of 51% and the jump in profit sit on a year end consolidation, so they are not an organic run rate. EBITDA margin rose from 6.4% on the restated FY25 base to 13.3% in FY26, and it held flat at about 13% in H1 FY27. Part of that gain came from other expenses falling from 23% to 13% of revenue, and the filings do not explain why. Accounting quality scored 4 of 10. A second audit cut FY25 profit from ₹5.5 Cr to ₹2.2 Cr, and the company changed four accounting policies in three years. On the other side, the balance sheet carries little hidden exposure: one GST demand of ₹0.6 Cr, no currency risk, no pledge and no going concern language.

Profit is not turning into cash. FY26 operating cash flow was ₹6.6 Cr negative against ₹18.4 Cr of profit, and H1 FY27 was ₹3.9 Cr negative. Standalone receivables grew 127% while standalone revenue grew 70%, and receivable days rose from about 80 to about 106. Group receivables reached ₹78.8 Cr at September 2026, up 34% in six months, in a half with no new acquisition. No provision for bad debts exists, ₹2.4 Cr has aged one to two years against ₹0.04 Cr a year earlier, and no balance can be shown as not yet due because no credit period is set in the system. Management says debtors age 60 days and cash flow turns positive "maybe by next year, H2". The RHP itself plans 95 debtor days for FY27 and FY28, and no credit rating report exists to set beside either claim. Borrowings tripled to ₹23.2 Cr in FY26, and ₹8.7 Cr of that was third party term debt at 13.5% to 16%. At September 2026 the balance sheet held ₹74.2 Cr of cash and bank, of which ₹72.25 Cr is unspent IPO money, and total debt stayed at about ₹21.6 Cr. Six days after the call said no deal was final, the board approved a ₹4.50 Cr unsecured loan to Kartavya Healtheon, outside the IPO objects, with an option to take up to 26% of its equity. The company says Kartavya is unrelated, and the run could not find who owns it.

Management's word and the filings disagree on six checkable points. The call said no customer exceeds 10%; the RHP and the annual report print 15.76% for FY26. The call said growth is organic, debtors age 60 days, ROE and ROCE are 42% and 50%, and the market is ₹1,000 Cr. The filings show acquired growth, about 100 days, ROE of 61.8% and ROCE of 40.0%, and the MD's own arithmetic gives a ₹10,000 Cr market. On dated promises the record is better: 2 of 7 delivered, 5 partly delivered and none missed, including the ₹6.00 Cr debt repayment from IPO money. The credibility grade is C. The red flag verifier reads it lower, at the C to D boundary, because the MD named two prepaid clients to one analyst while calling the names private information. The promoter verdict is CONCERN for the related party purchases, an auditor exit at management request, and a promoter who sat on the disqualified directors list from 2016 to 2021. Against that, no regulator order, litigation or pledge was found, and the new CFO is an outside professional who worked at Dr Lal, Metropolis and Pathkind. Two independent directors joined in 2025, and ten anchor investors bought about ₹26.5 Cr in the IPO.

Peers complicate the story more than they confirm it. Krystal, the manpower peer that grew 46%, runs about 118 receivable days, and TCI Express, the cleanest logistics peer, runs 58 to 60. Thyrocare runs 30 to 40 days blended, but that rests on a prepaid franchise model, not on outsourced service billing, so the under 45 day target has no precedent in a business shaped like Credent's. Margins point the same way. Krystal guides EBITDA of 6.25% to 7.5%, and TCI Express sits at 11% to 13% with 15% as a two to three year goal, so the 15% to 16% group margin plan needs a mix change that no segment data yet supports. No peer shows 45% organic growth, and market growth is cited at 10% to 11%. Thyrocare, a named Credent customer, describes itself as probably the largest phlebotomy network in India and sells home collection to its own partners, so it competes as well as buys. It is also adding labs so that fewer samples travel to its central lab, which works against Credent's long haul sample logistics. The claim that new cities carry no launch losses is only partly tested: peers report margin dips on new contracts, and Credent itself conceded some launch cost on the call.

The market opportunity is real but smaller than the plan. The run sized outsourced pre-analytics for organised chains at ₹2,770 Cr to ₹4,344 Cr. The serviceable slice is ₹434 Cr if outside vendors win 10% of that spend, and ₹869 Cr if they win 20%; the share is not evidenced either way. On the 10% reading Credent already holds 46% of its served market, revenue headroom is 2.2 times, and the runway class is MODERATE. The central three year obtainable revenue of ₹304 Cr by FY29 sits below management's own FY27 guidance of more than ₹320 Cr. The FY30 target of about ₹1,000 Cr would need more than the whole serviceable market on the 10% reading, and the ₹1,000 Cr that management calls its market is the same number as its revenue target. The market data itself is dated: the organised share figure of 17% comes from a November 2023 industry report.
