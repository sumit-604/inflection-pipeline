## 1. ACCOUNTING POLICIES & CHANGES

- Basis of preparation: Ind AS, historical cost convention, first full year as a listed company (IPO 3 Oct 2025). (Note 2, p.101 standalone / Note 2, p.156 consolidated) 🟢
- No policy changes with quantified P&L impact disclosed this year. Recent pronouncements (Ind AS 1, 12, 21, 107, amended Ind AS 7 on supplier financing) assessed as "no significant impact." (Note 4, p.114 standalone / Note 4, p.168 consolidated) 🟢
- Revenue recognition — Turnkey Contracts: single performance obligation, revenue and costs recognised "based on actual shipments as of the reporting date" for contracts spanning multiple accounting periods, not a cost-to-cost percentage-of-completion measure. This is a judgment area that gives management some timing latitude near period-end (e.g., front-loading shipments). No dedicated contract-asset/contract-liability reconciliation or "unsatisfied performance obligations" disclosure is presented anywhere in the standalone or consolidated notes — a gap under Ind AS 115 para 120 disclosure requirements. (Note 3(D), p.103-104 standalone / Note 3(D), p.158-159 consolidated) 🟡 Watch — disclosure gap on revenue recognised over time.
- Depreciation useful lives: Furniture & Fixtures 10y, Motor Vehicle 6y, Office Equipment 5y, Computer & Peripherals 3y (all per Schedule II — no deviation for these). One deviation disclosed: Leasehold Improvement depreciated over 9 years (lease term) vs Schedule II default of 10 years for Furniture & Fixtures — explained and immaterial. (Note 3(H), p.105-106 standalone / Note 3(H), p.160-161 consolidated) 🟢
- Capitalisation threshold: assets costing ≤ INR 5,000 are fully depreciated in year of acquisition. (Note 3(H), p.106 standalone) 🟢
- Impairment test assumptions (growth/discount rate) for the ~₹2,296.83 Lakh consolidated Goodwill (arising from the FTS Cleanrooms Systems LLC, UAE, and FT Institutions acquisitions): policy language only ("tested for impairment annually") — no FY26 quantified discount rate, growth rate, or sensitivity is disclosed anywhere in the consolidated notes. NOT FOUND IN DOCUMENT. (Note 3(G)/3(J), p.159-161 consolidated; Note 6 & Note 52-56, p.170, 198-199 consolidated) 🟡 Watch — disclosure gap on a material (5.5% of consolidated net worth) intangible.
- Ind AS 116 leases: incremental borrowing rate is referenced as the discounting method but no numeric IBR is disclosed for FY26 or FY25 in either standalone or consolidated lease notes. NOT FOUND IN DOCUMENT (rate). ROU assets net ₹2,585.38 Lakh (standalone, 2026) vs ₹2,998.50 Lakh (2025); Lease liability ₹2,676.44 Lakh (2026) vs ₹2,902.59 Lakh (2025). Consolidated: ROU ₹2,597.70 Lakh, Lease liability ₹2,690.20 Lakh (2026). (Note 49, p.136 standalone / Note 51, p.197 consolidated) 🟡
- ECL matrix: "simplified approach," provision matrix based on historically observed default rates adjusted for forward-looking information; bucket rates ~0.2-0.4% (<6mo) rising to ~10% (1yr+). Rates are broadly stable YoY per bucket, but the absolute allowance more than doubled because far more receivables migrated into the higher-provision 1-2 year bucket (see Section 4). Company itself flags this as a "critical accounting estimate" (Note 3B, p.103 standalone / p.157-158 consolidated), consistent with the auditor's Key Audit Matter on receivables recoverability (cross-referenced in the audit opinion, standalone p.85-86 / consolidated p.139-140, citing overdue trade receivables >365 days of ₹5,854.04 Lakh standalone / ₹5,887.55 Lakh consolidated before ECL adjustment). 🟡 Watch.
- No first-time standard adoptions this year beyond routine amendments assessed as immaterial.
- No product-specific warranty provision: warranties are "back-to-back" with the vendor, covered for 18 months from sale or 12 months from installation whichever is earlier; company records no separate warranty provision because risk sits with the vendor. (Note 51, p.137 standalone) 🟢 (if vendor warranty coverage is genuine, this is a clean pass-through; if vendor recourse ever fails, this is an unprovided contingent exposure — no quantification given).

## PASS 1 SUMMARY

Fabtech's FY26 notes present a business in transition: a clean-opinion, unmodified audit, straightforward single-segment turnkey pharma-infrastructure model, and a large de-leveraging/net-cash swing from a ₹23,029.65 Lakh IPO completed 3 Oct 2025. Against that backdrop, three interlocking themes recur across otherwise routine notes: (1) receivables quality is genuinely deteriorating (1-2 year ageing bucket +389%, ECL rate more than tripled, related-party share of receivables nearly doubling) even though the headline ">6 months" ratio looks better; (2) the related-party network is unusually large and economically significant (21% of purchases, 16% of standalone revenue, 99.4% of the loan book, on-demand terms, no disclosed interest rate on the largest loan tranche) for a company that just went public with no prospectus available in this review; and (3) customer concentration has not disappeared, it has rotated — three prior >10% customers fell below threshold in the same year a new one crossed it. A material portion (77.8%) of fresh IPO proceeds remained unutilised and parked in fixed deposits at year end, an auditor-flagged delay against the stated use-of-proceeds. A standalone Note 10 tax-reconciliation table shows an internal arithmetic gap (~₹75.6 Lakh) warranting a Pass 3 cross-check, though it does not appear to misstate the reported tax expense itself. MSME payment-timeliness is worsening (interest accrued +149%, a new MSME-interest provision line appearing for the first time). None of these, individually, rises to a going-concern or fraud-level concern on the face of the notes — the auditor's opinion is unmodified and the company is now net-cash — but taken together they argue for PROCEED WITH CAVEATS at minimum on notes quality, with receivables/related-party/customer-concentration named explicitly.
