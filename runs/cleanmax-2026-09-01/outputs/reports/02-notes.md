  Board's non-uncertainty conclusion is not independently corroborated or
  contradicted by the principal auditor, but IS contradicted at the single
  named subsidiary level by that subsidiary's own component auditor. This
  is a genuine internal tension the consolidated Note 58 paragraph does
  not surface or reconcile.

**4. Events after balance sheet date**: 2-Apr-2026 prepayment of ₹599 Cr
of 11.50% NCDs from IPO proceeds (Note 45, AR p.450), already correctly

**5. Deliberately vague vs granular disclosure — a cross-note pattern**:
placing Pass 1 and Pass 2 findings side by side shows a consistent split.
Where a disclosure is quantitatively favourable or routine (gratuity
sensitivity, EPS dilution, segment revenue, receivables ageing), the notes
are granular with full tables. Where a disclosure would let an investor
size a risk (ECL provision-matrix rate table, capitalisation threshold,
covenant-breach facility identity, cumulative NCI put-option liability,

54's Clause (ix)(d) items), the language is generic or the number is

individually defensible as "not required by Ind AS" — but the pattern,

the pattern is the single most important finding across all three passes:
three independent, compliant, disclosed accounting choices point the same
direction (lower reported expense) in the same year the investment thesis
needs profitability to look real.

PASS 3 yields one material new synthesis (item 6 above, the three-change
cluster) built from findings both prior passes already extracted

## E. NOTES-BASED RED FLAGS

- **Earnings management pattern (cluster, not a single item)**: three independently compliant, disclosed accounting choices — useful-life extension (25→30 yrs), a favourable impairment discount-rate methodology widening, and a jump in interest/cost capitalisation ratio (~7–10% → ~28–30%) — all land in the same fiscal year as the reported operating-profit crossover and the IPO. No single item is a violation; the concentration and direction are the flag.
- **Aggressive cash-flow classification**: reverse-factoring "Acceptances against capital and other creditors" (₹1,730.92 Cr, up 4.1x) classified in investing rather than financing cash flows, a policy choice that keeps short-term bank-intermediated payables financing out of the financing section.
- **Undisclosed risk indicators**: ECL provision-matrix rate table not disclosed (cannot test receivables-provisioning conservatism); asset capitalisation threshold not disclosed; no asset retirement obligation disclosed against a ₹11,744.51 Cr power-generation asset base; cumulative NCI put-option liability not disclosed as a standalone balance (only the annual incremental transfer, ₹8.52 Cr FY26); precise CARO clause text behind two flagged entities (Clean Max Power 4, Clean Max Terra) not reproduced in the Annexure summary.
- **103 group entities with qualified/adverse CARO remarks**, the single largest scale finding in the document, is disclosed (it is a statutory Annexure requirement, not a voluntary disclosure) but sits in the audit report's Annexure A rather than in the Notes proper, and is not referenced anywhere in the consolidated Note 58 going-concern paragraph despite one of the 103 (Patagonia) being a direct going-concern-adjacent flag.
- **Opacity requiring cross-document reading**: the true scale of intra-group parent-to-subsidiary transaction authorisation (≈₹14,800+ Cr in ceilings) and inter-SPV cash pooling (≈₹3,011 Cr+) is disclosed only in the AGM Notice, a document outside the Notes to Financial Statements and outside where a Notes-only read would look; the standalone-only parent loans-to-related-parties jump (4.3x to ₹1,363.66 Cr) is similarly outside the consolidated Notes' natural scope.

## F. ONE-LINE NOTES VERDICT

The notes reveal moderately concerning accounting practices: individually
compliant choices, collectively timed to flatter the crossover year.
Key concern: capitalised interest plus a useful-life extension plus 103
CARO-qualified entities mean reported FY26 profitability is materially
softer in substance than the headline P&L. Key strength: disclosure
volume and granularity are genuinely strong where the numbers are
favourable (segment, gratuity, EPS mechanics), and the CARO Annexure
itself is a transparent, if buried, red flag the auditor put on record.
Overall accounting quality: 4/10.

## RECEIVABLES / WORKING CAPITAL DETERMINATION (FLAG-CASH)

Receivables ageing itself is stable to mildly improving: >6-months share
of aged gross receivables is ~11.8% FY26 vs a proportionally worse-quality
mix in FY25 (smaller base, larger relative credit-impaired tail), and
company-memory's DSO trend (55→54→42 days) is not contradicted by the
notes (Note 13, Note 53, AR p.416–417, 486–487). Taken alone, receivables
would not trigger FLAG-CASH.

However, the broader working-capital and cash-conversion evidence across
all three passes is materially negative and warrants FLAG-CASH on group
liquidity/cash-conversion grounds, distinct from receivables quality:
consolidated current liabilities exceed current assets by ₹1,724.10 Cr
(Note 58); MSME late-payment interest was newly incurred in FY26
(₹1.12 Cr vs Nil FY25) even as MSME principal dues fell, i.e. payment
discipline to small suppliers worsened during the capex ramp (Note 39);
reverse-factoring payables financing (₹1,730.92 Cr, 4.1x YoY) sits outside
the financing section of the cash-flow statement, understating visible
near-term financing needs (Note 26(B)); aggregate NCI profit share is
negative in both years even as fresh minority capital is injected,
meaning a large share of the operating asset base is not yet a net cash
contributor (Note 20(B)); and 103 group entities carry CARO cash-loss
qualifications. FLAG-CASH is added on this combined working-capital/cash-
conversion basis, not on receivables ageing, which the YAML records
separately as "stable to improving."
