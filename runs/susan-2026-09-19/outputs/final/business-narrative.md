## Business understanding narrative

Susan Electricals makes power cables, overhead conductors and winding wire in three plants in Ghaziabad, and it also resells raw aluminium rod that it does not process. Low tension (LT) cables and aerial bunched cables carry power on the last stretch of a distribution network, from the local transformer to homes and shops, and LT cable was about 31% of FY26 revenue. Winding wire is the aluminium or copper wire that transformer makers wind into coils; it was 24% of revenue in Q1 FY27, down from 48% a year earlier. High tension cable up to 33 kV and medium voltage covered conductor (MVCC), an insulated overhead line that replaces bare wire for safety and reliability, form the new line and were 7% of Q1 FY27 revenue. The aluminium rod trading line brought in ₹72 Cr in FY26, and all bought in products resold together came to ₹103 Cr, or 38% of revenue.

A discom cannot upgrade its network without BIS certified cable from a registered vendor, so the licence and the registration matter more than the product. The buyers are state discoms in Uttar Pradesh, Haryana, Telangana, Jharkhand, Bihar, Madhya Pradesh and Rajasthan, EPC contractors such as Monte Carlo and RVNL, transformer makers, and buyers of raw metal. Discoms buy by tender at the lowest price, and the government share of revenue fell from 91% in FY24 to 36% in FY26 as private buyers took over. These buyers do not stay long: repeat customers fell from 89% to 45% of revenue over the same two years, and the top ten customers fell from 98% to 64%.

Present demand comes from grid upgrade spending under RDSS, the central scheme for discom reform, with ₹2.81 lakh Cr sanctioned across 46 discoms. The tracker names for that link are "RDSS sanctioned/disbursed outlay by DISCOM", "State DISCOM tender awards" and "EPC contractor order pipeline", while input costs follow "LME aluminium price" and "LME copper price" and every tender depends on "BIS product licence status / renewal". Demand should grow as MVCC replaces bare conductors under RDSS and as data centres, electric vehicles and rail electrification add load, in a market the company's own commissioned industry report puts at 8% a year to 2035. Whether that growth reaches Susan depends on capacity rising from 7,500 to 12,000 km a year by February 2027, which the "Monitoring Agency (Brickwork Ratings) quarterly IPO-proceeds utilisation report" will show. The scheme is also the main risk, because ₹2.81 lakh Cr of the ₹3.04 lakh Cr outlay was already sanctioned by January 2026 and the window closes in FY27 or FY28 unless extended.

The cable lines hold the only evidenced advantage: eight BIS product licences, ISO certificates, and vendor registrations in seven states that a new entrant needs time to earn. A funded rival can earn the same registrations and buy the same imported CCV line from the same Chinese vendor, so the cannibalization barrier (Category 22, what a rival must give up to copy it) scores zero, and talent asymmetry (Category 21) also scores zero. Winding wire shows no moat, and the one listed winding wire peer earns a company margin of 4% to 5%. Trading has no moat by the company's own prospectus, which calls its entry barriers relatively low. The emerging moat scan scores 10.7 against a 12 point floor for modest development, which the run classifies as no meaningful emerging moat.

Cable climb funded by debt, margins unproven

Susan listed on the BSE SME platform on 18 June 2026. FY26 revenue rose 98% to ₹269 Cr, and profit rose to ₹18 Cr from ₹5 Cr. Of that revenue, ₹161 Cr came from products it made and ₹103 Cr from metal and products it bought and resold. The aluminium rod trading line went from almost nothing to ₹72 Cr in one year, so it carried a large part of the FY26 jump.

The plan is to move out of winding wire and trading and into HT and MVCC cable. HT and MVCC rose from 2% to 7% of quarterly revenue in a year. Capacity is to go from 7,500 to 12,000 km a year by February 2027 on a ₹10 Cr project paid for from the IPO. Only ₹1.2 Cr of it was spent by 30 June 2026, but that window was 12 days after listing, and the monitoring agency reports no deviation. The balance sheet at 31 March 2026 showed no capital work in progress and no capital commitments. The proof that the climb is real has not arrived.

Gate 0 scores the business AVERAGE, with a core score of 52 out of 100. The growth block takes full marks, but off a FY21 profit of ₹8 lakh. The cash block scores zero out of 20. After the verifier correction, the total is 70 out of 160 with four moats confirmed. Operating margin rose from 3.5% in FY24 to 12% in FY26 and was 11.9% in Q1 FY27. The annual report's highlights page labels profit before tax as EBITDA; true EBITDA was about ₹32 Cr. The margin does not come from inventory accounting: Q4 FY26 earned about 14% with almost no inventory credit. Q1 FY27 was weaker than Q4, with revenue down 17.5%, profit before tax down 38% and margin down about 2 points. Management spoke only of the year on year gain. Peers say Q1 is the seasonal low for this kind of cable.

Operating cash flow was negative in FY24, FY25 and FY26 while profit rose. FY26 cash flow was minus ₹9.7 Cr on ₹18.3 Cr of profit. Borrowings rose from ₹25 Cr to ₹67 Cr in two years. CRISIL names a high working capital cycle, with gross current assets at 147 days and bank limits about 90% used. Finished goods stock rose 168% while revenue rose 98%, and Q1 FY27 put another ₹9.3 Cr into finished goods. On the other side, receivable days improved from 51 to 48, the IPO brought ₹60 Cr of fresh equity, and CRISIL expects net worth near ₹130 Cr by March 2027. The run cannot yet say whether the cash drain comes from growth or from the business model. The first post IPO cash flow statement, for the half year to September 2026, decides it.

Accounting quality scores 4 out of 10. The FY24 and FY25 accounts as first filed left out gratuity, bonus and interest owed to small suppliers, and the IPO restatement cut FY24 profit by 40%. A GST search, with ₹50 lakh of goods seized, appears in the annual report only as a routine demand notice. Fixed deposit totals do not agree across three notes. The promoter read is CAUTION. Filings with the Registrar of Companies ran up to 5,542 days late over 15 years. Co-promoter Mahak Jain's own firm, SMV Enterprises, competes in the same industry and sold ₹11 Cr of goods to Susan in FY26, 20 times the FY25 figure. Against that, promoter pledge is zero, promoter loans carry 6% interest, the SEBI record is clean, and a former NCLT member joined the board in August 2026.

Management credibility grades C, capped by having only one earnings call. That call gave the FY27 HT and MVCC target three ways: 15%, 50%, and an even 30% to 35% mix. The transcript is an edited summary, and the audio file settles which number was said. The winding wire margin of 24.39% matches winding wire's revenue share to two decimals, while the opening script said 10% to 15%. On delivery, the HT and MVCC share rose in the promised direction, and both IPO objects are being spent without deviation. Questions on debt and on metal price pass through got answers with no content.

All three peers pass metal prices through to buyers under named clauses. Susan named none. Vidya Wires, a listed winding wire maker, earns 4.3% to 4.7% company wide. Dynamic Cables puts the long run ceiling for this kind of B2B cable at 10% to 11%, which bears on Susan's 20% HT and MVCC claim. No peer runs a trading line like Susan's. All three peers are adding capacity at the same time. Their 12 to 17 month plant timelines apply to larger greenfield projects, so they do not show that Susan's ₹10 Cr add on an existing site will slip.

The served market is ₹28,770 Cr inside a total market of ₹48,490 Cr to ₹63,934 Cr, and Susan holds 0.56% of it. Demand is not the limit. Capacity is. The three year obtainable revenue is set near ₹700 Cr from management's own peak revenue reference of ₹700 Cr to ₹800 Cr, and no analyst tested that figure. Dynamic Cables' asset turns put ₹15 Cr to ₹20 Cr of capex at ₹90 Cr to ₹140 Cr of added revenue. Management's industry figure of ₹98,498 Cr is twice the conservative total market estimate for what Susan can actually serve.
