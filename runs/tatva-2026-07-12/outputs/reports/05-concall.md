**Pattern check:** Management does not routinely blame externals while crediting itself internally — genuine external shocks (tariffs, Iran-driven RM inflation) are disclosed with real numbers and proactively (the tariff impact on the hybrid-battery electrolyte was volunteered in the Q2 FY26 call before being directly asked). However, on company-controlled execution items (Jolva timeline, semiconductor dispatch date) there is a recurring pattern of reframing delays as process improvements rather than saying "we missed this." No instance across the three calls of management explicitly saying "we made a mistake" was found. Hard topics (PASC cost/revenue mismatch, ROCE, tax rate spike, dividend-vs-borrowing tension) were generally addressed when asked by analysts rather than raised unprompted, with the exception of the tariff-affected hybrid-battery disclosure.

## SECTION 3: COMPETITIVE INTELLIGENCE FROM CONCALLS

### 3A Competitor commentary

- **Sarchem (SDA/zeolite catalyst space):** Management describes the SDA market as an effective duopoly — Sarchem (a US manufacturer) holds an estimated ~85% domestic share versus Tatva's ~15%, but Tatva claims a pricing advantage versus the US manufacturer and expects to capture up to 70% share on new Euro7-approved models given early qualification (Q4 FY26 call, Chintan Shah/Jay Vaghasia Q&A, p.12-13). Credibility check: specific and internally consistent across the call, but entirely self-reported with no independent corroboration in the transcripts — a strong peer-verification candidate.
- **Domestic PTC competitors:** Management states PTC (Phase Transfer Catalyst) chemistry is easily replicated — competitor count grew from 2 companies (when Tatva started) to "9 or 10" today (Q2 FY26 call, Chintan Shah, p.13). This is presented candidly as the reason the company pivoted toward SDA/electrolytes/semiconductors, a credible and self-aware admission rather than a promotional claim.
- **Semiconductor chemicals customers/competition:** Two Indian semiconductor molecules are described as having "no global competition" currently and targeted at Make-in-India fab demand (Q2 FY26 call, p.12) — an unverifiable claim given at pilot stage, appropriate for peer/industry cross-check.

### 3B Industry and market intelligence

- **Chemical industry cycle:** Destocking "clearly over," restocking onset with "cautious optimism" (Q2 FY26); "stabilization and recovery" with more predictable order patterns (Q3 FY26); further improvement into FY27 albeit against a backdrop of new geopolitical raw-material shocks (Q4 FY26).
- **Pricing environment:** Chemical pricing erosion of "nearly 25%, 30%" over the prior 3 years cited (Q2 FY26, Chintan Shah, p.14); by Q3 FY26 management believes "we have found the bottom" though prices are not yet sustaining an uptrend; Q4 FY26 introduces a fresh raw-material shock (Iran-driven amines price +30-40%) that is being passed through with a lag.
- **Regulatory pipeline:** Euro 7 emission norms cited repeatedly as an SDA tailwind, implementation beginning in Europe with global rollout through 2027-2030 (Q2/Q3/Q4 FY26 calls consistently).
- **Import-export/China trend:** China SDA demand described as "practically negligible" post-downturn, with rest-of-world demand concentrating on hybrid/conventional vehicles rather than full-EV (Q2 FY26, Chintan Shah, p.8) — not revisited in later calls.
- **Agrochemical demand caution (analyst-sourced):** Sanjesh Jain (ICICI Securities) cited SRF's commentary that the crop-protection industry remains under pricing pressure with an uncertain recovery (Q3 FY26 call, p.5) — in tension with Tatva's own confident, product-specific agro-intermediate demand visibility; management's response leaned on its "insignificant volume to total demand" and product-specific import-substitution positioning rather than addressing the broader industry caution directly.
- **Semiconductor qualification cycle:** Management describes an 8-9-year development-to-first-commercial-dispatch cycle for its semiconductor chemicals program, with a two-tier qualification process (integrator MNC, then end fab customer in Taiwan via Japanese/Korean intermediaries) revealed for the first time in Q4 FY26 (p.14).

### 3D Customer and order book signals

- SDA: two new customers onboarding — one begins invoicing ~August 2026 (final contract signing stage), the other in final negotiations targeting H2 CY26 commercialization (Q3 FY26 call, p.16).
- Electrolyte Salts/ESS: described as sole India supplier of hybrid-battery electrolytes among a small global handful of suppliers (Q2 FY26, Chintan Shah, p.16) — an unverified, strong claim.
- Pharma intermediates: dossier-based, multistage synthesis (Stage 4-6 of a longer chain); Tatva is explicitly not a sole-source supplier — "two or three different companies globally offering the same product" (Q2 FY26, p.17) — a useful counterweight to some of the more bullish exclusivity claims elsewhere.
- Agro import-substitute product (Jolva-linked): samples "approved by a couple of customers," small trials validated, capex sanctioned on confidence of scale-up (Q3 FY26 call, p.6).
- Semiconductor: first genuine plant-scale trial order received, described explicitly as "a few thousand dollars" and possibly not leading to an ongoing order even if technically successful (Q3 FY26 call, p.10) — management's own framing tempers the segment's near-term materiality.
