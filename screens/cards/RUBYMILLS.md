# The Ruby Mills Ltd — shallow analysis

- Ticker: RUBYMILLS (BSE 503169, ISIN INE301D01026). Textiles and Mumbai commercial real estate.
- Analysed 2026-09-06 per `screens/SHALLOW_ANALYSIS_FRAMEWORK.md`, from the corpus in `screens/corpus/RUBYMILLS/`. Every number cites a file and page. No forensics, no valuation.
- Price +86% year to date, 4.8% below the 52-week high; market value about ₹1,432 crore (Trendlyne, 2026-09-04). Passes the under-150% filter.

## Business understanding narrative

Ruby Mills is two businesses in one 1917 company. One weaves premium fabric and interlining, the stiff inner layer of a shirt collar, from mills near Khopoli. The other owns The Ruby, a landmark office tower in Dadar in central Mumbai, with a large plot of land beside it. The real estate is the larger asset by far: ₹801 crore of segment assets against ₹297 crore for textiles. It earns rent from blue-chip tenants and sells fabric to garment makers.

What is changing is on the real-estate side. For years the tower was held under a development agreement with an outside developer who took a share of the rent. This year that agreement was cancelled and registered, the leased floors came fully to Ruby, and the rent now flows to the company. In August it sold a single vacant floor to Torrent Pharmaceuticals for ₹124 crore.

Why now: the dispute that had clouded the asset for years is settled, so the value is finally visible and monetisable.

For the thesis to work, the rent must keep servicing the debt taken to buy the asset outright, the big FY27 lease renewal must roll over, and the land beside the tower must be developed or sold.

What breaks it: leverage near five times, a tower at 67% occupancy where three tenants are 75% of the leased area with a renewal due, and an Enforcement Directorate attachment the rating agency names as the governance issue.

## 1. Corpus ledger

Held: FY26 and FY25 annual reports; the June-2026 and March-2026 quarterly results; the India Ratings rationale of 16 February 2026 and two Acuité documents; and the exchange filings for the Torrent sale, the registered cancellation of the development agreement, and the June and July work-stoppage and resumption. Gap: no concall exists, so there is no dated management guidance beyond the annual report; the value of the remaining floors and the land is not quantified in any held document, which is the one number the asset case turns on.

## 2. Business model and archetype

Two reported segments. In the June 2026 quarter, textiles revenue ₹57.6 crore and real estate ₹33.9 crore; segment assets textiles ₹297 crore, real estate ₹801 crore. (`results/RUBYMILLS-result-20260812-cc434364-c595-4d40-af05-91afd0f98539.txt`, page 3.) So the operating business is textiles, but the balance sheet is a Mumbai property company. It is "an integrated textile mill" with spinning and weaving at Dhamni and processing at Kharsundi, plus the tower. (`annual-report/Annual_Report_2026.txt`, page 99.)

Archetype: **a licence/scarcity asset, the Mumbai land and tower, with a brand-and-spec textile operating business attached**. On the ladder the real-estate value sits closest to R0 in operating terms, valued by NAV and rent rather than by an operating multiple; the textile arm is a modest R3 niche.

## 3. Competitive advantages

Two, both narrow and durable. The real estate is irreplaceable: The Ruby is a Grade A+ landmark in Dadar with tenants such as Ernst & Young, Cathay Pacific and DSP Asset Managers, who together hold 75% of the leased area, plus over a million square feet of development potential on the adjoining land. (India Ratings; `annual-report/Annual_Report_2026.txt`, page 52.) You cannot make more central-Mumbai land. The textile edge is a niche: a 51-year collaboration with Gygli Textile AG for Micro Dot Fusible Interlining, and vertical integration across spinning, weaving and processing. (`annual-report/Annual_Report_2026.txt`, page 29.) Neither is a growth engine; both are moats around value rather than around compounding.

## 4. The promoters

Promoters, directors and their relatives hold 74.90%. (`annual-report/Annual_Report_2026.txt`, page 72.) The Shah family runs it into the third and fourth generation: Hiren Manharlal Shah executive chairman, associated 51 years; Bharat and Viraj Shah managing directors; Purav Shah executive director, chief executive and chief financial officer; with Aruna Manharlal Shah as chairman emeritus. (Same file, pages 4 and 29.)

High skin in the game, and a long operating record. The caution is governance, not commitment: the loans that bought the tower outright carry personal guarantees from two promoter directors (`annual-report/Annual_Report_2026.txt`, page 127), and the Enforcement Directorate matter below is the item the rating agency flags for the promoters to resolve.

## 5. Financial trajectory

Revenue steady, margin swinging, leverage rising, earnings flat despite an asset that is now working harder (India Ratings; quarterly result):

- FY23 revenue ₹260 crore, EBITDA margin 19.9%.
- FY24 revenue ₹237 crore, margin 23.4%.
- FY25 revenue ₹243 crore, margin 18.7%; net leverage 4.9x, up from 2.5x.
- June 2026 quarter revenue ₹91.5 crore, up 31%; real estate revenue tripled to ₹33.9 crore; but pre-tax profit flat at ₹16.3 crore, because finance cost rose to ₹9.9 crore from ₹0.9 crore and depreciation to ₹12.9 crore from ₹2.7 crore. (`results/RUBYMILLS-result-20260812-cc434364-c595-4d40-af05-91afd0f98539.txt`, pages 2 and 3.)

Return on capital about 6%, as expected for an asset-heavy owner. Inflection type: **an asset event, not an earnings climb**. The revenue jump is the recovered rent; the profit did not follow because the debt that unlocked the rent is now being serviced.

## 6. The transition and the quality ladder

This is not an operating rung climb. It is an asset re-rating: turning a disputed, half-owned tower into a clean, fully owned, rent-producing asset, and unlocking the land beside it. The mechanism, in the accounts: "during the year, the Holding company has executed a cancellation agreement with the Developer... the ownership of the leased assets has been transferred to TRML at end-December 2025." (`annual-report/Annual_Report_2026.txt`, page 99; India Ratings.) The stated next leg is the development of "approximately over a million square feet" on the adjoining parcel, strategy not yet decided. (`annual-report/Annual_Report_2026.txt`, page 52.)

Ladder: this belongs in the R0 asset-value lane, resolved by a sum-of-the-parts on the real estate, not by a destination PE on earnings. That is a Section 1B and framework note, and it is exactly the work a shallow read cannot do.

## 8. Proof check

The asset thesis has its proof point: a real, arm's-length sale of one floor at ₹124 crore, an unrelated buyer, is the market pricing the asset. The rent proof is in the June segment: real-estate revenue tripled once it came to Ruby. What has not yet shown is earnings, because the leverage that bought the asset is eating the rental gain. So the proof supports the NAV story, not an earnings story; the two must not be conflated.

## 9. Flags from the documents

- Net leverage near 5x and rising, from a ₹250 crore lease-rental-discounting loan taken in December 2024. (India Ratings.)
- A sizeable FY27 lease renewal, the tower at 67% occupancy, three tenants at 75% of leased area. (India Ratings.)
- Enforcement Directorate attachment of about ₹2.6 crore of bank balances, linked to a 2012 transaction with Rajput Retail, which the company is contesting; the agency's positive rating trigger is "resolution of the outstanding governance issue." (India Ratings.)
- Single-asset concentration in one Dadar building. (India Ratings.)
- Working-capital cycle of 379 days from unbilled textile inventory. (India Ratings.)
- A one-week weaving stoppage at Dhamni: work stopped 30 June 2026, resumed 7 July 2026. (`filings/Ruby-20260630-Disruption-of-Operations-due-to-Work-Stoppage.txt`, page 1; `filings/Ruby-20260707-Resumption-of-Operations-of-Weaving-Division-at-Dhamni.txt`, page 1.)

## 10. Independent check: the credit rating

India Ratings upgraded to IND BBB+/Stable on 16 February 2026 and took the rating off watch, citing a first-half FY26 EBITDA margin of 17.4% and the settlement of the developer agreement. It weighs single-asset concentration and stretched credit metrics against reputed tenants and a cash-flow waterfall that rings-fences rent for debt service. Its two named watch items are the FY27 lease renewal and the ED attachment. (`credit-ratings/IndiaRatings-2026-02-16-upgraded-IND-BBB+-Stable.txt`.)

## 11. Posture: the Transition Decision Matrix

- Proof gate: FIRED on the asset, not on earnings. The settlement is registered, the rent is flowing, a floor sold at a price.
- Ugliness: the flat profit and high leverage are an ARTIFACT of the monetisation phase, the cost of buying the asset outright, not structural decay.
- Recognition gap: partly OPEN. The price is up 86% this year and near its high, so the rent recovery is priced, but the land parcel is not yet valued by the market, and that is the open part of the gap.

Posture: **RE-RATING LIVE, on the asset**. The proof is fired, the ugly optic is the temporary cost of monetisation, and the land value is not yet in the price. Constructive, but the value is an NAV question, and the whole thesis gates on that number.

## 12. Verdict

Candidate for /step1: **PROCEED, as an asset-value case**.

Of the three, this has the most decisive documented change: the blocking uncertainty of the FY25 accounts, the developer, is gone, the rent is on the books, and a floor has traded at a real price. But the read that decides it, a sum-of-the-parts on the remaining floors and the land against the ₹1,432 crore market value and the ₹314 crore of debt, is exactly what a shallow read cannot compute.

Load-bearing facts a full /step1 must verify first:
1. The FY27 lease renewal and occupancy, since the rent now services ₹250 crore of debt.
2. The Enforcement Directorate attachment, which the rater names as the governance issue and its positive trigger.
3. A floor-by-floor count of what is still owned, benchmarked to the ₹124 crore Torrent price, plus a value for the land parcel.

What the deep run decides: the net asset value against the market value. The land is the upside; the leverage and the renewal are the downside; and both are dated to this financial year. This is a name for the framework's SOTP treatment, not a growth-multiple case.
