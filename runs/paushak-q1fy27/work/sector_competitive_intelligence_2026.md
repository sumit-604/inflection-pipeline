## The five sector signals Paushak's own numbers throw off

1. **Input costs re-inflated in early CY2026.** The West Asia raw-material cost spike
   (flagged March 2026, memory) landed in Q1 FY27: material cost jumped to 34.7% of
   revenue from 19.4% a year earlier; gross margin compressed ~1,374 bps (82.4% -> 68.65%).
   This is a feedstock/solvent-side event, not company-specific mismanagement.

2. **Pricing power is currently soft and contract-lagged.** Paushak is the dominant
   domestic phosgene-derivatives player (structural, hazardous, hard-to-replicate
   chemistry — the basis of the MOAT-STRENGTHENING rating). Yet even it could not pass the
   input spike into selling prices inside the quarter. Read across: if the domestic
   near-monopoly cannot reprice fast, sector-wide pricing power is weak right now — demand
   is back but buyers still hold the pricing whip in the short contract cycle.

3. **Demand and volumes have genuinely turned.** Revenue +49.5% YoY (MPP-8 volume live);
   FY26 exports +68.5% YoY to 17.35% of revenue (memory) against domestic -4.1%. The
   CY2023-24 destocking bottom has passed; the recovery is export-led.

4. **China+1 is real but not yet contracted at Paushak.** FY26 memory validates the
   structural pull via peers — Aether Industries (10-year Milliken contract), SRF
   (Chemours relationship). Paushak's export inflection is empirical evidence of share
   gain, but it has NOT yet announced a named long-term sole-supplier contract (Growth
   Trigger 3 = partially firing).

5. **Supply is expanding into the recovery.** MPP-8 (multi-purpose plant) commissioned;
   the FY23-25 industry capex wave is now converting to live capacity. Supply growth meets
   returning demand — which is exactly why pricing has not yet re-tightened.

## Has the chemical sector become better? — the view

**Better on demand and volume; not yet better on margin and pricing.** The sector is in
the EARLY leg of an up-cycle:

- **Turned up (bull):** destocking is over, export/China+1 pull is real and showing in
  volumes, utilisation is climbing as new plants fill. This is a demand recovery, and it
  is genuine.
- **Not yet (bear):** input costs re-inflated, pricing power lags, and new supply is
  landing at the same time — so margins are compressing even as revenue rises. The profit
  recovery (the second leg) has not arrived. The re-rating comes when pricing catches
  costs, not when volumes return.

**The clean sector turn-signal to watch:** gross-margin recovery. Use Paushak's Q2 FY27
gross margin as one proxy — bull >= 74% (pricing catching up), bear <= 69% (input squeeze
structural). Margin recovery across the specialty-chem tape, not volume growth, is what
confirms the sector has actually "become better" in the way that pays shareholders.

## Competitive intelligence — where Paushak sits

- **Moat protects volume and share, not near-term price.** Phosgene chemistry is a high
  barrier (hazardous, permit-gated, captive know-how) — it defends Paushak's volumes and
  export share through the cycle. But Q1 proves the moat did NOT confer instant pricing
  power against an input shock; the Q1 review accordingly marked the pricing-power pillar
  WEAKENED. Structural moat, cyclical pricing.
- **Export inflection is the real competitive positive** — the China+1 share-gain shows up
  in Paushak's own export line (+68.5% FY26) before it shows up in a contract headline.
- **Peer set for verification:** Aether Industries, SRF, Navin Fluorine, Atul, Vinati
  (broad specialty chem); phosgene-specific competition is thin by design (the moat).
  These are the concalls to pull for a peer-verified sector read.
