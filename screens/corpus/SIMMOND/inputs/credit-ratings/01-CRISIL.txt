 Detailed Rationale 

 Crisil Ratings has upgraded its ratings on the bank facilities of Simmonds Marshall Limited (SML) to ‘ Crisil BBB/Stable/Crisil A3+’ from ‘Crisil BBB-/Positive/Crisil A3’ . 

 The upgrade factors in sustained improvement in the business risk profile, reflected in healthy revenue growth and improved profitability along with strengthening of financial risk profile through reduction in debt and better liquidity position. 

 Operating income grew by ~14% to Rs 238 crore in fiscal 2026 from Rs 208 crore in fiscal 2025, primarily driven by a higher share of business from existing OEM customers and improving demand across key automotive segments following GST rationalisation in H2FY26. Revenue is expected to grow by 7-8% over the medium term, supported by increasing business from existing customers and growth in exports. 

 Operating margin improved to 13.0% in fiscal 2026 from 12.2% in fiscal 2025, aided by operating leverage benefits, automation initiatives and productivity improvements. Margins are expected to witness a moderate decline in fiscal 2027 due to higher steel, freight and other overheads costs arising from ongoing geopolitical disruptions. However, these increased costs will be partially passed through to customers albeit with a lag. In addition to this, cost-efficiency measures, operating leverage benefits and increasing contribution from higher-margin export business will support margins to normalize in the medium term at 12.5-13%. 

 The company's net worth had declined in the past owing to sequential losses incurred since fiscal 2020, adversely affecting leverage metrics and the overall financial risk profile. However, the company has remained profitable at the PAT level sinc e fiscal 2024. The capital structure has strengthened due to improved profitability and debt reduction. Gearing and total outside liabilities to tangible net worth stood at 1.23 times and 2.01 times, respectively, as on March 31, 2026. Debt protection metrics also strengthened, with interest coverage improving to 3.8 times in fiscal 2026 from 2.9 times in fiscal 2025 and NCA/TD improving to 0.33 times from 0.20 times over the same period. 

 Liquidity remains adequate, supported by moderate bank limit utilisa tion and improvement in working capital management. Average bank limit utilisation moderated to 59% for the 12 months ended March 31, 2026, from around 70% earlier. Expected annual cash accruals of Rs 20-30 crore are sufficient to meet annual repayment obligations of Rs 2.5-4 crore and planned capex of Rs 8-11 crore over the medium term. While the promoters have infused funds in the past, timely support in case of weaker-than-expected performance is monitorable. 

 The ratings continue to reflect the establis hed market position of SML in the nuts-and-bolts segment and strong customer relationships with major automotive original equipment manufacturers (OEMs). These strengths are partially offset by the sub-par but improving financial risk profile, the modest scale of operations with limited revenue diversity, and large working capital requirement. 

 Analytical Approach 

 To arrive at the ratings, Crisil Ratings has combined the business and financial risk profiles of Simmonds Marshall and its 99% subsidiary, Stud (India) Ltd (SIL), together referred to as Simmonds Marshall. Unsecured loans from promoters have been treated as debt for analytical purpose.

 Key Rating Drivers - Strengths 

 Established position and market presence in the domestic fasteners segment 

 SML has an established presence in the domestic fasteners industry, supported by over six decades of operating track record and a leadership position in the nuts segment. The company manufactures a diversified range of fasteners catering primarily to the automotive sector and has built considerable expertise in the cold-forged fasteners business. Its long-standing presence, healthy product diversity and proven execution capabilities have enabled it to maintain a stable market position despite operating in a competitive and fragmented industry. 

 Strong and longstanding relationships with leading automotive OEMs 

 SML benefits from long-standing relationships with several leading domestic automotive OEMs, many of which have been associated with the company for over a decade. Revenue remains diversified across key automotive segments, with around 48% contributed by two-wheelers and 21% by commercial vehicles. The company derives a significa nt portion of its revenue from established customers such as Honda Motorcycle & Scooter India, Bajaj Auto Ltd (rated ‘Crisil AAA/Stable/Crisil A1+’), Hero MotoCorp Ltd (rated ‘Crisil AAA/Stable/Crisil A1+’), Ashok Leyland, TVS Motor and VE Commercial Vehicles. However, demand from OEM customers remains a key monitorable. 

 Key Rating Drivers - We a knesses 

 Exposure to cyclicality in the automotive sector and margin volatility 

 SML derives a significant portion of its revenue from the automotive sector, with nearly 70% of revenue contributed by the two-wheeler and commercial vehicle segments. Consequently, its business performance remains susceptible to demand fluctuations and cyclicality inherent in the automotive industry. Further, profitability has historically been vulnerable to fluctuations in raw material prices, particularly steel, which constitutes a si gnificant portion of the cost structure. While the company generally passes on increases in raw material costs to customers, such revisions occur with a lag. However, ongoing automation initiatives, productivity improvements and increasing operating leverage are expected to support profitability over the medium term. 

 Modest scale of operations and moderate financial risk profile 

 SML's scale of operations remains modest, reflected in revenue of around Rs. 238 crore and net worth of Rs. 55 crore as on March 31, 2026. While the financial risk profile has improved over past few fiscals, supported by stronger profitability, debt reduction and net worth accretion, it remains modest. Gearing stood at 1.23 times as on March 31, 2026. The company also has working-capital-intensive operations, necessitating reliance on external borrowings. Nevertheless, improving cash accruals, moderation in bank limit utilisation and continued promoter support through unsecured loans provide support to the overall credit profile. 

 L iquidity Adequate 

 Bank limit utilisation was moderate, averaging 59% during the 12 months ended March 31, 2026, as against 70% earlier. While the unutilised limit provides a cushion, timely support from promoters, in the event of moderation in performance, remains crucial. Expected net cash accrual of Rs 20-30 crore will sufficiently cover the debt obligation of Rs 2-4 crore and annual capex of Rs. 8-11 crore over the medium term. 

 Outlook Stable 

 Crisil   Ratings   believes SML’s credit risk profile will benefit with improving liquidity position and debt coverage indicators on account of improving profitability and lowering debt. Timely promoter support would also be critical.

 Up wa rd factors 

 Significant improvement in scale of operations with diversification of segments and sustaining operating margins above 13% 

 Sustained improvement in financial risk profile 

 Down ward factors 

 Sharp decrease in scale of operations or operating margins declining below 6% materially impacting cash generation and debt service coverage indicators. 

 Further stretch in the working capital cycle impacting liquidity or large capex with increasing reliance of external debt thereby impacting overall debt protection parameters 

 About the Company 

 SML, incorporated in 1960, by the promoter, Mr Shiamak Marshall, manufactures industrial fasteners for the automotive segment and caters to CV and two-wheeler manufacturers. The manufacturing unit in Kasarwadi, Maharashtra, has the capacity to produce 5,500 tonne per annum of nuts. 

 In 2012, SML acquired SIL that manufactures studs for heavy CV manufacturers. In 2014, the company entered into a joint venture with Francis Kirk and Son Ltd (Francis Kirk; UK) to manufacture fasteners for the UK market. Produc ts are manufactured at the Kasarwadi plant and products are marketed by Francis Kirk.
