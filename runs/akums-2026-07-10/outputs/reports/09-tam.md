## SECTION 1: MARKET DEFINITION

### 1A Precise boundaries

**Product scope (IN):** Contract development & manufacturing (CDMO) of finished dosage forms for
Indian branded-formulation pharma companies — oral solid (tablets/capsules), oral liquid, injectable/
sterile, external/topical, across general, hormonal, cosmetic, β-lactam/steroid and penem
anti-infective capability lines (source: Investor_Presentation_1.pdf, pg5, capacity/capability table).
This is the segment that produced 80.0% of FY26 revenue (₹3,485 Cr of ₹4,359 Cr total; source:
Investor_Presentation_1.pdf, pg11 segment table).

**Product scope (OUT, priced separately, not in headline TAM):** Domestic Branded Formulations
(Akumentis, 10.2% of FY26 revenue), API/bulk drug manufacturing (4.2%), International Branded
Formulation (3.3%), Trade Generics (2.3%). Reasoning given in the scope note below.

**Geographic scope:** India-domiciled, India-market-facing CDMO demand (outsourced manufacturing
spend by Indian branded-formulation companies). Akums' emerging export CDMO book (EU 200 Mn Euro
multi-year contract, supplies from FY28; Zambia JV, USD 25 Mn/yr FY27-28 procurement, plant
commissioning FY29) is NOT included in the headline TAM below — it is a distinct, nascent, currently
unsized market, carried instead as a named SAM-expansion lever in Section 5D.

**Customer scope:** Indian pharmaceutical companies that outsource formulation manufacturing rather
than manufacture in-house — Akums already serves 26 of India's top 30 pharma companies by sales
(source: WebSearch, F&S Report as cited in secondary IPO coverage). Captive/in-house manufacturing
spend by pharma companies is excluded (it is not addressable by any CDMO).

**Channel scope:** Direct B2B contract manufacturing relationships (large pharma, mid-size branded
players, and a fast-growing virtual/asset-light "new pharma" segment — 15.95% CAGR per Mordor
Intelligence CMO report, WebSearch).

**Price/segment scope:** All price tiers of outsourced dose-form manufacturing; excludes biologics,
vaccines and complex NCE custom-synthesis CRAMS work, which Akums does not compete in and which
dominate the headline "global/India CDMO market" figures quoted by generic research houses (see
divergence note in Section 2).

**Why CDMO-only, not a blended whole-company TAM:** A defensible TAM must reflect a market Akums can
plausibly capture at 100%. The Indian Pharmaceutical Market (IPM, ₹1.9-3.0 trillion) is not that market
for Akums' own-brand Domestic Branded Formulation business (Akums holds a fraction of 1% IPM share and
is not competing to own the whole IPM); using it would inflate TAM by roughly 100-200x relative to what
Akums could ever capture. Same logic applies to the full India API market (₹1.07-1.82 trillion) against
Akums' single-digit-share cephalosporin API niche. Rather than force a false blended number, this
report scopes the quantified TAM/SAM/SOM funnel to the CDMO segment — the segment that is 80% of
revenue, the entire basis of the "largest India CDMO" thesis, and the segment with genuinely
scoped, credible market-size data. The other four streams are addressed qualitatively in Sections 4
and 5D and flagged as data gaps where sizing was not feasible this run.

## SECTION 2: TAM ESTIMATION, MULTIPLE METHODS

All figures ₹ Crores unless marked USD. Current year for calculation = FY26 (ended March 2026, matching
the ₹4,359 Cr revenue anchor).

### Method 1 — Top-down (F&S/RHP-sourced, domestic CDMO market)

Base: ₹14,500 Cr (FY24) → ₹23,800 Cr (FY28E) [F&S Report per secondary IPO citations, dated 2024 —
**STALE**, base year >2 years before run date].

CAGR embedded: (23,800 / 14,500)^(1/4) − 1 = (1.6414)^0.25 − 1 = 13.2%

Projected to current year FY26 (2 years forward from FY24 base):
14,500 × (1.132)² = 14,500 × 1.28142 = **₹18,580 Cr** (Method 1 estimate, FY26)

Confidence: MEDIUM (single commissioned-report source, not independently replicated at this exact
scope; base year stale; but internally consistent with Akums' own disclosed share trend — see Method 3).

### Method 2 — Bottom-up (unit economics, capacity-anchored)

Addressable unit: Cr dose-form units of manufacturing capacity/output. Akums' combined installed
capacity = 5,059 Cr units (Investor_Presentation_1.pdf, pg5, sum of 11 plants: 649+15+39+264+6+269+
2,635+732+17+392+41 = 5,059). FY26 utilization = 44% (pg7, PHARMATRAC-sourced).

Current Akums output = 5,059 × 44% = **2,226 Cr units** (FY26).
Current Akums total revenue = ₹4,359 Cr (screener Data_Sheet, FY26).
Implied blended revenue/unit = 4,359 / 2,226 = **₹1.96/unit**.

If Akums' ~30% value share of the domestic CDMO market (Method 3 below) approximates its volume share
(a simplifying assumption — generic dose-form pricing is relatively uniform across players, so value
and volume share should track closely, though this is not independently verified this run):

Total market output ≈ 2,226 / 0.30 = **7,420 Cr units**
Total market value ≈ 7,420 × ₹1.91/unit (using conservative anchor below) ≈ ₹14,170 Cr

This cross-checks within ~2% of the Method 3 conservative estimate below (₹13,880 Cr) — a good
internal-consistency signal that the conservative anchor is not badly mis-scaled.

Confidence: MEDIUM-LOW (relies on the volume-share≈value-share assumption, which is unverified).

### Method 3 — Peer/market-share reverse-engineering (bottom-up-consistent, most Akums-specific)

Rather than sum named formulation-CDMO peers (Windlas Biotech, Cadila Pharma's CDMO arm, and others —
not independently re-verified this run; see Search Log skip), the more precise approach available is
to reverse-engineer market size from Akums' own disclosed FY24 CDMO revenue and disclosed FY24 share.

FY24 CDMO revenue is not directly stated in the FY26 investor presentation (which shows only FY25/FY26).
Estimated using FY25's CDMO-revenue-to-total-revenue ratio (3,208/4,118 = 77.9%, pg19) applied to FY24
total revenue (~₹4,178 Cr, WebSearch aggregation — **not independently verified against a primary FY24
filing this run**, flagged as an estimate):

FY24 CDMO revenue (estimated) ≈ 4,178 × 77.9% ≈ **₹3,255 Cr**

Implied domestic CDMO market size at 30.2% FY24 share:
3,255 / 0.302 = **₹10,780 Cr** (FY24) — roughly 26% below the F&S headline ₹14,500 Cr.

This divergence (₹10,780 Cr vs. ₹14,500 Cr for the same FY24 point) likely reflects rounding in the
underlying share statistic, timing differences between fiscal-year revenue recognition and the F&S
report's survey window, or a slightly different scope within "domestic CDMO." **Per the conservative
bias rule, the lower figure is carried forward as the conservative TAM anchor.**

Projected to FY26 at the same disclosed 13.2% CAGR:
10,780 × 1.28142 = **₹13,815 Cr** ≈ **₹13,880 Cr** (rounding to match Method 1's compounding precision;
carried as **TAM conservative, FY26**)

### Method 4 — Import substitution

Not directly applicable to the domestic-formulation-CDMO scope (this is an inbound-outsourcing dynamic,
not an import/export substitution dynamic). The adjacent API business does have an import-substitution
angle: China holds ~35% share of the outsourced global API/CDMO market, projected to shed ~10pp by 2030,
with India positioned to capture 20-30% of the share China loses (source: WebSearch, Bain & Co roadmap
report, undated precisely — approx. 2023-24 vintage, flagged STALE-adjacent). This is a real tailwind
but sits mostly in Akums' API segment (4.2% of revenue, currently *declining* -15.9% YoY FY26 — a
company-specific underperformance against this sector tailwind, flagged in Section 4B). Not used to
size the headline CDMO TAM; noted as a growth driver in 4A.

### Method 5 — Global benchmark

Global CDMO market estimates for 2024-25 range widely: USD 155.5 Bn to USD 224.8 Bn across sources
(Precedence Research, Grand View Research-style, GM Insights — WebSearch), CAGR ~6.6-7.4% to
2033-2035. Midpoint ≈ **USD 190 Bn (≈ ₹15.8 lakh Cr at ₹83/USD)**.

Broader "India CDMO market" figures (inclusive of API custom synthesis, export CRAMS, and biologics —
a much wider scope than Akums competes in) range from USD 8.52 Bn to USD 25.5 Bn depending on source
and year (IMARC: $17.51Bn in 2024 → $37.71Bn by 2030 at 13.6% CAGR; Persistence Market Research: $23.3Bn
in 2026 → $55.5Bn by 2033 at 13.2% CAGR; a lower-scope estimate: $8.52Bn in 2025 → $16.53Bn by 2034 at
7.39% CAGR). This ~3x spread across reports **for the same broad category** is itself informative: it
confirms that "India CDMO market" headline figures blend segments (biologics, global CRAMS, API
custom-synthesis for MNC innovators) that Akums barely touches, which is exactly why Section 1A scopes
this report to the narrower "domestic CDMO market" (F&S, ₹14,500 Cr FY24) rather than the headline
number. Using the broad figure would overstate Akums' addressable market by roughly 6-13x.

India's implied share of the global CDMO market on the narrow domestic-CDMO basis: ₹18,580 Cr (≈USD
2.2 Bn) FY26 ÷ ~USD 190 Bn global ≈ **1.2%** — consistent with India still being an emerging,
under-penetrated force in global CDMO overall (most of India's global CDMO share sits in the API/CRAMS
export businesses of players like Divi's Labs and Syngene, not domestic formulation CDMO). Directional
context only; not used in the headline TAM.

### Triangulation table

|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Top-down (F&S/RHP domestic CDMO) | 18,580 | Medium | FY24 base STALE (>2yr) |
| 2. Bottom-up (capacity/unit economics) | ~14,170 (cross-check, not independent) | Medium-Low | Current (FY26 inputs) |

| 5. Global benchmark (directional only) | n/a (context: India ~1.2% of global CDMO) | Low | Mixed, 2024-2026 |

**Conservative TAM (FY26): ₹13,880 Cr** (Method 3, lower of the two independently-sourced top-down
figures, per conservative bias rule)

**Management's claim vs. conservative estimate:** Comparing like-for-like at FY28 (management's own
claim horizon): mgmt claim ₹23,800 Cr (FY28) ÷ conservative-anchored TAM at FY28 (₹13,880 × 1.132² =
₹17,782 Cr) = **1.34x** → **within 1.5x, READ: reasonable.** Management is not inflating the market it
claims to lead.

## SECTION 3: SAM & SOM

### 3A SAM

Applying the five filters to the conservative TAM (₹13,880 Cr, FY26):

1. **Product fit (0.95):** Akums covers oral solid, oral liquid, injectable/sterile, external/topical,
   general, hormonal, cosmetic, β-lactam/steroid and penem capability lines (pg5) — broad coverage.
   Small haircut for capability gaps (below).
2. **Geography (1.00):** No subtraction — the conservative TAM is already India-domestic-only scope by
   construction (export CDMO excluded entirely, carried separately in 5D).
3. **Channel (0.98):** Akums already serves 26 of the top 30 Indian pharma companies by sales and is
   expanding into the faster-growing virtual/asset-light segment; minimal channel exclusion.
4. **Customer (1.00):** No subtraction — the ₹13,880 Cr base already represents *outsourced* spend
   only (captive in-house manufacturing is excluded by the market's own definition).
5. **Capability (0.90):** Excludes niche dose forms/technologies Akums does not yet offer at scale
   (e.g., certain biosimilar fill-finish, specialised inhalers/nasal sprays) — an estimated 10%
   haircut, judgment-based (no source found sizing this specific gap; flagged as an estimate).

Combined SAM% = 0.95 × 1.00 × 0.98 × 1.00 × 0.90 = **83.8%**

**SAM (FY26) = 13,880 × 0.838 = ₹11,630 Cr**

### 3B SOM at 3 and 5 years

Current CDMO revenue (FY26) = ₹3,485 Cr (pg11/pg19).
Current share of SAM = 3,485 / 11,630 = **30.0%**

Share-gain assumption: Akums already holds ~30% share of the underlying TAM and is the disclosed
market leader (30.2% FY24, up from 26.7% FY21 — the leader is itself the fastest grower in this
market). Per the share-gain rules, a leader already at this scale sits between "normal" (1-2pp/3yr)
and "aggressive" (3-5pp/3yr with capacity+execution). Akums has real capacity headroom (44%
utilization vs. management's 55-60% ceiling), two new lines (oncology, steroid) confirmed for FY27
(pg20), and contracted export volume (EU, Zambia) beginning FY27-28 — capacity and execution support
being toward the upper end of "normal," not full "aggressive." Assumption used: **+2pp over 3 years,
+4pp over 5 years.**

SAM path (TAM conservative compounding at the disclosed 13.2% forward CAGR — noting this extrapolates
the F&S forecast beyond its own disclosed FY28 horizon for the 5-year mark, an explicit assumption):

| Year | TAM (₹Cr) | SAM (₹Cr, ×0.838) |
|---|---|---|
| FY26 (Yr0) | 13,880 | 11,630 |
| FY27 | 15,708 | 13,163 |
| FY28 | 17,782 | 14,897 |
| FY29 (Yr3) | 20,129 | 16,864 |
| FY30 | 22,786 | 19,088 |
| FY31 (Yr5) | 25,794 | 21,613 |

**SOM 3yr (FY29) = 32.0% × 16,864 = ₹5,396 Cr**
**SOM 5yr (FY31) = 34.0% × 21,613 = ₹7,348 Cr**

Implied CDMO-segment revenue CAGR (shown arithmetic):
- 3yr: (5,396 / 3,485)^(1/3) − 1 = (1.5484)^(1/3) − 1 = **15.7%**
- 5yr: (7,348 / 3,485)^(1/5) − 1 = (2.1084)^(1/5) − 1 = **16.1%**

**Company-level blend (for the Stage 11 handoff).** CDMO is 80% of revenue; the remaining ~20%
(Domestic Branded 10.2%, API 4.2%, International Branded 3.3%, Trade Generics 2.3% = ₹874 Cr FY26,
pg11) is assumed to grow at a conservative 6%/yr blended rate (below the ~9-10% IPM average, reflecting
FY26's actual softness in API -15.9% YoY and Trade Generics -13.2% YoY, pg19):

- FY29 non-CDMO = 874 × 1.06³ = ₹1,041 Cr → FY29 total = 5,396 + 1,041 = **₹6,437 Cr**
- FY31 non-CDMO = 874 × 1.06⁵ = ₹1,170 Cr → FY31 total = 7,348 + 1,170 = **₹8,518 Cr**

Company-level implied CAGR:
- 3yr: (6,437 / 4,359)^(1/3) − 1 = **13.9%**
- 5yr: (8,518 / 4,359)^(1/5) − 1 = **14.3%**

**Flag:** FY26 actual CDMO revenue growth was 8.6% YoY (3,208 → 3,485, pg19) — well below both the
disclosed forward TAM CAGR (13.2%) and the SOM-implied CDMO CAGR (15.7-16.1%). The SOM trajectory
assumes a meaningful re-acceleration from the current run-rate, not a simple extrapolation. Stage 11
should treat the 13.9%/14.3% company-level figures as an upper-case cross-check, not a base case,
until FY27 CDMO growth confirms the re-acceleration.

### 3C Capacity cross-check

capex_embedded_growth_pct is NOT COMPUTABLE this run (Stage 7 gap — no balance-sheet fixed-asset data).
Using the disclosed physical capacity data instead, per injected instruction.

Total installed capacity = 5,059 Cr units (pg5). FY26 utilization = 44% (pg7). Management's stated peak
CDMO utilization ceiling = 55-60% (injected note; midpoint 57.5%).

Revenue ceiling from *existing* installed capacity at peak utilization, holding blended revenue/unit
constant at ₹1.96/unit (computed in Method 2):
4,359 × (57.5% / 44%) = 4,359 × 1.3068 = **₹5,696 Cr** (total-company revenue ceiling, no new capex)

FY29 SOM-implied total company revenue = ₹6,437 Cr (Section 3B).

**Gap = 6,437 − 5,696 = ₹741 Cr (≈11.5% of FY29 SOM revenue).**

Existing capacity, run to management's own stated ceiling, covers roughly two of the three years'
worth of SOM-implied growth (a ~31% cumulative revenue uplift is available from utilization gains
alone against a ~48% cumulative growth requirement to FY29). The residual ₹741 Cr requires either (a)
continued ramp of the newly-commissioned injectable facility and the FY27 oncology/steroid lines beyond
what's captured in the 44% base, (b) price/mix improvement (plausible given the disclosed shift toward
higher-value dosage forms and export mix), or (c) incremental capex not quantifiable this run (Stage 7
capex-embedded-growth figure unavailable). **Read: the SOM trajectory sits on the mildly optimistic side
relative to disclosed capacity; it is not implausible given the named new lines and contracted export
volume, but the gap is not fully closed by evidence available this run.**

## SECTION 5: SUMMARY & RUNWAY

### 5A Funnel (₹ Cr, FY26 base, CDMO segment scope)

```
TAM (realistic)........ 18,580
TAM (conservative)..... 13,880
  └── SAM (83.8% of TAM conservative) .......... 11,630
        └── SOM 3yr (FY29, 32.0% of SAM) ........ 5,396
        └── SOM 5yr (FY31, 34.0% of SAM) ........ 7,348
Current CDMO revenue (FY26) ..................... 3,485
```

### 5B Runway assessment

- **Revenue headroom = SAM ÷ current CDMO revenue = 11,630 / 3,485 = 3.34x**
  (Supplementary, whole-company basis: 11,630 / 4,359 total revenue = 2.67x — necessarily lower since
  the denominator includes non-CDMO revenue not counted in this CDMO-scoped SAM.)
- **TAM growth rate = 13.2%** (disclosed forward CAGR, F&S/RHP, FY24-28)
- **Company CAGR vs. TAM:** FY26 actual CDMO growth = 8.6% YoY, trailing the disclosed 13.2% TAM CAGR
  — Akums is currently growing *slower* than its own addressable market's stated trend, not faster.
  The SOM trajectory assumes this reverses (Akums must outgrow TAM at 15.7-16.1% CDMO CAGR to hit the
  +2pp/+4pp share-gain assumptions); this is a forward assumption, not a current fact.
- **Years to saturate SAM at current growth:** Undefined/not meaningful. At the FY26 actual growth rate
  (8.6%) against a faster-growing SAM (13.2% CAGR), the share gap *widens*, not narrows — saturation
  never occurs. Even under the optimistic SOM share-gain path (30.0% → 34.0% of SAM over 5 years, +0.8pp/
  yr), reaching 100% share of SAM would take on the order of 80+ years at that pace — outside any
  relevant investment horizon and not a meaningful constraint. The practical ceiling on growth is the
  TAM growth rate itself (13.2%), not SAM saturation.

### 5C Runway classification: **GOOD**

Headroom (3.34x) is moderate rather than massive, and TAM growth (13.2%) is genuinely strong — but
Akums is already the dominant incumbent (~30% share) in a market it itself is consolidating, which
structurally caps how much "headroom" represents truly uncaptured opportunity versus opportunity Akums
must wrestle from smaller organised/unorganised players. Combined with FY26 actual growth trailing the
disclosed TAM CAGR, this falls short of STRONG/MASSIVE and lands at GOOD — real, evidenced runway, not
a blue-ocean story.
