## The picture in one paragraph

Wednesday was the best day of the repair since the 15 September washout. Big
risers came back to 157, and only 10 stocks fell hard, the fewest since July.
The long-term floor turned. For the first time since 11 September, more stocks sit
above their two-hundred-day line than below it, 1,182 against 1,081. The
short-term count jumped to about 1,060 stocks above the twenty-day line, the
most since late August. Turnover rose back above its year-ago level. Large
companies came back to the leader lists in force. Fear sits near the floor,
and gold turned slightly down over the month. No thrust
day came yet. The market has moved from a thin repair of the short end to a
repair that now reaches the long end, on its first day there.

The story of the last seven sessions runs like this. On 15 September, 343
stocks fell four and a half percent in one session. That was the washout. The
next day was calm. Then the buyers came for four days: 123 big risers on
Thursday, 168 on Friday, 143 on Monday, 104 on Tuesday. The last brief said the
bounce was losing speed. Wednesday broke that fade. It brought 157 big risers,
the second best day of the run. Only 10 stocks fell that hard. The last time the
count of big fallers was that low was 10 July.

Still, no thrust day. A thrust day is a session where 300 or more stocks jump
four and a half percent. The last one came on 12 June, a month after the May
washout. Every recent low in this record had one. This repair has now run six
sessions without it. The best day, Friday, reached a little over half the mark.
A repair can hold without a thrust. It carries less proof. It is also less
likely to run out of breath in one burst.

The long line turned. The count of stocks above the two-hundred-day line rose
from 1,121 to 1,182. The count below fell to 1,081. The last brief named about

## What would change this read

The constructive read fails if the two-hundred-day count falls back under about
1,120 within the week. That would make Wednesday's cross a one-day event. It
fails if the fifty-day count falls back under about 700, its washout-day
reading. It fails if a second washout arrives, 300 or more stocks falling four
and a half percent in one session. It also fails if the VIX stays near 10 while
big fallers climb back above about 50 a day. That would show calm turning into
complacency.

The cautious read fails if a thrust day arrives, 300 or more stocks jumping four
and a half percent in one session. It fails if the two-hundred-day count holds
above about 1,150 for a week. It fails if the large-cap list above the day's
average price stays above about 60 while bank four-week breadth stays near half.
It also fails if heavy-volume stocks start to outnumber light-volume ones on up
days, with turnover above its year-ago level.
