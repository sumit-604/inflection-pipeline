## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

### 2A. Demand environment
All three peers describe FY26 as a year of real allocation friction (tender
shortfalls, an OMC waterfall/methodology shift, and litigation) rather than either
a demand boom or a demand collapse, and two of the three (GULPOLY, BALRAMCHIN)
disclosed shortfall risk a quarter or more before TruAlt's own May-2026 admission
(see Q1). TRIVENI is the most explicit on the mechanism: Cycle 1 nationally
near-fully subscribed (1,048/1,050cr L) in Nov-2025, but by Jun-2026 "the ethanol
tender this year has been a huge disappointment... we haven't even had R2," and by
Jul-2026 the national tender programme was effectively frozen behind a Supreme
Court stay (see 2E). This matches TruAlt's own narrative of an allocation shock
disclosed only after the fact, and dates the underlying mechanism (litigation, not
demand weakness) consistently with TruAlt's own account.

### 2B. Pricing and input costs
Consistent across GULPOLY and TRIVENI: the FCI rice mandate (40% floor) materially
softened maize and broken-rice prices through most of FY26, and grain-based
ethanol margins for both peers held up well through the Rs 18-22/kg maize window,
only showing the FIRST signs of the pressure TruAlt describes once maize moved
into the Rs 23-25/kg range in Q1FY27 (GULPOLY's own guidance implies margin giveback
over FY27 from Q1's print — see Q3). TRIVENI's maize margin, on a milder Rs 20-23/kg
range through the year, stayed "robust double-digit" and was "combated" against a
mid-single-digit late-FY27 maize rise by higher DDGS realisation — a smaller,
better-hedged version of the same input-cost story, not a contradiction of it.
Separately, BALRAMCHIN's cane-route ethanol (B-heavy, juice) prices have not been
revised for three years (BALRAMCHIN-Concall_Feb_2026_Transcript.pdf, p.3) despite a
record UP cane-price (SAP) hike — a real margin-pressure story, but on a different
feedstock route than TruAlt's grain-based business; included here as the closest
sector analogue to "margin squeezed by an input-cost/output-price mismatch."

### 2C. Capex cycle
None of the three peers is expanding core ethanol distillation capacity in the
period reviewed. GULPOLY has explicitly paused all new capex until FY28,
prioritising cash generation and deleveraging (repeated in all four calls).
BALRAMCHIN's capex is concentrated in PLA/bioplastics (Rs 3,080cr, funded partly by
a Rs 450cr preferential equity raise) — a diversification away from, not into, core
cane/ethanol capacity. TRIVENI's incremental capex (Rs 340cr) is in its
power-transmission/defence gearbox business, now demerged into TPTL; its
distillery growth this year is entirely from de-bottlenecking and reactivating the
idle acquired Shamli unit, not new-build ethanol capacity (Tarun Sawhney,
Jun-2026, p.17-18: "this is without incurring any new CapExes for greenfield or
brownfield expansion"). TruAlt, by contrast, is the lone aggressive expander in
ethanol-adjacent capex in this peer set (CBG Sumitomo, CBG GAIL, SAF) at a point
where three peers are each independently de-risking away from core ethanol capacity
exposure — a lone-expander situation, not an industry-wide capacity race (Part 5).

### 2D. Competitive mentions
No peer transcript names TruAlt or gives an unmistakable description of it.

### 2E. Risks peers discuss that TruAlt's own calls under-disclose
- **The Supreme Court stay's procedural detail.** TruAlt's own May-2026 call (p.11)
  DOES describe the stay's practical effect: "the Supreme Court has directed the
  OMCs that unless that matter is fully heard and disposed of, not to do any new
  allocation" — the first-run claim that TruAlt "never described" the stay was
  overstated and is corrected here. What TruAlt's four calls never do is name the
  petitioner or the underlying case: TRIVENI (TRIVENI-Concall_Aug_2026_Transcript.pdf,
  p.6, Tarun Sawhney) names it precisely: "the Supreme Court has stayed any
  alteration to the existing ethanol allocation framework and ordered a status quo
  as it examines BPCL's challenge to the Karnataka High Court ruling... The AG
  yesterday has said that 100 crore litres should be allocated and is seeking the
  Supreme Court's permission... Triveni will be a significant beneficiary if that
  does get allocated." This is very likely the same underlying litigation TruAlt's
  own 15cr L court-ordered allocation sits inside (TruAlt's Q1FY27 call blames it
  on "a parallel LTOA-vs-OMC Supreme Court case" without naming BPCL, the Karnataka
  HC ruling, or the AG's 100cr L proposal). A disclosure-DEPTH gap, not a
  disclosure-EXISTENCE gap: the underlying case is real, dated, and TruAlt does
  acknowledge its effect; a peer facing the identical litigation names it in
  materially more procedural detail.
- **Standalone-distillery solvency risk as PSU-bank interest moratoriums expire.**
  TRIVENI (TRIVENI-Concall_Aug_2026_Transcript.pdf, p.11): "the interest moratorium
  for a lot of the standalone distilleries... will expire next year... the survival
  of that and the operations of that comes under huge question." TruAlt's own calls
  never frame TruAlt's own balance sheet in these terms, despite carrying the
  CFO/DSCR profile as its most severe audited-financial-statement flag elsewhere in
  this corpus.
- **Ethanol price non-revision (cane route) for three years despite record
  cane-price hikes.** A real, dated BALRAMCHIN-specific pain point on a feedstock
  route TruAlt does not use, but it evidences that pricing power in this sector
  sits with the OMCs across feedstock routes, not just the grain route — a
  structural read that supports, rather than undercuts, TruAlt's own "OMCs have
  become like an oligarchy" framing from its Q4FY26 call.

## PART 5: CROSS-PEER HYPOTHESIS

All three established peers are independently rotating capital AWAY from core
grain/cane-ethanol capacity at the same point TruAlt is committing several hundred
crore to ethanol-adjacent capex (CBG, SAF): GULPOLY has frozen all new capex until
FY28 to build cash and deleverage; BALRAMCHIN is diverting its distillery-generation
cash flow into PLA/bioplastics, a business explicitly designed to reduce reliance
on the ethanol/sugar cycle; TRIVENI is demerging its highest-margin, highest-growth
business (power transmission/defence) away from the sugar-and-distillery parent
entirely, and its own management states outright that standalone grain-distillery
solvency "comes under huge question" once interest moratoriums lapse next year.
Read together, this is not simply "the sector has overcapacity" (each peer says
that individually); it is a specific, testable pattern that sector insiders are
reading the current overcapacity and price-freeze window as STRUCTURAL rather than
cyclical, and are visibly de-risking their own capital structures away from pure
ethanol exposure in response — a read TruAlt's own capex programme (raising
leverage to build MORE ethanol-adjacent capacity into this exact window) implicitly
disputes. Testable: if peer ethanol/distillery capex resumes and expands once their
diversification capex matures (FY28-29), the hypothesis fails; if peer ethanol
capex stays flat or falls further while TruAlt's own utilisation struggles to clear
50-60% through FY27-28, the hypothesis holds, and TruAlt's CBG/SAF diversification
should be read as the same defensive instinct arriving later and more leveraged
than its peers'.
