### PATTERN 1 — Ageing-schedule reconciliation failures: a confirmed PATTERN, not two isolated slips
Two independent ageing sub-schedules, in two different notes, in two different financial-statement sets, both
fail to reconcile to their own parent totals in the same reporting year:
- Standalone Note 15 (Trade Receivables): FY24 ageing sub-table totals ₹1,988.52L against a reported/BS
  balance of ₹1,359.21L — a ₹629.31L gap (Note 15 Standalone, p.~94; first identified Pass 1 Rank 5).
- Consolidated Note 22 (Trade Payables): FY24 ageing sub-table totals ₹101.24L against a reported/BS balance
  of ₹76.75L — a ₹24.49L gap that reconciles exactly to an un-eliminated intercompany payable to Gaudium
  International Private Limited (Note 22 Consolidated, p.~147; Note 42(c) Standalone; identified Pass 2
  Finding 2).
Both instances occurred in the same audit cycle (FY25, first year of SKGN & Associates LLP as statutory
auditor following a predecessor-auditor resignation, CARO clause (xviii), p.~65) and in the year of highest
transaction complexity in the Company's history (IPO preparation, bonus issue, new subsidiary, first-time Ind
AS adoption comparatives). 🔴 Treated as a disclosure-quality PATTERN per orchestrator instruction: this is a
control-process signal (note-drafting/cross-checking discipline) rather than a valuation or going-concern
issue on its own, but it is directly relevant to how much residual reliance can be placed on other,
unverified sub-schedules in the same notes (e.g., borrowings maturity ladders, lease maturity tables, MSME
ageing) that were not independently recomputed in this review.

### PATTERN 2 — Note-level narrative/numeric contradictions recur across at least four unrelated note-pairs
- Note 4.2 (Intangibles policy) contains internally contradictory language — it both prescribes 10-year
  straight-line amortisation for Trademarks AND separately invokes an "indefinite life intangible assets"
  rationale for the same asset class ("strength and durability of the Company") — while Note 8 confirms
  amortisation is actually being charged (Note 4.2 p.~78; Note 8 p.~90–91; Pass 1).
- Note 44 (Capital Management, Standalone) shows Net-Debt/Equity DETERIORATING (0.22x FY24 → 0.31x FY25,
  net debt rising ₹575.62L → ₹1,388.07L) while Note 48 (Analytical Ratios) frames the (gross) Debt-Equity
  Ratio as IMPROVING (0.65x → 0.45x) — both arithmetically correct on their own defined bases, but presenting
  opposite qualitative leverage narratives from two notes six pages apart, with the more favourable framing
  appearing in the ratio-commentary note that most readers would treat as the headline take (Note 44 p.~104;
  Note 48 p.~108–109; Pass 1).
- Goodwill is stated as ₹13.82L in the asset-movement table (Note 7 Standalone / Note 7 Consolidated) but as
  "₹13.08 Lakhs" in the narrative Business Combination note describing the identical underlying transaction
  (Note 47 Standalone / Note 48 Consolidated) — the ₹0.74L discrepancy recurs IDENTICALLY in both the
  standalone and consolidated note sets, indicating a systemic drafting inconsistency rather than a one-off
  transcription error (Note 7/47 p.~90/107; Note 7/48 Consolidated p.~145/160; Pass 2 Finding 3).
- Note 45/46 (Financial Instruments/Fair Value) carries the ₹500L "Optionally Convertible" Debenture (Winners
  Industries) entirely at amortised cost (Level 2), with no bifurcated equity-conversion-option component or
  explanation for why an instrument explicitly named "optionally convertible" was assessed as not requiring
  derivative/equity-option accounting (Note 45–46 p.~104–107; Pass 1).
None of these four items is individually material to net worth or PAT. Taken together as a pattern, they
indicate a note-preparation process with inconsistent cross-referencing/review between the note that states a
figure and the note that narrates or contextualises the same fact — relevant background for weighing the
company's other, more consequential disclosures (e.g., the trademark objection detail and the Winners
Industries/Baiju Trading counterparty silence discussed in Pattern 3) where the benefit of the doubt on
completeness is correspondingly lower.

### PATTERN 3 — Disclosure granularity is uneven: detailed where the company chooses, minimal elsewhere on
comparably consequential facts
The notes demonstrate the company CAN produce highly granular, case-by-case disclosure when it chooses to —
Note 38/39 (Contingent Liabilities) itemises five separate tax/litigation matters by assessment year, section,
demand amount, filing date and appeal status. Against that standard, three comparably consequential items are
disclosed with materially less granularity:
(i) Note 10/11 — the counterparties to the ₹500L OCD (Winners Industries Private Limited) and the ₹400L
    unsecured loan (Shri Baiju Trading and Investment Private Limited) receive only name, instrument terms,
    and a generic purpose ("for its business activities"/no stated purpose) — no relationship statement, no
    conversion terms/trigger for the OCD, no security or covenant for the loan, despite both entities being
    non-core, non-related-party (as disclosed) counterparties receiving a combined 19.8% of standalone net
    worth in the same year.
(ii) Note 8 — the trademark registration/objection footnote states that "one trademark has been objected"
    (stated twice, possibly a drafting duplication in itself) with zero detail on the grounds of objection,
    opposing party, or expected resolution timeline, for an asset class that is 21.7% of standalone net worth.
(iii) Note 26/42 (Consolidated) — the single-segment assertion is a one-line boilerplate citation of Ind AS
    108 with no supporting analysis, despite the Group now operating a trading subsidiary whose revenue grew
    500.6% YoY and whose working-capital profile (inventory turnover -43%, payables turnover -81%) is
    starkly different from the core hospital business.
🟡 This unevenness is itself a finding: the absence of detail on (i)–(iii) is a disclosure choice, not a
capability constraint, given the company's demonstrated ability to disclose granularly elsewhere in the same
document.

### PATTERN 4 — No dedicated subsequent-events note despite a 5-month gap to Board approval during an active
IPO process
Standalone/Consolidated financial statements were approved by the Board on 10 September 2025, roughly 5.3
months after the 31 March 2025 balance sheet date (Note 1, p.~73). Note 13 confirms the Company was, as at
the report date, "under the process of listing" (IPO). No dedicated Events After the Reporting Period note
with specifics (IPO launch/pricing date, post-year-end centre openings, developments in the Note 38 tax
appeals, or any other event between 31 March and 10 September 2025) was found anywhere in Notes 1–55
(standalone or consolidated) — confirmed on re-read (originally flagged as an absence in Pass 1's "Notes Not
Found" summary; elevated here to a pattern item because the length of the gap and the active IPO process make
the silence more notable than a routine annual report). 🟡 Watch.

### PATTERN 5 — Going concern: explicitly checked against source, confirmed absent
Direct verification against the source PDF (Independent Auditor's Report, Standalone, p.~55–58; Note 2 Basis
of Accounting, p.~73–74): the Auditor's Report contains only the standard SA 570 boilerplate describing
management's and the auditor's respective responsibilities regarding the going-concern basis of accounting —
there is no separate "Material Uncertainty Related to Going Concern" paragraph, and the single Emphasis of
Matter paragraph in the report relates exclusively to Note 38's ₹3,091.03 Lakh tax contingent liabilities, not
going concern. Note 2 (Basis of Accounting) does not contain an explicit going-concern statement either
(it addresses Ind AS compliance, standards adoption, and historical cost convention only). No going-concern
qualification, material uncertainty language, or explicit management going-concern assessment exists anywhere
in the document as reviewed. 🟢 Confirmed clean on this specific dimension.

### PATTERN 6 — The GAAT ₹250L item is a cross-year reclassification without disclosure continuity (see also
Pass 2 Finding 1)
Re-examined as a pattern rather than a single-note item: the FY24 Note 13 "Capital Advances to Related
Parties" balance of ₹250.00L to Dr. Manika Khanna is not described anywhere in the FY24 comparative notes as
being for GAAT, or for any specific purpose — it appears in FY25 for the first time, retrospectively, as
"Professional Fees for Development of GAAT," fully settled and capitalised. This is not a formal restatement
(no prior-period comparative figure was amended), but it is a change in the DESCRIBED NATURE of an existing
related-party balance across two annual reports with no bridging disclosure — functionally similar in effect
to a reclassification, and is logged under restatements_found below for that reason.

## D. FIVE QUESTIONS FOR MANAGEMENT

1. What is the nature of any relationship between the Company/promoter group and (a) Winners Industries
   Private Limited (₹500L zero-coupon OCD counterparty) and (b) Shri Baiju Trading and Investment Private
   Limited (₹400L unsecured loan counterparty), and what is the commercial rationale, security, and
   conversion terms for these two non-core FY25 deployments totalling ₹900 Lakhs?
2. Can management provide the independent technical-feasibility assessment or third-party valuation
   supporting the decision to capitalise ₹738.32 Lakhs of GAAT development costs (including ₹250 Lakhs paid
   to the Chairperson/MD as professional fees) as an intangible asset under development rather than expense
   it, and clarify the FY24-to-FY25 accounting history of the ₹250 Lakh capital advance to Dr. Manika Khanna?
3. What caused the standalone Note 15 FY24 trade receivables ageing schedule to not reconcile to the reported
   balance sheet figure (₹629.31L gap) and the consolidated Note 22 FY24 trade payables ageing schedule to
   not reconcile to its own summary total (₹24.49L gap), and what review process will be implemented ahead
   of FY26 given the change of statutory auditor?
4. Given the standalone trade receivables turnover ratio nearly halved in FY25 (6.34x to 3.17x) with 56.2% of
   the book aged over six months for an individual-patient-pay business, what specific collection actions are
   underway, and why was the first-ever ECL provision (₹9.83 Lakhs) not larger relative to the ageing
   deterioration?
5. Why does the Group maintain a single-segment/single-geography reporting position under Ind AS 108 when the
   consolidated trading segment (Sale of Products) grew 500.6% YoY to 16.6% of consolidated revenue with a
   materially different working-capital and margin profile from the core Healthcare Services business, and
   will segment reporting be introduced post-listing?

## E. NOTES-BASED RED FLAGS

- Related-party payment (₹250L, to Chairperson/MD) capitalised as an intangible asset rather than expensed,
  with no disclosed technical-feasibility gate — inflates FY25 reported profit versus the expensing
  counterfactual (Note 9, cross-ref Note 13/42).
- ₹900 Lakhs deployed to two undisclosed-relationship counterparties in non-core, low-transparency
  instruments in the same year the company stopped its dividend and executed a 30:1 bonus issue that stripped
  ₹2,970.70L from retained earnings ahead of listing (Notes 10–11, 17).
- Two independent ageing-schedule reconciliation failures (standalone receivables, consolidated payables),
  both uncaught by a first-year statutory auditor in the company's highest-transaction-complexity year
  (Pattern 1).
- Internally contradictory leverage narrative between two notes (Note 44 net-debt deterioration vs. Note 48
  gross debt-equity "improvement") and an inconsistent goodwill figure recurring identically across both
  standalone and consolidated note sets (Pattern 2).
- Revenue recognised materially ahead of cash collection, with near-zero customer-advance/contract-liability
  balance, structurally inconsistent with a prepay-heavy "bundled package" fertility-clinic model (Note 15
  discussion, Note 23).
- Lower audit and tax-audit fees in the same year as auditor turnover and the highest transaction volume in
  company history (Note 33; CARO clause xviii).

## F. ONE-LINE NOTES VERDICT

"The notes reveal concerning accounting practices. Key concern: related-party capital deployment (a ₹250 Lakh
promoter payment capitalised as a development asset, plus ₹900 Lakhs into two undisclosed-relationship,
non-core instruments) layered on top of sharply deteriorating receivables quality and two independently
uncaught ageing-reconciliation failures. Key strength: granular, well-anchored contingent-liability and
related-party transaction-table disclosure, disciplined cost-line reporting, and a confirmed-absent
going-concern issue. Overall accounting quality: 4/10."
