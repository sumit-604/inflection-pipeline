## BUSINESS A — THE ADHESIVE / PERFORMANCE POLYMERS BUSINESS (retained, to become Jubilant Industries Ltd)

### Downstream / demand analysis (what the growth rests on)

The PP&C segment is two different businesses. Anchored FY26 from the Q1/FY27
investor deck segment page and AR Note 39:

| Sub-business | FY26 revenue | Share | Recent growth | Character |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Adhesives (Jivanjor white glue, Charmwood/Ultra-Italia finishes, construction chemicals, packaging) | Rs 501 Cr | 28% | FY24 351 -> FY25 432 -> FY26 502; Q1 FY27 +19% rev, +40% EBIT | Growth engine, ~9% EBIT margin |
| Industrial backbone (VP latex, SPVA food polymer, sulphuric acid) | ~Rs 698 Cr | 72% | ~10% | Stable annuity, high margin, ~65% ROCE |

Customers and end markets:
- Adhesives -> carpenters and contractors (contractor-first model), furniture and
  fixtures makers, home improvement / renovation, packaging manufacturers. Routed
  through ~1,450 distributors and ~31,000 retailers. Competes with Pidilite
  (Fevicol), Astral, Jyoti Resins.
- VP latex -> tyre manufacturers (tyre-cord dipping) and conveyor belts. #1 India,
  #2 global ex-China. Competes with Apcotex, BSF India.
- SPVA food polymer -> chewing gum manufacturers; sole food-grade PVAc maker in India.
- SBR latex (new, capacity end-Q3 FY27) -> construction chemicals / waterproofing.

Why demand grows: housing demand, the repair-and-renovation cycle, rising
contractor sophistication, premiumization (unorganized -> branded), Tier-2/3
penetration, and new West-India (Samlaya) capacity. Runway is the point: JACPL
runs 80,000 MTPA against Pidilite's 600,000-plus, so adhesives is a share-gain
catch-up story, not a mature market. Indicators: 5-year adhesive revenue CAGR
~35% FY20-25 (fastest in India, vs Pidilite 13.5%, Astral 22.5%), Q1 FY27
adhesives +19% rev / +40% EBIT, distribution scaling, Samlaya commissioned on
time. (The 35% CAGR and the peer comparisons are from the MNCL initiating-coverage
note, a non-anchored broker lead used for market context only; the sub-segment
revenue/EBIT and Q1 growth are anchored to the deck, AR and results.)

Counterweight: 72% of the business grows ~10%, and catching a dominant incumbent
(Fevicol is a generational brand moat) is a low-base-rate bet, so the 19%
adhesives line only pulls the blend so far.

### Fair value, three-year hold (bear / base / bull)

Base year is the FY27 forward EPS. The three-year target applies 35x to the FY30
EPS, grown from the FY27 base. Entry is the target divided by 1.953 (Tier A, 25%),
MoS is 20% below entry.

| Case | FY27 EPS | FY27-30 EPS CAGR | FY30 EPS | Target at 35x | Entry (÷1.953) | MoS |

Illustrative equity value of the polymer business, base case, is about 35 x FY27

### Hurdle Ratio at CMP

The polymer business is not separately quoted, so its current price is imputed
from the sum of the parts. Take the whole company at Rs 3,549 Cr, subtract the
agri business at its base value of roughly Rs 180 Cr (section B below), and the
market is imputing about Rs 3,370 Cr to the polymer business, which on FY27
projected PAT of Rs 125 Cr is a current forward PE of about 27x.

HR = (1 + 0.13)^3 x (35 / 27) = 1.443 x 1.296 = **1.87.** That is below the 1.953
Tier A pass line. On the grounded base, the polymer business valued inside the
current price is a CONDITIONAL pass: it clears 25% only on bull earnings, and
clears comfortably on a 12 to 15 percent dip. On its own bear EPS the pass fails.

### Read on the adhesive business

At Rs 2,342 you are imputing about 27x forward for a business earning 67.5% on
capital, nearly debt free, with a branded-adhesives franchise (28% of the mix)
compounding high teens on a stable high-ROCE latex-and-food-polymer backbone
(72%), about to report as a clean standalone specialty chemicals company. That is
not expensive for the quality, but it is not the 25% entry either. The grounded
base-case entry zone for the polymer claim is roughly Rs 2,043, with a margin of
safety near Rs 1,634. CMP sits about 15 percent above that entry. The call is a
DEEP WATCH at zone that becomes a clear BUY-on-dips on a 12 to 15 percent pullback
or on the first standalone accounts confirming the 67.5% return and the Q1
acceleration. The whole thesis rests on the forward polymer earnings holding, and
that number is un-guided, built from one quarter.
