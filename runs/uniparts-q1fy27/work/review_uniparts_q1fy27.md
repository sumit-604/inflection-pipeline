# PLAIN-LANGUAGE BRIEF (mandatory standing deliverable)

## 1. SUMMARY NARRATIVE

Uniparts India, a maker of precision linkage parts and machined components for off-highway (mostly agricultural and construction) vehicles, had a strong first quarter of FY27 [this quarter's filing]. Consolidated revenue rose 26.9% year on year to Rs 347.4 crore, operating profit margin widened by about 4.5 percentage points to 23.6%, and — the number that matters most — core operating profit before other income grew 70.5%, which is faster than the reported profit growth of 64%. That tells us the growth is real operating improvement, not one-off treasury or dividend income. Reported net profit was Rs 56.6 crore versus Rs 34.5 crore a year ago. Compared with the prior quarter the step-up was smaller (revenue up 2.5%, core profit up 1.5%); the bigger sequential jump in headline profit was mostly other income, so the quarter-on-quarter figure flatters slightly while the year-on-year figure is genuinely strong. The company is sitting on roughly Rs 190 crore of net cash, has almost no debt, improved its working-capital days, and declared a Rs 9 per share interim dividend [this quarter's filing]. Auditors gave an unmodified (clean) limited-review conclusion.

The important qualifications are all about structure and disclosure, not about the operating numbers. First, about 46% of group profit and 65% of group revenue come from three foreign subsidiaries that were reviewed by other (component) auditors, not the main auditor — a verification gap worth watching [this quarter's filing]. Second, the standalone (parent-only) accounts are distorted by large intra-group dividends, so standalone profit is not a clean read of the parent's own operations. Third, there is a cluster of small disclosure-hygiene lapses (blank mandated ratio cells, an "Audited" label on unaudited results, and a promised standalone statement that never appeared in the investor deck). None of these is individually serious, but together they are a governance data point. There is no existing investment thesis for this company — this is a first look — so there is no buy/sell decision here. The verdict is PROCEED WITH FLAGS: encouraging operating quality, fortress balance sheet, but a real audit-scope flag and an indeterminate quarterly cash-conversion reading (Q1 filings do not carry a cash-flow statement; the next reading is the September-half). The right next step is a full workup and a set of pointed questions to management, not a position.

## 2. SECTOR INTELLIGENCE

The company sells into the global off-highway vehicle market, split across small agriculture, large agriculture, construction equipment, and aftermarket [this quarter's filing, slides 6-7]. The demand picture is genuinely divergent right now. Construction equipment is in an up-cycle, helped by US infrastructure/technology-linked spending and European government spending [this quarter's filing]. Small agriculture is strong in India (government subsidy programmes, higher-horsepower adoption) but only "measured" in Western markets, where big-ticket buying has been deferred; management believes the bottom is behind after three years of volume declines [this quarter's filing]. Large agriculture is still in a cyclical trough, and even the leading OEMs expect a meaningful recovery only from CY2027 [this quarter's filing; the CY2027 view is attributed to third-party OEMs, general industry knowledge, not company-guaranteed]. Aftermarket (~12% of revenue, down from ~19% two years ago) was hit by tariff-driven price volatility [this quarter's filing]. The broad structural tailwind for this kind of supplier is farm-equipment mechanisation and the outsourcing of complex, low-volume, high-SKU components by global OEMs (general industry knowledge, not from this filing). The main headwind flagged is input-cost and supply-chain pressure from the "West Asia situation" [this quarter's filing]. There is no prior peer/sector Notion work for this name, so all sector context here is either from this quarter's filing or clearly labelled general knowledge.

## 3. BUSINESS-MODEL INTELLIGENCE

Uniparts makes money by manufacturing engineered systems — three-point-linkage (3PL, ~47% of revenue) and precision machined parts (PMP, ~51%), plus smaller power-take-off, hydraulic-cylinder and fabrication lines — and selling them to global OHV OEMs, mostly under long-standing relationships (four of the top five customers for 10+ years) [this quarter's filing, slides 14-16]. The model is a "dual-shore" one: manufacture in low-cost India, warehouse close to customers in the US, Europe and now Mexico for just-in-time delivery [this quarter's filing]. The economics: gross margin is high (~66% of revenue) because these are complex, made-to-spec parts, but the business carries heavy working capital (~136 days) because of inventory-intensive, low-volume high-SKU production — so cash conversion, not margin, is the variable to watch [this quarter's filing]. Consolidated operating margin (23.6%) runs well above the standalone parent margin (15.7%), which tells us the foreign subsidiaries do the higher-value work; group profit quality therefore rides on those overseas entities [this quarter's filing]. This quarter shows no model drift — margin expanded on volume and mix, capex was light (CWIP winding down, depreciation flat), and the company stayed net cash. Two things to watch for future drift: the stated 20% long-term margin guidance for the newer synergistic product lines sits below the current 25% blend (so scaling them could dilute margin), and management is openly hunting for acquisitions with its cash pile [this quarter's filing]. Management also cites TTM ROCE "north of 27%" and ROE 20%, though reported annual ROCE has been volatile (13.7% to 35.9% across FY23-FY26), so a single-year figure should not anchor a valuation [this quarter's filing].

## 4. COMPETITION INTELLIGENCE

Uniparts positions itself as the global market leader in linkage systems for small (<70 HP) tractors and a leading supplier of precision machined parts in a fragmented market, with an Indian-plus-US manufacturing base serving all major geographies [this quarter's filing, slides 14-16]. Where it wins: deep, decade-plus OEM relationships, design-stage involvement, a dual-shore delivery model that de-risks customer supply chains, and leadership in a complex, low-volume, high-SKU niche that is hard for commodity suppliers to enter (this is the company's own framing; there is no independent peer benchmarking available for this new name — treat as company claim plus general knowledge, not verified). Where it is structurally weaker or exposed: customer concentration is high (top five customers ~78% of sales per the deck's own chart), it is a single-segment, single-end-market (OHV) supplier with no diversification cushion, and roughly two-thirds of revenue is generated through foreign subsidiaries whose results this quarter were not reviewed by the principal auditor — a verification and transparency vulnerability rather than a competitive one, but relevant to how much credit to give the numbers [this quarter's filing]. The competitive risk to watch is cyclical: in the large-agriculture down-cycle the company says its growth is "entirely" new-business wins, not market-driven — so if those wins slow, there is little market tailwind to fall back on until CY2027 [this quarter's filing].
