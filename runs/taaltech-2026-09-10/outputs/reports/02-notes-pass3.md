FOUR LOAD-BEARING FACTS — SETTLED AND OPEN
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

**(a) Is the ~30% operating margin genuinely operating?** Partly. Revenue
less Total Expenses only (Other Income excluded entirely) gives an EBIT
margin of 27.8% (₹54.95cr on ₹197.43cr, Note 24/Note 26 Consolidated,
p.132/142-143) — that figure is genuinely operating and is the number to
carry into any Role 1 model. Any wider "operating margin" reading that
nets Other Income into the numerator (pushing the ratio toward the
37.7% PBT margin, ₹74.36cr/₹197.43cr) is contaminated: ₹12.04cr of that
Other Income is an unrealised mark-to-market gain on the treasury book
(Note 25, p.143), 16.2 percentage points of PBT that will not recur at
this magnitude in a flat or down market, and is not cash today. The
notes settle the decomposition completely; they leave open only whether
the 27.8% EBIT margin itself is durable, which is a forward-looking
question the notes cannot answer.

**(b) Is the Q4 FY26/Q1 FY27 revenue acceleration cash-backed?** Not
fully, and the notes show why. Consolidated revenue did genuinely
accelerate — ₹45.79cr (Q3 FY26) to ₹57.04cr (Q4 FY26) to ₹64.81cr
(Q1 FY27), a real top-line trend (Results filings). But unbilled revenue
as a share of exit-quarter revenue more than doubled over the same
window, from 10.4% at Mar-25 to 24.1% at Mar-26 (Note 14, p.137, cross-
checked against the results filings) — roughly ₹7.8cr of the unbilled
build is not explained by the higher run-rate alone and reflects a
genuine lengthening of the billing cycle. Combined customer-related
working capital (receivables plus unbilled) rose 55.7% against 6.6%
revenue growth, and CFO/PAT fell from 84.1% to 33.0%. No contract-asset
rollforward exists in either note set to show whether this reverses in
FY27; this stays open and is a monitoring item, not a settled fact,
until at least the Q2/Q3 FY27 cash flow shows either recovery or further
build.

**(c) What does the ₹143.89cr investment book actually hold?** Fully
settled at name level, only partially settled on risk. Note 7
(Consolidated, p.132-134) names every holding: ₹122.96cr (85.5%) is
unquoted Level-3 paper, dominated by subordinated/high-yield NCDs of
small, largely unrated NBFC and fintech lenders (mPokket across three
tranches, KrazyBee, AYE Finance, Navi Finserv, Earlysalary, Whizdm,
Keertana Finserv, Spandana Spoorthy) and alternative-credit AIFs
(Ascertis x2, Avendus, Neo Treasury Plus), plus the single largest
holding, Aamsar LLP (₹29.52cr, roughly halved YoY on a large partial
redemption). The remaining ₹20.93cr (14.5%) is quoted mutual funds, gold
funds, and listed shares. No promoter-group holding (Taneja Aerospace or
otherwise) appears anywhere in this book. What is left open: no credit
rating is disclosed for any unquoted instrument, no concentration-limit
or treasury policy is stated, and the fair-value hierarchy note's
counterparty-credit-risk commentary addresses only security deposits,
never this ₹143.89cr book or the new ₹10cr Vishkul loan (Note 39/38,
p.99-100/152). The "what" is fully answered; the "how safe" is not.

**(d) What is the full related-party picture, including the ₹10cr
Vishkul ICD and the ₹51.71 lakh unreconcilable receivable?** Mixed: the
TAAL-the-listed-entity slice is settled and clean — one ₹1.50 lakh car
sale to Taneja Aerospace and Aviation Ltd in FY26, nothing in FY25, no
rent, royalty, management fee, guarantee, or cross-charge in either year
(Note 36, Consolidated, p.150-151). The Vishkul slice is not settled:
the ₹10cr unsecured loan to the 50.74% holding company is disclosed at
Ind AS 24 level with the amount and derived ~9.85% interest yield, but
no contractual rate, tenure, purpose, or board-approval date appears
anywhere in the 164-page document, and CARO's own annexure contradicts
itself on whether the loan was even given (clause 3(iii)(a) says yes,
clause 3(iii)(d) says no loans were given, p.60). AOC-2 (p.56) shows the
transaction as "NA," meaning the company's own governance framework
treats it as below the threshold for Section 188 material-RPT
particulars. The ₹51.71 lakh related-party receivable is also unsettled:
it cannot be reconciled under the standalone definition of related party
(which includes the wholly-owned subsidiaries) or the consolidated
definition (which does not), and appears identically in both books,
consistent with either an un-eliminated intercompany balance or an
undisclosed transaction. A fourth thread compounds the uncertainty: FY25
KMP remuneration comparatives differ between the standalone and
consolidated RPT notes on four separate lines with no consistent
direction (Note 37 Standalone p.98 vs Note 36 Consolidated p.151). Of
the four load-bearing facts, this is the least settled.
