## The picture in one paragraph

The market took a second hit on Wednesday and bounced back on Thursday, and the bounce came from the big companies. On Wednesday about seven stocks in ten fell. The long trend count slipped to 1,015 stocks, below the level the last brief named as the line where the November progress would be gone. On Thursday 33 of 35 indices rose and the Nifty gained about one percent to close near 25,510. Car makers led with a jump of more than four percent, and banks and staples followed. 76 stocks rose hard against 36 that fell hard. Yet the money behind the day was light, and fear rose with the market, with the VIX up to about 16. The short, middle and long trend counts all sit below half the market. New yearly highs and new yearly lows are level at 39 each. So the large companies are holding the index up, while the average stock is still waiting for a reason to climb. The bounce off the October low survives. It has not yet become a trend.

## Where the market stands

Think of each stock as having three clocks. The 20 day line is the short clock, the 50 day line is the middle clock, and the 200 day line is the long clock. A stock above a line is in an uptrend on that clock. On Thursday 1,122 stocks stood above the short line. 1,036 stood above the middle line, and 1,043 stood above the long line. All three counts sit short of half the market. A week ago the buyers held the short clock with room to spare. They have lost it.

The year gives these counts their meaning. 23 September was the last day when more stocks stood above the long line than below it. On 21 October, 411 stocks fell hard in one day. Two days later only 606 stocks held the long line. That was the low. The climb that followed lasted about three weeks. By 12 November the long count reached 1,149 and the short count reached 1,712. That was the high water mark of the bounce. Since then the market has given back part of the climb in two steps.

The first step came on Monday 16 November, when 216 stocks fell hard. Tuesday was quiet. The second step came on Wednesday 18 November. It was not a crash. 71 stocks fell hard, against 43 that rose hard. But the fall was wide. About seven stocks in ten closed lower, and only about a quarter closed higher. The short count fell below half the market, to about 1,080. The long count fell to 1,015. The last brief named about 1,050 on the long count as the line where the gains of mid November would be gone. Wednesday crossed it.

Thursday was the reply. About six stocks in ten closed higher. 76 stocks rose hard, the most in a week. Hard fallers dropped to 36. About one stock in seven traded four percent above its prior close at some point. The long count climbed back to 1,043, just under the 1,050 line. The short count rose to 1,122.

The quality of Thursday's lift matters as much as its size. Turnover came to about 464 crore, against about 518 crore on the same session a year ago. About one stock traded on heavy volume for every three on light volume. Only about a third of the stocks that traded four percent up kept that gain into the close. That is a calm, broad lift on light money. It is the shape of a relief day, not a thrust day.

Fear moved the wrong way. The India VIX measures what traders pay for protection against large swings in the Nifty. It rose about three and a half percent on Thursday to about 16.1, while the Nifty also rose. When protection gets dearer on an up day, some traders are buying the rally and the insurance at the same time. One cross asset gap keeps widening. Gold funds are down about twelve percent over the month. The Nifty is up about fourteen percent over the same month. Money keeps leaving the safe asset for the index.

## What would change this read

The read is a bounce off the October low that has lost its width and kept its height. Banks, car makers and large staples hold the index up, while the average stock has slipped below its short and long lines. The constructive view fails if the slide under the surface continues. A long count under about 1,000 would say Wednesday was the start of a new leg down, and Thursday closed at 1,043. A day of 300 or more hard fallers would match the October pattern. New yearly lows above about 100 would say sellers have returned in force. A VIX close above about 18 would say fear has taken charge. A sharp fall in banks or autos on heavy volume would remove the groups that carry the index. A long count under about 605, the October low, would erase the whole bounce.

The cautious view fails if the members catch up with the index. The first sign is the short count back above half the market, about 1,170, from 1,122 on Thursday. The second is the long count back above 1,050 and then turning toward half, from 1,043. The third is a wide up day, with 200 or more hard risers on turnover above about 600 crore. The fourth is new yearly highs pulling clear of new lows again, from level at 39 each. The fifth is a VIX close under 15, from 16.1. The first two signs together, with any one of the others in the same week, would put the repair back on track.
