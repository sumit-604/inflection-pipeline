# IOLCP: business narrative (Phase 1 lite, run 2026-09-19)

Phase 1 lite. The evidence stages and the phase 1 verifiers ran. No valuation ran, so this file has no entry zone, no decision and no destination PE. Where a verifier corrected an upstream figure, this file uses the corrected figure.

## BUSINESS UNDERSTANDING NARRATIVE

IOL makes bulk drug ingredients and industrial acetyl chemicals at one integrated site in Barnala, Punjab. Ibuprofen, the painkiller molecule, is about 38% of sales, and IOL runs a 12,000 tonne a year ibuprofen plant at about 95% utilisation, the largest single ibuprofen line in the world. A basket of other APIs is about 22% of sales: paracetamol, metformin, clopidogrel, pantoprazole and others, the active powders a generic drug maker presses into tablets. Acetyl chemicals sold to outside buyers, mainly ethyl acetate and acetic anhydride, are about 40% of sales and go into paints, inks, packaging, textiles, food processing and drug making. The same chemical plant makes the intermediates that feed IOL's own ibuprofen line, a captive transfer of ₹238 Cr in FY26, so IOL makes in house what a rival without that plant must buy. The API buyers are generic formulators in more than 80 countries, and exports were 24% of FY26 sales. A formulator names its API supplier's drug master file in its own product filing, and IOL holds 14 US DMFs and 21 European CEPs, so a switch forces the buyer to requalify; the run found no disclosure of how concentrated the top customers are. The chemical buyers are industrial users who buy on price. Demand today is generic drug volume, which the run will track through European and global generic formulator offtake volumes and through the capacity status of the other large ibuprofen makers, Solara Active Pharma, Shandong Xinhua and BASF. Input supply matters as much as demand: most chemical feedstock comes from China, and metformin's key input DCDA has no Indian supplier, so China DCDA export prices and the acetyl spread are live signals. Growth should come from three named steps: more non ibuprofen APIs filling the new paracetamol unit, a 1,500 million tablet contract manufacturing plant for European anchor customers, and a tolling unit for one global chemical company, the last two due in Q3 FY27. Each step has an outside check: USFDA DMF and EDQM CEP grants, the paracetamol global spot price index, PLI bulk drug scheme disbursement, and the naming of the CDMO European anchor customers. The market for this product basket grows about 5% a year, so IOL's growth must come from share and from new lines, not from the market. The API line has a real but moderate advantage: backward integration and a large regulatory filing base, which the emerging moat scan rates as a documented rare manufacturing capability and documented process innovation. The acetyl chemicals line has no moat; it is a converter that earns the spread between feedstock and product price. The two new lines rest on partnerships and an ESG base that supports European access, but both depend on unnamed customers, and the scan scored zero on talent asymmetry (Category 21) and zero on cannibalisation barrier (Category 22), so no rival would have to break its own business to copy IOL.

Ibuprofen maker diversifies, cash and ownership unresolved

IOL is a two speed manufacturer. Ibuprofen and ethyl acetate made up about 72% of FY25 revenue, and both sell at world prices IOL does not set. The other 28% and the new lines are where the company is trying to earn a better price for its capacity. FY26 revenue was ₹2,319 Cr and PAT ₹138 Cr.

The transition is a mix shift inside the pharma segment. Non ibuprofen APIs rose from 18% of pharma revenue in FY21 to 37% in FY26 and 43% in Q1 FY27, and that revenue grew 67% year on year. Management targets 50 to 55% by FY29. A ₹495 Cr programme is dated and itemised in a September 2026 exchange filing: ₹350 Cr for 6,000 more tonnes of ibuprofen by December 2027, ₹110 Cr for the tablet plant and ₹35 Cr for the tolling unit. A separate ₹1,200 to 1,400 Cr greenfield site has no product mix and no commissioning date after four calls. The paracetamol unit is the swing asset, and the record disagrees on its fill: the Q1 FY27 press release says 65% and the Q1 FY27 call says about 55%. The emerging moat scan first scored 26.3 (STRENGTHENING); the framework verifier corrected it to 23.4 (MODEST), and it corrected capex embedded growth from 143% to 39%, the dated tranche alone.

The audited record is middling. Gate 0 scored 71 of 160, classification GOOD, capped there because ROCE sat below 15% in four of the last five years. ROCE ran 54 to 68% in the FY19 to FY21 ibuprofen price spike and 9 to 17% since; the company's own FY26 ROCE is 9.7%. Revenue was ₹2,184 Cr in FY22, went sideways to FY25, then grew 11.5% in FY26 while EBITDA grew 29%. Q1 FY27 revenue grew 37% at a 14.6% EBITDA margin, against a 14 to 15% full year guide that management declined to raise. The moat score of 3 of 60 is not a finding of no moat: five of twelve tests scored zero only because the stage had no peer data.

Cash is the open question. FY26 CFO was about ₹214 Cr, 1.56 times PAT, but free cash flow was only ₹44 Cr after ₹173 Cr of capex and ₹73 Cr absorbed by working capital. Gross receivables grew 17.7% against revenue growth of 11.5%, debtor days rose from 79 in FY22 to 95 in FY26, and payable days fell from about 100 to 89. One reading says growth: Q4 FY26 revenue grew 17%, and receivables overdue past six months held flat at 1.6 to 1.7%. The other reading says a lasting squeeze: loss allowance coverage rose from 0.51% to 0.76%, and IOL paid suppliers faster while collecting slower. The half year balance sheet at 30 September 2026 separates the two, and the June 2026 CARE rationale, which the run does not hold, should carry the rating agency's own working capital read. The balance sheet has no term debt, and booked capital commitments are ₹69 Cr against a programme called internally funded.

Ownership moved fast. Promoter holding rose from 48% in September 2024 to 62% in June 2026 with no new shares. The run traced the last 4.8 points to an amalgamation of two vehicles that the March 2026 shareholding pattern classed as public into promoter entity NM Merchantiles; press reports called this "no change", which the filings contradict. Who controlled those vehicles before the merger is not found. In the same year, purchases from three promoter group entities rose 31% to ₹132 Cr, and NM Merchantiles nearly tripled its sales to IOL as its stake rose. Institutions voted 91% against one director's reappointment at the September 2026 AGM, and the CEO seat has been empty since September 2024. Against that: zero pledge in all eight quarters, a clean audit opinion, no restatement, and related party deals approved by an independent majority audit committee. The promoter verdict is CAUTION, not CONCERN.

Management mostly does what it says on the quarter. It beat the Q4 FY26 guide of ₹600 Cr with ₹619 Cr, delivered 15.2% margin against about 11%, and filed a capex breakdown that matches its call to the rupee. The concall stage graded it B. The red flag verifier puts it at the B/C line: management denied a Q4 inventory gain in May and admitted it in August, so part of the 15.2% beat came from inventory valuation. The same verifier found the R&D and new product pipeline unanswered on three straight calls, and two officers gave two analysts different answers on the acetyl spread in one May call. It also found the greenfield timeline is still inside its own window, so the stage's "missed" label is wrong. The greenfield is open, not failed. The grade needs an operator ruling at Halt 1.

Peers confirm the input story and dispute the price story. Aarti Drugs and Granules both confirm that the industry buys DCDA from China. Laxmi Organic confirms that the acetyl spread spiked in March and April 2026 and then eased. Granules says it got no paracetamol price increase and saw price erosion in the same quarters IOL claims a paracetamol price rise, so part of IOL's margin gain may be spread and inventory, not mix. A second contradiction on the acetyl spread was struck by the verifier: IOL never made that claim. Every peer is expanding capacity at the same time, so IOL is in a capacity race, not a lone build.

The market is big enough and grows slowly. The realistic addressable market is about ₹18,700 Cr, the serviceable part about ₹15,200 Cr, and IOL already holds about 15% of it. Market share discipline gives revenue of about ₹3,220 Cr in three years and ₹3,940 Cr in five, an 11 to 12% growth rate, below management's 15 to 20% guide. Runway class is GOOD. Management's own market slides quote a global API market 153 times the company specific estimate, and its two market figures for Indian specialty chemicals, filed five days apart, differ by more than two times. The tablet plant is under 0.3% of the output of just two large European contract makers, a niche entry.
