## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

**2A. Demand environment.** All three peers describe strong underlying secular demand in their own verticals through the period: MIDHANI's defense/aerospace order book and revenue hit records in Q4 FY26 (PEER-MIDHANI_Jun_2026 p.2); TDPOWERSYS describes outright equipment shortage — "tremendous shortage of power generation equipment" — driven by AI data centers and grid stabilization, with demand visibility "up to 2030" (PEER-TDPOWERSYS_Aug_2026 p.4, PEER-TDPOWERSYS_Feb_2026 p.9); SALZERELEC cites a broad India electrification/infrastructure upcycle (50%+ non-fossil installed capacity, RDSS, EV charging, data centers) as structural (PEER-SALZERELEC_Nov_2025 p.5-6, PEER-SALZERELEC_May_2026 p.4-5). The one clear divergence from an otherwise-bullish demand consensus is RDSS/smart metering specifically, where SALZERELEC's own new-segment execution has consistently disappointed guidance across all four quarters despite the "boom" framing at the industry level — this nuance (macro demand strong, specific-product qualification/execution slow) maps closely onto PML's own framing of its RDSS-adjacent and relay opportunities.

**2B. Pricing and input costs.** SALZERELEC and TDPOWERSYS both report sharp commodity inflation (copper, silver, aluminium, plastics) compressing margins in FY26/FY27 with a pass-through lag (PEER-SALZERELEC_Aug_2026 p.4, PEER-TDPOWERSYS_Feb_2026 p.6-7). TDPOWERSYS manages this materially better via price-variation clauses and a currency tailwind from rupee depreciation on its ~75-80% export book (PEER-TDPOWERSYS_Feb_2026 p.7). MIDHANI, by contrast, benefited from *falling* raw-material costs through most of FY26 (PEER-MIDHANI_Aug_2025 p.16-17) before flagging new energy/LPG cost pressure from the Middle East conflict by June 2026 (PEER-MIDHANI_Jun_2026 p.10). No peer's cost narrative directly maps onto PML's Alloys/Relay/Quantum Magnetics cost structure, but the pattern of 2025-26 being a volatile commodity-cost year across unrelated small/mid-cap industrials is a relevant macro backdrop.

**2C. Capex cycle.** This is **not** a lone-expander situation — all three peers are simultaneously running large multi-year capacity expansions funded mostly through debt/working capital rather than equity: MIDHANI is planning ~Rs.1,000cr of capex over three years plus debottlenecking (PEER-MIDHANI_Jun_2026 p.5); SALZERELEC is building a Saudi Arabia plant, a Bangalore energy-management SPV, and smart-meter capacity, alongside a demerger/deleveraging debate from its own shareholders (PEER-SALZERELEC_May_2026 p.7-11); TDPOWERSYS commissioned a third plant in Dec-2025 and is guiding toward Rs.3,200-4,000cr of capacity by FY28-30 (PEER-TDPOWERSYS_Feb_2026 p.3, PEER-TDPOWERSYS_Aug_2026 p.3-4). This is an industry-wide, cross-sector capacity race among small/mid-cap Indian industrials, not an isolated PML phenomenon — see Part 5 for the cross-peer hypothesis this pattern supports.

**2D. Competitive mentions.** No peer transcript mentions Permanent Magnets Ltd or PML by name or unmistakable description in any of the twelve calls. MIDHANI names PTC Industries as a competitor in single-crystal turbine-blade materials but frames the market as large enough for both (PEER-MIDHANI_Nov_2025 p.17, PEER-MIDHANI_Feb_2026 p.6).

**2E. Risks peers discuss that PML's own claims do not surface (for missing-risks analysis).**
- MIDHANI: heavy import dependency (75-80% of raw materials) for super-alloy elements (nickel, cobalt, moly, tungsten) exposed to geopolitical supply-chain disruption, prompting a customer-funded "metal bank" strategic-reserve initiative (PEER-MIDHANI_Nov_2025 p.6-7, PEER-MIDHANI_Feb_2026 p.11); energy/LPG constraints from the Middle East conflict flagged as a live risk to FY27 execution (PEER-MIDHANI_Jun_2026 p.10-11).
- SALZERELEC: severe single-commodity spikes (silver ~3x) causing 200bps+ margin hits with multi-month pass-through lag (PEER-SALZERELEC_Feb_2026 p.20-21); "new entrant" structural disadvantage in AMISP/DISCOM qualification versus incumbents like HPL and Zenith (PEER-SALZERELEC_Feb_2026 p.18); explicit investor pushback on repeated guidance misses/credibility ("do you think you have misled investors" — PEER-SALZERELEC_Feb_2026 p.9); shareholder pressure for demerger given a persistently sub-scale, debt-funded, low-margin wires & cables division (PEER-SALZERELEC_May_2026 p.10-11).
- TDPOWERSYS: global machine-tool capacity constraint delaying its own large-generator capex (15-16 month lead times because "everyone is expanding" — PEER-TDPOWERSYS_May_2026 p.9); a one-off but material logistics/shipping-delay LD penalty hit to consolidated margins (PEER-TDPOWERSYS_May_2026 p.4); persistently weak cash conversion (CFO/EBITDA <10-21%) during the high-growth phase due to working-capital build (PEER-TDPOWERSYS_Nov_2025 p.6, PEER-TDPOWERSYS_May_2026 p.10-11).

## PART 5: CROSS-PEER HYPOTHESIS (mandatory closing step)

**Hypothesis:** Three unrelated small/mid-cap Indian industrials — a defense/aerospace alloy maker (MIDHANI), an electrical-components/metering company (SALZERELEC), and an industrial generator/motor exporter (TDPOWERSYS) — are simultaneously running large, debt/working-capital-funded, multi-year capacity expansions (MIDHANI ~Rs.1,000cr over three years; SALZERELEC's Saudi plant, Bangalore SPV and smart-meter capacity; TDPOWERSYS's third plant plus a further ramp toward Rs.3,200-4,000cr of capacity by FY28-30) against long-duration secular demand narratives that are each, individually, outside company control to confirm: Indian defense-budget/indigenization cycles (MIDHANI), the RDSS political/tendering timeline (SALZERELEC), and global AI-data-center capex (TDPOWERSYS). None of the three frames this as a systemic phenomenon — each presents its own capex as company-specific opportunity-capture. But taken together, the pattern suggests a synchronized, debt-funded small-cap industrial capex upcycle across unrelated verticals in India through FY26-27, all betting on secular demand stories that could plausibly disappoint on similar timing (a defense-budget pause, an RDSS political delay, or an AI-capex digestion pause are each independently plausible in the next 2-3 years). If any one of these secular narratives falters, the correlated effect on debt-servicing capacity and margin across otherwise-uncorrelated small-cap industrial names financed in the same vintage would be a systemic risk that is invisible from any single company's disclosures — testable by tracking whether working-capital/debt metrics across this cohort (and PML itself, given its own capex plans across Alloys/Relay/Quantum Magnetics) move together if any one secular driver weakens.
