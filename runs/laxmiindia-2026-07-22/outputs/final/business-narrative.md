Rajasthan MSME lender leaning on assignment gains

Laxmi India Finance is a small NBFC based in Jaipur, lending to micro and small enterprises, vehicle buyers and mortgage borrowers across 176 branches in six states. The Baid family runs it and held about 60% after listing on 5 August 2025. FY26 total income was Rs 320 Cr, PAT Rs 50 Cr, AUM Rs 1,626 Cr, and interest income makes up 94% of revenue. MSME loans are 80% of the book and Rajasthan is roughly 82% of AUM, so both the product and the geography are concentrated.

The balance sheet was genuinely repaired at the IPO. CRAR moved from 20.80% pre-IPO to 26.12% in FY26, debt to equity fell from 4.42x to 2.87x, and the Rs 152 Cr of net proceeds went to onward lending exactly as promised, the one guidance item management hit. Cost of borrowing came down from 11.48% to 10.80% and Acuite lifted the rating one notch to A Stable in March 2026. The strengthening moat here is funding cost and capitalisation, not technology.

Reported earnings lean on direct assignment and ARC gains, and the pipeline could not clear how much. Upfront gain on sale drove 76% of PAT in FY23, 68% in FY24 and 27% in FY25, and a single Rs 41 Cr pool sale in Q4 FY26 booked Rs 8.66 Cr of upfront profit against only 4.8% customer growth that quarter. ARC Security Receipt investments grew 5.4x in FY26 while the loan book grew about 31%. Two notes in the prospectus disagree by roughly 180x on how much NPA moved to ARCs, and three write-off figures across filings do not reconcile. Core lending profit stripped of these gains is not disclosed anywhere.

Gate 0 scored the company AVOID, but that label is mechanical. The formula treats loan-book growth as an operating cash outflow, which zeroes the cash block for any scaling NBFC, and it applies the maximum history downgrade to a company with only four years of restated statements. Growth, capital adequacy and asset quality outside the one defaulted pool do not read as a weak business.

Management said one thing and delivered another in its first post-IPO year. Every quantified maiden guidance metric from the August 2025 call missed by FY26 close: AUM grew 27% against 35% to 40% guided, PAT grew 38% against 50%, branches rose 18 against 35 to 44, cost of borrowing fell 68 bps against 100 to 125, and the rating moved one notch against a promised two. Management promised NPA improvement in three straight calls while GNPA nearly doubled from 1.28% to 2.13%. The Up Money counterparty default that caused most of that came out reactively under analyst questions, not proactively. The credibility grade is D on that delivery record.

Peers contradict the excuse and confirm the structural risk. Management blamed Rajasthan floods and heavy commercial vehicle stress for the Q2 asset quality slide, but all four peers show flat to improving GNPA through FY26 and none names a flood. On the other side, UGRO Capital discloses that 50% to 60% of its historical ROA came from assignment and co-lending gains, watched its PAT fall from Rs 43 Cr to Rs 6 Cr as it pivoted away, and now treats that income as lower quality. That is the cautionary version of Laxmi's own dependency, and it argues the concern grows rather than fades.

The addressable market is large and the runway is long. The conservative NBFC-channel MSME lending market is around Rs 8.8 lakh Cr, Laxmi's serviceable slice is about Rs 71,000 Cr, and its current share is 2.3%, leaving revenue headroom of roughly 223x on the framework's math. Management's own commissioned figure of Rs 26 lakh Cr is 3x this stage's estimate and reads as inflated. The nearer limit is capital, not demand: the three-year growth path implies an AUM ceiling about Rs 930 Cr short of what current net worth supports at the 15% CRAR floor, so it needs more equity to get there. Part of this market work ran on blocked searches, so the sizing is partial.

Strengths and risks sit close together. The strengths are a repaired balance sheet, an improving funding cost, best-in-class headline asset quality of 0.80% GNPA outside the one bad pool, unusually candid compliance disclosure, and a new external CTO plus two credentialed independent directors. The risks are the unresolved earnings composition, a live criminal FIR against a sitting whole-time director, the managing director voting on the audit committee at a company with a fresh restatement history, a stock trading around 18% below its issue price with FII holding falling from 4.61% to 1.40%, and a management team that missed every number it gave.
