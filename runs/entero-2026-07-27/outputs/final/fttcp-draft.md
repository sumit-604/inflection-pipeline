## 3. THE CATALYST STORY IN PLAIN WORDS

Revenue keeps firing on three things that are documented, not hoped: organic
growth running 17-18% at about 1.4x the pharma market, the MedTech line
scaling past Rs 1,000 Cr, and share taken from unorganised distributors. What
would kill it: the organic multiple over the market falling to 1.0x, or a big
manufacturer pulling its distribution rights (99% of revenue depends on
manufacturer authorisation with no disclosed concentration).

Margin extends if the MedTech mix keeps rising (worth about 35bps of EBITDA
on the operator's arithmetic) and if the value-over-volume gap holds, where
the pharma market grows 8.6% in rupees but only 0.6% in units, handing a
distributor operating leverage because revenue scales with rupees and cost
scales with boxes. What confirms it: 5.0% EBITDA across two clean quarters.
What kills it: a slide back to 4% on integration cost, or a gross-margin
print that does not hold once the Q1 one-offs wash out.

Cash is the weak leg. FY26 turned positive, which is real, but there is no
documented working-capital programme, management twice refused to disclose
receivables ageing, and free cash flow cannot be computed. The single catalyst
is the 50% OCF-to-EBITDA guidance, which is a claim until H1 FY27 prints it.
This is why the determination is INDETERMINATE and why the whole verdict
carries a caveat.

ROCE recovers if the acquisition pause holds, because the goodwill base stops
growing while the working capital keeps earning. The catalyst and the
counter-catalyst are the same lever: a resumption of large M&A both funds the
next growth leg and re-buries reported ROCE under fresh goodwill.
