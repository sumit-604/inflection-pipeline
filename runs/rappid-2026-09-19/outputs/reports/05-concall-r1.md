### 2B Excuse pattern analysis

Classification: **external-blame-heavy**. Every miss traces to the same
external factor (non-ferrous commodity price volatility, once compounded by
a geopolitical LPG-supply disruption), stated consistently and with
specific, plausible numbers (NAB price ~1,250 to ~1,650 Rs/kg). Management
credits itself for the response ("conscious management decision," "we
firmly believe protecting shareholders' capital... is more important than
pursuing growth at any cost") while attributing the shortfall entirely to
the outside event. No instance across the sources read this stage where
management says a forecast itself was wrong. The 50% CAGR target was set in
Feb-2025 with no stated margin for commodity-price risk, in a business the
CMD himself later describes (same call) as running multi-year, PSU-dominated
contracts with no price-escalation clause and long, logistics-bound
execution cycles ("shipyards run in kilometers... the GRN passing takes
time," p.27). That structural risk was foreseeable at the time the target
was set; it is not named as a caveat in the Feb-2025 guidance itself.

Pattern check: hard topics (the capacity/utilization math, the abandoned
acquisition, the missed guidance) surfaced only under analyst pressure at
the one available call, not proactively in the Reg 30 updates between the
miss (H2 FY26, visible by the 16-Oct-2025 order-book print) and the eventual
explanation (01-Jun-2026 concall, roughly seven months later).

## SECTION 3: COMPETITIVE INTELLIGENCE

### 3A Competitor commentary and credibility check

Management names China as the direct international competitor for export
orders ("directly challenged with China... a very big challenge for the
Indian manufacturers," p.5) and claims domestic defence-valve competition is
self-limiting: "people who have entered last year, whatever orders we have
lost, they're not bidding for any more orders," citing the working-capital
and type-approval burden as the deterrent (p.28). This is plausible given
the certification list (API, DNV, IBR, ClassNK, UL) but is an unverifiable,
self-serving claim with no named competitor or evidence; flagged to
peer_questions for a check against KSB (the scaled incumbent) and Atam
Valves (a size-matched comp).

### 3B Industry and market intelligence

- NAB (nickel-aluminium-bronze) price: ~Rs 1,250/kg to ~Rs 1,650/kg, a ~32%
  rise, cited as the proximate cause of the H2 FY26 slowdown (p.6); a
  further 25-30% broad metals rise (including stainless/carbon steel) cited
  as extending into the new financial year (p.8).
- PSU tenders carry no price-escalation clause; private-sector orders can
  carry one, negotiated bilaterally (p.6-7, p.16-17).
- India "building more than 20 warships at a time" (p.21), an unverified
  scale claim; the FSS programme specifically involves 5 ships, with 2
  offloaded from Hindustan Shipyard to L&T Shipbuilding (p.19).
- Valve content per vessel: management estimates 2-3% of a ~Rs 4,000 Cr
  vessel cost but states this "keeps changing" and that shipyards
  themselves (L&T, HSL, Cochin) could not give a consistent answer (p.20).
- Repeat-customer rate stated at 75% (p.17), with all PSU relationships
  characterized as effectively repeat by default absent blacklisting.

## ANALYST NOTE

The single most load-bearing fact for stage 6/FTTCP is not the FY26 miss
itself but its structure: a numeric growth target was set (Feb-2025) with
no stated allowance for a commodity-price and PSU-execution-timing risk
that management itself later describes as structural to this business, and
the same pattern repeats at the Jun-2026 concall with the FY27 "50% or
more" figure, restated with confidence, then hedged hard the moment an
analyst extrapolated further upside from the CMD's own numbers ("if I
overcommit and underperform, that's a problem"). Grade C reflects a mixed
but miss-heavy record (1 delivered, 1 partial, 3 missed of the gradable
items), not a coroner's verdict: the marine/naval mix shift is real and
AR-corroborated, and the FSS orders won in Q1 FY27 are genuine, named,
purchase-order-backed evidence, not narrative. The Q1 FY27 pace (+28% YoY)
sitting well under the run-rate a 50%+ full year requires is this run's
central monitorable, testable at the H1 FY27 half-yearly print with no
inference required.
