## SECTION 1: THE BUSINESS MODEL IN PLAIN ENGLISH

### 1A. One-line description
MapmyIndia builds and licenses India's most detailed digital map database, sells that intelligence as embedded software to carmakers, APIs/subscriptions to enterprises and government, and layers a smaller hardware-plus-subscription IoT tracking business (via subsidiary Gtropy) on top.

### 1B. Money flow chain, by revenue stream

### 1E. The chai-stall-uncle version
Imagine your neighbourhood chai stall had, over 30 years, drawn the single most detailed map of every lane, shop and pothole in India — a map nobody else is legally allowed to draw as precisely, because the government says only India-based companies can hold this kind of data. Now every carmaker in India pays this chai-stall uncle a fee, per car, per navigation feature, to put that map inside the dashboard. Big companies and government offices also pay a monthly fee to use the map through an app. On the side, uncle also sells little GPS boxes for trucks and rents out tracking software — smaller, lower-margin, but growing fast. Because the map was already drawn years ago, printing one more copy costs almost nothing — so the map business throws off nearly half its revenue as profit, while the GPS-box business is a normal, thinner-margin hardware trade.

### 4D. Highest-value questions for management

1. What is the per-vehicle-module ARPU for NCASE licenses in India versus the stated 2.5-3x ARPU in the Indonesia JV, and how is it trending as attach rate scales toward the 25% FY28 target? Reassuring: ARPU stable or rising with volumes. Worrying: ARPU eroding under OEM price pressure even as volumes grow.

Reassuring: one-off timing, core demand intact per order book. Worrying: genuine competitive or pricing pressure in core map licensing.

Reassuring: bounded, seasonal, with a clear collection/order-to-cash plan. Worrying: structural deterioration tied to government payment cycles with no mitigation.
