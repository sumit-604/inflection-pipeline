## Business understanding narrative

Kronox makes high purity inorganic salt chemicals. The range covers phosphates, sulphates, chlorides and nitrates, about 185 products in all. Buyers use these salts as pharma excipients, as lab and reagent grade chemicals, and as food and nutraceutical ingredients. Purity grade and certification decide who can supply, so a qualified maker is hard to swap out mid formulation. All revenue sits in one segment, direct product sale to business customers. The largest named end market is pharma formulators. Exports were 32.39% of FY26 revenue, up from 24.75% two years earlier, and the rest sold in India. The annual report gives no customer concentration or product mix, so buyer names and top customer share stay unknown.

Present demand tracks Indian pharmaceutical formulation and API output, the IIP Pharma sub index, plus import substitution as buyers move sourcing away from China. Forward demand rests on the same China+1 shift, the rising export mix, and pharma excipient market growth near 7.5% a year. Externally verifiable signals for these are Pharmexcil export data and DGCIS trade statistics. USD/INR and raw material input prices swing the margin, since materials run about 46.6% of revenue with no disclosed hedge.

The competitive advantage is thin. The emerging moat scan scored 10.2 and classified NONE. Two categories cleared the bar: a war chest of Rs 64.5 Cr in deposits, and partial qualification lock in from the certificate stack. Every candidate moat fails the cannibalization test, because a funded rival can copy the certificates, raise its own cash, or take export share without giving anything up. No business line shows a durable moat. This is an execution led profile, not a locked one.

## Debt free fine chemicals maker, growth stalled

FY26 revenue was Rs 101 Cr, up 1% on FY25. PAT margin held near 26% and the company carries no debt beyond Rs 1.6 Cr of vehicle loans, with Rs 64.5 Cr in fixed deposits. EBITDA margin nearly doubled over four years, from 23% in FY23 to 34% in FY26. ROCE stayed high but fell every year, from 49% to 32%.

The growth stall has an owner cause, not an economic one. Dahej Unit IV is the single growth catalyst. Land was leased and clearances obtained by 2024 and 2025, but construction had not started when the FY26 report was signed in August 2026. Capital work in progress is Rs 87.6 lakh company wide and capital commitments are nil. The Chairman says new deadlines are finalised, two years to production and three to full use, with no capex figure behind the claim.

Promoters ran a 100% offer for sale IPO in June 2024 and took about Rs 130 Cr, with nil going to the company. In August 2026 they sold control to Indo Borax at Rs 103.22 a share, below the Rs 157.27 mandatory open offer price. Indo Borax is a 45 year old boric acid maker, backed by Edelweiss capital and a new outside CEO. The platform is under 18 months old as a roll up, so the effect on Kronox is unproven.

Gate 0 scored AVERAGE. The core fundamentals scored GOOD at 75 of 100, then dropped a grade on the four year limited history, since only the FY26 report is held. The moat score is thin at 11 of 60, mostly because four of twelve tests need peer data this run did not have.

Cash quality fell in FY26. True operating cash to pre tax profit dropped from 115.5% to 90.1%. Net operating cash fell 22.2% on flat revenue. Receivable days rose from 72.4 to 76.4 and inventory grew 31.4% against 1% revenue growth. The deposit pile keeps growing while the growth capex does not.

FY26 profit growth of 8.6% was almost all non operating, driven by other income roughly doubling, while operating pre tax profit rose 1.2%. Q1 FY27 PAT rose 38.5%, but a tax rate fall to 25.6% from 37.5% did most of that work, and operating pre tax profit rose 16.2%. Credibility grades C. Low bar promises like the dividend and flat revenue were met. The one load bearing promise, Dahej, was missed with no capital behind the restart date.

Peers break the macro excuse. Kronox blamed a difficult year for flat revenue. In the same window DMCC grew 35%, TANFACIND grew 27% and Neogen grew 11%, all facing the same input cost shock. The two expanding peers anchor their capex to rupee figures, tonnage and pre contracted offtake. Kronox gives none of that for Dahej.

The market runway is GOOD, not vast. The reachable market is about Rs 1,201 Cr and current share is 8.4%, so revenue could grow about 12 times at full penetration. The three year reachable path implies 13.6% revenue growth a year, above the flat history but well short of the 25% strategy target, and it needs the stalled Dahej capacity that does not yet exist.
