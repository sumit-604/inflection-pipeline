## PART 1: INDEPENDENT RED-FLAG LIST

Thirty items. Each is anchored (call, speaker, PDF p.N).

### Group A. Working capital, debt and the inventory promise

**IF-01. The INR300 Cr inventory promise was missed and reframed, not owned.**
Q3 FY26 call, Akshay Pitti, PDF p.7: "as of 31st December, we had approximately
INR500 crores worth of inventory, and we expect this inventory to go down to
our historic levels of about INR300 crores worth of inventory. So, about a
INR200 crores reduction in raw material is what we are looking at over the next
3 months." Repeated to a second analyst, PDF p.13: "we see that going down to
about INR300 crores by April." Outcome, Q4 FY26 call, PDF p.6, Akshay Pitti:
"We see the inventory levels maintaining at around INR390 crores, INR400 crores
level going forward." The miss surfaced only because Balasubramanian raised it
first (same page: "we targeted inventory level of INR300 crores... It's
remained at INR400 crores levels"). Management never called it a miss.

**IF-02. The normalisation date had already been missed once before.**
Q2 FY26 call, extra, Akshay Pitti, PDF p.9: "We had expected that this would
get stabilized by April, but it continues to persist." That is an April 2025
date already failed when the April 2026 date was set. Same call, PDF p.16: "my
expectation and hope is that by year-end, something starts easing out on the
raw material front." So the inventory unwind has now been dated three times and
missed twice. This makes it a repeated pattern across four calls, not a single
miss.

**IF-03. Net debt rose during the quarter in which INR200 Cr of release was
promised.** Q3 FY26 call, Akshay Pitti, PDF p.12: "the net debt is around
INR550 crores." Q4 FY26 call, Akshay Pitti, PDF p.14: "as of now, the net debt
is around INR570-odd crores." Up, not down, across the exact three months in
which INR200 Cr of raw material was to be liquidated.

**IF-04. The working-capital release mechanism was swapped, not delivered.**
Q3 FY26 call, PDF p.5 and p.12: release comes from liquidating excess inventory
and from factoring receivables. Q4 FY26 call, Akshay Pitti, PDF p.10: "what we
are looking at is about INR125 crores of creditors are increasing. So our
payables will increase by INR125 crores, which should help offset interest cost
to that extent." The promised release now comes from stretching creditors, a
lower-quality source than liquidating stock. The substitution is never flagged
as a change of plan.

**IF-28. Working-capital optimisation is capped, and the cap contradicts the
new project.** Q1 FY27 call, Akshay Pitti, PDF p.10: "there is still potential
to further rationalize our working capital to the tune of about Rs. 25 crores
to Rs. 30 crores." Same call, PDF p.14: "about Rs. 20 crores to Rs. 25 crores
is for the potential to optimize the working capital, and beyond that, there
would not be any opportunity to further optimize the working capital from
today." Two different figures in one call, and a hard cap admission. The cap
sits against the greenfield's own requirement, Q4 FY26 call, Akshay Pitti,
PDF p.12: "Working capital requirement would be roughly 90 to 120 days, net
working capital. So it's going to be a little heavier on the working capital
side."

### Group B. Guidance integrity

**IF-06. FY27 lamination guidance was cut in silence, then the recovery was
announced as an upgrade. (CRITICAL)**
- Q2 FY26 call, extra, PDF p.17. Rahul Kumar asks for the FY27 volume guidance.
  Akshay Pitti: "we would be targeting somewhere around 80,000 to 83,000 tons,
  around that level." Rahul Kumar: "Okay. That's for FY '27?" Akshay Pitti:
  "Yes."
- Q3 FY26 call, Akshay Pitti, PDF p.11: "As far as next year is concerned, we
  are targeting somewhere around 78,000 tons for lamination." No mention that
  this is a cut.
- Q4 FY26 call, Akshay Pitti, PDF p.9: "we have targeted 78,000 tons of sales."
- Q1 FY27 call, Akshay Pitti, PDF p.6: "we are revising our annual target to
  82,000 tons for Lamination from 78,000 tons, as previously stated in Q4
  Conference Call."

The 82,000 t "revision up" only restores a number that was quietly reduced by
2,000 to 5,000 t two quarters earlier. Measured against the original FY27
guidance, the current target is flat to lower. The base was moved, then the
return to the base was sold as an upgrade.

**IF-07. The casting and machine-component target was raised on a quarter that
grew 4.2%.** Q4 FY26 call, PDF p.4: FY26 casting and machine components 12,012
t. Q1 FY27 call, PDF p.5: "Total Casting and Machine Components volume stood at
3,191... tons during the quarter, a growth of 4.2% YOY basis." Q1 FY27 call,
Akshay Pitti, PDF p.9: "We would be upward guiding the Casting numbers to about
17,000 tons as well." To reach 17,000 t the remaining three quarters must
deliver 13,809 t against 8,950 t a year earlier, which is +54% year on year for
three consecutive quarters, starting from a quarter that grew 4.2%. Management
names the gating constraint in the same answer, PDF p.9: "the current capacity
utilization on Machining is almost 86.33%. That is the biggest bottleneck for
us... It is not the order book, the ability to build machines fast enough."

**IF-08. The FY27 EBITDA figure implies a margin below the stated margin
guide.** Only stated FY27 revenue: Q4 FY26 call, Akshay Pitti, PDF p.9, "roughly
INR2,300 crores of top line." Only stated FY27 EBITDA: Q1 FY27 call, Akshay
Pitti, PDF p.16, "an EBITDA of roughly Rs. 370-odd crores based on current
outlook." That pair gives 16.1%. The margin guide in the same two calls is
higher: Q3 FY26 call, PDF p.11, "Around 17% as a midpoint. For the full year, it
should be around 17%"; Q4 FY26 call, PDF p.9, "we should look at similar margins
in terms of percentage." The revenue figure predates the volume upgrade, so the
implied margin could be lower still. A margin downgrade sits inside an EBITDA
number that reads as guidance maintained.

**IF-23. The company's own stated margin mechanism did not appear.** Q3 FY26
call, Akshay Pitti, PDF p.12, on why gross margin fell: "high-value items have
shown a significant growth. Now those also use a more expensive raw material.
So, our margins are on a fixed margin basis. So, the gross margins are slightly
lower." Q4 FY26 call, Akshay Pitti, PDF p.16: "when the prices go up, the
percentage goes down. And the prices go down; percentage margin goes up." In Q1
FY27 lamination volume grew 19% while revenue grew 16% (Q1 FY27 call, PDF p.5),
so realisation per tonne fell. By management's own mechanism the percentage
margin should have risen. It fell, to 16.8% from 17.0% for FY26. Management
blamed manpower instead, PDF p.8: "our manpower cost is slightly higher... for
the Capex that we have incurred, we have put in all the manpower and expenses
in place."

**IF-09. The 17% plus or minus 50 bps band was undershot twice.** Guide: Q3 FY26
call, Akshay Pitti, PDF p.10, "EBITDA margin would remain steady around the
current levels, plus/minus 50 bps." Q4 FY26 delivered 16.6% (derived: FY26
adjusted EBITDA INR326 Cr less 9M INR241.8 Cr on Q4 revenue INR506 Cr). Q1 FY27
delivered 16.8% (Q1 FY27 call, PDF p.5).

**IF-05. Tax rate reversed from 33% to 25% with no reconciliation.** Q4 FY26
call, Akshay Pitti, PDF p.15: "looking at around 33%. Going forward, I think
this would be similar... This issue is not going to go away till the ESOP and
the difference in depreciation rates goes away." Q1 FY27 call, Akshay Pitti,
PDF p.13, answering an analyst who quoted the 33% back: "No, it will be about
25%, not 33%... the full year tax rate should be closer to 25%." An 800 bps
swing in guided tax in one quarter changes PAT by roughly 12%. The three
structural reasons given for 33% are never revisited.

**IF-19. The long-term net-debt target was withdrawn without being retracted.**
Q4 FY26 call, Akshay Pitti, PDF p.15: "if you don't look at any capex happening
in FY'28, I would estimate that your net debt should be down to about INR250-odd
crores." Q1 FY27 call, Akshay Pitti, PDF p.13, asked again: "the debt numbers
will not be dynamic... will depend on how quickly we do the Capex or we defer
the Capex." No number reaffirmed.

**IF-18. PAT guidance refused twice while EBITDA and revenue were given
freely.** Q4 FY26 call, Rahul Kumar, PDF p.13: "Can you share your guidance for
the PAT for FY '27 and '28?" Akshay Pitti: "That would be a little difficult for
me to predict." Q1 FY27 call, Rahul Kumar, PDF p.16: "Can you just give us your
targets for EBITDA and PAT for next two years?" Answer gives EBITDA, turnover
and margin. No PAT.

### Group C. Segment narratives that moved

**IF-10. Data centre share was restated downward and the growth guide was
halved.**
- Q2 FY26 call, extra, Akshay Pitti, PDF p.5: "data centers, I think about 4% of
  total revenue for Q2... we are given to understand further 50% to 60% growth
  in volumes."
- Q3 FY26 call, Akshay Pitti, PDF p.4, on the same Q2: "revenue contribution
  increasing from 2.7% in the previous quarter to 3.7% in Q3 FY '26."
- Q3 FY26 call, Akshay Pitti, PDF p.6: "over the next 12 to 18 months, we should
  look at least a 25% to 30% growth in this segment."

The prior quarter is restated from about 4% to 2.7%, which converts a flat
quarter into "particularly encouraging momentum." The growth guide is roughly
halved in one quarter. Neither change is acknowledged. Fair caveat: the 4% was a
spoken approximation and 2.7% may be the audited deck figure. The growth-guide
halving carries no such caveat.

**IF-11. Data centre share went backwards on the full year.** 3.7% in Q3 FY26
(Q3 FY26 call, PDF p.4) against 3% for FY26 (Q4 FY26 call, PDF p.4).

**IF-12. Exports have been flat in rupees for five straight quarters while the
export narrative headlines every call. (CRITICAL)**
- Q2 FY26 call, extra, Akshay Pitti, PDF p.12: Q2 FY26 operating revenue
  INR477.72 Cr, "total exports say about INR135 crores, that's 28%."
- Q1 FY27 call, Mohit Jain, PDF p.7: "In absolute terms, exports was a bit flat.
  Last year we did about Rs. 137 crores, this year Rs. 139... On the other hand,
  domestic grew 23%."
So exports ran INR135 Cr to INR139 Cr a quarter from Q1 FY26 through Q1 FY27,
while quarterly revenue rose from INR457 Cr to INR529 Cr. Export share fell from
about 30% to about 26%.
The recovery date has been moved: Q3 FY26 call, Akshay Pitti, PDF p.5, "Q 4
again looks to be strong" (Q4 exports then degrew, Q4 FY26 call, Dhiral Shah,
PDF p.8); Q1 FY27 call, Akshay Pitti, PDF p.7, "Exports will again pick up in
Q3-Q4."
The metric was then substituted. Q1 FY27 call, Akshay Pitti, PDF p.7: "The
direct exports are remaining steady, registering nominal growth. What is a
bigger opportunity for us is the indirect exports." Indirect exports are not
disclosed, not measurable by any reader, and not falsifiable. The measurable
line stalled and an unverifiable one replaced it, while the Q1 FY27 opening
remarks build the entire macro case on China Plus One and Europe-to-India
sourcing (PDF p.3).
Peer contrast in the same window: RKFORGE Q1 FY27 call, Naresh Jalan, Jul-2026,
PDF p.9, "almost 20% plus growth in terms of exports"; SANSERA Q1 FY27 call,
B.R. Preetham, Aug-2026, PDF p.3, "we are seeing some traction in our
international business getting back to a normal growth trajectory."

**IF-30. Railway share falls while the named customer roster grows.** 31.9% in
Q3 FY26 (PDF p.4), 33% for FY26 (PDF p.4), 28% in Q1 FY27 (PDF p.5). Over the
same period Progress Rail is added and Siemens Mobility and Alstom are named
(Q4 FY26 call, PDF p.13). RKFORGE moved the other way: Q4 FY26 call, May-2026,
PDF p.4, "the share of business from railways grown to 7.5% of revenue in this
year against 4.6% a year ago."

### Group D. Capital allocation

**IF-13. A stated capital-discipline limit was reversed at a higher debt
level.** Q2 FY26 call, extra, Akshay Pitti, PDF p.15: "right now, the balance
sheet is slightly stressed because of this raw material situation... But given
the elevated debt level, I would not be comfortable going beyond this at all."
Six months later, with net debt higher (INR570 Cr against the INR550 Cr of Q3
FY26), the company announced a INR290 Cr greenfield (Q4 FY26 call, PDF p.4) and
then added an unapproved further plan. Q1 FY27 call, Akshay Pitti, PDF p.14: "a
facility in Bangalore, that would entail a Capex of roughly about Rs. 200-odd
crores. Then in terms of another equipment, about Rs. 200-odd crores." The stated
discomfort threshold is never revisited.

**IF-14. Four different answers to "where does growth after FY27 come from," in
four calls.** Q2 FY26 call, extra, PDF p.7 and p.12: inorganic acquisition in
North India, "that is where the growth will come from"; PDF p.15: forging, "a
INR250 crores investment." Q3 FY26 call, PDF p.8: forging is now "very
longer-term strategies." Q4 FY26 call, PDF p.4: Hyderabad greenfield casting.
Q1 FY27 call, PDF p.8 and p.15: Bangalore greenfield, and on adjacent business,
"I am more inclined to stay in my lane and do the business that we know well."

**IF-20. ROCE fell from about 19% to about 15% and management described a
self-reinforcing capex drag.** Q1 FY27 call, Pulkit Singhal, PDF p.14: "the
return on capital has fallen from 19% to almost 15%." Akshay Pitti, PDF p.15:
"we are always having that Capex, which is pulling the ROCE and before the
operating leverage comes from the Capex done, the other Capex kicks in." With
INR230 Cr of the INR290 Cr still to spend plus about INR400 Cr unapproved, the
loop management describes has no stated exit.

**IF-26. The greenfield margin and asset-turn assumptions are the weakest in the
peer set.** Q4 FY26 call, Akshay Pitti, PDF p.12: "we are estimating a 1.2x asset
turn with approximately a 25% to 28% EBITDA margin." Against peers: RKFORGE
May-2026 call, Lalit Khetan, PDF p.8, "Margins in the casting will be somewhere
around 15% to 16%"; RKFORGE Jul-2026 call, Lalit Khetan, PDF p.19, "asset turn
of 2.5" on net block; SANSERA Aug-2026 call, Hari Krishnan, PDF p.8, "the ADS
asset turns are very comfortably between 2 to 2.25." Pitti assumes the highest
margin in the group on the lowest asset turn in the group. Fair caveat: Pitti's
25% to 28% is for machined castings, which its own per-tonne figures support at
the top of a band (Q4 FY26 call, PDF p.16, "INR80,000 to INR1 lakh a ton"),
while RKFORGE's 15% to 16% covers a broader casting mix.

**IF-27. "More than doubling" overstates the casting capacity step.** Q4 FY26
call, PDF p.4 and p.5: capacity increase of 11,400 MT taking total casting
capacity "to 36,000 metric tons, more than doubling the current capacity
levels." The stated current bases are 24,000 t after debottlenecking (same call,
PDF p.6) and 18,600 t before it (Q3 FY26 call, PDF p.12). 36,000 over 18,600 is
1.94x. Minor, but it is a wording lean.

### Group E. Disclosure quality and governance

**IF-16. A reclassification question was denied and partly conceded in the same
answer, then dropped.** Q4 FY26 call, Nikhil Purohit, PDF p.11: "I was just
comparing FY25 full year numbers and noticed some changes in figures to other
expenses and employee expenses like has there been any reclassification here?"
Akshay Pitti: "No, there is no reclassification there. Let me just check on
that. I'll make a note of it. But as far as I know, there is no
reclassification. All we have done is we have started giving you the adjusted
EBITDA on the investor PPT slide, wherein the employee cost is slightly reduced
to the extent of the impact of ESOP cost." He then adds, same page: "we classify
the forex loss on contracts on our potential receivables in OCI as per the
standard. However, last year there was hardly any value to that number. Most of
it is only in Q4." Two presentation changes are described inside a denial that
there were any. Routing forex losses to OCI in the exact quarter the margin
missed is an earnings-quality item. The promised check-back never appears in Q1
FY27.

**IF-17. Every call carries one named executive. No CFO speaks.** Q3 FY26 call
PDF p.2, Q4 FY26 call PDF p.2 and Q1 FY27 call PDF p.2 each list a single
manager: "MR. AKSHAY PITTI - MANAGING DIRECTOR AND CHIEF EXECUTIVE OFFICER."
The opening remarks say senior management is present, but nobody else is named
and nobody else answers, across three calls. Peer contrast in the same window:
RKFORGE May-2026 PDF p.2 lists five, including "Mr. Lalit Khetan - Whole-Time
Director and Chief Financial Officer," who answers the accounting questions;
SANSERA May-2026 PDF p.2 lists five, including a CFO and two division CEOs;
VILAS May-2026 PDF p.2 lists the CMD plus a second director who takes the
product questions. For a company carrying INR698 Cr of borrowings (Q4 FY26 call,
PDF p.8), hedge accounting, an OCI classification choice, deferred tax and a
state subsidy accounting decision, single-voice disclosure is a governance flag.
It is also the direct cause of IF-16.

**IF-24. Q1 FY27 PAT growth is tax-aided and management said so.** Q1 FY27 call,
PDF p.5: adjusted PAT INR32 Cr against INR26 Cr, up 23%. Same call, Akshay
Pitti, PDF p.13: "Quarter 1, we have a lot of right of use assets for our lease
assets, and that has incurred the deferred tax, and therefore we have a lower
tax rate on the P&L for Quarter 1. But the full year tax rate should be closer
to 25%." The headline PAT beat carries a tax tailwind that will not repeat at
the same rate. No analyst normalised it.

**IF-21. The FY27 other-income hole is larger than the slippage flagged.** Q1
FY27 call, Akshay Pitti, PDF p.10: the INR400 Cr expansion "would have typically
yielded us somewhere around Rs. 40 crores a year," and PDF p.11, "this year we
would not be taking the incentive income." Rahul Kumar then supplies the missing
half, PDF p.11: "The last year we had some Rs. 20 crores in the Quarter 2, that
was basis the previous Capex which will not reoccur this year." So FY27 loses
about INR20 Cr of FY26 other income and gains none of the new INR40 Cr. Also
disclosed only on prompting, PDF p.11: "we are yet to receive roughly about
Rs. 70-odd crores from the government, which is expected to come in the next
9-12 months."

**IF-29. Customer concentration is never raised on any of the three calls,**
despite management naming a small set of large accounts repeatedly (Q3 FY26 call

**IF-25. The steel-shortage justification for the inventory build is
peer-contradicted, and the price risk on that inventory was never discussed.**
Pitti's stated reason, Q2 FY26 call, extra, Akshay Pitti, PDF p.8: "this is
purely to protect ourselves from lack of availability of materials... there's a
huge disruption in the availability of various grades of steel in India due to

Q3 FY26 call, PDF p.5: "the continued uncertainty around the availability of BIS

Against VILAS, the only electrical-steel peer:
- VILAS FY25 call, May-2025, PDF p.6. An analyst asks directly: "one of your
  peer competitors, they face certain challenges in procuring raw materials...
  we don't envisage any raw material shortage in the coming quarters?" Nilesh
  Patel: "We don't see any kind of raw material shortage. We didn't see in this
  entire financial year, even we don't see for the future also."
- VILAS H1 FY26 call, Nov-2025, opening remarks, PDF p.5: "The recent opening of
  a BIS of Chinese mill into the Indian market has brought a major shift,
  helping reduce import dependency, and improve local availability... Over the
  past few months, CRGO prices have declined by nearly 15-20% from their
  previous highs."
- VILAS FY26 call, May-2026, PDF p.24: CRGO now "INR195 right now" against
  INR270 to INR290 about 18 months earlier.
Pitti struck its peak INR500 Cr inventory at 31-Dec-2025, into a falling
electrical-steel market. No call, and no analyst, ever raises inventory
valuation risk on that position. Fair caveat, and it is a real one: VILAS buys
CRGO for transformers, Pitti buys mainly CRNGO for motors, and Pitti gives a
specific CRNGO deficit (Q2 FY26 call, extra, PDF p.13, 8 lakh t demand against
6 lakh t capacity). The peer evidence weakens the shortage narrative for the
electrical-steel complex. It does not disprove a CRNGO-specific constraint.

### Group G. The value-added mix thesis

**IF-15. The value-added realisation premium narrowed and nobody said so.**
- Q2 FY26 call, extra, Akshay Pitti, PDF p.10: "loose lamination and low
  value-added assemblies would be about INR170,000 per ton. High value-added
  assemblies would typically yield about INR250,000 a ton."
- Q4 FY26 call, Akshay Pitti, PDF p.8: "a loose lamination would be selling at
  approximately INR1,70,000 to INR1,80,000 a ton... A high value-added assembly
  would trade somewhere between INR2,20,000 to INR2,30,000 a ton."
The loose-lamination price held. The high-value-added price fell about 10%. The
premium narrowed from roughly 1.47x to roughly 1.29x in six months. This is the
price of the exact product the mix-shift thesis depends on, and the mix shift is
B05's priority-1 trigger.
