## 1. 🔴 RED FLAG — KMP "% increase in remuneration" disclosure (Annexure C) does not reconcile with the actual remuneration figures in Note 34

Note 34(b)(iii)(a), p.237, gives KMP remuneration for FY26 vs FY25:
- Mr. Jitendra B. Divgi (MD): 21.49 vs 12.38 → **+73.6%**
- Mr. Hirendra Divgi (ED): 10.74 vs 6.18 → **+73.8%**
- Mr. Dipak Vani (COO): 8.60 vs 4.92 → **+74.8%**
- Mr. Sudhir Mirjankar (CFO): 6.19 vs 3.23 → **+91.6%**

Annexure C, Part A (Section 197(12) disclosure, p.88) states the "% increase in the remuneration of each Director/CFO/Company Secretary" for the same five individuals in the same fiscal year as: MD **15%**, ED **15%**, COO **15%**, CFO **42%**.

These two statutory disclosures, covering the identical individuals and the identical fiscal year, are wildly inconsistent: the actual year-on-year change computable from Note 34's own rupee figures (74-92%) is 4-6x the percentage Annexure C states (15%/15%/15%/42%). A separate remuneration table in the Corporate Governance Report (Section 9, p.163) states FY26 managerial remuneration for MD = Rs 17.75 Mn (Fixed 10.61 + Variable 7.14) and ED = Rs 8.69 Mn (Fixed 5.31 + Variable 3.38) — figures that are themselves *lower* than Note 34's 21.49/10.74, and consistent with the Annexure C Part B (p.89) "top 10 employees" table, which shows Mr. Jitendra Divgi's FY26 gross salary as Rs 1,77,50,898 (17.75 Mn), matching the Corporate Governance table but not Note 34.

So the annual report carries **three different FY26 remuneration figures for the Managing Director** (Note 34: 21.49 Mn; Corporate Governance Section 9 / Annexure C Part B: 17.75 Mn) and **two different stated YoY growth rates** (Note 34-derived: 73.6%; Annexure C Part A stated: 15%). A plausible benign explanation is scope: Note 34 follows Ind AS 24 (all KMP compensation, including retirement-benefit accruals), while the Corporate Governance table and Annexure C Part A follow the Companies Act Section 197 definition of "remuneration" (which by statutory proviso excludes certain retirement benefits). That would explain the *level* difference but does not obviously explain a 15% vs 74% *growth-rate* difference of this magnitude for the same fixed-plus-variable cash components. This is a genuine, well-anchored disclosure-integrity gap, not merely a rounding issue, and it sits directly on top of the governance concern Pass 1 already raised (KMP remuneration escalation, special incentive resolutions items 6-7 that themselves acknowledge breaching Section 197 limits). It should be put to management directly: which of the two YoY percentages is correct, and on what base is each calculated.

*Why it matters: this is the single most consequential item that a note-by-note read of the Notes alone (Pass 1's brief) could not surface, because it only appears when Note 34 is set against Annexure C. It materially changes how much weight the "KMP pay is outpacing revenue" finding should carry — the true increase could be as high as ~92% (CFO) rather than the 15-42% the statutory remuneration-ratio disclosure states.*

## 2. 🟡 WATCH — AOC-2 (Section 188 disclosure) understates the actual related-party rent income from Divgi Transmission Systems & Technologies Pvt Ltd by roughly 19x

Annexure A (Form AOC-2), p.84, states the "salient terms" of the Company's lease of land & building and lease of machinery **to** Divgi Transmission Systems & Technologies Pvt Ltd as "Annual Lease of ₹12 Lakhs" for each — i.e. ₹24 Lakhs (₹2.4 Mn) per year in aggregate.

Note 34(b)(iv), p.237, records the Company's actual rent income received from Divgi Transmission Systems & Technologies Pvt Ltd as **Rs 4.68 Mn**, static across both FY25 and FY26 — roughly **19.5x** the amount stated in the AOC-2 salient-terms disclosure. This is the same related-party leasing arrangement Pass 1 already flagged qualitatively (the "leasing loop": company leases equipment out, buys machining services back); Pass 2 adds the quantitative finding that the statutory Section 188 contract-terms disclosure and the audited note for the identical arrangement do not tie together by an order of magnitude. Either the AOC-2 figure is a stale reference rate from a much earlier point in the 10-year lease term, or the note materially exceeds the disclosed contractual terms.

*Why it matters: Section 188/AOC-2 exists precisely so shareholders can verify that related-party contract terms are arm's length. A ~19x gap between the disclosed rate and the booked amount undermines that verification for this specific relationship, on top of the already-flagged Tejal Transmission omission — a second data point suggesting the related-party disclosure package needs a line-by-line reconciliation before it can be relied on.*

## 3. 🟡 WATCH — A new related-party Leave & License Agreement (October 2025) explains the FY26 rent-expense increase to the holding company, and was not previously disclosed as a new arrangement in the Notes

Annexure A (AOC-2), p.84, discloses for Divgi Holdings Pvt Ltd (holding company) a **third** arrangement in addition to the land lease and the building lease already known: "Leave and License Agreement for use of office premises w.e.f October 01, 2025" at Rs 1.21 Lakhs/month.

This reconciles exactly with the YoY movement Pass 1 flagged only as "+15.8%" without identifying its driver: rent expense to Divgi Holdings rose from Rs 4.62 Mn (FY25, matching the building lease alone at Rs 3.85 Lakhs/month × 12) to Rs 5.35 Mn (FY26). The increase of Rs 0.73 Mn is almost exactly six months (Oct 2025-Mar 2026) of the new Rs 1.21 Lakh/month license fee (6 × 1.21 Lakh = Rs 7.26 Lakh = Rs 0.726 Mn). Note 34 itself (p.237) does not name this new agreement or its date; it is only visible by cross-referencing the AOC-2 annexure. This is a new related-party contract entered into during the year under review that increases the Company's recurring payments to its own promoter-controlled holding company for office space, on top of the existing building lease.

*Why it matters: a new promoter-related recurring payment stream initiated mid-year, not separately called out in the audited related-party note itself, is a disclosure-transparency point worth a direct question, independent of whether the amount itself (₹0.73 Mn for the stub period) is material in isolation.*

## 4. 🟡 WATCH — Raw material inventory reconciliation (Note 8 vs Note 27) reveals the entire non-moving inventory provision sits against raw materials, and the coverage ratio has fallen further than Pass 1's blended calculation showed

Note 8 (p.224) shows Raw Materials at Rs 312.99 Mn (FY26) and Rs 186.23 Mn (FY25), gross, with a single aggregate "Less: Provision for non-moving inventory" of Rs 37.55 Mn (FY26) / Rs 30.55 Mn (FY25) deducted from *total* net inventory. Note 27 (p.232), reconciling "Cost of raw material and components consumed," shows raw material closing balances of only Rs 275.44 Mn (FY26) and Rs 155.67 Mn (FY25) — each exactly Rs 37.55 Mn / Rs 30.55 Mn lower than Note 8's gross figures. This is not a rounding artefact: the gap in both years equals the non-moving provision to the rupee.

This tells us something Note 8 alone does not state directly: **the entire non-moving/obsolescence provision is attributed to raw materials**, none of it to work-in-progress or finished goods. Recomputed on that basis, the coverage ratio (provision ÷ raw material gross) is 37.55/312.99 = **12.0%** in FY26, down from 30.55/186.23 = **16.4%** in FY25 — a materially larger decline in obsolescence coverage than the blended figure Pass 1 calculated across total inventory (6.0% → 7.3%, using the wrong denominator direction; the true raw-material-specific ratio is falling, not rising, and from a much higher base).

*Why it matters: obsolescence risk is concentrated entirely in raw materials (consistent with the +68.1% raw material build already flagged), and the coverage ratio against that specific base is declining faster than Pass 1's total-inventory calculation implied — a sharper margin-risk signal than first read.*

## 5. 🟡 WATCH — Salaries and benefits payable (Note 21) grew far faster than the employee expense line or headcount it relates to

Note 21 (p.231), "Salaries and benefits payable," rose from Rs 59.55 Mn (FY25) to Rs 126.51 Mn (FY26) — **+112.5%**. By contrast, total Employee Benefits Expense (Note 29, p.233) grew only 65.4% (Rs 251.14 Mn → Rs 415.41 Mn) and headcount grew only 7.3% (259 → 278 permanent employees per BRSR A.4). As a proportion of the annual expense it funds, the payable balance rose from 23.7% (≈2.8 months) to 30.5% (≈3.7 months) of a full year's employee cost.

This is a new, previously un-surfaced divergence: the liability for unpaid compensation is lengthening faster than either the expense that generates it or the workforce that earns it. A plausible explanation is a year-end bonus/incentive accrual not yet paid out (consistent with the KMP remuneration-payable increase already flagged in Pass 1, +36%, but this payable line is company-wide and moves at a materially different rate than the KMP-specific one), but the Notes do not explain the driver.

*Why it matters: an accrued-but-unpaid compensation base growing 1.5x faster than the expense itself is a working-capital and earnings-quality data point that deserves a direct question, particularly given the pending special-incentive resolutions for the MD and ED already flagged as a governance item.*

## 6. 🟡 WATCH — Net foreign exchange position turned negative in the year the Company's central narrative is exports/internationalisation

Annexure D to the Board's Report (p.90), a Companies Act Section 134(3)(m) disclosure required to sit alongside the Notes, states: "Foreign exchange earnings: ₹66.90 crore; Foreign exchange Outgo: ₹67.91 crore." Foreign exchange OUTGO exceeds foreign exchange EARNINGS by ≈Rs 1.01 crore (≈Rs 10.1 Mn) for FY26 — a net foreign-currency cash outflow for the year in which the Company frames exports (+619% YoY, per the MD's letter) and internationalisation as the defining achievement of the year.

This is consistent with, and helps explain, the unhedged net USD payable position Pass 1 already flagged (Note 41, net USD exposure swinging to a Rs 156.49 Mn liability) — the outgo is plausibly driven by imported capital equipment for the capex ramp (CWIP up 11.8x) rather than by trade in finished goods, so it is not necessarily contradictory to the growth story, but the annual report's own big-number framing of internationalisation is not matched by the net cash foreign-exchange position for the year, and none of the value-enhancement or business-driver narrative sections mention this net negative position.

*Why it matters: it is a quantitative counterpoint to the "internationalisation success" framing that runs through the whole annual report, visible only by cross-referencing a mandatory Companies Act annexure against the FX-exposure note.*

## 7. 🟡 WATCH — New, first-time unhedged CNY exposure and a first-time foreign-currency (USD) bank balance appear in FY26, both unremarked in the Notes

Note 41 (p.241-242), the foreign-currency exposure table, shows a **CNY** trade payable exposure of 0.36 Mn CNY (Rs 4.91 Mn) in FY26 against **nil** in FY25, and a **USD bank balance** of USD 0.63 Mn (Rs 59.09 Mn) in FY26 against **nil** in FY25. Neither new exposure is called out in the note's narrative text, which only discusses aggregate sensitivity.

These are new data points that corroborate (for the first time in balance-sheet terms) two qualitative claims made elsewhere in the annual report: the MD's letter's reference to "coordinating across a broad international ecosystem involving China, Taiwan, Europe and the United States" for the automatic-transmission opportunity (new CNY payable, consistent with Chinese sourcing/collaboration beginning), and the new US subsidiary (first-ever USD bank balance). Useful confirming evidence, but also two new unhedged risk vectors layered on top of the already-flagged unhedged USD exposure, with no hedging policy disclosed for either.

*Why it matters: the FX risk footprint is diversifying into more currencies, not just growing in USD, and remains entirely unhedged across all of them.*

## 8. 🟡 WATCH — Possible internal unit inconsistency in the BRSR's stated R&D expenditure (Annexure E), which if resolved the other way implies R&D spend fell, not rose, in FY26

The "Future-ready R&D" business-driver box (p.42 of the annual report) states: "Sustained investments in research and development have laid the foundation for next-generation mobility solutions, with an R&D expenditure of **₹117.94 million** in FY 2025-26." The BRSR (Annexure E), Principle 2 essential indicator 1 (p.105), reports the same number in a table alongside FY25 and the Capex line: "R&D | **117.94 crore** | 136.50 crore" and "Capex | 443.16 | 265.03." The Capex row (443.16) is unambiguously in Rs Million — it matches the cash-flow statement's "Payments for acquisition of property, plant and equipment... 443.16" exactly — yet the R&D row in the very same table is labelled "crore," i.e. 100x larger, and is inconsistent with the Business Driver page's own explicit "million" statement for the identical figure.

An R&D spend of Rs 117.94 *crore* (≈Rs 1,179 Mn) would be roughly one-third of FY26 total revenue (Rs 3,528.88 Mn) against an EBITDA margin of only 24.6% — arithmetically implausible and not supported anywhere else in the financial statements (Note 4(e) shows total intangible-assets-under-development additions of only Rs 35.02 Mn for the year; Note 32A has no separate R&D expense line of that magnitude). The "million" reading (Rs 11.79 crore) is by far the more plausible figure and is consistent with the Business Driver page.

If "million" is correct throughout, the BRSR's own FY25 comparative of "136.50 crore" should also read "136.50 million" — meaning R&D expenditure **declined** from Rs 13.65 crore (FY25) to Rs 11.79 crore (FY26), a **-13.6%** fall, in the same year revenue grew 61.2% and the annual report devotes an entire "Business Driver: Research and Development" section (p.65) to touting DHT technology, EV transmission leadership and expanded engineering capability. This would directly contradict the R&D-led-innovation narrative with the one number that quantifies it in rupee terms.

*Why it matters: this is not a Note per se (it sits in the BRSR annexure), but it is the same style of cross-document reconciliation this pass exists to perform, and the two readings lead to opposite conclusions — R&D intensity either roughly flat/rising (if the "crore" label were somehow correct, which the arithmetic rules out) or falling 13.6% (if "million" is correct, which the arithmetic and the Business Driver page both support). This needs a direct management clarification before any R&D-intensity claim from this annual report is used downstream.*

## 9. 🟢 Minor — Note 13(e) "shareholders holding more than 5%" table includes an entity below the stated 5% threshold

Note 13(e), p.227, is captioned "Details of shareholders holding more than 5% of shares in the Company" but lists three names, one of which — NRJN Family Trust — holds only 3.00% (FY26) / 3.02% (FY25), below the 5% threshold the table's own caption states. This is a labelling/completeness inconsistency rather than a numerical error (the percentages themselves look internally consistent with the share counts given). Given the annual report has already been shown (Note 5/Tejal Transmission, Pass 1) to have at least one gap in related-party completeness, a family trust holding ~3% sitting inside a ">5%" disclosure table, with no stated relationship to the promoter group, is worth a direct question on beneficial ownership — not because the numbers are wrong, but because the pattern of "family"-named entities appearing adjacent to promoter and institutional holders without a stated relationship has already proven, once, to hide a related-party link.
