## 3. THE CATALYST STORY IN PLAIN WORDS

**What could keep the loan book growing.** Gold is doing the work. The gold
share of the book went from 31% to 38% in nine months as prices rose, because
a fixed loan to value ratio against dearer gold lends more rupees per gram
without finding a single new customer (ICRA p.1). The capital position genuinely
helps too: a capital adequacy ratio of 18.92% after the rights issue leaves room
to lend (results Q4 FY26 p.8). What would confirm it is advances growth holding
above 20% while the gold share stops climbing, which would mean the rest of the
book is working. What kills it is a gold price correction, which would take
away the growth, the margin and the capital relief at the same time, because
all three lean on the same thing. Two other limits are already visible: the
bank now lends out 80% of its deposits against 76% a year ago, and ICRA says
scaling up further depends on raising capital again, which nobody has
committed to.

**What could hold the margin.** The gold mix lifts the yield, and deposit costs
are turning favourable as the rate cycle repricing works through, which every
peer describes on their calls. The cost side is where the real money is: nine
months at 81.62% and a fourth quarter at 55.66% means either the bank found
permanent operating leverage in one quarter, or something one off landed in
that quarter. What confirms it is two consecutive FY27 quarters below 75%.
What kills it is a reversion to the eighties, which would say the fourth
quarter was an event. Peers running at 44% to 65% show how much room exists if
it is real, and how far behind the bank still is if it is not.

**What could fix asset quality.** Two documented things. The bank tripled
provisions to Rs 78.06 Cr and rebuilt coverage excluding write offs from
53.63% in December to 73.67% in March, which is a deliberate clean up paid for
by the better operating profit. And the shift into gold is a shift into
secured lending, which structurally lowers the risk of the book. What confirms
it is coverage staying above 70% while net NPA stays under 0.75%. What kills it
is the coverage ratio sliding back under 53.63% while net NPA climbs back over
1.11%, the pattern that would prove the headline improvement was write off
flattered. Two things are missing rather than negative: the bank never
discusses the written off pool anywhere in its own commentary, and every peer
is provisioning for the expected credit loss regime starting April 2027 while
Dhanlaxmi has said nothing about it.

**What could lift returns.** Only two levers exist and both are already named:
costs and margin. There is no third. A bank at 0.525% return on assets gets to
1% by cutting the cost to income ratio toward the peer band, and nothing else
moves the needle at this scale. What confirms it is return on assets holding
above 0.6% for a full year without a capital raise. What kills it is another
equity raise before earnings catch up, which mechanically resets return on
equity exactly as it did in FY25.
