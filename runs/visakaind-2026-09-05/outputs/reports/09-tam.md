## SECTION 1: MARKET DEFINITION

### 1A. Precise boundaries, per line

**Line 1 — Cement roofing sheets (chrysotile corrugated).**
Product scope: chrysotile-fibre corrugated roofing sheets and roofing
accessories for pitched-roof structural covering. Geographic scope:
India, pan-national but manufactured from 7 plants across AP, TN,
Karnataka, WB, Odisha, UP and Maharashtra (AR FY26 p.50), each serving a
roughly 500km logistics radius (AR FY26 p.63). Customer scope: rural and
semi-urban housing, agricultural/industrial sheds; explicitly NOT the
urban/premium roofing segment (metal standing-seam, tile, membrane),
which the company does not compete in. Channel scope: dealer/stockist
network (4,974 dealers FY26, down from 5,246 FY25, AR FY26 p.106).
Price segment: "among the lowest-cost roofing solutions available" (AR
FY26 p.50) — the affordability tier, by design. Explicit exclusion: metal
(GI/colour-coated) roofing sheets, clay tiles, membrane roofing, and any
non-cement roofing material are NOT part of this pond even though they
compete for the same roof — they are substitutes, priced and sized
separately in Section 4B, not summed into TAM.

**Line 2 — Fibre cement boards & panels (V-Next).**
Product scope: Hatschek-process, autoclaved fibre cement boards and
panels (V-Board, V-Premium, V-Designer, V-Plank, V-Panel, V-Infill) for
walls, ceilings, partitions, facades, cladding and modular/prefab
construction. Geographic scope: pan-India, 5 plants (Telangana,
Maharashtra, Haryana, TN, WB, AR FY26 p.53), plus exports (15% of segment
revenue FY26, down from 17% FY25). Customer scope: real-estate
developers, architects/contractors, institutional/industrial clients
(named: Google, Amazon, Tata Steel, NTPC, L&T, AR FY26 p.54) — a
higher-value, organised-construction customer base than roofing.
Explicit exclusion: gypsum board, plywood, MDF, calcium-silicate board
and brick/plaster partition systems are substitutes (Section 4B), not
summed into this TAM.

**Line 3 — Synthetic yarn (Wonder Yarn).**
Product scope: man-made-fibre (chiefly polyester-blend) SPUN yarn made
via twin air-jet spinning at the Nagpur plant (45 machines, 3,040
spinning positions, AR FY26 p.60) — melange, high-twist and blended
variants. This is a textiles-sector line, explicitly NOT a
building-materials line (B04 flag carried forward). Important scope
distinction found in web search during this stage: air-jet SPUN yarn
(Visaka's product, made from staple fibre) is a technically different
product from continuous-filament yarn (PFY) — most third-party "India
polyester yarn market" figures found in this stage size PFY, not
air-jet-spun blended yarn, and are therefore SCOPE-MISMATCHED to
Visaka's actual product (flagged below). Customer scope: "quality-
conscious fabric manufacturers" (AR FY26 p.55) — named: Donear, Siyaram
Silks, Raymonds, Grasim Bhivani, D'Decor, Arvind Mills (AR FY26 p.59) —
a specialty/branded-fabric customer base, not commodity yarn buyers.

**Line 4 — ATUM integrated solar roof (BIPV).**
Product scope: a patented (India/US/South Africa; China/Europe pending)
roof structure that is itself the solar-generating surface (Building-
Integrated Photovoltaics, BIPV), sold via Visaka (product) and two
subsidiaries, Visaka Green (EPC/turnkey) and Atum Life (retail
experience stores). Geographic scope: domestic plus exports to "Africa
and Asia" (AR FY26 p.56). Customer scope: industrial/warehouse roofing,
commercial/institutional buildings, residential/villas, agricultural
structures (AR FY26 p.57). Critical boundary condition carried from
B01/B04/B07: ATUM revenue has never been separately disclosed in 8 years
since 2018 launch, across 2 ARs and 3 transcripts read in this pipeline.
This line's TAM can be sized directionally (Section 2D); its SAM/SOM
cannot be computed from evidence in this corpus and is held at zero in
all blended numeric fields, per the operator's explicit instruction.

## SECTION 2: TAM ESTIMATION, MULTIPLE METHODS

### LINE 1 — CEMENT ROOFING SHEETS

**Method 1 — Top-down (third-party research).**
ICRA published a research note, "Asbestos-based Roofing Sheet Market"
(Jun-2024, title and figures found via web search; the icra.in source
page itself returned EGRESS_BLOCKED on direct fetch — see SEARCH LOG,
cited here by title/date from the search snippet only, unverified at
source): the asbestos fibre-cement (FC) sheets market grew at a 5-year
CAGR of ~8.5% to **₹5,200 crore during FY2020-FY2024(E)**. Same note:
top-5 companies hold 80-82% revenue share (oligopoly); asbestos fibre
price rose at 9.6% CAGR (FY2018-24) against realisation growth of only
3.1% CAGR over the same period — i.e., input costs structurally outran
pricing power, consistent with B04's "weak pricing power" read.
STALE flag: this note is dated Jun-2024, >2 years before the run date;
treated as a FY2024(E) anchor, rolled forward below.

**Method 2 — Bottom-up (installed capacity / market-share back-solve).**
Visaka's own claimed pan-India market share is 18%, "number two" (AR
FY26 p.50), a figure repeated unchanged since the Aug-2023 investor
deck (p.5-6: "market share of 18%... We are number two"). Visaka's FY26
roofing segment revenue is ₹886cr (AR FY26 p.51). Back-solving:
  ₹886cr ÷ 0.18 = **₹4,922cr** implied FY26 industry size.
This is remarkably close to Method 1's ICRA figure rolled forward two
years at the historical 8.5% CAGR (₹5,200cr × 1.085² ≈ ₹6,123cr — see
note below on why the rolled-forward figure is NOT used) or, more
appropriately, at the current, more credible go-forward growth rate of
2-3% cited directly by a competitor (see Method 3): ₹5,200cr × 1.025²
≈ **₹5,463cr**.

**Method 2b — Capacity/import cross-check (directional only).**
Visaka's FY26 installed capacity is 8,48,000 MT (AR FY26 p.51). At an
18% share, implied industry installed capacity ≈ 8,48,000 ÷ 0.18 =
**47.1 lakh MT**. Independently, India's chrysotile asbestos fibre
imports (100% import-dependent — domestic mining is banned) were 343.8
Kt in 2024 and annualise to ~383 Kt for 2025 (H1-2025: 191.48 Kt,
Russia 44.9%/Brazil 38.2%/Kazakhstan 16.4% of 2024 imports — trade-data
web search, not a primary DGCI&S fetch). If chrysotile fibre content is
assumed at ~10% of finished-sheet weight (a typical AC-sheet composition
ratio; NOT independently sourced within this corpus — flagged as an
assumption, used for direction only), implied total finished output ≈
343.8Kt ÷ 0.10 = **34.4 lakh MT** for 2024 — same order of magnitude as
the capacity-based 47.1 lakh MT (installed capacity always exceeds
actual output; at ~85-92% industry utilisation, ~40-43 lakh MT of actual
output would be implied, bridging most of the gap to the 34.4 lakh MT
fibre-derived figure). This method corroborates the scale of Methods 1-2
but is not precise enough to use as a standalone anchor.

**Method 3 — Peer revenue aggregation.**
DEGRADED. None of the three named peers (BirlaNu/HIL, Everest
Industries, Ramco Industries) disclose a roofing-only segment revenue
line in the screener-tier CSVs available in this corpus — all three
report only consolidated multi-segment totals (BirlaNu ₹2,426.53cr,
Everest ₹1,354.24cr, Ramco ₹1,443.50cr, all FY26). A clean bottom-up
peer sum for roofing alone is **NOT FOUND** in this corpus. ICRA's
"top-5 hold 80-82%" statistic is the best available third-party
concentration cross-check (used in Section 3A, SAM filter).

### Method 5 — Global/per-capita benchmark (macro context, all lines)

India's building materials market (USD 44.4bn, 2025, AR p.69) against a
global construction materials market of USD 1.46tn (2026, AR p.68):
India is ~3.0% of the global market. Using ~1.45bn current Indian
population (general external reference, not from this corpus) against
a global population of ~8.2bn (general external reference): India
building-materials spend per capita ≈ $44.4bn / 1.45bn ≈ **$30.6/head**
vs a global average of ≈ $1.46tn / 8.2bn ≈ **$178/head**. India sits at
roughly 17% of the global per-capita spend level despite holding ~17.7%
of global population — i.e., India's AGGREGATE share of the global
market (3.0%) is far below its POPULATION share (17.7%), the classic
underpenetration signature. Illustrative scenario only (not a
forecast): if India's per-capita spend closed halfway to the global
average by 2036 (population then ~1.52bn, AR p.69), implied India
building-materials market ≈ $104/head × 1.52bn ≈ **$158bn**, ~3.6x
today's $44.4bn. This is macro context for Section 4A; it is far too
broad to use as Visaka's TAM (see Section 1A boundaries) and is not
carried into the numeric blended totals.

## SECTION 3: SAM & SOM

### 3A. SAM — five filters applied per line

**Roofing.** Product fit: 100% (exact product match, TAM already scoped
to chrysotile cement sheets). Geography: Visaka explicitly operates
across 7 states with pan-India distribution claims; no named excluded
region — 0% cut. Channel: 4,974-dealer network plus institutional
accounts; no named channel exclusion — 0% cut. Customer: TAM is already
scoped to the rural/semi-urban affordability tier Visaka serves — 0%
further cut. Capability/structure: ICRA's own concentration statistic
(top-5 hold 80-82% of the organised market) is applied here as the one
sourced, defensible filter — the remaining 18-20% is fragmented among
small/regional/unorganised players whose hyper-local relationships a
large organised national player like Visaka cannot fully displace.
  SAM_roofing = TAM × 0.82 → conservative ₹4,900cr × 0.82 = **₹4,018cr**;
  realistic ₹5,400cr × 0.82 = **₹4,428cr**.

**Boards.** Same four filters at 0% cut (pan-India claimed presence, AR
FY26 p.53: "Presence Pan-India"; no named channel/customer exclusion;
product fit 100% within the already-scoped TAM). No independent
third-party organised/unorganised concentration statistic for boards
was found in this corpus or via web search (unlike roofing's ICRA
figure) — applying an arbitrary discount here without a source would
violate house rules, so **SAM_boards = TAM_boards** = conservative
₹1,500cr / realistic ₹1,750cr. This asymmetry (discount applied to
roofing, not to boards) is intentional: rigor is applied where sourced,
not fabricated where unsourced.

**Yarn.** SAM = TAM = ₹251cr (current-revenue floor, unchanged from
Section 2 — no market-level data exists to filter).

**ATUM.** SAM = NOT COMPUTABLE (₹0 in blended fields).

**Blended SAM**: conservative = 4,018 + 1,500 + 251 = **₹5,769cr**;
realistic = 4,428 + 1,750 + 251 = **₹6,429cr**.
**SAM as % of TAM (realistic basis)**: 6,429 / 7,401 = **86.9%** (blended;
this reflects that Visaka is not meaningfully geography/channel-
restricted within its own already-narrowly-scoped product categories —
the binding constraint on capture is competitive share and, for boards,
capacity, not addressability; tested next).

share-gain band; flat share is the management-stated intent.

  ₹5,010cr → SOM_5yr = 20.5% × 5,010 = **₹1,027cr**.
  Implied revenue CAGR: yr3 = (954/886)^(1/3) - 1 = **2.5%**; yr5 =
  (1,027/886)^(1/5) - 1 = **3.0%**.

against the ₹1,675.59cr FY26 standalone revenue anchor:
  yr3: (2,069 / 1,675.59)^(1/3) - 1 = **7.3%**
  yr5: (2,441 / 1,675.59)^(1/5) - 1 = **7.8%**

This blended figure sits between B07's two capex-embedded-growth
scenarios (2.5% anchored/contracted-capex basis; 66.1% narrative/
uncommitted-capex basis) — which makes sense: it blends near-flat
roofing (53% of revenue, capacity-sufficient, management-capped
ambition) with double-digit boards growth (30% of revenue, capacity-
constrained, see 3C) using market-share-gain logic rather than pure
capex-turnover logic. **This is the central, load-bearing finding of
this stage: a rigorously-triangulated, bottom-up TAM/SAM/SOM exercise
finds a realistic ~7-8% blended revenue CAGR ceiling for Visaka's four
product lines combined — far below the strategy's 25% CAGR hurdle.**
Any thesis clearing that hurdle must rest on quality-ladder re-rating
(Section 1B destination-PE math, outside this stage's scope) or on
ATUM converting from undisclosed optionality into real revenue — not
on organic, TAM-driven volume/share growth in the two disclosed
building-materials lines.

### 3C. Capacity cross-check (against B07's capex-embedded-growth figure)

**Roofing — SUFFICIENT.** Installed capacity 8,48,000 MT at 92%
utilisation FY26 (AR FY26 p.51) leaves ~8% headroom (≈68,000 MT) to
reach full utilisation — nameplate-equivalent ceiling ≈ 9,21,739 MT.
The SOM_3yr trajectory implies only ~2.4% cumulative volume-equivalent
growth over 3 years, comfortably inside this headroom with zero new
capex. No gap.

**Boards — GAP, and the SOM is the optimistic side.** Installed
capacity 3,55,750 MT at 86% utilisation FY26 (AR FY26 p.55) implies
actual output ≈ 3,05,945 MT; headroom to 100% utilisation ≈ 49,805 MT.
At Visaka's own derived realisation (~₹16,500/tonne, B04 unit-
economics line), that headroom is worth ≈ 49,805 × ₹16,500 = **₹82cr**
of maximum incremental revenue from EXISTING capacity alone (a
realistic ceiling is somewhat lower still, since no plant truly runs at
100%). Existing capacity therefore supports revenue up to roughly
₹506cr + ₹82cr ≈ **₹588cr** at best.
  SOM_3yr_boards (₹784cr) exceeds this ceiling by **≈₹196cr**.
  SOM_5yr_boards (₹1,046cr) exceeds this ceiling by **≈₹458cr**.
Cross-referencing B07 Section 2C: the only capex that clears the
📄(documented/contracted) evidence bar company-wide is ₹16.87cr (Note
39, FY26 year-end), implying just ₹41.5cr of incremental revenue at
the historical 2.462x fixed-asset turnover — nowhere near enough to
close a ₹196-458cr boards-specific gap, even under the generous
assumption that 100% of that ₹16.87cr is earmarked for boards (which
B07 states cannot be confirmed; the note is not broken down by
division). The narrative ₹450cr-by-2030 capex plan (B07 Scenario 2:
₹1,108cr implied incremental revenue) COULD close this gap and more —
but it is not a committed, board-approved, location-specific project;
it is a repeated MD&A statement with no matching Reg 30 filing or
broken-down capital-commitment line found in this corpus, and it sits
against a Grade-D promise-delivery record (Midnapore ran 31% over
budget and 3-6 months late; the ₹1,000cr-Vnext-by-2030 target has been
reiterated since Aug-2022 without a matching capex trail; B05/B07).
**Verdict: the SOM trajectory for boards is the optimistic side of this
comparison. It is achievable only if the narrative capex plan converts
to committed, funded, on-schedule capacity — which has not yet
happened.**

**Yarn — insufficient data to fully verify, but plausible.** No FY26
utilisation % is disclosed (only a single Q1 FY24 figure of 88% exists
in this corpus); installed capacity (45 machines, 3,040 positions) has
been flat FY23-FY26. The modest 5%/yr assumed growth is small enough
that it plausibly fits within normal utilisation/realisation
variation without new capacity, but this cannot be rigorously verified
from the capacity data available.

**ATUM — not applicable** (no revenue base, no disclosed capacity
line specific to ATUM; the product reportedly shares fibre-cement
board capacity per some AR framing, but this is not quantified).

### 4C. Market structure

**Roofing**: Oligopoly. ICRA: top-5 companies hold 80-82% revenue share.
Visaka is #2 at 18% (self-claimed, consistent across 2023-2026
disclosures). 100% import-dependent on chrysotile fibre (mining banned
domestically). Overcapacity flagged industry-wide by BirlaNu (Q2 FY26
call). Not consolidating further in any evidenced way (already
oligopolistic); price competition dominates over differentiation
(commodity-adjacent, per B04's own classification).

**Boards**: Formalising, not yet concentrated. Visaka self-claims
"largest player"/33% share and domestic rank #1; NO independent

the AR's own words as shifting "from unorganised to organised,
quality-certified materials" (p.54) — i.e., still absorbing
unorganised share, a different (earlier-stage, less concentrated)
structure than roofing. Growing 10-14% (BirlaNu), with at least two
large diversified players (Visaka, BirlaNu) actively expanding
capacity — competitive intensity is likely to rise as new capacity
comes online across the industry, not just at Visaka.

**Yarn**: Structure effectively UNKNOWN from evidence in this corpus.
Visaka's own "80%" share claim is almost certainly scoped to a
self-defined niche too narrow to characterise the true competitive

**ATUM**: Self-claimed "limited direct competition... high entry
barriers" (AR p.57) — an unverified, self-assessed claim; no named
competitor, no independent BIPV-India competitor count found via web

## SECTION 5: SUMMARY & RUNWAY

### 5A. Funnel diagram (₹Cr, realistic scenario unless noted)

*Blended excludes ATUM's directional pond from the sum, per Section 1/2.

### 5B. Runway assessment

- Revenue headroom = SAM ÷ current revenue = 6,429 ÷ 1,675.59 = **3.84x**.
- Blended TAM growth (revenue-weighted: roofing 53.9% of segment revenue
  @ 2.5%, boards 30.8% @ 12.0%, yarn 15.3% @ 5.0% assumed) = 0.539×2.5 +
  0.308×12.0 + 0.153×5.0 = **5.8%**.
- Company CAGR vs TAM: Visaka's own FY26 standalone revenue growth was
  8.9% (AR FY26 p.28/65), above the blended TAM growth of 5.8% — the
  company is net gaining share overall, but this is driven entirely by
  boards outgrowing its market (19% vs 10-14%); roofing merely tracks
  (or slightly trails) its own near-flat market.
- Years to saturate SAM: mechanically, at a ~7-8% blended growth pace
  (Section 3B), ln(6,429/1,675.59)/ln(1.075) ≈ 18.8 years. This number
  is presented with an explicit caveat: it is not a meaningful "runway"
  in the usual growth-stock sense, because roofing's SAM sits inside an
  entrenched 5-player oligopoly (ICRA) that Visaka will structurally
  never fully displace, and boards' SAM growth (12%/yr) itself erodes
  the "saturation" concept before Visaka could ever approach full
  capture. The honest reading is that SAM headroom is not the binding
  constraint here — competitive share ceilings (roofing) and capacity
  (boards, Section 3C) are.

### 5C. Runway classification: **MODERATE**

Explicit thresholds used (not given verbatim in the framework, stated
here for transparency): MASSIVE = headroom >8x AND TAM growth >15%;
STRONG = 5-8x AND 10-15%; GOOD = 3-5x AND 7-10%; MODERATE = 2-3x OR
4-7% on either dimension, or structurally capped; LIMITED = <2x OR
<4% growth. Visaka's ~3.84x headroom sits at the low end of the GOOD
band, but blended TAM growth (5.8%) sits in the MODERATE band, and two
structural facts pull the overall call down further: (i) the largest
revenue line (roofing, 53.9%) sits in a management-acknowledged,
explicitly NOT-growth-managed, oligopolistic market; (ii) the one line
with STRONG standalone characteristics (boards: 10-14% market growth,
real pricing power, demonstrated share gain) is capacity-constrained
per the 3C gap. Net: **MODERATE**, not GOOD or STRONG.

### 5D. SAM expansion levers actually being pursued

- **Boards capacity expansion** (₹450cr "by 2030" narrative, AR p.29/36):
  actively narrated by management, but this is a CAPACITY/capture lever,
  not a genuine SAM-expansion lever — it does not grow the underlying
  boards demand pool (already sized independently in Section 2), it
  only raises the ceiling on how much of the existing SAM Visaka could
  physically capture (already tested in 3C, where it remains uncommitted
  beyond ₹16.87cr).
- **Non-asbestos mix shift** (44%→47% of revenue, FY23-FY26, AR p.28):
  a genuine, actively-pursued lever, but it is a mix-shift BETWEEN
  Visaka's own existing lines (roofing customers migrating toward
  boards-type products), not a new addressable pool beyond what is
  already counted in the boards TAM.
- **Export markets**: NOT being pursued — actively RETREATING (17%→15%
  of V-Next revenue, explicit "recalibration"/"moderation" language,
  AR p.22/55). Flagged because most SAM-expansion sections assume
  export growth as a lever; here it is a lever the company itself is
  walking back.
- **ATUM/BIPV**: the only genuine NEW-pond lever (directional ~₹26,700cr
  India BIPV market, Section 2), nominally "pursued" via intensified
  marketing (AR p.54), but 8 years of zero disclosed revenue means it
  cannot be credited as an evidenced, active SAM-expansion driver yet —
  it remains pure, unresolved optionality (B07's finding, unchanged).

No revised-headroom calculation is presented for any of these levers:
boards capacity expansion and non-asbestos mix-shift do not expand SAM
(they affect capture, already modelled in 3B/3C); exports are
contracting, not expanding; and ATUM's revenue base is zero, so no
headroom multiple can be computed from it.

### 5E. Final output card

- **TAM (blended, conservative/realistic)**: ₹6,651cr / ₹7,401cr
  (roofing + boards + yarn; ATUM's ~₹26,700cr directional BIPV pond
  excluded from the sum and held as separate optionality).
- **SAM (blended, realistic)**: ₹6,429cr (86.9% of realistic TAM).
- **SOM**: ₹2,069cr (3yr) / ₹2,441cr (5yr), including the pro-rated
  unallocated-revenue residual.
- **Current SAM share**: ~25.6% (₹1,643cr segment revenue ÷ ₹6,429cr
  blended SAM).
- **Runway**: MODERATE (3.84x headroom, 5.8% blended TAM growth,
  structurally capped on the largest line).

**Valuation implication line**: At **~7.3-7.8%** revenue CAGR implied
by SOM (yr3-yr5, blended), with an FY25→FY26 EBITDA margin trajectory
of **7.1%→8.9%** (₹109.04cr→₹148.48cr EBITDA on ₹1,526cr→₹1,661cr
revenue, AR FY26 p.27 — no forward margin guidance found, so continued
improvement at this pace is not anchored beyond FY26), the earnings
growth embedded here is on the order of **7-10% CAGR** at best (revenue
growth plus modest further margin gains, not independently verified
beyond FY26), which **does not support** the current valuation on an
underlying (pre-exceptional) basis of **~20-22x P/E** — FY26 reported
PAT (₹87.83cr, EPS ₹10.16, headline P/E ~9.2x at CMP ₹93.37) includes a
₹51.16cr net-of-tax exceptional land-sale gain (Board's Report, AR FY26
p.65); stripping it out gives underlying PAT ≈ ₹36.67cr, EPS ≈ ₹4.24,
and underlying P/E ≈ **22.0x** (a cross-check using B04/B07's
pre-exceptional-PBT lens, ₹50.55cr PBT taxed at the ~20.3% average
effective rate, gives a similar ≈₹40.3cr/₹4.66 EPS/≈20.0x range — both
lenses land in the low-20s). A ~7-10% earnings CAGR against a ~20-22x
underlying multiple does not clear the strategy's 25% CAGR hurdle from
organic growth alone. **Any thesis that clears the hurdle must rest on
Section 1B quality-ladder re-rating (boards' rung migration) or on ATUM
converting from undisclosed optionality into real, priced revenue —
not on the TAM/SAM/SOM-derived organic growth path modelled here.**
