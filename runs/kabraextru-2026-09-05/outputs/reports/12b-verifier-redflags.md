## PART 1: INDEPENDENT RED-FLAG LIST

Built from the sources alone, before reading B05 or B06. Fifty-two items. Grouped by weight.

### 1A. Main company — items of thesis weight

**A1. The battery division's revenue halved in FY25 and nobody said so.** AR FY25 Note 38 (p.104)
reports Battery Division segment revenue of Rs 12,698.12 lakh for FY25 against Rs 26,616.45 lakh
for FY24, a fall of 52.3%, and a segment result that swung from a profit of Rs 651.55 lakh to a
loss of Rs (2,553.28) lakh. Neither number appears in prose anywhere in AR FY25. The chairman's
letter reports only the mix ratio: "The revenue mix between the Extrusion Business and Battery
Division was 74:26 in FY25 as against 57:43 in FY24" (AR FY25 p.4), which encodes the collapse as
a percentage shift. The MD&A Business Outlook in the same report says "Geon, the Battery Division
of KET focused on advanced lithium-ion battery packs and modules for electric vehicles, is
gaining traction from EV OEMs" (AR FY25 p.38). AR FY26 never mentions the FY24 battery base at
all. This is the growth engine the whole thesis rests on. Its revenue more than halved, its
result went from profit to loss, and two consecutive annual reports pass over it in silence while
one of them calls it "gaining traction" on the same page as the outlook.

**A2. The segment-revenue basis changed between the two reports with no disclosure.** In AR FY25
Note 38 (p.104) "Total Segment Revenue" is Rs 48,983.14 lakh for FY25 and Rs 61,465.49 lakh for
FY24. Both equal that year's TOTAL INCOME (revenue from operations plus other income), not
revenue from operations. In AR FY26 Note 38 (p.105) the FY25 comparative is still Rs 48,983.14
lakh, but the FY26 figure is Rs 45,099.83 lakh, which equals revenue from operations ALONE and
excludes Rs 2,367.55 lakh of other income (AR FY26 P&L p.67; Note 23 p.95). The comparative was
not restated and no note explains the change. The consequence is direct: the headline "extrusion
revenue fell 13.2%" (Rs 31,488.99 vs Rs 36,285.02 lakh) and "battery revenue grew 7.2%"
(Rs 13,610.84 vs Rs 12,698.12 lakh) are not like-for-like. Rs 2.37 Cr of other income sits inside
the FY25 base and outside the FY26 figure. Every downstream use of the 13.2% decline inherits
this defect, including B06's Claim 3 market-share arithmetic.

**A3. AR FY26 Note 28 carries the wrong year in its comparative column.** Note 28 (Depreciation
and amortisation expenses, AR FY26 p.95, verified from the PDF page image) shows a total of
Rs 1,557.18 lakh under the heading "For the Year Ended 31 March 2025". The face of the same
report's standalone P&L (p.67) and the Directors' Report table (p.17, verified from the PDF page
image) both show Rs 2,027.04 lakh for FY2024-25. Rs 1,557.18 lakh is exactly the FY2023-24 figure
printed in AR FY25's Directors' Report table (AR FY25 p.19). Note 28's prior-year column
reproduces FY24, not FY25, and understates the comparative by Rs 469.86 lakh, 23.2%. The note
that supports the depreciation line does not agree with the line.

**A4. Rs 1,668.41 lakh of unexplained "Other" income sizes the reported loss.** Note 23 (AR FY26
p.95) shows "Other ... 1,668.41" for FY26 against "22.91" for FY25, inside total other income of
Rs 2,367.55 lakh. The standalone pre-tax loss is Rs (423.21) lakh (p.17). No section of the
report names or explains the line.

**A5. Receivables ageing against the turnover framing.** Of Rs 9,052.43 lakh gross trade
receivables at FY26 close, Rs 4,428.68 lakh (471.76 + 2,021.56 + 1,935.36) is more than one year
past due, 48.92%, and Rs 1,935.36 lakh is more than three years past due against provision of
Rs 455.13 lakh, 23.5% cover (Note 9, AR FY26 p.87-88). The MD&A reports only a debtors-turnover
improvement of 5.20% (p.37). On my own read the FY25 comparative was 41.8% over one year
(2,037.30 + 1,146.22 + 952.68 over 9,893.75), so the ageing profile deteriorated by 7.1
percentage points.

**A6. The HEVPL exposure against the total provision.** Note 9 (AR FY26 p.87) states
"As at March 31, 2025, the Company has outstanding trade receivables amounting to Rs 3,039 lakhs
from Hero Electric Vehicle Pvt. Ltd. (HEVPL)", a customer admitted to CIRP by the NCLT on
20-Dec-2024. The total allowance for expected credit losses across the ENTIRE Rs 9,052.43 lakh
book is Rs 1,053.44 lakh (p.87). That is 34.7% cover against the single insolvent counterparty
alone, before any provision for the rest of the book. The note is still dated "As at March 31,
2025" inside the FY26 report and the balance is unrefreshed.

**A7. Note 9 contradicts itself.** The balance-sheet lines split receivables into

increase in credit risk" (AR FY26 p.87). The ageing table immediately below puts Rs 9,031.04 lakh
