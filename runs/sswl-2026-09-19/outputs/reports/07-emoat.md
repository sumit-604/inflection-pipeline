# STAGE 7 — EMERGING MOAT SCAN (22 CATEGORIES): Steel Strips Wheels Ltd (SSWL)

Run date: 2026-09-19. This is the Emerging Moat scan (Categories A1-R1,
~0-92 scale). It is NOT FTTCP. FTTCP is separate and runs later, inside
the valuation stage.

Units: figures from the FY26 Annual Report are converted from Rs lakh to
Rs Cr (÷100) at first use; figures from concalls and investor decks are
already in Rs Cr. Every number below carries a source anchor.

Evidence taxonomy used throughout: DOCUMENTED (committed capex, signed
agreement, disclosed metric — cited "DOC"), MANAGEMENT CLAIM (concall or
deck, not yet backed by committed capital or contract — cited "CLAIM"),
ANALYST INFERENCE (this stage's own inference from patterns — cited
"INFER").

### 2C Growth embedded in capex — arithmetic

Standalone net fixed assets (Property, Plant & Equipment + intangibles),
31-Mar-2026: Rs 1,821.89cr (AR p.9666 note 3, converted from
Rs 1,82,188.73 lakh).
Standalone Revenue from Operations, FY26: Rs 5,182.80cr (AR p.1190,
converted from Rs 5,18,280.25 lakh).

Implied historical fixed-asset turnover = 5,182.80 / 1,821.89 = **2.85x**.

Total capex under execution (FY27 programme, per 2A) = **Rs 600cr**.

Implied incremental revenue = 600 x 2.85 = **Rs 1,710cr**, or
**~33% above current (FY26) revenue** (1,710 / 5,183).

Caveat (this stage's own INFER, not management's): this is a mechanical
proxy, not a forecast. Roughly Rs 80cr of the Rs 600cr is maintenance
capex with no attached capacity, the Bhuj lines are only at trial
production in Oct-2026 with OEM approvals still pending, and management's
own 80%-utilisation target for the new aluminium capacity is dated FY28,
not FY27 (Q4 FY26 call, p.18-19). The ~33% figure is a multi-year ceiling,
not a one-year number.

### 2D New geography or market entries

Europe >58% of export revenue as of Q3 FY26 (Q3 FY26 call, p.4-5), a
concentration shift away from the US after the 2025-26 tariff episode.
Management states an intent to grow LatAm and Asia and, over "the next 10
years," to sell more wheels abroad than domestically (Q1 FY27 call,
p.8-9) — CLAIM, aspirational, no committed capital tied specifically to a
new-country entry beyond Bhuj's stated aftermarket/export orientation.

## SECTION 3: THE 22-CATEGORY SCAN

### FAMILY A — PRODUCT & TECHNOLOGY

**A1 Rare manufacturing capability** — NO EVIDENCE FOUND. No global
manufacturer count, qualification-timeline, or long-lead-equipment
disclosure located. Wheel-rim and knuckle manufacturing is an established,
multi-player global industry; SSWL's scale (broadest listed product span
per B04) is a scale advantage, not rarity.

**A2 Patent and IP pipeline** — NO EVIDENCE FOUND. No patent filing,
filing trend, or licensing revenue found anywhere in the AR, decks or
transcripts. The AR names a "Government of India approved R&D Centre"
(AR p.2363) but discloses no patents.

**A3 Process innovation** — WEAK. The Board's Report names generic
benefits from R&D efforts ("better yield," "reduced cost of products,"
"reduction in process wastages," AR p.2367-2378) with no yield % or unit
cost figure attached (DOC form, but qualitative only). The Jul-2026 deck
separately claims "Development of Robotic Automated Operation process for
Operating Cost Rationalization" (deck p.15, CLAIM, no metric). Management
separately claims to be "80% to 90%" the lowest-cost producer ex-China
(Q1 FY27 call, p.10, CLAIM, unverified against peer cost data in this
corpus). Time to materialise: ongoing, unquantified.

**A4 Product platform / modular architecture** — NO EVIDENCE FOUND. No
SKU-growth or launch-frequency data disclosed.

### FAMILY B — SUPPLY CHAIN

**B1 Backward integration and RM security** — MODERATE. Two
board-approved captive-power investments are documented: further
investment in Echanda Urja Pvt Ltd, a renewable-power SPV (Reg 30 board
outcome, 09-Apr-2026, per B00 filings_of_note), and a further Rs 6.93cr
investment in Clean Max Astria Pvt Ltd (CMAPL), the wind/solar/hybrid SPV
that supplies captive renewable power (AR p.13238, converted from
Rs 693.10 lakh). Rooftop solar is installed across all plants (AR
p.5339-5344). This is RM/power security at the margin, not raw-material
(steel/aluminium) security — those inputs remain a disclosed pass-through
with no captive source (AR p.7607). Jamshedpur is labelled a "Backward
Integrated Plant for Steel Wheels" on the facility map (Jul-2026 deck
p.10, CLAIM, mechanism not described). Time to materialise: ongoing,
already partly in place.

**B2 Qualification lock-in** — STRONG (evidence tier capped at CLAIM).
The Jul-2026 investor deck discloses "share of business" by OEM model
line that is, on its face, extreme lock-in: MG Motors 100%, Honda 96%,
Suzuki Scooters 96%, JCB 62%, Ashok Leyland 64%, Tata Motors 58%, Mahindra
56% (PV) / 56% (tractor) / 81% (3W), Hyundai group 74%, Kia group 51%,
Escorts 60%, HMSI 39% (deck p.11-17). Management separately claims to be
"the sole supplier for knuckles right now in the market" with 2 OEM
customers (Q4 FY26 call, p.19) and "almost a monopoly" (~80%) in EV
scooter wheels (Q4 FY26 call, p.7-8). None of these percentages are
audited, certified, or contract-referenced; no IATF/TS or NADCAP-style
quality certification is disclosed anywhere in the AR (the AR's ISO
references are ISO 9001/14001/27001/45001/50001, general management-system
certifications, not automotive design-in credentials). B05's Stage-5
record shows the steel-vs-alloy EBITDA/wheel split — which would help
verify this claim's economics — stonewalled in every quarter asked
(Q2 FY26, Q3 FY26, Q1 FY27; B05 repeated_evasions). Time to materialise:
already active; durability unverifiable beyond management's own numbers.

**B3 Supply chain network effect** — NO EVIDENCE FOUND. Not a platform
business; SSWL is a direct OEM-spec manufacturer.

### FAMILY C — CUSTOMER

**C1 Customer ecosystem / embedded relationships** — MODERATE. Several

**C2 Customer concentration improving** — NO EVIDENCE FOUND, and the

Export revenue fell from Rs 561cr (FY25) to Rs 454cr (FY26) per B04. No
top-5/top-10 customer share trend is disclosed.

### FAMILY F — TALENT & ORGANISATIONAL

**F1 Talent density** — NO EVIDENCE FOUND, and the disclosed trend runs

DOC-tier negative signal, not a gap.

**F2 Execution moat** — MODERATE. Cross-referencing the injected B05
promise-delivery record: EBITDA/wheel guidance was beaten in 3 straight
quarters (Rs 270 guided -> Rs 282 delivered Q4 FY26; Rs 300 guided ->
Rs 314 delivered Q1 FY27; B05 promise_delivery rows), and FY26

5,183cr delivered; Rs 470-480cr guided -> Rs 523cr delivered). Against

internal expectation, by his own admission (Q1 FY27 call; B05
timeline_slippages), and an earlier steel-wheel capacity plan (205 to
270 lakh units) was quietly curtailed to ~210 lakh without proactive
disclosure (B05 red_flags). Net: real delivery on the hard numbers
management chooses to disclose, alongside undisclosed plan revisions on
the numbers it does not want to discuss. DOC tier (delivered numbers are
reported/audited results), CLAIM tier for the guidance itself.

### FAMILY H — ECOSYSTEM & EXTERNAL

**H1 Industry consolidation beneficiary** — WEAK, AND CONTRADICTED
WITHIN THE CORPUS. Management stated in the Q4 FY26 call that "there are
certain investigations going on against our competitors in Southeast
Asia for antidumping duties" (p.4, CLAIM), implying a cost/tariff
advantage for India. On the very next call (Q1 FY27, 16-Jul-2026),
management stated "there is no differential advantage that Vietnam or
Thailand has today as compared to India... we are at a level playing

quarter on management's own telling. Scored WEAK with an INFER-tier
discount for the internal contradiction, not credited a second time
under E2.

**H2 Strategic partnerships** — MODERATE-TO-STRONG. A Tripartite
Agreement with Liuzhou Arays Technology Co. Ltd (equipment
selection/supply/installation and technology transfer) and Hainan Jihoo
Import & Export Co. Ltd (export/invoicing/compliance), both China-based,

also board-approved equity investments (DOC tier, Section 3 B1). Set

resign in Mar-2026 (B00 filings_of_note) — a partnership-weakening signal
in the same period, not offsetting the Arays/Hainan and power SPV

**H3 ESG moat** — WEAK. FY27 renewable-energy capex is guided at

### FAMILY I — STRUCTURAL ASYMMETRIES

**I1 Talent asymmetry** — 0, NO EVIDENCE FOUND on either leg. No named
inventor on any patent filing (none found, see A2), no ex-DRDO/HAL/global-
major staff concentration claim, and no remuneration-above-sector-norm
disclosure for technical staff located. The R&D expenditure trend (F1)
runs the opposite direction from a talent-investment story.

**I2 Cannibalization barrier** — 0. For the strongest moat claim in this
scan (B2, qualification lock-in), the honest answer to "what must the
best-resourced competitor destroy to copy this" is: nothing identifiable.
A competitor can bid the next platform's RFQ on its own merits; SSWL's
OEM share-of-business is a revealed outcome of winning bids, not a
configuration a rival is structurally blocked from matching. The same
applies to the claimed knuckle "sole supplier" position — a competitor
can build knuckle capacity without sacrificing an existing product line,
pricing regime, or internal cost structure. This is execution lead, which
the framework explicitly does not credit under I2.

## SECTION 4: REGULATORY & POLICY TAILWINDS (R1)

**4A Regulatory approvals in pipeline.** OEM approvals for the Bhuj alloy
wheel and knuckle plants are pending, targeted for Q4 FY27 alongside
trial production from Oct-2026 (Q4 FY26 call). Statutory/regulatory
clearances for the Bhuj units are described as "in process" (AR p.24).
No named competitor-held approval that SSWL lacks was found.

**4B Government policy tailwinds.**
- GST 2.0 rate cuts (Oct-2025): management credits broad-based domestic
  demand recovery across CV, tractor, PV and 2W/3W segments directly to
  this reform, repeated across the Q4 FY26 and Q1 FY27 calls ("the GST
  cut really buoying the market," Q4 FY26 call p.9; "structural boom in
  the CV market and the agriculture," Q1 FY27 call). This is a DOC-tier
  policy fact (the GST reform itself is public record) with a CLAIM-tier
  attribution of the demand effect to SSWL specifically. Duration:
  ongoing; enrolment: automatic (a rate cut, not an application-based
  scheme); competitors share the benefit fully — this is a rising tide,
  not a company-specific moat.
- DVA (Duty Value Addition) certification under a PLI scheme is
  administratively passed to customers (AR p.4471, Certificate Charges
  note: "includes Rs 1.35 Lakhs for DVA certification under PLI scheme as
  given to the customers") — DOC, but immaterial in scale and not a
  captured benefit to SSWL's own P&L.
- Antidumping investigations against Southeast Asian wheel exporters
  (H1, above) would be a competitor-cost-disadvantage tailwind if durable;
  management's own Q1 FY27 statement that "there is no differential
  advantage" today undercuts this as a current, reliable tailwind.

**4C Regulatory moat assessment.** GST 2.0 is active now, shared by all
peers, and its durability depends entirely on the tax structure staying
in place — not an SSWL-specific edge. The antidumping/tariff angle is
emerging-but-unstable, having already reversed once within the four
transcripts read. Net: R1 is a real, currently-active tailwind for the
sector broadly, with a modest, non-durable company-specific overlay from
the tariff situation.
