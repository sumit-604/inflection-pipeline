### 2A Accounting policy aggressiveness — extension
B02 already covers revenue recognition (Ind AS 115 point-in-time, conservative), depreciation (Schedule II
ranges, standard), ECL matrix (the zero-ECL question, Item 1), and the technical-knowhow write-off (Item 5).
This stage's extension, tracing the technical-knowhow item to its narrative source (not covered by B02,
which works only within the Notes): the Rs 477 Lakh (Rs 4.77 Cr) technical-knowhow intangible added in FY25
and 100% amortized in the same year corresponds, on the balance of the front-matter evidence, to the "EFI
ECU" technology licensing deal INEL entered during FY24-25. The FY25 AR's own front matter states: "We have
entered into a technical licensing partnership with a globally renowned automotive supplier for the
Electronic Fuel Injection Control Unit (EFI ECU). This strategic collaboration marks our entry into a new
product segment..." (FY25 AR, "Entering into New Technical Partnerships," ~p.28 PDF) and separately "EFI
ECU: We have successfully introduced an EFI ECU tailored for motorcycles, leveraging a technology licensing
agreement with a global partner" (FY25 AR, R&D section, ~p.24 PDF). INEL's own "Our Journey" timeline names
a "Partnership with Athena, Italy for EFI" in an earlier era bucket (FY26 AR, ~p.16-17 PDF, line 827) — the
AR text does not itself equate the FY24-25 "globally renowned automotive supplier" partner with "Athena,
Italy," so this stage does NOT assert the two are definitively the same counterparty; it states both facts
and flags the ambiguity. Regardless of the counterparty's identity, the accounting question B02 raised
stands: NO note anywhere explains why a stated-5-year-life licensed technology asset was fully written off
in its year of addition. This stage adds the operational context: FY26 AR then reports "we initiated
in-house software development for EFI controllers, which will enable greater flexibility in calibration and
fine-tuning across multiple vehicle variants" (FY26 AR, "Advancing Our Controller Technologies," ~p.23 PDF,
line 1294-1299) — i.e., in the very year after the licensed technology's book value was fully expensed, INEL
began building the same capability in-house. This is a material Second-Order observation (elaborated in
Phase 6E below): either normal technology-maturation sequencing (license to enter, then insource to scale)
or a signal that the licensed partnership under-delivered — the AR text supports either reading and does not
resolve it.

of gap that should draw a direct management question before crediting the Company's product-roadmap
execution record at face value.

**2. "Electric Motor and Controller (≤3kW Applications)... on track for commercial rollout by the end of
2026."**
Opening claim (FY25 AR, R&D section, p.24 PDF): "these systems are scheduled for customer trials and remain

Annexure (p.82 PDF) reports "successfully completed the validation and vehicle trials of its patented
ferrite-based motor and controller" — a genuine, verifiable step forward — but immediately follows with

motor control precision," which describes the CONTROLLER as still in development, and the specific "end of
2026" commercial-rollout timeline is not reaffirmed, re-dated, or addressed anywhere else in the FY26 AR.
Classification: **HEDGED RETREAT** — the milestone reported (trials complete) is real and positive, but it
is a lesser claim than "on track for commercial rollout," and the timeline commitment itself is quietly
dropped rather than reaffirmed or explicitly pushed out. Materiality: MODERATE — this is one of the
Company's few EV-specific, dated product commitments, and dated commitments in EV hardware are exactly the
kind of claim a transition thesis should hold management to.

**3. EFI ECU: from "technology licensing agreement with a global partner" to "we initiated in-house software
development."**
Opening claim (FY25 AR, twice: "leveraging a technology licensing agreement with a global partner," R&D
p.24 PDF, and "a strategic technical licensing partnership. Our R&D team is actively advancing this platform

for EFI controllers, which will enable greater flexibility in calibration and fine-tuning across multiple
vehicle variants." No scooter-specific deployment update is given; the FRAMING shifts from "leveraging a
partner's licensed technology, extending it to new segments" to "building the capability in-house."

topic IS addressed in FY26) nor a pure hedge (the FY26 language is confident, not weak) — it is a genuine
change of strategic approach on the identical technology area, described without any acknowledgment that it
represents a change from the prior year's "licensing partnership, extend to scooters" framing. Materiality:
HIGH — this is the same technology area B02 flagged as carrying an unexplained 100%-write-off accounting
anomaly (Phase 2A above); the operational narrative now shows the Company moving away from dependence on
that licensed technology in the same year the licence's book value was fully expensed. Whether this
sequence is normal technology-maturation practice or reflects the licensed partnership under-delivering
cannot be determined from the AR text alone, but the coincidence of accounting treatment and strategic pivot
in the same technology area, across the same two-year window, is exactly the kind of Second-Order linkage
this framework asks to be named, not summarized past.

No other quiet abandonments were identified with sufficient evidence to name; the remaining front-matter

## PRIORITY D: CAPITAL ALLOCATION HISTORY — JUDGMENT

The record, consolidated from Phases 3 and 5F above: debt-free; Rs 531.53 Cr investment book (49.7% Lucas
TVS, the remainder a genuinely diversified treasury of debentures/listed equities/mutual funds/gold ETF/
alternative investment funds) against Rs 821.33 Cr net worth (64.7% of net worth in securities); dividend
payout 31.5% of PAT (FY26, Rs 35.06 Cr / Rs 111.26 Cr) vs 34.5% (FY25, Rs 28.28 Cr / Rs 82.03 Cr) — both
"roughly 35%" as characterised in the task brief; capex Rs 42.15 Cr (FY26) vs Rs 23.25 Cr (FY25), a genuine
step-up.

**Judgment:** the "why is a third of the balance sheet in a Level-3 stake in the promoter's own parent-
adjacent entity rather than deployed or returned" question has three partial answers in the evidence, none
of them fully satisfying:
1. **It generates real income.** The full investment book (not just Lucas TVS) contributed Rs 25.53 Cr of
   P&L income in FY26 (Phase 3C) — this is not a dead asset, and management does actively manage the
   non-Lucas-TVS portion of the portfolio (Note 8's holdings turn over meaningfully year to year: new mutual
   fund positions entered, gold ETF nearly quadrupled from Rs 6.16 Cr to Rs 23.50 Cr, several bond positions
   rolled).
2. **The Lucas TVS stake specifically is NOT actively managed — it is static.** The 97,351-share holding is
   UNCHANGED for at least the two years shown; there is no evidence in either AR of any consideration to
   trim, add to, or otherwise actively manage this specific position, in contrast to the rest of the
   portfolio. Combined with the interlock finding (Phase 5F: INEL's own MD is also Lucas TVS's MD), this
   reads less like an active capital-allocation decision each year and more like a legacy holding that is
   simply carried forward, marked up, and never revisited as a capital-allocation question in the AR's own
   narrative.
3. **Capex is now accelerating, which is the more constructive capital-allocation signal.** The FY26 capex
   step-up (+81.3%) and the 7.2x jump in capital commitments suggest management IS beginning to redeploy
   toward the operating business rather than continuing to build the securities book at the prior pace (non-
   current investment additions were Rs 962.85 Cr GROSS purchases against Rs 960.30 Cr sales in FY26 — i.e.,
   the portfolio's SIZE grew only modestly net, Note 8/Cash Flow Statement, consistent with active churn
   rather than continued net accumulation).

**Overall: the capital allocation record is disciplined on the treasury-management dimension (diversified,
income-generating, actively churned ex-Lucas-TVS) but passive and unexplained on the single largest position
(Lucas TVS), and the dividend payout at ~32-35% of PAT, while reasonable, is not aggressive relative to a
debt-free company generating this level of ROE (15%) — a case could be made for either a higher payout or a
faster capex/M&A redeployment, and the AR itself does not make that case either way.** This is a genuine
open question for the deliberation stage, not a red flag in the fraud/mismanagement sense.
