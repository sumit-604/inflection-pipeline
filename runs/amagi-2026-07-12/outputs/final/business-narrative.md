Media cloud SaaS crossing into profitability

Amagi runs a cloud SaaS platform for broadcast to streaming media operations, covering cloud playout, OTT and FAST channel unification, and connected TV ad monetization, sold B2B to broadcasters and content owners with about 73% of revenue from the Americas. FY26 revenue was ₹1,506 Cr, up 30% reported and 23% in constant currency, gross margin about 69%. The shares trade at ₹580 for a market cap of ₹12,552 Cr. Three divisions carry it: Streaming Unification at 57% of revenue, Monetization and Marketplace at 24%, Cloud Modernization at 19%. The model is asset light, carries zero debt, and holds net cash of ₹1,664 Cr.

FY26 was the first full year of profit after three years of losses, PAT ₹72 Cr on a swing of about ₹140 Cr year on year. Operating loss narrowed about 87%, from ₹359 Cr in FY23 to ₹17 Cr in H1 FY26. Sales and marketing plus customer success fell from 44% to 24% of revenue between FY24 and FY26, and R&D from 33% to 23%. Adjusted EBITDA margin crossed into double digits at about 10%, or ₹156 Cr.

Gate 0 scores AVERAGE, with core 44, moat 12, and a grand total of 56 out of 160. The classification is dragged by the FY22 to FY25 window: median ROCE of negative 26%, negative PAT in FY24 and FY25, and cumulative CFO to PAT of 0.19. Those years carry non cash ESOP charges and a CCPS liability classification artifact from the pre-IPO capital structure, not operating cash losses of that size. FY26, the first clean post-IPO year, scored a full 20 of 20 on the balance sheet block, and only that one clean year exists so far. The AVERAGE rating itself hangs on a scoring convention. The 28% PAT growth rate is measured endpoint to endpoint across a loss window; scored zero instead, the core falls to 39 and the rating to AVOID.

The cash statement diverges hard from the profit statement. In H1 FY26, the first profitable half, reported operating cash flow was negative ₹201 Cr against PAT of ₹6.5 Cr, and the reported profit itself leaned on ₹29 Cr of other income equal to 250% of pre-tax profit while the operating line stayed a ₹17 Cr loss. Nine month reported operating cash flow was negative ₹76 Cr, and the positive cash story rests on an adjusted figure that strips out IPO and ESOP buyback cash. FY26 adjusted free cash flow of ₹38 Cr is only about 24% of adjusted EBITDA. Trade receivables jumped 35.7% in six months, from ₹281 Cr to ₹381 Cr between March and September 2025, against broadly flat revenue, and the credit loss allowance flipped from an ₹1.1 Cr net release in FY25 to a ₹7.6 Cr net addition in H1 FY26. A one time pre-IPO ESOP and SAR cash settlement of about ₹135 Cr distorts the half. Whether that working capital build is growth induced or structural cannot be settled on the evidence in hand, and no credit rating rationale or full year receivables ageing schedule was available to close the question.

Management earns a B on delivery. The one specific, testable figure it gave, steady state adjusted EBITDA margin of about 10%, landed almost exactly the next quarter, and every cost leverage trend it described tracked. Set against that, two investor commitments were dropped silently, a top five client renegotiation was first framed as purely strategic and then revealed a quarter later to have cost gross margin, and the same non specific deflection on three year guidance ran across two consecutive calls. Independent review also found the reported growth is FX flattered, 30% reported versus 23% constant currency, that net logo adds decelerated more than 50% from 67 to 29 and turned net negative in Q4, and that net revenue retention ticked down from 127% to 126%.

Promoter conduct verified TRUSTWORTHY with zero deal breakers: no pledge, no SEBI or criminal action, promoters not selling in the IPO, an outside CFO and two credentialed independent directors added before listing. Four items stay on the watch list rather than fire a flag. Promoter group entity Vinculum Advisors bought a roughly 10% stake at ₹25 a share against the ₹361 offer price about six months before the IPO, lifting promoter plus group holding from 21.72% to 31.74%. The Rule 11(g) audit trail and books of account auditor modification has run unremediated for three years. Company level transfer pricing and tax disputes total ₹118 Cr, or 13.7% of net worth. Board independence sits at the regulatory floor of 2 of 6.

Twelve peer transcripts across Affle, Newgen, RateGain and Tata Elxsi produced zero contradictions of Amagi's claims, and the net effect complicates rather than contradicts. Peers confirm that declining three year guidance is sector standard, that impression volume outpacing monetization revenue is a real ad tech dynamic, and that absolute no competition framing is a recognizable sector style rather than a verifiable fact. RateGain disclosed a customer loss worth 4% of MarTech revenue and an NRR decline more openly than Amagi handled its own renegotiation, though those RateGain figures were first raised by analysts rather than volunteered by management. No peer describes an industry wide demand boom matching Amagi's framing, and the cross read runs mixed to cautious from October 2025 to May 2026.

The addressable market is large and early. Amagi's disclosed three funnel TAM is about $16.9bn with a $5.1bn SAM, converting on a realistic basis to about ₹194,820 Cr of TAM and ₹48,705 Cr of SAM, against a current SAM share near 2.8%. Runway class reads MASSIVE, revenue headroom about 32 times, TAM growing about 13% a year, and management's own TAM claim reads reasonable at a ratio of 1.0. The investor presentation's $42.5bn CTV ad spend headline is gross ad spend flowing through connected TV, not Amagi's software market, and using it would overstate the opportunity about 2.5 times. This read is partial, since several verification searches were skipped, including independent confirmation of the roughly 10% cloud penetration claim.
