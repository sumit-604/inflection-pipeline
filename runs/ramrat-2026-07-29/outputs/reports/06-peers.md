# Stage 6: Peer Concall Verification — Ram Ratna Wires Ltd (RAMRAT)
Run date: 2026-07-29 | Protocol v1.1 | Model: claude-sonnet-5

Peer set: BHAGYANGR (Bhagyanagar India Ltd) x3 calls, VIDYAWIRES (Vidya Wires Ltd) x2 calls.
RAMRAT held no concall this cycle (no-concall mode); claims below originate from RAMRAT's
AR/results/presentation as handed off in B05.peer_questions.

**Segment-mapping caveat, stated up front**: Neither peer manufactures copper tubes (the
HVAC/refrigeration product at the centre of RAMRAT's Bhiwadi capex/import-substitution thesis).
BHAGYANGR makes copper rods, bus bars, enamelled wires/strips, transformer windings, auto
components, and (since 2007) copper pipes/solar collectors for solar water heaters — an adjacent
but distinct application from HVAC copper tubes. VIDYAWIRES makes enamelled copper/aluminium
wires, winding wires, PV ribbons, copper bus bars, and is entering CTC (continuously transposed
copper conductors) — again winding-wire/conductor products, not copper tubes. Both peers are
therefore adjacent-segment evidence for general copper-industry claims (pricing pass-through,
capex cycle, demand) but cannot speak to the copper-tube-specific claims (1 and 3) at all. This
is stated plainly per operating rule 4 and drives two UNVERIFIABLE verdicts below — not a
downgrade on plausibility, a hard evidentiary gap.

## PART 1: CLAIM-BY-CLAIM VERIFICATION

### Claim 1
**Claim**: Is India's copper-tube market growing ~16-17% CAGR, and is India still ~70%
import-dependent in copper tubes? (check BHAGYANGR, VIDYAWIRES) — underpins RAMRAT's Bhiwadi
capex/import-substitution thesis.

**Verdict**: UNVERIFIABLE

**Peer evidence**: None. Copper tubes for HVAC/refrigeration are never mentioned in any of the
five transcripts. BHAGYANGR's only "pipe" reference is a legacy solar-water-heater copper-pipe
line dating to 2007 (BHAGYANGR, Q3 FY26 call, Jan 2026, opening remarks) — a different end
application (solar thermal, not HVAC/AC) and never quantified or discussed again across the
three BHAGYANGR calls. Neither peer states an India copper-tube market size, growth rate, or
import-dependency figure anywhere.

**Peers silent**: Both BHAGYANGR (all 3 calls) and VIDYAWIRES (both calls) — neither operates in
copper tubes, so silence here is structural (segment mismatch), not informative about the
claim's truth.

**Net read**: This is the single most important gap in the peer set. RAMRAT's headline
capex/import-substitution thesis rests on a specific product-market claim (copper-tube CAGR,
import dependency) that this peer set is structurally incapable of testing. No peer evidence
exists either way; this cannot be upgraded on plausibility grounds even though both peers'
copper-demand macro framing (AI/EV/green energy) is directionally consistent with a growing
copper-derivatives market generally.

### Claim 2
**Claim**: How do peers describe copper/raw-material pass-through, and do they confirm/contradict
RAMRAT's claim that "dynamic pricing strategies" preserved margin through copper volatility?
(BHAGYANGR, VIDYAWIRES)

**Verdict**: PARTIALLY VERIFIED

**Peer evidence**:
- VIDYAWIRES strongly corroborates a pass-through/hedged model: "we have a completely back to
  back pricing model. So about the pricing pass on, it would be 100% whatever price increase in
  the LME is there will be passed on to the customer... about our margins so they are quite
  intact" (VIDYAWIRES, Q2 FY26 call, Dec 2025, Shailesh Rathi, MD). Repeated in the FY26 call:
  "basically the copper prices or the aluminium prices going up or down really does not affect
  much on the margin terms because our model of business is completely back-to-back" (VIDYAWIRES,
  Q4/FY26 call, May 2026, Shailesh Rathi).
- BHAGYANGR also describes a pass-through mechanism — "the increasing raw material prices... is a
  straightaway pass-through to the customer" (BHAGYANGR, Q2 FY26 call, Nov 2025) — but its own
  numbers complicate a clean "pricing alone preserves margin" story. Management, pressed on a
  near-doubling of EBITDA margin (1.92% to 3.88% in H1 FY26), attributed it mainly to product-mix
  shift toward value-added products and a copper-scrap customs-duty removal, and separately
  conceded rising prices themselves creates a "second-level ripple effect" / surge in demand that
  lifts margin: "whenever there is an uptrend in the commodity prices... there is a surge in
  demand from the end customers who want to buy, and that's how there is a slight increase in the
  EBITDA margin" (BHAGYANGR, Q2 FY26 call, Nov 2025). By the Q4 FY26 call, management is explicit
  that pass-through is for price increases only, not incremental margin: "we have a pass-through
  mechanism of the copper prices to the finished products. However, passing on the additional
  EBITDA per tonne is a little bit of a stretch" (BHAGYANGR, Q4/FY26 call, May 2026).

**Peers silent**: Neither peer comments on RAMRAT specifically or on the phrase "dynamic pricing
strategies"; this is inference from analogous business-model descriptions.

**Net read**: Direction confirmed by both peers — pass-through/back-to-back pricing against LME
is standard practice across this industry segment and does protect against straightforward
margin erosion from rising copper. Magnitude and mechanism diverge: VIDYAWIRES presents a nearly
frictionless hedge with margin "quite intact"; BHAGYANGR's own margin expansion in the same
period came predominantly from value-added mix shift and a one-off duty change, with pricing
providing only a secondary, partial lift. This is useful context for RAMRAT: an industry-standard
pass-through mechanism existing does not by itself prove RAMRAT's margin resilience was driven by
"dynamic pricing" rather than a mix or one-off effect — synthesis should treat the RAMRAT claim as
plausible in mechanism but unproven in magnitude attribution.

### Claim 3
**Claim**: Do peers corroborate strong/accelerating copper-tube/HVAC demand and rising
utilisation in their segments? (BHAGYANGR, VIDYAWIRES) — tests whether RAMRAT's implied Q4 FY26
copper-tube deceleration is temporary or structural.

**Verdict**: UNVERIFIABLE

**Peer evidence**: None on copper tubes/HVAC specifically — same segment-mismatch as Claim 1. As
context only (not a substitute for direct evidence): both peers do report strong/accelerating
demand and high utilisation in their own (different) segments. VIDYAWIRES ran at ~90% capacity
utilisation as of the Dec 2025 call ("currently our capacity is in like on an average 90% or so we
are already utilizing it," Shailesh Rathi) and reported Q4 FY26 revenue growth of 58% YoY (May
2026 call, Praveen Motwani Q&A). BHAGYANGR's H1 FY26 volume rose to 12,400 MT from 8,955 MT prior
year (+38%, Nov 2025 call), full FY26 revenue crossed Rs.2,000 crore for the first time (Jun 2026
call), and value-added capacity is "almost fully utilized" (Nov 2025 call).

**Peers silent**: Both peers, on copper tubes/HVAC specifically.

**Net read**: General copper-products demand across two independent, non-competing sub-segments
was strong and accelerating through FY26 (H2 FY26 in particular, coincident with the copper price
spike), which is directionally consistent with a buoyant copper-industry backdrop RAMRAT could be
riding. But this cannot be read across to validate or refute the specific claim about copper-tube
demand deceleration in Q4 FY26 — that segment is simply not present in this peer set. Flag as an
open evidentiary gap for synthesis, not a false negative.

### Claim 4
**Claim**: Are peers running a similar capex cycle (new capacity, CTC/HVDC-type segments) on a
similar timeline? (BHAGYANGR, VIDYAWIRES) — company-specific execution vs industry-wide capacity
race.

**Verdict**: VERIFIED

**Peer evidence**:
- VIDYAWIRES is "nearly doubling our manufacturing capacity from 19,680 metric tons per annum to
  37,680 metric tons per annum through our new facility Narsanda via our subsidiary called as
  ALCU Industries" (VIDYAWIRES, Q2 FY26 call, Dec 2025, Shailesh Rathi), funded by Rs.140 crore of
  its Dec-2025 IPO proceeds. Manufacturing at ALCU commenced Feb 7, 2026 (VIDYAWIRES, Q4/FY26
  call, May 2026). The expansion explicitly targets CTC (continuously transposed copper
  conductors) — a transformer/HVDC-adjacent product — with Phase 1 capacity of ~3,000 MT, expected
  to start contributing "maybe September or October, or probably before Diwali" (VIDYAWIRES,
  Q4/FY26 call, May 2026, Shailesh Rathi, in response to Rajesh Mudaliar). Management sizes
  India's CTC demand at "roughly I believe 30,000 tons or 40,000 tons... and it is growing at a
  faster pace" (same call).
- BHAGYANGR grew installed capacity from 30,000 to 35,000 MT within FY26, with a further Rs.40
  crore capex earmarked over the next two years, alongside new value-added lines (silver/tin-plated
  bus bars for AI data centers, plastic/aluminium recycling) (BHAGYANGR, Q4/FY26 call, May 2026).
  An analyst explicitly raised industry-wide capacity as a risk: "many other copper recyclers have
  also expanded capacity. Even Adani and Hindalco are coming live with their smelters... do we see
  overcapacity risk or margin compression in copper over the next 2-3 years?" (BHAGYANGR, Q4/FY26
  call, May 2026, Manan Shah); management acknowledged growing competition while arguing demand
  growth outpaces it.

**Peers silent**: Neither ties its own capex specifically to copper tubes; both map to the general
"capacity expansion in copper-derivative products" pattern, not the copper-tube sub-segment.

**Net read**: Both peers are running capacity expansions on essentially the same FY26 timeline as
RAMRAT's Bhiwadi capex, funded similarly (fresh IPO proceeds for VIDYAWIRES; internal accruals for
BHAGYANGR) and aimed at similar macro narratives (EV, green energy, transformers, AI/data
centers). This is an industry-wide capacity race across the broader copper-derivatives value
chain, not a lone-expander situation — which reframes RAMRAT's Bhiwadi investment as riding a
sector-wide capex wave rather than a differentiated, company-specific bet. It also raises the
overcapacity/margin-compression risk that BHAGYANGR's own analyst flagged, a risk RAMRAT's B05
claims do not appear to address (see Part 2E).

## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

### 2A Demand environment
Consensus of strength, not contradiction. Both peers report accelerating revenue and volume
through FY26, concentrated in H2 FY26 as copper prices rose (VIDYAWIRES Q4 FY26 revenue +58% YoY,
May 2026 call; BHAGYANGR record Rs.2,000+ crore FY26 revenue with Q4 alone at Rs.735 crore, Jun
2026 call). Both attribute demand to the same three buzzwords: AI/data centers, EV, and green
energy/renewables. This matches RAMRAT's general "demand boom" framing at the macro level but
says nothing about copper tubes specifically (see Claim 3).

### 2B Pricing and input costs
Both peers confirm the same copper-price backdrop: LME/COMEX copper described as touching
"all-time high of around USD 12,000" (VIDYAWIRES, Dec 2025 call) rising further to ~$13,000 by
early 2026 (BHAGYANGR, Jan 2026 call) and referenced near $1,300/kg equivalent by May 2026
(BHAGYANGR, Jun 2026 call). Both use LME-linked, largely hedged/back-to-back pricing. However, as
detailed in Claim 2, BHAGYANGR's realised margin gains track value-added mix shift and a
copper-scrap customs-duty removal at least as much as pure price pass-through — a caveat on any
narrative (RAMRAT's included) that leans solely on "dynamic pricing" to explain margin resilience
through a volatile copper cycle.

### 2C Capex cycle
Industry-wide capacity race, not a lone expander (see Claim 4 for detail). Both peers are
expanding at a similar magnitude (VIDYAWIRES ~92% capacity increase; BHAGYANGR ~17% capacity
increase this year with further capex planned) on the same FY26 timeline. BHAGYANGR's own
Q&A surfaced overcapacity risk given Adani, Hindalco, and other recyclers (Jain Resource
Recycling, Sunlight Recycling) simultaneously entering copper value-added products (BHAGYANGR,
Q4/FY26 call, May 2026).

### 2D Competitive mentions
One direct, high-value peer mention of RAMRAT: VIDYAWIRES names Ram Ratna Wires as a peer with
meaningful product overlap: "few of the peers namely are Precision Wires, Ram Ratna Wires, KSH
International, and there are many more, but they don't compete in each and every product of Vidya
Wires... for 50 to 60% commonness of our product is there with them" (VIDYAWIRES, Q4/FY26 call,
May 14 2026, Shailesh Rathi, in answer to Nikhil Chowdhary). This confirms RAMRAT is recognised
by a listed peer as a genuine, substantial competitor in enamelled/winding-wire products — but the
comment is scoped to winding-wire product overlap, not copper tubes, and is neutral in tone
(neither praise nor concern). BHAGYANGR separately names Precision Wires and KSH International as
EBITDA/tonne benchmarks (Jun 2026 call) but never mentions RAMRAT.

### 2E Risks peers discuss that RAMRAT does not
- **Scrap/raw-material logistics disruption**: BHAGYANGR flagged Gulf-region shipping delays
  (war-related transhipment disruption) affecting copper-scrap sourcing industry-wide, though it
  claims low direct exposure (<5% of material from Gulf) (BHAGYANGR, Q4/FY26 call, May 2026,
  Manan Shah and Raj Sarraf Q&A). Not directly applicable to RAMRAT (which is not scrap-based) but
  signals a live sector-wide input-supply risk.
- **Overcapacity/margin compression from new entrants**: Adani and Hindalco entering copper
  recycling/smelting, flagged as a 2-3 year risk to industry margins (BHAGYANGR, Jun 2026 call).
- **Aluminium substitution**: long-run secular risk of aluminium displacing copper in some
  electrical applications, though management argues the easy-substitution areas are largely
  exhausted after 15 years (BHAGYANGR, Q3 FY26 call, Jan 2026, Mitesh Bhandari Q&A).
- **Rising working-capital/interest-cost burden from copper price inflation**: both peers
  acknowledge that a rising copper price mechanically inflates working-capital requirements and
  short-term debt even when the pricing cycle is fully pass-through/hedged (VIDYAWIRES, Dec 2025
  call, Mihir Manohar Q&A; BHAGYANGR, Jan 2026 call, Atul Raval and Pakshal Jain Q&A). This is a
  cash-flow/leverage risk that a pure "margin is protected" narrative can obscure.
- **US tariff exposure on exports**: BHAGYANGR flagged 50% US tariffs on Indian copper exports as
  a live risk, though it says its own US exposure is small (BHAGYANGR, Q3 FY26 call, Jan 2026,
  Rahul Mehta Q&A).

## PART 4: TRIANGULATION SUMMARY

- **Claims verified**: 1 of 4 (Claim 4 — industry-wide capex cycle)
- **Claims partially verified**: 1 of 4 (Claim 2 — pass-through pricing, direction confirmed,
  magnitude/attribution nuanced)
- **Claims unverifiable**: 2 of 4 (Claims 1 and 3 — both are copper-tube/HVAC-specific and no
  peer operates in that segment)
- **Claims contradicted**: 0

**Single most consequential contradiction**: None found. No peer statement directly contradicts a
RAMRAT claim.

**Single strongest independent confirmation**: The industry-wide capex cycle (Claim 4).
Both VIDYAWIRES (near-doubling of capacity via ALCU, funded by its Dec-2025 IPO) and BHAGYANGR
(30,000 to 35,000 MT capacity growth plus further planned capex) are expanding aggressively on
essentially the same FY26 timeline as RAMRAT's Bhiwadi investment, targeting overlapping macro
narratives (EV, green energy, transformers, data centers). This is genuine independent evidence
that RAMRAT's capex decision sits inside a real sector-wide expansion cycle, not an isolated bet.

**Overall**: this peer set complicates more cleanly than it either supports or undercuts RAMRAT's
narrative. The two claims that matter most to RAMRAT's differentiated investment thesis — the
copper-tube market's growth rate and import dependency, and whether copper-tube/HVAC demand
specifically is decelerating — are structurally untestable with this peer set, because neither
peer operates in copper tubes. That is itself an important finding: RAMRAT's most distinctive,
highest-conviction claims are precisely the ones peer triangulation cannot reach. On the general
copper-industry claims that these peers can speak to (pricing pass-through, capacity cycle), the
evidence is directionally supportive but adds nuance rather than blanket confirmation — pass-through
pricing is industry-standard but is not, on peer evidence, sufficient on its own to explain margin
resilience (mix shift and one-off effects matter at least as much at BHAGYANGR), and the capex
cycle underway is industry-wide rather than RAMRAT-specific, which shifts the read from "unique
execution edge" toward "riding a sector-wide wave with the attendant overcapacity risk peers
themselves are beginning to flag."

## PART 5: CROSS-PEER HYPOTHESIS

**Hypothesis**: The Indian copper-intermediate-products sector is undergoing a synchronised,
sector-wide race into the same narrow set of "value-added" product niches — bus bars for AI data
centers, CTC conductors for transformers, PV wire/ribbon for solar — rather than genuine strategic
differentiation by each player. BHAGYANGR (recycled-copper commodity player) and VIDYAWIRES
(enamelled-wire manufacturer) operate in different historical sub-segments with no direct product
overlap in their core business, yet both independently pivoted, in the same fiscal year, toward
almost identical growth vectors (data-center bus bars/silver-tin-plated bus bars for BHAGYANGR;
CTC and PV ribbon for VIDYAWIRES), each citing the same three macro drivers (AI, EV, green
energy) and each reporting materially higher EBITDA/tonne in these categories (5-12%+) versus
their legacy commodity lines (1-3%). Combined with BHAGYANGR management's own disclosure that
additional named entrants — Jain Resource Recycling (copper bus bars), Adani, and Hindalco
(copper smelting/recycling) — are moving into the same or adjacent value-added spaces
concurrently, the pattern suggests the "value-added premium" both companies are currently
enjoying is a temporary first-mover margin rather than a durable structural edge. Testable
prediction: EBITDA/tonne for these specific high-margin sub-categories (data-center bus bars, CTC
conductors, solar wire/ribbon) should compress measurably toward blended commodity-like levels
within 2-3 years as capacity from these newly-announced entrants comes online, even as overall
sector revenue keeps growing on volume. Neither BHAGYANGR nor VIDYAWIRES frames this compression
risk in its own disclosures (each describes its value-added pivot as a durable margin lever); the
risk only becomes visible when the two peers' independent VA narratives are read together against
BHAGYANGR's own list of new entrants.
