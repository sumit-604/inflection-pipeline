## THE CATALYST STORY IN PLAIN WORDS

**What could make margin fire.** Finolex spent about Rs 500cr building its own optical fibre preform plant and doubling fibre draw capacity from 4 to 8 million fibre km. The plant was commissioned in mid March 2026, and global fibre prices moved from under 3 dollars to around 5 dollars per fibre km as data centre and defence demand tightened supply. That combination showed up immediately: communication segment margin went from 1.19% to 29.76% in a year. Confirming evidence would be that margin holding above 15% for two straight quarters. What kills it is the germanium supply constraint management disclosed only in the Q4 FY26 call, with a few months of inventory and Chinese and European export restrictions on the other side, or the large domestic contract renegotiating badly when it comes up.

**What could make revenue fire.** Solar cable lines are at 80 to 85% use and the capacity doubling is funded inside the Rs 300cr FY27 capex. Auto and industrial flexible cables grew about 30% and 17% in volume. Exports tripled from a tiny base. Confirming evidence is Q2 and Q3 FY27 holding double digit growth on volume rather than copper price. What kills it is copper reversing, which would deflate reported revenue mechanically, and the arrival of UltraTech and Adani in the core wires business around CY2027.

**What could make cash fire, and why it probably will not this window.** The mechanical case is simple: a one time raw material pre buy consumed over April and May should release cash, and the business has no debt and Rs 4,195cr of investments, so nothing forces the working capital to stay stretched. Against that, there is no named programme, no target, no facility, the receivables half has never been discussed on a call, and the only new datapoint shows inventory building further. The honest read is that the catalyst is passive. It depends on management not repeating a decision rather than on management doing something.

**What could make ROCE fire.** Capital work in progress halved to Rs 105.20cr as the preform plant moved into service, and Q1 FY27 profit before tax rose 62.4%, so earnings are growing several times faster than the capital base. That alone lifts ROCE toward 17%. The catalyst that does not exist is the one that matters most: nothing in four transcripts, and nothing in the FY27 plan, deploys or returns the Rs 4,195cr investment book. Until that changes, every profitable year mechanically dilutes the consolidated return.

### THE EARNINGS BASIS QUESTION, FOR THE OPERATOR TO DECIDE

The multiple above has to be applied to something, and the choice is not mine to make.

- **One year forward P/E**, applying the multiple to FY27 or FY28 expected EPS. This fits a business in the middle of a visible recovery, where Q1 FY27 profit rose 59% and the communication segment margin inflected, and it avoids anchoring on a year distorted by a one off working capital shock.
- **Trailing P/E**, applying the multiple to FY26 audited EPS of Rs 46.67 consolidated. This is the more conservative choice and it refuses to capitalise a recovery that is one quarter old and still unconfirmed on cash.

The tension in one line: this is a copper pass through cyclical whose revenue and margin both swing with metal prices and channel stocking, so trailing avoids capitalising a price cycle peak while forward avoids capitalising a working capital trough. Both errors are real and they point in opposite directions.
