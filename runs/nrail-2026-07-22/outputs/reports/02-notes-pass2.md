## A. HEADLINE NEW FINDING: A second material non-recurring gain was not netted into Pass 1's "underlying PBT" calculation

Pass 1 (Red Flag #2) correctly identified that net investment-portfolio gains of ₹1,338.98L (Note 31, p.142; Cash Flow Statement, p.100) account for ~84% of FY25 reported PBT (₹1,595.62L), and estimated "underlying business PBT" at roughly ₹256.64L excluding this item.

That calculation missed a second, separately disclosed non-operating gain in the same note:

- **Profit on Sales of Assets (Net): ₹603.03L** (Note 31, "Other Non-operating income," p.142) — confirmed by the Cash Flow Statement adjustment "Loss/(profit) on sale of property, plant & equipment (603.03)" (p.100) and by "Proceeds from sale of property, plant and equipment ₹953.24L" (Cash Flow Statement, investing activities, p.100). No comparable gain existed in FY24 (Note 37 FY24 instead shows a **loss** on sale/discard of PPE of ₹259.47L, now Nil in FY25). The FY25 gain is materially attributable to the sale of the Unit III plant (carrying value ₹185.00L after the FY24 impairment, Note 50, p.161, "fully sold during FY25") plus routine PPE disposals (net book value of disposals per Note 2A, p.118 ≈ ₹139.5L against the same disposal pool).

**Combined effect**: Investment gains (₹1,338.98L) + profit on asset sales (₹603.03L) = **₹1,942.01L** of non-operating/non-recurring pre-tax gains, against a reported PBT of only ₹1,595.62L. **Stripping both items out implies the core paper-manufacturing business ran a pre-tax LOSS of approximately ₹(346.39)L in FY25** — not the modest positive residual (~₹256.64L) that Pass 1's partial netting suggested. 🔴 **Escalates Pass 1's Red Flag #2 from "profit is largely non-operating" to "core operations were loss-making before non-operating gains."**

Anchors: Note 31 (p.142), Cash Flow Statement (p.100), Statement of Profit and Loss (p.99), Note 37 FY24 comparative (p.144), Note 50 (p.161), Note 2A (p.118).

## B. NEW FINDING: Other Income grew +325.8% and is now almost entirely non-core

Total Other Income rose from ₹737.35L (FY24) to **₹3,139.66L (FY25), +325.8%** (Note 31, p.142; P&L, p.99). Decomposing the FY25 total: net investment fair-value gain ₹1,338.98L, profit on asset sales ₹603.03L, net FX transaction/translation gain ₹376.97L, interest income (including a new ₹306.45L interest-on-income-tax-refund line, itself a one-off tied to the current-year tax credit position) ~₹544.4L, and other non-operating income ₹276.30L. Essentially the entire increase in Other Income — now the single largest swing factor behind reported profitability — is non-recurring or non-core to paper manufacturing. 🔴 New synthesis point; strengthens the earnings-quality concern in A above. Anchor: Note 31 (p.142).

## C. NEW SUPPORTING DETAIL: PMS cash drawdown quantifies the scale of capital deployed into the investment book

Note 15 footnote (p.130, Other Current Assets): cash balance held with the Portfolio Management Service (PMS) fell from **₹1,319.61L (FY24) to ₹106.72L (FY25)**. This is granular evidence that roughly ₹1.2 Cr of previously-idle PMS cash was actively deployed into the equity/mutual-fund book during FY25, consistent with — and quantifying part of — the current-investment growth already flagged in Pass 1 (₹2,133.39L → ₹7,271.31L, +241%). 🟡 Watch, supports existing capital-allocation flag. Anchor: Note 15 (p.130).

## D. NEW CROSS-REFERENCE: KMP rent is ~41% of total short-term lease expense, up from ~29%

Pass 1 flagged rent paid to KMP rising +145.7% (₹1.80 Cr → ₹4.42 Cr, Note 40, p.146) as disproportionate to revenue growth. Cross-referencing against Note 48 (Ind AS 116 leases, p.160), "Rental expense recorded for short-term leases was ₹1,073.20 lakhs (F.Y. 2023-24: ₹617.76 lakhs)" — since this rent is not reflected in Right-of-use asset additions (Note 2C shows negligible building additions in FY25, p.119), the KMP rent almost certainly sits within this short-term-lease pool. On that basis, **KMP rent represents ~41.2% of total short-term lease expense in FY25 (₹442.20L / ₹1,073.20L), up from ~29.1% in FY24 (₹180.00L / ₹617.76L)**. This is a growing structural concentration of a P&L cost line in related-party hands, not merely a percentage-growth observation. 🟡 Watch, escalates existing RPT flag with a new anchor. Cross-anchor: Note 40 (p.146) + Note 48 (p.160).

## E. NEW CONTEXT (downgrades severity, not a new red flag): Natasha Agarwal's salary jump is likely a base-year annualization artifact

Pass 1 flagged the +273% YoY jump in salary to Natasha Agarwal (₹6.30L → ₹23.50L, Note 40, p.146) as a Watch item. AOC-2 (Annexure 5, p.60) discloses her place-of-profit appointment was approved/re-approved by the Board on **28.07.2017 and 22.01.2024**, at a stated rate of ₹2,00,000/month (= ₹24L/year). The FY25 figure (₹23.50L) is close to a full-year run-rate at this stated salary; the FY24 figure (₹6.30L) is consistent with roughly ~3 months at the confirmed rate following the 22.01.2024 board action, not a genuine within-year escalation. This context should temper — though not eliminate — the RPT scrutiny already flagged; the underlying monthly rate (₹2 lakh/month, disclosed and Board-approved) did not change between FY24 and FY25. 🟢/🟡 (nuance, forward to pass 3 as context rather than a fresh red flag). Anchor: Note 40 (p.146), Annexure 5 AOC-2 (p.60).

## F. NEW QUANTIFICATION: Working capital contracted despite revenue growth

Note 57's own ratio workings (p.162) disclose Working Capital (Total Current Assets − Total Current Liabilities) of **₹12,590.88L (FY25) vs ₹13,504.49L (FY24), a 6.8% decline** — even as revenue grew 28.27% and current assets grew substantially — because current liabilities (driven by the trade-payables/acceptances spike already flagged) grew faster than current assets. This is the Company's own disclosed figure corroborating the structural point in Pass 1 that the FY25 growth cycle was substantially payables-financed. 🟡 Watch, new supporting quantification. Anchor: Note 57 (p.162).

## H. MINOR DISCLOSURE-QUALITY OBSERVATIONS (pattern-pass candidates)

1. **Current Ratio explanation is internally inconsistent.** Note 57 (p.162) attributes the Current Ratio decline (1.53x → 1.33x, −13.41%) "Primarily on accounts of increase in trade Receivables in current assets and Increase in current maturities of long borrowings." An increase in trade receivables (a current asset) would mechanically improve, not worsen, the current ratio; the explanatory text appears to conflate an asset-side increase with the liability-side driver (current maturities of long-term debt, which did rise) that is the actual arithmetic cause. Minor drafting imprecision, not a numeric error in the ratio itself. 🟡

2. **Withdrawal-rate sensitivity footnote does not match the table.** Note 41.XI (p.151) shows a ₹46.03L movement in the gratuity obligation for a 1% change in the withdrawal-rate assumption in FY25, immediately followed by a footnote stating such movement "is negligible." A ₹46.03L swing (~3.8% of the ₹1,212.29L obligation) is not obviously negligible; the footnote language appears to be carried over from a prior year (FY24 showed a much smaller ₹1.27L/₹4.33L movement, where "negligible" was accurate) without being updated. Minor. 🟡

3. **Two different "EBITDA margin" percentages are presented in the same Annual Report without reconciliation.** The MD&A "Key Numbers" table and Chairman's letter both state FY25 EBITDA/Turnover of **8.59%** (turnover base = full Revenue from Operations, ₹1,65,903.17L). The Schedule V "Additional Information" table (Annexure to Notice, p.39) computes **8.70%** for the same year, using a narrower turnover base of ₹1,63,811.27L (Sale of Products only, excluding other operating revenue such as scrap sales, export incentives, and the "Profit on Sale of Raw Material" line already flagged in Pass 1). Both figures are internally consistent with their own denominators, but the Annual Report does not flag that two different "turnover" definitions are in use across its sections. Minor cross-document inconsistency worth noting for completeness. 🟡
