## SECTION 1: THE BUSINESS MODEL IN PLAIN ENGLISH

### 1A. One line
CNL is a technology-product middleman that imports and sells other companies' IT and consumer-tech brands across India (85.9% of FY26 sales), and is trying to shift toward selling brands it licenses or owns, mainly Honeywell-branded accessories made by a Hong Kong subsidiary (14.1% of sales but 59.1% of segment gross profit) (AR p.15, p.112; calc).

### 1E. Chai-stall-uncle version
Think of CNL as a big wholesale shop that stocks other companies' phone chargers, monitors and memory cards and sells them to thousands of small shops. The shop earns about Rs 4 on every Rs 100 it sells, so it only makes real money by selling a lot and not getting stuck waiting for payment. Now it has also taken a licence to put the famous Honeywell name on its own range of chargers and air purifiers. On those, it keeps about Rs 33 of every Rs 100 before costs, which is why it wants more of them. The catch is that last year it sold on credit so heavily that customers owed it 76 days of sales, and it borrowed from banks to bridge the gap. Also, half the profit now sits in a Hong Kong company, where the books are done by different auditors.

### 2D. Industry lifecycle
Technology distribution is a mature, low-margin, high-turnover industry; the AR frames the global IT market growth at 7.5% CAGR 2025-26 and 8.3% to 2030, and India tech revenue at about USD 315 billion with 6.1% growth (AR p.44). CNL's own growth (revenue +50.85%, AR p.44) far exceeds the market, which means share gain or credit-fuelled sales; see Section 3 and 4. Within the industry, CNL is attempting a move from the mature distribution stage into growth sub-segments (surveillance, which the deck sizes at USD 4.8 billion in 2025, a 20.60% CAGR to 2030, slide 21; data centres; IoT) and into brand ownership. These market sizes are third-party figures quoted by the company. Note that the AR text (USD 14.25 billion by 2031, 19.88%, AR p.44) and the deck (USD 12.25 billion by 2030, 20.60%, slide 21) quote different surveillance forecasts.
