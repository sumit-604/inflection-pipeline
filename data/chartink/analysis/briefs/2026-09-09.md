The previous brief covered Tuesday, 8 September. This one covers one new

## The picture in one paragraph

The market broke the level the last brief named. Only four of thirty-five
indices rose today, and one of the four was the fear index. The count of
stocks above their two-hundred-day line fell to 1,194, from 1,233 last night.
That is the first reading under 1,200 since 28 July, and 1,200 was the line
the last brief called the break point. The strong end thinned on the same day.
Eighty-four stocks closed at a one-year high, down from 110, and fresh
one-year lows rose by more than half. Yet the fast tail got fuller. Forty-two
stocks are now up more than twenty percent over five days, the most in a
month. This is a market whose floor is giving way while its fastest few
percent keep running. The middle keeps emptying. An index bet buys the
emptying middle.

## Where the market stands

The three layers by size have not changed shape, and the large end is still
the weak one. The Nifty closed at 23,431, down about nine tenths of a percent
on the day and about two percent on the week. It sits roughly three percent
below its thirty-week trend line. The mid-sized companies sit about two
percent above theirs, the small companies about seven percent above, and the
very small ones about nine percent above. The microcap index fell only a sixth
of a percent today, so the small end again lost less than the large one.

The day itself was the narrowest in a fortnight. Four indices of thirty-five
rose, against nineteen last night. Metals led, up nearly two percent, and
commodities and energy added small gains. Everything else fell. IT fell more
than three percent, the worst single index of the day. Realty fell more than
two, and financial services one and a quarter. Fifteen of thirty-five indices
now sit below their thirty-week line, and only seven of thirty-four rose over
the week.

The base broke. The count of stocks above their two-hundred-day line stands at
1,194 against 1,071 below. Last night the count was 1,233. The last brief said
the constructive view fails if that count breaks under about 1,200. It broke.
The count peaked at 1,372 on 7 August, so about 180 stocks have left the
long-term uptrend in a month. The last time this reading sat lower was 28
July, at 1,178. A little more than half the market is still in a long-term
uptrend, so this is a crack and not a collapse.

The short-term layers gave back part of their repair. Nine hundred and four
stocks sit above their twenty-day line against 1,361 below. That is four in
ten, and the count is down forty-five on the day. It bottomed at 803 on 2
September, so the repair is not dead. It has now fallen in three of the last
four sessions. The fifty-day count also fell, to 1,051 against 1,214 below.

The five-day tape rolled over hard. About thirty-seven stocks rose for every
sixty-two that fell over the last five sessions, against forty-six to
fifty-three last night. Today 104 stocks jumped four and a half percent or
more, and 58 fell that hard. Hard risers still outnumber hard fallers. The gap
closed by half in one session, and 58 is the most hard fallers since 17
August.

There has still been no thrust day. In this history a new leg announces itself
when three hundred or more stocks jump four and a half percent in a single
session. The last one was 12 June, with 346. Before that came 8 April with 896
and 1 April with 1,142, the pair that ended the March crash. The last washout
was 12 May, when 506 stocks fell that hard in a day. Three months have passed
with neither event. Tonight's break in the base did not come with panic. It
came with drift.

Fear rose from a cheap level and gold keeps disagreeing with stocks. The India
VIX closed at 11.92, up six percent on the day and nearly twelve percent on
the week. It still sits about thirteen percent below its own thirty-week
average, so insurance stays cheap even after the jump. Gold is up about eight
percent over the last month while the Nifty is down about two and a half. That
gap was about eight and a half points last night and is about ten now.
Turnover is about 1.1 times a year ago, and light-volume stocks outnumber
heavy-volume ones roughly two to one. Most of the market is standing still
while a short list runs and a growing list breaks.

## What would change this read

The constructive view has already failed one of its four tests. It fails
further if the count above the two-hundred-day line closes under about 1,150,
because that would take out the 28 July low and turn a crack into a downtrend.
It fails if the count above the twenty-day line rolls back under about 850,
giving up the repair that started on 2 September. It fails on any session
where hard fallers outnumber hard risers, which has not happened since May. It
also fails if healthcare or defence loses its short-term breadth lead.

The cautious view fails if a real thrust day arrives, with three hundred or
more stocks jumping four and a half percent in one session. It fails if the
two-hundred-day count climbs back above about 1,250 within a week, which would
mark tonight as one bad print rather than a break. It fails if more than half
the market, near 1,130 stocks, reclaims the twenty-day line while heavy-volume
names stop being outnumbered two to one. It also fails if the Nifty closes
back above its thirty-week line while banks stop leading the falls, because
the large end is the piece that broke the floor.
