### 2B. Excuse pattern analysis

For every miss above, the explanation given leans on **external factors**: monetary tightness (Q1), floods in Rajasthan/MP (Q2), heavy-commercial-vehicle industry-wide stress (Q2), a single counterparty's (Up Money) default on a secured DA pool (Q3-Q4), and generic "competitive intensity" (Q4). Management rarely uses language equivalent to "we made a mistake" or "we misjudged the pace"; misses are reframed as reasonable given circumstances, and forward guidance is quietly reset (branch counts, ROE target) rather than explicitly reconciled against the original figure. On the other hand, management does **not stonewall** — when pressed (e.g., on the Q4 DA-driven NIM spike, Aryan Ajmera's questioning, p.12) they eventually give the specific number (INR41cr pool, INR8.66cr upfront profit). Net classification: **external-blame-heavy**, with reasonable specificity once pressed but no proactive ownership of the original guidance miss.

## SECTION 3: COMPETITIVE INTELLIGENCE FROM CONCALLS

### 3A. Competitor commentary and credibility check

Management named HDFC and Five Star only indirectly, via an analyst's framing (Jehan Bhadha, Q2 FY26 call, p.11: "I have been attending calls of other companies which are in similar segment of INR5 to INR10 lakh ticket size, fully secured loans like HDFC and Five Star, so even they are witnessing some sort of stress in the asset quality"). Management's response agreed stress is sector-wide ("this is very true... in our segment also, in our geographical also, our balance sheet has shown a little bit, 30 bps as compared to last quarter") rather than disputing or minimizing it — credible in that it did not claim immunity from an industry-wide trend, but it also did not name specific competitive share gains or losses in any call.

### 3B. Industry/market intelligence dropped in the calls

- GST-regime change cited as boosting MSME customer purchasing capacity and credit demand (Q2 FY26 call, Kuldeep Sikarwar, p.6; reiterated Q3 Knowledge Session, p.9) — asserted, no data given.
- Heavy commercial-vehicle segment described as under industry-wide stress; LIFL says it has already exited heavy-CV funding in favor of private/multi-utility vehicles (Q2 FY26 call, Deepak Baid, p.5-6).
- Flood impact on Rajasthan and Madhya Pradesh rural operating areas cited as a Q2 FY26 (Sep-25 quarter) sector event affecting collections (Q2 FY26 call, p.5).
- Competitive intensity from NBFCs, HFCs and fintech lenders described as "increasing" but "healthy" across every call from Q2 onward; management claims a moat via non-income-proof (NIP) customer underwriting and in-house sourcing/collection (no third-party DSA dependence for MSME) — asserted repeatedly, not benchmarked against named peers.
- Up Money (Jalandhar-based NBFC, AUM INR500-600cr) DA-pool default described as affecting "30-35 lenders" industry-wide, not unique to LIFL (Q3 FY26 Knowledge Session, Deepak Baid, p.19-20) — a genuine systemic data point worth checking against peers.

### 3D. Customer and order-book signals

- Customer base expanded to >42,800 by FY26 close (Q4 FY26 call, Gopal Sain, p.3); 37% of borrowers are first-time borrowers (Q4 FY26 call, Kuldeep Singh, p.5).
- Ticket size held stable at INR7-8 lakh across Q2, Q3, and Q4 calls — management explicitly says this is a deliberate strategy, not drift.
- Geographic concentration: 82% of AUM in Rajasthan, 11% MP, 7% Gujarat as of Q4 FY26 (Deepak Baid, p.11) — barely changed from "80% mother state Rajasthan" disclosed in Q1 FY26, despite the addition of UP and Maharashtra as new states across the year.
- Heavy commercial vehicle exposure being deliberately wound down (Q2 FY26 call onward) — confirmed real product-mix shift, not just talk, since Q4 vehicle portfolio is described as "very minuscule" (9%, Q4 FY26 call, Piyush Somani, p.16).
- Company declined, when asked twice (Q2 FY26 and again implicitly Q3 FY26 Knowledge Session), to enter gold loans or affordable housing — a consistent, non-evasive "no" both times, in contrast to the deflections noted in 2E.

### 4B. Questions for peer verification (feeds Stage 6 — peers ARMANFIN, MANBA, MONEYBOXX, UGROCAP)

1. **Question:** Did peers experience similar Q2 FY26 (Sep-25 quarter) asset-quality deterioration attributed to Rajasthan/MP floods and heavy-commercial-vehicle stress, and how did their GNPA trend across Q1-Q4 FY26 compare to LIFL's 1.28%→2.13%? **Why it matters:** tests whether LIFL's asset-quality miss is sector-wide (mitigating) or company-specific (aggravating). **Check peers:** ARMANFIN, MANBA, MONEYBOXX, UGROCAP.
2. **Question:** How dependent is each peer's quarterly PAT/NIM on gain-on-sale from DA/securitization transactions — did any peer recognize a single-transaction upfront profit comparable in scale to LIFL's INR8.66cr from one INR41cr DA pool in Q4 FY26? **Why it matters:** directly tests the upstream B02 concern that DA/ARC gain-on-sale materially drives reported PAT quality. **Check peers:** all four, especially UGROCAP and MONEYBOXX given similar MSME/secured-lending models.
3. **Question:** Did any peer have direct or indirect counterparty exposure to Up Money (Jalandhar-based NBFC) or a comparable DA-counterparty default — management's Q3 FY26 disclosure states "30-35 lenders" industry-wide were affected? **Why it matters:** establishes whether this is an idiosyncratic risk-management failure at LIFL or a genuine sector event. **Check peers:** all four.
4. **Question:** What credit-rating notch movement did peers see following their most recent capital raise/IPO, versus LIFL's one-notch move (Acuite A-/Positive to A/Stable) against a management-promised "two-notch" upgrade? **Why it matters:** benchmarks whether LIFL's guidance miss reflects overpromising specific to LIFL or a realistic/typical outcome for the peer set. **Check peers:** ARMANFIN, MANBA (both recent-IPO peers most comparable).
5. **Question:** What cost-of-borrowing reduction did peers realize in the 12 months following their most recent capital infusion, versus LIFL's realized -68bps against a guided -100 to -125bps? **Why it matters:** tests whether LIFL's guidance was unrealistic from the outset or execution fell short of a reasonable peer benchmark. **Check peers:** all four.
6. **Question:** Is the GST-regime tailwind and rural-liquidity improvement management cites (boosting MSME purchasing capacity and credit demand) corroborated independently in peer concalls, or is it LIFL-specific commentary not echoed elsewhere? **Why it matters:** any industry growth rate or macro tailwind claimed by management must be independently checked per pipeline rules. **Check peers:** all four.
7. **Question:** What per-branch AUM levels, cost-to-income ratios, and branch breakeven timelines (LIFL cites 7-8 months to breakeven at INR1.5-2cr branch AUM, current productivity ~INR8.5cr/branch) do peers report? **Why it matters:** LIFL was unable to give a specific long-term AUM/branch target despite being asked twice (see 2E); a peer benchmark would supply the missing number. **Check peers:** MANBA, MONEYBOXX (closer branch-count scale to LIFL).
