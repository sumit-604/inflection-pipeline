### 2B. Excuse Pattern Analysis

For the two clear misses (#3 interest cost, #4 debtor days), management did **not** externalize blame — both were attributed to the company's own deliberate strategic choices (elevated inventory positioning; matching distributor credit terms). The CFO gave exact rupee figures for the interest-cost overshoot unprompted in the Q3 FY26 answer to Pritesh ("as against what we had estimated it is higher by about INR4-INR4.5 crore in this quarter") — a specific, quantified, honest admission rather than a vague deflection. The debtor-days slippage was disclosed only reactively (in response to a direct analyst question in Q4 FY26), not proactively flagged in opening remarks — a mild deflection-by-omission pattern, though the answer itself was direct once asked.

Pattern check: management does credit itself heavily for the memory-price tailwind (external, macro-driven) while owning the misses as internal strategic trade-offs — this is actually the *opposite* of the classic external-blame pattern (they don't blame the memory shortage for cost overruns; if anything they call the elevated inventory a deliberate bet). The one recurring soft dodge is the "we like to give conservative estimates" refrain used whenever analysts push on whether guidance is too low (Q2 FY26 re: FY27, Q3 FY26 re: growth acceleration and distributor upside, Q4 FY26 re: FY27 guide) — a repeated deflection pattern on a specific topic (guidance conservatism), even if not a factual misstatement.

**excuse_pattern: honest** (leaning honest-admission on hard misses; mild recurring deflection only on "why is guidance conservative").

## SECTION 3: COMPETITIVE INTELLIGENCE FROM CONCALLS

### 3A. What Management Says About Competitors

Management consistently minimizes competitive intensity: "We are not encountering any competition as such, but yes, there is regional competition" (Q2 FY26, Sharad Khandelwal, responding to Abhi Mevawala on supplier-pricing competition). No competitor — refurbisher, OEM buyback program, or informal-market player — is ever named in any of the three calls. Credibility check: this is a low-specificity, self-serving framing; the refurbished-ICT space is known to include large fragmented informal/unorganized players (management itself cites "20-25% of global PC shipments... not covered by top five brands" as an *unorganized-market* opportunity in Q3 FY26, implicitly acknowledging a large but unnamed competitive base it does not otherwise discuss). Treat competitive-moat claims as management assertion, not verified fact.

### 3B. Industry & Market Intelligence Dropped in the Calls

- IDC global new-PC shipment data: ~284.7-285mn units in 2025; 2026 forecast decline revised from -8.9% (Q3 FY26) to -11% (Q4 FY26), implying a ~55-60mn unit demand gap versus a "normal" AI-PC growth year (Q4 FY26, Sharad Khandelwal).
- Memory/component price trajectory (repeated, escalating disclosure): DDR5 8GB $23.35 (Oct-1-25) → $86.61 (Jan-1-26, Q3 FY26 call) → ~$120 (Apr-1-26, Q4 FY26 call); DDR5 16GB $54.85 → $189.99 → ~$210; 1TB SSD $70 → $180 → $249; high-end processor $165 → $240, lead times stretching to 6 months (Q4 FY26).
- New-PC retail price inflation: entry-level India laptop Rs25,500 (Dec-1-25) → ~Rs40,000 (by Apr-2026, +57% in 6 months); comparable international laptop $315 → $440 (+40% in 6 months) (Q4 FY26, Sharad Khandelwal).
- Cross-industry analogy: used-car sales exceed new-car sales 2x in mature auto markets; refurbished smartphones have moved from niche to meaningful share (Q3 FY26, Sharad Khandelwal) — used to argue refurbished-PC penetration will follow a similar curve; directionally plausible but not GNG-specific evidence.
- Regulatory/sustainability tailwinds: right-to-repair frameworks, COP28 climate agenda cited as structural demand drivers (Q2 FY26) — not revisited with specifics thereafter.
- Management's own view on price-cycle timing: memory prices "not going to come back to shape" before Jan-2028, and even then "not... to what it used to be" in Nov-2025 (Q4 FY26, Sharad Khandelwal) — a long-duration bullish call embedded as base case, unverified against independent forecasts.

### 3D. Customer & Order Book Signals
