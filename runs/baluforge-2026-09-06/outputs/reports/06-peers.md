Balu Forge holds no earnings calls. Every subject-side claim tested below comes from four

## PART 1: CLAIM-BY-CLAIM VERIFICATION

### Claim 1 (the priority question)

**Claim (verbatim, Concall PPT May 2026 Chairman's letter p.6 / Key Updates p.7, per Stage
5):** Q4 FY26 (Jan-Mar 2026) revenue decline (-2.3% YoY) is attributed to "geopolitical
developments in the Middle East... disrupted global logistics and temporarily impacted the
Company's India-UAE operations." This was offered only after 9M FY26 growth (29.0%) had
already run below the 40-45% guidance floor.

**Verdict: CONTRADICTED** (on the revenue-decline mechanism; the underlying event itself is
independently corroborated)

**Peer evidence:**
- RKFORGE, Q4 FY26 concall (May 2026), p.3: "the outbreak of conflict in Middle East
  adversely impacted operating conditions towards the latter part of the quarter. As a
  result, the energy price volatility, persistent inflationary pressures, and renewed supply
  chain disruptions created uncertainty." This is the clearest independent confirmation in
  the whole corpus that a real, named, dateable Middle East conflict existed in Q4 FY26.
- RKFORGE, same call, p.4: in that same quarter, consolidated revenue was Rs.1,216.78cr,
  **up 28% YoY**, EBITDA up 111% YoY — the company's best quarter of the year, not a
  decline.
- RKFORGE, same call, p.19 (Saket Saurabh Q&A): "Do we have any West Asia exposure in
  terms of clientele or revenue?" — "No, no revenue and no exposure." RKFORGE felt the
  conflict entirely through energy and shipping COSTS, with zero direct India-Middle East
  trade-lane exposure, and still grew revenue 28%.
- RKFORGE, Q1 FY27 concall (Jul 2026), p.3: "the resurgence of the West Asia conflict
  remains an overhang," continuing into Apr-Jun 2026. p.11: "Only risk, which we are
  looking at is the geopolitical issues, which is leading to shipping delays... working
  capital pressure, as well as the prices of the energy." Revenue that quarter: Rs.1,217cr,
  **up 19.84% YoY**. Growth, not decline, in the quarter immediately following.
- MMFL, investor-conference transcript dated 5 Mar 2026 (mid-Q4 FY26), p.3: "we also see,
  recently, in the last few days, availability of fuels, fossil fuels coming into question...
  that could be a challenge if the Hormuz strait[sic], blockade continues on... Iran being
  one of the top few suppliers in the world." Same call: "fourth quarter seems to be
  strong... US market is very strong... Class 8 truck orders have shot through the roof in
  February." A second peer independently names the same Middle East/Hormuz risk in the
  same quarter, while simultaneously describing STRONG, not weak, demand.
- MMFL, Q1 FY27 concall (Aug 2026), p.7: "There has been a huge increase in fuel cost in Q1
  on account of the West Asian conflict. That has been, of course, over after Q1, it has
  reasonably stabilized." p.12: "freights going around the Strait of Hormuz" added to other
  expenses. MMFL's Q1 FY27 revenue: Rs.427cr vs Rs.369cr, **up ~16% YoY** — again growth,
  not decline, in the very quarter the cost hit landed.
- HAPPYFORGE, Q4 FY26 concall (May 2026), p.4: "In recent months, we have seen some
  increases in raw material prices and in other manufacturing costs due to ongoing U.S.
  Iran issue." Same call, p.6: Q4 FY26 revenue Rs.424cr, **up 20.4% YoY**, the company's
  best-ever quarterly performance across every metric.
- HAPPYFORGE, Q1 FY27 concall (Aug 2026), p.4: "On the export front, growth was impacted
  by transit delays due to geopolitical conditions, resulting in higher inventory in transit
  and lower sales conversion during the quarter" (CV segment). p.7 (Mihir Vora Q&A): CV
  export de-grew ~12% because contracts are DDP and "because of the geopolitical
  condition... almost a month of deliveries were pending." This is the one instance across
  all three peers where a geopolitical/logistics disruption is tied to an actual REVENUE
  effect — but it lands in Q1 FY27 (Apr-Jun 2026), a full quarter AFTER Balu Forge's claimed
  Q4 FY26 (Jan-Mar 2026) disruption window, and it is confined to one segment (CV exports),
  not the whole company (HappyForge's total Q1 FY27 revenue still grew 27% YoY).

**Peers silent:** None of the three peers' Q2/H1 FY26 (Nov 2025) or Q3/9M FY26 (Feb 2026)
calls mention any Middle East disruption at all; where those calls cite geopolitics, it is
US tariffs, Section 232, Russia/Ukraine-adjacent customer softness, or generic "geopolitical
tensions," never the Middle East specifically. The Middle East/West Asia/Hormuz language
appears in the peer set for the first time only in transcripts covering Q4 FY26 (Jan-Mar
2026) onward — consistent with the event's real timing, inconsistent with it explaining a
9M shortfall that predates it.

**Net read:** The underlying event is real. Two independent peers (RKFORGE, MMFL), neither
of which shares Balu Forge's excuse-writer, name a Middle East/West Asia conflict and a
Hormuz Strait-adjacent energy/shipping risk in the identical Jan-Mar 2026 to Apr-Jun 2026
window, unprompted. That much corroborates Balu Forge's story is not invented from nothing.
But every peer that names the event describes it as a COST-side shock (energy, fuel,
freight, shipping days, working capital), never as a REVENUE-side logistics disruption large
enough to cause a quarterly decline — and all three peers posted their strongest or
near-strongest quarterly revenue growth of FY26 in the exact quarter Balu Forge declined.
RKFORGE, which has zero West Asia client or revenue exposure by its own account, still felt
the cost shock and still grew 28%. The one peer data point that DOES tie a geopolitical
event to an actual revenue effect (HappyForge's CV export transit delays) lands a full
quarter later than Balu Forge's claimed window and is confined to one segment. Per the
Stage 5 test ("if peers report normal or strong demand, it is company-specific"): peers
report strong demand in the same window. The Q4 FY26 revenue decline (-2.3% YoY) is not
explained by an industry-wide Middle East demand shock; the credibility grade of C does not
improve on this evidence and the missing 9M-shortfall explanation (Stage 5's separate,
larger finding) is untouched by anything peers say.

### Claim 2: crankshaft/machined-forging export realisation and pricing, FY26 Q3-Q4

**Verdict: PARTIALLY VERIFIED**

**Peer evidence:**
- HAPPYFORGE, Nov 2025 concall, p.5-6: realisation held at Rs.251/kg despite raw material
  falling ~Rs.10/kg, gross margin up ~150bps to 60.3%, "product mix... higher share of
  value-added machining of around 88%." Q3/Q4 FY26 calls confirm realisation stayed near
  Rs.245-253/kg through the year with EBITDA margin above 30% for four consecutive
  quarters (May 2026 p.6, Aug 2026 p.3).
- MMFL, Nov 2025 concall, p.3-4: sales-per-ton up from Rs.1.8 lakh to Rs.1.93 lakh H1 FY26,
  attributed to higher value-add within machined parts even as machined-parts VOLUME fell.
  Aug 2026 concall, p.3: sales per ton further up to Rs.2.02 lakh; gross margin up 350bps
  sequentially on better realisation and mix.
- RKFORGE, Feb 2026 concall (Aditya Kumar Q&A): standalone gross margin dipped to 45% on
  product mix and rejections, "hopefully... back to normal level next quarter"; Q1 FY27
  concall p.9-10: gross margin recovered sharply (+535bps QoQ) on better product mix, though
  management stresses the EBITDA improvement lagged because non-steel costs (energy,
  shipping) were not fully passed on.

**Peers silent:** none on this specific question; all three discuss realisation/mix
directly.

**Net read:** Direction matches Balu Forge's claim — margin resilience through a
value-added/mix shift is a genuine, sector-wide FY26 pattern, corroborated by all three
peers independently. Magnitude and mechanism do not fully match: peers show this as a
gradual, multi-quarter grind with visible sequential volatility (MMFL's and RKFORGE's
margins both dipped before recovering), not the flat ~27% EBITDA margin Balu Forge claims
held steady straight through a revenue slowdown. The peer pattern makes the DIRECTION of
Balu Forge's margin-resilience claim plausible, not its specific STEADINESS.

## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

### 2A Demand environment

Consensus across all three peers: H1 FY26 (Apr-Sep 2025) was weak on exports (US tariff
uncertainty, Class VIII/farm destocking), domestic was resilient throughout, and from
Oct-Dec 2025 onward (post GST-rate cut) the whole sector describes a broad-based domestic
and eventually export recovery running into Q1 FY27. No peer describes an industry-wide
demand slowdown coinciding with Balu Forge's Q4 FY26 decline; if anything, the peer set was
accelerating exactly then (RKFORGE Q4 FY26 +28% YoY; HappyForge best-ever quarter).

### 2B Pricing and input costs

Steel/raw-material pass-through (with a lag) is universal and matches Balu Forge's framing.
The genuinely new information from peers is the non-steel cost channel: energy, gas, and
freight/shipping costs rose sharply and were NOT fully pass-through in Q4 FY26/Q1 FY27,
tied explicitly by two peers to the Middle East/West Asia conflict and Hormuz Strait risk.
Balu Forge's own materials (per Stage 5) show a flat raw-material ratio through the
slowdown but never discuss this cost channel — worth flagging to synthesis as an
undisclosed exposure, whichever way it cuts.

### 2C Capex cycle

Industry-wide capacity race, not a lone expander. HappyForge (Rs.650cr+ multi-phase
program, now guiding Rs.800cr for the next two years), MMFL (Rs.1,260cr program, 16,500-ton
press), and RKFORGE (Rs.800cr+ program plus a rail-wheel JV and aluminium/titanium forging)
are all simultaneously expanding and diversifying away from pure-CV/export exposure into
industrial, passenger vehicle, energy/data-centre, and (for RKFORGE) railway segments.
Balu Forge's parallel diversification narrative (defence/aerospace/railway) sits inside a
genuine sector-wide capex and diversification wave — the WHICH direction differs (peers
lean railway/industrial/PV; Balu Forge claims defence specifically) but the FACT of a
capacity race is corroborated.

### 2D Competitive mentions

No peer names Balu Forge or gives an unmistakable description of it. Notably, MMFL's
management, asked directly to name domestic competitors (Mar 2026 investor conference),
answered: "our competition would be Bharat [Forge], Ram Krishna, and Happy Forge" —
naming two of the three peers in this set but not Balu Forge, despite Balu Forge's own
claimed FY26 revenue scale (~Rs.1,107cr) sitting between MMFL's and HappyForge's. This
silence does not prove or disprove anything about Balu Forge's business but is worth
carrying to synthesis: a company of the claimed scale, in an overlapping product space
(forgings serving CV/auto and diversifying toward defence), is not on its most comparable
peer's own competitor list.

### 2E Risks peers discuss that Balu Forge does not

- A live, continuing Middle East/West Asia conflict risk to energy and shipping costs,
  explicitly still "an overhang" as of Jul 2026 (RKFORGE) — framed by peers as ongoing, by
  Balu Forge (per Stage 5) as a closed, one-quarter-only past event.
- Currency/forex hedging policy and realised hedging losses (HappyForge, RKFORGE both
  discuss long-term hedges and booked forex losses); Balu Forge's materials do not disclose
  a hedging policy per Stage 5.
- US Section 232 tariff exposure and evolving trade-deal fine print, discussed at length by
  all three peers; not addressed in Balu Forge's available decks (though its export
  geography — 25 OEMs, 80+ countries, Europe/Middle East emphasis — may differ from the
  US-heavy peer set).
- Manpower/labour availability constraints affecting production ramp (MMFL, RKFORGE, Q1
  FY27) — not discussed by Balu Forge.
- Proactive ECL/receivables provisioning against a company's OWN prudential judgment
  (RKFORGE books a discretionary Rs.42cr ECL provision in Q4 FY26 "on a prudent basis
  considering the current economic scenario," May 2026 p.16) — a contrast worth noting
  against Stage 3/5's flagged concern about Balu Forge's ECL cut against a rising overdue
  receivables bucket in the same period.

## PART 4: TRIANGULATION SUMMARY

- Claims verified: 0 of 6
- Claims contradicted: 2 of 6 (Claim 1, the Middle East revenue-decline excuse; Claim 4,
  demand-momentum framing for Oct-Dec 2025/Jan-Mar 2026)
- Partially verified: 3 of 6 (Claim 2, margin resilience via mix; Claim 5, raw-material
  pass-through; Claim 6, capacity-ramp calibration)
- Unverifiable: 1 of 6 (Claim 3, defence/aerospace order-book shift — informative peer
  silence, not evidence either way)

**Single most consequential contradiction:** The Q4 FY26 "Middle East geopolitical
disruption" excuse. Two peers independently confirm a real Middle East/West Asia conflict
hit the sector's energy and shipping costs in the identical window — but every peer that
names it describes a cost-side hit, not a revenue-side one, and all three peers posted
their strongest quarterly revenue growth of the year in that same window (RKFORGE +28% YoY
with explicitly zero West Asia exposure). The specific claim that this event explains Balu
Forge's revenue decline is not supported; the event is real, its use as an excuse for a
revenue miss is not.

**Strongest independent confirmation:** The margin-resilience-through-mix narrative
(Claim 2). All three peers, unprompted, describe the same FY26 pattern — value-added
product mix offsetting falling raw-material prices to hold or improve gross/EBITDA margins
— which is directionally consistent with, and lends plausibility to, Balu Forge's own
27%-EBITDA-through-the-slowdown claim, even though peer volatility (dips and recoveries)
does not match the flat steadiness Balu Forge describes.

**Overall:** The peer set undercuts the company's central credibility defence more than it
supports it. It corroborates that a Middle East event was real, but strips away its use as
a revenue explanation, since peers with comparable or larger export exposure grew through
the identical quarter and describe the event only as a cost item. It also shows the
sector's demand backdrop improving, not weakening, in the exact months Balu Forge
decelerated. Against that, the peer set does lend some plausibility to Balu Forge's
mix-driven margin story and confirms that multi-year capacity ramps are normal for the
sector, which cuts the other way, tempering (not resolving) the unbridged capacity-tonnage
concern from Stage 5. On balance: the peer evidence complicates the picture on capacity
timing but undercuts it sharply on the one claim Stage 5 flagged as MAJOR.

## PART 5: CROSS-PEER HYPOTHESIS

All three peers, independently and without prompting, describe the identical pattern for
Jan-Mar 2026 through Apr-Jun 2026: a real, named Middle East/West Asia conflict raised
energy, fuel, and freight costs sector-wide, while each peer simultaneously posted its
strongest or near-strongest revenue quarter of the year, driven by a domestic GST-cut demand
recovery and a US Class 8 truck rebound that outran the cost shock. None of the three states
this combination as a general rule; each only describes its own quarter.

**Hypothesis:** the Indian forging sector in this window experienced a genuine but purely
COST-side Middle East shock that was fully absorbed by a simultaneous, larger, DEMAND-side
domestic recovery — meaning any company in this sector whose narrative pins a REVENUE
decline (not a margin/cost squeeze) on the same Middle East event either (a) carries a
distinct, concentrated trade-lane exposure (e.g., an actual India-UAE logistics
dependency) that none of these three peers has, or (b) is using a real, industry-wide cost
event to explain away a demand-side shortfall that is specific to itself. This is testable:
if Balu Forge's own FY26 cost lines (fuel, power, freight) do NOT show the same input-cost
spike every peer independently reports for the identical quarters, the "logistics
disruption" explanation for its revenue decline loses its last remaining support, since the
one channel by which this event demonstrably hit the sector (costs) would then be absent
from the company that claims to have been hit hardest (revenue).
