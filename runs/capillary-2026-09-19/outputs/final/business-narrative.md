# CAPILLARY business narrative (phase 3, finalize, 10 Oct 2026)

## Business understanding narrative

Capillary sells a cloud platform that runs loyalty programmes for large consumer brands, and subscriptions made 89% of FY26 revenue. The core product, Loyalty+, is the points ledger itself: every point a shopper earns, spends or lets expire sits in Capillary's system, audited like a financial ledger. Engage+, Insights+, Rewards+ and the aiRA AI layer sit on top, and the brand pays per member or per transaction, not per user seat. A brand cannot drop it quickly, because the ledger holds live member balances and an average customer runs about nine integrations into its tills, online stores and data systems. Installation fees, about 10% of revenue, pay once to connect a new or migrated customer, and campaign services, under 1%, run as agent. The buyers are about 415 brands in 49 countries, around 20 of them Fortune 500 names, across retail (under a third of revenue), banking, telecom, travel, hospitality and healthcare. They sign contracts of three to seven years, and buying is concentrated: the top 10 customers made 58.71% of FY25 revenue, the FY26 figure is undisclosed, and the United States supplied about 56% of FY26 revenue. Demand exists because brands move money into keeping customers, and the company's cited industry data says a new customer costs five times as much to win as an old one costs to keep. The run tracks present demand through "Fortune 500/large-enterprise new-logo referrals via System Integrators and Consulting Partners", which brought about 40% of FY26 new customers, and through "US enterprise MarTech/customer-engagement software budget growth". It also tracks "Named large/flagship end-customer renewal and expansion decisions", because one large renewal moves retention and revenue at this level of concentration. Growth should come from software taking share in a loyalty market that management says is still over 90% agency spend, a claim no peer transcript could verify. It should also come from buying and migrating fragmented agency rivals, and from privacy rules, tracked as "Data-privacy regulatory activity (GDPR enforcement, US state privacy laws, India DPDP Rules rollout)", that push brands toward owned customer data. Two outside signals cut both ways: "Enterprise generative-AI/agentic-AI adoption rate among marketing organisations" can lift aiRA or let brands build their own tools, and "USD/INR and other cross-currency rates" added about 6% to Q1 FY27 growth. The subscription line holds the one clear moat: switching costs from the owned ledger and the integrations, rated high durability. The emerging moat scan landed in the MODEST band, carried by two rows: embedded customer relationships and the acquisition rollup. The rollup row fails the sacrifice test, because a well funded rival could run the same rollup without giving anything up, so it is an execution lead, not a barrier. The security certifications are table stakes, aiRA's usage pricing is an untested counter to seat priced rivals, and the scan scored talent asymmetry and the cannibalisation barrier (Categories 21 and 22) at zero. The installation and campaign services lines have no moat.

Sticky loyalty ledger, price ahead of value

Capillary keeps the loyalty points ledger for about 415 brands and bills almost all of it as subscription. FY26 revenue was Rs 735 Cr, up 23%. Q1 FY27 revenue was Rs 257 Cr, up 43%, because SessionM joined on 1 May 2026. Acquired books made about 40% of Q1 FY27 revenue. The company listed on 21 Nov 2025 and lost money every year from FY21 to FY24.

The transition is a rollup climbing toward platform margins. Capillary buys small loyalty firms cheaply and moves their customers onto its own platform. Three of five earlier deals reached 40 to 45% contribution margin. The two deals that carry the next three years, Kognitiv and SessionM, are unproven. Kognitiv's seller already refunded Rs 25 Cr because customers left above the agreed limit. The company's own deck shows a 68% revenue retention record on acquired books. The proof gate has not fired. It needs H1 FY27 organic growth of at least 15% in constant currency and operating ROCE above 10% annualised in Q2 FY27, both due around November 2026. Organic growth has three live readings: 17% claimed, about 11% in constant currency, and lower on plan arithmetic. The company also counts migrated acquired customers as organic, so the gate metric is not like for like.

The backward record is weak and the forward record is three quarters old. Gate 0 scores 66 to 68 of 160, graded AVERAGE, with three deal breakers from the loss years. Operating ROCE on the operator's basis was 1.5% in FY25, 3.9% in FY26 and 6.8% on a trailing basis, and the Q1 FY27 run rate annualises to 13.6%. Adjusted EBITDA margin rose from 14.6% in FY26 to 17.2% in Q1 FY27. FY26 profit of Rs 52 Cr includes the Rs 25 Cr Kognitiv refund. Q1 FY27 shows a Rs 9.6 Cr loss after a Rs 33 Cr fraud at the SessionM Czech unit. Operating cash flow swung from minus Rs 46 Cr in FY25 to plus Rs 150 Cr in FY26. The operator ruled the cash pattern growth induced, driven by upfront billing. Against that, customer prepayments fell from 24% of revenue in FY24 to 13% in FY26. Net cash is about Rs 57 a share.

Management delivers on direction and stays loose on numbers. The operator graded delivery B; a verifier reads B minus to C plus. FY27 guidance held and was sharpened to "definitely beat" Rs 1,065 Cr. Against that, aiRA sizing, the SessionM base and platform gross margin all moved between calls without comment. SessionM's price appears five ways, from USD 20 m to about Rs 17 Cr net. The KPMG forensic report on the fraud was due in August and is not filed. The Head of Finance and the Head of Corporate Development both left in the fraud window. The promoter vehicle sold 4.14% in August 2026 to fund a PE exit, and the founder did not sell.

Peers confirm the cost engine and not the market story. NEWGEN and INTELLECT both grow costs well below revenue, the same mechanism behind Capillary's margin climb. No peer supports the claim that software is under 10% of loyalty spend, or that 110% net retention is top decile. UNIECOM reports retention above 100% only after leaving out one top 10 client, the same adjustment Capillary uses. The peer stage found no outright contradiction. The live peer table for valuation is still pending: one peer, RateGain, is held at 42 times trailing earnings.

The market is large on the right base, and the company's own plans are smaller than the run's base case. The software only pool is Rs 8,550 to 11,115 Cr, growing about 12% a year, and Capillary holds about 12.6% of the serviceable slice. The obtainable market reaches Rs 1,361 Cr in three years, a STRONG runway. Management's USD 18.2 bn figure counts all loyalty spend and runs 18 times the software pool. Management's own May 2026 outlook builds FY30 revenue near Rs 1,323 Cr, below the approved bear case of Rs 1,358 Cr. Its FY30 EBITDA of Rs 372 to 412 Cr sits above the base. The company's audited goodwill test assumes 4 to 10% annual growth.

The valuation says the price is about 21 months ahead of value. Run rate operating EPS is Rs 6.96, so the stock trades at 81 times. The approved base case grows EPS to Rs 21.3 by FY30. At the operator's 30 times exit, that gives Rs 697 by end FY29, 7% a year from Rs 567. Weighted with the DCF it gives Rs 640, 4% a year, and the expected return across cases is 1.3% a year. The framework's own pillar build gives 16.8 times on the additive track and 13.1 times on the risk track, worth Rs 415 and Rs 336. The DCF lands at Rs 413, about 17 times. Upside to downside is 0.24 against the 2 required, and the hurdle test sits in the STOP band. The recognition gap is closed, so the matrix cell is PRICED NARRATIVE (TRAP). The entry zone is Rs 291 to Rs 328, with a first small starter gate at Rs 415. Valuation says WATCHLIST and the thesis says AVOID; the operator must rule between them. Neither reading takes a position at Rs 567.

The thesis and the devil's advocate agree on no position and differ on what would change it. The thesis waits for a price in the zone and a fired proof gate, then a small starter only. The devil's advocate rates the thesis WEAKENED BUT ALIVE. The switching cost moat survives. Growth, management trust and valuation safety all weaken. The core organic engine runs near 11 to 13% once the healthcare account, currency and migrations are removed. The zone returns 25 to 30% a year only at 30 times. At the pillar 16.8 times, the same zone returns 8 to 13% a year.
