# SHHARICH — Shree Hari Chemicals Export Ltd
Run: runs/shharich-2026-09-05 (phase 1, evidence stages only)

## BUSINESS UNDERSTANDING NARRATIVE

Shree Hari Chemicals Export converts basic chemicals into H Acid, the bulk intermediate that dye makers turn into reactive and acid dyes for colouring textiles. Manufactured H Acid and other manufactured chemicals were 78.5 percent of FY26 revenue. A dye plant either buys this molecule or stops running, because it is the standard building block for that dye family. A second line, 18.2 percent of FY26 revenue, is finished H Acid the company buys and resells without processing at a gross margin near 1 percent, which serves a buyer's supply need rather than any chemistry of its own. Neither line is hard to replace. H Acid is a globally traded commodity, the company names no patent, no exclusive input and no customer specific specification, and its own management report names price competition from Chinese makers as a threat.

The buyers are dye and textile chemical manufacturers in India and in four export countries the annual report does not name. The report discloses no customer name, no concentration share and no long term supply contract, so qualification cycles and switching costs are not established by this run. Export sales moved from zero in FY25 to Rs 86 crore, 46.4 percent of FY26 revenue, in one year, with no customer level explanation anywhere in the document.

Present demand comes from textile dyeing volumes downstream, and this run can watch it only through external series, because the company discloses none. The named candidates are DGCIS monthly foreign trade statistics for India H Acid export and import volumes, CHEMEXCIL dye and dye intermediates export bulletins, and the production and sourcing disclosures of Indian reactive, acid and direct dye manufacturers. The realisation the company earns is set outside it, and the ChemAnalyst global and China H Acid spot price index is the direct read on that ceiling and floor.

Two forward drivers are visible. Peer call evidence records that China's VAT refund withdrawal made Chinese H Acid exports uncompetitive, and that India has been net exporting H Acid to China since the middle of 2025, a flow that DGCIS data and the DGTR anti dumping case list both track. Against that, the market itself grows slowly: this run's sizing puts demand growth at 3.6 percent a year and the company's own achievable share path at about 5 percent revenue growth a year over three to five years. US tariff policy on Indian chemical and textile exports is the third named external series, and it carries more weight because the four export destinations are undisclosed.

Competitive advantage sits nowhere this run could evidence. On the manufactured line the screen confirmed 5 of 12 moat tests and failed cost position, scale and brand against Bodal Chemicals and Shree Pushkar Chemicals. The one test at full marks was switching costs, on the thin proxy that revenue grew while receivable days fell. The traded line has no moat by construction: buying and reselling at a 1 percent spread carries no advantage that survives a supplier selling direct. The forward scan of 22 emerging moat categories touched 6 of them and scored 8 points out of 92, with only captive solar power of 3.35 MW reaching moderate strength, and the year's research and development expense is reported as nil.

## Revenue grew 31 percent, profit fell 18

Shree Hari makes one bulk chemical at one plant at Mahad in Raigad, Maharashtra, and has done so since 1987. It sells H Acid factory to factory, to dye makers in India and four unnamed export markets. Raw materials are 68 percent of revenue and power is another 11 percent, so the business is a spread: the H Acid price it gets, minus the naphthalene chain and energy it pays. Nothing in the annual report describes a cost edge, a patent, a brand or a contracted customer.

The claimed change is volume. Promoters put Rs 40 crore of warrant money in, a wholly owned subsidiary called Shakambhari Dyechem is building a new unit, and the story is that a cyclical Rs 185 crore converter becomes a larger and steadier chemicals base. The evidence for that climb is thin. The subsidiary was incorporated in April 2024, still shows nil turnover, a small loss and construction in progress that has aged into the one to two year bucket. No tonnage, no capacity addition and no commissioning date is disclosed for the subsidiary or for the Rs 10.24 crore direct expansion the warrants fund. The single figure the whole thesis needs, production volume against the stated 270 tonnes of capacity, appears nowhere in 181 pages, and the capacity figure itself carries no unit of time.

FY26 revenue rose 31 percent to Rs 185 crore. Manufactured sales inside that rose about 5 percent. The rest of the increase is a new resale line of Rs 33 crore bought in and sold on at roughly 1 percent, which the company's own product note labels as manufactured H Acid. Profit went the other way: net profit fell 18 percent to Rs 4.2 crore, and EBITDA fell from Rs 11.5 crore to Rs 9.8 crore on 31 percent more revenue. Other income was 57.7 percent of pre tax profit, over half of it a foreign exchange gain the report does not explain. The Gate 0 screen scored 56 out of 100 on the core block, 17 out of 60 on moats and 73 out of 160 overall; only three years of data exist, which forces a one tier downgrade and puts the screen in its lowest bucket. A second reader recomputed the core at 51 after removing a promoter holding item scored on a one year proxy, with the tier unchanged.

Cash is the part that stays unresolved. Operating cash flow was minus Rs 1.5 crore in FY25 against Rs 5.1 crore of profit, then plus Rs 11.1 crore in FY26, and a Rs 3.8 crore release of receivables did much of that work in one year. Free cash flow was negative in both years the corpus can compute, minus Rs 12.4 crore in total, as capex went from Rs 5.3 crore to Rs 12.9 crore. The current ratio stayed under 1.0 in both years. No dividend was paid or proposed. Receivables improved on the surface, falling 13.5 percent while revenue rose 31 percent, but the FY25 one to two year bucket appears to have aged into the FY26 two to three year bucket rather than being collected. Two years is not enough to call this structural or temporary, and there is no rating agency working capital commentary in the corpus to settle it.

What management writes and what its own numbers show do not match. The board report and a note both credit the interest cover improvement to a decrease in borrowing, while borrowings rose 8.4 percent. A falling return on net worth is explained by favourable market conditions in one place and by share dilution in another. A risk note states there is no major foreign currency exposure and no hedging, in the same report that shows 46 percent of sales exported and a foreign exchange gain of Rs 1.7 crore. The chairman states the revenue comparison against last year and omits the profit comparison. Capital deployment was the honest half: the earlier debenture money reached its stated destinations broadly on time, and the no dividend rationale matches the cash flow. The communication grade for the year is D on the no concall scale, which is its floor.

Peers complicate the volume story rather than supporting it. H Acid and related intermediate prices rose 40 to 45 percent between February and August 2026 on a sulphur and ammonia cost shock of two to four times, and the closest listed comparison reported the June 2026 quarter with chemical volume down 38.6 percent and revenue up 17.1 percent, which it called price led with volume given up on purpose. The same peer names a 7,000 to 8,000 tonne India to China H Acid flow starting mid 2025, which is a sector explanation for an export jump this company never explains. Peer margins sit above Shree Hari's 5.3 to 8.1 percent band, and one peer is commissioning 72,000 tonnes a year of extra chemical capacity into the same price window. The peer stage also contradicted the company on one point: its annual report frames Chinese price competition and possible anti dumping duty as the threat, while the peer record shows China losing export competitiveness and the trade flow reversing toward India. A second reader found parts of this run's own peer read overstated, and the rerun list carries those corrections.

The market is real but slow. Global H Acid demand sizes at Rs 6,600 crore to Rs 8,500 crore, the slice this company can address at about Rs 1,180 crore, and the share it could plausibly hold in three to five years at Rs 170 crore to Rs 189 crore. Current revenue is 12.4 percent of that addressable slice, so headroom is about 8 times, and the runway class is moderate. Demand itself grows 3.6 percent a year and the achievable share path implies about 5 percent revenue growth a year. Management makes no market size claim of any kind, in a document that asks shareholders to fund a Rs 40 crore expansion. The market work is also incomplete: web fetching was blocked on every market report and trade data site tried, so the sizing rests on search snippets rather than primary pages.

The promoter picture is concentration, not exit. There is no pledge, and searches across SEBI orders, courts, tax, surveillance and proxy advisers found no adverse action. Promoter family pay, relatives' salaries and rent together were Rs 6 crore in FY26, 112.7 percent of standalone pre tax profit, up from 84.9 percent the year before. Every dilutive instrument went to the family: debentures converting at Rs 79 and warrants priced at Rs 176.10, against a Rs 248 traded price, taking the holding from 54.2 percent to 59.3 percent to 64.3 percent, with a path to 73.8 percent. The company is asking for a Rs 100 crore related party ceiling against a Rs 156 crore market value, half of it for an entity with nil turnover and about Rs 1,163 crore of net worth, which the family's Rs 1,522 crore sale of its polyester business to Reliance in March 2023 most plausibly explains. A loan of Rs 1.88 crore went to the subsidiary during the year and came back before year end, quantified only in the governance report. Three of the four warrant holders also draw senior management pay.
