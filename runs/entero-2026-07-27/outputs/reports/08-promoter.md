## SECTION 3: BUSINESS CONDUCT & ETHICAL TRACK RECORD

### 3A. RPT history

The RPT footprint is large by transaction count (Consolidated FS Note, "Related Party
Disclosures," pp.232-239/PDF pp.236-243; Standalone FS Note 50, pp.148-161/PDF pp.152-165)
but the great majority is ordinary-course: sale/purchase of stock-in-trade with subsidiaries
and step-down entities, rent to acquired-company founders/relatives (see 1B), corporate
guarantees issued by the parent for subsidiary bank facilities (₹6,327M FY26 vs ₹4,657M
FY25 — VERIFIED, Standalone Note 55B, p.166/PDF p.170), and intercompany working-capital
loans at 9% (Standalone Note 55A, p.164/PDF p.168).

**The one genuinely adverse RPT finding: the Curever Pharma loan write-off.**
- Standalone FS Note 55A (p.164/PDF p.168) shows a ₹364.69M loan to **Curever Pharma Private
  Limited**, a 100%-owned subsidiary, carried at that same balance from 01-Apr-2025 to
  31-Mar-2026 with zero repayment, fully offset by a matching "Less: Impairment loss
  accounted (Refer Note 9 and 39B)" of (₹364.69M) — i.e. the loan's book value was written
  to nil. VERIFIED, AR.
- Standalone FS Note 39B(III) ("Exceptional Items," for FY ended 31-Mar-2025, p.140/PDF
  p.144) states the actual rationale disclosed: **"Exceptional items also includes
  impairment loss towards loan given to Curever Pharma Private Limited, wholly owned
  subsidiary of the Company amounting to ₹364.69 million and interest receivable on the
  said loan amounting to ₹95.82 million."** VERIFIED, AR. Total write-off: ~₹460.5 million
  — larger than the company's entire FY26 standalone PAT of ₹268.75 million.
- **This is where the disclosure stops.** The note states the amount and the accounting
  treatment (impairment, not a formal legal waiver) but gives **no business explanation**
  for why a wholly-owned subsidiary's loan became unrecoverable. The only explanation found
  anywhere is a single third-party analyst blog (congruenceadvisers.com, undated,
  accessed 29-Aug-2026), which describes Curever as Entero's **private-label generic
  medicines venture that failed on relentless price competition and weak brand pull**, and
  was subsequently scaled down. This is ❓ UNVERIFIED / single-source for the "why" —
  plausible and consistent with a legitimate failed internal venture rather than fraud or
  diversion, but **not confirmed by the company itself anywhere in the AR, an exchange
  filing, or a second independent source.**
- **Finding, recorded at VERIFIED tier for the fact/amount, UNVERIFIED tier for the
  rationale:** a ~₹460M related-party write-off (>100% of one year's standalone profit) with
  no company-sourced explanation is a disclosure-quality gap, not confirmed misconduct. It
  should be a standing question for management (what was Curever's business, why did the
  ₹365M go in, why is none of it coming back, was any of it disbursed to promoter-linked
  parties) that the operator should put to the company directly, since the AR itself will
  not answer it.

**RPT approval process:** Standalone AR states (CG Report, p.58/PDF p.62) all Senior
Management confirmed no undisclosed personal-interest RPTs, and the Board's philosophy
statement is standard boilerplate. No RPT was flagged in the AR as requiring minority
shareholder approval or as contested — a structurally quiet RPT-approval record (subject to
the volume/opacity concern above).

### 3B. Capital allocation behaviour

**The most material adverse finding in this report.**
- CG Report (p.63/PDF p.67): Prabhat Agrawal total FY26 compensation (fixed salary + bonus +
  sitting fees + others) = ₹4,72,74,478 (₹47.27M). Prem Sethi = ₹2,57,86,082 (₹25.79M).
  VERIFIED, AR.
- Consolidated FS RPT note, "Remuneration Paid" (pp.235-236/PDF pp.239-240): individually
  discloses Prabhat Agrawal FY26 = ₹94.50M vs FY25 = ₹43.74M (+116.05%); Prem Sethi FY26 =
  ₹51.52M vs FY25 = ₹23.85M (+116.06%). VERIFIED, AR.
- **These two AR-internal figures for Prabhat Agrawal's FY26 pay (₹47.27M vs ₹94.50M) are
  roughly 2x apart and are not reconciled anywhere in the AR.** Both are from audited
  statements (CG Report is board-certified; the KMP note is auditor-signed), so this is a
  VERIFIED internal-consistency gap, not a rumour — the AR contradicts itself on what the
  CEO was actually paid, and no note cross-references or explains the difference (e.g. via
  ESOP fair value, gratuity/superannuation accrual, or a Companies-Act Section-197
  disclosure-basis difference). This is exactly the kind of item that should have been
  reconciled by the company/auditor and was not.
- Independent of which of the two numbers is "the" number, both consistently show CEO and
  COO cash compensation growing ~116% year-on-year, against consolidated PAT growth of
  ~21.3% FY26 (₹145.8 crore, operator-ferried digest, to be confirmed against the FY26
  results release) — pay growing roughly 5.4x faster than the profit it is nominally tied
  to.
- This did not go unnoticed by the market: at the 19-Aug-2026 AGM, "public institutional
  investors opposed the remuneration packages for the MD/CEO and WTD/COO with roughly
  one-third of their votes," while the promoter block voted unanimously in favour; the
  resolutions passed 93.58% (Agrawal) and 93.95% (Sethi) only because of the ~52% promoter
  vote [scanx.trade, "Entero Healthcare shareholders approve all seven AGM resolutions,"
  19-Aug-2026 — MEDIA REPORTED, corroborated by the exchange-disclosed vote percentages
  themselves, so treated as VERIFIED for the vote outcome].

**Dividend/dilution:** No dividend paid (growth-stage distributor, standard for the sector).
No preferential allotments or promoter warrants found; ESOP scheme (Entero ESOP 2023) is
broad-based, not promoter-directed (Standalone Note 52, p.162/PDF p.166). No
value-destructive related-party acquisition identified — the MedTech acquisitions (Ace
