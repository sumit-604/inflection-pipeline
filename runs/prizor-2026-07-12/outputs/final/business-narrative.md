Fast growing CCTV maker with broken cash

Prizor Viztech makes and sells video surveillance hardware from Gandhinagar, Gujarat. Cameras, NVRs and DVRs, in analog, IP and AI form, are 77% of revenue. Display products, LED TVs, monitors and touch panels, are 16%, and the rest is unallocated. The company sells through more than 11,000 dealers across 21 states, a network built over nine years, and holds BIS and STQC certification that keeps non certified foreign brands out of parts of the market. It listed on the NSE SME board in July 2024 and raised Rs 25 Cr. Market cap is Rs 891 Cr at Rs 825 a share.

Revenue tripled in two years. FY24 was about Rs 36 Cr, FY25 about Rs 71 Cr, up 99%, and FY26 about Rs 148 Cr per the investor presentation, up 108%. PAT rose from Rs 5.5 Cr to Rs 10.2 Cr to Rs 20.8 Cr across the same years. The FY26 figures come from an unaudited presentation, since no FY26 annual report and no quarterly results reached this run.

The transition is from trading toward own manufacture. Prizor is backward integrating into camera assembly and PCB level SMT at Gandhinagar, with installed capacity of 16 lakh cameras and 50 lakh PCB units a year. Almost all of that plant was built inside FY26. Net plant and machinery was near nil at FY25 close, about Rs 0.69 lakh, so the manufacturing story is real but unproven at scale.

Gate 0 lands AVERAGE, core 63 and grand 82, with 4 moats confirmed. Three deal breakers fired: the cash quality block scored zero of 20, cumulative CFO to PAT is negative at minus 1.02 times, and history is under three years. Two of the ugliest inputs are historical artefacts, not deterioration. FY24 ROE of 141% and ROCE of 70% come off a near nil pre IPO equity base, and the promoter holding fall from 99.99% to 68.28% is entirely IPO float and bonus dilution, with both promoters holding their full pre IPO share count. The negative cumulative CFO to PAT is not an artefact. It is a genuine signal.

Cash conversion has been broken for three straight years. Operating cash flow was minus Rs 1.8 Cr in FY24, minus Rs 14.1 Cr in FY25 against PAT of Rs 10.2 Cr, and plus Rs 0.3 Cr in FY26 against PAT of Rs 20.8 Cr, a conversion of about 1.4%. Finished goods inventory grew 282% in FY25 while revenue grew 99%. Receivables rose from Rs 15.7 Cr to Rs 38.6 Cr into FY26, and the second half of FY26 carried 72% of full year revenue, so sales are concentrated late. Short term borrowings jumped from Rs 2.8 Cr to Rs 31.8 Cr, about 11.5 times, funding the working capital build. The growth is running on the IPO proceeds and short term debt, not on cash the business throws off.

Management credibility grades C, the default when there is no concall and no quantified guidance to test. The company's own April 2026 presentation shows two different FY26 ROCE and ROE pairs, 47.4% and 38.0% on the cover against 37.2% and 35.5% in the ratio chart. A Rs 3 Cr pre IPO loan was converted to 4,00,000 equity shares at Rs 75 in May 2024, and the counterparty could not be traced to any director, KMP or named related party across three independent cross checks. This is the single largest open governance question. Promoter family remuneration was approved for an increase of about 150%, to up to Rs 2.5 lakh a month each from Rs 1 lakh, effective July 2025, and was not disclosed as a subsequent event. Related party revenue from Om Security Solutions was Rs 3.55 Cr, about 10% of FY24 sales, then vanished to zero in the IPO year. The CMD's claimed Ph.D. from Frankford International University could not be verified. The promoter review is partial, since shareholding and pledge aggregator pages returned 403 on every fetch.

Peers contradict the margin claim and confirm the cash pattern. Prizor's implied 21 to 23% EBITDA and 14% PAT sit above the only scaled direct peer, CP Plus, which ran 8.7 to 18.0% EBITDA across four quarters, 13.7% EBITDA and 8.72% PAT for full year FY26, and called 14 to 15% EBITDA the new normal. No peer transcript names Prizor, even when analysts asked CP Plus to name certified competitors gaining share. Sahasra shows negative operating cash flow as a multi year structural feature of scaling in this cohort, with no return to positive guided before FY2028, which cuts against reading Prizor's burn as a one year event.

The market runway is large and, read correctly, not inflated. Domestic video surveillance TAM is Rs 12,370 to 17,425 Cr, the served market about Rs 3,580 Cr, and a realistic five year share about Rs 309 Cr. Prizor's own cited market size implies a claim ratio of 1.03 against the sized market, so it is reasonable rather than stretched. An earlier read that Prizor's domestic market CAGR was double the peer figure was corrected: read off the chart properly it is about 14.4%, close to CP Plus's Frost and Sullivan 16.5%. The share implied five year revenue CAGR of 16 to 17% sits far below Prizor's recent 108%, and installed capacity already covers many times the near term opportunity, so the debt funded capex is the optimistic case.
