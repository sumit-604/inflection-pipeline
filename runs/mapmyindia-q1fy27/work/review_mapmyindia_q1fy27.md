## Part 2 — SECTOR INTELLIGENCE

MapmyIndia sells digital maps, geospatial software, location APIs, and IoT/telematics hardware into three end-markets it now labels Automotive, Enterprise, and Government (a reporting change made this quarter, replacing the old A&M/C&E split [deck slide 10]). The demand read this quarter is uneven: Automotive grew ~29% YoY [Rs45.7→58.8 Cr, deck line 503], driven by connected-mobility and EV wins (Tata Sierra EV, Suzuki, Vespa, VinFast named); Enterprise grew ~6% [Rs60.6→64.2 Cr]; but Government — a structurally lumpy, tender-driven, slow-paying customer base — grew only ~9.2% and is seasonally weakest in Q1 [deck line 633]. The Government softness plus a Rs4 Cr write-off on a government customer is the sector's classic risk showing through: public-sector demand is real but collection and timing are poor. The structural tailwind the company leans on is AI and India's digital-mapping sovereignty (indigenous maps, defence, PSU digital-twin projects) — management framed a heavy "AI-native" push [deck slide 5], which is a genuine multi-year demand driver but also an opex commitment that pressures margin. Regulation is mostly favourable (data-localisation, indigenous-mapping policy). Payer mix matters: the more the mix tilts to Government and to lower-margin IoT hardware, the more the sector's cash-conversion and margin profile deteriorates. Provenance: segment growth and customer names from this quarter's deck; the "lumpy government payer" characterisation and AI/sovereignty tailwind framing from prior Notion sector work; no independent third-party channel data was supplied this run.

## Part 3 — BUSINESS-MODEL INTELLIGENCE

The company makes money two ways, with very different economics. The **Map-led** business (maps, navigation, analytics, GIS/digital-twins, sold as licences and subscriptions — MaaS/SaaS/PaaS) is the profit engine: Rs98.7 Cr revenue this quarter at a 51.4% EBITDA margin [deck line 408]. The **IoT-led** business (GPS telematics, dash-cams, trackers, plus mobility/logistics SaaS) is the growth engine but structurally lower-margin: Rs41.1 Cr revenue, up 75% YoY, at just 13.1% EBITDA margin [deck lines 399-423]. This quarter shows real model drift: IoT's share of revenue jumped from 19.2% to 29.4%, and because it earns roughly a quarter of the Map-led margin, the blended operating margin compressed 574 bps even though total revenue grew. So the model is trading margin for growth. Two more model facts stand out. First, profit is increasingly a treasury story — other income is 29.6% of pre-tax profit and rising, and the Rs745 Cr cash hoard earns a return that flatters PAT while core operations stall. Second, value creation is concentrated in the standalone parent; the consolidated subsidiaries (notably Gtropy, the ~96%-owned IoT/telematics arm) are a net drag this quarter, pulling consolidated PAT below standalone. Unit economics summary: high-margin, high-retention software core that is barely growing, wrapped around a fast-growing but thin-margin hardware/telematics business, funded by a large idle cash balance with no allocation plan. Provenance: product-segment margins and revenue from this quarter's deck; Gtropy stake, treasury size, and CFO/PAT history from prior Notion work (not re-disclosed in this filing).

## Part 4 — COMPETITION INTELLIGENCE

MapmyIndia's structural moat is its proprietary, continuously updated India map database (down to building/address level, 30+ years of accumulation), an indigenous-data and defence/PSU trust position that global players (Google Maps, HERE, TomTom) cannot easily replicate for sovereign and regulated Indian use-cases, and named OEM design-wins that create switching costs (Tata, Suzuki, Vespa, VinFast).
