## SECTION 1: THE BUSINESS MODEL IN PLAIN ENGLISH

### 1A. One-line description
AESL sells engineering expertise, crews and equipment to help oil, gas, coal and mineral companies find, build, run and eventually decommission energy infrastructure — and, increasingly, it also owns a direct slice of the oil wells and mines it services.

### 1B. Money-flow chain for each revenue stream

**Seismic services (legacy business, ~31 years, AR p.6):**
[ONGC/Oil India/private E&P award a seismic survey block] → [AESL crews acquire 2D/3D seismic data using owned survey equipment, subcontracted labour, transition-zone and wireless techniques (AR p.6)] → [Delivers processed subsurface data/interpretation reports] → [Client pays] → [Milestone billing per km surveyed/shot points; historically 8% of standalone order book, ₹74cr, as of Mar'25 (AR p.7-8)]

**Operations & Maintenance (O&M), including Kuiper manpower from Sept 2025:**
[Asset owner — Vedanta, Oilmax, Hardy Oil/Svetah, AGCL, global EPCI/drilling majors via Kuiper — needs onshore/offshore facilities run or staffed] → [AESL operates/maintains production facilities or supplies integrated manpower under multi-year (2-5 yr) Master Service Agreements (AR p.6, p.9-10; Inv. Pres. slide 26)] → [Delivers uptime/output or staffed positions] → [Client pays] → [Recurring monthly/milestone billing; largest single order-book category — 56% of standalone order book (₹550cr) as of Mar'25 (AR p.7-8), and Kuiper adds ~US$60-65mn/yr of international revenue visibility from Sept 2025 (Inv. Pres. slide 6, 35)]

**Mineral / Coal Handling Plant (CHP) & material handling infrastructure:**
[Coal India subsidiaries/power utilities (ECL, SCCL, CCL, HEC) or private miners award CHP/material-handling contracts] → [AESL builds and operates coal/mineral handling systems, often via joint-venture consortia (Zuberi-Asian JV, AESL FFIL JV, Asian Indwell JV) (AR p.7, Note 44 standalone)] → [Delivers evacuation/handling capacity] → [Client pays] → [EPC + O&M milestone billing; 36% of standalone order book (₹350cr) as of Mar'25 (AR p.7-8), rising to 32% (₹566cr) of a much larger ₹1,750cr standalone order book by Mar'26 as Integrated Oil & Gas Services grows faster (Inv. Pres. slide 33)]

**Upstream oil & gas production (Indrora participatory interest; Oilmax merger pending):**
[AESL holds a 50% Participatory Interest in the Indrora Revenue Sharing Contract with parent Oilmax Energy, acquired June 2023 for ₹1,770 Lakhs (AR Note 55 standalone; consolidated Note 54)] → [Operates wells, extracts crude — NM-01 well producing ~100 BOPD, targeting ~1,000 BOPD block-level by FY27 (Inv. Pres. slide 32)] → [Sells crude to the market] → [Buyer pays market/Brent-linked price] → [Revenue recognized net of Government's contractual share under the RSC (AR Note 29 standalone: gross sale ₹515.75L, govt share ₹41.85L, net ₹473.90L, FY24-25)]

### 1E. The chai-stall-uncle version
Imagine a contractor who doesn't build houses — he sends skilled crews to help oil companies find where to dig, builds the pipes and platforms, then keeps the whole thing running for years under a service contract, getting paid by the client for that work. He also now sends manpower abroad to do the same job for foreign oil companies in the Gulf. And on the side, he's bought a small stake in one actual oil well, so instead of only earning a service fee, he sometimes also sells the oil itself. The catch: he doesn't own most of his trucks and cranes — he hires them and passes the cost through — and his big customers are mostly government companies who can delay paying him or cancel a job, which is exactly what happened once with ONGC (AR p.8-9).

### 3A. Ignore-these-track-these

| Commonly tracked ratio | Status | Why |

## SECTION 4: RISKS, VALUATION APPROACH & MONITORING

### 4A. Business-model-specific risks

| Category | Risk | First financial line item to deteriorate |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue model | PSU client contract suspension/holiday order (precedent: ONGC 2024, AR p.8-9) | Order book value or seismic-segment revenue declining QoQ without replacement contracts |
| Margin | Kuiper/lower-margin revenue mix dilution, or project cost overruns on fixed-price CHP contracts | Consolidated EBITDA margin below prior-year comparable quarter — already occurred (FY26: 12.5% vs FY25: 15.6%, Inv. Pres. slide 12) |
| Balance sheet | Capex-driven leverage build-up as E&P assets scale (term loan for plant/machinery, Oilmax merger integration) | Borrowings (current + non-current) rising faster than cash/liquid balances — already occurred (borrowings up ~6.7x FY25→FY26, Inv. Pres. slide 13) |
| Execution | Billing/collection lag on project milestones | Contract assets (unbilled WIP) growth rate persistently exceeding revenue growth rate — already occurred (WIP nearly doubled FY25→FY26 vs 70% revenue growth, Inv. Pres. slide 13) |
| Structural | Oilmax merger delay (NCLT/SEBI process, targeted Sep/Oct 2026 completion) or Kuiper Middle East disruption from the West Asia conflict | NCLT/SEBI approval-timeline slippage announcements, or Kuiper segment revenue/margin decline attributed to conflict disruption (Inv. Pres. slide 7, 35) |

### 4B. Valuation method applicability (handoff to Role 1 valuation stage)

| Method | Applicable? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| EV/EBITDA (blended) | **PRIMARY** | Captures operating leverage across a capital-intensive, cyclical services-plus-upstream mix without earnings-line distortions from exceptional items (Kuiper acquisition cost, write-offs) that currently blur P/E (Inv. Pres. slide 9 adjusted-PAT add-backs) |
| Sum-of-the-parts (services EV/EBITDA + E&P DCF/NAV on reserves) | **SECONDARY** | Business is structurally bifurcating into a fee-based services arm (seismic/O&M/CHP/Kuiper) and a reserve-owning E&P arm (Indrora, pending Oilmax merger ~70mn boe reserves, Inv. Pres. slide 37); a blended multiple misprices the two risk/return profiles once the merger completes |
| P/E | **TERTIARY** | Useful cross-check only once earnings normalize post-Kuiper integration and Oilmax merger; currently distorted by exceptional items, warrant-driven share-count changes, and the Kuiper margin-dilution effect |
| Dividend Discount Model | Not applicable | Dividend track record only 2 years old (₹1/share FY25, AR p.3; ₹1.25/share FY26, Inv. Pres. slide 4) with no stated payout policy |
| Replacement cost | Not applicable | Not meaningful for an asset-light services business; would understate contract/relationship value and overweight the smaller E&P asset base |
| PEG ratio | Not applicable | Current earnings growth is driven by inorganic consolidation (Kuiper) and one-off items rather than a clean organic growth rate |
| Cycle stage that matters for valuation | Company is early in a self-described "upstream supercycle" re-rating narrative (Inv. Pres. slide 17-19); valuation should explicitly test sensitivity to a Brent price reversal and to Oilmax-merger completion risk |

### 4C. Quarterly monitoring checklist (10-15 items)

1. Consolidated order book (₹cr) QoQ — good: growing; trouble: flat/declining
2. Order-book segment mix (O&G vs Mineral vs Seismic %) — good: O&M/long-duration share stable/rising; trouble: seismic share rising again (return to volatility)
3. Standalone EBITDA margin — good: sustained >15%; trouble: below 12%
4. Kuiper segment revenue & margin (once disclosed separately) — good: margin expanding toward stated synergy targets; trouble: flat/declining
5. Contract assets (unbilled WIP) growth vs revenue growth — good: WIP growth ≤ revenue growth; trouble: WIP growing materially faster
6. Trade receivable turnover / DSO-equivalent — good: stable or improving; trouble: continued decline (already falling)
7. Net debt/EBITDA (or net cash position) — good: remains net cash or <1x; trouble: >1.5x
8. Indrora/Mewad production (BOPD) — good: tracking toward ~1,000 BOPD FY27 target; trouble: stalled below 200 BOPD
9. Oilmax merger milestone status (NCLT/SEBI approvals) — good: on track for Sep/Oct 2026; trouble: delays/objections
10. Customer concentration (top-1/top-3 % of revenue) — good: declining or stable; trouble: rising further from FY25's ~53% (top-3)
11. PSU dispute status (recall the 2024 ONGC holiday-order precedent) — good: no new disputes; trouble: fresh contract suspensions
12. West Asia conflict impact on Kuiper's Middle East operations — good: no disruption; trouble: contract suspensions/evacuations
13. CWIP/capex spend (oil wells in progress, plant & machinery) vs plan — good: on-budget; trouble: cost overruns
14. Dividend payout consistency — good: maintained/growing (₹1.00→₹1.25/share so far); trouble: skipped
15. MSME/statutory-dues payable ageing — good: no build-up beyond normal terms; trouble: rising overdue payables (early stress signal)

### 4D. Highest-value questions for management

1. What is the realized per-barrel production cost and netback for the Indrora block today, and how does that compare to the ~US$5/bbl target cited for Oilmax's own assets? — *Reassuring:* costs trending toward the $5-10/bbl range with rising margins. *Worrying:* costs still well above $20-30/bbl, making the ramp uneconomic at current oil prices.
2. Post-consolidation, what is Kuiper's standalone EBITDA margin trajectory, and how much of FY26 consolidated EBITDA growth is Kuiper versus organic AESL services? — *Reassuring:* Kuiper margins improving toward management's stated synergy targets. *Worrying:* Kuiper margins flat/declining, masking weakness in core AESL services.
3. What is driving the near-doubling of contract assets (unbilled WIP) relative to revenue growth, and what is the expected cash-conversion timeline? — *Reassuring:* a clear contractual milestone-billing schedule with near-term conversion and no disputed items. *Worrying:* milestone-certification delays or disputes with PSU clients extending realization beyond 12 months.
4. What are the realistic risk factors that could delay the Oilmax merger beyond the targeted September/October 2026 completion? — *Reassuring:* NCLT and SEBI approvals on track with no objections raised. *Worrying:* unresolved regulatory queries or shareholder disputes threatening the timeline.
5. Given seismic has fallen to a low single-digit share of the order book, what is the strategy for this legacy vertical — continued run-off, selective bidding, or divestment? — *Reassuring:* maintained as a small, disciplined, high-margin niche. *Worrying:* a persistent drag with no clear strategic rationale.
6. How much of the ₹1,750cr standalone order book is executable within FY27, and what bid-pipeline conversion rate underpins FY27 guidance (30-40% standalone growth, Kuiper US$60-65mn)? — *Reassuring:* conservative, contract-backed assumptions. *Worrying:* guidance relies heavily on unsigned pipeline.
7. Will the "net zero-debt" positioning be maintained as capex needs grow (term loan for plant/machinery, Indrora/Mewad development, post-merger reserve development), or is a shift to a leveraged E&P capital structure anticipated? — *Reassuring:* a stated, disciplined leverage ceiling. *Worrying:* an open-ended commitment to debt-funded E&P growth.
