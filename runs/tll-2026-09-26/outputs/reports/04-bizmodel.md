SECTION 1: THE BUSINESS MODEL IN PLAIN ENGLISH
===============================================================================

1A. ONE-LINE DESCRIPTION
TLL makes and sells pharmaceutical tablets, capsules, and liquids to distributors in Africa, Asia, and
Latin America, and is now trying to build four more small factories (medical devices, injectables,
herbal supplements, cosmetics) around that core (AR p.2, p.16, p.26).

1B. MONEY FLOW CHAIN, EACH REVENUE STREAM

Stream 1: Pharmaceutical formulations, export (parent + TNS Pharma Pvt Ltd, 51% owned)
[Active Pharma Ingredient bought from suppliers] -> [TLL formulates and packs tablets/capsules/liquids,
own plant or third-party contract manufacturer, under a product registration filed in the destination
country] -> [ships finished pack to an in-country distributor] -> [distributor resells to pharmacies/
hospitals in that country] -> [distributor pays TLL, on credit, in the country's currency, converted to
INR] (AR p.2, p.16-17, p.79; Note 17.1 ageing schedule).

Stream 2: Pharmaceutical formulations, domestic (parent)
[Same manufacturing input] -> [same manufacture] -> [sold to Indian distributors/B2B partners] ->
[distributor resells domestically] -> [distributor pays TLL, shorter credit cycle, INR] (AR p.16-17: FY26
domestic revenue share rose to ~49% of consolidated revenue from 30% in FY25).

Stream 3: Medical devices (Trident Mediquip Ltd, 59.77-60% owned, acquired 04.02.2025)
[Plastics/components] -> [Mediquip manufactures IV cannulas and infusion sets at its own WHO-GMP-track
facility] -> [sold to distributors/B2B partners] -> [distributor pays on credit] (AR p.14, AOC-1 p.40-41;
Q1 FY27 deck p.13).

Stream 4: Injectables (TLL Parenterals Ltd, 51% owned, acquired 02.12.2024)
[Glass ampoules/vials + API] -> [planned in-house manufacture of injectables] -> [not yet selling: FY26
turnover Rs 0, AOC-1 p.40] -> [no payer yet]. Commercial start claimed for FY27 (AR p.14).

Stream 5: Herbal/nutraceutical supplements (TLL Wellness Ltd, 51% owned, acquired 10.08.2023)
Same structure as Stream 4: FY26 turnover Rs 11.78 lakh only (AOC-1 p.40-41), product development stage,
commercialisation claimed from FY27 (AR p.14).

Stream 6: Cosmetics/personal care, "YES" brand (TLL Elements Pvt Ltd, 100% owned, acquired 15.04.2024)
[Cosmetic ingredients] -> [Elements manufactures/trades skin and hair products] -> [sold under the "YES"
brand through dealers/distributors] -> [distributor pays]. FY26 turnover Rs 2.46 lakh only, and the
subsidiary is loss-making with negative net worth (AOC-1 p.40-41).

1E. THE CHAI-STALL-UNCLE VERSION
TLL is like a chai-stall owner who has spent ten years getting a permit to sell chai on 46 different
streets, in 1,091 spots exactly, with 2,534 more permit applications in the queue. That permit book is
the real asset. The chai itself (the tablets) is not special; anyone with a stove (a factory) can make
it, so the uncle cannot charge more than the next stall. Lately the uncle also bought four small extra
carts (a device cart, an injection cart, a herbal-tea cart, a soap cart) hoping they grow into separate
businesses. One cart (medical devices) is already selling well. Two carts (injections, herbal tea) have
not sold a single cup yet. One cart (soap) is losing money. And the uncle's customers, who resell his
chai on credit, are taking longer and longer to pay him back, so the cash in his pocket has grown much
slower than the profit on his ledger (AR MD&A p.16-18, 27; B01/B02 receivables findings).

2B. COMPETITIVE POSITIONING MAP
Named peers used by the pipeline's own peer set (B00/B01) are Caplin Point Laboratories (CAPLIPOINT),
Senores Pharmaceuticals (SENORES), and Innova Captab (INNOVACAP), all Indian formulations exporters to
semi-regulated/regulated markets, selected by the earlier stages as the closest financial comparators.
The AR itself names no competitors. TLL differs from CAPLIPOINT (regulated-market focus, larger scale) by
sitting earlier in scale and in semi-regulated/frontier markets (Ghana, Kenya, Peru, Venezuela) with a
smaller, faster-growing but thinner registration base and negative-to-marginal cash conversion (B01
Block B = 0/20) versus the peer set's better-established margins. NOT FOUND: a stated market-share
number for TLL in any of its geographies.

2D. INDUSTRY LIFECYCLE STAGE
Generic formulations exports to semi-regulated markets is a mature, fragmented industry (many WHO-GMP
generic makers compete for the same distributor relationships). TLL's OWN position within it is early-
growth: it is mid-way through converting a large registration pipeline into revenue (AR MD&A Opportunities,
p.18) and mid-way through building four new manufacturing verticals, two of which are still pre-revenue
(AOC-1, p.40-41).

4D. HIGHEST-VALUE QUESTIONS FOR MANAGEMENT

1. What are the actual credit terms offered to export distributors, especially in Venezuela/Africa, and
why has consolidated debtor days risen from 116 to 208? Reassures: a stated, tightening credit policy or
factoring arrangement. Worries: no formal policy, ad hoc extensions to protect volume.
2. What is the realistic, committed (not peak-target) revenue trajectory for TLL Parenterals in FY27?
Reassures: signed off-take or supply agreements already in hand. Worries: still no named customer, debt
already drawn against the plant.
3. Of the 1,091 registered products, how many generated any revenue in FY26, and what is the average
lag from registration to first commercial sale? Reassures: high conversion, short lag. Worries: a large,
aging, unconverted registration backlog.
4. Gross margin fell 690 bps in FY26 while EBITDA margin rose on cost control alone; is that overhead
absorption repeatable, or was FY26 a one-off cut (employee costs -11%)? Reassures: a structural,
repeatable fixed-cost base still under-absorbed. Worries: a one-off headcount cut that cannot repeat.
5. What is the true, restated FY25 and FY26 operating cash flow excluding the "Changes in Working
Capital Facilities" line, and will the FY27 AR disclose this as a named, explained line? Reassures: a
clear restatement note and a return to conventional presentation. Worries: recurrence without explanation.
6. What is the business rationale, exit plan, and cap on the corporate guarantees to Talon Healthcare
LLP and Tench Lifesciences LLP, two entities the Group does not consolidate? Reassures: a dated exit/
unwind plan. Worries: an open-ended, growing exposure.
7. With domestic revenue rising from 30% to 49% of FY26 revenue, does management expect the domestic
segment's margin and working-capital profile to converge with, or stay better than, the export segment's?
Reassures: domestic margins already comparable or better. Worries: domestic growth is margin-dilutive and
masked inside a single blended segment.
