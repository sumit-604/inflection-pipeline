### 2B. Excuse pattern analysis

For the misses: working-capital deterioration is attributed to growth/ODM-model inventory needs and supply-chain stocking (external/structural, partially honest — management does not deny the negative cash flow number, states it plainly). The undelivered press release/quarterly-reporting promises are attributed to SME-phase/documentation readiness — a soft, repeatable excuse that has now been used across all three calls without a firm date ever being set. The fundraising reversal is explained with a reasonable business rationale (pre-funding a third facility) but was **not proactively disclosed** — it surfaced only because an analyst cross-referenced the prior call's transcript.

Positive counter-evidence of honesty: management volunteers unprompted, unflattering detail on occasion — e.g., the Nidec/Airbus qualification-failure anecdote (a customer rejected them years ago over a calibration issue, told candidly), and Mukesh Vasani's admission "I don't have a clear answer, I don't have a clear voice" when asked about capex-to-revenue ratio (H1FY26 call) rather than fabricating a number.

**Pattern check**: management does not conspicuously blame externals while crediting itself for successes — revenue beats are credited to team execution and "SWOT analysis," while misses (cash flow, margin volatility) draw a mix of external/structural explanation and plain acknowledgment. Hard topics (receivables, RPT customer %, defence margin risk, competitor entry) are mostly raised only when analysts ask, not proactively — with the notable exception of the Nidec story and the explicit "defence has a lot of things plus and minus" caution volunteered unprompted. Net classification: **balanced**, tilting toward deflection specifically on the two repeated transparency commitments (press release, quarterly reporting).
