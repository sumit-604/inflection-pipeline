## SECTION 1: THE BUSINESS MODEL IN PLAIN ENGLISH

### 1A. One-line description
Kronox makes high-purity inorganic specialty fine chemicals in its own
factories in Gujarat and sells them as finished products to pharma,
nutraceutical, food-chemical, and laboratory-reagent buyers in India and
abroad (AR p.15-16, chairman's letter).

### 1B. Money flow chain
Kronox discloses one reportable business segment and one revenue line
("Sale of Products") in its financial statements (AR p.107, Note 32; AR
p.104, Note 22). The AR does not break this single line into

### 1E. The chai-stall-uncle version
Think of Kronox like a small factory that cooks up very pure chemical
powders instead of food. Pharma companies, food companies, and labs buy
these powders because they need them extra clean and certified safe. The
factory sits in Gujarat, and about a third of what it sells now goes
abroad, up from about a quarter a few years back (AR p.08). It buys raw
material, cooks it into a purer form, and sells the finished powder, much
like a sweet shop buys sugar and milk and sells sweets, except this shop
needs a stack of quality certificates before any big buyer will place an
order (AR p.15-16). The company has also bought land in Dahej for a fourth
factory, but building it has been delayed for reasons the AR does not fully
explain (AR p.15-16, Chairman's Letter).

### 4C. Quarterly monitoring checklist

1. Revenue from Operations, QoQ and YoY — good: continued growth; trouble: two consecutive flat-to-down quarters
2. EBITDA margin — good: holding or expanding past 33-34%; trouble: contraction >300bp without stated raw-material reason
3. Cost of Materials Consumed as % of revenue — good: stable/falling; trouble: rising faster than revenue
4. Export % of revenue — good: stable-to-rising; trouble: sharp reversal
5. Net Gain/(Loss) on Foreign Currency Transactions — good: positive/stable; trouble: turning materially negative
6. Trade Receivables ageing — good: >95% under 6 months; trouble: any credit-impaired or disputed bucket appearing
7. Cash Conversion Cycle (days) — good: near 75-85 day range; trouble: sustained move past 90+ days
8. Capital Work-in-Progress (Unit IV, Dahej) — good: visible quarter-on-quarter capitalisation progress; trouble: balance stuck near Rs 87.6 lakh for another year
9. Director remuneration vs PAT growth — good: remuneration growth roughly tracks PAT growth; trouble: remuneration growing much faster than PAT (FY26 already shows 120% YoY jump in director remuneration vs 8.6% PAT growth by the Board's Report figure)
10. Current Ratio / debt levels — good: stays high, no new borrowing for Unit IV beyond plan; trouble: sudden debt-funded capex spike
11. Related-party transactions — good: stays nil/immaterial as disclosed; trouble: any new material RPT appears
12. Segment disclosure — good: stays single segment as business model is understood today; trouble: company begins reporting a second segment (would mean the business model itself has changed and needs re-decoding)

### 4D. Highest-value questions for management

1. What is the installed capacity at Ekalbara (Units I-III) today, and what is current utilisation? Reassuring: utilisation in the 70-85%+ range with room to grow before Unit IV is needed. Worrying: utilisation already near 95%+ with Unit IV delayed, meaning growth is capacity-capped now.
2. What specifically has delayed Unit IV, Dahej, given clearances were already obtained? Reassuring: a discrete, resolved issue (e.g., contractor mobilisation) with a firm date. Worrying: a recurring or open-ended reason (funding, demand uncertainty, regulatory re-approval).
3. Who are the top 5-10 customers by revenue, and what is customer concentration? Reassuring: no single customer over ~10-15% of revenue. Worrying: one or two customers materially over that level with no long-term contract disclosed.
4. What products/end-markets make up the ~185-product portfolio by revenue (pharma vs nutraceutical vs food vs reagent)? Reassuring: diversified mix, no single end-market dominance. Worrying: heavy concentration in one end-market that is itself cyclical.
5. Is there a hedging policy for the growing export/forex exposure? Reassuring: a stated, systematic hedging approach. Worrying: no hedging, relying on natural offsets only.
6. Why did director remuneration more than double (Rs 180.0 lakh to Rs 396.0 lakh) in a year when PAT grew ~8.6% (per Board's Report)? Reassuring: a one-time market-correction to historically low pay, disclosed and approved. Worrying: no clear rationale, recurring pattern of pay outpacing profit growth.
7. What is the raw-material sourcing structure — number of suppliers, any long-term contracts, any single-supplier dependency? Reassuring: diversified, multi-supplier base. Worrying: single or few-supplier dependency with no long-term pricing agreement.

## ANALYST NOTE

This AR is unusually thin for a business-model decode: the graphics-heavy
front pages (clientele, capacity, manufacturing infrastructure) carry no
extractable text, so product mix, capacity, utilisation, and customer
concentration are all NOT FOUND rather than merely under-anchored. The one
genuinely load-bearing structural fact is the stalled Unit IV, Dahej: land
was leased years ago, clearances obtained, but CWIP sits at only Rs 87.6
lakh and the Chairman's Letter admits the build "could not be started" for
unstated reasons, with a fresh (unverified) 2-3 year timeline now promised.
Margin expansion (EBITDA 22.72% to 33.86%, FY23-FY26) is real and evidenced,
but ROCE and Fixed Asset Turnover are both declining, meaning the capital
base is growing faster than the earnings it produces are growing off it —
