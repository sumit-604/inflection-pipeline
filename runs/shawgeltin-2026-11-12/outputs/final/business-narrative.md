# Narmada Gelatines Ltd (SHAWGELTIN): business narrative, Phase 1

Run 2026-11-12. Phase 1 lite: evidence stages only. This file has no valuation, no entry zone and no decision; those form in Phase 3 after Halt 1. Units: annual report and results figures are in ₹ lakh; screener and CARE figures are in ₹ crore. Each figure states its unit.

## BUSINESS UNDERSTANDING NARRATIVE

Narmada Gelatines turns crushed animal bone into two products at one plant near Jabalpur. Gelatine is 71% of FY26 revenue, sold in pharma and edible grades to capsule makers, who use it to make gelatine capsules. The only named substitute is the plant based HPMC capsule, which is about 5% of one capsule maker's volume. DCP, the di-calcium phosphate that comes out of the same bone, is 29% of revenue and is sold at spot prices to local customers as an animal feed grade product. The gelatine buyers are capsule makers such as Geltec, ACG Associated Capsules and Fortcaps, and the top ten customers took about 42% of FY25 sales, up from 39% in FY24. They buy on repeat orders, and the run did not establish any contract term, qualification period or sole source position. One buyer imports 18 to 20% of its gelatine duty free and uses that access as a check on domestic prices. Present demand comes from capsule volumes, which a listed capsule maker puts at about 6% a year in India and 4% abroad, so hard capsule maker volumes and gelatine buying is the first signal to track. DCP demand follows poultry and animal feed production volumes. Forward growth rests on three drivers. The first is a ₹33 Cr expansion that adds 600 MT to 2,400 MT of gelatine capacity and 1,500 MT to 10,600 MT of DCP capacity from January 2027; only the rating agency's release describes it, and a commissioning notice on BSE would confirm it. The second is the BIS IS 5470 mandatory standard for feed grade DCP, dated 1 April 2027 by a secondary source, which can move DCP volume toward compliant producers. The third is share gain, because gelatine volume rose 16% in FY26 against a market near 6%, and India HS 3503 gelatine import value and volume and peer capacity commissioning at IGCL and Nitta will show whether that gain holds. DCP has no moat: it is a spot priced commodity and the company takes the market price. In gelatine the one evidenced edge is cost, where a 2.15 MW solar plant and lower coal prices cut power and fuel from 13.0% to 10.8% of revenue. The Emerging Moat scan scored 11.7, just under the 12 line for a modest moat, rated no category Strong or Moderate, and scored zero on talent asymmetry and on the cannibalisation barrier. Certifications such as EDQM, Kosher and Halal are table stakes, and the group know how that the rating agency credits runs through a related party with undisclosed pricing, so the bone and crushed bone supply price, which sets cost for both lines, stays outside the company's control.

## Gelatine margin reset, cause not yet proven

Narmada Gelatines is a five decade old bone gelatine maker, once Shaw Wallace Gelatines, with one plant in Madhya Pradesh. FY26 standalone revenue was ₹215 Cr and PAT ₹28 Cr. In June 2023 the Pioneer Jellice group and Ashok Matches bought 75% from Alfamont of Mauritius, an absentee financial owner. The buyers are gelatine operators with a Japanese gelatine partner, Jellice. Before the sale, the old owner paid out 396% of FY23 profit, and reserves fell from ₹139 Cr to ₹88 Cr. The new owners kept the managing director, who has run the company since 2009, and the statutory auditor. The company holds no earnings calls and issues no investor presentations.

Operating margin went from 13.0% in FY25 to 19.2% in FY26, then 22.3% in the March 2026 quarter and 20.8% in the June 2026 quarter. ROCE reached 28% in FY26, against 4% to 12% in FY16 to FY22. The cost lines explain the move. Materials fell 511 basis points as a share of revenue and power and fuel fell 217. Selling prices did little: DCP prices rose 17% and gelatine prices fell 5%, a net gain of about ₹84 lakh against a ₹1,540 lakh rise in profit before tax. Two readings fit. Under the first, the group's know how, the solar plant and better yield built a lasting cost position. Under the second, bone and global gelatine prices fell for a while. The evidence splits by line. The materials ratio fell even further at IGCL, 727 basis points, which points to an industry effect that can reverse. The fuel line fell at Narmada while IGCL's rose 87 basis points, which points to a company effect. CARE had forecast the bone price fall in April 2025, before it happened. The separating observation is the materials ratio in the coming quarters: at or under 52% of revenue while IGCL's ratio does not rebound.

Gate 0 scores the company 66 of 160, GOOD, with a core of 60, the exact floor of that band. The low parts are history. Return on capital scores 6 of 20 because of the FY16 to FY22 years under the old owner. Growth scores 5 of 20, with revenue compounding 4.4% a year over eleven years. The balance sheet scores 19 of 20. Net debt is ₹381 lakh, interest cover is 38.5 times and the current ratio is 3.59. The notes score 7 of 10 for accounting quality with no red item. The audit opinion is unmodified, with no key audit matter and no adverse CARO remark. Against that, the moat score is 6 of 60, THIN, and the core score sits one reading away from AVERAGE on the promoter holding test.

Cash lags profit. CFO was 86.4% of PAT in FY26, down from 99.0% in FY25. Three items drove the gap. The company paid down suppliers it had stretched in FY25: MSME payables fell from ₹952 lakh to ₹477 lakh. Receivables grew 16.9% against revenue growth of 14.0%, but the overdue share fell from 23.1% to 13.4%. Inventory stands at 156 days of materials cost. CARE calls the operating cycle "elongated at 113 days in FY25". Over twelve years CFO was 0.78 times PAT, and over the last three years 0.95. The most evidenced path reads FY26 as growth plus a one time payables unwind, not decay. The half year cash flow filed with the September 2026 quarter tests that reading.

Management makes no forward promise, so its credibility grade is the default C. The solar plant and the fuel saving were delivered and show in the accounts. Every other test runs the other way. The annual report's front sections contradict its own notes. The MD&A says materials consumed rose 1.29% when the note shows a 1.29% fall. It prints EBITDA of ₹40.88 Cr against ₹42.08 Cr from the accounts. It shows the managing director's pay up 10.41% while the related party note shows 49.7%. The ₹33 Cr expansion does not appear in the annual report at all. Between CARE's April 2025 and March 2026 releases its scope moved and its date moved to January 2027, yet the CWIP note says there is no time delay. CARE says the company has started importing raw material, but the annual report shows no raw material import and no foreign currency exposure.

The promoter review returns CAUTION, with no red finding. On 5 December 2024 Narmada put ₹41.15 Cr into IGCL, a listed rival, on the same day the promoter bodies signed to buy control of it. The stake is 29.1% of Narmada's equity, earns a 1.2% cash yield, carries no impairment test, and sits ₹1,378 lakh above its share of IGCL's net worth. Narmada's minority holders helped fund a control position whose benefit sits with the promoters. Purchases from Pioneer Jellice were ₹1,482 lakh, 12.7% of purchases, with no pricing benchmark, and two new lines appeared in FY26 for consultancy and rent. The audit committee chair also sits on IGCL's board. Against this, no SEBI order, court case, pledge or auditor event turned up in any searched source. The public float was restored to 25%. The managing director is 78 by the AGM notice and 79 by the May 2026 results filing, on a two year term to May 2028, with no successor named.

Peers confirm the price side and say nothing on bone. Only one peer holds calls, Natural Capsules, and it buys gelatine rather than makes it. It says gelatine prices fell about 4% in FY25 and 4% to 5% in the first half of FY26, softening for six quarters. On screener data, Nitta Gelatin's quarterly operating margin rose from 18% in March 2025 to 28% in March 2026. Producer margins widened while selling prices fell, so the gain looks like a spread from cheaper bone that the whole industry shared. No peer claim was verified by two peers and none was contradicted. No peer gives a bone price series, the one figure that would separate the two readings.

The market has room but the company's own figure overstates it. India bone gelatine plus feed DCP sizes at ₹3,647 Cr to ₹3,896 Cr, growing about 6.9% a year, and Narmada holds about 7% of the ₹3,087 Cr serviceable part. Runway grades GOOD. Management quotes a global gelatine market of USD 3.60 bn, 9.4 times the India figure, which is a scope mismatch rather than a guide. The three year obtainable revenue of ₹339 Cr sits above the ₹263 Cr the expanded plant can make at flat prices, and the run found no utilisation figure to close that gap. Two India gelatine estimates differ by 1.83 times, and the DCP market rests on one secondary source.
