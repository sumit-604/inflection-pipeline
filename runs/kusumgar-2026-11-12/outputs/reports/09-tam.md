## SECTION 1: MARKET DEFINITION

1A Scope.
- Product: woven, coated, laminated synthetic engineered fabrics, plus A&D finished solutions (parachute systems, camouflage, rapid deployment) (Pros p.190).
- Geography: India domestic consumption including imports and excluding exports (the report's own basis, Pros p.152). Global is shown separately in Section 2. Kusumgar exports are 39.99% of FY26 contract revenue (B04 via Pros p.190); export SAM is not sized.
- Customers: Indian and foreign militaries and government buyers, private integrators, auto and industrial converters, global outdoor brands that nominate Kusumgar to garment makers (Pros p.190).
- Channel: direct tenders, PSU buyers (Gliders India, Troop Comforts), overseas agents, brand nomination (Call p.13).
- Included: A&D fabrics, A&D solutions, industrial custom and MRG fabrics, outdoor and lifestyle fabrics. Excluded: automobile product class (airbags, seat belts, tyre cord, tapes: 51.4% of the industrial pool, Pros p.172); medical, construction and other end uses (4.9% of the India pool, Pros p.152). Caveat: Kusumgar sells automotive tapes (Pros p.190), which sit in the excluded automobile class. Size of the tape slice: NOT FOUND.

1B Management claim. The prospectus quotes the India engineered fabrics market at ₹990.0 bn (₹99,000 Cr) in FY26, rising to ₹1,864.7 bn at 13.5% CAGR to FY31; global US$67.8 bn in 2025 to US$112.2 bn in 2030 (Pros p.152, p.149, p.189). No company-specific TAM is given in the calls or decks. Credibility read: broad. It is the whole industry pool, including product classes Kusumgar does not make. The "Rs 2,000 Cr by FY30" statement is memory from the Step 1 brief, not a filed claim, and is not used here. Filed management statements: "large underlying demand" in outdoor (Call p.4), no formal guidance (Call p.6-7), FY27 margin "very similar" to FY25-26 (Call p.7).

## SECTION 3: SAM AND SOM

3A SAM. Five filters on realistic TAM 99,229 Cr:
1. End-use: realistic TAM already excludes "others" 4,900 Cr (Pros p.152).
2. Product fit: remove the automobile class, 51.4% of 56,000 = 28,784 Cr (Pros p.172). Keep custom technical 20.4% plus MRG 15.7% = 36.1% x 56,000 = 20,216 Cr. The remaining 12.5% (7,000 Cr, filtration, bolting cloth, abrasives and similar) is also dropped. Tapes sit in the dropped class (size NOT FOUND). Build: A&D 5,850 + industrial 20,216 + outdoor 32,250 = 58,316; plus solutions proxy 5,129 = 63,445.
3. Geography: India only; no further cut (plants serve all India). Exports excluded from SAM.
4. Channel and customer: tender qualification takes 2 to 10 years (Pros p.164); the share of the pool Kusumgar is qualified for is NOT FOUND. Not applied.
5. Capability: sub-splits of tactical gear and outdoor (footwear, mattresses, workwear) NOT FOUND. Not applied.

SAM = 63,445 Cr = 63.9% of realistic TAM. Because filters 4 and 5 are not applied, SAM is an upper bound.

3B SOM. Base FY26 contract revenue 674.81 Cr (6,748.14 mn, Pros p.190). Segments (Cr): A&D fabrics 213.70, A&D solutions 155.02, industrial 164.86, outdoor 125.32, other 15.92. Domestic 60.01% = 404.9 Cr; export 39.99% = 269.9 Cr. Current domestic share of SAM = 404.9 / 63,445 = 0.64%.

Share by segment (total revenue incl. exports, so an upper bound): A&D fabrics 3.65% of 5,850; solutions 3.02% of 5,129 (proxy); industrial 0.82% of 20,216; outdoor 0.39% of 32,250.

Share-gain pp rules fail at this share: +1pp of SAM = 634 Cr, more than the 582 Cr of capacity headroom at FY26 mix (3C: 1,257 - 675). Capacity binds first. So the SOM holds share by segment and grows each segment at its market rate.
- Domestic: mix-weighted India CAGR. A&D fabrics and solutions 20.0% (solutions use the A&D rate as proxy), industrial 14.0%, outdoor 10.0%, other 13.5%. (54.64 x 20.0 + 24.43 x 14.0 + 18.57 x 10.0 + 2.36 x 13.5) / 100 = 16.52%.
- Export: global CAGRs with solutions at 2.5%: (31.67 x 10.0 + 22.97 x 2.5 + 24.43 x 8.5 + 18.57 x 12.5 + 2.36 x 10.6) / 100 = 8.39%. The export split by segment is NOT FOUND; the company mix is applied uniformly.

Two readings of the path (rule: name both, and the separating observation).
- Reading A (carried, central): the market-ride SOM above. Evidence: FY26 revenue fell 11.2% from FY25 (7,789.97 to 6,920.03 mn, Pros p.185); management gives no guidance (Call p.6-7); non-order H1 FY27 revenue is at least 63.6% of 498.19 = 316.8 Cr (B04), which annualises to 633.6 Cr.
- Reading B: run-rate anchored. H1 FY27 498.19 Cr x 2 = 996.4 Cr (derived; Q2 FY27 250.9 Cr per B04). Grow at the blended 13.5%: FY29 1,283 Cr, FY31 1,653 Cr. CAGR from 692.0: (1,283 / 692.0)^(1/3) = 22.9%; (1,653 / 692.0)^(1/5) = 19.0%.
- Separating observation: H2 FY27 revenue excluding the 2,371.96 mn A&D Solutions order. The non-order floor in H1 FY27 was 316.8 Cr (about 158 Cr a quarter). If H2 ex-order revenue clearly exceeds that floor, B is closer; if it stays near it, A holds. This stage does not choose on forward evidence; Stage 11 owns that.

3C Capacity cross-check. Installed final-output capacity 127.80 mn m, production 63.26 mn m, 49.50% in FY26 (Pros p.204). Management: 55-60% in Aug 2026, "large capex came fully online last year", no large capex planned, maintenance 5-10% of gross block (Call p.11-12). Revenue per metre produced FY26 98.4 INR, FY25 165.5 (B04, derived).
- Ceiling at FY26 mix: 127.80 x 98.4 = 1,257.5 Cr at 100%; 1,006 Cr at 80%.
- B07 capex-embedded growth 5.0% (CWIP 101.04 mn x 3.40x = 343.5 mn = 4.96% of FY26 revenue; 7.83% with the 58.33 mn commitment; dates differ). Committed capex adds little; growth must come from filling idle capacity.
- Reading A: FY29 984 Cr = 78.3% of the 100% ceiling: sufficient. FY31 1,273 Cr = 101.2%: gap 15.6 Cr at 100%, 267 Cr against the 80% ceiling. Needed revenue per metre at 100% fill: 99.6 INR (FY26: 98.4).
- Reading B FY31 1,653 Cr: gap 396 Cr at 100% on FY26 mix; needs 129.3 INR per metre at 100% fill, 161.7 at 80% (close to FY25's 165.5). B is reachable only with a richer A&D mix.
- Which side is optimistic: under A, the SOM is optimistic only if mix stays fabric-heavy; the capex plan is not the optimistic side. Under B, the SOM is the optimistic side unless revenue per metre returns to FY25 levels. Single utilisation figures are not comparable across runs (B04), so treat metres as a bound, not a forecast.

## SECTION 4: DRIVERS, RISKS, STRUCTURE

4A Drivers (impact, evidence).
- Import substitution / regulatory tailwind: high for A&D. 75% of capital acquisition (₹1.39 lakh crore) reserved for domestic industry in the FY27 budget (web: indiasworld.in/defence-budget-2026-27-higher-capital-outlay-to-encourage-local-production/); the report cites duty cuts on looms (Pros p.176).
- Penetration/new applications: India A&D fabrics 17.0% FY20-26 and 20.0% to FY31 (Pros p.161).
- Geographic expansion / China+1: outdoor brands shifting supply to India (Pros p.178, p.182); US tariff swings cut both ways (Pros p.154; Call p.6).
- Premiumisation: forward integration into solutions (Call p.3). Formalisation: no evidence in corpus (unorganised share NOT FOUND).
- Technology: partner-licensed designs, no registered IP (B04 flag).

4B Risks (monitoring signal).
- Order lumpiness and tender timing: quarterly revenue under 250.9 Cr.
- US tariffs (Section 301 proposal of 12.5% for India, Pros p.154): the US is 10-15% of revenue (Call p.7); watch the Federal Register.
- Import competition and pricing pressure from large global players (Pros p.188).
- Raw materials: imported inputs 38.91% of materials (Q2 Pres Q13); nylon and polyester price moves.
- Substitution/technology: nanofibre and smart textiles could obsolete capacity (Pros p.188).
- Concentration: two single orders drove FY25 (B04 FLAG-LUMPY-MIX).

4C Structure. Competitors: global leaders DuPont, Freudenberg, TenCate, Milliken (Pros p.149); domestic peers Gliders India (mandated products; management calls it a "peer", Call p.13), Garware, others. Top-3 concentration: NOT FOUND. Organised vs unorganised: NOT FOUND. Direction: management says competition rises for simple fabrics such as rainwear and is lower for parachute systems (Call p.13). Import share trend: NOT FOUND. Entry barriers: qualification cycles of 2 to 10 years in defence (Pros p.164).

## SECTION 5: SUMMARY AND RUNWAY

5B Runway. Headroom = SAM / revenue: 63,445 / 404.9 = 156.7x on domestic revenue (94.0x if total revenue 674.8 is used). TAM growth 13.1% (FY26-31, report). Company: FY24 4,679.08 to FY26 6,920.03 mn = 21.6% CAGR (Pros p.185), gaining share, but FY26 fell 11.2% from FY25. Years to saturate SAM at 13.5%: not reached in any horizon (SAM itself grows 13.1%).

5C Class: MASSIVE on market headroom. The market is not the constraint; qualification, order timing and capacity fill are.

5D SAM expansion levers. Pursued per management: new areas including carbon prepreg and decoys with a Russian and a Japanese partner (Call p.8), and possible capex "still taking shape" (Call p.12); sizes NOT FOUND. Export SAM (global A&D US$5.4 bn, global outdoor US$21.5 bn) is not sized into SAM.
