### 1C. Trigger evolution across the three quarters

- **aiRA — strengthening, quantification rising each quarter.** Q3 FY26: 10-15
  of 115 customers in POC/post-POC, no revenue figure, "probably a good
  question to answer in a couple of quarters" (Q3 FY26 call, Aneesh, p.12).
  Q4 FY26: "about a fourth of our customers" piloting or paying, revenue "a
  few million dollars" (Q4 FY26 call, Aneesh, p.3). Q1 FY27: $2-2.5m ARR, 26
  of ~150 customers live, fewer than 10 actually paying, target 5-10% of
  revenue this year (Q1 FY27 call, Aneesh, p.4,10). NOTE: the Q4 "a fourth
  piloting or paying" framing and the Q1 "26 of 150 live, <10 paying" framing
  are not on a consistent basis (piloting bucketed with paying in Q4, split
  apart in Q1) — a specificity inconsistency, not necessarily a reversal.
- **M&A — strengthening, new trigger added.** Kognitiv (closed May-2025) →
  SessionM ($20m gross / Rs 17 Cr net after debt true-up, closed 01-May-2026,
  Q4 FY26 call p.4 and Q1 FY27 call p.16) → CustomerGlu/Experiences+ tuck-in
  ("less than a few 100K", Q1 FY27 call, Anant, p.22), a NEW trigger that
  appeared only in Q1 FY27. Rationale given (low-code front-end SDK
  complementing aiRA Action Agents) is coherent and the spend is immaterial,
  so this reads as a genuine capability tuck-in, not a discipline slip.
- **Tech+corporate cost as % of revenue — quietly dropped as a standalone
  metric.** Given explicitly in Q3 FY26 call (28%→18%, "definitely more
  leverage left", Aneesh p.15) but never repeated in that exact form in Q4 or
  Q1; management switched to reporting blended and organic EBITDA margin
  instead. Margin did keep expanding (see 2A), so this looks like a reporting
  simplification rather than a cover for a miss, but the specific metric is
  no longer independently trackable.
- **Cash-conversion ratio — raised once, never revisited.** Q3 FY26 call named
  105-110% as the "sustainable" OCF/EBITDA range and called the 9M-FY26 142%
  figure "an anomaly...on back of higher collections" (Anant, p.8). FY26
  full-year OCF Rs 150 Cr / adjusted EBITDA Rs 107 Cr ≈ 140% (Q4 FY26 call,
  Anant, p.9) — still well above the "sustainable" range management itself
  named, and neither the Q4 nor the Q1 call reconciles this. Flagged in 4D.
- **FY27 revenue guidance — sharpening, not slipping.** Rs 1,000-1,050 Cr
  range (Q4 FY26 call) sharpened to a specific "will definitely beat Rs 1,065
  Cr" reaffirmation one quarter later (Q1 FY27 call, Aneesh, p.23), with Q1
  ARR already at Rs 1,026.6 Cr. Direction of travel is upward, not a walk-back.
- **Kognitiv migration timeline — modest slippage, disclosed, not hidden.**
  Q4 FY26 call spoke generally of a "12-to-18-month type period" for
  AI-assisted upgrades (Aneesh, p.5). Q1 FY27 call gives concrete dates: first
  customer live 01-Sep-2026, full completion targeted "latest September of
  next year" (2027) — roughly 28 months from the May-2025 acquisition date,
  longer than the 12-18 month framing voiced in Q4. Management volunteered
  the more specific (and longer) timeline itself rather than being pressed
  into it, which is a mitigating point on credibility even though the
  original framing understated the cycle.

### 2B. Excuse pattern analysis

No genuine "miss" required an external-blame excuse in these three calls;
the one clear miss (cash-conversion ratio, above) was simply not addressed,
which is closer to **silence** than to blame-shifting. Where results
underperformed a prior-year comparison (Q3 FY26 PAT down YoY), management
attributed it to two named, quantified one-offs (Direct Labour Code gratuity
impact Rs 1.6 Cr, IPO expenses Rs 2 Cr — Q3 FY26 call, Anant, p.7) and showed
the normalised comparison was flat — an **honest-admission** pattern, not
deflection. The Q1 FY27 fraud loss is attributed to an external criminal act
(deepfake social engineering) with no attempt to minimise its occurrence,
but management also did not proactively name the parallel forensic audit
already announced four days before the call (see 2D). Overall pattern:
**balanced to honest on operating misses; understated on the governance
event.**

Pattern check: management raises hard topics only when the regulatory
disclosure clock forces it (fraud, forensic audit — both via separate Reg 30
filings, not volunteered in extra depth on the call); on operating misses
(PAT dip, margin softness) they proactively flag and quantify before analysts
ask. They credit "we" for organic wins and blame external/criminal actors for
the fraud — a normal pattern, not an inflated one, since the fraud genuinely
was external. No instance across three calls of "we made a mistake" language
tied to an internal decision.

### 2D. What they are NOT saying

- **The forensic audit.** KPMG was engaged by the Audit Committee on
  31-Jul-2026 (Reg 30 filing) to probe the EUR 3.0m cyber-fraud, four days
  before the 04-Aug-2026 Q1 FY27 call. Management discussed the fraud on the
  call (Shaurya Yadav and Jayesh Sagar questions, p.9-11) but never named the
  forensic audit, KPMG, or the internal-control review already underway.
  Likely reason: the audit's scope explicitly covers "internal financial
  controls," and naming it invites questions management cannot yet answer.
- **Customer concentration.** Top-10 concentration (58.71% of FY25 revenue
  per filings) is never named or quantified in any of the three calls. The
  "one large healthcare customer" whose flat growth dragged Q1 FY27 organic
  NRR from 116% to 111% (Q1 FY27 call, Aneesh, p.4) is discussed repeatedly
  but never named or sized as a % of revenue. Likely reason: naming the
  account or the concentration ratio invites a re-rating of single-customer
  risk that management would rather keep implicit.
- **aiRA churn/retention.** Sanjay Kumar Elangovan directly asked about churn
  and retention on the early aiRA cohort (Q1 FY27 call, p.18); management
  answered only on gross margin ("upwards of 80%"), not on churn. Likely
  reason: cohort is too new and too small (<10 paying customers) to have a
  meaningful churn number, but the gap was not stated as such.
- **Exact rupee quantum of the fraud loss, on the call itself.** The Rs 33.4
  Cr Q1 FY27 exceptional loss (per filings) is referenced only qualitatively
  on the call ("one-time exceptional loss which happened due to a
  cyber-banking fraud incident and a one-time deferred tax liability" —
  Anant, Q1 FY27 call, p.8) without breaking the two items apart in rupee
  terms.

## SECTION 3: COMPETITIVE INTELLIGENCE FROM CONCALLS

### 3A. Competitor commentary and credibility check

Management names competitors specifically and consistently: marketing
agencies Epsilon ("last reported revenue... $2 billion"), Merkle/Dentsu
("upwards of $0.5 billion"), Kobie, Bond Brand (Q1 FY27 call, Aneesh, p.20);
software-only players Salesforce ("small loyalty product") and Oracle
CrowdTwist ("haven't seen in competition for many years now" — Q1 FY27 call,
p.20). The figures cited are specific and plausible (order of magnitude
matches public knowledge of Epsilon/Merkle scale) but are not independently
verified in this corpus — flagged for peer/live-web verification (4B).
Credibility: moderate-high; specific names and numbers rather than vague
claims, consistent across calls, but self-serving (frames rivals as
people-heavy agencies against Capillary's software model).

### 3B. Industry and market intelligence

- Loyalty software is "far less than 10% of actual revenues" in the loyalty
  market versus agencies (Q1 FY27 call, Aneesh, p.20) — an unsourced claim,
  a peer-verification candidate.
- "Roughly about 90 companies" globally in the $10m-$100m revenue bracket
  that fit Capillary's M&A thesis (Q1 FY27 call, Aneesh, p.18) — pipeline
  sizing claim, unsourced.
- Enterprise AI adoption is "abysmal" because "most large enterprises live
  on risk... you don't get fired for doing nothing" (Q1 FY27 call, Aneesh,
  p.17) — a general sector observation, not specific to Capillary's
  customers, offered as a reason large enterprises won't leapfrog to
  in-house AI-built loyalty systems.
- US healthcare payer sector pressure (rate increases, Medicare book
  weakness) was flagged by an analyst; management said Capillary has no
  Medicare exposure and instead saw a member-base step-up (~50%) at its
  large healthcare customer from a 1-Jan renewal cycle (Q3 FY26 call,
  Aneesh, p.9-10).

### 3C. Toughest analyst questions

1. **AI/agentic disruption risk to SaaS** (Rishi Jhunjhunwala, IIFL, Q3 FY26
   call, p.8-9). Management's answer (system-of-record vs
   system-of-engagement/intelligence framing) was specific and internally
   consistent, and was reaffirmed unprompted in the Q1 FY27 call (agentic
   commerce question). Satisfactory in depth; the underlying risk (AI
   compressing loyalty-agency and analytics headcount, and eventually
   loyalty software margins) is real and cannot be fully resolved by
   management's own framing — a live risk to monitor, not one to dismiss.
2. **US healthcare payer exposure** (Rishi Jhunjhunwala, Q3 FY26 call, p.9).
   Answered with specifics (no Medicare exposure, direct member-base data).
   Satisfactory.
3. **NRR/ACV/organic definitions confusion** (Akshay Jogani, Q1 FY27 call,
   p.15-17). Not adversarial but revealing: the metrics require several
   follow-up questions to pin down precisely (e.g., an upgraded Kognitiv
   customer's revenue is excluded from NRR in the year of migration but
   included in "organic" only the following year). Answered fully but shows
   real complexity in how the headline growth/retention numbers are built —
   a real risk for anyone taking NRR/organic figures at face value without
   the full bridge.
4. **Guidance-raise pressure** (Kumar Saurabh, Q1 FY27 call, p.23). Management
   declined to raise guidance despite Q1 outperformance, a conservative and
   credible stance rather than a dodge.
