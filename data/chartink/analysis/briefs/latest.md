## Where the market stands

Think of each stock as having three clocks. The 20 day line is the short clock, the 50 day line is the middle clock, and the 200 day line is the long clock. A stock above a line is in an uptrend on that clock. On Tuesday 1,253 stocks stood above the short line, a little over half the market. 1,103 stood above the middle line and 1,065 above the long line. So the buyers hold the short clock and the sellers hold the other two. The gap on the long clock is about 210 stocks.

The year gives these counts their meaning. The long count peaked at about 1,360 stocks on 26 August. 23 September was the last day when more stocks stood above the long line than below it. October broke the floor. On 21 October, 412 stocks fell hard in a single day. Two days later only 605 stocks held the long line, and only 188 held the short line. That was the low. From there the market climbed for three weeks. The long count has gained about 460 stocks since the low. It still sits about 105 stocks short of half.

The climb had a shape. Early November brought wide up days. On 4 November, 319 stocks rose four and a half percent or more in one session. The short count crossed half the market that day for the first time since 26 August. By 12 November about three stocks in four stood above their ten day line. Then the pace slowed. Monday 16 November was the first hard test of the bounce. 215 stocks fell hard, the most since 30 October, and the short count fell by more than 300 stocks in one day. Tuesday was the answer to that test. The selling did not come back. Only 46 stocks fell hard, an ordinary number for this month. But no strong lift came either.

The detail inside Tuesday's session tells the same story. About four stocks in ten stayed inside a narrow three percent band for the day, up from three in ten on Monday. About one stock in nine traded four percent below its prior close at some point. About a third of those closed down there. On Monday more than half did. So the sellers pushed less often, and buyers met them when they did. On the other side, about one stock in six traded four percent above its prior close. Only about a third of those kept the gain into the close.

The money behind the moves was small. Turnover came to about 456 crore, against about 523 crore on the same session a year ago. About four stocks traded on heavy volume for every ten that traded on light volume. The thrust days of early November came on turnover near 510 to 605 crore. Tuesday was well short of that.

Fear stayed cheap. The India VIX measures what traders pay for protection against large swings in the Nifty. It fell about one percent to 15.5. That is about eight percent above its thirty week line. It is up almost forty percent over three months, yet it still costs little against this year's range. One cross asset gap stands out. Gold funds are down about nine percent over the month. The Nifty is up about thirteen percent over the same month. Money has left the safe asset and gone into the index.

## What would change this read

The read is an early uptrend that took a hard hit on Monday and rested on Tuesday. Banks lead it, and a narrow group of small leaders keeps climbing the screens. The constructive view fails if hard selling comes back soon. A day of 300 or more hard fallers would match the October pattern. A long count under about 1,050 would say the progress of mid November is gone, and Tuesday closed at 1,065. A short count under half the market, about 1,170, would hand the last clock back to the sellers, and that count is drifting down from 1,253. A long count under about 605, the 23 October low, would erase the whole bounce. New yearly lows above about 100 would say sellers have returned in force. A VIX close above about 18 would say fear has taken charge. A sharp fall in banks on heavy volume would remove the group that carries the index.

The cautious view fails if the repair picks up again. The first sign is the 50 day count back above half the market, about 1,170, from 1,103 on Tuesday. The second is the long count turning toward half, also about 1,170, from 1,065. The third is a wide up day, with 200 or more hard risers on turnover above about 600 crore. The fourth is a VIX close under 15. The fifth is gains holding into the close, with about half of the stocks that trade four percent up keeping the gain, against a third on Tuesday. The first sign, with any one of the others in the same week, would put the repair back on track.
