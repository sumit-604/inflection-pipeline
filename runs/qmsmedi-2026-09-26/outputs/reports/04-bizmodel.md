## SECTION 1: THE BUSINESS MODEL IN PLAIN ENGLISH

### 1A. One-line description
QMS buys and resells branded medical devices to pharma companies and
hospitals, and separately rents out its own field force and call centre to
pharma companies who want to run patient health camps and follow-up
programmes (Inv. Pres. Q4&FY26 slide 11 "leading distributor and marketer
... expanded into ... Patient Service Program"; AR p.51 MD&A).

### 1E. The chai-stall-uncle version
Picture a wholesaler who buys blood-pressure machines from famous foreign
brands and sells them on to hospitals and chemists, keeping the markup as
profit. That is roughly seven of every ten rupees QMS earns. Now picture a
separate business next door: pharma companies pay this wholesaler's cousin
to run free health check-up camps and to call patients every month, making
sure they keep taking the medicine the doctor prescribed. That is the other
three rupees in ten. The two businesses share a name, a balance sheet and a
call centre, but they are not run on the same money-making logic: one is
"buy low, sell a bit higher" (Products), the other is "rent out people's
time and a phone line" (Services). The company's own annual report never
tells shareholders how much profit comes from each side (B02 Finding 1).

### 2D. Industry lifecycle stage
The underlying market (Indian healthcare distribution + pharma-funded
patient support programmes) is in a growth phase: AR p.54 describes
"significant investment from both the private and public space, with a
specific interest in preventative healthcare as well as patient support
solutions," and the Inv. Pres. cites a "$70B" worldwide PSP market figure
(slide 21, unverified for the Indian sub-market specifically, PENDING LIVE
VERIFICATION). QMS itself sits in an early-scale-up position within that
growth market: revenue crossed Rs 100 Cr only in 2020 and Rs 173 Cr in
FY26 (Inv. Pres. slide 13 timeline), and the Services line (PSP/camps) is
the newer, faster-narrative-growth half of the business even though it is
still the minority of revenue (31% FY26).

### 4B. Valuation method applicability

| Method | Applicable? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| SOTP (sum-of-the-parts) | **PRIMARY** | The business is genuinely two economically different models (trading-margin Products at 69% of revenue vs fee/programme Services at 31%) with materially different profitability profiles at the subsidiary level (Saarathi standalone net margin ~20.7% vs parent standalone ~4.4%, B02 Finding 11). A single blended multiple hides this; B00's own sector-cap note already flags the Services slice as a SOTP candidate under a different row (Consulting/Engineering services or Hospitals/dialysis/healthcare services) than the Products slice (Distribution/trading). The actual exit multiples for each slice are a Section 1B / Stage 11 decision, not this stage's. |
| EV/EBITDA relative (blended) | **SECONDARY** | Useful cross-check against the chosen peer set (INDGN, ENTERO, POLYMED) at the consolidated level, but no single peer matches QMS's Products+Services combination (see 2B), so this must be read alongside the SOTP, not instead of it |
| DCF | **TERTIARY, low confidence currently** | Cash-conversion quality is currently unreliable: normalized CFO/PAT (ex short-term-borrowings) is 0.23x-0.31x FY26 (B03), and the audited-note DSCR has fallen below 1.0x. A DCF built on reported cash flows would overstate quality; revisit once normalized operating cash flow is confirmed for 2+ quarters |
| Asset-based / NAV | NOT APPLICABLE | This is a genuine operating business (R0 non-operating rung does not apply); no holding-company or accounting-driven earnings issue is evidenced |
| Dividend discount model | NOT APPLICABLE | Company is in a reinvestment/scale-up phase (rights issue Sep-2025 to fund the Saarathi stake increase); dividend is not the return driver for this thesis |
| Cycle stage relevant to valuation | Early growth-scale-up within a structurally growing sub-sector (see 2D); the valuation approach should not anchor to trailing multi-year averages (B01: "downstream stages should treat FY26, not the multi-year average, as the base case starting point") |

### 4D. Highest-value questions for management

1. **Q:** Will QMS disclose a segment result (revenue and profit by Products vs Services) in the FY27 AR? **Reassures:** yes, with FY26 comparatives restated. **Worries:** "not currently planned" or another year of single-segment boilerplate.
2. **Q:** What drove the 3.5x YoY jump in consolidated employee cost in Q1 FY27 (Rs 3.2 Cr to Rs 11.2 Cr) while COGS stayed flat? **Reassures:** a one-time Saarathi-integration/backend build-out that should normalize as a % of revenue. **Worries:** a structural, recurring cost base that keeps rising faster than Services revenue.
3. **Q:** What interest rate applies to the inter-corporate deposit QMS (parent) owes Saarathi, and how was it set given Saarathi's minority shareholders? **Reassures:** a disclosed, market-benchmarked rate (e.g., matching the 9% p.a. charged on the unrelated One Billion Diagnostics loan). **Worries:** no rate, or a below-market rate favouring the parent.
4. **Q:** What is Q-Devices' standalone revenue and gross margin, separate from third-party brand distribution? **Reassures:** a growing, clearly better-margin own-brand line. **Worries:** "not separately tracked" or a still-negligible contribution despite three years since launch.
5. **Q:** Now that receivables >6-month aged share has doubled and DSCR has fallen below 1.0x, what is the collection and deleveraging plan? **Reassures:** a specific timeline and provisioning policy. **Worries:** no plan, continued zero ECL provisioning.
6. **Q:** What is the actual per-camp and per-PSP fee structure, and has pricing changed as camp/PSP volumes scaled? **Reassures:** stable or rising per-unit fees alongside volume growth. **Worries:** volume-only growth with flat or falling per-unit economics.
7. **Q:** Beyond the CDSCO licence, what stops a "smaller trader or independent local supplier" (the LOF's own words) from replicating the Products distribution model? **Reassures:** a named, durable advantage (e.g., an exclusivity clause with 3M/Heine with a long remaining term). **Worries:** no durable advantage beyond incumbency.
