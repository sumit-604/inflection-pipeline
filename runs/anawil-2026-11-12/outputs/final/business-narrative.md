# Business understanding narrative

Anawil Wire and Engineering Ltd (ANAWIL). Run 2026-11-12. Phase 3 final, written 2026-11-17.

Anawil rolls and welds thick steel plate into tubular towers that hold onshore wind turbines up; it sells no wire. A turbine cannot stand without its tower, and the sections are so large and costly to move that a plant near the wind states has an edge. In integrated supply, 64% of first half FY27 revenue, Anawil buys the plate and sells a finished tower for Rs 3.0 to 3.5 Cr. In pure conversion, 21% of revenue, the customer sends the steel and Anawil charges Rs 50 to 60 lakh a tower, while conversion plus internals, 11%, adds Anawil's own ladders, platforms and cable racks for Rs 1.0 to 1.1 Cr. The buyers are wind turbine makers and developers: the order book names Envision and Sany towers, and ten customers took 97% of FY26 revenue. No customer has a long term agreement, one customer holds 37% of the book, and each plant needs customer approval before it ships, which the Kutch plant still awaits. Present demand comes from installations, and India added a record 6.05 GW of wind in FY26, which the MNRE monthly installed wind capacity, state-wise (Gujarat and Karnataka) signal tracks. Envision towers are 49 of the 320 units in the book and Sany towers are 111. The Envision India wind installations and tower sourcing (EN-156 3.3 MW, EN-182 5 MW) signal and the Sany Renewable Energy India installations and tower sourcing signal therefore set the near term pull, and new orders show first in the Anawil order intimations and H1 FY27 call transcript (Customer A 36.6% of book). The industry roadmap rises from 8 GW in FY27 to 13.2 GW in FY31, about 17% a year, and the Wind and RTC/FDRE tender awards (SECI, NTPC, NHPC, state agencies) signal shows awards that become tower orders 12 to 18 months later. Heavier towers for 5 MW turbines carry more steel and more value per tower, but the run did not establish the average turbine rating that converts gigawatts into towers. Against that growth, turbine makers that build their own towers cap the outsourced pool: Inox Wind is building a South India tower plant and KP Green is entering tubular towers, both read through the Suzlon and Inox Wind quarterly deliveries, order books, tower make-or-buy and new tower plants signal. Peers name cleared sites, not fabrication, as the limit on installations, an inference that would make tower supply loose and priced by the buyer. No business line has a moat. Pure conversion earns the highest margin percent, but its fee stayed flat at about Rs 58.7 lakh a tower across two order books, and a new plant near the same states can copy it. Integrated supply earns about 3.4% on the extra steel it carries, the run did not establish whether its contracts pass steel moves to the customer, and the Hot-rolled steel plate price (Model 3 steel input) signal watches that exposure. Location, customer approvals and weld certification give partial protection at company level, and the ALMM or localisation rules extended to wind towers; tower vendor approvals signal decides which fabricators turbine makers may use. The Emerging Moat scan scored 10.8, below its lowest band at 12, with only execution and the post IPO balance sheet active, and Categories 21 and 22, talent asymmetry and the cannibalisation barrier, scored zero.

# Real tower volumes, priced ahead of proof

Anawil makes towers at Koppal in Karnataka and has a second plant at Kutch in Gujarat. It moved from wire mesh into towers in 2023 and listed on NSE Emerge on 10 August 2026. Tower output went from 114 in FY24 to 135 in FY25 and 210 in FY26. Revenue rose from Rs 54 Cr to Rs 143 Cr over the same years. The first half of FY27 gave 107 towers, Rs 154 Cr of revenue and Rs 14.4 Cr of PAT. No annual report and no call transcript exist yet, so the record rests on the Prospectus, the audited FY26 statements, the half year results, two presentations and the Crisil rating rationale.

Three things are changing. Revenue per tower doubled, from Rs 68 lakh in FY26 to Rs 1.44 Cr in the first half of FY27, because integrated supply puts steel into the bill. EBITDA margin fell from 42.7% to 23.4%, but that sets a full year against a half: like halves read 24.5% and 23.4%. EBITDA per tower was Rs 33.7 lakh in the first half of FY27, level with Rs 34.5 lakh in the second half of FY26. The second change is Kutch, rated at 192 towers a year. The Prospectus calls it commercial from March 2026, and the November release says trial runs are underway. The third change is unresolved. One steel price clause in the integrated supply contracts decides whether 64% of revenue is a steel spread business, and nobody has found it.

The backward scorecard reads 71 of 160: 66 of 100 on core financials and 5 of 60 on moats. The class is AVERAGE after a one tier cut for a four year record. Growth and returns score full marks: revenue compounded 77% a year from a FY23 ramp base, and ROCE was about 27% in FY26. Cash scored 3 of 20. FY26 capex was Rs 93 Cr, free cash flow was minus Rs 75 Cr, and working capital days rose from 68 to 109. FY26 operating cash flow printed Rs 12.5 Cr audited and Rs 18.4 Cr restated against PAT of about Rs 36 Cr; with the capex items taken out it is Rs 28 to 31 Cr. The first half of FY27 turned the other way, Rs 28.3 Cr of operating cash on Rs 14.4 Cr of PAT, mostly from a stock release. Debt fell from Rs 128 Cr to Rs 34 Cr after the IPO. The chairman then lent Rs 21.68 Cr at 6% to fund capex, on terms not disclosed. Crisil calls the 292 day working capital cycle a business requirement. The run could not say whether that cycle is structural or a growth effect.

Management delivers on the plant floor and discloses loosely. Over three months the promise record shows 2 delivered, 3 partly delivered and 1 missed, grade C. A 38 tower letter of intent became an Rs 134 Cr order. Rs 115 Cr of IPO money repaid debt in 53 days. Kutch is the miss: 16 towers in March 2026, then about Rs 3 Cr of Gujarat revenue in the whole first half. FY27 guidance of Rs 450 to 500 Cr needs 206 to 280 towers in the second half, against a best half of 148. The headline "EBITDA per tower" of Rs 32.82 lakh is a cash flow subtotal. A 24 October filing says there is no tax litigation while a Rs 3.67 Cr tax demand stands. The promoter verdict is CAUTION.

Peers contradict no claim, but they complicate it. Inox Wind, Suzlon and KP Green confirm the October to March skew, and both turbine makers missed or reset plans loaded into the second half. The two tower facts in the peer set both add capacity near Anawil. Live checks found 13 approved tower makers on Envision's EN-156 model and 6 on Sany's, against the Prospectus claim of a limited number of qualified suppliers. The market is best counted in towers: about 1,512 to 2,353 a year, or Rs 1,870 to 2,909 Cr on the book's average bill. The share rules give Anawil Rs 477 Cr by FY29 and Rs 646 Cr by FY31. The runway is GOOD, but the FY27 guidance midpoint already equals the FY29 share figure.

The valuation runs two exit multiples. The governing one is 16.1 times earnings, a cost of capital track that sets the entry zone. The additive track gives 20.3 times. The framework as written reads lower. A failed Year 3 return check gives 14.4 times on the first track, and dropping the order book's second credit gives 18.3 times on the second. At Rs 381 the stock trades at 22 times the FY27 base EPS of Rs 17.33. Fair value today is about Rs 314, so the price sits 21% above it. The Year 3 base target of Rs 443 gives 5.2% a year. Weighted across bear, base and bull, the expected price return is minus 3.0% a year on the governing track and 4.7% on the other. The entry zone is Rs 202 to Rs 227, 27% below Rs 313, the lowest price since listing. Upside against downside is 0.26 times.

The decision of record is AVOID. Two rules fire on their own: the AVERAGE backward score, and an upside under twice the downside. The valuation stage read the same facts as WATCHLIST, a hard watch with no position, and the operator still has to rule between the two. Both readings take no position today. The first leaves the zone shut until a re-scored FY27 balance sheet. The second lets it open on price. The gate recommendation is PROCEED WITH CAVEATS, because cash conversion stays INDETERMINATE.

The thesis stage found a business that compounds about 15% a year at a constant multiple, priced as if it had already climbed a rung. The devil's advocate left it WEAKENED BUT ALIVE. Its sharpest counter: the base case prices Kutch as a failure but keeps a multiple that only a Kutch success supports. In that failure world the entry sits near Rs 205. One customer holds 37% of the book and can fail the FY27 cash test by itself, billed on time or late. On a bear case priced at bear multiples, upside reaches twice downside only near Rs 216. The devil's advocate also contests the cash optic as structural. If the operator agrees, the zone does not open on price alone.
