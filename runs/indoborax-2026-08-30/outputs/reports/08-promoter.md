## SECTION 3: BUSINESS CONDUCT & ETHICAL TRACK RECORD

### 3A. Related-party transaction history — the exit-period extraction question

This is the load-bearing finding of this stage. AR FY26 Annexure-III
(AOC-2) and Note 40 to the financial statements (p.108-109) disclose, under
the explicit heading "Details of contracts or arrangements or transactions
**NOT AT ARM'S LENGTH BASIS**," a cluster of asset sales between the
company and the departing Jain family, all executed in the second half of
FY26 — **before** the SPA was signed (15-Dec-2025) but **after** the
company had already begun the exit process:

| Transaction | Counterparty | Consideration | Board approval | Special resolution |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sale of residential property | Sajal Jain, Saumya Jain, Sreelekha Jain | Rs 42.50 cr | 21-Jul-2025 | 26-Aug-2025 |
| Sale of office premises | Sreelekha Jain, Pranika Jain | Rs 14.00 cr | 21-Jul-2025 | 26-Aug-2025 |
| Leaseback of the same office premises | Sreelekha Jain, Pranika Jain | Rs 55 lakh/yr, 3-yr term, 5% p.a. escalation | 21-Jul-2025 | 26-Aug-2025 |
| Sale of 3 vehicles | Sajal Jain, Pranika Jain, Sreelekha Jain | Rs 5.52 cr | 21-Jul-2025 | 26-Aug-2025 |

Total consideration to the departing family: **Rs 62.02 cr** (42.50 + 14.00
+ 5.52), ✅ VERIFIED, cross-checked line-by-line against Note 40's
individual-party breakdown (Sale of Residential Flat: Sajal 14.45 + Sreelekha
14.025 + Saumya 14.025 = 42.50 cr; Sale of Office Premises: Pranika 7.00 +
Sreelekha 7.00 = 14.00 cr; Sale of vehicles: Sajal 1.09 + Pranika 0.284 +
Sreelekha 4.15 = 5.524 cr — all figures reconcile to the pound).

**Reading the direction of value transfer.** The office-premises sale
generated a company profit of Rs 9.35 cr on Rs 14 cr consideration (Note 34,
p.106) — implying a carrying book value of only ~Rs 4.65 cr, i.e. the
company sold at roughly 3x book. The residential-property sale sits inside
"Profit on sale of Investment Property," Rs 1.20 cr on Rs 42.50 cr
consideration — a carrying value of ~Rs 41.3 cr, i.e. sold near book. **The
pattern is asymmetric**: the office premises (large gain over book, then
leased back at an escalating rent for three years) reads as the company
divesting an appreciated asset to the departing family and then paying them
rent on it going forward — an annuity income stream for the family that
outlives their ownership. The residential flat, by contrast, priced close
to book, reads as closer to ordinary monetisation. No independent valuation
report is disclosed in the AR for either leg, so whether the *prices* were
fair to the minority cannot be confirmed from filed evidence — this is
recorded as an **open question**, not resolved either way.

**Sequencing matters.** All four transactions were approved by special
resolution on **26-Aug-2025** — nearly four months **before** the SPA was
signed (15-Dec-2025) and while the Jain family still fully controlled the
board and the vote (subject to the related-party exclusion from voting
under SEBI RPT rules — majority-of-minority approval, not unanimous
family vote). The family monetised personal-benefit RPTs first, then
negotiated their exit. This is the pattern the operator's question names:
"ordinary exit monetisation or extraction." On the evidence, it reads as
**extraction with procedural compliance** — every step was disclosed, board-
and shareholder-approved, and audited without qualification — but the
substance (sale-leaseback annuity to a departing family, timed ahead of the
control sale) is a real governance flag, not a clean handover.

**Additional item**: Rs 3.30 cr commission remains **payable** to Sajal
Jain at FY26 year-end (Note 40c, p.109: Outstanding Payables, Mr Sajal Jain,
KMP column, 330.00). Per the AR's own remuneration policy note (p.50), MD
commission is paid only after shareholders approve the financial statements
at the AGM (28-Jul-2026) — so this is a **timing artefact**, not an
irregularity, but it means the departed MD still has an active financial
claim on the company nine months after his resignation.

Company memory also flags a further ~Rs 28 cr RPT flat sale in FY25. This
AR (FY26) does not carry a FY25 comparative for the flat-sale line items
(all FY25 columns in Note 40 read nil for the sale transactions) — the FY25
transaction, if it occurred, would be disclosed in the FY25 AR, which is
not in this run's corpus. **NOT FOUND in the documents held; recorded as an
input gap**, not confirmed or refuted here.

**Other legacy RPT-adjacent item**: **Radius Estates Projects Pvt Ltd**
(formerly Vishwaroop Realtors Pvt Ltd) — an Rs 509.04 lakh Inter-Corporate
Deposit, unprovided, with the counterparty under IBC insolvency (Note 47,
p.113; Secretarial Audit Report, p.67, flags the same item as an audit
observation). Web search identifies Radius Estates/Vishwaroop Realtors as a
Mumbai real-estate group headed by Sanjay Chhabria, acquired out of
insolvency by Adani Goodhomes in a contested NCLT resolution 📰 MEDIA
REPORTED (Moneylife, AdaniWatch). **No connection found, in filed documents
or web search, between Radius Estates/Chhabria and either the Jain family
or the Malhotra family.** This reads as a legacy treasury-placement
misjudgment by the old promoter era (an ICD to an unrelated but ultimately
insolvent counterparty), not a related-party self-dealing item — but it is
a capital-allocation quality finding (Section 3B) regardless of relation.

### 3B. Capital allocation behaviour

  announced dividend is expected to be around INR440 million net of TDS and

### 3C. Promoter share transactions / pledge

**100% of Zenrock's entire Indo Borax shareholding is pledged** to Catalyst
Trusteeship Ltd to secure the acquisition financing — ✅ VERIFIED, stated
plainly by India Ratings (23-Jul-2026, p.1: "100% of the promoter
shareholding is pledged to secure Zenrock's acquisition debt, comprising
outstanding non-convertible debentures (NCD) principal worth around
INR2,550 million and a bullet repayment due in FY31"; repeated p.3-4 as a
named "key rating monitorable"). Company memory records the pledge filings
at 1,23,26,764 shares (100% of the post-open-offer Zenrock holding) via Reg
31 filings 03-Feb-2026 and 15-May-2026. **This is a rising pledge from a
zero base** (the Jain family carried no pledge in their forty-year tenure,
per the absence of any pledge disclosure in the corpus for the pre-2026
period) **to 100% within the first year of the new promoter's tenure** —
the opposite of a pledge-reduction transition signal. The rating agency
frames this as **acquisition-financing structure**, not distress: the
lenders ARE the co-investing AIFs (same EAAA house), and NCD principal has
already been paid down from Rs 390 cr to Rs 255 cr (~35% reduction) within
the ownership's first year (rating.pdf p.4). This is a real structural
risk (bullet repayment FY31, full promoter-block encumbrance until then)
but it is disclosed, rated, and monitored by an independent SEBI-registered
CRA — a materially different risk profile from an opaque or undisclosed
pledge. **Recorded as a deal-breaker trigger on the mechanical ">40%"
threshold (Section 6C), with the qualifying context stated here.**

capital at Rs 256.30/share) followed standard SEBI SAST Regulations

Advertisement in corpus).
