### 1A Boundaries
- Product scope: finished-dose generic formulations. KPL FY26 mix: injectables 48%, tablets 38%, capsules 5%, liquid orals and externals 3%, creams 2%, others 4% (AR p.92 product-wise revenue mix; the file page marker reads [page 95], printed folio 92). Oncology 22% of Q2 FY27 revenue (B04). Biologics (EPO) and hormones are pre-revenue.
- Geographic scope: LATAM (Mexico, Colombia, Chile, Venezuela named) and MENA (Algeria, Morocco, Tunisia, Saudi Arabia and GCC) per Aug 2026 call p.3-7, plus Africa and CIS per AR p.91 ("LATAM, MENA, Africa, Asia, and CIS"). EU is a small, new leg (7-8 EU registrations by CY2027, B04).
- Customer scope: distributor and licensee partners holding or co-holding registrations, plus tender buyers. End patients are not the customer. KPL-held vs partner-held revenue split: NOT FOUND (B04, B05).
- Channel scope: B2B out-licensing and supply (about 90%) and CDMO (about 10%) (Q2 Pres slide 27 via B04).
- Price segment: first and second generic in semi-regulated markets. Mgmt: "masters in the first generic and second generic market" (Aug call p.4).
- Inclusions: generics sold in LATAM and MEA, all dosage forms (realistic TAM). Injectables only (conservative TAM, see Section 2).
- Exclusions: patented drugs, OTC retail brands, the US, EU generics beyond what KPL files, CIS (no sized pool found), India-for-India sales. Domestic turnover was 50.3% of FY26 revenue (AR p.154) but management says India is about 5% and the Indian buyer SD Pharma re-exports (B05 FLAG-DOMESTIC-SHARE). End-market geography is therefore mostly export. Country split of revenue: NOT FOUND.
- Biologics and hormones are treated as option layers (Section 5D). They are not in the base TAM.

## SECTION 2: TAM ESTIMATION

### Method 1: top-down
Source A: LATAM generic drug market US$39.4 billion in 2025, 5.70% CAGR 2026-2034 (IMARC, imarcgroup.com/latin-america-generic-drug-market, fetched 2026-11-16).
Source B: Middle East and Africa generic drugs market US$35.45 billion in 2025 (Expert Market Research, search snippet 2026-11-16; page not fetched).
Calculation (realistic TAM):
- LATAM: 39.4 bn x 95 = ₹3,743 bn = ₹374,300 Cr (1 Cr = 10 mn; 3,743 bn = 374,300 Cr).
- MEA: 35.45 bn x 95 = ₹3,367.75 bn = ₹336,775 Cr.
- Sum: ₹711,075 Cr (derived). Confidence M. Staleness: 2025 data, current.
- Not subtracted, because no sourced split exists: branded generics vs commodity, retail vs tender channel, Brazil and Argentina scale, and GCC localisation. These stay in the SAM filters (Section 3A).
Narrow cut (conservative TAM, injectables only):
- Global generic injectables US$54.5 bn in 2025, CAGR 6.67% 2026-2034; regional shares LATAM 7.5%, MEA 5.8%; oncology 34.9% of the segment (IMARC, imarcgroup.com/generic-injectables-market, fetched 2026-11-16).
- LATAM: 54.5 x 7.5% = 4.0875 bn x 95 = ₹38,831 Cr. MEA: 54.5 x 5.8% = 3.161 bn x 95 = ₹30,030 Cr. Sum ₹68,861 Cr (derived). Confidence M.
- Why this is the floor: it drops the oral solids that are 43% of KPL revenue (tablets 38% + capsules 5%) because no regional oral-solid pool is sourced. Oral-solid regional pool: NOT FOUND.
Cross-source spread: global generic sterile injectables are quoted at US$46-52 bn (Coherent 46.43; Towards Healthcare and Precedence about 51; Market.us 42.9 for 2024), against IMARC 54.5. Spread is about 17% and comes from scope. The IMARC figure is used because it is the only one with a regional split. Research and Markets quotes US$131.68 bn, a different definition; not used.

### Method 2: bottom-up (registration unit)
Addressable unit: one product registration in one country. Total units and penetration of the whole market: NOT FOUND (no sourced count of generic registrations in LATAM or MEA). A full-market bottom-up TAM cannot be built.
KPL-side calibration (used to test management's runway story):
- Revenue per cumulative filing, FY26: 503.08 / 700 = ₹0.72 Cr (revenue Q2 results p.16; filings 700+ Q2 Pres slide 21). H1 FY27 annualised: 343.6 x 2 / 750 = ₹0.92 Cr (derived; 750+ filings slide 21).
- Management's per-registration figure is 1.5-2 million a year (Aug call p.4). If rupees: ₹0.15-0.20 Cr, which is 3.6x to 6.1x below the observed ₹0.72-0.92 Cr (0.72/0.20 = 3.6; 0.92/0.15 = 6.1). If US dollars: ₹14.3-19.0 Cr each (1.5-2 x 95), and 750 filings would give over ₹10,000 Cr. That is impossible. The figure does not reconcile to filed revenue on either reading. B06 reached the same split.
- Step-up test. FY29 SOM (below) adds 1,000 - 503.08 = ₹497 Cr over FY26. New registrations at the stated pace of 6-7 a quarter (Aug call p.7) give 12 quarters x 6.5 = 78 registrations; at the 60-70 in three quarters pace (Aug call p.4) about 240-280. At ₹0.72 Cr each: ₹56 Cr (78) or ₹173-201 Cr (240-280), which is 11% or 35-40% of the ₹497 Cr step. [INFERENCE] The remainder must come from ramping approvals already held (Mexico: 18-19 of 70 approved, Aug call p.7) and from oncology and mix, not from new registrations. Separating observation: revenue per registration disclosed for one quarter, plus the share of revenue from registrations under 12 months old.
Confidence in Method 2 as a TAM method: L. As a test of the runway story: M.

### 3B SOM
Current position (FY26):
- Injectable revenue = 48% x 503.08 = ₹241.5 Cr (AR p.92 mix; derived). Share of SAM = 241.5 / 54,105 = 0.45%.
- Total revenue ₹503.08 Cr as a share of realistic TAM = 0.071% (503.08 / 711,075).
Share-gain rules: the 1-2 pp (normal) and 3-5 pp (aggressive) rules do not bind. A rise from 0.07% to 0.14-0.17% of TAM is 0.07-0.10 pp. The binding limits are capacity (3C) and registration flow (Section 2, Method 2), not market share.

Two readings of the FY29 and later path (Master "no shading" rule):
- Reading A (management): ₹1,000 Cr FY29; ₹1,300-1,500 Cr FY30 including add-ons (Aug call p.6, p.13-15; B05 guidance). FY29 and FY30 core guidance excludes hormones and biologics (Q2 Pres slide 16; Aug call p.13).
- Reading B (capacity and milestone-gated): core revenue capped by plant capacity at about ₹1,080 Cr; add-ons count only after their gate is observed.
- Separating observations: (1) Q4 FY27 revenue at or above ₹200 Cr with reported blended utilisation at 80% or higher; (2) Unit 6 manufacturing licence in Nov 2026 and EPO trial start in Nov-Dec 2026 (Aug call p.3, p.7). These are the first two checkpoints.

Central SOM (most evidenced path):
- Year 3 (FY29, three years after the FY26 audited base): core ₹1,000 Cr. Evidence: H1 FY27 actual ₹343.6 Cr (Q2 Pres slide 6-7); revenue promises delivered in B05 (Q1 162.4 and Q2 181.2 Cr against 150-160 and 160-170); FY29 core sits at 93% of the physical ceiling in 3C. It also sits below management's own "25% minimum" growth for FY28 (720 x 1.25 = 900), so the guide is not the aggressive reading. SOM yr3 = ₹1,000 Cr. Optional layer: EPO India 90 and hormone tender-led 65 = +₹155 Cr, held outside the central number until the two gates in observation (2) clear.
- Year 5 (FY31): no guidance or filed evidence exists beyond FY30 (NOT FOUND). Central = committed-capacity ceiling 1,080 + gated add-ons 155 = ₹1,235 Cr. This is a ceiling-type number. Realistic utilisation below 100% lowers it. Management's ₹1,300-1,500 Cr is above it by ₹65-265 Cr and depends on uncommitted capacity (Unit 7 mega general plant: land acquired, completion by FY29 per Q2 Pres slide 23-24, spend not found) and on international EPO (₹200-250 Cr, no pool sized).
- CAGR yr3 from the FY26 base: (1,000 / 503.08)^(1/3) - 1. Ratio 1.9878; ln 0.6869; divided by 3 = 0.2290; exp = 1.2573. 25.7%. From the FY27E guide of 720 over two years: (1,000/720)^(1/2) - 1 = 17.9%.
- CAGR yr5 from the FY26 base: (1,235 / 503.08)^(1/5) - 1. Ratio 2.4549; ln 0.8982; divided by 5 = 0.1796; exp = 1.1968. 19.7%.
- History for scale: FY23 ₹251 Cr to FY26 ₹503.08 Cr = 26.1% CAGR (deck p.21; (503.08/251)^(1/3) = 1.2607).
- These are cross-check inputs for stage 11, not a base case. Stage 11 builds the base on the Amendment 26.1 hierarchy.
- SOM as a share of realistic TAM: yr3 0.14% (1,000 / 711,075); yr5 0.17% (1,235 / 711,075).

### 3C Capacity cross-check
Injected figure (B07): ₹115 Cr plant capex (hormones 65 + oncology 50) at FY26 net-PPE turnover 3.35x adds ₹385 Cr (77% of ₹503.08 Cr); at the FY23-26 average 2.56x adds ₹294 Cr (58%).
- Method X (turnover): 503.08 + 385 = ₹888 Cr (3.35x) or 503.08 + 294 = ₹797 Cr (2.56x). FY29 SOM ₹1,000 Cr exceeds this by ₹112 Cr (vs 888) to ₹203 Cr (vs 797).
- Method Y (utilisation): overall capacity utilisation about 70% in H1 FY27 (Q2 Pres slide 21: ~35% FY23, ~65% FY26, ~70% H1 FY27). Annualised H1 revenue 343.6 x 2 = 687.2. At 100%: 687.2 / 0.70 = ₹982 Cr existing-plant ceiling. The blend weights by unit are not filed (derived; blend caveat).
  - Oncology expansion (completes June 2027, Q2 Pres slide 5): oncology 22% of Q2 FY27 revenue = 0.22 x 181.2 = ₹39.9 Cr a quarter, ₹159.5 Cr annualised; at the 75-80% utilisation stated (Aug call p.15, mid 77.5%) full capacity = 159.5 / 0.775 = ₹205.8 Cr; the +45-50% capacity (Aug call p.15) adds ₹92.6-102.9 Cr at full use. Oncology ceiling about ₹298-309 Cr, which is level with management's FY29 oncology target of ₹300 Cr (B05). That target needs about 99% utilisation.
  - Core ceiling = 982 + about 98 = ₹1,080 Cr. FY29 SOM ₹1,000 Cr needs 93% of it (1,000 / 1,080).
  - General plant +20% capacity (Aug call p.15): revenue by plant NOT FOUND, so not added.
- Verdict: capacity_check is a conflict between two methods. Gap of ₹112-203 Cr on Method X; no gap on Method Y but at 93% of ceiling, which no filed plant data shows being reached. The SOM is the optimistic side if blended utilisation stays under about 90%. The capex figure is the incomplete side in other respects: ₹115 Cr omits biologics (₹195 Cr+) and Unit 7, and B05 shows management total capex of 260-270 Cr (May) and 185-190 Cr plus 150 Cr clinical (Aug), with ₹500-600 Cr through FY30 in the Step-1 brief (unanchored).
- Year 5 (FY31) ₹1,235 Cr: Method X gives no support (ceiling 797-888 plus unfunded add-ons). Method Y supports it only at 100% utilisation of all plants. Management's ₹1,500 Cr is ₹420 Cr above the Method Y core ceiling (1,500 - 1,080) before add-ons: gap ₹265 Cr over the central SOM.
