## NEW FINDING 1: CARO Clause (vii)(a) qualification is a small, resolved TDS delay, not an unresolved statutory-dues arrears problem

Pass 1 flagged the CARO (vii)(a) qualification across the Holding Company, FT Institutions
Pvt Ltd, and Mark Maker Engineering Pvt Ltd as 🔴 with the detail "NOT FOUND IN DOCUMENT
within the notes themselves." The standalone auditor's report (not a note, but part of the
same filed document) gives the exact detail for the Holding Company:

"There were no undisputed amounts payable in respect of Provident Fund, Employees' State
Insurance, Income Tax, Goods and Service Tax, Customs Duty, Cess and other material
statutory dues in arrears as at March 31, 2026 for a period of more than six months from
the date they became payable except as given below: Income Tax Act 1961 Section 194C
(TDS), Rs 22,371, FY2025-26, due date not applicable, date of payment 23-04-2026, remarks
'Rs 4,979 and Rs 17,392 paid'." (Standalone Independent Auditor's Report, CARO Order para
3(vii)(a), AR p.92-93 txt, txt lines 3683-3699.)

🟡 REVISED RATING (was 🔴 in Pass 1): this is a single TDS line of Rs 22,371 (i.e. Rs 0.22
lakh), paid within weeks after year-end, not a material or recurring statutory-dues arrears
pattern. It still qualifies as a CARO exception across three entities in the group (holding
company, a subsidiary, an associate — the FT Institutions and Mark Maker detail is not
separately reproduced in this AR, only the summary table naming the clause), which is a
compliance-process observation worth keeping in the audit-quality section, but the earlier
🔴 rating overstated the standalone-entity item's severity. The two co-qualified entities'
own arrears amounts remain NOT FOUND IN DOCUMENT (their own CARO reports were not filed
inside this AR) — stage 3/8 should note this as a smaller residual gap, not the Rs-unknown
open question Pass 1 implied.

## NEW FINDING 2: the Rs 2,350.83 lakh "Loan from related party" cannot be the UAE subsidiary, because intercompany loans eliminate on consolidation — narrows, but does not close, the disclosure gap

Pass 1 flagged this consolidated Note 26 current-borrowings line ("Loan from related party
(Unsecured): Loan from Others 2,350.83 / 1,987.99") as untraceable to a named counterparty.
This pass found the closest numeric candidate and shows why it is NOT the answer:

Standalone Note 47 "Balances as on year end" (AR p.134 txt, txt line 6349) discloses a loan
GIVEN by the Holding Company TO Fabtech Technologies LLC, UAE (its 100%-owned subsidiary)
of Rs 2,358.59 lakh (FY26) / Rs 1,963.61 lakh (FY25) — close to, but not identical to, the
consolidated borrowing figures (Rs 2,350.83 / Rs 1,987.99).

This proximity is a coincidence, not the answer: Fabtech Technologies LLC, UAE is fully
consolidated (100% subsidiary), so any loan between it and the parent is an intercompany
balance that must eliminate in full on consolidation — it cannot survive as a net Rs 2,350.83
lakh liability on the consolidated balance sheet. [INFERENCE] The true counterparty must
therefore sit OUTSIDE the consolidation perimeter: an equity-accounted associate (Mark Maker
Engineering Pvt Ltd, FABL International Technologies LLP) or a non-consolidated
promoter-group entity (FTIPL, Fabtech Turnkey Projects LLP, Fabtech Technologies Cleanrooms
Ltd, Fabsafe), since only those entities' balances survive line-item consolidation. Neither
the consolidated Note 49 "Balances as on year end" table (AR p.194 txt, txt lines 9631-9676)
nor the standalone Note 47 table names a payable/borrowing of this size to any of those
parties — the closest consolidated-table item is "Loans and advances... FABL International
Technologies LLP 671.20" (an asset, not a liability, and the wrong sign and size). 🔴 The
disclosure gap Pass 1 identified stands: a Rs 2,350.83 lakh unsecured related-party
borrowing is disclosed at the aggregate level but its counterparty cannot be traced in
either the standalone or consolidated related-party balance tables. This sharpens the
question for stage 3/8: ask management to name the counterparty directly, since the notes'
own cross-references rule out the two most obvious subsidiary candidates.

## NEW FINDING 3: LBF3 Saudi entity naming fully resolved — TWO separate Saudi step-down subsidiaries exist, not one misnamed entity

Pass 1 flagged a mismatch between company memory ("51% Specialized Contracting Activities
LLC") and the AR's disclosed Saudi entity ("Fabtech Lifecare Company, KSA," 100% owned).
Reading the two announcement files named in the task brief resolves this completely: these
are TWO DIFFERENT Saudi entities, and both facts are correct for their respective dates.

- **Fabtech Lifecare Company, KSA**: incorporated 26-Aug-2025 (inside FY26), 100% owned via
  Fabtech Technologies LLC (UAE), consolidated in this FY26 AR as a step-down subsidiary.
  FY26 contribution: revenue Rs 1,370.91 lakh, loss Rs 455.52 lakh (Note 52(a), AR p.198
  txt). Line of business not separately stated in the AR beyond "geographical business
  expansion" (Note 53(a), consolidated, txt line 9850).
- **Specialized Contracting Activities LLC**: a SEPARATE, NEW Saudi entity, announced
  02-Jun-2026 (three months after the 31-Mar-2026 balance sheet date, inside the gap between
  the audited-results filing of 27-Apr-2026 and the AR's Reg 34 filing of 31-Jul-2026).
  Per the Regulation 30 disclosure (20260602-Acquisition_ToBeIncorporated.txt, Annexure A):
  incorporation "under process" as of the announcement date, in "Mechanical, Electrical,
  Plumbing and Civil for Non-pharma Industries" — an explicitly different line of business
  from Fabtech Lifecare's pharma/healthcare-adjacent activity. **51%** of the shareholding
  will be held by Fabtech Technologies LLC (UAE), with the balance held by a named
  individual, Mr. Saleh Mousa Eidhah Al-Zahrani (a local partner, consistent with Saudi
  foreign-ownership/contracting-license norms for non-pharma sectors).

Conclusion: company memory's "51% Specialized Contracting Activities LLC" line and the
AR's "100% Fabtech Lifecare Company, KSA" line are BOTH accurate — they describe two
different entities in two different industries, incorporated roughly nine months apart.
Neither the memory file nor the AR is wrong; the earlier Pass 1 framing as a "mismatch
needing reconciliation" is superseded. Stage 3/8 should track BOTH entities as separate
Saudi exposures going forward: one already loss-making and consolidated (Lifecare), one
not yet incorporated and not consolidated as of the FY26 balance sheet date (Specialized
Contracting). (Sources: consolidated Note 52/53, AR p.198 txt; 20260602-
Acquisition_ToBeIncorporated.txt Annexure A, txt lines 61-111.)

## NEW FINDING 4: the counterparty (buyer) of the FABL 51% stake sale is not named anywhere in the notes

Pass 1 covered the Rs 177.49 lakh (consolidated) / Rs 90.52 lakh (standalone) exceptional
gain from selling 51% of FABL International Technologies LLP on 1-Apr-2025, and the
resulting subsidiary-to-associate reclassification. This pass specifically searched for the
buyer's identity across every mention of the transaction (Note 40/59 exceptional-items note,
Note 52/53 subsidiary-movement note, Note 49 related-party note) and found the transaction
described only as "the Company sold 51% of its stake in FABL International Technologies
LLP" with no counterparty named in any of the three notes. NOT FOUND IN DOCUMENT. This
matters because if the buyer is itself a related party (e.g. a promoter-family member or
promoter-group entity not required to be named under related-party rules if the transaction
predates their "related party" status), the "exceptional gain" quality concern flagged in
Pass 1 (Section 12, LBF4) would be materially worse than a simple stake sale to an
unconnected third party. Flag for stage 3/8 follow-up: identify the FABL 51%-stake buyer
from sources outside the AR notes (RHP, exchange filings, or MCA records).
