### 2B Utilisation trajectory per facility

- New 900 TPD Duplex Board Plant (Unit V PM-2): ~95% average capacity utilization by close of FY24-25, closing finished-paperboard inventory equivalent to only 12 days of production, described by management as evidence the plant "sold virtually all that it could produce" pan-India (Chairman's letter p.7, 🎙️).

- Unit III: previously classified "asset held for sale," plant & machinery of ₹977.46 lakh sold during FY24-25 (Note 50, p.161, 📄) — confirms an earlier closed unit was fully exited this year.
- Company-wide total production: 384,880 MT (FY24-25) vs 248,694 MT (FY23-24), +54.8% (Directors' Report p.44, 📄), consistent with the new plant's ramp dominating the volume growth.

### 2C Growth embedded in capex — arithmetic shown

Formula per instruction: total capex under execution × historical fixed-asset turnover = implied incremental revenue, as % of current revenue.

- Capex currently under execution (not yet reflected in a full year of revenue): the ₹150cr Pulp Mill/converting balancing program (Directors' Report p.44, 📄), the clearest disclosed "in progress" figure. (Outstanding capital commitments per Note 38 are ₹2,550.15 lakh = ₹25.50cr, which likely sits within/near completion of this same program — not added separately to avoid double-counting.)
- Historical fixed-asset turnover: Revenue FY24-25 ₹1,659.03cr (Note 30, p.142) ÷ average Net PPE [(₹1,17,082.07 + ₹1,10,385.03) lakh ÷ 2 = ₹1,13,733.55 lakh = ₹1,137.34cr] (Note 2A, p.118) = **1.459x**.
- Implied incremental revenue = ₹150cr × 1.459x = **₹218.8cr**, or **13.2% above current FY24-25 revenue of ₹1,659.03cr**.

🔍 ANALYST INFERENCE — this uses a company-wide historical turnover ratio dominated by the 900 TPD greenfield build; a debottlenecking/balancing program (pulp capacity + converting upgrades on existing lines) typically yields a different, often lower, revenue-per-rupee than a new paper machine, so 13.2% should be read as an upper-bound estimate, not a guided figure. No management revenue figure is attached to the ₹150cr program in the AR.

## SECTION 3: THE 20-CATEGORY SCAN

**FAMILY A — PRODUCT & TECHNOLOGY**

**A1 — Rare manufacturing capability**: NO EVIDENCE FOUND. Paperboard manufacturing from recycled fibre is not a qualification-gated or long-lead-time-equipment business in the sense this category describes (no global manufacturer count, no 12-36 month qualification cycle, no 3-5 year regulatory approval evidenced). The company's own framing — "largest single paperboard manufacturing machine at a single location in India" and a claimed lowest capital cost per ton (₹40,000/ton vs ₹1,00,000/ton greenfield benchmark, AR p.9, 🎙️ unaudited management claim) — is a scale/cost-leadership claim, not a rare-capability barrier-to-entry claim. Not force-fit here; captured instead under Family G-adjacent commentary in Section 6.

**A2 — Patent and IP pipeline**: NO EVIDENCE FOUND. Annexure 3 explicitly states "Expenditure incurred on Research and Development: Nil" and "The efforts made towards technology absorption – Nil" (AR p.56-57, 📄).

**A3 — Process innovation**: Moderate. 📄 DOCUMENTED. Annexure 3A lists concrete, ongoing measures: VFD installation to cut power draw, venturi-based steam-trap systems, mill-wide LED replacement, energy-efficient motors/pumps, suction-press shower modification to extend roll life, steam/condensate recovery optimization, high-pressure showers to cut water use, RO plant optimization, and a dewatering screw that reuses DIP fine-screen rejects as fibre feedstock in the Board unit (AR p.56, 📄). This is genuine waste-to-value / unit-cost work, though none of it is separately quantified in ₹ terms (no stated cost-per-ton decline).

**A4 — Product platform / modular architecture**: NO EVIDENCE FOUND. A single new SKU (value-added FBB) does not establish an accelerating-launch platform pattern; no second or third variant, no shared-architecture language.

**FAMILY B — SUPPLY CHAIN**

**B1 — Backward integration and RM security**: Moderate. 📄 DOCUMENTED. The 7.5 MW plastic-waste captive power plant addresses 36% of the new facility's power needs at a stated ₹4/unit generation cost, "half the cost at which power is available through the state electricity grid" (AR p.9, 📄) — a specific, quantified captive-power claim, distinct from the general process-optimization measures counted under A3. RM security more broadly rests on locally sourced waste paper (AR p.16, risk-mitigation language, 🎙️, no 5+ year supply agreements evidenced).

**B2 — Qualification lock-in**: NO EVIDENCE FOUND. The company's boards are CFTRI-certified for post-print/food-contact applications (AR p.3, 📄) — a base quality certification, not a qualification-lock-in moat. None of the sub-criteria (design-in language, sole-source status, customer-funded tooling, multi-year post-qualification agreements) are evidenced anywhere in the AR.

**B3 — Supply chain network effect**: NO EVIDENCE FOUND.

**FAMILY C — CUSTOMER**

- **Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR, 2024)** and the **National Packaging Initiative**: sector-level tailwinds favouring paper-based packaging over plastic (AR p.15), shared by all organized paper-packaging competitors — not a company-specific moat.
- **Anti-dumping duty**: flagged in Risk Management as a potential government mitigant against "product dumping risk" (AR p.16) — a possible future protection, not yet active; NOT FOUND evidence of any anti-dumping order currently in force benefiting NRAIL.

**4C Regulatory moat assessment**: Active but narrow and shared-eligibility. Time to kick in: already active (booked in FY24-25). Sustainability: **NOT FOUND** — the AR does not disclose the multi-year duration of the Aatmanirbhar Gujarat Scheme benefit, so whether this recurs in FY25-26 and beyond cannot be assessed from this document set.
