## SECTION 3: BUSINESS CONDUCT & ETHICAL TRACK RECORD

**3A. Related-party transaction history.** Two RPT threads, pulling in
**opposite directions**, both material:

(i) **Historical flow: AREDL lends TO the company.** Standalone Note 21
(FY25 AR, p.115-116) shows APTL borrowing Rs 15.97 Cr from AREDL during
FY25 (repaying Rs 46.60 Cr of a prior balance) and paying AREDL Rs 62
Lakh in interest on those borrowings (down from Rs 129 Lakh in FY24).
VERIFIED. This is the promoter entity funding the listed company, not
the reverse — an unremarkable, declining-balance RPT.

(ii) **Forward-looking authorisation: the company would lend TO AREDL.**
The 12th AGM Notice (dated 01-Sep-2025, bundled with the FY24-25 AR,
p.16-19) seeks shareholder approval for the company to provide **"financial
assistance" to AREDL up to Rs 50 Cr** in one or more tranches, unsecured,
tenure up to 1 year, interest per Companies Act 2013 provisions. The
notice states **Rs 10 Cr already utilised, representing 18.95% of APTL's
FY24-25 consolidated turnover** — a materiality level that itself
triggered the Regulation 23 disclosure requirement. VERIFIED (AGM
Notice text), but the **reporting period for the Rs 10 Cr figure is
ambiguous in the extracted text** (unclear whether this is FY25 or
FY26-to-date as of the Sept-2025 notice) — input gap. **This is the
single most flag-worthy RPT item in this review**: a large omnibus
approval for the listed company to extend loans to its own promoter
entity is a direction of fund flow that requires active minority-
shareholder monitoring, independent of whether the historical pattern
(promoter lending to the company) has been benign. Rated 🔴 Red pending
disclosure of the actual amounts drawn and repaid in the FY26 AR.

K2V2 Technologies (subsidiary, not promoter-controlled) generates a
long, granular RPT trail (investment increases from 44.44% to 81.94-
81.27% stake, intercompany loans at 9.25-15% p.a., ESOP schemes) — routine
subsidiary consolidation mechanics, not promoter self-dealing.

**3B. Capital allocation behaviour.** Mixed but net constructive over the
period reviewed:
- Rights Issue (Rs 343.56 Cr, 2022-2025 call structure): promoter group
  **publicly committed to subscribe their full entitlement plus any
  unsubscribed portion** (📰 MEDIA REPORTED, Business Standard/EquityBulls,
  2021-22) — a supportive, not extractive, capital-allocation signal.
  Final call completed by the Mar-2025 record date; FY25 AR shows Rs
  113.48 Cr received into securities premium "on rights issue" during the
  year (Note 9, consolidated, p.243), consistent with the promoter
  commitment having been honoured.
- **CARE Ratings monitoring report (as of 31-Dec-2025), published ~Feb-2026:**
  identified that Rs 57.56 Cr of unutilised Rights Issue proceeds had
  been parked in **debt mutual fund schemes, contrary to SEBI ICDR
  regulations** (which permit temporary parking only with scheduled
  commercial banks). 📰 MEDIA REPORTED (scanx.trade summary of the CARE
  report; the CARE report and underlying Reg 30 filing were not collected
  in-run — operator-context corroboration only). The company **redeployed
  the full unutilised amount to Axis Bank fixed deposits by 23-Jan-2026**,
  ahead of or promptly following the observation, and later disclosures
  (Q3/Q4 FY26 rights-issue-utilisation filings) report "no deviation" with
  the unutilised balance down to Rs 37.57 Cr, fully bank-deployed. This is
  a genuine compliance miss (a listed company should not need an external
  monitoring agency to catch an ICDR parking-rule breach), but it is a
  **rectified, disclosed, self-corrected deviation**, not concealment —
  rated ⚠️ Watch, weighed as a governance-process gap rather than intent
  to mislead.
- **Non-core asset monetisation:** Board approved (12-Mar-2026) sale of
  Buildings Q5 & Q6 (Millennium Business Park, Navi Mumbai) for ~Rs 112
  Cr, **~15% above independent valuer estimate**, against a book value of
  ~Rs 27 Cr; completed 21-May-2026, proceeds used to pre-pay the Bajaj
  Finance LRD debt (~Rs 56 Cr) and fund an "AI war chest," with the
  company declaring itself debt-free on completion. 📰 MEDIA REPORTED
  (scanx.trade; underlying Reg 30 filing not collected in-run) —
  **selling above independent valuation and using proceeds for debt
  paydown rather than promoter-directed uses is a constructive capital-
  allocation signal**, though the "AI war chest" framing is itself flagged
  by B07 as thin on committed spend detail.
- No promoter warrant issuances, preferential allotments to promoter
  entities, or buybacks were found in this run.
- Standalone KMP pay ratio: CEO-to-median employee pay of 15.72x (per
  B03-ardeep.yaml, sourced from this AR) is modest and not a red flag.

**3C. Promoter share transactions.**
- **Pledge: 0%.** The FY25 AR's own CARO confirmation states the company
  "has not raised loans during the year on the pledge of securities held
  in its subsidiaries" (Auditors' Report, standalone, p.[CARO x.f]) —
  VERIFIED but this is about subsidiary-share pledges by the company, not
  a direct statement on AREDL's promoter-share pledge. Trendlyne's
  shareholding-pattern aggregator (📰 MEDIA/aggregator-sourced, not the
  primary exchange SHP Table II directly reviewed) states "no pledged
  promoter holdings." Two consistent but secondary-tier sources; no
  primary Table II filing was independently pulled in this run. **Net:
  pledge appears to be 0%, moderate confidence.**
- **Shareholding trend, the key judgment call this stage was asked to
  make:** Promoter holding was stable ~49.9-50.3% from Jun-2023 through
  Jun-2025, then stepped down sharply from 49.81% (Jun-2025) to 47.04%
  (Sep-2025), a ~2.77pp single-quarter decline (screener shareholding
  screenshot, operator-supplied, ANCHORED). **This is NOT rights-issue
  non-subscription dilution** — the Rights Issue's final call closed by
  the Mar-2025 record date, a full quarter before the Jun-2025 reading,
  and the promoter had publicly committed to (and by the AR's own Mar-25
  standalone note, appears to have largely honoured) full subscription.
  The far more plausible explanation, cross-checked against the timeline:
  **REA India received 42,42,537 new Aurum Proptech shares (a 5.5% equity
  stake) in an all-stock share-swap consideration for the PropTiger
  acquisition**, announced/completed in the Aug-2025 window (📰 MEDIA
  REPORTED, mnacritique/therealtytoday/mediabrief, Jul-Aug 2025; also
  documented in B07-emoat.yaml as H2). A back-of-envelope check supports
  this: if AREDL's 3.60 Cr shares (49.90% as of Mar-2025, ~7.22 Cr total
  shares then) stayed unchanged in absolute count while ~7.71 Cr total
  shares implied by REA's stake (42,42,537 / 5.5%) were outstanding
  post-swap, AREDL's post-swap holding computes to ~46.7% — closely
  matching the observed 47.04% Sep-2025 print. **Conclusion: the Jun-Sep
  2025 promoter dilution is new-share issuance to a strategic acquirer
  (REA/News Corp Australia-backed), not open-market promoter selling and
  not rights-issue under-subscription.** This reframes the dilution as a
  **transition-evidence-positive event** (bringing in a credible
  international real-estate-platform strategic shareholder) rather than
  an insider-selling red flag. Confidence: moderate-high (arithmetic
  reconciles closely; the underlying Reg 30/allotment filing was not
  directly pulled in-run).
- Reinforcing the non-selling read: a **co-promoter acquired 25,000
  shares via open-market purchase on 10-Mar-2026** (📰 MEDIA REPORTED,
  scanx.trade), and promoter holding ticked back up 0.09pp from Dec-2025
  to Mar-2026. Net promoter behaviour over the run's visibility window is
  **accumulation/dilution-by-M&A, not distribution**.
- No pre-announcement suspicious trades, no warrant lapses, no creeping-
  acquisition-via-preferential-allotment pattern found.

**3D. Minority treatment.** The 2021 Majesco open offer (Section 2E) gave
public shareholders the same exit price as the outgoing promoter sale —
a clean precedent. The Q5/Q6 building sale at 15% above independent
valuation is minority-friendly (extracts full value rather than an
undervalued related-party disposal). No proxy-advisory (IiAS/SES/
InGovern) adverse report, AGM controversy, or SCORES complaint pattern
was found in web search — but this search was not exhaustive (see
searches_skipped); treat as a directional clean read, not an exhaustive
clearance.

**3E. Auditor relationship — the most serious finding in this section.**
M/s M S K A & Associates were APTL's statutory auditors from the 6th AGM
(Aug-2019) for a first 5-year term, **reappointed by the Board on
29-Apr-2024 for a second 5-year term**, and then **withdrew their consent
to continue as auditors via email dated 25-Sep-2024** — under five months
after the reappointment, and before the 12th AGM at which the
reappointment would have been formally ratified by shareholders. VERIFIED
(AR FY24-25, Directors' Report Item 22, p.53, verbatim: "M/s. M S K A &
Associates, Chartered Accountants, who were reappointed by the Board of
Directors at its meeting held on April 29, 2024, for a second term of
five consecutive years, subsequently withdrew their consent to act as
Statutory Auditors of the Company, vide their mail dated September 25,
2024."). **No reason for the withdrawal is disclosed anywhere in the AR**,
and repeated web search did not surface a stated reason in any Reg 30
filing or media report (the specific PDF filings were 403-blocked to
this run's tools). One data point of note, not asserted as causally
connected: **the same firm, MSKA & Associates, also resigned as BYJU'S
auditor in the same period (Sept-2024), citing unresolved financial and
governance concerns at that unrelated company** (📰 MEDIA REPORTED,
Inc42/YourStory) — this establishes MSKA was actively exiting at least
one other client engagement over governance/financial concerns at the
same time, which is context, not evidence, for the Aurum withdrawal.
**An unexplained auditor withdrawal within a year of a second-term
reappointment is one of the six standard deal-breaker criteria in this
framework** and is recorded verbatim below. The successor, M/s Kirtane &
Pandit LLP, appointed from the Oct-2024 EGM, is a **first-year auditor in
FY25** who (per B02-notes.yaml, this run) directly audited only ~56% of
consolidated Group assets, relying on component auditors for the
remainder, and did not itself audit the FY24 comparative figures. Rated
🔴 Red.

No other auditor-relationship issues (non-audit fee dominance, disclosed
whistleblower complaints, restatement) were found; the FY25 audit
opinion itself is unqualified on both standalone and consolidated
financials, though B02/B03 (this run) independently found six internally
-inconsistent disclosures within the same signed AR (accounting quality
scored 4/10 in B02).
