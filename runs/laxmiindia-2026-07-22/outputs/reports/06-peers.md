## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

**2A Demand environment:** the peer set gives a broadly cautious-to-selectively-positive demand picture, not an industry-wide boom. Moneyboxx and Manba describe rural recovery and GST-linked demand upticks in specific product lines (livestock/cattle, two-wheelers); UGRO explicitly tightens underwriting and curtails disbursement due to "overleverage" stress in adjacent segments — a supply-side self-restraint, not a demand shortfall, but one that directly contradicts any notion of unconstrained sector-wide credit appetite. No peer describes anything resembling the demand strength implicit in LIFL's original 35-40% AUM growth guidance; if anything, the peer set collectively signals *more* caution about disbursement pace over FY26 than LIFL's initial framing implied.

**2B Pricing and input costs:** the dominant "input cost" discussed across all four peers is cost of borrowing, not raw materials (this is a lending business). All four describe a gradual, multi-quarter, sub-100bps-per-year decline trajectory toward eventual single digits as a multi-year aspiration — directly consistent with the peer benchmark established in Q5 above and reinforcing that LIFL's original 100-125bps-in-months guidance was out of step with realistic sector experience.

**2C Capex cycle (branch expansion):** the peer set is genuinely split, and LIFL sits on the less common side of the split. UGRO explicitly states its branch buildout phase is "largely complete," shifting to a "productivity, not expansion" focus (Q3 FY26 call). Moneyboxx explicitly halted branch expansion mid-year: "we don't have any plans to increase branches at current level" (Q2 FY26 call). Manba does not describe an aggressive new-branch program. Only Arman continues opening branches and raising capital-market debt for growth. This makes LIFL's continued (if repeatedly cut) branch-expansion narrative through FY26 (159→176 branches) a **comparative outlier**: this is a lone-expander situation, not an industry-wide capacity race — three of four peers are in a "digest and mature the existing footprint" phase while LIFL kept adding branches, and its own branch productivity (~INR8.5cr/branch, per B05) lags the vintage-adjusted peer benchmarks established in Q7 above (Manba mature-state concentration, Moneyboxx ~INR7cr/24-months, UGRO ~INR15.4cr/>18-months).

**2D Competitive mentions:** no peer transcript names LAXMIINDIA or gives an unmistakable description of it. This is consistent with genuine segment separation (LIFL's secured MSME + declining-heavy-CV mix in Rajasthan does not closely overlap any single peer's stated geography or product focus) rather than evidence of no competitive awareness either way.

**2E Risks peers discuss that LIFL's management does not:**
- UGRO explicitly discloses, quantifies, and is actively correcting the structural earnings-quality risk of DA/co-lending derecognition-gain dependency (Q3 FY26 call) — LIFL's own DA dependency was disclosed only reactively, once, under analyst pressure, with no stated plan to reduce reliance on it (B05 4D).
- Moneyboxx and UGRO both name "over-leverage"/customer-leverage stress as an explicit, sector-wide risk factor with specific mitigating actions (tightened underwriting, ticket-size floors, stricter bureau-score cutoffs); LIFL's management never frames its own GNPA deterioration in over-leverage terms, consistently using external/one-off framing instead (floods, one counterparty default) (B05 2B).
- Arman and Moneyboxx both discuss active legal-recovery infrastructure (Section 138 filings, dedicated recovery/legal teams, SARFAESI action) as a named, quantified lever for post-NPA collection; LIFL's B05 record contains no comparable discussion of a legal-recovery infrastructure buildout.
- UGRO explicitly discloses and quantifies its US-tariff exposure assessment (concluding minimal impact, <5% export-oriented customers); LIFL's four concalls (per B05) do not address tariff exposure at all.

## PART 5: CROSS-PEER HYPOTHESIS (mandatory closing step)

**Hypothesis:** within this small/micro-ticket secured-MSME NBFC peer set, earnings quality appears to be bifurcating along a "gain-on-sale dependency" axis that becomes visible to rating agencies and capital markets *before* it shows up as a headline ROA or GNPA number — and this axis, not segment or geography, is what is actually driving each peer's rating/cost-of-borrowing trajectory.

Evidence for the pattern: Arman and Manba, the two peers with negligible-to-modest DA/co-lending income share (near-zero and ~3-4% of AUM respectively), show the flattest, most boring GNPA trajectories and the steadiest (if unspectacular) rating narratives in the set — Manba's outcome was an outlook shift, not a notch, and Arman's rating was never even raised as a topic across four calls. Moneyboxx uses a related but distinct mechanism (ARC sales) purely for balance-sheet cleanup rather than income generation, and shows the most volatile GNPA prints of the four but the clearest month-on-month operational recovery narrative once past its NPA cycle. UGRO — the peer most dependent on DA/co-lending derecognition gains, historically 50-60% of ROA — is the one peer whose own management discloses that its capital-markets cost of funds sits 150-200bps above its rating-adjusted peer set (Q1 FY26 call), and it is the only peer executing an explicit, named strategic pivot away from gain-on-sale dependency specifically to normalize its cost-of-funds and rating trajectory.

This is testable and specific: track LIFL's DA-transaction share of quarterly PAT over the next several quarters. If it rises rather than falls — the path UGRO was on before its correction — expect LIFL's rating-notch progression to plateau regardless of what its GNPA trend does, exactly as happened at UGRO before its Q3 FY26 realignment. If DA share falls or stays flat as a percentage of PAT, the analogy does not apply and LIFL's DA event was more plausibly a genuine one-off rather than the leading edge of a structural dependency. This is not stated by any single peer — it emerges only from comparing DA/co-lending income share across all four peers against each one's independently-disclosed cost-of-borrowing and rating trajectory.
