## SECTION 1: THE BUSINESS MODEL IN PLAIN ENGLISH

### 1A. One-line description

HCP Plastene Bulkpack (HPBL) manufactures woven plastic (polypropylene, "PP") industrial
bulk-packaging — FIBC jumbo bags, small woven sacks, and PP fabric/yarn — mostly for
cement, sugar, food grain, fertiliser, chemical and pharma customers, selling both
domestically and (increasingly, since FY25) directly to export markets, after 40 years
of operation and a 2019 insolvency (CIRP) resolution that installed the current Plastene
Group promoters (AR p.57).

### 1B. Money flow chain, by revenue stream

**Stream 1 — Woven Sacks Division (FIBC bags, small PP woven bags, AD Star bags, PP
fabric/webbing/yarn):**
[Input: polypropylene granules, sourced domestically from Reliance Industries and
imported from Saudi Arabia/China (Rating p.4)] → [Company extrudes/weaves/laminates PP
into fabric, converts into bags to customer specification — food/pharma-grade FIBC (A/B/C/D
type), cement/fertiliser sacks, or export-grade jumbo bags] → [delivered to cement, sugar,
food grain, fertiliser, chemical, pharma end-users, both in India and via direct export
(53.70% of FY25 revenue) and via related-party trading/job-work channels] → [payer: either
the end customer directly (annual rate contracts or routine orders) or a related Plastene
Group entity that resells/exports on] → [payment: negotiated conversion rate with raw
material cost pass-through; export receivables backed by ECGC credit insurance] (AR p.7,
p.57-58; Rating p.3, p.6).

**Stream 2 — Woven Label Division (Jacquard Labels):**
[Input: yarn/PP tape] → [Company weaves labels for garment/textile-style tagging use,
consolidated within the Woven Sacks facilities] → [delivered to label-buying customers] →
[payer: direct customer] → [payment: routine trade terms]. This stream is immaterial in
size — ₹249.82 lakh of ₹46,343.54 lakh consolidated FY25 revenue, 0.54% — but is disclosed
as the company's second reportable segment (Results-FY26 p.11, audited FY25 column).

### 1E. The chai-stall-uncle version

Think of this company like a tailor who doesn't sell finished clothes off a shelf — every
bag is stitched to the customer's exact order, in the fabric (plastic weave) the customer
wants. The raw plastic granule (like cloth) is bought, mostly from Reliance in India or
imported from Saudi Arabia, and woven into big industrial sacks used to move cement,
sugar, grain, fertiliser and chemicals around the world. Until last year the company mostly
did "job work" — stitching for its bigger sister companies in the same group, almost like
a subcontractor tailor working only for one big shop. From FY25 it started selling directly
to its own export customers too, which is a bigger, riskier, but more profitable step —
like the tailor finally opening a shop-front instead of only doing piecework. The catch:
tailoring takes working capital (cloth bought and stored, finished bags sitting with the
buyer before payment comes in) and the company still leans heavily on its own group for a
large chunk of sales, so it hasn't fully left the shelter of the family shop yet.

### 2A. Five forces, plainly

### 2D. Industry lifecycle stage

FIBC/bulk-packaging is a growth industry globally: global FIBC market projected to reach
US$12.6 billion by 2033 (5.4% CAGR) and global PP woven-sack market to surpass US$6.1
billion by 2034 (4.2% CAGR) (AR p.56). Within India, FIBC usage is described as
under-penetrated domestically relative to Europe/US, "presenting a significant opportunity
to grow awareness" (AR p.56) — i.e., early-growth domestically, mature-and-export-led
globally where India already dominates. HPBL's own position: post-CIRP recovery/turnaround
stage, only 31% capacity utilisation in FY25 (Rating p.5), meaning the company is early in
its own capacity-utilisation curve even though the broader industry is more mature.

| Driver | Direction | Impact |

### 4B. Valuation method applicability (handoff to Stage 11)

| Method | Applicability | Notes |
