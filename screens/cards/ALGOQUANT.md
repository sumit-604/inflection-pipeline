## Business Understanding Narrative

Algoquant is a proprietary trading book inside a listed company. Its single reported operating
segment is "trading in financial instruments and Stock-broking" (FY26 p.10, note 6). In the year to
March 2026 it earned Rs 235.46 crore of revenue, of which Rs 194.13 crore, or 82.4%, was one line:
"Net gain on fair value changes" (FY26 p.6). That line is trading profit and loss. Fees and
commission income, the broking business, was Rs 32.04 crore, or 13.6%.

The listed shell is old and the business inside it is new. A composite scheme approved by the NCLT
Ahmedabad on 3 October 2024, with an appointed date of 1 April 2023, amalgamated Algoquant
Investments Private Limited into the listed company and demerged the stock broking business of
Growth Securities Private Limited into it (FY26 p.10, note 7). The broking licence itself only moved
across on 3 November 2025, and the depository participant business on 22 December 2025
(Intimations, 3 Nov 2025 and 22 Dec 2025).

What is changing, on the stated plan, is the addition of a client franchise to a house trading desk.
That is a real and recent corporate event, not a slogan, and it is the only transition the corpus
evidences.

Why now: the licence transfers completed in the December 2025 quarter, so FY27 is the first full
year in which a client business could contribute.

What must be true: fees and commission income must compound, so that earnings stop being a function
of how the house trades.

What breaks it: the first full quarter after the transfers went the wrong way. Fees and commission
income was Rs 5.17 crore in the June 2026 quarter, against Rs 14.99 crore in the March 2026 quarter
and Rs 5.51 crore a year earlier (Q1FY27 p.3). It fell 65% sequentially and 6% year on year. Over the
same quarter net gain on fair value changes rose 49% year on year to Rs 69.20 crore, and took its
share of revenue to 88.9%. Profit after tax rose 263%. Every rupee of that came from the house desk,
not from clients.

## 2. Business model and archetype
The company puts its own capital at risk in financial markets and keeps the gains. Total equity was
Rs 134.63 crore at March 2026 and total assets Rs 275.21 crore, of which Rs 129.99 crore sat in bank
balances other than cash, the usual shape of exchange margin, and Rs 55.39 crore in investments
(FY26 p.7). Borrowings were Rs 30.80 crore. The engine is capital times trading return.

The cash is real, which matters and is the strongest fact on the bull side. Operating cash flow was
Rs 60.13 crore in FY26 against profit after tax of Rs 32.74 crore, a 1.84 times conversion, and the
unrealised component was a Rs 0.71 crore credit, not a large paper gain (FY26 p.8). These are
realised trading profits, not marks.

Archetype: the library has no clean fit, and forcing one would mislead. The closest is
**Platform/network** for the broking slice, but that slice is 13.6% of revenue and shrinking. The
honest classification is a **proprietary trading firm**, whose dials are capital employed, return on
capital, drawdown, strategy capacity and leverage. The corpus discloses the first two and none of the
last three.

## 3. Competitive advantages
**NOT FOUND**, and not because the business has none, but because nothing filed describes them. A
proprietary trading edge is a real and valuable thing. It is also unobservable from outside, and this
company publishes nothing about it: no strategy description, no capacity limit, no risk framework, no
Sharpe or drawdown history. A 24.3% return on equity for FY26, computed from profit after tax of
Rs 32.74 crore on equity of Rs 134.63 crore (FY26 p.6, p.7), is the only evidence that an edge
exists, and one year of it does not separate skill from a favourable market.

The broking business has no advantage on the evidence held: no client count, no pricing, no service
differentiation, and its revenue line went backwards in the first quarter it ran.
Verdict: **unobservable, which for a transition thesis is the same as absent.**

What the filings do show is capital action rather than capital raising. In August 2025 the company
sub-divided each Rs 2 share into two Re 1 shares and then issued bonus shares eight for one, allotting
24,98,63,136 bonus shares on 19 August 2025 out of free reserves (FY26 p.9, note 4). Authorised

rose roughly nine times without a rupee of new money entering the business. That is a liquidity and
optics action, not a funding one, and the operator should read it as such.

Skin in the game is therefore unquantified. For a business whose entire value rests on who is running
the trading book, an unknown promoter holding is a first-order gap.

Read the two years first. Revenue went from Rs 234.55 crore to Rs 235.46 crore, up 0.4%. Profit after
tax went from Rs 32.58 crore to Rs 32.74 crore, up 0.5%. Earnings per share was Rs 1.16 in both
years. FY26 was, to the rupee, a flat year.

Then read the quarters. Q1 FY27 profit after tax of Rs 16.52 crore is up 263% on Q1 FY26's Rs 4.55
crore. That is a genuine step, and half the year's FY26 profit in one quarter.

Two cautions on the quarters. Q4 FY26's reported profit of Rs 15.88 crore carries a deferred tax
credit of Rs 7.06 crore, because the company recognised previously unrecognised MAT credits that
quarter (FY26 p.6, p.9 note 5). Total tax for the quarter was Rs 0.34 crore on pre-tax profit of
Rs 16.22 crore. That is a one-off. And the quarterly swings are wide in both directions: Q4 FY25
pre-tax profit was Rs 0.36 crore. A book that can earn Rs 22.16 crore in one quarter and Rs 0.36
crore in another is not a compounding earnings stream; it is a trading result.

Leverage is low: borrowings of Rs 30.80 crore against equity of Rs 134.63 crore, roughly 0.23 times
(FY26 p.7). Cash conversion is **STRONG**, at 1.84 times profit after tax, and unusually for this
card the cash flow statement is held, so this is evidenced rather than assumed.

**Inflection classification: MARGIN-LED at the quarter, and the margin is a trading return.** Revenue
grew 42.9% year on year in Q1 FY27 while total expenses grew 16.5%, so operating leverage is real
(Q1FY27 p.3). But the revenue growth is 96% attributable to the trading line. This is not an earnings
inflection in the sense the framework means; it is a good quarter on a house book.

## 6. The transition and the quality ladder
FROM **R1 COMMODITY PRICE-TAKER.** A proprietary trading desk has no pricing power, earns a return
that swings with market conditions, and cannot charge a customer anything. FY25 to FY26 is flat and
FY25 Q4 was near break-even; that is the R1 signature.
TO **R3 VALUE-ADDED / SPEC'D SUPPLIER** is the implied claim: a broking and depository franchise with
recurring client fees alongside the house book. Nothing states this in words, because management has
not spoken since 2023. It is inferred from the corporate actions: the scheme, the licence transfer of
3 November 2025 and the depository transfer of 22 December 2025.
Mechanism: client fees replace house gains as the earnings driver.
**Management's claim, quoted once, is the only one available:** "The Company is engaged in the
business of trading in financial instruments and stock-broking, which in the context of Indian
Accounting Standards (IND AS) 108 - Operating Segment, is the only operating segment of the Company"
(Q1FY27 p.4, note 3). A company that describes its transition only inside an accounting-standards
note is not communicating a transition.
**Ladder ruling: the FROM rung is evidenced, the TO rung has one quarter of counter-evidence.**

## 8. Proof check
The last two quarters move, and they move against the transition.

The proof point, named: **fees and commission income.** It is the only disclosed line that measures
the client franchise, and it is the line the whole transition rests on. Q4 FY26, the first quarter
with both the broking and depository businesses in hand, delivered Rs 14.99 crore, up 91% on Q4
FY25's Rs 7.84 crore (FY26 p.6). That looked like the gate firing. Q1 FY27 delivered Rs 5.17 crore,
down 65% sequentially and down 6% on the year-earlier quarter (Q1FY27 p.3). The step up did not hold
for a single further quarter.

Meanwhile the trading line took the load. Net gain on fair value changes rose to 88.9% of revenue in
Q1 FY27, the highest share in the table, above FY26's 82.4% and FY25's 81.8%. The mix moved away from
clients, not towards them.

**The proof gate has NOT FIRED.** One quarter of client-fee growth reversed in the next. The earnings
jump is real and it is a house trading result.

A second proof point the operator should note in fairness: the company converts profit to cash at
1.84 times, and it did so in both FY25 and FY26 (FY26 p.8). Whatever the trading book is doing, it is
settling in cash. That argues against the marks being soft.

## 11. Posture: the Transition Decision Matrix
- **Proof gate: NOT FIRED**, with counter-evidence. Step 8.
- **Ugliness: absent.** Profit after tax nearly quadrupled year on year in the June quarter, return on
  equity is 24.3%, cash conversion is 1.84 times, leverage is 0.23 times, the audit opinion is
  unmodified. The optics are good.
- **Recognition gap: CLOSED, and then some.** Rs 1,837 crore of market value against equity of about
  Rs 151 crore after the June quarter is roughly 12 times book. Against FY26 profit after tax of
  Rs 32.74 crore it is 56 times; against the last four reported quarters it is about 41 times. Twelve
  times book for a proprietary trading book means paying twelve rupees for one rupee of the capital
  that generates the return. A trading firm's franchise value is its edge, and this one publishes
  nothing about its edge.

**Posture: PRICED NARRATIVE (TRAP).** Proof not fired, no ugliness to warn you off, gap closed. The
framework: "the most seductive cell... the downside is double (the transition fails AND the multiple
compresses). AVOID or hard WATCH with no position until the proof gate fires AND the price re-opens
the gap."

## 12. Verdict card

**PASS.**

Give the bull case its full weight first, because it is not weak. This company earned a 24.3% return
on equity in FY26, converted profit to cash at 1.84 times in each of two years, carries almost no
debt, holds an unmodified audit opinion, and grew profit after tax 263% year on year in the June 2026
quarter. It has also done something concrete: it acquired and integrated a live broking and depository
business, completed in December 2025. Those are facts, not a story.

The pass rests on three things the bull case cannot answer from the filings.

First, **what you would be buying is a trading book at about twelve times its own capital.** The
earnings are 89% house trading gains, the strategies are undisclosed, the risk limits are undisclosed,
and the drawdown history is undisclosed. A 25% CAGR thesis over three to five years needs the edge to
be durable, and durability is exactly what cannot be observed here.

Second, **the transition ran backwards in the quarter it was meant to prove itself.** Fees and
commission income fell 65% sequentially and 6% year on year in the first full quarter after both
licence transfers completed. One quarter is not a verdict, but it is the only reading available, and it
points the wrong way.

Third, **the filings contradict themselves in four places, including on who audits the company.** A
results statement that names one audit firm while carrying another firm's signed report is not a
detail. Until that is resolved, no number on this card can be relied on at the weight a deep run needs.

A full `/step1` would read the same statutory filings and hit the same three walls. That is the
ground for the pass.

**Load-bearing facts to verify first, if the operator overrides this pass:**
1. Who actually audited FY26 and Q1 FY27. Reconcile O P Bagla & Co. LLP, Raghav & Co. LLP and
   Bagla & Co., FRN 000183N.
2. Promoter identity, holding and pledge. All NOT FOUND in the corpus.
3. The trading book: strategies, gross and net exposure, leverage, value at risk, worst monthly and
   quarterly drawdown since FY23.
4. Why fees and commission income collapsed in Q1 FY27 after the licence transfers completed.
5. The FY26 annual report, for the related-party schedule and the directors' report.
6. Who holds the 24.99 crore bonus shares issued in August 2025, and whether the register changed
   materially around the split and bonus.
7. Any credit rating, from any agency. None exists in the corpus.

**What would change the view.** To WATCH: the auditor question is resolved cleanly, and the company
resumes earnings calls. To PROCEED: two consecutive quarters of fees and commission income above
Rs 15 crore, taking the client line past 25% of revenue, plus a published risk framework for the
trading book. On the current run rate that is a long way off.

**This is not a position.** No valuation, no target price, no entry zone. The shallow read decides
only that a full `/step1` is not worth running on this name today.
