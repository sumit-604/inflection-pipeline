## PASS 3 PATTERN RE-READ

Targeted re-check against the pattern brief (contradictions, notes not matching main statements,
deliberately vague vs. detailed disclosure, restated/reclassified prior-year figures, post-balance-sheet
events, going concern language anywhere in the document), plus a full-text sweep of the sidecar for
"restat", "going concern", "material uncertainty", "reclassif", "regroup", and both years' trade
receivables ageing tables.

**Going concern — confirmed NONE.** Full-text sweep finds only standard boilerplate: management/board
responsibility language in both audit reports (standalone PDF ~p.62-63 text zone / consolidated
~p.94-95 text zone) and the Directors' Responsibility Statement's routine "prepared on a going concern
basis" line. One affirmative statement outside the notes (Directors' Report, "SIGNIFICANT AND MATERIAL
ORDERS" section): "There are no significant and material orders passed by the regulators or courts that
would impact the going concern status of the Company." Standalone CARO clause (xix) independently
confirms no material uncertainty on 12-month liability coverage. No emphasis-of-matter, no qualification,
in either audit opinion. This matches Pass 1's conclusion; nothing in the wider document contradicts it.

**Restatements — confirmed none in the financial notes.** Sweep confirms only: (a) the PP&E useful-life/
residual-value change (Note 25.12) is explicitly "accounted for prospectively... comparative figures...
have not been restated," both statement sets; (b) first-year hedge accounting (Note 25.7B) is explicitly
"applied prospectively... comparative information... has not been presented/restated, as such information
was not applicable in the previous reporting period"; (c) prior-year figures were "regrouped/rearranged...
to conform with current period's classification" (Note 25.19 standalone / 25.17 consolidated) — the
company's own wording frames this as a presentation reclassification, not a restatement. One unrelated
restatement appears in the BRSR (Business Responsibility and Sustainability Report) section, outside the
Notes to Financial Statements: FY2024-25 fuel-consumption figures were restated "to include all fuel
types, previous disclosures covered only fuel used for energy generation" — a sustainability-metric
restatement, immaterial to the financial notes and out of this stage's scope, noted here only for
completeness.

**New pattern-level findings from the re-read (2 items, building on data both passes already anchored):**

1. **Two leverage ratios in the SAME note set move in opposite directions, without cross-reference to
   each other.** Standalone Note 25.21 "Debt-Equity Ratio" (gross debt / average equity) IMPROVED
   0.85x→0.76x. The Capital Management note's net-debt/equity (period-end equity, net of cash) WORSENED
   0.53x→0.63x (consolidated) / 0.53x→0.63x is also standalone-worsening per Pass 1. Both are correctly
   computed under their own stated definitions — this is not an arithmetic error — but a reader following
   only the Key Ratios note would conclude leverage improved, while a reader following only the Capital
   Management note would conclude the opposite, in the same annual report, same year. This is the
   "notes contradicting each other" pattern the Pass 3 brief specifically asks for. (Note 25.21 vs Capital
   Management note, PDF p.90 standalone) 🟡
2. **Disclosure granularity is inversely correlated with investor decision-relevance.** The notes give
   granular, well-modelled disclosure on items of lower decision weight — gratuity actuarial sensitivities
   to two decimal places, ESOP fair-value Black-Scholes inputs, hedge ineffectiveness to the lakh — while
   staying silent on exactly the three items most load-bearing for the transition thesis: goodwill
   impairment assumptions (13.8% of consolidated net worth), customer identity/end-market split (the
   ~40% railway/traction claim), and any single cumulative capex-programme figure. Individually each
   silence was already flagged (Pass 1 findings #3, #5; Section 3 capex-commitments discussion); naming
   the pattern across all three together is the Pass 3 addition: the silences are not randomly distributed,
   they cluster on the same three questions an investor would ask first. 🟡

No further material items emerged from the pattern re-read beyond these two and the going-concern/
restatement confirmations above; PASS 3 does not invoke the no-findings guard because these two items are
genuine new synthesis, but nothing here changes any Pass 1/Pass 2 number.

### D. FIVE QUESTIONS FOR MANAGEMENT

1. What is the total cumulative machined-components/casting capex programme (in Rs Cr), and how do the FY26 gross PP&E additions of Rs 175-200 Cr map against it? No single programme total appears anywhere in the notes.
2. Can you name the top customers behind the 40.7% (standalone) / 25.2% (consolidated) revenue concentration, and confirm or correct the ~40% railway/traction (Wabtec/Alstom) characterization used in company memory and external commentary?
3. What is the multi-year glide path for net debt toward the guided ~Rs 300 Cr, given FY26 net debt rose 26.3% (consolidated) against a working-capital reversal that includes Rs 83.85 Cr of TReDS-financed payables?
4. What CGU-level assumptions (discount rate, growth rate, headroom) support the Rs 136.09 Cr goodwill carrying value, and why does none appear in the notes?
5. Why does Note 25.21's variance-disclosure formula compute a percentage-point difference (not a relative % change) for ROE, Net Profit Ratio, and ROCE, while using relative % change for every other ratio row — and on a like-for-like relative basis, what explains the -32.5% ROE decline?

### E. NOTES-BASED RED FLAGS

- **Disclosure-integrity flaw, not manipulation of underlying numbers:** Note 25.21's variance-disclosure formula is applied inconsistently between ratio-type rows and percentage-denominated rows, and the inconsistency happens to exempt the year's steepest profitability decline (ROE) from any management explanation.
- **Leverage-optic risk:** the TReDS/supplier-finance arrangement embedded in trade payables nearly doubled YoY; the headline "payables down 25.7%" narrative partly coexists with, rather than replaces, growing financier-intermediated credit.
- **Concentrated disclosure silence:** goodwill impairment testing, customer identity/end-market split, and the cumulative capex-programme total are all absent from the notes, clustering on the three questions most load-bearing for the transition thesis, in a document otherwise granular on lower-stakes items (gratuity sensitivities, ESOP model inputs, hedge ineffectiveness to the lakh).
- **Subsidiary-level exposure invisible to standalone-only readers:** the Rs 4.91 Cr GST dispute (FY2018-2023) appears only in the consolidated notes.
- None of the above constitutes established earnings management or fraud on the evidence in these notes; the flags are transparency/disclosure-quality gaps, not proof of manipulation. No going-concern language and no restated P&L figures were found anywhere in the document.

### F. ONE-LINE NOTES VERDICT

The notes reveal moderate accounting practices, clean on core earnings recognition but with a cluster of transparency gaps concentrated on the transition thesis. Key concern: goodwill, customer identity, capex-programme size, and true leverage (TReDS-inflated payables) are all under-disclosed, alongside one internally inconsistent ratio-variance formula. Key strength: no exceptional items, no restatements, no going-concern language, and a genuine, well-evidenced improvement in receivables/DSO. Overall accounting quality: 6/10.
