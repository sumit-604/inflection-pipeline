# Diffusion Engineers (DIFFNKG) — Business Narrative

## Business understanding narrative

Diffusion Engineers makes the parts that stop heavy industrial machines from wearing out. Its core products are welding consumables, the hardfacing electrodes and flux cored wires that lay a hard protective layer onto surfaces that grind, crush and abrade. It also makes wear plates and wear parts, and it builds and reconditions heavy engineering equipment like kilns, crushers, rollers and conveyors. Manufacturing is 76% of revenue, job work and repair services 8%, exports 8%, and trading of allied products 7%. The customer cannot easily skip these parts, because a kiln liner or a crusher jaw wears down in normal use and must be replaced or re welded to keep the plant running. The customers are steel, cement, power, mining, sugar, rail and defence plants that run this kind of equipment. Management says more than 80% of customers repeat, that specifications are approved to the customer's equipment standards, and that relationships run 44 years, though no customer concentration figure is disclosed anywhere. Present demand comes from India's industrial capex cycle, tied to named external signals: cement capacity additions, steel Vision 300 MT capacity expansion, coal output targets, and power sector capex. Demand should grow with those same drivers plus railway electrification and rolling stock programmes, defence indigenisation, and sugar sector ethanol capex, each of which has an externally verifiable source. The competitive advantage sits mainly in the welding consumables and wear parts lines, where switching costs come from approved specifications and repeat relationships, and where backward integration gives a cost edge because the company's own wire feeds its own wear plate production. The heavy engineering line, now the fastest growing and about three quarters of the order book, is 70% new project build and only 30% spares, so it is more cyclical and less sticky than the consumables narrative implies. The forward moat scan scored MODEST, with moderate strength in backward integration, qualification lock in, customer relationships, execution, and a defence hardware partnership through Tejorup Sunmay. The run did not establish a per product margin or revenue split, because the company reports a single segment.

## Wear part maker grows, cash conversion weak

Diffusion Engineers makes wear resistant parts and welding consumables for steel, cement, power and mining plants from Nagpur. FY26 consolidated revenue grew 21% and consolidated PAT was about Rs 50 Cr. The shares trade near 35 times FY26 earnings at a market value of about Rs 1,777 Cr. The company listed in October 2024, so only two audited years exist for a backward read.

The business is shifting shape. The 44 year core is welding consumables, a reorder driven line, but the order book is now 76% to 82% heavy engineering, a lumpy project execution line billed on milestones. Capacity is set to double from 9,000 MT to 18,000 MT at the Nimji site. Management guides to about 20% revenue growth and revenue doubling in three years, with a longer aspiration of Rs 650 Cr and beyond. It is also opening a UAE plant and a Turkey operation and holds a small defence hardware stake.

The reported numbers read two ways. Leverage is low at 0.07 debt to equity, the audit and CARO were clean on every clause including IPO proceeds application, and revenue and profit growth are broad based. Gate 0 scored the company AVERAGE at 57 of 100 on the core, with a MODERATE moat base of 13 of 60, though four moat tests were blocked purely by missing peer data rather than by weakness. Two of the worst optics, the FY25 return dip and the FY26 negative free cash flow, trace to the enlarged post IPO equity base and to IPO funded capacity capex, not to operating decay.

Cash conversion is the weak point. Operating cash flow ran between 0.15 and 0.45 of PAT across FY25 and FY26 on both standalone and consolidated books, all four readings below the 0.7 line, and free cash flow was negative both years. Consolidated trade receivables grew 51% against 21% revenue growth, and receivables turnover fell from 4.55 times to 3.82 times. The ageing mix itself improved slightly, with the over six month bucket easing from 13.9% to 11.8% of gross, so the quantum deteriorated while the ageing quality did not. Reported PAT is also flattered by non operating income: interest on about Rs 67 Cr of unutilised IPO cash held in deposits, a one time Singapore subsidiary dividend near Rs 5 Cr, and an associate profit spike, which is why PAT growth ran far ahead of EBITDA growth.

Management says more than it delivers on the operating items. The concall credibility grade is C. Revenue guidance was cut then beaten, a credibility positive. Against that, the Unit 4 heavy engineering commissioning date slipped twice across three calls without ever being called a delay, an EBITDA margin gain of 100 to 200 basis points was promised three calls running and delivered none, and the railway order to revenue timeline stretched from 3 to 5 months out to 9 to 12 months. Management could not produce current top 10 customer concentration on a direct request.

Peers confirm some claims and break others. Both welding peers describe a recovering to improving 2025 and 2026 demand cycle, broadly consistent with the company's framing. But GEE Ltd put the Indian welding industry at Rs 15,000 Cr to Rs 20,000 Cr, roughly 100 times the Rs 160 Cr consumables market the company cited to frame its own scale. The company's claim of a tungsten price spike above 300% of the 2024 average found zero corroboration across six peer transcripts spanning nearly two years. Debtor days of 98 sit well above Ador Fontech's roughly 50 to 53.

The addressable market is real but far smaller than management frames it. Scoped to anti wear consumables, wear parts and heavy engineering repair, the niche TAM is about Rs 2,200 Cr to Rs 2,600 Cr, with a serviceable market near Rs 1,130 Cr. The three year serviceable obtainable market is about Rs 502 Cr and the five year about Rs 609 Cr, an implied niche revenue growth near 10% a year and a MODERATE runway with about 2.8 times revenue headroom. Management's own MD&A backdrop implies a market 38.7 times the conservative niche estimate, because it mixes in the full construction equipment OEM market it does not serve.

Governance is concentrated and drew a CAUTION read, not a concern. Three family directors hold 64% combined and sit on three of six board seats, the Managing Director chairs the Risk Management Committee, the one committee not chaired by an independent director, and about 35% of FY26 CSR ran through two family linked foundations. The CEO resigned after about one year with no successor named. No pledge and no adverse legal finding surfaced on any searchable item, though the IPO prospectus litigation schedule and the multi quarter pledge trend could not be read this run.
