# SATIN Q1 FY27 — Plain-Language Summary + Sector Knowledge

## PLAIN-LANGUAGE SUMMARY (10-12 lines)

Satin just posted its 20th profitable quarter running, with consolidated profit
of Rs 123 Cr, up 172% on last year. But that big jump is mostly because last
June quarter was unusually weak, not because this quarter was unusually strong.
Against the March quarter, profit actually fell 24%, and the key return ratios
cooled off their March peak (ROA 4.7% to 3.3%, ROE 23% to 17%). The loan book
grew a strong 27% to Rs 15,935 Cr, yet the number of borrowers barely moved
(+3%), so the growth is coming from bigger loans to the same customers, not new
customers, which is an early warning sign in microfinance. On the surface asset
quality looks clean and better (bad loans 2.18%, capital very strong at 26.7%,
auditors fully clean), but the company did not disclose its write-offs,
provisioning movement, or stage-2 loans, so we cannot yet prove the bad-loan
improvement is real rather than cosmetic. A Rs 36 Cr buffer provision quietly
grew Rs 15 Cr this quarter and is excluded from the headline return ratios,
which flatters them. Profit from subsidiaries collapsed to almost nothing,
hinting at losses in the newer tech and fund arms. The promoter is committing
Rs 100 Cr through warrants, a genuine confidence signal, though it dilutes about
3.5% later. All the pre-set escalation conditions (profit >= Rs 110 Cr, bad
loans <= 3.05%, ROE >= 16%) were met, so by our own rules this is an
at-or-above-plan quarter, but the ROE cleared by a whisker and the asset-quality
proof is missing. Net read: a decent-looking quarter with real unanswered
questions underneath about how both the growth and the clean bad-loan number
were produced. Verdict: PROCEED WITH CAVEATS.

## SECTOR KNOWLEDGE (Indian NBFC-MFI)

Appended to sectors/NBFC-MFI.md (2026-07-30 SATIN entry). Points:
- Cycle: sector climbing out of an FY26 stress trough (Q1 FY26 worst, credit
  costs ~4.5%); collection efficiency ~99.9%, PAR falling. FY27 headline profit
  growth is largely low-base recovery.
- Seasonality: Q1 (Apr-Jun) is a seasonally soft disbursement quarter; Q4 is
  strongest and flatters return ratios. A Q1 QoQ disbursement dip is normal.
- Economics: yield ~21-23% less cost of funds ~8% gives NIM ~13-15%; through-
  cycle ROA 2.5-3.5% healthy, ~4.7% is a peak not a run-rate. Leverage D/E 3-4x
  normal, CRAR ~25%+ comfortable.
- Off-book / DA lever: direct assignment moves ~25% of the book off balance
  sheet and drives gain-on-sale; inflates ROA in lumpy quarters, means headline
  GNPA understates exposure. Ask for assignment volume, not just DA income.
- Core risk (over-indebtedness): AUM growth via larger tickets on a static
  borrower base precedes credit blow-ups. Track borrower count vs AUM, new-
  client run-rate, ticket size, exposure per borrower ahead of GNPA.
- Asset-quality optics: GNPA can be cleaned with write-offs and ARC sales; the
  honest read needs the ECL/stage-movement walk, not the headline number.
- Regulation: RBI MFI guardrails (lenders-per-borrower, indebtedness caps) cap
  growth; priority-sector status funds MFIs cheaply via bank DA.
- Concentration: UP/Bihar/Assam/West Bengal exposure means loan-waiver politics
  are a recurring sector shock; foreign borrowing adds INR-USD hedge risk.
- Competitive frame: peers CreditAccess, Spandana, Muthoot Microfin, Fusion;
  MFIs rank themselves favourably on their own decks, cross-check peer filings.
