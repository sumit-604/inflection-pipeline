## BUSINESS UNDERSTANDING NARRATIVE

GRP takes end-of-life tyres and turns them back into industrial raw material.
The main product is reclaim rubber. It is devulcanised rubber, milled into
sheets, that a tyre maker blends back into a new compound in place of virgin
natural or synthetic rubber. The customer buys it because it cuts compound
cost and carries a recycled-content claim. The blend ratio is fixed inside an
approved tyre recipe, so the grade must be qualified before it can be used.
The second product line, sold under Pyrova Energy, is newer. A continuous
pyrolysis reactor cracks scrap tyre into three streams: tyre pyrolysis oil,
recovered carbon black and recovered steel wire. Crumb rubber sits alongside
it. A third line, grouped as "Others", recycles nylon and polyolefin into
engineering plastics. Reclaim rubber was 92% of standalone revenue in Q1 FY27
and plastics the remaining 8%.

The customers are tyre and rubber-product manufacturers. Management names
contract cycles with tyre companies as quarterly or six-monthly, which tells
you price resets often and the producer holds little pricing power between
resets. Exports were 51% of standalone revenue in Q1 FY27 and North America is
the largest export block. Qualification is the switching cost: a grade sits
inside an approved recipe, so displacing it takes a requalification the buyer
does not want. That advantage is real but it is narrow, because the product
underneath it is a cost-driven commodity input.

Demand today comes from two places. Tyre makers want compound cost down, and
regulation wants recycled content up. GRP books a separate EPR income line,
Rs 4.9 cr in Q1 FY27 against Rs 4.6 cr in Q1 FY26, which is the Extended
Producer Responsibility credit the Indian plastic and tyre waste rules create.
Demand should grow if the recovered carbon black plant lands, because rCB
substitutes virgin carbon black in tyres, pigments and plastics, and it sells
at a higher value per tonne than pyrolysis oil.

The competitive advantage is not spread evenly. It sits on the qualified
reclaim grades, where customer approval creates switching cost. It does not
sit on the pyrolysis output, which is a young process business still short of
standalone profitability. It does not sit on the plastics line, which is
sub-scale and was loss-making at the segment level in FY26. This shallow pass
did not establish an independent moat scan, so no moat category is scored.

## ARCHETYPE AND QUALITY LADDER

Archetype: COMMODITY CONVERTER. The economics are spread, utilisation, cycle
position and cost-curve rank. GRP buys scrap and sells a converted output, and
the contract resets quarterly or six-monthly.

This classification has a hard framework consequence. Section 1B v3.7
Amendment 17 binds. Spot-year ROCE and rupee-denominated working-capital
trends must NOT be fed into Section 1B or FTTCP for a CONVERTER-classified
name. The FY26 ROCE of 6% is exactly the spot-year number the amendment
exists to exclude. Any later run that anchors Pillar 1 to FY26 ROCE has
broken the framework.

Quality ladder placement:

- FROM: R2 COST-ADVANTAGED CONVERTER. Margin comes from cost position, not
  price. ROCE has run 5, 9, 14, 16, 6 across FY22 to FY26. It has never held
  above the mid-teens for two consecutive years.
- TO: R3 VALUE-ADDED / SPEC'D SUPPLIER. The claim is that recovered carbon
  black, plus higher-value reclaim grades inside approved recipes, convert
  part of the book from spread economics to spec-in economics.

That is a one-rung claim. The rung-jump base rate is one rung per two to
three years, so the claim is inside the base rate and does not itself raise a
flag. Note that the TO rung applies to a slice, not the whole company. Reclaim
rubber remains R2 whatever Pyrova does.

Section 1B sector cap row: Recycling / Manufacturing, 25x. The fit is clean.
The cap is the ceiling only. Stage 11 derives the destination PE; this file

## THE ENGINE AND THE PROOF GATE

The engine is one thing. Pyrova must turn a pyrolysis process that currently
loses money into a recovered-carbon-black business that earns 18-20% EBITDA
margin, and it must do so while reclaim rubber volumes grow mid-teens.

Proof gate, metric by metric, quarter by quarter:

1. rCB production commences and is sold. Management points at Feb 2027. Not
   yet met.
2. Pyrova reaches standalone EBITDA break-even. Management said in Q1 FY27
   that the business is "shy of achieving stand-alone profitability". Not yet met.
3. Consolidated EBITDA margin holds above 12% for two consecutive quarters.
   Q1 FY27 printed 11.0%. Not yet met.
4. Consolidated ROCE returns above 14% for a full year. FY26 printed 6%. Not
   yet met.
5. Operating cash flow covers capex in a full year. Negative for two years
   running. Not yet met.

Gate status: NOT FIRED. Zero of five. The evidence FOR the climb is real and
it is recent. The pyrolysis plant achieved a 25-day continuous run in July
2026, which management names as the industry performance threshold. Export
volumes rebounded 20% as United States tariffs eased. EBITDA margin expanded
233 basis points year on year while gross margin fell 179 basis points, which
means the gain came from operating leverage and cost, not from price. That is
one quarter of evidence, not a fired gate.

## UGLINESS VERDICT

FY26 was ugly. PAT fell 85%. ROCE fell from 16% to 6%. Net worth fell Rs 13.3 cr.
Debt rose Rs 60.2 cr.

Reading one, ARTIFACT-OF-CLIMB. The fall traces to named, dated, external and
one-off causes. United States tariff disruption cut reclaim revenue from key
customers by about 33% and hit the corresponding raw-material margin by about
44%. FY25 carried Rs 21.4 cr of prior-period EPR accrual, inflating the base.
Pyrova commercialisation cost Rs 2.4 cr of EBITDA and PAT. One-offs included
a Rs 1.4 cr labour-code provision, a Rs 0.79 cr polymer-composite write-off,
Rs 0.42 cr of abandoned QIP expense and Rs 4.1 cr of forex loss. Q1 FY27
recovered on all three of volume, margin and profit.

Reading two, STRUCTURAL-FEATURE. Reclaim rubber is a converter. ROCE has
touched 16% once in five years and averaged about 10%. Debt is financing a
capex programme whose returns are unproven. A converter whose customer resets
price every quarter cannot hold margin when input costs move, and raw material
costs rose 31% in Q1 FY27.

The one observation that separates them: whether consolidated EBITDA margin
holds above 12% through a quarter in which raw-material cost rises and export
volumes do NOT grow. Q1 FY27 had a 20% export rebound carrying it. The next
quarter without that tailwind is the test.

Verdict: ARTIFACT-OF-CLIMB, leaning, NOT PROVEN. The causes are named and
external. The recovery is one quarter old.

## SECOND-ORDER CHAINS

1. Capex Rs 90-100 cr guided for FY27, against FY26 operating cash flow of
   Rs 29.6 cr and net debt already at 1.08x equity, means the funding gap is
   roughly Rs 60-70 cr. The company abandoned a QIP in FY26 and expensed
   Rs 0.42 cr on it. [INFERENCE] Either debt rises again toward 1.4x equity,
   or an equity raise returns to the table within four quarters. Both outcomes
   damage the per-share arithmetic the current price assumes. Watch the Q2 FY27
   borrowings line and any fresh QIP enabling resolution.

2. Tyre-company contracts reset quarterly or six-monthly, and raw-material
   cost rose 31% year on year in Q1 FY27 while gross margin fell 179 basis
   points. [INFERENCE] The pass-through works with a lag of one to two
   quarters, so a sustained rubber-price rise compresses two quarters before
   it is recovered. A margin print that holds through a rising-input quarter
   would be strong evidence for the R3 claim. A margin print that falls would
   confirm the converter classification.

3. The company books EPR income as a separate line, Rs 4.9 cr in Q1 FY27 and
   about Rs 21.4 cr of FY25's figure being prior-period accrual. [INFERENCE]
   A material slice of reported profit depends on a regulatory credit whose
   recognition timing management has already restated once. EPR income is
   policy-dependent earnings, not operating earnings, and Section 1B operating
   rules would strip it. A run must isolate EPR from operating EBITDA before
   any pillar is built.

4. Exports were 51% of standalone revenue and North America is the largest
   block, with the Q1 FY27 rebound explicitly attributed to United States
   tariffs easing. [INFERENCE] Roughly a quarter of group revenue is exposed
   to a single trade-policy decision that reversed once already inside
   eighteen months. Position sizing, not input shading, is where that risk
   belongs.

5. Net worth fell Rs 13.3 cr in FY26 while profit after tax was positive at
   Rs 4.6 cr and the dividend paid was Rs 7.73 cr. Those three numbers leave
   about Rs 10 cr unexplained [COMPUTED]. [INFERENCE] The residual is most
   likely a foreign-currency translation reserve movement on overseas
   subsidiaries, consistent with the Rs 4.1 cr of forex loss already disclosed,
   but the cause is NOT established by the documents read. This is a real gap,
   not a rounding artefact.
