## PART 1: CLAIM-BY-CLAIM VERIFICATION

### Q1: Raw-material cost inflation / margin compression FY2025, matching AARTISURF's raw-material ratio 75.6%->82.3% and EBITDA margin 10.70%->7.56%

**Verdict: PARTIALLY VERIFIED (direction corroborated by two peers, magnitude far smaller for both; third peer contradicts by segment)**

Peer evidence:
- GALAXYSURF (Nov 2025 call, Q2 FY26): H1 FY26 EBITDA declined 5% YoY to ~INR251cr vs ~INR265cr, attributed to customers reformulating away from oleochemical/fatty-alcohol-based surfactants toward petrochemical alternatives amid "persistently high feedstock prices."
- GALAXYSURF (May 2026 call, Q4 FY26): EBITDA/MT fell to ~INR20,114 vs ~INR21,715 in Q4 FY25, explicitly linked to "sharp feedstock price escalation across raw materials."
- GALAXYSURF (Aug 2025 call, Q1 FY26): EBITDA/MT held near flat (~INR20,000 vs ~INR20,200) even as feedstock (fatty alcohol) prices were "buoyant," with margin defended mainly through reformulation and mix, not fully passed through.
- ROSSARI (May 2026 call, Q4/FY26): EBITDA margin 11.3% vs 12% in Q4 FY25; FY26 full year 11.9% vs 12.7% FY25. Ketan Sablok (CFO), on the call: "March saw some raw material price increases, some of them were to the tune of 25% to 30%, which impacted the costs... Some of these cost increases have since been passed on to the customers and some of them are in the process of happening over the month of April and May."
- ROSSARI (Jul 2026 call, Q1 FY27): EBITDA margin 11.6% vs 12.5% in Q1 FY26 (YoY compression continues). Sunil Chari (MD): "we have had a lot of volatility in buying our raw materials and in freight costs... this is causing us some degree of margin loss for us."
- FCL (Feb 2026 call, Q3/9M FY26): blended gross margin 36% for 9M FY26 vs 38% prior year, but Sanjay Tibrewala (CFO) attributes this explicitly to the CrudeChem consolidation effect plus discretionary discounting to Indian textile customers during the tariff period ("margin pressures we got in India in the textiles because many companies, we had to support them in the times of tariff, which we are rolling back"), not raw-material cost inflation per se.
- FCL (Jul 2026 call, Q1 FY27): gross margin actually IMPROVED to 35.42% vs ~33% in Q1 FY26; EBITDA margin improved to 15.70% vs 13.93%. Management states the company "successfully pass[ed] on higher raw material costs while maintaining healthy blended margins" — the opposite direction to AARTISURF's claim, though this is a blended, CrudeChem-heavy number, not a clean India-surfactants read.

Peers silent: none of the three peers discuss AARTISURF's specific raw-material ratio (75.6%->82.3% of revenue) or the magnitude of margin collapse AARTISURF describes (10.70%->7.56%, a ~29% relative EBITDA-margin decline).

Net read: Two of three peers (Galaxy, Rossari) independently and repeatedly describe raw-material/feedstock cost inflation compressing margins across FY25-26, corroborating the DIRECTION of AARTISURF's claim. But the magnitude at both peers is an order smaller — Galaxy's EBITDA/MT declines run roughly 5-7%, Rossari's EBITDA-margin compression runs 0.7-0.9 percentage points per period — versus AARTISURF's 3.14-point (29% relative) EBITDA-margin collapse and 6.7-point raw-material-ratio jump. FCL does not corroborate at all: its margin issue was discounting/mix, and by Q1 FY27 its blended margin had improved. The peer set supports that raw-material inflation was a real, sector-wide pressure in this window, but not one of the magnitude AARTISURF reports.

### Q2: Indian specialty-surfactants/personal-care demand growth rates peers cite now, vs AARTISURF's stale 2020-vintage figures (global 4.5% CAGR; India 8-9.6% CAGR through 2022)

**Verdict: CONTRADICTED**

Peer evidence:
- GALAXYSURF (Feb 2026 call, Q3 FY26): direct quote from management in response to an analyst question on India demand: "India market, even today, if you see all of them are reporting 2% to 4% volume growth, underlying volume growth."
- GALAXYSURF (Aug 2025 call, Q1 FY26): India volume growth ~3% YoY, described as broadly in line with FMCG peers.
- GALAXYSURF (May 2026 call, Q4 FY26): India volumes recovered to +8% YoY in the quarter specifically, aided by GST rate rationalization — but this is described as a sequential bounce off a soft base, not a structural step-up to high-single/low-double-digit trend growth.
- ROSSARI (Jan 2026 call, Q3 FY26): "softer domestic demand environment," domestic growth of 10% vs export growth of 26% YoY for the 9-month period, with management noting the export/domestic gap would persist "unless domestic sentiment really changes, especially in Textiles and FMCG business."
- ROSSARI (Oct 2025, May 2026, Jul 2026 calls): recurring language of "relatively soft" or "muted" domestic demand conditions across FY26.

Peers silent: none of the three peers cite a current India specialty-surfactants or personal-care demand growth figure in the 8-9.6% range AARTISURF's stale figures imply; none reference the global 4.5% surfactants CAGR figure either way.

Net read: The peer set directly contradicts the currency of AARTISURF's demand figures. Current, repeatedly-cited India volume growth in the closest comparable peer (Galaxy) runs 2-4% underlying (with a one-quarter GST-driven bounce to 8%), and Rossari's domestic growth (10% for 9M FY26, mostly price/mix-driven per company commentary) is also well below AARTISURF's 8-9.6% figure, with management explicitly framing domestic demand as soft. AARTISURF's 2020-vintage growth assumption is stale by more than direction — it overstates the current run-rate by roughly 2-4x based on this peer read.

### Q3: Export-share decline, Chinese pricing pressure, or export demand weakness FY2024-25, matching AARTISURF's export share 28.2%->20%

**Verdict: PARTIALLY VERIFIED**

Peer evidence:
- FCL (May 2026 call, Q4/FY26): "Indian textile export grew 2.1% year-on-year in FY '26 to [Rs] 3.16 trillion... growth occurred across 120-plus destinations despite challenges last year like US tariff and West Asia disruption." Management separately characterized FY26 as "one of the worst years of the decade for the textile companies" due to tariff and geopolitical disruption. This corroborates broad export-demand weakness in the same window as AARTISURF's claimed decline, though it is an industry-wide textile-export statistic, not FCL's own chemical-export share (FCL's own reported export share actually rose to 48% in Q3 FY26 from 25%, but this was driven entirely by the CrudeChem US consolidation, an unrelated M&A effect, not organic Indian surfactant exports).
- GALAXYSURF (Nov 2025, Feb 2026 calls): reciprocal US tariffs (reduced from 50% to 18% by Feb 2026) were cited as a live drag on the company's international business, with a cumulative FY26 EBITDA impact estimated at 3-5% of FY25 EBITDA.
- GALAXYSURF (Nov 2025 call): in response to an analyst question (Divyansh Gupta), management noted one of its biggest US customers "has very clearly talked about the US being a cost of concern for them and they're looking at China as their way to be able to compensate" — a direct reference to Chinese cost competitiveness reshaping customer sourcing decisions, though framed around a US customer's own tariff arbitrage, not a China-vs-India price war in Indian surfactant exports specifically.
- ROSSARI (May 2026 call): management describes the competitive landscape as including "global surfactant majors like BASF, Dow, Lubrizol, Syensqo, Evonik... Chinese companies are there. In the Indian market there is imports coming from all the sources" — confirming Chinese import competition is a live, named feature of the Indian surfactants/specialty-chemicals market, without quantifying pricing impact.
- ROSSARI (Jan 2026 call): a raw-material sourcing anecdote (not a demand/export claim) references China as a supplier of phenol, and a May 2026 call anecdote describes a Rs5cr hit from delayed Chinese phenol shipments — supply-side, not export-competition evidence.

Peers silent: no peer quantifies an export-share decline for India-made surfactants sold into FMCG/personal-care channels comparable to AARTISURF's 28.2%->20% move, and no peer frames Chinese competition as a direct price-undercutting threat in Indian surfactant exports specifically (as opposed to a general presence in the import mix, or a tariff-arbitrage dynamic for a third country's customer).

Net read: The peer set corroborates the broad backdrop — Indian textile/specialty-chemical exports were under real pressure from US tariffs and geopolitical disruption in FY25-26, and Chinese competitive presence in Indian surfactants/specialty chemicals is confirmed as real by two peers — but nobody speaks to the specific magnitude or channel (FMCG/personal-care surfactant exports) that AARTISURF's claim concerns. Directionally supportive, not confirmatory of scale.

### Q4: New-entrant/capacity-driven margin compression, consistent with AARTISURF's AR claim of intensifying competition

**Verdict: PARTIALLY VERIFIED (strong evidence, but in an adjacent geography)**

Peer evidence:
- GALAXYSURF (Aug 2025, Nov 2025, Feb 2026, May 2026 — all four calls): recurring, escalating description of a "backward-integrated," "aggressive" local competitor in Egypt/AMET taking share from Galaxy. By Feb 2026, AMET volumes were described as down roughly 30-35% from peak; by May 2026, AMET volumes fell a further 15% YoY, driven by a mix of this competitive erosion and West Asia supply disruption. This is a clear, repeatedly-cited, multi-quarter case of new/backward-integrated competitive entry compressing volumes and margin for a directly comparable surfactants producer.
- ROSSARI, FCL: neither peer describes a "new entrant" dynamic in comparable terms. Rossari's margin pressure is framed around RM cost and a challenged institutional/B2C segment; FCL's is framed around tariff-driven discounting to support customers.

Peers silent: Rossari and FCL do not corroborate a new-entrant/capacity-driven competitive intensification story in India domestic surfactants specifically.

Net read: Galaxy's AMET/Egypt experience is a strong, real, and repeatedly-documented instance of exactly the dynamic AARTISURF's AR describes (new/backward-integrated capacity compressing an incumbent's volumes and margins), which lends real-world plausibility to the claim as a sector mechanism. But it is evidenced in the AMET export market, not the India domestic FMCG market AARTISURF operates in, so it maps adjacently rather than directly. Weighted as partial corroboration of the mechanism, not the specific India-market claim.

### Q5: Are peers in a parallel capacity-expansion cycle, or is AARTISURF's CWIP ramp (7.64->14.65->41.03cr FY24-26) out-of-cycle?

**Verdict: CONTRADICTED — AARTISURF looks like a lone expander, not part of an industry-wide race**

Peer evidence:
- GALAXYSURF (Aug 2025 call): guided FY26/27 capex to just ~INR120-150cr/year of maintenance/debottlenecking, stating the company was "not planning anything significant" beyond that, citing tariff and demand uncertainty. GALAXYSURF (Nov 2025 call): CWIP stood at ~INR260cr as of FY25, mostly from projects initiated roughly 1.5 years earlier — i.e., Galaxy's big capex wave (cumulative ~INR480cr over the prior 3 years, per the Nov 2025 call) was already winding down, not accelerating, through the period covered.
- ROSSARI (Jan 2026 call): was still in an active capex cycle at this point — 15,000 MTPA ethoxylation capacity at Unitop Dahej just commissioned, and a ~Rs192cr capex plan across Rossari/Unitop/Tristar on the books.
- ROSSARI (May 2026 call): explicitly REPHASED that plan down. Ketan Sablok (CFO): "we internally have decided to rephase our earlier CAPEX spend, which was announced in April of Rs. 192 crore across Rossari, Unitop and Tristar. The re-evaluation of this said investment plan is being done in light of the evolving business requirements and the market conditions." FY27 capex guidance was cut to just Rs50-75cr.
- ROSSARI (Jul 2026 call): confirmed the retreat — "in India currently we have slowed down on all the CAPEX spends... Only certain new products or new molecules that are in R&D, we will go ahead with." The KSA greenfield project was also being paced cautiously, "given the prevailing geographical uncertainty."
- FCL: capacity expansion was aggressive and real (Texas facility capacity taken from 80,000 to 148,000 to a planned 268,000 MTPA across the four calls), but this is entirely in the US oilfield-chemicals segment acquired via the CrudeChem deal — a different product line and geography, not organic Indian surfactants/textile-chemicals capacity. FCL's own India-domestic textile/hygiene segment saw no comparable capex acceleration in the transcripts.

Net read: The two peers that are direct segment comparables (Galaxy, Rossari) were both winding down or explicitly rephasing capex during the same window AARTISURF's CWIP was reportedly accelerating (7.64->14.65->41.03cr, FY24-26). Rossari's move is the most direct and dated: management pulled back a formally announced Rs192cr plan citing market conditions in the same fiscal year AARTISURF's capex appears to be ramping fastest. This is a genuine contradiction of an "industry-wide capacity race" framing — the direct evidence points to AARTISURF as a lone expander against a cautious-to-retreating peer set, which is a materially different risk read than "everyone is building."

### Q6: Receivable/payable stretch or cash-conversion deterioration with FMCG customers FY2024-25, matching AARTISURF's CFO 51.96->11.14cr while payables grew 27.8% vs 12% revenue

**Verdict: PARTIALLY VERIFIED**

Peer evidence:
- ROSSARI (Oct 2025 call, Q2 FY26): working capital stretched to 102 days vs 95 days in March; OCF turned negative for the period. In direct response to an analyst question, Ketan Sablok (CFO) confirmed: "we had... a little stretch on the working capital... that led to this negative cash flow," attributing part of it to a large institutional sale carrying a long payment cycle. By the Jan 2026 call (Q3 FY26), management reported working capital had improved sequentially — so this was a one-quarter, partially self-corrected episode, not a sustained multi-quarter deterioration.
- FCL (May 2026 call, Q4/FY26): working capital held stable at 79 days, described by management as "at par with industry." OCF was negative for FY26, but management was explicit this reflects a one-time Ind AS accounting treatment (acquisition-related investment deducted from operating cash flow), not a receivable/payable or collections problem: "that entry will not be there [going forward]... you will see the positive cash flows from operations. Actually, till now also it's positive only." FCL's WC further improved to 72 days by Jul 2026.
- GALAXYSURF: no explicit receivable/payable stretch or cash-conversion commentary was found in any of the four calls reviewed.

Peers silent: Galaxy is silent on this topic across all four calls. Neither peer discusses a payables-growth-outpacing-revenue dynamic specifically with FMCG customers, and neither describes a cash-conversion deterioration of the scale or duration AARTISURF reports (CFO falling from Rs51.96cr to Rs11.14cr).

Net read: Rossari's Q2 FY26 episode is real, dated, and directly on-topic (working capital stretch, negative OCF, explicitly confirmed by the CFO on the call) and falls within the same broad FY24-25/FY26 window as AARTISURF's claim — a genuine, if partial, corroboration of the mechanism (cash-conversion strain from stretched collections in India specialty chemicals during this period). But it was transient (one quarter, then improving) rather than the sustained multi-year deterioration AARTISURF describes, and its driver (one large institutional customer) differs from AARTISURF's broader FMCG-customer-payables narrative. FCL's negative OCF is a false positive on inspection — explicitly not a working-capital story per management. Galaxy's silence on the topic, given it is the closest segment peer, is itself notable: if a cash-conversion problem were structural to India surfactants-for-FMCG relationships broadly, it might be expected to surface in Galaxy's commentary too, and it does not.

This silence is informative: in the one instance where a peer's own analyst base surfaced a comparably-scoped India specialty-chemicals name for comment, it was Fineotex, not AARTISURF — suggesting AARTISURF does not currently register as a name-of-note in its closest peers' investor conversations.

- China-sourcing supply risk causing one-off cost spikes independent of pricing competition (Rossari's Rs5cr phenol-shipment-delay anecdote).
- Institutional/B2C segment drag requiring active portfolio rationalization (Rossari, all four calls) — a diversification-quality risk adjacent to, but not identical to, AARTISURF's own segment mix.

## PART 4: TRIANGULATION SUMMARY

- Claims verified: 0 of 6
- Claims partially verified: 4 of 6 (Q1 raw-material margin compression, Q3 export/China pressure, Q4 competitive intensification, Q6 cash-conversion stretch)
- Claims contradicted: 2 of 6 (Q2 demand growth currency, Q5 capex-cycle framing) — these go to synthesis as priority items
- Claims unverifiable: 0 of 6

**Single most consequential contradiction:** Q5, the capex cycle. AARTISURF's CWIP is reported accelerating (7.64->14.65->41.03cr, FY24-26) in the same window its two closest domestic peers were pulling back — Galaxy had already wound down its prior capex wave to maintenance-only spend, and Rossari explicitly rephased a formally announced Rs192cr plan down to Rs50-75cr, citing market conditions. This reframes AARTISURF's capacity build from "keeping pace with an industry-wide race" to "expanding counter-cyclically against a cautious peer set" — a materially different risk read.

**Single strongest independent confirmation:** Q1, raw-material cost inflation direction. Two independent peers (Galaxy, Rossari), across multiple quarters each, describe feedstock/raw-material cost escalation compressing margins in the same FY25-26 window AARTISURF reports its own margin collapse. The mechanism is real and sector-wide. The magnitude is not: both peers show margin effects an order smaller than AARTISURF's reported swing.

**Overall assessment:** The peer set complicates the main company's narrative more than it supports or undercuts it wholesale. It corroborates the directional story that raw-material inflation and competitive intensity (including Chinese-import and backward-integration dynamics) are real, sector-wide pressures in FY25-26 — but at every point where AARTISURF's own figures are unusually large (its margin collapse, its export-share decline, its cash-conversion crisis), the peer set shows a much smaller version of the same mechanism or none at all. And on the two points where a clean read was possible, the peer set actively contradicts AARTISURF's framing: current India demand growth (2-4% underlying, per Galaxy) is far below AARTISURF's stale 8-9.6% claim, and the peer capex posture (retreating/rephasing) contradicts an industry-wide capacity-race framing for AARTISURF's own CWIP ramp. No peer mentions AARTISURF by name, even where a comparably-scoped peer (Fineotex) was raised unprompted by an analyst — a silence that, combined with the contradicted claims, argues for treating AARTISURF's own figures as outliers requiring independent explanation rather than assuming they simply reflect a shared industry condition.

## PART 5: CROSS-PEER HYPOTHESIS

Reading across all three peers together (not visible in any single one's disclosures): each of the three, independently and for different stated reasons, is de-emphasizing the exact segment AARTISURF is expanding into — plain-vanilla, India-domestic surfactants/specialty chemicals sold into FMCG and textile end markets — in favor of adjacent, higher-value chemistries. Galaxy has wound down new India capex to maintenance-only levels while its AMET/export volumes erode to backward-integrated local competition, effectively holding position defensively rather than expanding. Rossari is explicitly rationalizing its lower-margin institutional/B2C consumer business and redirecting new investment toward pharma, aroma chemicals, and oil-and-gas-adjacent chemistries, while cutting its own India specialty-chemicals capex plan by more than half. FCL has pivoted the large majority of its incremental capital and growth narrative entirely into US oilfield chemicals via the CrudeChem acquisition, effectively treating its legacy India textile/hygiene business as a stable-but-non-priority base rather than a growth vector.

**Testable hypothesis:** the India-domestic, commodity-adjacent surfactants-for-FMCG/textile segment is approaching a structural margin and growth ceiling that better-capitalized incumbents are quietly rotating away from — toward pharma intermediates, animal nutrition, aroma chemicals, and oilfield/industrial chemistries — rather than defending or expanding within it. If this holds, plain-surfactants-for-FMCG segment EBITDA/MT and volume growth across the industry (AARTISURF included) should continue to lag the adjacent segments these three peers are now prioritizing over the next 2-3 fiscal years, and AARTISURF's counter-cyclical capacity expansion (Q5 finding) would be entering a segment its more diversified peers are actively exiting, not one they are racing to fill.

This hypothesis is not stated by any single peer — each only describes its own strategic pivot — but emerges from the combined pattern of all three moving the same direction (away from core India surfactants/textile chemicals, toward adjacencies) at the same time.
