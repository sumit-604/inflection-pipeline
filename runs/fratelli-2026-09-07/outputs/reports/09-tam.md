## SECTION 1: MARKET DEFINITION

### 1A Precise boundaries

- **Product scope**: Still wine, all price tiers (entry/Chardonnay-Sangiovese,
  premium/Sette, luxury/J'NOON, above Rs 2,000), sparkling wine (Fratelli Brut),
  and wine-based RTD/canned formats (Shotgun, TiLT). Excludes beer, IMFL/spirits,
  and any non-wine-based alcobev. Fruit wine is included where corpus sources
  bundle it with "local wine" (IWSR convention); this stage does not attempt to
  separate it out, as neither Fratelli nor the industry sources it cites do so.
- **Geographic scope**: India, domestic production and domestic consumption.
  Fratelli manufactures only in India (Akluj and Jambhali, Maharashtra) and
  does not import; exports (17 countries, unquantified) are a minor reverse
  flow, held out of the core TAM/SAM and treated as a SAM-expansion lever
  (Section 5D).
- **Customer scope**: Off-trade retail (state excise-licensed shops), on-trade
  HoReCa, the CSD (Canteen Stores Department) institutional channel, and
  emerging e-commerce/D2C.
- **Channel scope**: All of the above; no channel excluded, since Fratelli's
  own disclosed footprint (31,000 touch points, 29 states/UTs, CSD access)
  already spans them.
- **Price segment**: All tiers. Fratelli's portfolio spans entry to luxury
  (above Rs 2,000), so no price band is excluded from SAM on product-fit
  grounds.
- **Explicit inclusion**: The wine-based RTD/canned category (Shotgun, TiLT)
  is treated as a second, additive addressable segment layered on top of the
  still-wine market, per management's own framing ("a whole new category")
  and per this stage's own read of the evidence (different manufacturing
  economics, different consumer, near-zero overlap in occasion).
- **Explicit exclusion**: Imported wine is excluded from SAM (not TAM) because
  Fratelli does not import or distribute third-party imported wine; it is a
  domestic producer competing against imports, not a participant in the
  import channel. Beer, IMFL, and spirits are excluded from both TAM and SAM;
  Fratelli's own investor deck states wine is under 1% of India's alcohol
  market (Investor_Presentation_Q1FY27_Aug2026.pdf p.45), which is cited here
  as directional context for Section 4, not as a TAM boundary.

### 1B Management's own TAM claim

Management does not state a single "TAM" figure in dollar or rupee terms in
the classic sense; instead it cites three separate third-party market-size
statistics, consistently across the AR and both decks, and confirms one of
them live on a concall:

- Total Indian wine market: USD 229 million (2024) rising to USD 803 million
  (2032), "~16% CAGR" (Annual_Report_FY2026.pdf p.24-25, sourced to "Ken
  Research, Pragma market research, abd India" and, separately in the same
  document, to "IMARC, Retail.com"). Also cited as USD 195 million (2023) to
  USD 803 million (2032) in Investor_Presentation_Q1FY27_Aug2026.pdf p.45.
- Premium wine market: USD 71.9 million (2024) to USD 301.7 million (2032)
  (Annual_Report_FY2026.pdf p.24).
- Wine RTD market: "already Rs 500 crores" market size, confirmed twice —
  Investor_Presentation_Q4FY26_May2026.pdf p.7 ("Aligns with India's growing
  wine RTD TAM ~ Rs 500 Cr") and directly on the concall: "It is already
  INR500 crores, the market size" (Concall_Q4FY26_Jun2026_Transcript.pdf,
  internal p.7 of 14, CFO Aditya Sekhri responding to a direct analyst
  question).

**Credibility read: SPECIFIC.** These are dated, third-party-sourced, and (per
Section 2 below) this stage's own independent web search corroborates the
USD 229 million (2024) figure almost exactly (IMARC Group cites the identical
USD 229.0 million, 2024, in a live public report). The RTD figure is
independently plausible too (see Section 2, Method 1). **This is an important,
counter-intuitive finding: management's macro market-size citations are not
the inflated part of the story.** The inflated claims sit one level down, in
management's SHARE of these markets (Section 2 and 3B), not in the market-size
figures themselves.

### Method 4 — Import substitution (EU FTA)

India's wine import duty is set to fall from ~150% toward 20% (wines above
EUR 10 CIF) or 30% (EUR 2.50-10 CIF) over a phased ~7-year schedule; wines
below EUR 2.50 CIF get no relief (web search, corroborated independently of
the corpus; also discussed in Investor_Presentation_Q4FY26_May2026.pdf
p.18-19 and by Sula on its concalls). Sula's own math: even after duty cuts,
EU wines are expected to land around **Rs 2,000 MRP in Maharashtra** — which
is precisely the boundary Fratelli itself uses to define its own "luxury,
insulated" tier ("more than 90% of our revenues are comprised of products
which are less than INR2,000 MRP," Concall_Q4FY26_Jun2026_Transcript.pdf
internal p.7). **This is a direct contradiction of the "insulated" framing**:
the FTA does not threaten Fratelli's 90% sub-Rs 2,000 book much, but it
threatens exactly the >Rs 2,000 luxury slice Fratelli claims >50% share of
and calls its highest-growth engine (J'NOON +40%+ YoY). The addressable
premium segment above Rs 2,000 gains a new, better-financed entrant class
over the FTA's 7-year window; this stage cannot quantify the erosion (no
volume/value split by price band disclosed) but flags it as a live TAM risk,
not a closed question, contrary to management's "we don't anticipate much
impact at all" framing (same concall page).

### Method 5 — Global benchmark (directional only, per staleness/quality rule)

India's per-capita wine consumption sits far below the 2018-2023 global
average of 2.9 litres/year (web search, OIV-adjacent sourcing) and even below
China's already-low 0.5 litres/year. This supports the "long runway, early
stage" narrative directionally but is NOT used to size a headline TAM number
(per-capita comparisons carry the framework's lowest confidence tier and
cultural/regulatory factors specific to India, e.g. state-by-state excise
regimes, make direct extrapolation unsound).

## SECTION 3: SAM & SOM

### 3A SAM

Filters applied to TAM:
1. **Product fit**: no cut — Fratelli's portfolio spans all price tiers plus
   RTD, matching the full product scope.
2. **Geography**: no material cut — 29 states/UTs is close to full national
   coverage.
3. **Channel**: no cut — off-trade, HoReCa, CSD, e-commerce all already
   accessed per the injected PRODUCTS description.
4. **Customer/price segment**: no cut, for the same reason as (1).
5. **Capability**: the one real cut. Fratelli manufactures domestically and
   does not import; imports are excluded from SAM (not TAM). Using the AR's
   own imported/domestic split chart (Investor_Presentation_Q1FY27_Aug2026.pdf
   p.45: 26%/74% import/domestic for 2025P, similar trend into 2024), domestic
   share ≈ 74%:
   - Domestic wine 2024: Rs 1,917cr × 0.74 = **Rs 1,419 Cr**
   - Domestic wine 2025: Rs 2,320cr × 0.74 = **Rs 1,717 Cr**
   - RTD (fully domestic, no cut): Rs 500-600cr retained in full

**SAM conservative = Rs 1,419cr + Rs 500cr = Rs 1,919 Cr**
**SAM realistic = Rs 1,717cr + Rs 600cr = Rs 2,317 Cr**
**SAM (conservative) as % of TAM (conservative) = 1,919 ÷ 2,417 = 79%**

### 3B SOM at 3 and 5 years

**Current share of SAM**: Rs 181.29cr (FY26 revenue) ÷ Rs 1,919cr (SAM,
conservative) = **9.4%**. (Compare: management's own deck slide claims "1/3."
This is the single most important number this stage produces — it is
derived entirely from management's OWN cited market-size sources, not from
an adversarial external one.)

**Share trajectory** (share-gain rules applied): Fratelli's credibility grade
is D (B05, carried forward): four consecutive quarters of guidance walked
down, an unreconciled PBT/PAT claim on the Q3FY26 call, an undisclosed
basis-change repeated live on the Q4FY26 call, and the wine segment itself
running at a loss for two straight years (B04). Against that, the one
genuinely clean positive is real: Shotgun RTD delivery has tracked close to
plan (B05 analyst_note). Net: **normal, not aggressive, share-gain rate**
applies — 1.5pp cumulative by year 3, extending to 2.5pp cumulative by year
5 (below the 3-5pp "aggressive" band, which this stage does not award given
the credibility and profitability record).

- Year 3 share: 9.4% + 1.5pp = **10.9%**
- Year 5 share: 9.4% + 2.5pp = **11.9%**

**SAM growth assumption — this is the load-bearing judgment call.** The
AR/deck cite a forward-looking ~16% CAGR (2024/25-2032/34), sourced to
third-party industry models. But Sula — the same-belt, same-era peer with by
far the most credible independent voice in this corpus — states on its most
recent call (Aug-2026) that "the last 2 years have not seen much growth in
the overall wine industry, neither in domestic nor in imports as per IWSR…
there has been maybe low single-digit growth" and separately confirms its
OWN revenue has been "almost flat broadly in that INR 600 crore range" for
3-4 years (SULA-Concall_Aug_2026_Transcript.txt p.7-9). Per the operator
brief's explicit instruction, this stage tests Vision 2030 against the
REALIZED trend, not the projected one. **Working SAM CAGR used for SOM
arithmetic: 4%** (blends a near-flat still-wine base per Sula's disclosed
reality with a modest, credibility-discounted contribution from RTD growth,
which itself is separately contested — B06 found Sula's canned-wine
franchise showing "slow traction," the opposite of Fratelli's claimed 30%
QoQ RTD growth). The 14-16% third-party figure is retained in the report
as the industry's own forward projection, not this stage's working
assumption, and is reported separately in `tam_growth_pct` context.

Arithmetic:
- SAM at year 3 = Rs 1,919cr × 1.04³ = Rs 2,158.6cr
- SOM year 3 = 10.9% × Rs 2,158.6cr = **Rs 236 Cr**
- SAM at year 5 = Rs 1,919cr × 1.04⁵ = Rs 2,334.8cr
- SOM year 5 = 11.9% × Rs 2,334.8cr = **Rs 279 Cr**

**Implied revenue CAGR from FY26 base (Rs 181.29cr)**:
- Year 3: (236 ÷ 181.29)^(1/3) − 1 = **9.2%**
- Year 5: (279 ÷ 181.29)^(1/5) − 1 = **9.0%**

This is the formal handoff figure for Stage 11. It sits far below every
management growth claim in the corpus: the FY27 guided 30% (Concall_Q4FY26,
p.6), the "20%+ CAGR" Vision 2030 growth target (Investor_Presentation_
Q4FY26_May2026.pdf p.20), and dramatically below the ~28.9% CAGR arithmetic
actually requires to hit Rs 500cr by FY30 from the Rs 181.29cr FY26 base
(computed: (500/181.29)^(1/4)−1 = 28.9%).

## SECTION 5: SUMMARY & RUNWAY

### 5A Funnel

```
TAM (conservative, total wine + RTD, incl. imports): Rs 2,417 Cr
   -> less imports (Fratelli does not import; ~21% of total wine value):
SAM (domestic wine + RTD, conservative):              Rs 1,919 Cr  (79% of TAM)
   -> current captured share (9.4%):
Current revenue (FY26, wine-only, audited):            Rs   181 Cr
   -> 3-year execution-adjusted share gain (+1.5pp):
SOM Year 3:                                            Rs   236 Cr  (9.2% CAGR)
   -> 5-year execution-adjusted share gain (+2.5pp):
SOM Year 5:                                            Rs   279 Cr  (9.0% CAGR)
```

### 5B Runway assessment

- **Revenue headroom** = SAM ÷ current revenue = 1,919 ÷ 181.29 = **10.6x**
- **TAM growth rate**: this stage's working (conservative, Sula-anchored)
  assumption is **4%** near-term; the third-party industry-projected figure
  (14-16% per IMARC/Ken Research) is real but NOT YET REALIZED per the most
  credible same-belt peer's own disclosure of a flat 2-year actual trend —
  reported here as a separately-flagged, contested figure, not blended
  silently into the headline.
- **Company CAGR vs. TAM**: this stage's own SOM-implied CAGR (~9%) sits
  ABOVE the 4% conservative TAM-growth working assumption (genuine share
  gain, consistent with the RTD-led second engine) but BELOW the industry's
  own 14-16% projected CAGR. If the optimistic 14-16% industry CAGR proves
  real and Fratelli grows only at the ~9% this stage supports, **Fratelli's
  share of the market would shrink over time**, not rise toward "a third" —
  the opposite of the "growing dominance" narrative management's decks
  imply.
- **Years to saturate SAM at current growth**: at a ~9% company CAGR against
  a ~4% SAM CAGR, Fratelli would need roughly 25-30 years to reach 50%+ of
  SAM — i.e., saturation risk is not a near-term constraint; headroom is
  ample in absolute terms.

### 5C Runway classification: **GOOD**

Reasoning: headroom alone (10.6x) would suggest STRONG or higher on a pure
multiple basis. This stage downgrades one notch to GOOD because (a) the
REALIZED near-term TAM growth rate, per the most credible independent
witness in the corpus (Sula), is flat-to-low-single-digit, not the 14-16%
the company cites; (b) execution credibility is graded D (four consecutive
quarters of guidance cuts, an unreconciled concall PBT/PAT claim, an
undisclosed basis change repeated live); and (c) the wine segment itself
has posted two consecutive years of loss (B04). The headroom is real and

## ANALYST NOTE

The single most decision-relevant finding: management's macro market-size
citations (Rs 1,917-2,320cr total wine, Rs 500-600cr RTD) are accurate and
this stage's own independent search corroborates them almost exactly — the
TAM story is not inflated. The SHARE story is. Applying management's own
cited market size to management's own audited revenue yields a 9.4% share,
not "a third." The Vision 2030 Rs 500cr/FY30 target requires a 28.9% revenue
CAGR against a SOM-supported ~9%, a physical capacity ceiling (via B07's 10%
capex-embedded-growth proxy) of only ~Rs 265cr at the same horizon, and a
same-belt peer (Sula) independently disclosing a flat, not accelerating,
category over the last two years. Every one of these checks points the same
direction. The mgmt_claim_ratio (1.9x, crore basis) undersells the gap;
the required-CAGR-multiple (~3.2x) is the more honest read for Stage 11.
