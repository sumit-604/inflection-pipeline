# SECTION 1: THE BUSINESS MODEL IN PLAIN ENGLISH

## 1A. One-line description

Pitti Engineering turns electrical steel, iron and steel scrap into the laminated cores, castings and
machined parts that go inside motors, generators and locomotives, then sells them to global equipment
makers, and is trying to move from selling the raw stamped sheet (commodity) to selling the whole
assembled part (engineered, higher margin) (AR FY26, PDF p.7, p.11).

## 1E. The chai-stall-uncle version

Think of Pitti as the workshop that makes the "guts" of a motor, the metal disc-stack and metal shell
that spins and generates power, not the motor itself. A railway locomotive, a wind turbine, a power
generator, even a data-centre backup generator, all need this disc-stack and shell inside them; Pitti
makes it and ships it to the company that builds the finished machine. Some of what Pitti sells is like
selling plain wheat flour: cheap, commodity, anyone with a mill can make it (that's the "loose lamination"
business, the company's own word for it). But increasingly Pitti is trying to sell the finished roti
instead of the flour: it welds, machines and assembles the disc-stack into a near-ready part, which earns
more money per kilo and is harder for a customer to walk away from once they have approved it (getting
approved takes years of testing, so once you are in, you tend to stay in). The catch: to bake more rotis
you need a bigger kitchen, and Pitti just spent a lot of borrowed and internal money (Rs 290 crore, on
top of an existing Rs 150 crore programme) building that bigger kitchen, which is why debt went up this
year even as the underlying business grew.

# ARCHETYPE CLASSIFICATION BY PRODUCT LINE

CLAUDE.md's archetype library lists "Build-to-spec component maker" and "Commodity converter (Section
1B v3.7 Amendment 17 binds)" as distinct entries. Pitti's own documents describe a business that
straddles both, by product tier, not by end market. This matters because Amendment 17 governs how
Section 1B and FTTCP must read ROCE and working-capital trend for a converter-classified line; applying
it to the wrong slice of revenue would misstate the whole company's earnings quality.

| Product line | Volume evidence | Archetype | Reasoning (anchored) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Loose laminations & low value-added assemblies | 12,920 MT of 19,240 MT total lamination & assembly volume, Q1FY27 (67% of that sub-total); Inv. Pres. slide 6 | COMMODITY CONVERTER | The company's own text calls this tier "engineering commodities" (AR PDF p.11). Margin is the spread between the steel input price and the stamped-sheet output price; India's largest-scale claim is a cost-curve/utilisation argument, not a pricing-power argument. MD&A explicitly frames this input as subject to Section 232 tariff-driven global steel-price volatility, managed through "price pass-through arrangements" (AR PDF p.20) — a converter's mechanism, not a spec-in premium. |
| High value-added assemblies / integrated sub-assemblies (rotor assembly, traction motor sub-assembly, shaft & spider, stator/rotor sub-assemblies) | High value-added assemblies +21.8% volume FY26; shaft/stator-frame integrated assemblies +31.9% volume FY26 (AR PDF p.4); Q1FY27 detail: 4,143 MT (+37.1%) and 1,212 MT (+21.7%) (Inv. Pres. slide 6) | BUILD-TO-SPEC COMPONENT MAKER | AR describes a four-stage customer relationship ("Qualify, Deliver, Expand, Integrate") gated by "extended cycles of rigorous sampling, audit and validation" that is "high by design" (AR PDF p.15). Each step up the value-chain ladder "increases engineering content, deepens customer integration, and improves realisation per tonne" (AR PDF p.11) — the defining signature of a build-to-spec, design-win business, not a converter. |
| Raw castings / foundry | Raw castings 1,350 MT of 3,191 MT total castings & machined, Q1FY27 (42% of that sub-total) (Inv. Pres. slide 6) | COMMODITY CONVERTER | Casting business described as processing "ductile iron, grey iron, and alloy steel" from 50 kg to 3 tonnes (AR PDF p.10); the margin driver named in the AR is utilisation (71% FY26) and capacity absorption, not customer-specific spec premium. This is a metal-input conversion spread business. |
| Machined components (shafts, gear cases, stator frames, wheel hubs, windmill pedestals) | Machined Components 1,562 MT of 3,191 MT total castings & machined, Q1FY27 (49%, +26.4% YoY) (Inv. Pres. slide 6); business achieved 81% capacity utilisation FY26 (AR PDF p.11) | BUILD-TO-SPEC COMPONENT MAKER | The Rs 290 Cr expansion is explicitly justified by "demand visibility from Caterpillar, Voith, Progress Rail, Siemens Mobility and Medha Servo" (AR PDF p.14) — named-customer, capex-cycle-linked, spec-driven demand, the textbook build-to-spec signature; also the AR's own language calls this tier "margin accretive and value additive" (AR PDF p.13). |
| By-products & scrap | 15,574 MT, Q1FY27, +36.3% YoY — larger by physical weight than either finished-product category (Inv. Important caution: because this line is large by TONNE but almost certainly small by RUPEE (scrap sells far below finished-product price per kilo), volume share must never be read as a proxy for revenue share for this line. |

Net read: Pitti is not a single archetype. Roughly the bottom half of its physical volume (loose
laminations, raw castings) behaves as a converter, where Amendment 17's ROCE/working-capital treatment
should bind; the top half (integrated assemblies, machined components, and the entire capex
justification for the new Rs 290 Cr programme) behaves as build-to-spec, where standard ROCE and
spot-year WC readings are appropriate. No document discloses the rupee split between these two halves
(NOT FOUND); the physical-volume mix shift toward the higher tiers (+21.8% to +37.1% volume growth in
value-added lines versus lower growth in loose laminations) is the best available evidence that the
converter share of revenue is shrinking, not the majority.

## 2D. Industry lifecycle stage

The core Indian rotating-electrical-equipment and machined-components industries are mature but growing
on the back of new demand pockets: electrification, data-centre power, and localisation/China+1 sourcing
shifts. Indian electric motor market projected USD 4,134.03 million (FY25) to USD 9,549.05 million (FY30),
a 9.4% CAGR (AR PDF p.21). Indian metal casting market projected USD 13,923.07 million (2025) to
USD 22,141.37 million (2034), a 5.29% CAGR (AR PDF p.22). Pitti's own position within this lifecycle is a
scale incumbent attempting to migrate up the value chain (from stamped-sheet commodity to integrated,
qualified assemblies) rather than a new entrant chasing volume growth alone; this is consistent with the
company's own "value creation model" framing of "higher-value product portfolio, entry into complex
applications and new markets" as the stated destination (AR PDF p.12).
