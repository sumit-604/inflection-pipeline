## SECTION 1: THE BUSINESS MODEL IN PLAIN ENGLISH

### 1A. One-line description
Capillary sells long-term subscription access to a cloud loyalty and
customer-engagement platform to large enterprise brands, priced on
members or transactions rather than seats, and it grows partly by buying
smaller loyalty-tech competitors and migrating their customers onto its
own platform to capture a margin uplift (AR p.75-76, p.78-80).

### 1B. Money flow chain, one chain per revenue stream

**1. Subscription (retainership and other services), 89.3% of FY26
revenue.**
Enterprise brand signs a 3-7 year contract, priced on members or
transactions on the platform (AR p.76) -> Capillary hosts the
Loyalty+/Engage+/Insights+/Rewards+/aiRA suite and operates the points
ledger of record (earn, burn, expiry) (AR p.79) -> delivers an always-on
loyalty program, campaign execution, analytics and, increasingly,
AI-driven recommendations -> the brand's marketing/loyalty team is the
payer -> paid through recurring subscription billing over the contract
term, with usage-based overage on top for aiRA (AR p.80-81).

**2. Installation revenue, 9.8% of FY26 revenue.**
A newly signed or newly migrated (acquired-platform) customer needs
onboarding -> Capillary's implementation team configures and integrates
the platform with the customer's point-of-sale, e-commerce and other
data sources (AR p.77) -> delivered as a one-time setup, recognised at a
point in time (AR Note 22.2, p.233) -> the same brand pays -> a one-off
implementation fee, distinct from the ongoing subscription.

**3. Campaign services on an agent basis, 0.9% of FY26 revenue.**
A customer wants a paid-media or rewards campaign run on its behalf ->
Capillary procures the media/reward inventory and executes the campaign
as agent, not principal -> delivered as a completed campaign -> the
customer pays -> revenue recognised net, agent-basis (AR p.190, Note
22.1).

**4. Acquired ARR (the M&A engine): Persuade, Brierley, Rewards+,
Kognitiv, SessionM.**
Capillary buys a sub-scale, agency-model-adjacent loyalty-tech company
with existing enterprise contracts, at 0.4x-3x its revenue (AR p.79-80)
-> it migrates the acquired company's customers onto the native
Capillary platform over a typical 3-4 year window (AR p.78) -> the
acquired customer keeps paying, now on Capillary's infrastructure ->
Capillary captures the spread as the book's gross margin rises from
roughly 30% as-acquired to the 65-70% Capillary posts at steady state
(AR p.79). This is not a fifth revenue stream on the P&L (it lands inside
streams 1-3); it is the mechanism by which the other three streams grow
faster than organic demand alone would produce.

### 1E. The chai-stall-uncle version
Think of Capillary as the company that runs the "punch card" for big
brands, but the modern digital version: every time you earn or redeem
points at a store, an airline or a bank, there is a good chance
Capillary's software is the ledger keeping score behind the scenes. The
big brand pays Capillary a subscription, like a shopkeeper paying rent
for a shop, not a one-time price for a shirt. Because the loyalty
"points bank" lives inside Capillary's system, it is a hassle for the
brand to rip it out and switch, the same way you would not want to
switch banks in the middle of a running relationship. Capillary also
buys smaller versions of itself, moves those brands' customers onto its
own shop, and pockets the difference between the smaller company's thin
margin and its own fatter one. The risk: a handful of big brands still
account for a large share of the till, so losing even one hurts more
than it would for a shop with a thousand small customers.

### 2D. Industry lifecycle stage
Mixed. The Zinnov-sourced TAM data (via UDRHP-I and Investor Presentation
May-2026, p.9-10) frames global loyalty management as a mature, still
largely agency-driven $18.2bn market, of which roughly $5.6bn is
agency-led and $2.4bn is a mid-market segment Capillary is actively
targeting for further M&A. Within that mature category, the AI-native
layer (aiRA) is early-stage: it is generating usage growth (9.2x
month-over-month cumulative activity at a flagship brand, November 2025
to April 2026, AR p.78) but no disclosed revenue line of its own.
Capillary's position: a product-recognised challenger consolidating a
fragmented, agency-dominated segment, while still mid-pack on scale and
profitability against its own financial peer set (3rd of 4 by market
cap, last of 4 on FY26 EBITDA margin, per B01 Gate 0).
