# Yasho Industries, business narrative (Phase 1)

One MNC contract carries the whole thesis

Yasho Industries makes specialty chemicals at Vapi and Pakhajan in Gujarat. Industrial Chemicals, meaning rubber chemicals, lubricant additives and stabilisers, is 89% of sales. Consumer Chemicals, meaning aroma, food additives and personal care intermediates, is the other 11%. FY26 revenue was about Rs 830 Cr, EBITDA margin 17.4%, and net margin only 3.0%. Revenue grew 18% a year over FY21 to FY26, but profit grew 3% a year over the same span.

The transition claim is a climb from commodity converter to a value added, spec driven supplier. The proof point is one thing: a 15 year, customer funded supply contract with an unnamed global MNC, worth about Rs 150 Cr a year from Q1 FY28. The emerging moat scan scored 17 out of a higher range, and rests almost entirely on this single pillar. Strip out the MNC deal and the moat case falls toward nothing.

Gate 0 returned a mechanical AVOID, core score 35 out of 100. The AVOID is not a quality verdict. It traces to the FY24 Pakhajan capex cycle, Rs 337 Cr spent in one year, which pushed FY26 net debt to EBITDA to 3.74x and interest cover to 1.61x, tripping a binding leverage deal breaker. Growth quality is separately weak on its own terms, with FY25 a near trough year.

Cash is the open wound. Operating cash flow swung from minus Rs 41 Cr in FY25 to plus Rs 151 Cr in FY26, but the recovery leaned on working capital release and a new customer advance liability, not clean earnings. Receivables are deteriorating: standalone trade receivables rose 37% against 20% revenue growth, and subsidiary receivables rose 79% against 16% sales growth to those subsidiaries. The MNC advance figure does not reconcile across three sources: Rs 29.52 Cr in the audited notes, Rs 51.4 Cr in the MD&A, Rs 98.12 Cr in the investor presentation.

Management credibility is a B. Seven of ten tracked promises were delivered, several beaten early, and the A minus rating upgrade was independently corroborated. Against that, the FY28 revenue target crept up every quarter from Rs 1,500 Cr to above Rs 1,600 Cr as margin confidence rose. Plant level utilisation was withheld under repeated questioning. A "we have shut Vapi" remark in Q1 FY27 sits unreconciled next to an earlier "Vapi 95 to 98%", which means the margin ramp may be a Pakhajan only story. The 24.2% Q1 FY27 EBITDA margin is one quarter of print against a multi year 17 to 19% band.

Peers cut the other way on the central number. Under the same war driven raw material inflation, NOCIL and Camlin Fine saw EBITDA margins compress to single digits, while Yasho reports margin expansion from 17.4% to 24.2%. No peer shows growth near the 30 to 40% guide; sector volume growth ran low single digit to low double digit. If Yasho's growth is real, it is share taking and capacity execution, not sector beta.

Market runway is GOOD. The addressed market is large, SAM is about Rs 22,700 Cr, and revenue headroom is roughly 27 times current sales. The catch is the pace: bottom up SOM implies a 22% three year and 19% five year revenue CAGR, both below the 30 to 40% guide and the FY28 target. Closing that gap depends on undisclosed new Pakhajan capacity ramping on schedule, which this phase cannot verify.
