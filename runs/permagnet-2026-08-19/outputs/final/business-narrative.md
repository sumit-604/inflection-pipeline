# Magnet maker funds two unproven growth bets

Permanent Magnets makes magnetic and electro mechanical components on a build to spec basis, mostly for electricity meters, autos, and current transformers. Meters are 40% of sales, autos 23%, current transformers 9%, a new specialty Alloys line 10%, other verticals 16%, and legacy gas meters 2%. FY26 standalone revenue was Rs 225 Cr, up 13%, with exports near 47% of sales. The company was incorporated in 1960 and the promoter family holds 58%.

The transition is a move out of mature meter and auto parts into three newer lines. Specialty Alloys grew from about 1% of sales two years ago to about 10% in FY26 after a furnace was commercialised. Latching relays are guided to start commercial ramp in H2 FY27. Rare earth NdFeB magnets sit inside the Quantum Magnetics subsidiary, with a 5,000 tonne target by FY31. Two of these three lines, relays and the rare earth magnets, are behind management's own original timelines.

Gate 0 scored AVERAGE, 57 of 160. The one real fundamental drag is growth quality. Over five years PAT compounded at 2.5% a year while revenue compounded at 14.9%. Return on capital fell from 31% to 16% on the earliest to latest comparison. Against that, the standalone operating story improved in FY26, with EBITDA margin rising from 14% to 17% and standalone PAT up 36% to Rs 20.7 Cr.

The cash side deteriorated at the group level. Consolidated operating cash flow fell 55% to Rs 17.6 Cr while consolidated PAT fell only 6% to Rs 15.1 Cr. Consolidated debt to equity rose from 0.17 times to 0.56 times. The driver is one subsidiary that drew a Rs 47.81 Cr foreign currency loan to fund pre revenue capex, then booked Rs 0.78 Cr of revenue and a Rs 5.62 Cr loss for the year. The parent stayed cash positive on its own. Standalone receivables are the second signal to watch, up 39% against 13% revenue growth, with days rising from 72 to 76, though 99% of the book is under six months old.

Credibility grades C, held down by the absence of any earnings call. Management is candid on misses, self disclosing the relay delay and naming China rare earth export controls as the reason the subsidiary made no revenue. But Q1 FY27 profit fell 14% standalone and 38% consolidated on 19% revenue growth, with no explanation on file because the results carry no commentary. Governance draws a CAUTION on three items: a same sector private entity controlled by the Managing Director that is not named in the related party list, an independent director who is cross membered with a family company, and Managing Director pay up 28% against 13.7% for employees. A new independent Chairman with a JSW Group background is the offset. A 2015 Bombay High Court winding up order over a Rs 0.13 Cr claim remains under interim stay and is cited as the going concern basis, and a Rs 22 Cr disputed excise interest contingency sits unresolved.

Peers support the story rather than fight it. Mishra Dhatu corroborates strengthening alloys demand. Salzer Electronics corroborates the smart metering runway, with only 65 million of 250 million RDSS meters installed as of April 2026. The rare earth and China concentration claims stay untested, because the assigned peer TD Power Systems shows no rare earth exposure in its own input basket and is a weak fit for that check. Across the set, none of PML's claims are contradicted, and the whole peer group is running debt and working capital funded capex at the same time.

The market runway is STRONG. Serviceable market is about Rs 5,574 Cr, current share is 3.4%, and revenue headroom is about 29 times. That implies a roughly 20% revenue CAGR to fill the serviceable slice over the medium term. The rare earth magnet and relay lines are excluded from this sizing as far dated optionality, so the runway rests on the existing meter, auto, and alloys demand rather than on the two bets that are behind schedule.
