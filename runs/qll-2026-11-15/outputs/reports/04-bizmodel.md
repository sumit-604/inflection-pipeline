## SECTION 1: THE BUSINESS MODEL IN PLAIN ENGLISH

### 1A One line
QLL runs 16 accredited testing labs (10 owned, 6 government labs run on PPP contracts) that test cars, minerals, food, drugs and electronics for businesses, and it grew from 4x revenue in two years mostly by buying other labs (AR26 p.70; Deck pp.4, 19, 41).

### 1E The chai-stall-uncle version
Think of a company that rents out exam halls and examiners, but for products. A carmaker sends a battery. QLL shakes it, heats it and burns it, then signs a paper that says it passed. QLL gets paid per test, once the paper is out. The examiners are costly and need training, so people cost most. The exam halls (labs) are costly too, so QLL borrows money to build or buy new ones. In FY26 it bought a lot of halls fast, so it now has more bills to pay than cash coming in, and the owners hope the halls fill up with test-takers soon (Deck pp.19, 24, 41; B02, B03).

### 2D Industry lifecycle
Indian TIC is early-growth for outsourced independent labs and fragmented, driven by regulation and export needs (AR26 p.70; Prosp pp.136-137). The prospectus market data are thin: Straits Research quotes USD 26 million in 2021 rising to 47 million by 2030 at 6.9% CAGR, a figure too small for the business in question (QLL alone is Rs 125 Cr) and unusable as market size. Treat it as NOT USABLE. QLL sits in the roll-up and build phase: 6 cumulative acquisitions and 16 labs by 2026 (Deck p.41), now in an integration and utilisation phase (AR26 p.73).

Primary: forward P/E. Secondary: EV/EBITDA. Tertiary: reverse DCF. Cycle stage that matters: capex and utilisation cycle (new labs filling), plus the mining and auto cycles on the largest verticals.

**Sector cap row check.** Intake chose "Consulting / Engineering services" 25x (B00). My business-model read supports that row.
