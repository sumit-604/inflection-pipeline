# ESCONET business narrative (phase 1, run 2026-10-05)

Phase 1 lite. Stages 10 and 11 have not run, so this file carries no valuation, entry zone, decision or destination PE. Figures in ₹ Cr unless stated; company filings print ₹ Lakhs (100 Lakhs = ₹1 Cr).

## BUSINESS UNDERSTANDING NARRATIVE

Esconet buys servers, storage, network gear and NVIDIA GPU systems from distributors and OEMs, then configures, installs and supports them at the client site. It also assembles servers under its own HexaData brand, including GPU systems, and has sold more than 6,000 HexaData units since 2018. Product sales were 97.6% of FY26 consolidated revenue of ₹354 Cr and service charges were 2.4%, and no filing, deck or call splits HexaData from traded hardware. Beside the core sit ZeaCloud, a cloud subsidiary with ₹5.3 Cr of FY26 turnover, Fluidech, a 70% owned cybersecurity firm with ₹5.4 Cr, and a Singapore trading arm with about 14% of revenue at a 0.5% net margin. A buyer needs a vendor who delivers working compute and sits on the tender lists, but many resellers clear that bar. The customers are government bodies and PSUs such as NIC, ONGC, Indian Oil and C-DAC, enterprises such as AB InBev, universities such as Amity, and the Indian Army on a seven year secure cloud engagement. Government was 36% of FY23 revenue and the top ten customers were 54%, and the run found no FY26 split. These buyers use tenders that go to the lowest qualified bid, place purchase orders with no long contracts, pay slowly and ask for bank guarantees. Present demand comes from AI compute buying and government digitisation, and India data centre systems spend rose 29% in 2025. The run ties that demand to three downstream signals: IndiaAI Mission GPU tenders, the ₹25.74 Cr C-DAC order with other GeM awards, and NVIDIA data centre results with the memory price trend. Part of the rupee demand is price, because memory prices rose two to three times without adding units. Forward growth rests on the 20,000 GPUs announced under IndiaAI Mission 2.0, a MeitY cloud empanelment for ZeaCloud, and NCIIPC and CERT-In directives for Fluidech, each visible in a public notice. The quarterly results and order books of Netweb, Rashi Peripherals and Orient Technologies show the same buyers' demand before Esconet reports. The serviceable market of Indian enterprise and government servers outside hyperscale is about ₹21,139 Cr, growing near 10% a year, and Esconet holds about 1.4% of it. No business line shows a moat in the margin, and traded hardware has none, since its gross margin fell from 15.2% to 13.2% when component prices rose in FY26. HexaData has weak signs, a Red Hat certified server and a launched backup appliance, but R&D is under 2% of revenue and its NVIDIA Elite tier is shared with peers. ZeaCloud has flat revenue, a loss and no empanelment, and Fluidech rests on an NCIIPC accreditation the run did not see on file. The emerging moat scan scored 5.5 with no category at Moderate, and Category 21 (talent asymmetry) and Category 22 (cannibalization barrier) both scored zero.

## Thin margin integrator, one quarter of proof

Esconet is a Delhi IT hardware integrator that listed on the NSE SME board on 23 February 2024 with a ₹28 Cr fresh issue. FY26 consolidated revenue grew 54% to ₹354 Cr while profit after tax fell 23% to ₹6.2 Cr. Revenue has compounded at 52% a year from FY21 to FY26, so the growth is real. The profit has not followed it, and the cash has gone the other way.

The claimed change is a shift from traded boxes toward HexaData AI servers, cloud and cybersecurity. Q1 FY27 is the first print that shows it. Standalone gross margin rose to 29.2% from 13.7% for FY26, and consolidated EBITDA excluding other income rose to 7.0% from 2.5%. Standalone Q1 profit of ₹6.8 Cr beat the whole of FY26 standalone profit of ₹6.5 Cr. Three readings explain it: a better HexaData mix, a one time gain on stock bought before prices rose, or fixed price contracts repricing as at Orient. The mix reading has no filed support. At the margins management gave in June 2025, HexaData would need about 69% of sales to reach 29%. The MD said hardware price moves and inventory "contributed to some extent" but would not size them. If the margin fell back to the FY26 level, the quarter would lose ₹9.4 Cr of gross profit, about all of its ₹9.4 Cr standalone pre tax profit. The separating observation is the Q2 FY27 gross margin after stock is replaced at current cost, plus HexaData revenue and margin by line.

The backward record scores AVERAGE: a core score of 60 out of 100, held there by cash conversion. Return on capital fell from 61% in FY23, on only ₹9.6 Cr of capital, to 12% in FY26. Debt to equity of 0.17 and interest cover of 8.3 times are sound. Debt still rose from ₹0.5 Cr to ₹13.2 Cr in FY26, and the June 2026 deck says an ₹11.9 Cr loan was repaid in April 2026. Accounting quality scores 4 out of 10. That is a disclosure and consolidation finding, not a fraud finding, and both audit opinions are unmodified.

Cash is the weak point. Over six years operating cash flow added up to minus ₹8 Cr against ₹22 Cr of profit. FY26 operating cash flow was minus ₹8.8 Cr as inventory rose from ₹19 Cr to ₹52 Cr. About ₹24 Cr of that stock sits outside the parent and does not reconcile in the notes; the Singapore arm is the likely holder. Receivables older than six months rose from 4% to 20% of the parent's book in two years, with no provision. The parent's positive cash flow came from cashing ₹17.5 Cr of fixed deposits. The results filing and the annual report print FY26 balance sheets that differ by ₹24.6 Cr, so debtor days read 20 on one and 46 on the other. CRISIL says the ₹7.5 Cr working capital limit is unused and the company runs on its own accruals. The run cannot yet call the drain growth or structure, because no Q1 balance sheet or cash flow was filed.

Management has missed every number it put on a forecast. In June 2025 it guided ZeaCloud revenue up 50% to 60%, Fluidech at ₹15 to 20 Cr with 25% to 30% margins, and staff cost up about 30%. ZeaCloud grew 1.8% and made a loss, Fluidech made ₹5.4 Cr at a loss, and staff cost rose 43% on a like for like basis. The credibility grade is C at its lower edge, and the red flag audit puts it at the C and D boundary. The MD was candid on the August 2026 call about flat ZeaCloud and price help. Press releases and decks were not: they claimed "strengthening operational cash flows" and "privileged allocation of scarce GPUs", which the filings do not support. Governance carries more weight than the forecasts. Fluidech was bought from an independent director who owned 99% of it, and a tax summons on the IPO and use of proceeds is missing from the annual report. Promoter pay doubled while pre tax profit fell 3%. The promoter group held 89.2% before the IPO, 64.9% after it, 60.2% in June 2026, and about 59.2% on the AGM record date. There is no pledge and no SEBI or court record, so the promoter verdict is CAUTION, not CONCERN.

Peers complicate the story but formally contradict nothing. Netweb denies any memory price gain in its margin. Orient's EBITDA rebound from 3.2% to 7.6% came from fixed price contracts that ended on 31 March 2026. Rashi allows a stock gain "to some extent" and will not size it. Netweb also says its large AI orders earn 150 to 200 basis points less at the pre tax line, which cuts against a richer AI mix. In the latest quarter Netweb grew 172% and Rashi 62% year on year, while Orient, the closest integrator, shrank 5%. Esconet's standalone print looks like Orient's. Netweb and Orient disclose order books and cash flow; Esconet does neither.

The market is not the limit. The serviceable market is about 70 times Esconet's Indian revenue, and the run rates the runway STRONG. Three published server market sizes differ 3.9 times and most come from aggregators, so the size carries medium confidence at best. Working capital binds first: in FY26 each ₹100 of added revenue tied up ₹26 of inventory. FY27 guidance of ₹370 to 400 Cr implies 4% to 13% growth. Q1 annualises to ₹465 Cr, but Singapore trading made 46% of Q1 revenue at a 0.56% net margin. Standalone Q1 revenue sat 18% below the FY26 quarterly average.
