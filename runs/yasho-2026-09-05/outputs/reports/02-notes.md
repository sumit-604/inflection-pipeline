PATTERN-LEVEL SYNTHESIS (combining items each pass raised individually, now read together):

- CLUSTER 1, subsidiary cash-conversion doubt: Pass 1's related-party receivables red flag
  (+78.7% vs +15.9% sales) and Pass 2's consolidation-adjustment finding (Note 46 CON: -66.15% of
  combined profit erased in FY25, only -6.15% in FY26, opposite direction to the receivables
  build) are the SAME underlying phenomenon read from two different notes. Neither note
  cross-references the other. Read together, they raise a single, sharper question: how much of
  the profit Yasho books on "sales to subsidiaries" is real, remitted cash, versus an unrealised
  or elimination-pending construct. This is now the single most important accounting-quality
  question in the file, ranking above its Pass 1 framing as a receivables-ageing issue alone.
- CLUSTER 2, liquidity cushion into a capex ramp: Pass 2's DSCR of 1.04x, the 43.2% "on demand"
  share of the financial-liability book (Note 41E), and Pass 1's Rs 31.7-52.7 cr quarterly
  drawing-power variance (Note 46 SA) describe the SAME liquidity structure from three angles: a
  thin debt-service cushion, sitting mostly in callable facilities, on top of a lender-monitoring
  process the company's own books have diverged from every quarter this year. Individually each
  item carries a benign company explanation (FX timing, capacity monetisation, comfortable gearing
  band). Together, they describe a capital structure with limited absorptive capacity if FY27
  earnings from the Pakhajan/MNC ramp disappoint or slip past Q4 FY27.
- CLUSTER 3, documentation-quality pattern, not isolated defects: the DSO-explanation
  contradiction (Pass 1), the identical boilerplate reused across 5 of 11 Note 45 ratio rows
  (Pass 2), and the MSME interest internal inconsistency between accrued and "due and payable"
  fields (Pass 2) are not three separate small issues; they are one recurring pattern of
  low-care disclosure completion that lowers confidence in any single unverified number in the
  notes, including the ones that read cleanly.

No wholly new dollar-figure finding emerged from this pattern read beyond the three clusters
above; all three clusters are genuine new synthesis, not restatements of Pass 1/2 items, so this
pass is not empty, but no new NOTE NUMBER required first-time extraction.

D. FIVE QUESTIONS FOR MANAGEMENT

1. Reconcile the Rs 98.12 cr customer advances / Rs 70.89 cr vendor advances carried in prior
   verification records against the audited AR's Rs 29.52 cr (of which Rs 27.45 cr is explicitly
   tied to Note 48) / Rs 25.84 cr. Is the larger figure from a later quarter, a different
   entity-level cut, or an estimate since revised down?
2. What specifically comprises "Adjustments arising out of consolidation" in Note 46 (CON), and
   why did the P&L-side adjustment shrink from -Rs 403.86 lakh (FY25, 66% of combined profit) to
   -Rs 155.34 lakh (FY26, 6%) while intercompany trade receivables grew 78.7% in the same year?
3. Trade payables for expenses rose 90.1% and MSME overdue principal quadrupled in a year secured
   borrowings fell 12.9% — is working capital being funded by stretching vendor terms rather than
   bank debt, and is that a deliberate treasury choice?
4. What is the contractual interest rate on the Rs 46.50 cr of director loans, and what are the
   minimum-supply-commitment and penalty terms the auditor's Key Audit Matter references but Note
   48's own text never states?
5. What shelters FY26's current-year income tax to nil despite Rs 28.99 cr of profit before tax,
   given no brought-forward-loss schedule, unabsorbed-depreciation carry-forward, or MAT-credit
   entitlement is disclosed anywhere in the notes, and for how many more years does that shelter run?

  matter.
- Undisclosed risk indicator: the auditor's KAM references minimum-supply-commitment and penalty
  terms on the MNC contract that Note 48's own substantive text never states or quantifies
  (Finding 1) — a genuine gap between what the auditor evaluated and what the note discloses.
- No fraud, no wilful-defaulter status, no benami findings, no going-concern qualification: CARO
  and the audit opinion are clean on all classic red-flag categories; the concerns above sit inside
  a formally clean audit, not outside it.
