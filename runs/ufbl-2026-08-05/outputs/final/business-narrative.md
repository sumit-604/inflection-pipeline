Lease optics mask a recovering restaurant chain

United Foodbrands, the erstwhile Barbeque Nation, runs 262 casual dining restaurants at FY26 close and 266 by Q1 FY27, across three lines: Barbeque Nation India, an International arm in the GCC and Southeast Asia, and Premium CDR under Toscano and Salt. Revenue was about Rs 1,233 Cr in FY25. Customers pay at the table by cash or card, so the model runs on negative working capital. The stores sit on long leases, which makes the balance sheet look heavy.

SSSG turned. Same store sales contracted 3.8% in FY25 and 3.4% in Q1 FY26, then recovered to plus 8.2% in Q3 FY26, plus 14.4% in Q4 FY26, and plus 28.7% in Q1 FY27. Consolidated PAT attributable to owners turned positive at plus Rs 3.09 Cr in Q1 FY27 after a full year FY26 loss of Rs 59 Cr. Own digital channel share of dine in rose from 33.6% to 65.1% over the same window.

Gate 0 scores AVOID on a Core of 19 out of 100, and most of that AVOID is mechanical. The screener Borrowings line of Rs 885 Cr bundles Rs 751 Cr of Ind AS 116 lease liabilities into debt. Actual interest bearing debt is only about Rs 70 Cr. Cumulative operating cash flow over FY17 to FY26 was plus Rs 1,477 Cr against cumulative PAT of minus Rs 267 Cr, so the losses are lease and depreciation driven, not an operating collapse, and EBITDA margin rose year on year. Separately, the forward moat scan found nothing meaningful and scored NONE, which does not pre judge the operating recovery.

Cash reads as two pictures. Consolidated operating cash is strong on the negative working capital model, with CFO of about Rs 223 Cr in FY26. Standalone liquidity tightened at the parent: cash fell from Rs 150 mn to Rs 22 mn, the company drew its first ever working capital loan of Rs 80 mn, the current ratio fell to 0.46, and gearing rose at both levels. That squeeze tracks the parent funding store rollout capex of about Rs 125 Cr in FY26 and support to subsidiaries, not a cash conversion failure. ICRA rates liquidity adequate with no debt funded capex planned.

Management earns a B on credibility. It delivered 5 of 9 tracked promises, including the net debt ceiling, the H2 EBITDA margin, the International and Premium CDR store targets, and the SSSG recovery itself, which it exceeded. Against that, the 67 to 68% gross margin band was reiterated across four calls and missed at 65.5% for FY26, and store count guidance slipped from about 270 to 275 down to 262. Accounting quality is 4 out of 10: the auditor states it cannot confirm audit trail integrity of books held on third party hosted software, while still issuing an unmodified opinion; a MENA subsidiary called fully impaired still carries Rs 250 mn of net value; four note tie out inconsistencies appear in one cycle; and discount rates were cut across every CGU with a new 12x terminal floor in the same year the largest subsidiary swung to loss.

Promoter posture is CAUTION. The Dhanani and Sayaji group controls about 34.6%, the managing director draws zero salary, pledge is near zero, and next generation family members bought on market. Against that, the 2017 SEBI order that kept the IPO in abeyance was never explained, and the nominally independent chairman plus two promoter directors sit on the boards of Sayaji Hotels, which is both an RPT counterparty for rent and purchases and, through Sayaji Housekeeping, the single largest shareholder at 29.69%.

Peers complicate the recovery read. The SSSG turn came partly from a shared GST cut tailwind that RBA, Westlife and Speciality all cited in H2 CY2025. UFBL's claim of unchanged competitive intensity is contradicted by those peers and by UFBL's own Q1 FY26 call, where management volunteered competitor price drops and South India pricing pressure. UFBL's gross margin compressed to 65.5% from 68.5% while peer gross margins expanded, and its back end cost step from 6.1% to 7.1% is company specific, since peer overhead fell over the same period.

Market runway is large but execution bound. The realizable SOM points to revenue CAGR of about 18 to 21%, an order of magnitude below the theoretical 87.8x headroom, because store rollout pace and 112% FY25 attrition are the binding constraints, not market size. Management framed the opportunity against a Rs 6.6 lakh Cr total food services number, 4.55x the Rs 1.45 lakh Cr CDR specific TAM its own filing discloses.
