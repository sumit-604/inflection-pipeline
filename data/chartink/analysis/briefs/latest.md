## The picture in one paragraph

Almost two stocks in three now stand above their 20 day line, the most since early August. The count above the 200 day line rose to 977 and passed the mid October repair high. ## Where the market stands

Each stock in the record has three trend lines: 20 days, 50 days and 200 days. A stock above a line is in an uptrend on that clock. On Thursday 1,444 stocks stood above the short line. That is almost two in three. 1,016 stood above the middle line, a little less than half. 977 stood above the long line, a little more than four in ten. Most stocks still sit in long downtrends. But the short trend clearly belongs to the buyers, and the middle trend is close to even.

The year gives these counts their scale. On 23 March came the spring low, and on 1 April a great thrust day, when 1,125 stocks rose hard. The long count then climbed for four months to 1,367 on 7 August. It fell to about 840 on 8 October. A mid October repair lifted it to 943, and then the floor broke. On 23 October only 582 stocks held the long line. Thursday's 977 sits about 395 stocks above that low and above the mid October repair high. The last time the long count stood this high was 6 October.

The short count has climbed for ten sessions. On 23 October only 177 stocks held the 20 day line, the low of the autumn. On Tuesday 877 did. On Wednesday 1,215 did. On Thursday the count rose by about 230 more, to 1,444. The last time it stood this high was 5 August, just before the summer peak. The middle count, at 1,016, is the best since 23 September. In under two weeks the market has gone from almost no stock in a short uptrend to almost two in three.

Thursday was wide, but less wide than Wednesday. 233 stocks rose four and a half percent or more, against 309 the day before. 55 fell that hard. Risers still beat fallers by more than four to one. The record shows the last pair of days this wide in April 2025. About four stocks in ten traded four percent above Wednesday's close at some point, the most of the past month. ## Thirty points

### For the trader

**1. The market strung two wide days together.** 309 stocks rose hard on Wednesday and 233 on Thursday. The record shows the last such pair in April 2025. One wide day can be a burst. Two in a row look more like a change in who is in charge.

**2. Almost two stocks in three hold the 20 day line.** 1,444 stocks stand above it, the most since 5 August. Ten sessions ago only 177 did. A short count this high often marks the start of a rally, and it can also mark short term exhaustion. A pullback that keeps the count above 1,000 would say the first.

## What would change this read

The read is an early uptrend on the short and middle clocks. The long clock has now joined, led by the large companies, on money that improved but has not matched the sellers. It turns worse if a day of 300 or more hard fallers returns and the next session fails to answer it. A short count back under about 1,000 would say the two wide days were a single burst. A long count back under about 870, Wednesday's level, would undo Thursday's gain. A long count under about 580, the 23 October low, would erase the bounce. New yearly lows back above about 100 would say sellers have returned. A VIX close above about 18 would say fear has taken charge.

The cautious read fails if the money arrives and the fear gauge falls. The first test is a wide up day on turnover above about 610 crore. Thursday reached about 570. The second test is the long count above about 1,000. Thursday reached 977. The third test is a VIX close under 15. Thursday closed near 15.6, and it rose. The fourth test is gains held into the close, with about half of the stocks that trade four percent up keeping it. Thursday held fewer than four in ten. Two of these in the same week, with the short count holding above 1,200, would change the read from an early uptrend to a confirmed one.
