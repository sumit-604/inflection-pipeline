## 3. THE CATALYST STORY IN PLAIN WORDS

What could make each transition fire, what the evidence is, what confirms it, and what kills it.

Revenue is the strongest story. The company has already spent the money and built the plants. PVC capacity went from 44,000 to 69,000 tonnes, HFFR doubled to 10,000 tonnes, and a new 48,000 tonne XLPE plant at Bhiwadi started up at the end of April 2026. That is documented, commissioned capacity, not a promise. On top of it sits export momentum of about 30% in FY26 and a stated FY27 target of roughly 15% more volume. What confirms it: the Q1 and Q2 FY27 volume prints tracking toward 231,000 tonnes. What kills it: utilization on the new lines staying stuck, the way PVC and HFFR sat near 49% through FY26.

Margin is a hold-the-line story, not an expansion story. The lever is the mix shift into higher value HFFR and XLPE grades and operating leverage as the new lines fill. Management itself guides the margin flat at about 11% for FY27 and only sees 10.5 to 11.5% from FY28. What confirms an upside surprise: EBITDA per tonne breaking above Rs 16,000 for two quarters. What kills it: a fresh raw material spike that the cost plus model cannot pass through fast enough, which is exactly what happened in the second half of FY26.

Cash conversion is the whole call. The bull view is simple and mechanical: the FY26 cash drag is a one time inventory build tied to filling the new lines, and as that inventory ships, cash comes back. The bear view is equally simple: the finished goods are piling up because they are not selling through, and the receivables story is murkier than it looks because the bad debt allowance fell by more than the disclosed write-offs explain. There is no documented working-capital program, no factoring facility, no debtor-day target that management has hit. What confirms growth-induced: working capital days falling back toward the mid 70s as volume rises. What kills the benign read: working capital days above 90 on flat volume.

ROCE is a byproduct of the other three plus the capex cadence. If revenue accelerates, margins hold and cash normalizes, ROCE re-expands from the current 29% as the deployed capacity earns. The counter is the BESS build and further XLPE capex, which keep adding capital that will not earn for a year or two. What confirms recovery: ROCE printing above 31% while capex stays on plan. What kills it: a second capex cycle layering on before the first one earns.

BESS is optionality, not a transition. It is a brand new assembly business with zero operating history, first revenue not due until the second half of FY27, and guidance that has already been cut twice in two quarters (FY27 revenue from Rs 300 to 500 Cr down to Rs 200 to 250 Cr, Phase 1 capex up from Rs 150 Cr to about Rs 200 Cr). It is a real call option on the energy storage build, anchored to a genuine national target, but it is not evidence of a firing transition today. Treat it as a separate SOTP slice in Phase 3, valued on revenue or capacity, not blended into the core multiple.
