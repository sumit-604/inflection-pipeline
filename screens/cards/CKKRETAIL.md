## Business Understanding Narrative

**What does it do, and how does it make money?** The corpus does not say. No
annual report, result, presentation or transcript is held (MANIFEST.md, Known
gaps). The cost lines describe the shape. FY2026 revenue was Rs 270.19 crore
(DS line 86). Materials consumed were Rs 248.29 crore, 91.9% of revenue
(computed, DS lines 87, 88). Employee cost was Rs 1.45 crore and power was
Rs 0.03 crore (DS lines 89, 91). Net block was Rs 0.98 crore (DS line 119).
That is a buy-and-resell book, not a factory and not a staffed store chain. The
name says retail. The numbers say trading or distribution. The company earns a
thin spread: operating margin 4.6% in FY2026 (computed).

**What is changing?** On the numbers, the capital base. Net worth went from
Rs 41.91 crore to Rs 117.32 crore in FY2026 (DS lines 114, 115). Share count
went from 74.8 lakh to 1.94 crore (DS line 127). Financing inflow was
Rs 65.45 crore (DS line 134). That reads as a fresh equity issue, probably the
listing. The corpus holds no offer document, so the purpose is NOT FOUND.

**Why now?** Nothing in the corpus marks an inflection. FY2026 revenue fell
10.2% and profit after tax fell 32.1% (computed, DS lines 86, 99). The price
moved instead. The Data Sheet carries Rs 124.80 at 31 March 2026 and Rs 280.95
now (DS lines 136, 82). That is a 125% rise on falling earnings.

**What must be true for the thesis to work?** No thesis is stated anywhere in
the corpus. For the price to make sense, the Rs 65 crore raise must fund a
business that earns far more than Rs 10.53 crore. The trailing multiple is
51.7x (computed, Bull AI market cap over DS line 99). A trader with a 4.6% margin
sits on the R1 rung, near 12x.

**What breaks it?** Cash. Six reported years produced Rs 45.47 crore of profit
after tax and minus Rs 12.83 crore of operating cash flow (computed, DS lines
99, 132). Receivables more than doubled in FY2026 while revenue fell.

## 12. Verdict card

**PASS.**

The corpus shows a thin-spread trader whose last year shrank, whose cash
conversion is poor, and whose price more than doubled after the year closed.
No document states a transition. The multiple already pays for a climb that
nobody has claimed.

The corpus is very thin, and that limits the verdict. A pass on a data sheet
alone is a pass on the evidence held, not a finding that the business is bad.

**Load-bearing facts to verify first, if it is ever reopened**
1. The H1 FY2027 result (April to September 2026) from NSE. Revenue and PAT
   must have turned up sharply to explain the price.
2. What the company sells, to whom, and through which channel. The offer
   document or the FY2026 annual report.
3. The stated use of the roughly Rs 65 crore raise.
4. Promoter holding, pledge and related-party purchases or sales.
5. The reason for the FY2026 receivable build and the 21.0% tax rate.

**What would change the view**
- A filed half-year with PAT at an annualised rate above Rs 25 crore and
  positive operating cash flow.
- A deployed use of proceeds that moves the business off a resale spread, such
  as an owned brand or a manufactured product.
- Evidence that the FY2026 decline was one lost customer or one channel, since
  replaced.

No Role 1 valuation was run and no target price is set. This verdict decides
only whether the name earns a full `/step1`. It is not a position.
