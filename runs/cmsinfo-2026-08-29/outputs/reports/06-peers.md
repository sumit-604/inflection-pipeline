# Stage 6 — Peer Concall Verification: CMS Info Systems (CMSINFO)
Run date: 2026-08-29 | Protocol v1.1 | Model: claude-sonnet-5

Corpus: 24 peer transcripts confirmed present (RADIANTCMS x4, SIS x4, AGSTRA x4,
QUESS x4, UDS x4, AURIONPRO x4). All read in full or grep-swept for the priority
terms (ATM, currency/indent/CATMi/fulfilment, AGS, cash management/logistics,
minimum wage/labour code, DSO/receivable/debtor, transaction fee/interchange,
CMS/CMS Info) before claim verification.

Structural note carried into every verdict below: of the six peers, only
RADIANTCMS and SIS touch the physical cash/ATM value chain at all, and neither
is a true like-for-like. RADIANTCMS explicitly disclaims being in the ATM
segment ("We are not in the ATM segment at all," Q2 FY26 call) — its core
business is reverse logistics (retail pickup to bank), not forward logistics
(bank to ATM replenishment), the exact link where CMS's Q1 FY27 shock is
claimed. SIS's Cash Services subsidiary is pre-IPO; management explicitly
declines to disclose its financials or operating metrics on every call in the
window ("I cannot share the financial performance details," Aug-2026 call).
AGSTRA has no calls after Jul-2024 (CIRP filed 25-Aug-2025); its four
transcripts are FY24 vintage, a different economic era, useful only as
historical counterfactual. QUESS, UDS and AURIONPRO have no cash/ATM segment
at all. This means the peer set can speak with real authority to the
sector-wide wage/labour-code and DSO questions, but is structurally unable to
verify or contradict the ATM-specific currency-supply, fulfilment-percentage,
transaction-fee and AGS-share-capture claims — a finding in itself, not a
research failure.

## PART 1: CLAIM-BY-CLAIM VERIFICATION

### 1. Currency-supply shortfall ~30% (fulfilment 70%→80-85%), Q1 FY27
**Verdict: UNVERIFIABLE**
Peer evidence: None. RADIANTCMS's four calls (Nov-2025 through Aug-2026)
never mention a currency-supply or indent shortfall of any kind — consistent
with its reverse-logistics model sitting one step removed from bank-to-ATM
replenishment. SIS's Cash Services financials and operating commentary are
undisclosed across all four calls in the window. AGSTRA has no calls in the
relevant period.
Peers silent: RADIANTCMS, SIS (structurally unable to speak), AGSTRA (no
current-period data).
Net read: The three peers B05 named cannot test this claim because none of
them discloses forward cash-logistics operations in this window. This
narrows, not widens, independent confidence in LBF-2; it remains a
management-only assertion.

### 2. ATM cash-fulfilment % matching CATMi's ~57% (Apr-2026 national low)
**Verdict: UNVERIFIABLE**
Same structural gap as above. Neither RADIANTCMS nor SIS discloses an
ATM-fulfilment metric at any point in the four-quarter window.
(RADIANTCMS-Concall_Aug_2026, p.2-4/16; SIS-Concall_Aug_2026, p.1-3/11 — both

### 3. Transaction-fee pricing model "dead" / RFP cleared ~Rs25/txn vs Rs19 interchange
**Verdict: UNVERIFIABLE**
AURIONPRO's "cash management" references (e.g. iCashpro mandate, CSB Bank

### 4a. Minimum-wage increases 6-60%, sector-wide
**Verdict: VERIFIED**
- SIS (Q1 FY27, Aug-2026 call): "States like Haryana, Uttar Pradesh,
  Karnataka, Telangana, Uttarakhand… have passed through minimum wage hikes
  ranging from 20% to 47%," against a "sub-5%" post-COVID annual baseline.
  Karnataka "in excess of 20%," Haryana "40% plus," UP "23%, 24%."
  (SIS-Concall_Aug_2026, p.4-5/11, Rituraj Sinha.)
- RADIANTCMS (Q1 FY27, Aug-2026 call): "sharp increase in minimum wages in
  few states affected our margins," plus "shortage of licensed gunmen in
  several southern states was driving higher manpower costs."
  (RADIANTCMS-Concall_Aug_2026, p.2-6/16, Alexander David / Venkataramanan.)
Two independent peers, same quarter, corroborate both direction and rough
magnitude (SIS's 20-47% range sits inside CMS's claimed 6-60% band).
Net read: this is real, sector-wide, and contemporaneous — not a CMS excuse
dressed as an external event.

### 4b. Fuel cost increase ~8%, sector-wide
**Verdict: PARTIALLY VERIFIED**
RADIANTCMS (Q4 FY26, Jun-2026 call): "the fuel crisis and the consequent cost
of fuel… our cash logistics association or the currency cycle association
has jointly now approached all our banks to relook at how best they can
accommodate this." (RADIANTCMS-Concall_Jun_2026, p.13/16, Col Devasahayam.)
Direction confirmed, industry-association-level response confirmed; no peer
gives a specific percentage, so magnitude is unconfirmed. SIS does not
discuss fuel (guard/FM labour cost structure, not vehicle-heavy).

### 4c. Has any peer secured repricing ahead of CMS?
**Verdict: PARTIALLY VERIFIED (in-lockstep, not ahead)**
RADIANTCMS: "IBA has formed a committee for looking into the price revision
and promised to revert within a very short period of time. And we are
expecting these negotiations to get completed in Q2." (Aug-2026 call, p.7/16,
Muthuraman Natarajan.) This is the SAME mechanism (an IBA committee) and the
SAME timeline (end Q2 FY27) CMS itself cites in its own guidance. No peer
has actually completed repricing yet — RADIANTCMS still awaiting Q2
completion, SIS still working state-by-state wage-hike pass-through. So the
claim is not that a peer got there first; it is that CMS's promised
resolution path is identical to, and moving in lockstep with, its direct
listed peer's. That is a meaningfully different (weaker but still
corroborating) finding than "verified ahead of CMS."

### 5. ATM count trend (growth/decline, on/off-site mix)
**Verdict: UNVERIFIABLE (current period); informative historical counterfactual only**
RADIANTCMS has no ATM count (not in the segment). SIS Cash undisclosed.
AGSTRA (historical, FY24-25): ATM count declined "because a lot of
consolidation has been done across in the industry by public sector banks…

(26,000 ATMs referenced) rolled out. (AGSTRA-Concall_Nov_2024, p.~9/mid,
Ravi Goyal-line response to Naitik Mutha.) This shows ATM count decline in
this sector has historically been driven by bank-side consolidation
decisions, not by a currency-supply shock — a different causal mechanism
than CMS's stalled-74-75k narrative, and two years removed from the current
period. Cannot confirm or deny CMS's specific 2026 stall.

### 6. AGS-vacated ~12% share: do peer capture claims sum to <100%?
**Verdict: UNVERIFIABLE (trivially <100%, but not a real test)**
None of RADIANTCMS, SIS or UDS claims any AGS-share capture. RADIANTCMS
explicitly disclaims competing with AGS at all: "it's not a peer. We don't…
have any line of business which is common with AGS. They are more in the ATM

disclosed capture across the peer set = 0%, which sanity-checks trivially
but does not test whether CMS's own (also unquantified) capture claim is
plausible; it only confirms none of the LISTED peer set is a rival claimant.

### 7. DSO/receivables deterioration — industry-wide or CMS-specific?
**Verdict: CONTRADICTED**
- RADIANTCMS: debtors "reducing to 65 days' revenue in June 26, down from 70
  days' revenue in March 2026." (RADIANTCMS-Concall_Aug_2026, p.6/16,
  Venkataramanan.)
- SIS: DSO "69… down by 2 days… to 67" (Q3 FY26, Feb-2026 call, p.4/15);
  "one of the best DSO days, which is 63 days, down by 4 days" (Q4 FY26,
  May-2026 call, p.2-3/16); "DSO stands at 66 days. These are up by 3 days,
  but that's not at all surprising because Q4 is always the best… and Q1
  obviously dips" (Q1 FY27, Aug-2026 call, p.3/11).
Both direct listed peers show DSO IMPROVING or seasonally stable in the same
Mar-Jun 2026 window that CMS's DSO is reported (per company memory G-1) to
have worsened 116→126 days. This directly contradicts an "industry-wide
collections stress" framing and points toward a CMS-specific cause.
AGSTRA's historical pattern adds weight to why this matters: its own
persistent, unresolved receivables deterioration and repeated ECL
write-offs (multiple quarters, INR100m+ each) preceded its 25-Aug-2025 CIRP
filing — in this specific sector, chronic DSO deterioration has been a
leading indicator of distress, not noise. (AGSTRA-Concall_Jul_2024, p.mid,
Amit / Saurabh Lal exchange on "high receivable days.")
Net read: this is the single most consequential finding in this stage. It
does not prove CMS's DSO problem is terminal, but it removes the easiest
excuse (industry-wide pressure) and raises, rather than lowers, the priority
of B05's DSO flag and company memory Gap G-1.

## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

**2A Demand environment.** Currency in circulation growth is independently
confirmed (RADIANTCMS cites ~8% YoY as of Aug-2025 and ~Rs42 lakh crore as of
Aug-2026 — consistent with the company-memory Rs42.56 lakh crore figure).
But the peer with the clearest read on cash-COLLECTION volumes (RADIANTCMS)
reports flat-to-muted cash volume for multiple consecutive years despite
that macro tailwind, attributing it to digitization at Tier-1/Tier-2 and
banks de-emphasising RCM outsourcing — "the throughput is not growing as
what it was earlier because of… digitization" (Q3 FY26 call, p.5-6/13, Col
Devasahayam). This complicates a simple "cash volume growth = demand
tailwind" reading of CMS's own thesis: at least one peer with the identical
macro tailwind has not converted it into volume growth.

**2B Pricing and input costs.** Wage/statutory cost inflation from the new
Labour Codes is confirmed sector-wide well beyond the two direct cash-
logistics peers — QUESS took a one-time Rs176cr revenue/cost pass-through
(May-2026 and Feb-2026 calls) and SIS took a Rs290cr exceptional gratuity
charge (Feb-2026 call) — both far larger absolute events than CMS's own
guided drag, underlining that this is a structural, national-scale change,
not a CMS-specific problem. But the margin PATH differs sharply: RADIANTCMS
is still running EBITDA margins of 10-15%, down from a historical 25-28%,
explicitly waiting on the still-incomplete IBA repricing to recover. CMS, by
contrast, claims its margin has already been RAISED (to ~27%) in the same
quarter it disclosed the cost shock (LBF-3). No direct cash-logistics peer
is showing a comparably fast recovery; this divergence is worth carrying to
Stage 11 as a calibration check on how credible a same-quarter cut-and-raise
combination is against what peers in the identical cost environment are
actually delivering.

**2C Capex cycle.** No industry-wide capacity race is visible. SIS is
acquisitive/consolidating (AP Securitas acquisition, cash-business IPO still
pending). RADIANTCMS is deliberately conservative on M&A ("this particular
instance is probably too large for us to absorb," Q4 FY26 call). QUESS and
UDS are non-capex-heavy staffing models. No peer with ATM-specific capex
exists in the set to compare against CMS's own FY27 capex guidance
(Rs100-125cr, down from Rs350cr) — this specific comparison remains
unavailable from the peer set.

**2D Competitive mentions.** CMS is named directly by analysts twice:
- AGSTRA, Jun-2024 call: "there is another company, listed company CMS in
  the exact same business, but they have very different financial profile,
  EBITDA margin, cash flow balance sheet… is it that we have done a lot of
  transaction-based contracts… leading to losses" (Savi Jain, analyst).
  Management (Saurabh Lal) points to its Securevalue India subsidiary as the
  more comparable unit and offers to share standalone financials — a
  pre-CIRP acknowledgment, over a year before AGS's collapse, that CMS's
  capital discipline and AGS's were already diverging.
  (AGSTRA-Concall_Jun_2024, ~mid-document, line 989-1015.)
- RADIANTCMS, Feb-2026 call: an analyst asks directly "is it at least for
  the top 2, you and CMS, is there any differentiation of prices… or is it
  pure L1 price based?" Management declines detailed competitive comment but
  clarifies "90%, 95% of our business is not tender- or L1-driven… except a
  few public sector banks." (RADIANTCMS-Concall_Feb_2026, p.8-9/14, Col
  Devasahayam / Muthuraman.)

**2E Risks peers raise that CMS's own calls do not.**
- Licensed-GUNMEN/armed-guard manpower SHORTAGE as a distinct supply-side
  constraint, separate from wage cost inflation (RADIANTCMS, Aug-2026 call,
  Diya Jain question). CMS's own transcripts (per B05) frame the Q1 FY27
  cost pressure purely as a price/wage issue, not a labour-availability
  issue.
- Chronic, unresolved receivables deterioration as a LEADING INDICATOR of
  sector distress, not a lagging nuisance — AGSTRA's own history shows this
  played out over several quarters before CIRP. CMS management has not
  connected its own DSO drift to this precedent in any transcript reviewed
  by B05.
- Subsidiary-level fraud/receivables write-off becoming a standalone
  governance risk line (UDS's "Avon" logistics-brokerage subsidiary,
  INR23cr provisioned over two quarters, explicit investigation disclosed).
  A structurally similar risk sits inside CMS's own recurring cash-
  embezzlement CARO line (company memory LBF-4), but CMS management has
  never framed it as a receivables-quality risk the way UDS's disclosure
  forces investors to.
- Labour-code-driven consolidation as an explicit STRATEGIC OPPORTUNITY to
  actively pursue (SIS's repeated framing: organized players gain
  addressable market as compliance arbitrage collapses), rather than only a
  cost headwind to be endured. CMS management, per B05, discusses the AGS
  vacancy but does not extend the same logic to the broader Labour Code
  shift as a moat-widening event beyond that one already-known opportunity.

## PART 4: TRIANGULATION SUMMARY

- Claims assessed: 10 distinct sub-claims (from B05's 8 peer_questions, with
  Q4 split into wage / fuel / repricing-timeline sub-claims for precision).
- Verified: 1 of 10 (minimum-wage inflation, sector-wide, magnitude
  corroborated).
- Partially verified: 2 of 10 (fuel-cost direction; IBA repricing mechanism
  and timeline, in lockstep with rather than ahead of CMS).
- Contradicted: 1 of 10 (DSO/receivables — flows to synthesis as a priority
  item).
- Unverifiable: 6 of 10 (currency-supply shock magnitude, ATM fulfilment %,
  transaction-fee-dead/RFP pricing, ATM count trend, AGS-share-capture sum,
  NCR-Brinks/payment-aggregator threat) — all fail for the same structural
  reason: no peer in the set discloses current-period ATM-replenishment
  operations.

**Single most consequential contradiction.** CMS's DSO reportedly worsened
116→126 days across the same window (Mar-Jun 2026) in which its two closest
listed peers, RADIANTCMS and SIS, both report DSO improving or holding at
multi-quarter lows. This removes the "industry-wide collections stress"
explanation and, combined with AGSTRA's history of chronic receivables
deterioration preceding its 2025 CIRP filing, raises rather than lowers the
priority already assigned to this issue by B05 and by company memory Gap
G-1.

**Single strongest independent confirmation.** The minimum-wage inflation
magnitude (SIS: 20-47% by state) and the sector-wide, association-mediated
repricing mechanism and Q2 FY27 timeline (RADIANTCMS: same IBA-committee
channel, same completion quarter CMS itself cites) are corroborated by two
independent peers on the same calendar window. This is genuine external
validation of one of CMS's most exculpatory claims — that its Q1 FY27 cost
shock is real, sector-wide, and has a defined, already-in-motion resolution
path.

**Overall assessment.** The peer set both supports and complicates CMS's
narrative, on different claims, without ever fully undercutting the whole
story. It supports the cost-inflation and repricing-timeline claims
convincingly. It contradicts the framing that CMS's DSO problem is an
industry-wide phenomenon — two direct peers moved the opposite direction in
the identical window. And it leaves the highest-stakes, most CMS-specific
claims (the currency-supply shock's exact magnitude, the ATM fulfilment
percentage, the transaction-fee-model-is-dead framing, and the AGS-
share-capture number) entirely untested, because none of the six peers
operates a disclosed, current-period ATM-replenishment business. That last
point is itself a finding for Stage 11 and Halt 1: the load-bearing external-
shock claim (LBF-2) rests on CMS's own transcript and RBI/CATMi-referenced
third-party data (per company memory), not on any peer corroboration
available in this corpus.

## PART 5: CROSS-PEER HYPOTHESIS

**Hypothesis:** the cash-logistics/security-outsourcing sector is
bifurcating by PRICING MECHANISM, not by sub-segment. Businesses priced as a
percentage of a pass-through statutory cost base (SIS: guards and facility
management priced as a fixed markup on wage-plus-statutory cost) capture
labour-cost inflation automatically as a revenue and margin tailwind, with
no negotiation required — SIS's own framing that minimum-wage hikes are
"not a negative… it's a positive" is a direct structural consequence of this
pricing design, not managerial skill. Businesses priced on a fixed
per-point, per-machine or per-transaction basis (RADIANTCMS's core retail
cash-management contracts; CMS's ATM per-transaction/per-machine contracts)
must fight a slow, multi-quarter, association-mediated repricing battle
(RADIANTCMS's CLA appeal to banks; CMS's IBA committee) to recover the same
cost inflation — and during that fight, margins compress (RADIANTCMS: 25-28%
historical down to 10-15% current) and, on the evidence of this stage,
collections friction (DSO) can deteriorate in tandem for the party still
waiting on repricing.

This is testable: whichever cash-logistics/security peer moves furthest
toward percentage-of-cost or cost-plus pricing (SIS structurally; CMS
partially, via its shift toward Tech & Payments/software revenue, which is
priced differently from its legacy ATM per-transaction book) should show
both faster margin recovery AND better DSO behaviour than peers still
locked into fixed unit-price contracts. No single peer states this; it only
emerges from comparing SIS's costless pass-through against RADIANTCMS's and
(by inference) CMS's fought-for repricing, read alongside this stage's DSO
finding.
