## THE ONE CORRECTION THAT MATTERS MOST: THE RAW-MATERIAL "ABNORMAL SPIKE" CLAIM DOES NOT SURVIVE THE THREE-YEAR SERIES

Run 2 gave the raw-material half of LBF-2 (the FY26 inventory build)
"external, evidenced support" by citing CIF raw-material imports up 60.2%
year on year. That comparison is FY26 versus FY25 only. The three-year
series, read from the Foreign Exchange Earnings and Outgo note in each
annual report:

| Year | CIF raw material (Rs lakh) | Source |
|---|---|---|
| FY24 | 2,885.65 | AR2024 p.29 |
| FY25 | 1,823.74 | AR2025 p.33 |
| FY26 | 2,921.18 | AR2026 p.42 |

I read all three pages myself and rebuilt the series. FY26 over FY25 is
+60.2% (matches run 2's figure). FY26 over FY24 is +1.23%. FY25 is the
outlier, sitting 36.8% below FY24. The 60.2% headline is a rebound off a
depressed FY25, not an abnormal spike against the company's own normal
run rate two years prior.

The FY26 MD&A itself claims: "commodity prices (namely Solid Carbide,
Steel and other related commodities) increased abnormally in very high
proportion in the Financial Year in consideration" (AR2026 p.25). That
sentence does not survive the three-year series above. It survives even
less against the nearest in-window peer datapoint the run has on file:
Wendt India's AGM on 21-Jul-2025 stated "Commodity prices stayed largely
stable" (per the injected peer note; I did not re-read the Wendt transcript
myself, as it is stage 6's corpus, not mine).

**Ruling: the auditor is right.** The raw-material explanation for the FY26
inventory build (LBF-2) loses its external, evidenced support. FY26 CIF
raw-material cost was essentially flat against the last normal year (FY24)
and only looks dramatic against a trough year the company itself never
explained. This makes LBF-2 MORE open, not less: if raw-material price
inflation did not abnormally spike, the FY26 inventory build (Rs 31.9 cr to
Rs 56.4 cr per company memory, verify at stage source) needs a different
explanation — most plausibly pre-build for the drill-capacity ramp that
delivered in Q1 FY27, or slower-than-planned sell-through, or both. The
MD&A's own "abnormal" language is the kind of externally-attributed excuse
that Section 2B below classifies as external-blame, and on this occasion
the number does not back the claim.

## THE PLEDGE-AND-DIVIDEND CHAIN

Run 2 carried the promoter pledge and the maiden dividend as two separate
HIGH flags. I verified each leg independently and rule that this is **ONE
CHAIN, not two flags**.

1. **The pledge.** SHP_30Jun2026 declaration page confirms: "Whether any
   shares held by promoters are encumbered under 'Pledged'? Yes" (p.2).
   The Table I detail (p.5) shows 35,967,172 shares pledged, 94.4% of the
   promoter group's total shareholding of 38,102,764 shares. Cross-checked
   against AR2026's promoter shareholding note (p.94-95): Shapoorji
   Pallonji and Company Private Limited itself holds exactly 35,967,172
   shares as at 31-Mar-2026. The pledged count equals SP and Co's entire
   own holding to the share. **100% of SP and Co's residual stake is
   pledged**; none of Forbes Campbell Finance's 2,135,592 shares carry the
   pledge.

2. **The FY26 share movement.** During FY26, SP and Co moved its only
   unpledged block, 1,470,000 shares, to Forbes Campbell Finance Limited:
   SP and Co went from 37,437,172 shares (72.56%) to 35,967,172 (69.71%);
   Forbes Campbell went from 665,592 (1.29%) to 2,135,592 (4.14%), a
   +220.86% change (AR2026 note 11, p.94-95). The exact transfer date
   within FY26 is not disclosed anywhere in the corpus I hold.

3. **The maiden dividend.** The Board declared an interim dividend of Rs
   5/share on 24-Apr-2025, record date 2-May-2025, total cash outflow Rs
   2,579.72 lakh (AR2025 p.22 for the original announcement; AR2026 p.52
   confirms no final dividend followed, so this is the entire FY26
   dividend, and the company's first ever). Against FY26 standalone PAT of
   Rs 2,877.30 lakh (AR2026 note 12, Other Equity roll-forward, p.94), the
   payout ratio is 89.66%, matching the audit's cited 89.7%.

4. **What went to the pledged parent.** Using SP and Co's year-end
   (31-Mar-2026) holding of 35,967,172 shares at Rs 5/share gives Rs
   1,798.36 lakh, matching the audit's "roughly Rs 1,798 lakh." I flag one
   precision point the audit's framing glosses over: the dividend record
   date was 2-May-2025, very early in FY26, while the 1,470,000-share
   transfer to Forbes Campbell is dated only "during the year" with no
   specific date given anywhere in the corpus. **Two readings separate by
   one observation.** Reading A: if the transfer happened after the record
   date (the more likely reading, since AR2025, signed the same day the
   dividend was declared, still shows SP and Co's pre-transfer 37,437,172
   figure with no mention of an imminent transfer), SP and Co collected
   the dividend on its FULL pre-transfer holding, Rs 1,871.86 lakh, not
   Rs 1,798 lakh. Reading B: if the transfer happened before 2-May-2025 (no
   evidence found for this), the lower Rs 1,798 lakh figure holds. The
   observation that separates them — the exact transfer date — is not
   disclosed in any filing in this corpus. Either way, the order of
   magnitude (Rs 1.8-1.9 cr to a 100%-pledged holding company) is not in
   dispute.

**Ruling: one chain.** A promoter whose only unencumbered block was moved
to a group finance vehicle in the same year the operating company declared
its first-ever dividend, sending Rs 1.8-1.9 crore to a fully pledged
holding, is a single liquidity-and-governance story, not two independent
observations. For a minority holder, the dividend does not read as a
capital-allocation signal about the operating business; it reads as a cash
extraction consistent with debt service at the parent, from a company that
is itself "net debt free" and did not need to leverage its own balance
sheet to make the payment. That is not proof of misuse — the dividend is a
pro-rata payment to ALL shareholders, including minority holders, at the
same Rs 5/share — but the pledge means the parent's need for cash from the
subsidiary should be watched, not assumed benign, especially if a future
dividend is proposed in a year the parent's pledge position changes.
