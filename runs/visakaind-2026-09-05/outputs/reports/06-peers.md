## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

### 2A. Demand environment
BirlaNu's four calls trace a single, coherent cycle: trough in H1 FY26 (Jun-Sep
2025 — "testing market conditions," "weak demand," "market remains difficult,"
BIRLANU Q1 FY26 call p.3 and Q2 FY26 call p.3), followed by a bounce in Q4 FY26
(Mar-2026 quarter, "strong bounce back from H1," BIRLANU Q4 FY26 call, Akshat
Seth, p.6) that becomes a record Q1 FY27 (Jun-2026 quarter, roofing revenue
+17% to Rs517cr, BIRLANU Q1 FY27 call, Akshat Seth, p.3). Because Visaka's own
transcript record ends Aug-2023, there is no overlapping-quarter comparison
possible; this is useful as forward industry context only, not as a
contemporaneous check on Visaka's FY24-FY25 numbers.

### 2B. Pricing and input costs
Consistent theme across all four calls: realisations fell 3-7% YoY in most
segments through FY26 (e.g. roofing price erosion "4% to 5%," BIRLANU Q1 FY26
call p.7-8; "about 5% lower" YoY, BIRLANU Q2 FY26 call p.17), while input costs
(fibre, cement, resin, freight, forex) stayed a live headwind, worsening
sharply in Q4 FY26/Q1 FY27 due to the Middle East conflict (PVC resin +60% in
three weeks in March 2026, BIRLANU Q4 FY26 call, Akshat Seth, p.3-4; repeated
in Q1 FY27 call, Akshat Seth, p.3). Margin gains in this period came from cost
control (a BCG-led "value enhancement" programme, BIRLANU Q2 FY26 call p.6)
and mix, not from pricing power recovering. This matches Visaka's own implicit
narrative of a cost-squeezed, not demand-collapsed, margin story — but "costs
elevated and volatile" is the opposite of "stabilised."

### 2C. Capex cycle
BirlaNu is unambiguously an expander, not a holder or cutter: Chennai Line 2
(blocks, commissioned ~FY26), OPVC plant Patna (commissioned by Q4 FY26),
Nellore boards greenfield (Andhra Pradesh, targeted Q4 FY27 commissioning),
a second, newly approved Hyderabad/Telangana boards plant (Rs167cr, BIRLANU
Q1 FY27 call, Ajay Kapadia, p.8), and the Clean Coats acquisition (Nov-2025).
Because Visaka is simultaneously running its own ~Rs450cr boards programme
through 2030, this is not a lone-expander situation: it looks like an
**industry-wide capacity race in cementitious/fibre-cement boards**, with at
least two of the sector's larger listed names adding boards capacity on
overlapping timelines, both funded debt-first. That raises a genuine
category-level oversupply risk if the new capacity from both companies lands
in the same window — a risk neither company's own materials appear to
address directly.

### 2D. Competitive mentions
Zero. Visaka is never named in any of the four transcripts. BirlaNu also
never names Everest, Ramco, or any other specific competitor — a consistent,
explicit policy ("Without naming names...," BIRLANU Q1 FY26 call p.11) rather
than an oversight.

### 2E. Risks peers raise that the main company does not (in its stale corpus)
- Structural low long-run roofing demand growth, ~2-3% per year, explicitly
  named as a ceiling on the whole category (BIRLANU, Q2 FY26 call, Akshat
  Seth, p.17) — below even Visaka's own downward-revised 3-5% floor.
- Roofing-industry overcapacity traced to COVID-era capacity additions across
  the sector (BIRLANU, Q2 FY26 call, Akshat Seth, p.17).
- Steel-roofing price substitution as a two-way risk: fibre-cement gains
  share when steel is dear, is exposed when steel is cheap (BIRLANU, Q4 FY26
  call, Akshat Seth, p.9; Q1 FY27 call, Saurabh Ginodia/Akshat Seth, p.15).
- Imported-fibre and freight cost exposure to rupee depreciation and
  energy/petroleum prices, named as a specific FY27 margin risk (BIRLANU,
  Q4 FY26 call, Akshat Seth, p.9-10).
- Middle East conflict-driven input-cost and freight-rate volatility as a
  live, quantified FY26-27 risk (PVC resin +60% in three weeks, then -30% the
  following month) (BIRLANU, Q4 FY26 call, Akshat Seth, p.3-4; Q1 FY27 call,
  Akshat Seth, p.4).
- An industry-wide, debt-funded boards-capacity race (see 2C above), which
  neither company frames as an oversupply risk to itself.

One notable **shared silence**, not a peer-raised risk: none of BirlaNu's
four calls, across roughly two years of investor conversation, raises
chrysotile-asbestos regulatory, health, or litigation risk at all, despite
chrysotile being a banned substance in most developed markets. Visaka's stale
corpus is silent on the same point. This is not evidence the risk is
immaterial; it is evidence that neither company's investor-facing conversation
addresses it, which is itself worth flagging to synthesis as an unaddressed
structural tail risk for 53% of Visaka's revenue base.

### Cross-read items requested beyond the handoff

**(a) Rural/monsoon roofing demand and pricing, FY26-FY27.** The arc is
material and was unavailable to Visaka's stale corpus: Q1 FY26 (Jun-2025
quarter, the monsoon-critical quarter for roofing) was weak — "industry-wide
demand contraction and price erosion" (BIRLANU, Q1 FY26 call, Akshat Seth,
p.3-4), volume down ~1.5-2%, price down 4-5% at the industry level (BIRLANU,
Q1 FY26 call, Akshat Seth, p.7-8). By Q2 FY26, management flagged "a good
monsoon this year" as an early positive read for rural demand into H2
(BIRLANU, Q2 FY26 call, Akshat Seth, p.17). By Q4 FY26, "underlying resilience
in the rural economy" was cited alongside a price advantage versus steel
roofing (elevated steel prices) as a substitution tailwind (BIRLANU, Q4 FY26
call, Akshat Seth, p.9). By Q1 FY27 (the following monsoon-critical quarter),
roofing delivered a record Rs517cr, +17% YoY, with rural demand and the steel
price gap both cited as drivers (BIRLANU, Q1 FY27 call, Akshat Seth/Saurabh
Ginodia, p.3, p.15). Read together, this describes a genuine cyclical
recovery in rural fibre-cement roofing demand through FY26 into FY27, aided
opportunistically by elevated competing steel-roofing prices rather than by
any structural strengthening of fibre-cement's own competitive position.

**(b) Own roofing profitability and structural view on the chrysotile-asbestos
category.** BirlaNu's roofing margin trajectory is itself a caution against
reading any near-term profitability recovery as durable: in Q1 FY26 an
analyst directly challenged management on whether "12%-13% is a new normal"
after being told a "first milestone" of 15-16% previously, noting the segment
was then running "sub 10%" (BIRLANU, Q1 FY26 call, Sanjay Kumar, p.20).
Management's answer conceded "this is now the second consecutive year where
there is a year-on-year price drop... the route to improve profitability has
to come from better pricing in the market" (BIRLANU, Q1 FY26 call, Akshat
Seth, p.20) — an admission of multi-year structural pricing weakness, not a
one-off. By Q1 FY27 the segment margin had recovered to 18.2% (+390bps,
BIRLANU, Q1 FY27 call, Ajay Kapadia, p.7), but a Q1 FY27 analyst noted
BirlaNu (and, by his account, its peers) is still stuck near 17% versus
20-25%+ margins the category delivered in FY22-FY23 (BIRLANU, Q1 FY27 call,
Sai Ganesh, p.13) — management's response reframed the comparison (mixing
boards into peer numbers) rather than disputing the structural margin
compression. On the category's own durability, when asked point-blank
whether oversupply or falling preference for asbestos products explained
roofing weakness, management answered with overcapacity and raw-material cost
framing, explicitly citing a low (2-3%) long-run demand growth rate, but did
**not** address chrysotile-asbestos regulatory or health risk at all (BIRLANU,
Q2 FY26 call, Akshat Seth, p.17-18) — see 2E above.

## PART 4: TRIANGULATION SUMMARY

- Claims partially verified: 4 of 8 (Claims 1, 2, 4, 7)
- Claims contradicted: 1 of 8 (Claim 5)
- Claims unverifiable: 3 of 8 (Claims 3, 6, 8)
- Claims verified (2+ independent peers): 0 of 8 — structurally unreachable
  this run (see Coverage Ceiling)

**Single most consequential contradiction.** BirlaNu's Chennai Line 2 (AAC
blocks) ramped from commissioning to near-full utilisation in roughly two
quarters with no reported dip, directly contradicting the premise that
Visaka's multi-year, dip-then-recovery Midnapore ramp (96%→72%→79%→86%) is
normal industry pacing. The caveat (blocks vs. boards, different
manufacturing technology) keeps this from being conclusive, but it shifts the
burden back toward company-specific execution as at least a partial
explanation for Visaka's slow ramp.

**Single strongest independent confirmation.** BirlaNu confirms two of
Visaka's own framings as industry-wide, not company-specific: persistent,
still-elevated (not stabilised) fibre/cement/forex input costs through FY26
into FY27 (Claim 1), and a debt-funded-first, accruals-later capex financing
approach carrying a comparable proportional debt run-up during a comparable
boards-capacity buildout (Claim 7).

**Overall.** The peer set complicates Visaka's own narrative more than it
simply supports or undercuts it. It corroborates the industry-wide framing on
cost pressure and debt-funded capex (helping Visaka's case that its FY24-FY25
margin weakness and debt run-up were not unique). It undercuts the framing
that Visaka's slow capacity ramp is standard industry pacing (BirlaNu's
adjacent evidence suggests faster is achievable). And it surfaces two risks
neither company's own investor materials address: an emerging industry-wide
capacity race in boards that could pressure the category's returns just as
both firms' new plants come online, and a total silence, on both sides, on
chrysotile-asbestos regulatory and health risk despite it underpinning over
half of Visaka's revenue.

## PART 5: CROSS-PEER HYPOTHESIS

With only one peer covered by transcripts, a true cross-peer hypothesis — a
pattern visible only when comparing multiple peers against each other — is
not honestly derivable from this run. Stated plainly, per protocol: **no
cross-peer hypothesis identified in the strict sense** — the peer set does
not reveal a pattern beyond what BirlaNu individually discloses, because
there is no second or third peer disclosure to triangulate against.

One narrower, testable observation is available from BirlaNu's own internal
cross-segment disclosure gap, offered here explicitly as single-peer evidence
and not as satisfying the cross-peer requirement: BirlaNu quotes an industry
board-segment margin range of 15-20% (Q1 FY27) while its own blended Walls
segment (which includes the same boards) runs at 10-11%, and Visaka's own
Building Products segment never isolates Vnext at all. The testable
hypothesis for a future run with more peer coverage: **the "mid-teens to
20%" board-margin figure that circulates in this sector's analyst
conversations describes a single new, fully-ramped plant's economics, not
any listed company's actual portfolio-wide board economics** — every
disclosed portfolio-level number (BirlaNu's blended 10-11%, Visaka's
undisclosed-but-implied blended Building Products margin) sits well below it.
If a second boards-disclosing peer's transcripts become available, this is
directly checkable: does that peer's own reported segment margin also trail
its own quoted industry range by a similar 400-900bps gap?
