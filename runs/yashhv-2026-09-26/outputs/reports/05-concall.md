### 2B. Excuse pattern analysis

Two distinct patterns run through the three-plus calls:

1. **Reframing without acknowledgment**, the pattern identified in run 1
   and confirmed on re-read: the greenfield date and the greenfield capex
   both moved substantially, restated each time in confident language
   ("we don't see any delay," Call-2; the CMD frames the new capex figure
   as "close to INR80 crores is already done" without confirming or
   disputing the analyst's Rs153cr total, Call-3) and never named as a
   slip or an overrun.
2. **Different, sometimes incompatible, answers to different analysts on
   the same load-bearing topic within a single call.** This is a
   materially different and more serious pattern than reframing, and run
   1 did not surface it: in Call-3 alone, management gives 40-45% FY27
   growth to one analyst and Rs360-400cr invoicing (a higher implied
   growth rate) to another (Correction #3); gives a purely seasonal/cyclic
   explanation for the H2 margin gain to one analyst and a
   shortage-pricing-power explanation to another (Correction #8); and
   across all four calls gives three different accounts of who owns the
   RIP core technology (Correction #4).
3. The one clean external-blame instance remains as run 1 found it: the
   Middle-East-linked input-cost escalation is named and immediately
   paired with "we are also able to successfully communicate to our
   customers" (pass-through, not absorption) — a balanced answer.
4. No instance across the four calls of management volunteering "we made
   a mistake." The JIT-abandonment admission (Call-2) remains the closest
   genuine self-correction found in the record.
5. Analyst pressure, not proactive disclosure, still surfaces the two most
   sensitive topics (cyber-fraud, DRHP-vs-press-release volume question),
   and now also surfaces the order-book number itself, which took two
   calls of direct pressure (Call-A, then Kumar Divyanshu's two-part push
   in Call-2) before being given as a specific figure (Correction #11).
