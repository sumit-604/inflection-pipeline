Federal Bank NBFC already prices the recovery

Fedbank Financial Services is a gold and property backed lender majority owned by Federal Bank, which holds 60.8% and staffs the board. Gold loans are about 45 to 53% of a roughly Rs 20,000 Cr book, with the rest in medium ticket loan against property, a small ticket LAP book that broke in FY25, and a residual home loan line. FY26 total income was Rs 2,227 Cr and PAT was Rs 344 Cr, recovering 53% after a year the profit fell 8%. The shares trade at Rs 164, which is 2.09 times book and 16 times earnings, on a 12.6% ROE.

The transition is a credit reset, not a growth story. Management exited a Rs 770 Cr unsecured business loan book in a single quarter of FY26, is pushing the mix toward gold, and is rebuilding small ticket LAP collections after that segment drove credit cost to 1.8% of average assets in FY25. Gold branches are being added at pace, 113 through the third quarter against a 100 to 150 target for the year. The whole case rests on one number arriving clean: Q4 FY26 credit cost at or below about 1.1% with a real FY27 guidance figure attached.

The numbers say a good operator carrying a real asset quality scar. Gate 0 lands at AVOID, core score 38 of 100, dragged by two structural zeros that apply across the whole six year window rather than one bad year: ROCE is not computable on an NBFC balance sheet, and operating cash flow is negative every year because loan disbursement is an operating outflow under Ind AS 7. Revenue compounded 26% and PAT 41% over five years, which is genuine. The scar is provision coverage thinning from 40% to 32% while the impairment charge jumped 229% in FY25, and a Stage 3 loan figure that does not tie out across three notes in the same annual report.

Management says the right things and mostly does them, graded B. Credit cost stayed inside the 1% band every quarter, the unsecured book left cleanly, and direct assignment income was cut hard, all framed with unprompted self blame. Against that, collections in-housing slipped two full quarters, the CRAR and Tier 2 disclosure vanished in the exact quarter leverage rose most, two dated technical commitments were dropped without mention, and the asset quality data most relevant to the thesis, Stage 2 balances and the old versus new LAP split, was refused on two calls as not public information.

The peers confirm the stress is real and contradict the growth headline. Small ticket LAP stress shows up across Five Star, SBFC and MAS as MFI sector spillover, so that part checks out. The 52% gold AUM growth is roughly 90% gold price, not tonnage: Manappuram disclosed tonnage up just 2.8% year on year against the 10 to 12% CAGR Fedfina cited. Cost to income at 56 to 57% sits 15 to 25 points worse than every quantified peer, none of whom name Maharashtra or Tamil Nadu as their stress state the way Fedfina does.

The market runway is large and the moat is thin. The addressable organized gold plus secured LAP market runs into the trillions and Fedfina holds under 2% of its own defined slice, so headroom is not the constraint. What is missing is pricing power and proprietary edge: the regulatory tailwind, Federal Bank funding, and co-lending are all shared or partner dependent, and the emerging moat score of 25 sits on the bottom edge of its band with the two hardest promises deferred to a call that has not happened in this record.

The valuation says avoid at this price. On the primary lens the stock trades at 2.09 times book against a justified 0.9 to 1.2 times on a 12.6% ROE that roughly equals its 13.5% cost of equity, so the economic spread is near zero. The earned destination PE comes out at 14.3 times on the conservative reverse risk margin track and 15.3 times on the additive track, both below the current 16 to 18 times, which is a de-rating headwind, not upside. The Hurdle Ratio is 1.21 against a required 1.953, a STOP. Fair value sits near Rs 150 to 156 base, Rs 117 to 122 bear, Rs 186 to 193 bull, and the probability weighted three year return is minus 3%. Entry only makes sense at Rs 68 to 77, with a margin of safety price of Rs 61, roughly 35% below where the stock has ever traded.

The thesis review and the challenge both land on the same side. The independent thesis returns AVOID with a Small position ceiling and a clean break trigger: Q4 FY26 credit cost above 1.1%. The devil's advocate dismantled the bull case, weakened the growth, moat and management legs, and still could not break the valuation safety: even the most generous non-anchored ROE of 14 to 16% implies only 1.0 to 1.2 times book, so the stock is close to twice its own justified value at Rs 164.
