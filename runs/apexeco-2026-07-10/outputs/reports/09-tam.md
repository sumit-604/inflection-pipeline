## SECTION 1: MARKET DEFINITION

### 1A. Precise boundaries

- **Product scope**: Turnkey EPC of water and wastewater treatment plants — Water Treatment Plants (WTP: filtration, ultrafiltration, RO, demineralisation, EDR), Effluent Treatment Plants (ETP), Sewage Treatment Plants (STP, incl. prefabricated/MBR), Zero Liquid Discharge (ZLD) systems (MVR evaporators, crystallizers), and membrane-based water/wastewater recycling systems — plus attached Operations & Maintenance (O&M)/AMC and spares/chemicals supply. This is Apex's disclosed scope (B04-bizmodel.yaml; Investor Presentation pp.9-10; AR FY25 MDA p.32-33). **Excluded**: large-scale seawater/brackish-water desalination (no disclosed Apex track record), municipal bulk water-supply distribution/pipeline networks, drinking-water bottling, and water treatment chemicals as a standalone (non-project-embedded) product line.
- **Geographic scope**: India, domestic industrial/commercial/institutional clients (primary, ~99% of revenue by inference from a wholly India-headquartered, India-listed order book). Minor export exposure: FY25 export sales ₹167.10 Lakh (₹1.67 Cr) to Egypt and Nigeria (AR FY25, Note 22, p.59); FY24 export sales ₹407.71 Lakh mostly Vietnam — export revenue **fell** FY24→FY25, and AR risk factors flag international expansion (Bangladesh, Vietnam) as an outlook item, not yet a proven revenue vector (AR FY25 MDA "Future Prospects," p.34; B04 mgmt_questions).
- **Customer scope**: Industrial and commercial clients across ~15 industries served (Investor Presentation p.6) — process manufacturing, auto/auto-ancillary, FMCG/food, pharma, chemicals, steel, paper, construction — plus some institutional/campus STP work. **Not** retail/residential point-of-use water purifiers; **not** a bulk municipal-utility water-supply-and-distribution contractor (though STP work for institutional/commercial campuses is in scope).
- **Channel scope**: Direct turnkey EPC contracting (integrator model using OEM technology from Veolia, DuPont, Pentair, Grundfos), not equipment-only supply or chemicals distribution.
- **Price segment**: Mid-ticket EPC projects; disclosed reference orders range from ~₹40 Cr (L&T, WTP/ETP/ZLD) to ~₹100+ Cr (Reliance, advanced water treatment) (Investor Presentation p.7). Apex is not currently disclosed as winning mega-tickets (>₹150-200 Cr single contracts) that larger balance-sheet peers (Thermax, VA Tech Wabag, L&T) typically capture.
- **Inclusions**: WTP/ETP/STP/ZLD/membrane-recycling turnkey EPC (India, industrial/commercial); attached O&M/AMC/spares.
- **Exclusions**: Desalination mega-projects, municipal water-supply distribution infrastructure, chemicals-only supply, residential/retail water purification, and non-India geographies (treated as immaterial/unproven for SAM purposes).

## SECTION 2: TAM ESTIMATION — MULTIPLE METHODS

All figures ₹ Crore unless stated. USD conversions use the July 2026 spot rate of **₹95.5/USD** (exchangerates.org.uk, historical rate table, accessed 2026-07-10) unless a period-specific rate is separately noted.

### Method 1 — Top-down (technology market, industrial slice)

- India Water and Wastewater Treatment (WWT) Technology Market size: **USD 3.27 Bn in 2026**, growing at a **9.62% CAGR** to USD 5.17 Bn by 2031 (Mordor Intelligence, "India Water and Wastewater Treatment Technology Market," current report as of 2026 — STALE check: current, <2 yrs).
  - ₹ conversion: USD 3.27 Bn × ₹95.5 = **₹31,229 Cr** (2026).
- Segment split: Municipal utilities held **58.22%** share in 2025 (AMRUT 2.0 / Swachh Bharat Mission 2.0 capital allocations); implying **Industrial + other ≈ 41.78%** (same source).
- Apex is overwhelmingly an industrial/commercial EPC contractor (client list: L&T, Reliance, HUL, Honda, Hero MotoCorp, PepsiCo, Reckitt Benckiser, Ashok Leyland, JSW, Aditya Birla/Hindalco, Tata Chemicals — Investor Presentation p.11). Applying the industrial slice:
  - **₹31,229 Cr × 41.78% = ₹13,048 Cr** (2026, industrial-only technology-market estimate; before SAM filters).
- Subtractions already embedded: municipal-utility water supply/distribution (58.22% removed).

### Method 2 — Top-down (CRISIL sector-investment cross-check)

- CRISIL Intelligence: Indian "water and wastewater treatment" sector revenues/investment projected to rise from **₹3,946 Bn cumulative in FY2020-24 to ₹6,310-6,510 Bn cumulative in FY2025-28** (~1.6-1.7x), cited in Vishvaraj Environment's "Assessment of the water and wastewater sector in India," September 2025 — STALE check: current, <1 yr.
  - In ₹ Cr: **₹3,94,600 Cr cumulative FY20-24 → ₹6,31,000-6,51,000 Cr cumulative FY25-28.**
  - Annualised (÷4 years): **₹15,775-16,275 Cr/year**, midpoint **₹16,025 Cr/year**.
- **Scope caveat (important)**: this CRISIL figure is drawn from a broader "water and wastewater sector" assessment that likely includes government water-supply-and-sanitation programme spend (Jal Jeevan Mission, AMRUT, Swachh Bharat Mission — CRISIL separately sizes ~₹7.5-8 lakh Cr of urban infrastructure spend FY25-29 of which WSS is "more than half"), which would include distribution pipelines and non-treatment infrastructure outside Apex's EPC scope. This method is used as an **upper-bound context anchor**, not a central estimate, because of this definitional breadth.

### Method 3 — Bottom-up (STP capacity-gap unit economics)

- Addressable unit: 1 MLD of sewage-treatment capacity.
- National sewage-treatment **capacity shortfall: >72,000 MLD/day** (PL Capital research, cited in national business press, June 2026 — current).
- Turnkey STP capex per MLD: **₹1.0-1.5 Cr/MLD**, triangulated from multiple current (2025-26) industry cost guides (e.g., 10 MLD plant ≈ ₹10-15 Cr; 50 MLD plant ≈ ₹50-75 Cr — implying ₹1.0-1.5 Cr/MLD across scale bands).
- One-time buildout backlog = 72,000 MLD × ₹1.0-1.5 Cr/MLD = **₹72,000-1,08,000 Cr** (cumulative, one-time, STP segment only — not an annual flow).
- Annualised over an assumed 10-year execution window (standard infra rollout planning horizon; this 10-year window is an explicit modelling assumption, not a sourced figure): **₹7,200-10,800 Cr/year**, midpoint **≈₹9,000 Cr/year**.
- **Scope caveat**: this method covers **STP (municipal/institutional sewage) only** — it excludes ETP (industrial effluent, Apex's largest disclosed order category), WTP, ZLD, and membrane recycling, i.e., roughly 1 of Apex's 5 product lines. It materially **understates** full-scope TAM and is used as a sub-segment floor check, not the headline conservative estimate.

## SECTION 3: SAM & SOM

### 3A. SAM — five filters applied to conservative TAM (₹11,250 Cr)

| Filter | Retention | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Product fit | 95% | Apex's 5-line scope (WTP/ETP/STP/ZLD/membrane) closely matches the technology-market definition; minor exclusion for chemicals-only/consumables revenue pools embedded in some TAM sources |
| Geography | 97% | Apex is ~99% India-revenue; FY25 export revenue was ₹1.67 Cr and *declining* YoY (AR FY25 Note 22) — treated as immaterial, not a growth vector yet |
| Channel | 82% | An estimated ~18% of the technology market is captured by OEMs (Veolia, Suez, Grundfos, DuPont) selling/executing directly to large clients without an integrator like Apex — this is an explicit assumption, not a sourced split |
| Customer/capability | 75% | Apex's net worth was ₹63.25 Cr at FY25-end (AR FY25 Balance Sheet) and its largest disclosed single orders are ₹40-100+ Cr (L&T, Reliance) — an estimated ~25% of the addressable market consists of mega-ticket projects (>₹150-200 Cr) requiring bank-guarantee and balance-sheet capacity Apex does not yet demonstrate; this is an explicit assumption |

SAM = ₹11,250 Cr × 0.95 × 0.97 × 0.82 × 0.75 = **₹6,375 Cr**

**SAM as % of TAM (conservative basis) = 6,375 / 11,250 = 56.7% (≈57%)**

### 3B. SOM at 3 and 5 years

- **Current SAM share**: FY26 revenue ₹148.65 Cr ÷ SAM ₹6,375 Cr = **2.33% (≈2.3%)**.
- **Share-gain trajectory**: Standard rules — 1-2pp in 3 years is "normal"; 3-5pp in 5 years requires demonstrated capacity/execution; faster only on competitor exit/acquisition or where unorganised share (>40%) is formalising quickly. Apex's evidence base argues for the **normal/lower** end of the range, not aggressive:
  - Order book grew only ~₹100 Cr → ~₹125 Cr over the period revenue more than doubled (B04 flag) — replenishment lagging execution.
  - Operating cash flow was negative in FY25 (-₹14.08 Cr) and remained materially below PAT in FY26 (₹6.77 Cr OCF vs ₹17.02 Cr PAT) — weak cash conversion constrains funded working-capital capacity for aggressive growth.
  - B07 capex-embedded-growth is **0%** — no committed capacity expansion programme (asset-light integrator model).
  - Partial offset: Apex's ZLD/EDR niche is claimed (self-reported, unverified — B04) to have limited competition, which could support faster-than-normal gain if the unorganised long tail is genuinely formalising toward technology partners like Apex.
  - **Conservative-bias resolution**: +1.0pp over 3 years, +2.0pp over 5 years (bottom of the normal range, reflecting the capacity/cash-conversion caveats above).
- SAM is grown forward at the Method-1 TAM growth rate (9.6% CAGR, Section 2/3C) for consistency:
  - SAM (Yr 3) = ₹6,375 Cr × (1.096)³ = **₹8,394 Cr**
  - SAM (Yr 5) = ₹6,375 Cr × (1.096)⁵ = **₹10,090 Cr**
- **SOM (3-year)** = ₹8,394 Cr × 3.3% share = **₹277 Cr**
- **SOM (5-year)** = ₹10,090 Cr × 4.3% share = **₹434 Cr**

**Implied revenue CAGR (arithmetic)**:
- 3-year: (277 / 148.65)^(1/3) − 1 = **23.1%**
- 5-year: (434 / 148.65)^(1/5) − 1 = **23.9%**

This is the **formal handoff figure for Stage 11**: `som_implied_revenue_cagr = {yr3: 23.1%, yr5: 23.9%}`.

### 3C. Capacity cross-check

B07 capex-embedded-growth is **0%** (asset-light integrator; no committed capacity programme). Per the injected instruction, the order book (~₹125 Cr closing FY26, Investor Presentation p.6) is used as the physical-capacity proxy instead of capex.

- Current order-book-to-trailing-revenue ratio: ₹125 Cr ÷ ₹148.65 Cr = **0.84x** — already thin for an EPC business (a book-to-bill of 1.5-2x is a more typical comfort level for forward revenue visibility in project-based EPC), and this ratio has been **declining**, since order book grew only ~25% (₹100 Cr → ₹125 Cr) while revenue **more than doubled** over the same period (B04 flag).
- To sustain SOM (3-year) revenue of ₹277 Cr at even a conservative 1.5x book-to-bill comfort level, Apex would need an order book of roughly **₹415 Cr** at that point — a gap of approximately **₹290 Cr** versus the current ₹125 Cr book, that must be filled through new order origination (not capex, since the model is asset-light) against a backdrop of demonstrated replenishment underperformance and weak/negative operating cash conversion that would need to fund the associated working capital.
- **Capacity check: gap of ≈₹290 Cr in required order-book coverage by Year 3; the SOM trajectory is the optimistic side of this comparison.** The order-book replenishment pace and cash-conversion profile evidenced in FY25-FY26 do not yet support the SOM's implied growth without either (a) a step-change in order intake pace, or (b) materially improved receivables/retention conversion to fund the working capital of a larger book.

### 5B. Runway assessment

- **Revenue headroom** = SAM ÷ current revenue = ₹6,375 Cr ÷ ₹148.65 Cr = **42.9x**.
- **TAM growth rate**: **9.6%** CAGR (Method 1, Mordor Intelligence, 2026-2031; broadly corroborated by CRISIL's implied ~1.6-1.7x over the FY20-24→FY25-28 comparison, which annualises to a similar order of magnitude).
- **Company CAGR vs. TAM**: Apex's FY26 revenue grew **+109.5% YoY** (concall, May 2026) — vastly ahead of the 9.6% TAM growth rate, i.e., Apex is currently **gaining share**, not merely riding the market. Caveat: this pace reflects a small base plus lumpy large single orders (Reliance ₹100+ Cr, L&T ₹40+ Cr) and is not evidenced as a repeatable annual cadence — the historical 2-year revenue CAGR (FY24 ₹53.08 Cr → FY26 ₹148.65 Cr) is ~67%, also far above what the order-book evidence (Section 3C) suggests is sustainable without a step-change in origination.
- **Years to saturate SAM** at the SOM-implied forward pace (23-24% CAGR, a more defensible forward assumption than the unsustainable historical 67-109% rates): n = ln(6,375/148.65) / ln(1.231) ≈ **17-18 years**. Market size is not the binding constraint on Apex's growth over any 3-5 year investment horizon — **execution capacity (order-book origination, cash conversion) is the binding constraint**, per Section 3C.

### 5C. Runway classification

Using headroom (42.9x, very large) cross-checked against TAM growth (9.6%, moderate — not explosive) and the capacity/cash-conversion caveats from Section 3C, this is classified as:

**STRONG** (not MASSIVE) — headroom alone would suggest MASSIVE, but TAM growth is moderate (not double-digit-plus explosive) and the company's own execution evidence (thin/declining book-to-bill, weak operating cash conversion, zero committed capex) argues against assuming the company can realistically capture that headroom quickly. This is a **conservative-bias downgrade from a raw headroom-only read.**

### 5D. SAM expansion levers actually being pursued

- **O&M/AMC mix shift**: currently ~4% (3.6% per B04) of revenue; a genuine lever being pursued (Investor Presentation p.10 lists "Aftersales Services" as a business segment) but modest in absolute ₹ terms today and not separately sized.
- **International expansion** (Bangladesh, Vietnam per AR outlook): cited as a forward intention, but **not yet evidenced** — export revenue fell FY24→FY25 (Section 4A) — treated as a low-confidence lever.
- **New technology adoption** (MVR, EDR, membrane recycling) expanding the complex-project segment addressable to Apex, supported by active OEM partnerships.
- **Potential addition**: modest — an O&M-mix shift to, say, 10-15% of revenue and a stabilised (not yet proven) export contribution would add low-single-digit percentage points to SAM, not materially change the headroom conclusion (revised headroom ≈45-48x rather than 42.9x). Given the low evidentiary confidence on both levers, this is **not built into the primary SAM/SOM figures above**.

### 5E. Final output card

- **TAM**: ₹11,250 Cr (conservative) / ₹14,500 Cr (realistic)
- **SAM**: ₹6,375 Cr (57% of conservative TAM)
- **SOM**: ₹277 Cr (Year 3) / ₹434 Cr (Year 5)
- **SOM-implied revenue CAGR**: 23.1% (3yr) / 23.9% (5yr) — **formal handoff to Stage 11**
- **Runway class**: STRONG
- **Margin trajectory**: EBITDA margin has been **compressing**, not expanding — 16.7% (FY24) → 15.6% (FY25) → 14.6% (FY26); PAT margin similarly 12.5% → 12.1% → 11.4% over the same three years (all company-reported figures, Investor Presentation p.13-14 / AR FY25). This is consistent with the Cost-of-Material-Consumed rise already flagged in B04 (68.9%→75.6% of revenue).
- **Valuation implication line**: "At **23-24%** revenue CAGR implied by SOM, with a margin trajectory that has been **compressing** (EBITDA margin 16.7%→14.6%, PAT margin 12.5%→11.4% over FY24-FY26), the earnings growth embedded here is approximately **16-20% CAGR if margin compression continues at its recent pace** (versus ~23-24% CAGR in a flat-margin scenario), which **[supports / does not support — NOT FOUND: current market P/E for APEXECO was not provided in the injected inputs for this stage]** the current valuation of **NOT FOUND x P/E**.
