**Discrepancies: 0 of 15** in the sense of "the triple pass got a number wrong." One
important **comparability break** was found during this stage's independent reading of
both ARs' Note 35 side by side, which the triple pass (working from AR FY2025-26 alone)
had no way to catch:

**NEW FINDING — DSCR is not comparable across the two Annual Reports for the same fiscal
year.** AR FY2025-26's Note 35 (standalone, p.136) shows Debt Service Coverage Ratio as
1.67x for FY25 and 0.62x for FY26 (used by Stage 1/Stage 2 as the basis for the "DSCR fell
below 1.0x" finding). AR FY2024-25's OWN Note 35 (standalone, p.215), covering the SAME
FY25 year, shows DSCR as 0.15x for FY25 and 0.20x for FY24 — using an identically-worded
formula ("Earning for Debt Service** / Debt service = Interest & Lease Payments + Principal
Repayments"). The FY25 DSCR is reported as 1.67x in one Annual Report and 0.15x in the
other, an 11x discrepancy for the identical historical year with an identically-described
formula. The most likely mechanical explanation: FY24-25 carried large revolving
working-capital and packing-credit facilities that were drawn and repaid multiple times
within the year (cash flow statement, AR FY2024-25 shows current-borrowing proceeds of
Rs10,080.78mn against repayments of Rs9,758.27mn in that one year alone), so if "Principal
Repayments" in the denominator counts every rollover repayment rather than only the
net/scheduled reduction, the FY25 denominator used in AR FY2024-25 would be far larger than
the denominator used when FY25 is re-presented as a comparative column in AR FY2025-26
(by which time those facilities were largely repaid down, not rolled over). **This means
the "1.67x to 0.62x" DSCR decline that Gate 0 and Stage 2 both cite as evidence of a sudden
one-year covenant-coverage collapse is not a reliable like-for-like trend** — the FY25
figure itself is disclosed two different ways in two consecutive Annual Reports. What is
NOT in doubt, because it comes from the primary cash flow statements directly (Phase 3
below): CFO was negative in FY24 and FY25 and DSCR on any reasonable definition was weak in
all three years FY24-FY26. The specific "1.67x" comparator point is not trustworthy;
the sub-1x FY26 level and the multi-year negative-CFO pattern are.

### 2F. Borrowings
Standalone borrowings fell to Rs NIL (both current and non-current) at 31-Mar-2026, from

## PHASE 3: FINANCIAL STATEMENTS (cash flow first, then balance sheet, then P&L)

### 3A. Cash flow
Consolidated CFO: FY26 +Rs539.02mn, FY25 -Rs623.94mn (Statement of Cash Flow, AR FY2025-26,
p.145) — these figures match Gate 0's cumulative-series inputs exactly (+Rs53.90cr and
-Rs62.39cr). Standalone CFO: FY26 +Rs515.07mn, FY25 -Rs620.34mn (AR FY2025-26, p.112).
CFO/PAT: consolidated FY26 = 539.02/732.82 = 0.74x; FY25 = -623.94/680.76 = -0.92x (negative
PAT-conversion, well below the 0.7x flag threshold in the negative direction). Even FY26's
0.74x sits at the flag threshold, and is only positive at all because of the working-capital
swing described below, not because of an improvement in underlying accrual-to-cash quality.
Across FY23-FY26 (Gate 0's four-year window), cumulative CFO -Rs25.07cr against cumulative
PAT +Rs234.29cr confirms structurally poor cash conversion, not a one-year event.
**CFO quality checks:**
- **One-time inflators:** FY26's positive CFO swing versus FY25 is driven substantially by
  a Rs527.13mn favourable trade-receivable movement (a collection catch-up, partly the
  mirror image of FY25's Rs920.74mn unfavourable receivable build) and a Rs122.39mn
  favourable trade-payable movement — i.e., working-capital timing swings, not an
  improvement in the underlying revenue-to-cash cycle (Statement of Cash Flow, AR
  FY2025-26, p.145).
- **Interest classification:** Interest paid is presented within financing activities
  (Rs234.96mn consolidated, AR FY2025-26, p.145) and interest income within operating
  adjustments — a standard, non-aggressive Ind AS 7 classification choice.
- **Unsustainable payable stretching / inventory rundown:** The opposite pattern is
  present — inventory BUILT (a Rs664.49mn cash use within operating activities,
  consolidated) even as CFO improved, meaning the FY26 CFO improvement happened despite,
  not because of, inventory management; the CFO improvement rests on receivables and
  payables timing.
- **FCF:** Consolidated capex (purchase of PP&E and intangibles) Rs446.63mn (FY26) vs
  Rs500.10mn (FY25); FCF (CFO minus capex) = Rs92.39mn (FY26) vs -Rs1,124.04mn (FY25).
  Capex/Depreciation ratio: 446.63/427.52 = 1.04x (FY26) — capex is barely keeping pace
  with depreciation, consistent with management's own statement that capacity utilisation
  sits at 50-60% and further capex is not currently required (MD&A, AR FY2025-26, p.53).
- **M&A spend:** Rs0 in FY26 (the Rs83.78mn "Payment towards acquisition of business" line
  is a POSITIVE/inflow adjustment in FY26, i.e. a working-capital-linked earn-out true-up,
  not new M&A spend); Rs1,158.80mn in FY25 (the Altek acquisition).
- **Financing flows:** Consolidated net financing outflow -Rs1,875.67mn (FY26) vs
  -Rs587.28mn (FY25) — FY26 saw the company aggressively pay down debt (repayments of
  current borrowings Rs1,522.32mn, non-current Rs813.62mn) using the IPO cash pile built

- **Cash pile trend:** Consolidated cash and cash equivalents Rs800.33mn (FY26) vs
  Rs471.17mn (FY25), but total cash+bank balances (including the FY25 escrow/FD balances

**Line walk (consolidated, YoY):** Revenue -17.0% (Rs15,196.26mn→Rs12,614.85mn); Cost of

evidence of pricing power; → EBITDA margin 10.1% reported / 10.3% normalised (company

**Phase 3 verdict: 🔴 Red Flag.** Primary financial statements independently confirm the

management directly on cash conversion before any valuation work proceeds — three of the

## PHASE 4: RISK FACTORS & MD&A

### 4A. Disclosed risks — real vs boilerplate
The MD&A carries a structured 10-category risk table (AR FY2025-26, p.55-57): Geopolitical
& Supply Chain, US Tariff & Trade Policy, Component Supply Chain, Customer Concentration,
Currency Risk, Technology Obsolescence, Talent & Attrition, Competition, Operational &
Execution, Financial/Liquidity. Most are REAL and specific (e.g., the 85-90% import
dependence figure, the named 700+ supplier base, the specific Citibank/corporate-guarantee
liquidity architecture implied but not fully spelled out under "Financial/Liquidity").
Some mitigation language is boilerplate-adjacent: "Net cash positive position maintained as
a core financial discipline" is listed as a Financial/Liquidity mitigant (p.57) while the
Group in fact carries a growing US-dollar term loan backed by a parent corporate guarantee
(2F) — technically true at the standalone/India level (borrowings are Rs NIL there) but
incomplete at the Group level where the risk actually sits.

### 4B. Missing risks
Risks that Phases 1-3 make evident but that do NOT appear, named as such, in the MD&A risk
table:
- **Cash conversion / working capital deterioration risk** — despite NWC days rising
  48→79→127→145 over four years (company's own chart, Board's Report p.27) and CFO negative
  in two of the last three years, there is no named "working capital" or "cash conversion"
  risk category in the MD&A's 10-category table. It is implicitly folded into
  "Operational & Execution" ("elevated working capital requirements from business
  complexity" appears only as an IMPACT bullet, not a named risk) — a real dilution of a
  risk this stage and Stage 1/2 both treat as the single most important fact in the file.
- **Related-party dependency risk** — the new Rs537.56mn subcontracting flow to the
  ultimate parent (2B) and the parent's Rs1,188.60mn corporate guarantee to the US
  subsidiary's lender (2F) are not named as a risk anywhere in the MD&A, despite meeting a
  reasonable definition of concentration/dependency risk on a single counterparty that is
  also the controlling shareholder.
- **Acquisition-integration / earn-out risk** — Altek's underperformance against its
  acquisition case (evidenced by the earn-out markdown, 2H) is not named as a risk despite
  goodwill of Rs718.77mn sitting on that same CGU.
- **Leadership transition risk** — CEO and CFO both changed within the year (Phase 5); no
  succession/key-person risk is named in the MD&A risk table, though the Board's Report
  does address succession planning in general governance terms (p.93).
- **DSCR / debt-service risk** — despite the company's own Note 35 showing DSCR at 0.62x
  (whatever its year-over-year comparability issues), no debt-service-specific risk is
  named; "Financial/Liquidity" focuses on cash position and credit lines, not on
  debt-service coverage.

The likely reason for these omissions is structural rather than deliberate concealment: the
risk table is written at the India-standalone/Group-strategic level and does not
systematically walk through Schedule III ratio deterioration or RPT concentration as
distinct risk lines. That said, management omission of a metric the company itself
discloses and features prominently in its own MD&A financial-ratio section (NWC days,
DSCR) from the separate risk-factor table is itself a signal worth naming.
