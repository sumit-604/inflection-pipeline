# MPS Limited (MPSLTD) — Business Narrative

## Business understanding

MPS Limited sells outsourced knowledge services to publishers, educators, and enterprises. Research Solutions is about 60 percent of revenue. It runs the production line behind scientific, technical, and medical journals: typesetting, copy editing, peer review management, and the hosting platforms (HighWire, DigiCore Pro) publishers use to put content online. A publisher cannot drop this work easily because it sits inside the daily flow of moving an article from author to reader. Education Solutions is about 27 percent of revenue. It supplies content, assessment, and, since the Unbound Medicine buy, a point of care medical knowledge subscription that hospitals and nursing schools use at the bedside. Corporate Learning is about 13 percent of revenue. It builds and delivers managed training programmes for enterprises on a project basis.

The customers are STM publishers, education publishers, hospitals and nursing institutions, and corporate training buyers. The annual report names no individual customers. The top customer is 17.3 percent of consolidated revenue and the top 15 are 58.1 percent, so concentration is real but easing at group level. Standalone concentration is higher, with the top three at 39 percent. Switching costs are high in Research because the work is embedded in the publisher's own production system.

Present demand comes from the volume of research being published and the money spent digitising education. Global STM journal and article output, about 3.5 million articles a year, drives Research production demand. K-12 and higher education digital procurement drives Education. Hospital and nursing point of care tool adoption drives the Unbound subscription. Enterprise L&D budgets drive Corporate Learning.

Demand should grow with more articles published each year, research integrity and open access mandates like Plan S, wider point of care tool adoption, and AI pushing fragmented work toward specialist providers. These forward drivers are checkable against outside signals: STM Association output reports, HolonIQ EdTech trackers, HIMSS adoption surveys, and the quarterly disclosures of the named peers Datamatics, eClerx, Indegene, and NIIT MTS.

The moat sits in Research, not across the whole company. Research has embedded customer relationships and gains from industry consolidation toward a few technical suppliers, the two best anchored advantages the scan found. Education is moderate, with a recurring subscription that is still small and unproven on economics. Corporate Learning has no moat. It declined in FY26, its goodwill was impaired, and it runs project to project with low predictability. The BridgeAI and AR VR leg inside MPS Europa AG is small, shrinking, and loss making, so it carries no advantage either.

## Knowledge services compounder funding growth with debt

MPS Limited earned Rs 768 Cr of consolidated revenue in FY26 and reported record EPS of Rs 102. Strip a one time positive exceptional of about Rs 8 Cr and underlying EPS was closer to Rs 97 to 98, below the Rs 100 the company celebrated. EBITDA has compounded about 17 percent a year since FY21. ROCE was 38 percent and return on net worth 32 percent, funded almost entirely from internal cash until this year.

The company is repositioning as an AI first knowledge firm and rolling up smaller businesses. It closed Liberate Group and Unbound Medicine inside FY26 and pruned the AJE editing business down on purpose. Five AI products were said to reach production during the year. Revenue is growing faster than headcount, up 20.4 percent against 2.7 percent more staff in Q1 FY27, which is the clearest sign the AI throughput claim is real. The AR discloses no separate revenue or margin for any of these products, so the economics stay unproven.

The backward scorecard reads 62 of 160, an AVERAGE grade, but most of the shortfall is missing data, not weak business. One whole block scored zero for a shareholding file that was not in front of that stage, and capex and trade payables were absent for all ten years. Returns scored 20 of 20 and the balance sheet 19 of 20. Median ROCE over the period was above 30 percent.

Cash conversion stayed healthy, with consolidated operating cash flow to profit of 1.14 times in FY26 and a ten year cumulative near 1.0 times. Liquidity weakened sharply the same year. The consolidated current ratio fell from 1.97 to 1.45 and standalone from 4.67 to 2.53, both past the company's own 25 percent materiality line. Two things drove it: the first external borrowing in the company's history, a Rs 40 Cr ICICI term loan to fund Unbound, and a one time jump in labour code liabilities. Separately, the bad debt allowance grew 35.4 percent against receivables up 15.0 percent, a credit quality slip sitting inside the AJE subsidiary and invisible in the standalone accounts.

Goodwill now sits at 40 percent of consolidated assets and 63 percent of net worth after three deals in two years. One purchase price allocation was already cut 28 percent within its measurement window. A full year of amortisation on the new intangibles is still to hit FY27 earnings.

Management delivered on five of seven dated promises and walked away from one deal on ethics, which supports the record. Against that, the Unbound margin ramp slipped from an early 20s promise to 18 to 20 percent delivered, a February pledge to keep paying dividends was reversed in May, and a segment mix target changed quietly from 40:40:20 to 55:35:10. The credibility read is a B, and one verifier argues for B minus given the H2 earnings decline behind the record EPS.

Peers complicate the growth story more than they confirm it. All four named comparables grew faster in FY26 than MPS's roughly 13 percent organic pace: Datamatics 15 percent, eClerx 23 percent, Indegene 24 percent, NIIT MTS 18 percent. Three of them quantify 2 to 3.5 percent a year of wage inflation as a margin drag that MPS's own AI margin story never nets out. Demand across the set is split, not a shared boom, with Indegene accelerating while Datamatics decelerated sharply.

The market MPS actually sells into is large. A conservative independent estimate puts the addressable market near Rs 2.5 lakh Cr, giving roughly 148 times current revenue as headroom, a MASSIVE runway class. Management's own claim of more than USD 800 bn is about 30 times that honest figure and reads as inflated. The three year revenue path the sizing implies is about 23 percent a year, and it leans on buying companies at the recent pace, which the first ever debt draw suggests is now constrained by financing, not by capacity.
