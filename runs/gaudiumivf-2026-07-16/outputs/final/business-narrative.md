# IVF growth undercut by cash and price

Gaudium IVF runs 7 fertility hubs and 28 spokes across 9 states, built around founder clinician Dr Manika Khanna and the Gaudium brand. FY26 revenue was Rs 104 Cr, PAT Rs 24 Cr, and the shares trade at 41 times trailing earnings and about 32 times one year forward at Rs 137. IVF treatment is 64% of revenue, a pharmacy trading subsidiary is 31%, and a single women's health hospital is 4%.

The transition is a hub build out paid for with IPO cash. The company raised about Rs 165 Cr in February 2026 and earmarked Rs 50 Cr to take hubs from 7 toward 26 by FY29, a plan of 19 new centres phased 10, 8 and 1 across FY27 to FY29. Revenue grew 48% in FY26 and compounded 33% a year over three years. As of 31 March 2026 none of the Rs 50 Cr hub capex had been spent.

Gate 0 scored AVERAGE. The core score is 63 out of 100 with 3 moats confirmed, downgraded one tier for holding only four years of audited history since the company listed in February 2026. Cash generation is the weak block at 5 out of 20. Working capital days rose from 11 in FY23 to 156 in FY26, trade receivables went from 2% to 51% of revenue, and cash from operations covered only 30% of FY26 profit. Receivable days sit near 187 against 105 to 115 at HCG and a prepaid model at Kaya.

Return on capital is genuinely high once idle cash comes out. Reported FY26 ROCE fell to 20% from 54%, but about Rs 81 Cr of unspent IPO money sits in the denominator; strip it and operating ROCE is about 37%, in line with the standalone core near 39%. The core IVF business earns a 51% standalone EBITDA margin. The consolidated margin fell from 45% to 36% only because low margin pharmacy trading grew from under 1% to 31% of revenue.

Management earns a C credibility grade on a maiden call. Every headline FY26 figure ties to the audited filing. Against that, a zero bad debt claim was contradicted by a Rs 32 lakh expected credit loss booked in the same week, the receivables problem surfaced only under analyst questioning, and the international patient mix stated at 25 to 30% is not supported by FY24 and FY25 forex earnings recorded as nil. No multi quarter delivery record exists to grade yet.

Governance carries a CONCERN verdict. Combined company and promoter contingent tax exposure is about Rs 50 Cr, roughly 85% of net worth, from a 2022 survey alleging unaccounted cash from surrogacy patients. Rs 9 Cr was placed in FY25 into two unrelated Mumbai entities with no disclosed rationale, two independent directors resigned within 12 weeks each in the pre IPO window, and the promoter who is the largest related party beneficiary and the sole IPO seller sits on the audit committee and chairs the risk committee. Against that sits real professionalisation: an external CFO from the KPMG network, an external company secretary, and a credentialed independent board, all added in the year before listing.

Peers confirm the doubts, not the story. HCG runs receivable days at 105 to 115 and international mix near 3.5%; Rainbow reports international revenue near 1.7% and discloses its own IVF vertical every quarter with no AI embryo mention. None corroborate the 150 day collection cycle as normal, the 25 to 30% international mix, or the AI embryology edge. Rainbow does show a fast build rate has precedent, and also that 9 to 12 month execution delays are routine.

The market runway is real and not the binding constraint. India IVF is a Rs 14,600 to 19,700 Cr opportunity growing about 13% a year, with Gaudium at 3.5% of its served market and roughly 28 times revenue headroom. The demand implied growth of 31% sits above the 25% the thesis needs, so capacity and cash quality, not demand, are what can break it. Only 5 embryologists run the whole network today with no disclosed hiring plan tied to 19 new centres.

Valuation lands on WATCHLIST, not a buy at Rs 137. The four pillar exit multiple is 22 times on the additive track, built from a 24x ROCE base, a 0.80x cash multiplier, a +2x growth premium and a +1x strategic premium; the conservative reverse rate track sets a floor near 13.5 times. On one year forward earnings and a 25% base EPS path, the base target is about Rs 181 by FY29, a return near 10% a year from CMP, so the Hurdle is STOP on both base and bull. The entry zone is Rs 90 to 93 with a margin of safety price of Rs 74. That entry sits about 18% above the Rs 79 IPO price and about 32% below CMP, reachable on a normal drawdown rather than a market unlikely level.

The devil's advocate left the thesis weakened but alive. The strongest counter is that the 37% ROCE and the +2x growth premium cannot both hold in time: deploying the Rs 81 Cr into half built hubs pushes ROCE back toward the reported 20% during the very window the growth is booked. Cash drag may be structural rather than indeterminate, and the tax allegation about unaccounted patient cash rhymes with the receivables opacity, so governance risk and cash quality may be one problem wearing two labels. Two prints would turn the name toward a buy nearer CMP: receivable days falling as the doubled upfront payment and late transfer fee bite, and a second clean quarter lifting credibility toward B.
