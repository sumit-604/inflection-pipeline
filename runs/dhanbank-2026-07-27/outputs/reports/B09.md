# B09 — TAM / SAM / SOM Market Sizing: Dhanlaxmi Bank Ltd (DHANBANK)
Run date: 2026-07-27 | Stage 9, pipeline mode | Model: claude-sonnet-5

## SECTOR REFRAME (per stage brief)
DHANBANK is a scheduled commercial bank (SCB), not a product company. This
report sizes the market in banking terms: the addressable CREDIT (advances)
opportunity, primarily (i) the India bank-addressable gold-loan lending
market, and (ii) the Kerala/South-India systemwide deposit & SME/retail
credit market — not a product-unit TAM. TAM/SAM/SOM figures below are
addressable ADVANCES-BOOK size (₹ Cr); the SOM-implied revenue/NII CAGR is
derived separately by relating SOM book growth to the bank's own current
advances base, per the injected caveat.

## SECTION 1: MARKET DEFINITION

### 1A Precise boundaries
- **Product scope (included):** Secured retail lending with gold loans as
  the core, scalable product (38% of book as of Dec-25, source: ICRA rating
  note 141787, p.1); other retail (housing, personal), agri, MSME/SME, and
  small corporate lending; CASA and term deposits including NRI deposits;
  treasury income (15.4% of revenue per B04). Product scope **excludes**:
  large/wholesale corporate banking, investment banking, wealth management,
  asset management, insurance underwriting (only bancassurance referral fee
  income, immaterial) — Dhanlaxmi has neither the balance-sheet capacity
  (Tier-I ₹1,223 Cr, Dec-25, ICRA p.2) nor the franchise for these.
- **Geographic scope:** South India is the realistic operating geography.
  261 branches as of Dec-25, >80% in Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana,
  Kerala and Karnataka; Kerala alone has 147 branches = 56% of the network
  (ICRA rating note 141787, p.1). The bank is licensed pan-India (AR FY25,
  "261 branches spread across 14 States and 2 Union Territories," p.12) but
  has not opened new branches in years (regulatory restrictions history,
  ICRA p.3) — physical distribution capacity is a South-India franchise.
- **Customer scope:** Retail (gold-loan borrowers, NRI depositors — Kerala
  has deep Gulf-remittance linkage, explicitly flagged by ICRA as a credit
  risk channel, p.1), MSME, agri, small corporate. Excludes large corporate/
  institutional lending (RWA-constrained) and private/HNI banking.
- **Channel scope:** Branch-led (261 branches, 282 ATMs, 17 BCs, Dec-25,
  ICRA p.1); nascent digital (Dhan Smart app, DCAMS 2.0, UPI features, AR
  FY25 pp.13-14) but not a scaled digital-acquisition engine.
- **Price segment:** Mass retail / small-business, not premium/private
  banking.
- **Explicit inclusions:** India bank-addressable organised gold-loan
  market; Kerala/South-India systemwide bank deposits and credit; NRI
  deposit pool.
- **Explicit exclusions:** National wholesale/corporate banking; non-South
  India retail expansion (no disclosed plan); investment banking/wealth
  management/AMC revenue; unsecured lending scale-up (no meaningful
  franchise disclosed).

## SECTION 2: TAM ESTIMATION, MULTIPLE METHODS

### Method 1 — Top-down: India bank-addressable organised gold-loan market
- Organised gold-loan market (banks + NBFCs): ICRA projects it crossing
  ₹10,00,000 Cr (₹10 lakh Cr) in the current financial year (FY26) and
  reaching ₹15,00,000 Cr (₹15 lakh Cr), a year ahead of prior schedule
  (ICRA special comment, Oct-2025, id=6582; Business Standard, "Gold loan
  market to hit Rs 15 trillion in FY26 ahead of schedule: ICRA," Dec-2025).
  Both dated within 12 months of run date — not stale.
- Overall organised gold loans expanded at ~26% CAGR in FY24-FY25 (ICRA,
  same source).
- Bank share of the *organised* segment (as opposed to NBFC share): a
  CRIF High Mark-based industry report (via Zeebiz, "PSU banks tighten grip
  on India's gold loan market as NBFCs drive volume growth," reporting
  Q4FY26 sourcing-value shares) puts PSU banks at 37% and NBFCs at 44% of
  sourcing value in Q4FY26; residual (private banks + others) ≈ 19%. Total
  **banks (PSU+private) ≈ 56%** of the organised gold-loan pool.
- Calculation:
  - Conservative (current-year organised pool, bank share): ₹10,00,000 Cr
    × 0.56 = **₹5,60,000 Cr**
  - Realistic (FY26 exit-rate organised pool, bank share): ₹15,00,000 Cr ×
    0.56 = **₹8,40,000 Cr**

### Method 2 — Bottom-up: South-India systemwide bank credit (Dhanlaxmi's
realistic footprint, all products)
- Kerala systemwide (SLBC) FY26 close: deposits ₹10,62,694 Cr (+12% YoY),
  advances ₹7,74,507 Cr (+13% YoY); MSME credit ₹1,05,545 Cr (+11%),
  agri credit ₹1,73,931 Cr (+12%), NRK/NRI deposits ₹3,24,636 Cr (+11%)
  (Outlook Business, "Kerala Banking Sector Posts Healthy Growth," reporting
  SLBC review, current within 12 months).
- Dhanlaxmi has 147 of 261 branches (56%) in Kerala and the remaining ~24%
  of its network spread across TN/AP/Telangana/Karnataka (ICRA p.1: >80%
  combined in these five states). Grossing Kerala up pro-rata to the other
  four southern states by branch-count ratio (~24/56 = 0.43x of Kerala):
  - South-India-footprint systemwide advances ≈ ₹7,74,507 Cr × 1.43 ≈
    **₹11,08,000 Cr** (rounded ₹11,00,000 Cr)
  - This is a rough, branch-count-weighted extrapolation (Kerala banking
    penetration is higher than the southern average given NRI remittance
    density), flagged **Medium confidence**.

### Method 3 — Peer aggregation: regional/old-private-bank peer set +
informal-sector overlay
- South Indian Bank: gross advances ₹1,00,274 Cr (FY25-26 Q4 results,
  PNI press release, "South Indian Bank Achieves Highest Ever Net Profit…")
- Karur Vysya Bank: net advances ₹84,491 Cr (as of 31-Mar-2025, Business
  Standard, "Karur Vysya Bank Q4 net profit up 13%…", May-2025)
- CSB Bank: net advances ₹28,639 Cr (as of 31-Dec-2024, CSB Bank business
  update, cited via search of csb.bank.in disclosures)
- Dhanlaxmi Bank: net advances ₹13,912 Cr (Dec-2025, ICRA p.1)
- Peer-group organised sum ≈ ₹1,00,274 + ₹84,491 + ₹28,639 + ₹13,912 =
  **₹2,27,316 Cr**
- Grossing up for informal/cooperative/local-moneylender competition in the
  same regional retail-secured-lending segment: applying the low end of the
  standard India informal-sector range (30-60%; using 40%, conservative
  choice given this is bank-comparable credit not pure retail gold):
  ₹2,27,316 Cr ÷ (1-0.40) ≈ **₹3,78,860 Cr** (rounded ₹3,80,000 Cr)
- This method is structurally the **narrowest** of the three — it only
  captures banks of Dhanlaxmi's own peer tier and excludes SBI/large
  private banks (HDFC, ICICI, Axis, Federal Bank) which also operate
  heavily in the same Kerala/South-India geography. It is not a true market
  ceiling; it is closer to "the pool of business realistically contestable
  among like-sized regional players."

### Triangulation table

| Method | Estimate (₹ Cr) | Confidence | Staleness |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Top-down, bank-gold-loan TAM (India) | ₹5,60,000 (conservative) – ₹8,40,000 (realistic) | High | Not stale (Oct-Dec 2025 sources) |
| 2. Bottom-up, South-India systemwide credit | ~₹11,00,000 | Medium (branch-weighted extrapolation) | Not stale (FY26 SLBC data) |
| 3. Peer aggregation + informal overlay | ~₹3,80,000 | Medium (narrow peer definition) | Not stale (FY25/26 results) |

**Divergence is material (₹3.8L Cr to ₹11.0L Cr) and is not averaged.**
Reason: the three methods measure different things — Method 1 is a
national product ceiling (gold loans only, bank share only); Method 2 is a
full-product, full-geography systemwide ceiling (all banks, all products,
in Dhanlaxmi's footprint states); Method 3 is a narrow peer-comparable
ceiling excluding large banks that also compete in the same geography.

**Conservative estimate = ₹3,80,000 Cr (Method 3, per conservative-bias
rule: take the lower, most narrowly-matched-to-actual-competitive-set
figure).**
**Realistic estimate = ₹8,40,000 Cr (Method 1 upper bound — most
rigorously sourced, and tied directly to the product actually driving
Dhanlaxmi's recent growth, gold loans).**

Management's claim vs conservative estimate: **NOT APPLICABLE** — no
management claim exists (Section 1B). mgmt_claim_ratio = NOT FOUND.

## SECTION 3: SAM & SOM

### 3A SAM — five filters applied to realistic TAM (₹8,40,000 Cr, Method 1)

1. **Product fit filter:** TAM is already gold-loan/bank-specific — retain
   100%. → ₹8,40,000 Cr
2. **Geography filter:** restrict to the five southern states where
   Dhanlaxmi has >80% of its branches. Five southern states (TN, AP,
   Telangana, Kerala, Karnataka) account for ~75% of total (bank+NBFC,
   organised+unorganised) gold-loan outstanding nationally — ₹13,94,000 Cr
   of ₹18,60,000 Cr total, per RBI-data-based industry reporting (source:
   gold-loan market coverage citing RBI data on regional concentration,
   Jan-2026). Applying 75%: ₹8,40,000 Cr × 0.75 = **₹6,30,000 Cr**
3. **Channel + capability filter (combined):** Dhanlaxmi's 261-branch,
   capital-constrained franchise cannot realistically address the full
   South-India bank-gold-loan pool — large banks and NBFCs with deeper
   distribution and digital-lending capability hold most of it. Applying a
   40% realistic-reach factor (judgment cross-checked against Dhanlaxmi's
   0.2% national loan-book share, ICRA p.3, and its static branch count for
   years): ₹6,30,000 Cr × 0.40 = **₹2,52,000 Cr**

**SAM = ₹2,52,000 Cr. SAM as % of TAM (realistic) = 2,52,000 / 8,40,000 =
30.0%.**

### 3B SOM at 3 and 5 years
- Current SAM share = Dhanlaxmi net advances (₹13,912 Cr, Dec-25, ICRA
  p.1) ÷ SAM (₹2,52,000 Cr) = **5.52%**
- Share-gain assumption: **normal band (1-2pp in 3 years)** applied at
  the middle-upper end (+1.5pp) given (a) no demonstrated aggressive
  execution capacity — branch network flat for years, weak RoA
  (0.40-0.42%), history of PCA-era book contraction 2011-2019 (ICRA p.3),
  and (b) the gold-loan segment's unorganised share (~65% nationally) is
  >40%, which per the share-gain rules permits *somewhat* faster
  formalisation-driven gains than a fully mature market — balancing these,
  1.5pp (not the low end, not aggressive) is used for 3 years, and a
  cumulative +3.0pp (still within normal-to-moderate band, not the
  aggressive 3-5pp reserved for demonstrated capacity/competitor-exit
  scenarios) for 5 years.
  - 3yr SAM share = 5.52% + 1.5pp = 7.02% → SOM_3yr = 7.02% × ₹2,52,000 Cr
    = **₹17,690 Cr**
  - 5yr SAM share = 5.52% + 3.0pp = 8.52% → SOM_5yr = 8.52% × ₹2,52,000 Cr
    = **₹21,470 Cr**
- **Implied advances/NII CAGR** (vs current base ₹13,912 Cr):
  - 3yr: (17,690 / 13,912)^(1/3) - 1 = **8.34%**
  - 5yr: (21,470 / 13,912)^(1/5) - 1 = **9.07%**
- **Cross-check against actuals:** these implied CAGRs are deliberately
  *below* Dhanlaxmi's recent reported book growth — 25.68% YoY to Dec-2025
  and 18.39% in FY25 (ICRA p.3) — because that recent growth is
  substantially a **gold-price effect** (gold-loan share of book rose from
  31% to 38% between Mar-25 and Dec-25 on rising gold prices, ICRA p.1,
  which lifts collateral value and permissible loan size against the same
  physical gold, not a pure customer/volume/share gain). The SOM-implied
  CAGR (8.3%/9.1%) approximates the underlying organic, market-share-based
  growth once the gold-price tailwind is stripped out, and sits close to
  Dhanlaxmi's long-run net-advances CAGR of 12.48% (Mar-2019 to Dec-2025,
  ICRA p.1) — itself partly price-boosted in the most recent two years.
  **This is the FORMAL handoff to stage 11**: use 8.3%/9.1% as the
  market-share-based revenue-growth cross-check, not the 18-26% recent
  reported rates, which are not repeatable without continued gold-price
  appreciation.

### 3C Capacity cross-check
No B07 (Emerging Moat) capex-embedded-growth figure was provided in the
injected inputs for this run (physical capex is not the relevant capacity
constraint for a bank in any case). The bank-equivalent capacity
constraint is **capital adequacy** (Tier-I/CRAR headroom to grow
risk-weighted assets), and it was substituted here.
- Incremental advances needed by Yr5 to hit SOM_5yr: ₹21,470 Cr − ₹13,912
  Cr = ₹7,558 Cr
- Illustrative blended RWA density: NOT FOUND precisely disclosed by
  segment; used as an estimate for this cross-check only (flagged, not a
  hard financial fact) — ~50%, reflecting a mix of low-RWA gold loans
  (~25%, ICRA p.1 notes "lower RWA density of gold loans" as supportive of
  capital) and higher-RWA retail/SME/corporate exposure.
  → Incremental RWA ≈ ₹7,558 Cr × 0.50 ≈ ₹3,779 Cr
- Target Tier-I ratio to maintain a rating-safe buffer (ICRA flags
  downgrade risk if capital cushion falls within 2pp of regulatory Tier-I/
  CRAR minimums, p.4): ~13%, in line with Dhanlaxmi's current 14.47%
  (Dec-25, ICRA p.2).
  → Additional Tier-I capital required ≈ ₹3,779 Cr × 0.13 ≈ **₹491 Cr**
  over 5 years
- Organic capital generation capacity: net profit ₹67 Cr (FY25), ₹59 Cr
  (9M FY26, annualising to ~₹79 Cr), no dividend paid (AR FY25 p.13: "the
  Board of Directors...has not recommended any dividend"), so ~100%
  retention. Five-year cumulative retained earnings, even with modest
  profit growth, plausibly falls in the **₹350-500 Cr** range — roughly
  in the same order as the ₹491 Cr requirement, but with limited margin of
  safety and high sensitivity to the (unverified) RWA-density assumption.
- **Read:** ICRA states explicitly that "its ability to meaningfully
  improve its market position will depend on the raising of fresh capital
  in the future" (p.2) — i.e., management/the rating agency itself does
  not treat organic accruals as sufficient for meaningful scale-up. No
  further capital raise beyond the completed Feb-2025 rights issue
  (₹297.5 Cr) is currently disclosed/committed.
  **capacity_check: tight / marginal gap of ~₹0-150 Cr Tier-I capital by
  Yr5 depending on RWA-density and profit-growth assumptions; the SOM is
  the mildly optimistic side of the ledger absent a further capital raise,
  which is not currently committed.**

### 4C Market structure
- **Gold loan segment:** Fragmented at the top (PSU banks 37%, NBFCs 44%,
  private banks/others ~19% of Q4FY26 sourcing value) but consolidating
  share-wise toward NBFCs and away from PSU banks (PSU share fell from 45%
  in Q4FY25 to 37% in Q4FY26, per CRIF-based reporting). Organised vs
  unorganised: 35%/65% nationally, organised share rising (formalising).
- **South-India regional banking:** Fragmented among SBI, large private
  banks (Federal, HDFC, ICICI, Axis) and a tier of small/regional private
  banks (South Indian Bank ~₹1,00,274 Cr advances, Karur Vysya ~₹84,491 Cr,
  CSB ~₹28,639 Cr, Dhanlaxmi ~₹13,912 Cr) — Dhanlaxmi is the smallest of
  this regional peer tier by a wide margin.
- **Price vs differentiation:** Gold loans are largely a distribution/
  turnaround-time/price competition (NBFCs winning on speed and reach);
  Dhanlaxmi has no disclosed differentiated technology or underwriting
  edge (B04: 261 branches, standard retail/gold/SME model, niche
  Devaswom Board/Sabarimala relationship only).
- **Entries/exits:** No new entrants or exits observed in the search;
  incumbents are simply reallocating share (PSU → NBFC/private).

## SECTION 5: SUMMARY & RUNWAY

### 5A Funnel
```
TAM (realistic, India bank-addressable gold-loan market): ₹8,40,000 Cr
   -> geography filter (South India, ~75%):                ₹6,30,000 Cr
      -> channel/capability filter (~40% realistic reach):  ₹2,52,000 Cr  = SAM
         -> current Dhanlaxmi share of SAM (5.52%):         ₹13,912 Cr    = current book
            -> SOM 3yr (7.02% of SAM):                      ₹17,690 Cr
            -> SOM 5yr (8.52% of SAM):                      ₹21,470 Cr
```

### 5B Runway assessment
- Revenue headroom, book-basis (SAM ÷ current advances): ₹2,52,000 Cr ÷
  ₹13,912 Cr = **18.1x**
- Revenue headroom, literal schema basis (SAM ÷ current total income of
  ₹1,489.09 Cr, FY25, AR p.12): ₹2,52,000 Cr ÷ ₹1,489 Cr = **~169x** — this
  figure is presented for schema completeness but is not economically
  meaningful for a leveraged deposit-taking institution; the book-basis
  18.1x is the informative number and is what is carried into the YAML.
- TAM growth rate: ~26% CAGR (organised gold-loan market, FY24-FY25, ICRA)
  — flagged as substantially price-driven in the most recent period, not
  pure volume/penetration growth.
- Company CAGR vs TAM: Dhanlaxmi's long-run net-advances CAGR (Mar-2019 to
  Dec-2025) is 12.48% (ICRA p.3) — below the market's recent ~26%, i.e.
  Dhanlaxmi has broadly been **riding a smaller/slower trajectory than the
  market**, not gaining share, over most of that period (the PCA-recovery
  years, 2019-2023, were flat-to-slow; the recent acceleration to
  25.68% YoY, Dec-2025, is gold-price-aided, not sustained share gain).
- Years to saturate SAM at SOM-implied 5yr CAGR (9.07%): ln(18.1)/ln(1.0907)
  ≈ 2.90/0.0868 ≈ **~33 years** — i.e., on pure book-size math the ceiling
  is decades away.

### 5C Runway classification: **MODERATE**
The raw headroom ratio (18.1x) and decades-long saturation horizon would,
taken alone, suggest STRONG or even MASSIVE. That reading is rejected here
because the real-world constraints that cap *realistic* capture are
binding and structural, not incidental:
1. Capital constraint — capacity check (3C) shows growth beyond the
   current trajectory needs further dilutive capital raises; none is
   committed.
2. Weak profitability (RoA 0.40-0.42%, cost/income ~83-85%) limits the
   speed at which capital can be self-generated to fund growth.
3. A meaningful share of recent book growth (FY25-9M FY26) is a gold-price
   effect, not durable share gain — the market-share-based SOM CAGR
   (8.3%/9.1%) is roughly a third of the recently reported book growth
   rate (18-26%).
4. Branch network has been static for years and the bank's national loan-
   book share is 0.2% (ICRA p.3) with no disclosed expansion plan.
This combination — large nominal ratio, small realistic executable
capture — is precisely the MODERATE band, and the classification flags the
gold-price-tailwind risk explicitly rather than letting the ratio imply a
rosier runway class.

### 5D SAM expansion levers actually being pursued
- NRI/Gulf deposit franchise: "Dhanam Global Connect" NRE savings product,
  explicitly "to increase the HNI-NRI portfolio" (AR FY25, p.13). Potential
  addition: **NOT FOUND** (unquantified in AR).
- Digital account opening/servicing (DCAMS 2.0, Dhan Smart app relaunch
  with UPI, AR FY25 pp.13-14) — could lower channel-filter drag over time
  but no quantified target disclosed. Potential addition: **NOT FOUND**.
- Corporate/MSME relationship focus "for a strong CASA ratio" (AR FY25,
  p.13, Board's Report strategy section) — could expand the customer-filter
  slice of SAM; no quantified target. Potential addition: **NOT FOUND**.
Because none of these levers carries a management-quantified addition,
the SAM used in this report (₹2,52,000 Cr) does **not** include any
upside from these levers; if pursued successfully they would raise SAM
and/or the SOM share-gain assumption above the "normal" band used here.

### 5E Output card
At **8.3%** (3yr) / **9.1%** (5yr) revenue/NII CAGR implied by SOM, with a
roughly flat-to-mildly-improving margin trajectory (net margin on total
income 10.5% FY25 → 10.8% 9M FY26; RoA 0.40% → 0.42%, ICRA p.4), the
earnings growth embedded here is approximately **8-11% CAGR** (revenue
growth plus a small positive margin drift), which **does not clearly
support** the current valuation of **~12.3-13.1x P/E** (share price ₹33.5-
₹34, market cap ₹1,346-1,348 Cr, as of Jun-Jul 2026, multiple online
brokerage/data-terminal sources — treat as directional, not primary
anchored financial data) for a bank with RoA of 0.4-0.5% and a capital-
constrained growth path. The gap between the recently *reported* growth
rate (18-26%, gold-price-aided) and the *market-share-based* SOM growth
rate (8-11%) is the key valuation risk this stage hands to Stage 11: if the
market is pricing the recent reported rate as sustainable, that
extrapolation is not supported by the underlying TAM/SAM mechanics here.
