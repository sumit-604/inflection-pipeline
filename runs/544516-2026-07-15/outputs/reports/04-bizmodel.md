## SECTION 1: THE BUSINESS MODEL IN PLAIN ENGLISH

### 1A. One-line description
Airfloa is a Chennai-based contract manufacturer that makes and fits the "guts" of a railway coach — seats, AC ducting, doors, roller blinds, panels, windows, luggage racks — mostly for the Integral Coach Factory (ICF) arm of Indian Railways, and is now using the same precision-fabrication skillset to build simulators and structural parts for aerospace and defence customers (AR p.116-118, p.138).

### 1B. Money flow chain, by revenue stream

**Stream 1 — Railway rolling stock components & turnkey interior fit-out (64.54% of FY25 revenue, AR p.138)**
[Input: steel/aluminium sheet, FRP composites, foam, LED lighting, electricals sourced domestically (94.5% domestic FY25) and via imports (5.5%) (Minerva IPO Note p.2)] → [Airfloa cuts, welds, machines, paints and assembles components to railway-specific drawings across a 15-step production process — laser cutting, deburring, CNC machining, priming, painting, CMM inspection (AR p.129-130)] → [delivers individual components (seats, doors, panels) or full turnkey interior furnishing for a coach set] → [ICF, other coach factories (RCF/MCF), metro corporations (Chennai Metro/BEML), or private OEMs pay] → [payment is milestone/inspection-triggered against a tender-awarded work order, invoiced with ~150-250 day realization (AR p.117 receivable/payable days table)].

**Stream 2 — Aeronautical, Defence & Others (35.46% of FY25 revenue, up from 5.10% in FY24 and 0.44% in FY23, AR p.138)**
[Input: aerospace-grade aluminium alloys, titanium, composites, quality-certified per HAL/DRDO/CVRDE vendor norms] → [Airfloa machines and assembles simulator bodies (AMCA ground simulators for HAL), artillery/tank body structures (T90/HVF Avadi), and precision structural assemblies under a separate, more rigorous QA workflow — NDT, PDI, environmental simulation (AR p.131-135; Inv. Pres. slide 29-30)] → [delivers to HAL, DRDO establishments and defence primes] → [payment on inspection/PDI milestones under government/PSU procurement terms].

**Stream 3 — BBBS JV (optionality, pre-revenue)**
[Input: BBBS's electronic-warfare/AI-defence IP + Airfloa's manufacturing infrastructure] → [51:49 JV (Airfloa majority stake) industrializes EW systems, AI-driven autonomous defence platforms, advanced materials/nanotech products; IP resides with the JV] → [JV commercializes and sells to defence customers] → [payment mechanism not yet contracted; commercialization targeted mid-FY27 (Inv. Pres. slide 18)]. **This stream contributes NOT FOUND revenue as of this run** — it is a pipeline item, not a current revenue stream, and must not be conflated with the Aerospace & Defence segment already generating revenue.

Top-5 customer concentration was 82.48% and top-10 was 92.53% in FY25 (AR p.117, Operating Metrics table).

### 1E. The chai-stall-uncle version
Think of Airfloa like the trusted local tailor who has spent 20 years earning the right to stitch uniforms for the Indian Army — not anyone can just walk in and bid; you first need years of approvals, inspections and a track record. Once you're on that approved list, the Army (or here, Indian Railways) keeps giving you repeat work because switching tailors is a hassle and risky. But here's the catch: every single order still gets awarded to whoever quotes the lowest acceptable price, so being "approved" gets you into the room, it doesn't guarantee you a fat margin once you're there. And because the customer is a slow-paying government body, Airfloa has to buy the cloth (raw material) and stitch the whole uniform (build the coach part) months before it actually gets paid — so growth eats cash even while the order book grows. Airfloa is now trying to use the same tailoring skill to stitch a very different, much higher-margin garment: defence and aerospace parts, where it's still a newcomer.

## SECTION 2: INDUSTRY DYNAMICS & COMPETITIVE POSITION

### 2A. Five forces, plainly

| Force | Assessment | Helps / Hurts / Neutral |
|---|---|---|
| Competition intensity | AR names no specific SME-scale rail-component competitors; Minerva's peer set (Jupiter Wagons, Titagarh Rail Systems) is explicitly flagged as "not comparable given scale of peers" (Minerva IPO Note p.3) — meaning Airfloa competes in a fragmented sub-scale segment of ICF-approved vendors that is not disclosed by name | Neutral-to-Hurts (competitive intensity among approved vendors for each tender is real, per AR p.1850: "new entrants may also bid at lower margins") |
| Entry barriers | High — new vendors must clear a lengthy technical qualification process per component per railway production unit (AR p.881-887); Airfloa holds IRIS, AS9100D, ISO 9001, and vendor approvals from HAL, CVRDE, DRDO and IREPS (AR p.212-213) | Helps |
| Supplier power | Moderate — top-10 suppliers were 64.37% of FY25 material cost with no single supplier above ~14.4% (AR p.140); 94.5% of raw material sourced domestically (Minerva IPO Note p.2), reducing FX exposure but not price-inflation exposure | Neutral |
| Customer power / concentration | High and concerning — ICF alone is 53.60% of FY25 revenue, top-5 customers 82.48%, top-10 92.53% (AR p.117, p.139); customer power is amplified because Airfloa, not the customer, absorbs the working-capital burden | Hurts |
| Substitutes | Low direct substitution risk for qualified components (a coach cannot be built with an unqualified part), but Indian Railways can and does shift volumes among its approved vendor pool, and adjacent players (Jupiter Wagons, Titagarh) could move down-market into Airfloa's component niche given their scale | Neutral-to-Hurts |

### 2D. Industry lifecycle stage
Indian Railways rolling-stock manufacturing sits in a **structural capex upcycle, not early-stage or mature-decline**: FY26 railway capex is ₹3.0 lakh crore with a ₹16.7 lakh crore investment pipeline through 2031 (Inv. Pres. slide 35); the sector is described as targeting to become the 3rd-largest rail market globally within five years (AR p.114). Airfloa itself is in an **early scale-up stage within this cycle** — 27 years old but only recently crossed ₹300 Cr revenue (FY26) and is actively diversifying into a second industry (aerospace/defence) that is itself in early-stage build-out for the company (order-book share of just 3.99% versus 35.46% of FY25 revenue implies the defence pipeline is thin relative to current run-rate, AR p.127, p.138).

| Driver | Direction | Impact on Airfloa |

| Vande Bharat/Amrit Bharat/metro rollout | Positive/rising | Direct — Airfloa is an incumbent supplier on named platforms (AR p.118-124) |
| Defence indigenization (Atmanirbhar Bharat, ₹3 lakh cr defence production target by FY29) | Positive/rising | Indirect optionality via Aerospace & Defence segment and BBBS JV (Inv. Pres. slide 36) |
| Government fiscal consolidation pressure (8th Pay Commission from FY27) | Negative/uncertain | Could constrain railway capital budgets in the medium term (AR p.108, p.6406-6410) |
| Private-sector/PPP entry into railways | Mixed | Could bring new customers (private coach OEMs) but also new competitive dynamics |

### 4D. Highest-value questions for management

1. **Q: What is driving the EBITDA margin compression from 25.1% (FY25) to 20.1% (FY26)?**
   Reassures: A temporary mix effect from ramping up lower-margin defence/simulator orders that will normalize as the JV and defence book mature.
   Worries: Structural pricing pressure from tender competition or unhedged raw material cost inflation that will persist.

2. **Q: Why did standalone operating cash flow turn sharply negative (-₹57.45 Cr) in FY26 despite PAT growth of 52%?**
   Reassures: Timing — large government-linked receivables (trade receivables rose ₹86.4 Cr) are collectible, with the company already reporting 20% of FY26 receivables collected and 50-60% of the outstanding balance expected by end-June (Inv. Pres. slide 4).
   Worries: A chronic, worsening collection cycle from ICF/PSU customers that structurally caps free cash flow regardless of revenue growth.

3. **Q: What is Airfloa's committed capital exposure and revenue timeline for the BBBS JV, targeted for mid-FY27 commercialization?**
   Reassures: Capital commitment is modest, IP/upside is shared appropriately for a 51:49 structure, and near-term revenue is not assumed in guidance.
   Worries: Open-ended capital commitments to an unproven JV with IP ownership sitting at the JV level rather than with Airfloa.

4. **Q: How will the company reduce dependency on ICF, which was 53.6% of FY25 revenue?**
   Reassures: A visible, diversifying pipeline of non-ICF orders (private OEMs, metro, export) already growing faster than ICF orders.
   Worries: ICF share continuing to rise, increasing single-customer risk.

5. **Q: What caused employee attrition to spike to 81% in FY24, and is 40% in FY25 stabilizing or still elevated?**
   Reassures: Attrition is concentrated in lower-skill production/contract labor with minimal impact on core engineering/design retention.
   Worries: Loss of skilled machinists/design engineers threatening quality and delivery consistency.

6. **Q: With capacity utilization at 90% in FY26, what is the funded, dated plan for the new 14-acre facility, and how does any delay affect the ₹500 Cr FY27 revenue guidance?**
   Reassures: Land, clearances and machinery orders are already in place using IPO proceeds.
   Worries: Land acquisition, environmental clearance, or machinery delivery delays that cap growth just as demand visibility improves.

7. **Q: Given fixed-price/tender-based contracting, what protects margins against raw material cost inflation (steel, aluminium, composites)?**
   Reassures: Contracts include re-pricing mechanisms or short enough lead times that input cost risk is limited.
   Worries: Genuinely fixed-price, long-lead-time contracts with no escalation clause, leaving Airfloa fully exposed to commodity swings.
