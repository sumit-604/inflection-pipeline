## SECTION 1: MARKET DEFINITION

### 1A Precise boundaries

- **Product scope**: (i) RE Power Sales — solar, wind, hybrid and emerging BESS electricity sold
  under 15–25yr PPAs via group-captive/open-access/CTU/VPPA/EAPA structures to Commercial &
  Industrial (C&I) customers; (ii) RE Services — EPC (CAPEX/BOO for customer-owned assets), O&M,
  and carbon services. (B04-bizmodel.yaml revenue_streams).
- **Geographic scope**: India (all states with open-access regulations); overseas (4 countries) not
  separately sized, immaterial to consolidated revenue base.
- **Customer scope**: Commercial & Industrial corporates able to sign 15–25yr PPAs, group-captive
  minimum-26%-equity structures, or bilateral open-access contracts. Skews to investment-grade:
  "81% customers above AA category" (Inv. Pres. p.10, cited in B04).
  Excludes: residential rooftop, DISCOM-routed utility-scale tenders, agricultural/subsidised power.
- **Channel scope**: Open access, group captive, CTU-connected, VPPA/EAPA (virtual/energy-as-a-
  product agreements for hyperscalers). Excludes DISCOM-tendered utility-scale PPA supply (a
  different buyer and a different competitive set — Adani Green, NTPC Renewable, etc. compete there
  too, but that is not CleanMax's core channel).
- **Price segment**: Contracted long-tenor tariffs, currently ~₹3.57–3.59/kWh on newly-commissioned
  capacity trailing-12m, ~₹4.20/kWh operational-book average (Inv. Pres. p.27, via B04).
- **Explicit inclusions**: C&I renewable power sales (solar/wind/hybrid/BESS), RE Services (EPC+O&M)
  for C&I RE assets, carbon services.
- **Explicit exclusions**: Utility-scale/DISCOM RE, residential/rooftop-only, non-power carbon
  trading unrelated to CleanMax's own assets, overseas operations (not sized, data not disclosed).

## SECTION 2: TAM ESTIMATION, MULTIPLE METHODS

### Revenue-per-MW conversion factor (used across methods)

Derived, not directly disclosed, from CleanMax's own FY28 guidance: minimum 4.6 GW opex capacity
(as of 1-Apr-2027) → minimum ₹3,000 Cr run-rate EBITDA (Inv. Pres. p.11). Portfolio EBITDA margin
83–84% (Inv. Pres. p.10, via B04).

Revenue/MW = (3,000 ÷ 0.835) ÷ 4,600 = **₹0.78 Cr/MW/year** (run-rate, steady state).

Cross-check against B04 unit economics (Solar ₹50–55 Lakh/MWp EBITDA, Wind ₹100–110 Lakh/MW
EBITDA, 70/30 solar-wind mix per Inv. Pres. p.11 footnote): blended EBITDA/MW = 0.7×52.5 +
0.3×105 = ₹68.25 Lakh/MW → revenue/MW = 68.25 ÷ 0.835 = ₹81.7 Lakh/MW. Consistent (within 5%)
with the ₹78 Lakh/MW figure above. **Used: ₹0.78 Cr/MW/year.**

### Method 1 — Top-down (India C&I open-access RE capacity, CRISIL)

CRISIL: India's C&I open-access renewable capacity projected to reach **57 GW by FY28**, up from
**~40 GW by end-2026** (search snippet via taiyangnews.info, WebFetch to primary CRISIL/taiyangnews
page **blocked by egress proxy** — relied on the search-engine summary, which itself cites CRISIL;
logged as a search-skip). Cross-checked against Mercom India's directly-fetched data: cumulative
installed **solar-only** open access reached 36 GW by June-2026 (Mercom, dated), consistent in
order of magnitude with the ~40 GW all-technology (solar+wind+hybrid) CRISIL figure once wind/hybrid
open-access is added.

- Current (≈FY26, ~40 GW): 40,000 MW × ₹0.78 Cr/MW = **₹31,200 Cr**
- FY28 forward (57 GW): 57,000 MW × ₹0.78 Cr/MW = **₹44,460 Cr**
- Implied 2-year capacity CAGR: (57/40)^0.5 − 1 = **19.4%**

This is a **stock-based, near-certain, currently-being-built market** — the most defensible
near-term TAM anchor, not a theoretical ceiling.

### Method 2 — Bottom-up (full-penetration ceiling)

Addressable unit: India's total Commercial + Industrial electricity consumption.
CEIC/CEA (2025 vintage, secondary aggregator; not fetched from CEA primary report directly — logged
as a search-skip): Industry 655,562 GWh + Commercial 135,163 GWh = **790,725 GWh/year** = 790.725
TWh = 79,072.5 Cr units (1 TWh = 100 Cr kWh units).

At a conservative blended open-access-equivalent tariff of ₹3.80/kWh (below CleanMax's own
operational-book average of ₹4.20/kWh, per the CONSERVATIVE BIAS instruction):

Revenue at 100% penetration = 79,072.5 Cr units × ₹3.80 = **₹3,00,476 Cr (~₹3.0 lakh Cr)**.

At CleanMax's own 83.5% portfolio EBITDA margin, this implies an EBITDA pool of ≈ **₹2.51 lakh Cr**
at full (100%) penetration.

**This is a theoretical structural ceiling, not a usable 3–5yr TAM** — it assumes every unit of
India's industrial and commercial power converts to intermittent open-access RE, which grid
stability, storage economics and daytime-generation physics make impossible without a firm/BESS
layer far beyond today's cost curve. Held apart from the headline TAM figures below; used only to
sanity-check management's own ₹3 lakh Cr claim (see triangulation table).

### Method 3 — Peer revenue aggregation

| Peer | FY26 Revenue (₹ Cr) | Source | Comparability note |
|---|---|---|---|
| CleanMax (self) | 1,912.87 | Consolidated FY26 P&L | Pure-play C&I open-access IPP + services |
| ACME Solar Holdings | 2,507 | WebSearch, FY26 results (59.2% YoY growth) | Mixed utility-scale + C&I; not pure C&I |
| KPI Green Energy | 2,742 | WebSearch, FY26 results (56% YoY growth) | Gujarat-focused C&I + EPC + emerging green H2; partial comparability |
| Adani Green Energy | 11,602 | WebSearch, FY26 results (22% YoY growth) | **Excluded from sum** — overwhelmingly DISCOM/utility-scale PPA revenue, not C&I open-access; included here for scale reference only |

Sum of reasonably comparable pure-play/near-pure-play names (CleanMax + ACME + KPI Green) =
₹7,161.87 Cr. This is the **known organised-sector revenue** actually booked in FY26 by three
identifiable listed C&I-adjacent RE players. Add an estimated unorganised/private-player pool
(Amp Energy, Vibrant Energy, Serentica, O2 Power, CleanMax's own JV partners' captive share, and
numerous unlisted developers — no revenue disclosed for any): using the standard 30–60% India
unorganised-sector convention, apply the **low end (30%, conservative)**:

Estimated total organised+unorganised current market ≈ 7,161.87 ÷ (1 − 0.30) = **₹10,231 Cr**

This method reads far **below** Method 1's ₹31,200 Cr because it only captures revenue actually
booked by RE Power Sales IPPs and misses (a) EPC/services revenue booked by pure-EPC players not
listed, and (b) the large base of privately-held C&I RE IPPs with no disclosed financials — a
structural undercount, flagged **Low confidence**, kept as a floor check only.

### Method 4 — Import substitution: **Not applicable.** Domestic electricity generation has no
import-substitution dynamic in the conventional sense (India is not importing C&I power).

### Method 5 — Global benchmark (light-touch)

India's non-fossil installed capacity crossed 50% of total installed capacity in mid-2025, five
years ahead of its own 2030 NDC target (PIB, Dec-2025/press release), en route to a 500 GW
non-fossil target by 2030. No clean India-vs-China/global per-capita C&I-specific benchmark was
found in the searches run; this method is used for directional context only (structural tailwind,
Section 4A), not as a headline number. **Confidence: Low, informs direction only.**

**TAM conservative = ₹31,200 Cr** (Method 1, current). **TAM realistic = ₹44,460 Cr** (Method 1,
FY28 forward, same method, near-certain capacity trajectory).

Method 2's ceiling (~₹3.0 lakh Cr) and management's own "~₹3 lakh Cr addressable EBITDA pool"
claim are **within ~1.15x of each other** on a like-for-like full-penetration basis (my ₹2.51 lakh
Cr EBITDA-at-100%-penetration vs their ₹3 lakh Cr) — i.e., management's number is a **plausible
order-of-magnitude match for the theoretical ceiling**, but they present it without the 100%-
penetration caveat or a stated timeline, which is the "TAM = SAM = growth runway" dishonesty this
stage's own operating rules warn against. **Flagged.**

Against the conservative, buildable TAM (₹31,200 Cr), management's ₹3,00,000 Cr claim is a ratio of
**9.62x** — by the standard read (>2x likely inflated), this reads **inflated** when presented (as
management presents it) as a near-term opportunity rather than a 100%-penetration ceiling.

**Independent market-share cross-check** (against management's 12%→14% claim): CleanMax's FY26
year-end operational capacity was 3,088 MW (AR/Inv. Pres.); "on-books" contracted capacity including
under-execution was ~6,003–6,500 MW (Inv. Pres. p.9, p.13; AR CFO quote, "6.5 GW Total Portfolio").
Against the ~40 GW all-India C&I open-access base:
- Operational-only basis: 3,088 / 40,000 = **7.7%**
- Total on-books/contracted basis: 6,003–6,500 / 40,000 = **15.0–16.3%**

Management's claimed 12%→14% sits inside this bracket depending on which capacity base is used —
**plausible order of magnitude, but not independently pinned to a single verifiable definition**,
consistent with B06's "UNVERIFIABLE" finding. The unnamed source for the 14% figure remains a gap.

## SECTION 3: SAM & SOM

### 3A SAM (five filters applied to TAM realistic, ₹44,460 Cr)

| Filter | Cut | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Product fit | 95% retained | Core = solar/wind/hybrid/BESS + EPC/O&M; excludes adjacent technologies some peers pursue (e.g., KPI Green's green hydrogen), not in CleanMax's B04 scope |
| Geography | 90% retained | Pan-India footprint (22 states) but material state-level open-access friction (cross-subsidy surcharge, banking restrictions) makes a meaningful slice of the national market uneconomic in practice |
| Channel | 100% retained | TAM already scoped to open-access/group-captive/CTU/VPPA channel; no further cut |
| Customer (creditworthiness) | 75% retained | CleanMax's own book skews to AA-and-above corporates (81%, per B04); the smaller/lower-rated C&I load segment is not the underwriting profile this PPA-tenor model currently serves |
| Capability | 90% retained | Balance-sheet/project-debt capacity and execution bandwidth cap simultaneous pursuit of the full addressable base, especially against newly-active large entrants (Adani, Reliance, NTPC — named in B07) |

Cumulative: 0.95 × 0.90 × 1.00 × 0.75 × 0.90 = **57.7%**

**SAM = 44,460 × 0.577 = ₹25,650 Cr** (58% of TAM realistic).

### 3B SOM at 3 and 5 years

Current SAM share = FY26 revenue ÷ SAM = 1,912.87 ÷ 25,650 = **7.46%**.

Share-gain assumption: CleanMax sits in the **aggressive tier (3–5pp in 3 years)**, justified by
disclosed capacity guidance (FY26 operational capacity 3,088 MW growing to a guided minimum 4,588
MW by Apr-2027 — a near-49% one-year jump) and a delivered 31% YoY operational-capacity growth
track record (AR p.13), both well above the sector's ~19.4% TAM growth rate. Used: **+4pp over 3
years, +6pp over 5 years** (cumulative), i.e. within the "aggressive, capacity+execution justified"
band, not the ">5pp only on competitor exit/acquisition" tier.

SAM is grown forward at the Method-1 TAM CAGR of 19.4%/year (constant SAM/TAM ratio assumed):

- SAM at Yr3 (~FY29) = 25,650 × (1.194)³ = ₹43,657 Cr
- SAM at Yr5 (~FY31) = 25,650 × (1.194)⁵ = ₹62,203 Cr

**SOM Yr3** = 11.46% × 43,657 = **₹5,003 Cr**
Implied 3yr revenue CAGR = (5,003 / 1,912.87)^(1/3) − 1 = **37.8%**

**SOM Yr5** = 13.46% × 62,203 = **₹8,373 Cr**
Implied 5yr revenue CAGR = (8,373 / 1,912.87)^(1/5) − 1 = **34.3%**

### 3C Capacity cross-check (against B07 capex_embedded_growth_pct = 123%)

Assume RE Power Sales holds its FY26 mix share of consolidated revenue (73.2%, B04) through Yr3.
Power-Sales-only revenue implied by SOM Yr3 = 0.732 × 5,003 = ₹3,662 Cr.
Required run-rate operational capacity = 3,662 ÷ 0.78 Cr/MW = **≈4,695 MW**.

Company's own guided floor for **Apr-2027** (roughly 1 year out, well before the ~FY29 Yr3 SOM
target) is a **minimum 4,588 MW** (Inv. Pres. p.11) — within 2% of the Yr3 SOM requirement, but
reached over a year earlier than SOM needs it, and B07's capex_embedded_growth_pct of 123% points to
continued capacity growth beyond that FY27 floor through FY28.

**Capacity check: SUFFICIENT.** The committed capex/capacity plan is the more optimistic side of
this comparison, not the SOM estimate — management's own guided trajectory reaches (and is built to
exceed) the capacity this independently-derived SOM requires, ahead of schedule. This is consistent
with the earlier finding that the SOM-implied revenue CAGR (34–38%) sits below management's own
implied EBITDA CAGR (FY26→FY28 guidance of 2.3x reported EBITDA = ~52% 2yr CAGR) — the independent
estimate here is the conservative one.

## SECTION 5: SUMMARY & RUNWAY

### 5A Funnel

```
TAM (conservative, current ~40GW)  ₹31,200 Cr
TAM (realistic, FY28 ~57GW)        ₹44,460 Cr   [Method 2 ceiling ~₹3.0 lakh Cr held apart, theoretical]
  -> SAM (58% of TAM realistic)    ₹25,650 Cr
    -> SOM Yr3 (11.46% of SAM-Yr3) ₹5,003 Cr   (37.8% 3yr revenue CAGR)
    -> SOM Yr5 (13.46% of SAM-Yr5) ₹8,373 Cr   (34.3% 5yr revenue CAGR)
Current FY26 revenue:              ₹1,912.87 Cr  (7.46% of current SAM)
```

### 5B Runway assessment

- Revenue headroom = SAM ÷ current revenue = 25,650 ÷ 1,912.87 = **13.4x**
- TAM growth rate = **19.4%/year** (Method 1, CRISIL capacity trajectory)
- Company vs TAM: CleanMax's own delivered operational-capacity growth (31% YoY, AR p.13) **exceeds**
  TAM growth (19.4%) — the company is **gaining share**, not merely riding the market, consistent
  with B07's STRENGTHENING emerging-moat read (score 29).
- Years to saturate SAM at current growth: using a ~35% blended growth assumption against a static
  SAM of ₹25,650 Cr, n = ln(13.4)/ln(1.35) ≈ **8.7 years** — and SAM itself is growing throughout,
  so effective saturation sits beyond any visible planning horizon.

### 5C Runway classification: **STRONG**

Double-digit headroom (13.4x), healthy double-digit TAM growth (19.4%), company demonstrably gaining
share against that growing base. Falls short of MASSIVE (which would need >20x headroom or >25%
TAM growth); clears STRONG comfortably.

### 5D SAM expansion levers actually being pursued

- **BESS-as-a-service**: ~₹3–4/kWh incremental tariff, 3 client MOUs signed, not yet material to
  capex (B07 optionality register). Potential addition size: **NOT FOUND** — no MW/GW figure
  disclosed for the BESS pipeline as of this run.
- **Hyperscaler VPPA/EAPA-to-physical-PPA conversion**: contracts carry an option to convert to
  direct-supply PPAs as data-centre load ramps (B07 optionality). Potential addition: **NOT FOUND**
  — no disclosed MW figure tied to this specific conversion path.
- **CTU-connected/inter-state projects** (Bikaner and similar): extend addressable geography beyond
  intra-state open access, contingent on resolving the currently-flagged curtailment risk.
- **RE Services as a standalone growth line**: EPC/O&M for third-party-owned C&I RE assets, already
  26.0% of consolidated revenue (B04) — expands SAM beyond CleanMax's own owned-and-operated fleet,
  though B04 flags this revenue as lower-predictability (percentage-of-completion, Key Audit Matter).

### 5E Final output card

At **37.8% revenue CAGR** implied by SOM (3yr), with an underlying RE Power Sales EBITDA margin of
**83–84%** (B04/Inv. Pres. p.10), the earnings growth embedded here runs materially ahead of the
CleanMax's own current valuation multiple.

Note on multiple used: per B04's Method Suitability Matrix, headline (uncapped/standalone) **P/E is
explicitly flagged not_applicable** for CleanMax (EPS distorted by young-fleet depreciation/interest
timing and the NCI wedge); **EV/EBITDA is the primary method**. Current EV/EBITDA, computed from a
web-searched market cap of ₹14,882 Cr (as of 23-Aug-2026) plus an estimated net debt of ~₹6,158 Cr
(derived from the disclosed Debt(net)/Adjusted EBITDA ratio of 4.75–4.76x applied to FY26 reported
EBITDA of ₹1,295 Cr, Inv. Pres. p.27, via B04) ≈ **₹21,040 Cr EV ÷ ₹1,295 Cr FY26 EBITDA ≈ 16.3x
trailing EV/EBITDA**.

At a SOM-implied revenue CAGR of ~38% against a stable 83–84% segment margin, and set against
~16.3x trailing EV/EBITDA, the growth trajectory **broadly supports** the current valuation on a
growth-adjusted basis (implied PEG-style ratio well under 1x). This reading carries two named
caveats from earlier stages that this stage does not resolve: (1) consolidated growth systematically
overstates per-share economic growth because parent economic ownership per new SPV is trending down
(B04 flag — Apple 49% of Clean Max Taurus, Toyota Tsusho 49% of CMT JBIN); (2) new-PPA tariffs are
still falling (₹4.12→₹3.57/kWh), so this growth rests on volume and financing-cost improvement, not
pricing power. Stage 11 should carry both caveats into any EV/EBITDA-based valuation read.
