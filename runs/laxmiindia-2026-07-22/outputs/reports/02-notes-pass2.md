## NEW FINDINGS

### 1. Stressed-loan transfers to ARC exploded in FY25 far beyond what the SR-investment-growth figure alone implied — and for the first time included pre-NPA (SMA) accounts, not just NPA
[PROSPECTUS] Note 98(c), p.379 — "Details of stressed loan transferred during the period/year," split by
NPA and SMA (Special Mention Account, i.e., 1-89 DPD, not yet regulatory-NPA), to Asset Reconstruction
Companies:
- FY25: NPA — 413 accounts, aggregate principal ₹109.54cr, net book value ₹57.24cr, consideration
  ₹66.85cr. SMA — 470 accounts, aggregate principal ₹155.27cr, net book value ₹154.64cr, consideration
  ₹144.29cr. **Combined NPA+SMA principal transferred to ARC in FY25: ≈₹264.8cr.**
- FY24: NPA — 150 accounts, principal ₹5.02cr. SMA — 68 accounts, principal ₹2.25cr. Combined ≈₹7.27cr.
- FY23: NPA — 425 accounts, principal ₹12.15cr. SMA — nil. Combined ≈₹12.15cr.

That is a **>36x jump in stressed-loan-transfer volume FY24→FY25** (₹7.27cr→₹264.8cr), and the first year
in which the Company sold SMA (early-delinquency, sub-90-DPD) accounts to an ARC rather than only fully
provisioned NPA. Pass 1's Rank-7 finding captured the resulting SR *investment* balance roughly doubling
(₹14.20cr→₹28.62cr FY24→FY25) but did not surface the much larger underlying gross principal volume being
moved off-book, nor the change in the population being sold (SMA now included). 🔴 Red Flag — selling
not-yet-NPA accounts to an ARC is atypical (ARCs conventionally acquire post-default assets) and, combined
with the scale jump, raises the question of whether asset-quality stress was being pre-emptively
transferred off the reported book in FY25, ahead of the FY26 Up Money DA-pool stress event, rather than
allowed to season into the reported GNPA ratio.

### 2. Note 98(c)'s NPA-transferred-to-ARC figure and Note 75/79's own NPA-movement roll-forward do not reconcile — a ~180x unreconciled gap in the same notes package
[PROSPECTUS] Note 98(c) (p.379) states ₹109.54cr of NPA principal was transferred to ARC in FY25.
[PROSPECTUS] Note 75 (p.368), the Gross-NPA movement schedule, shows the *entire* Stage-3 gross carrying
amount closing at only ₹12.18cr at FY25-end, with a "Less: Sold to ARC" line of just ₹0.61cr for the year
(Net NPA movement) / ₹0.13cr (a separate sub-line); Note 5.7's Stage-3 roll-forward (p.318) similarly
shows "Sold to ARC" of only ₹0.61cr (Stage 3 column) against total NPA closing gross carrying amount of
₹12.18cr. Note 98(c)'s ₹109.54cr NPA-transferred figure is roughly 9x the Company's *entire* Stage-3 book
at year-end and ~180x the ₹0.61cr "Sold to ARC" line in the Stage-3 roll-forward for the same year. The
notes package gives no narrative cross-reference reconciling these two views of "NPA sold to ARC" in the
same fiscal year. 🔴 Red Flag / Question for Management — this is exactly the kind of "numbers in the
notes that do not match" pattern that warrants direct management clarification: is Note 98(c) reporting
gross original principal (pre-write-down, pre-restructure) of accounts that were mostly already
written off/derecognised via other routes before the ARC sale, cumulative multi-year pools sold in one
transaction, or a different population than Ind AS 109 Stage 3? Until reconciled, the ₹264.8cr FY25
transfer figure and the ₹12.18cr Stage-3 closing balance cannot both be read as full descriptions of the
same underlying asset-quality reality.

### 3. Write-off figures for stressed/ARC-sold loans do not reconcile across three separate disclosures — and this resolves (differently than Pass 1 assumed) the reconciliation gap Pass 1 flagged
Three distinct "write-off" numbers exist for overlapping scope:
- [PROSPECTUS] Note 29.1, p.339: core loan-book write-offs (net of recoveries) — FY25 ₹2.768cr net
  (₹4.219cr gross less ₹1.450cr recovered), FY24 ₹1.031cr net, FY23 ₹(0.254)cr (net recovery).
- [PROSPECTUS] Note 98(c) footnote, p.379: "Company had sold Stress loan assets in which write off assets
  was ₹46.14 Millions, ₹7.10 Millions and ₹14.10 Millions in FY 2024-25, FY 2023-24 and FY 2022-23
  respectively" — i.e., **₹4.614cr (FY25), ₹0.71cr (FY24), ₹1.41cr (FY23)**, specifically the write-off
  component of ARC stressed-loan sales.
- [FY26RESULTS] Note 9.4 footnote: "Company had sold Stress loan assets in which write off assets was
  ₹276.92 Lakhs for the year ended March 31 2025" (₹2.77cr) — inside a table headed "As at Mar 31, 2026,"
  i.e., a year-labelling inconsistency (the footnote says "year ended March 31, 2025" while the table it
  sits in is dated FY26).

Pass 1 (Section 12, [FY26RESULTS cross-reference]) flagged the ₹2.77cr figure as "for FY26" and noted it
was "materially lower than the ~₹4.53cr FY26 write-off figure cited in the operator's non-anchored
summary," calling this a reconciliation gap. Re-reading the source: the DRHP's Note 98(c) FY25 ARC-sale
write-off of **₹4.614cr** is a much closer match to the operator's ~₹4.53cr figure than the FY26RESULTS
₹2.77cr number is. This suggests the operator's figure likely referred to **FY25**, not FY26 as Pass 1
assumed — and that the FY26RESULTS Note 9.4 footnote itself may be a carried-over/mislabelled figure from
the FY25 disclosure rather than a genuine FY26 number. 🟡 Watch — flag for Pass 3/synthesis: do not
anchor the "~₹4.53cr write-off" claim to FY26 without confirming which year the FY26RESULTS footnote
actually describes; the DRHP Note 98(c) FY25 figure (₹4.614cr) is the better-evidenced match.

### 4. Fraud disclosure — missed entirely in Pass 1
[PROSPECTUS] Note 97, p.378: "Frauds amounting to Rs.0.69 Millions reported during the year ended March
31, 2024 and no frauds were reported during the period/year March 31, 2025 and March 31, 2023." I.e., a
₹0.069cr fraud incident in FY24, nil FY25/FY23. Nature (internal/employee vs external/customer) is not
disclosed. 🟡 Watch — immaterial in amount, but this is a standalone RBI-mandated fraud-disclosure note
that Pass 1's twelve-section walk-through did not extract at all.

### 5. Pattern of BSE regulatory penalties for delayed listing-compliance filings, dating to 2021 — new governance-hygiene finding
[PROSPECTUS] Note 80, pp.373-374: no RBI penalties in any year, but BSE imposed penalties on the Company
(in respect of its listed NCDs, pre-equity-IPO) as follows —
- Attributed to "year ended March 31, 2024": ₹5,25,100 (delay in submission of financials, Reg 52(1));
  ₹1,05,020 (delay in Reg 52(4) report); ₹1,05,020 (delay in Reg 54(2) report). Total ≈₹7.35 lakh.
- Attributed to "year ended March 2023" (dated Aug-21, Nov-21, Mar-21 in the table itself): ₹11,800 x2
  (delay in record-date intimation, Reg 60(2)); ₹1,180 (Reg 50(1), noted "Under Consideration for waiver
  off with BSE as compliance was on time").
This is a recurring, multi-year pattern of SEBI LODR listing-compliance lapses on the Company's
already-listed debt securities, disclosed for the first time in this note. 🟡 Watch — new finding, not
covered in Pass 1's borrowings/governance sections.

### 6. Explicit going-concern paragraph whose self-assessed language sits in tension with the Company's own rising-stress disclosures elsewhere in the same notes package
[PROSPECTUS] Note 59, p.364 ("Disclosures regarding COVID-19 related measures" / Going Concern): "The
Company, at this juncture, is focused on capital preservation, balance sheet protection and operating
expenses management. Given its healthy capital adequacy, strong liquidity position, **lower gross NPA
and net NPA**, diversified portfolio mix, geographical distribution and strong risk metrics." This
appears to be templated, carried-forward COVID-era disclosure language, largely unchanged across the
three restated years. It asserts "lower gross NPA and net NPA" in the same filing whose own Note 75/93(d)
show the Gross Stage-3 ratio nearly doubling FY23→FY25 (0.58%→1.07%, per Pass 1 Rank-3) and whose Note
98(c) (Finding #1 above) shows a >36x jump in stressed-loan transfers to ARC in FY25. 🟡 Watch — this is
the specific "going concern language" the Pass 3 pattern-check calls for; Pass 1 did not extract it.

### 7. Mitigating, new: 100% win rate before the RBI Ombudsman across all three years, despite the surge in raw customer complaints
[PROSPECTUS] Note 96(a), p.377: of complaints that escalated to the RBI's Integrated Ombudsman Scheme (an
independent forum, distinct from the Company's own customer-complaint intake that Pass 1's Rank-4 finding
covers), **all were resolved in the Company's favour** in every year: FY25 11/11, FY24 12/12, FY23 9/9
(32/32 cumulative, zero resolved against the Company, zero awards unimplemented). 🟢 Clean — a genuine
balancing data point against Pass 1's complaint-growth red flag: when complaints are independently
adjudicated rather than self-reported, the record is clean so far.

### 8. Correction to Pass 1: CSR shortfall figure was overstated by 10x
Pass 1 (Section 9) stated: "CSR shortfall provisioning: FY24 had a ₹0.21cr under-spend." The source table,
[PROSPECTUS] Note 45, p.349 ("D. Shortfall for the period/year"), shows the FY24 figure is **₹0.21
million = ₹0.021 crore (₹21,000)**, not ₹0.21 crore — a 10x scale error in Pass 1's conversion. For the
record: FY24 CSR required ₹0.383cr, spent ₹0.362cr, shortfall ₹0.021cr; fully carried forward and spent in
FY25 (FY25 required ₹0.460cr, spent ₹0.467cr). 🟢 Clean (correction only, no change to overall conclusion
that CSR compliance is immaterhere).

### 10. Credit ratings stagnant at "A-" through 3x book growth; one rating agency's coverage was withdrawn for reasons external to the Company
[PROSPECTUS] Note 71, p.366: Bank Loan rating and NCD rating from Acuite Ratings & Research held flat at

### 11. FY26 balance sheet: Investments (dominated by ARC Security Receipts) grew 5.4x in one year, a far more disproportionate jump than the FY24→FY25 trend Pass 1 flagged
[FY26RESULTS] Balance Sheet: total Investments ₹158.91cr as at 31-Mar-2026 vs ₹29.27cr as at 31-Mar-2025

(₹1,480.10cr vs ₹1,126.91cr). Investments as a share of Total Assets rose from ~2.1% (FY25) to ~8.7%

doubling"); the FY26 data available in the same input set shows the disproportionality between
SR/investment growth and loan-book growth worsened dramatically in the very year of the Up Money DA-pool
stress event. 🔴 Red Flag — materially updates and reinforces Pass 1 Rank-7 with data Pass 1 had access
to but did not extract at this level of comparison.

### 12. "Upfront Gain on ARC" cash-flow line jumped over 100x in FY26 — a distinct metric from the DA-assignment gain Pass 1 already covered

### 14. Board has sought shareholder approval for a large forward NCD private-placement authorization: up to ₹400 crore within one year
[FY26RESULTS] Board-outcome letter dated 13-May-2026, item 2: "approved the limit for issuance of

in the restated notes. 🟡 Watch — new forward-looking capital-structure signal, disclosed only in the
FY26 board-outcome filing (not the DRHP notes, since it postdates the Restated Financial Statements'

pace of actual drawdown against this authorization (the Feb-2026 ₹5cr private-placement NCD tranche,
per the Reg 52(7) Annexure, is the only utilisation disclosed so far and was fully deployed with no

## PASS 2 NEW FINDINGS SUMMARY

Fourteen new findings surfaced on the second pass, none of which were covered (even partially) in Pass 1:
(1) a >36x FY24→FY25 jump in stressed-loan (NPA+SMA) transfers to ARC, including — for the first time —
pre-NPA SMA accounts; (2) an unreconciled ~180x gap between two notes both purporting to measure "NPA
sold to ARC" in FY25; (3) three separate, non-reconciling write-off figures across two filings, which
also reframes (rather than resolves) Pass 1's flagged write-off reconciliation gap — pointing to FY25,
not FY26, as the likely correct year for the operator's non-anchored ~₹4.53cr figure; (4) an
undisclosed-in-Pass-1 fraud incident (₹0.069cr, FY24); (5) a recurring pattern of BSE listing-compliance
penalties dating to 2021; (6) an explicit going-concern paragraph whose "lower gross NPA" language
conflicts with the Company's own rising-stress disclosures; (7) a clean, mitigating 100%-win-rate record
before the RBI Ombudsman; (8) a 10x scale correction to Pass 1's CSR-shortfall figure; (9) CFO and
Company Secretary turnover during the exact window the restated GAAP errors originated; (10) stagnant
"A-" credit ratings through 3x book growth, plus an unrelated rating-agency delisting; (11) a 5.4x FY26
jump in ARC-dominated Investments, far worse than the FY23-FY25 trend Pass 1 saw; (12) a >100x FY26 jump
in the ARC-specific (as opposed to DA-specific) upfront gain line; (13) a previously-unextracted 5%
Default Loss Guarantee on the nascent co-lending book; and (14) a forward-looking ₹400 crore NCD
private-placement authorization sought at the May-2026 board meeting.

The most consequential of these for the overall accounting-quality assessment are Findings #1, #2, and
#11: taken together, they show that the scale of loans being moved off the reported book via ARC sales in
FY25-FY26 is an order of magnitude larger, and internally less reconciled, than Pass 1's DA/ARC-gain
finding (already its Rank-1 red flag) had captured. This does not change Pass 1's overall direction — DA/
ARC-related accounting remains the dominant swing factor in reported profitability and asset quality —
but it substantially raises the stakes of that finding and adds an unresolved cross-note discrepancy
(Finding #2) that should be put to management directly before Pass 3's consolidated verdict is finalised.
