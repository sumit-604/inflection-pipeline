## The picture in one paragraph

Wednesday was the fourth repair day since the 8 October break, and the
strongest one underneath. The Nifty slipped about half a percent, yet most
stocks rose. 145 stocks jumped four and a half percent or more, and only 10
fell that much. New yearly lows dropped from 98 to 28, the fewest in three
weeks, and new highs beat new lows for the first time since the break. The
count of stocks above the 200 day average rose to 919, about two thirds of the
way back to where it stood before the break. The short trend has already fully
repaired. Two things did not fit. The big names paused, with pharma, finance
and auto pulling the index down. And the VIX, the price of index protection,
rose while most stocks rose. The market under the index is healing faster than
the index. It is still well short of the late September high, and it has not
had the one big day that marks a real turn.

## Where the market stands

Picture about 2,260 stocks, each with three trend lines under its price. The
short line is the 20 day average. The middle line is the 50 day average. The
long line is the 200 day average. A stock above a line is trending up on that
clock. On Wednesday 919 stocks stood above the long line, about four in ten.
733 stood above the middle line, about one in three. 799 stood above the short
line, also about one in three. All three counts rose for a fourth straight day.

The repair has run in the textbook order. The short count stood at about 720
the day before the break. It fell to about 410 on 8 October. It now stands at
about 800, above where it started. The middle count went from about 760 to
about 560 and back to about 730. It is nearly home. The long count went from
about 965 to about 835 and back to 919. It is about two thirds of the way back.
Short lines heal first because they move fastest. Long lines heal last.

Now step back three weeks further. On 23 September, the best day of the late
September bounce, about 1,190 stocks held the long line. About 1,060 held the
short line. That was the top of the last range. Then came two waves of
selling, on 28 September and 1 October, each with about a hundred hard fallers.
Then came the 8 October shock, with about 310. Each wave knocked the long count
lower. So "back to before the break" means about 965. "Back to the September
high" means about 1,190. Tonight the market has done the first job two thirds
of the way. It has not started the second.

The longer story gives a sense of scale. On 7 August about 1,375 stocks held
the long line, the high of this whole history. On 23 March only about 300 did.
Tonight's 919 sits a little past halfway between them. That is a market in a
long correction, not a collapse.

The day itself was strong by the count of big moves. 145 stocks rose four and
a half percent or more. That is the most since 6 October, when about 200 did.
Only 10 fell that much, the fewest in the last month. About 56 stocks in a
hundred closed higher. Still, a strong day is not a thrust day. A thrust is the
kind of day that marks a real turn, when buyers sweep in everywhere at once. On
1 April more than 1,100 stocks jumped four and a half percent. On 8 April
almost 900 did. Wednesday had 145.

Inside the session the buyers kept control. About one stock in five traded
four percent above its prior close at some point. About four in ten of those
held the gain into the close. Only about one stock in eighteen traded four
percent below its prior close. A quarter of those closed near the low. Dips
were scarce. Where they came, sellers did not press them.

Volume came back to normal. Turnover was about 465 crore, level with a year
ago, after two days below it. The share of stocks trading on heavy volume rose off the
floor of its recent range, but it still sits in the lower part. Buyers are
returning. They are not rushing.

New lows gave the clearest signal. 98 stocks made a new yearly low on Tuesday.
On Wednesday only 28 did, the fewest since 23 September. New highs held at
about 40. So new highs beat new lows. On 8 October new lows outran new highs by
about six to one.

Fear moved the other way. The India VIX measures what traders pay to protect
against index swings. It rose about four percent to about 14.6. It sits about a
quarter above its level three months ago. On Tuesday the VIX fell while the
Nifty rose. On Wednesday it rose while the Nifty fell. Index traders still buy
cover even as the broad market heals. Gold gave no shelter either. Gold funds
are down about six percent over the month, a little more than the Nifty's fall
of about five.

## Thirty points

### For the trader

**1. The repair is four days old and still unproven.** 145 stocks rose four and
a half percent and only 10 fell that much. That is the best day of the repair.
In this history, the days that marked real turns had several hundred risers.
Until one arrives, the bounce is a repair, not a new leg up.

**2. The index and the stocks parted ways.** The Nifty fell about half a
percent while most stocks rose. The microcap index rose about 0.7 percent. A
red index on a strong breadth day means the selling sat in a few big names.
The index chart alone hides the better day underneath.

**3. Size leadership keeps flipping.** Tuesday favoured big names. Wednesday
favoured small ones. No size group is in charge yet. Two or three days in a row
with the same size leading would say who drives the repair.

**4. Volume is back to normal, not more.** Turnover matched the year-ago figure
after two thin days. The share of stocks on heavy volume rose off its floor.
A day well above the year-ago figure would turn this into a demand signal.

**5. Sellers almost vanished.** Only about one stock in eighteen traded four
percent down at any point. Only 10 closed down four and a half percent. When
sellers stop pressing, modest buying moves prices. A return of a hundred or
more hard fallers would end that.

**6. New lows collapsed.** New yearly lows fell from 98 to 28, the fewest since
23 September. New highs beat new lows for the first time in the repair. A jump
back above about 100 would undo this signal.

**7. The long count held above 900 a second day.** 919 stocks sit above the
200 day line. The next mark is about 965, the level before the 8 October break.
Above that, the late September high near 1,190 is the real ceiling.

**8. The short trend has fully repaired.** About 800 stocks hold the 20 day
line, more than the 720 before the break. The middle count sits near 730
against 760 before. Swing setups now run with the short trend, not against it.

**9. The VIX rose against the calm.** Index protection got about four percent
dearer while most stocks rose. It closed near 14.6. A VIX that keeps rising
while breadth improves says index traders still pay for cover. A close under
14 would say that worry is fading.

**10. IT has two strong days behind it.** The IT index crossed its thirty week

**12. Group moves on volume are the setups the data favours.** Textiles,

**13. Index-versus-breadth traps sit in media and pharma.** The media index

the four week line. In both, the index misreads the stocks.

**14. Shorting stays hard.** Only 6 stocks are down twenty percent in five
days, against 16 up that much. Only 10 fell hard on the day. The data
penalises broad short setups this week. A shock day would change that fast.

**15. The big names faded into the close.** The large company list above the

Big stocks closing under the day's average price show sellers working late in
the session.

**16. Healthcare missed a second broad rally.** Pharma and healthcare were the

About one healthcare stock in five holds the four week line. The data

## What would change this read

The read is a repair that has turned strong underneath while the index lags.
It turns worse if the long count falls back below 900, and worse again below
about 835, the low of the break. It turns worse if new yearly lows climb back
above about 100, or if the count of stocks down twenty percent in five days
grows past about 15. A shock day with about 300 hard fallers would repeat the
September and October pattern and open the way to a lower low. A VIX above
about 15 while breadth also weakens would say the index worry has spread under
the surface.

The cautious read fails if the repair gets a thrust and the big names join.
The first test is a day with about 300 or more stocks up four and a half
percent, on turnover well above its year-ago level. The second is the long
count clearing about 965, the level before the 8 October break, and then
moving toward the September high near 1,190. The third is the VIX closing
under 14 while new lows stay under 100. A Nifty that rises with the broad
market, not against it, would support all three. Until those arrive, the data
favours patience, with the edge tilted toward repair.
