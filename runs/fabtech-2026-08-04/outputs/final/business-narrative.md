Fabtech exports pharma factories on stretched cash

Fabtech is an asset light turnkey contractor that designs and builds pharmaceutical factories. It supplies cleanrooms, HVAC, process utilities and process equipment, and runs the project start to finish. About 89 percent of revenue is export, sold into the Middle East, Africa and the GCC. The company listed around October 2025 through a fresh issue that raised Rs 207.5 Cr, with no promoter selling. FY26 total income was Rs 431 Cr, up 28 percent, EBITDA Rs 55.6 Cr, and reported net profit Rs 38.4 Cr. Turnkey projects are about 76 percent of revenue and standalone equipment supply about 24 percent. The order book sits near Rs 900 Cr. The shares trade at Rs 152 for a market cap of Rs 675 Cr.

The sector tag is wrong on the screen. This is not a pharma or CDMO business. It is a capital goods and EPC contractor, and it should be read against Anup Engineering, HLE Glascoat, Praj Industries and Ion Exchange, not against drug makers. About 63 percent of income is pass through equipment cost, so a drug maker's gross margin is not a fair comparison. The valuation exit multiple runs through EPC comps at a 20 times sector cap, not the 38 times a pharma tag would have carried.

Several things are changing at once. The company is pushing into Saudi Arabia through a 51 percent local entity called SACE to qualify under Vision 2030 rules, and it has won one civil order there. It is chasing an Africa vaccine pipeline with three more projects in talks. Average order tickets have grown from about 1.5 million dollars to 3 million dollars a few years ago toward 7 million to 10 million dollars now, with the largest orders quoted up to 70 million dollars. A Europe acquisition in Italy has been promised since early 2026 and is still not closed. On governance, independent directors went from 3 to 5, a non family CEO from Grasim and Piramal runs operations, promoter pledge is zero, and the lead promoter stepped from executive director to a non executive role before the IPO.

The backward looking score is weak. Gate 0 lands at AVOID, grand total 63 out of 160, on two mechanical caps rather than broad quality failure. Cumulative operating cash flow to profit across FY23 to FY26 is only 8.1 percent, which trips the cash conversion deal breaker. Only four usable fiscal years exist because the company restated just FY23 to FY25 for the prospectus and listed in October 2025, which forces a one tier downgrade. FY26 return on capital of 11.3 percent, down from 29.3 percent in FY23, and return on equity of 12.9 percent, down from 24.4 percent, are depressed by the Rs 207.5 Cr of IPO cash sitting largely undeployed and inflating the denominators. Those two ratio drops read as post IPO rebase and history length, not deterioration in the operating business.

Cash conversion is the live problem, and the rating agency calls it structural. Operating cash flow was about 1 percent of profit on a consolidated basis in FY26, and was negative in FY23 and FY25. It was 222 percent in FY24, so the business can convert cash when milestones bill and collect. Trade receivables grew 38.5 percent against revenue growth of 25.7 percent. The auditor's sole key audit matter is Rs 58.9 Cr of receivables overdue more than 365 days, which is about 27 percent of gross receivables. There is a Rs 37.18 Cr gap, 18.2 percent, between the receivables figure on the consolidated balance sheet and the supporting Note 13 schedule, and that gap is not reconciled inside the annual report itself. CRISIL, dated 15 October 2025, ties the high debtor days to Q4 loaded billing and retention money held for two years, and expects the working capital pattern to continue over the medium term. Both causes are contractual, not a growth artifact, so this is read as structural, not a timing build.

Earnings quality is thin on top of the cash problem. About Rs 21 Cr of non operating other income, roughly Rs 12 Cr foreign exchange and Rs 7 Cr interest on IPO deposits, is about 55 percent of reported profit and 42.6 percent of pre tax profit. Operating EBITDA is Rs 35 Cr, an 8.52 percent margin, against the headline Rs 55.6 Cr and roughly 12.9 percent that the topline implies.

Management says more than it delivers, and the credibility grade is C. FY27 growth guidance was walked down twice inside two quarters, from 30 to 40 percent, to about 25 percent, to 20 to 25 percent, before FY27 printed a single quarter. The revenue recognition question, shipment based versus percentage of completion, has stayed open across three straight calls, with the resolution horizon pushed out to years. The Rs 58.9 Cr overdue receivables key audit matter was never addressed on any of the three calls. Questions on the order book geography split and on investor participation were deflected each time. Against that, the company beat its FY26 revenue guidance, hit its Q4 profit target, and did close the Saudi localization.

Peers confirm the direction of the cost story but not its size. All four cite West Asia conflict linked cost pressure in FY26, yet the largest peer magnitude is Praj at about 3 percent raw material inflation, far below Fabtech's claimed 33 percent raw material and 43 percent execution cost rise. Two peers, Ion Exchange and Praj, treat milestone and percentage of completion billing as a settled industry norm, which sits against Fabtech framing the same choice as a multi year evaluation. Two management claims, the 30 billion dollar plus 70 billion dollar ten year opportunity and the assertion of no listed Indian peer, were checked against all four peers and neither could be triangulated. One data point to keep straight: the sister listed company Fabtech Technologies Cleanrooms, BSE 544332, is a related party supplier, not an arm's length peer.

The market runway is real but smaller than the pitch. The addressable market is Rs 2,000 Cr to Rs 4,400 Cr a year, the serviceable slice about Rs 2,150 Cr, and a five year serviceable share about Rs 787 Cr. That share implies revenue growth of roughly 13 to 14 percent a year, below management's own 20 to 25 percent guidance and its Rs 1,000 Cr by 2030 target of about 23 percent. Management's 30 billion dollar plus 70 billion dollar framing works out to about 14 times the block's realistic yearly market and reads as inflated, likely a total market presented as if it were serviceable.

The promoter picture carries a concern. Purchases from promoter group entities, including the sister listed company with no non compete in place, were about 16.7 percent of FY25 revenue. Loans to related parties were 99.37 percent of all loans given, and 55.7 percent of current borrowings were owed to a related party. The lead promoter, holding 41.52 percent, is a named accused in a pending, currently stayed criminal complaint alleging false invoices, with summons issued in December 2021 and the revision stayed in March 2025. The board's report asserts no material related party transactions, which cannot be squared with the related party footprint the notes themselves disclose.

The valuation decision is AVOID at Rs 152. The operator fixed the exit multiple at 13 times one year forward FY27 earnings, the top of the framework additive range of 11 to 13 times and under the 20 times EPC sector cap. On that basis fair value is Rs 150.2 in the base case, Rs 122.9 bear, Rs 157.4 bull. The more conservative reduced re-rating track puts base fair value at Rs 98.2. Either track leaves the stock at Rs 152 already trading at its own forward destination multiple, so there is no re-rating headroom. The Hurdle Ratio is 1.46 against the 1.953 needed for a 25 percent return, and it fails even on capped bull earnings, so the hurdle verdict is stop. Probability weighted three year return works out to about 12.5 percent. The entry zone is Rs 62.9 to Rs 80.6 with a margin of safety price of Rs 61.5, roughly 47 percent below today.

The devil's advocate strengthens the AVOID rather than weakening it, and the thesis survives the challenge. It argues the base fair value is too generous because 13 times capitalises the 42.6 percent of pre tax profit that is non operating income, worth about Rs 48 a share; on operating only earnings honest fair value is nearer Rs 105 to Rs 133, so downside from Rs 152 is understated. It notes the net cash of about Rs 31 a share is mostly not shareholders' money, since about Rs 110 Cr is customer advances and about Rs 16 Cr is encumbered by related party guarantees, leaving distributable net cash near Rs 3 a share. Moat durability, management trust and valuation safety all fail the challenge, and growth triggers weaken but hold. The analysis is worth keeping on file; the stock is not a buy at this price.
