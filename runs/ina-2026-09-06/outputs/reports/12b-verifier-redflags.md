## PART 1: INDEPENDENT RED-FLAG LIST

### 1.1 CRITICAL — items that would change a decision

**C1. The CFO answered "Yeah" to a direct question on whether the 2,000 MW
production guidance was on track, when it was not, and revised revenue down
40% eight pages later in the same call.**
Disha Bhordia (Sapphire Capital): "we had earlier guided that we're targeting
around, I think, 2,000 MW of production this year. So, are we on track for
that?" Ravi Dusad: "Yeah." (Feb 2026, p6). Immediately after, Manish Gupta
gives H1 dispatch of "nearly, H1, 360 MW", Q3 at 356 MW produced / 364 MW
dispatched, and a Q4 plan of "450 to 500 MW" (Feb 2026, p5-6). DERIVED FY26
volume: 360 + 364 + 500 = ~1,224 MW against 2,000-2,100 MW guided (Manish
Gupta, Jun 2025, p9), a ~41% shortfall. On p13 of the same Feb 2026 call the
same CFO tells Kapil Advani revenue will be "close to INR 2,000 crores"
against Rs3,300cr guided. The "Yeah" was not a forecast that later went wrong;
it was contradicted by the speaker's own numbers inside the same hour.

**C2. The CFO redefined usable capacity as 50-55% of nameplate, which voids
the 5.5 GW headline, and every peer contradicts the norm he cited.**
Ravi Dusad: "as per normal standards, capacity -- out of total installed
capacity, 50% to 55% can be utilized capacity, right" (Feb 2026, p18).
Against this: Waaree, "on an ongoing basis, module capacity if you look at
track record also on basis around 70%, 75% utilization has been there"
(Abhishek Pareek, Aug 2026, p14); Websol, "as per industry average around 70%
to 75% is a comfortable level to be operating at", with 81% achieved
(Sanjana Khaitan, Aug 2026, p7); Premier, "On module utilization, we have
consistently been between 75% to 80%. And on cell, we have consistently been
about close to 90%" (Chiranjeev Saluja, Jan 2026, p8). Premier also discloses
effective capacity explicitly, at 90% of nameplate (Saluja, Jan 2026, p22).
This matters because the 5.5 GW figure is the input to every forward volume
claim. On the CFO's own 50-55% rule, 5.5 GW nameplate is 2.75-3.0 GW of real
output, and the FY28 revenue plan needs more than that.

**C3. The margin guidance basis switched from PAT to EBITDA between calls
while the numerals stayed the same, concealing a roughly 4-5 point downgrade.**
Jun 2025, Ravi Dusad, explicitly on PAT: "our PAT margins will be close to
11.1%... For FY26-FY27, again we have given a guidance of a margin expansion
of 250 basis points from 11.1% to 14.01%" (p8), and "from 9.5% to 11% for this
year and it will continue to 14% in FY26-27 and then 16% in FY27-28 with the
integration of cells" (p16). Jun 2026, same speaker, same numerals, different
basis: "we are close between 14% to 15% with aluminum integration", "with Cell
Line, the EBITDA margins will be 20% plus" (p16), confirmed to Siddhart Jain
(YES Securities) as "14%-15% for this year and around 20% for next year"
(p17). FY26 actuals give the conversion: EBITDA margin 14%, PAT margin 9.3%
(Ravi Dusad, Jun 2026, p5). DERIVED: a 14-15% EBITDA guide for FY27 maps to
roughly 9-10% PAT on FY26's own relationship, against the 14.01% PAT once
guided for the same year. The downgrade is never stated. The drift had already
begun in Feb 2026, where Bhavesh Shah asked about "PAT margin guidance" and
received an EBITDA answer, and said so on the call (p7).

**C4. The FY27 capital plan does not close arithmetically, inside one call.**
Ravi Dusad states FY27 capex of "approximately INR1,000 crores... all-in-all,
INR1,500 crores of capex for cell line and INR1,000 crores of capex in KUSUM.
So, all-in-all, INR2,500 crores capex this year" (Jun 2026, p9). He then
guides peak debt: "It is close to INR1,500 crores peak debt in this year,
'26-'27" (p11). Against that: existing borrowings of Rs835cr at 31-Mar-2026
(analyst-stated, uncorrected, p10); undrawn IREDA of Rs794cr (DERIVED: Rs1,134cr
sanctioned less Rs340cr drawn, p10); and IPP debt of "the average debt will be
INR500 crores" on the Rs1,000cr KUSUM capex (p20). DERIVED total: 835 + 794 +
500 = Rs2,129cr, already Rs629cr above the stated peak, before any increase in
working-capital debt on 50-60% revenue growth. Separately, Rs2,500cr of capex
is being funded by a company that earned Rs201cr PAT in FY26 and whose free
cash flow an analyst described as negative on the same call, uncorrected
(Hemanshu Srivastava, p9). No equity raise was mentioned on any of the three
calls.

**C5. The Managing Director denied being in a business the company has
Rs1,000cr of capex committed to, in the same call.**
Vikas Jain: "But we are not working as an EPC or IPP. We are currently
focusing on this backward integration of solar modules" (Jun 2026, p8).
In his own opening remarks on the same call: "In the EPC and IPP segment, we
continue to scale our execution capabilities with a targeted IPP portfolio of
400 megawatt by FY27" (p4). Elsewhere on the same call: Rs1,000cr of KUSUM IPP
capex (Ravi Dusad, p9), a third-party EPC order of "more than INR300 crores"
(Ravi Dusad, p9), and Rs135cr of FY27 IPP revenue (Manish Gupta / Ravi Dusad,
p20). Manish Gupta corrects him two lines later ("we have got order for KUSUM
projects, KUSUM A", p8). This is not a slip of terminology; the MD gave a
factually wrong answer about the company's own segment composition to an
analyst asking about storage requirements in that segment.

**C6. Cell and wafer capex per GW sit far below two independent peer
benchmarks, and the wafer figure is below both by more than half.**
INA cell: Rs1,300cr for 4.5 GW, and Manish Gupta confirms the scope is "only
plant machinery and building and utilities" (Jun 2026, p12). DERIVED:
Rs289cr/GW. Premier's greenfield comparator: "our total capex for the cell
line of 7 gigawat alone is about INR3,000 crores" (Vinay Rustagi, May 2026,
p5), DERIVED Rs429cr/GW, and Premier adds "our capex for the first 7 gigawatt
line is already at about 35% lower than the industry benchmark" (Vinay
Rustagi, May 2026, p8), DERIVED industry benchmark ~Rs660cr/GW. INA is 33%
below the cheapest disclosed greenfield builder in India and ~56% below the
benchmark that builder cites.
INA wafer/ingot: "something around INR1,000 crores to INR1,200 crores" for
"Same 4.5 gigawatt" (Manish Gupta, Jun 2026, p12). DERIVED: Rs222-267cr/GW.
Premier: 10 GW ingot-wafer at "around INR5900 crores" (Vinay Rustagi, Jan
2026, p6), DERIVED Rs590cr/GW. Waaree: 10 GW ingot-wafer at Nagpur "with a
capex of INR6,200 crores" (Jignesh Rathod, May 2026, p3), DERIVED Rs620cr/GW.
INA's per-GW wafer estimate is 55-62% below both. INA's own DPR is not
complete ("DPR is under progress", p12), so this is an unpriced number
presented with a range.

**C7. INA's DCR cell supply, the only margin-bearing product it sells, comes
from a competitor that has said it will stop selling cells.**
Manish Gupta: "we have already have tie-up with the cell factories like Emvee,
like Premier and other companies we have already have tie up for their DCR
cells" (Jun 2026, p15). Premier, ten weeks later: "Once our cell line gets
operational, we're almost 10 gigawatt cell and 11 gigawatt module, which means
we will not have a lot of cells to sell" (Chiranjeev Saluja, Aug 2026, p4),
and "that proportion is going to get more and more skewed towards domestic
consumption as ALMM Phase 2 gets implemented and DCR demand picks up" (Vinay
Rustagi, Aug 2026, p18). DCR is 15% of INA's FY26 volume (Manish Gupta, Jun
2026, p17) but carries the entire price premium: Rs20-22/W DCR against
Rs13-14/W non-DCR (Manish Gupta, Jun 2026, p12). INA has no cell of its own
until Q4 FY27 at the earliest. B05 explicitly handed this to stage 6 for
checking; it was not checked.

### 1.2 MAJOR — wrong or unexplained, thesis-relevant

**M1. The volume, price and revenue triangle does not close for H1 FY26, and
no segment revenue split was ever given for FY26.**
DERIVED H1 FY26 revenue: Rs1,352cr (9M) minus Rs575cr (Q3) = Rs777cr (both
figures, Ravi Dusad, Feb 2026, p4). H1 dispatch: "Nearly, H1, 360 MW" (Manish
Gupta, Feb 2026, p6). DERIVED realization: Rs21.58/W. That exceeds INA's own
top DCR price of Rs22/W on a mix that was 85% non-DCR for the year (Manish
Gupta, Jun 2026, p17), and it exceeds a blended peer price for the same
period: Premier quotes non-DCR at "INR1.4 crores to INR1.5 crores per
megawatt" and DCR at "INR2.2 crores to INR2.3 crores per megawatt" (Saluja,
Jan 2026, p16), DERIVED blended at 85/15 = Rs15.2-16.2/W. Full year is the
same shape: DERIVED Rs2,146cr / ~1,224 MW = Rs17.5/W against a DERIVED
blended grid price of ~Rs14.65/W, a gap of roughly Rs350cr. Either a material
block of revenue is not module sales, or the disclosed volumes are not the
volumes behind the revenue. Management gave a revenue split by product in Jun
2025 (module Rs2,800cr / EPC Rs400cr / other Rs100cr, p16) and gave none at
all for FY26 actuals on either later call.

**M2. Revenue is concentrated in the last month of each quarter, and
management normalised it rather than explained it.**
Nirav Khandhar: "in December we had some peak production plus sales and it was
around INR350 crores in a month... in Q4, we touched around INR790 crores in
three months and in December itself we had touched around INR350 crores"
(Jun 2026, p14). Manish Gupta confirms and adds March: "we have done again the
peak revenue in the March month where we have supply dispatch around INR440
plus crores... In our sector sir, it's very common that dispatches are
cumulative in the end of the quarter month" (p15). DERIVED: December was
Rs350cr of Q3's Rs575cr (61%), so October plus November together were Rs225cr;
March was Rs440cr of Q4's Rs794cr (55%), so January plus February together
were Rs354cr, barely equal to December alone. The quarter-end argument
explains March. It does not explain why two months after a Rs350cr December
produced Rs354cr combined. Set against receivables up 156% to Rs280cr and a
shift to 45-day LC terms (analyst-stated and confirmed, p10), month-end
concentration of this degree is a cut-off and channel-loading question, and it
was answered as an industry habit.

**M3. "There are no long-term loans in the books" in February, Rs468cr of
long-term debt seven weeks later.**
Ravi Dusad, Feb 2026, p18: "So, the finance cost is almost short-term working
capital. There are no long-term loans in the books. On a consolidated level,

tranche drawn "till 31st March" (p9). The February statement was made about a
balance sheet seven weeks from a year-end that carried Rs468cr of long-term
debt.

**M4. The CFO's own borrowing bifurcation does not add up.**
Analyst Dharmendra Kumar Jain states borrowings rose "from INR108 crores to
INR835 crores in one year" (Jun 2026, p10), uncorrected. Ravi Dusad's answer:
short-term "has risen from INR100 crores to INR300 crores", long-term "INR468
crores is the total debt in the books". DERIVED: 300 + 468 = Rs768cr against
the Rs835cr he did not dispute. A Rs67cr gap in a bifurcation offered as a
complete answer.

**M5. PAT margin went backwards year on year, and nobody said so.**
FY25 PAT margin "somewhere close to 9.5%" (Ravi Dusad, Jun 2025, p8). FY26:
"Our PAT margin for the year stood at 9.3%" (Ravi Dusad, Jun 2026, p5). The
entire transition thesis is margin expansion from integration and scale.
Revenue grew 61%, capacity grew from ~1 GW to 5.5 GW, and the profitability
metric the company itself chose to guide on fell 20 basis points. Neither
management nor any analyst on the call stated the year-on-year direction.

**M6. ROCE fell from a claimed 60% to 19% in one year, unmentioned and
unasked.**
Vikas Jain, Jun 2025, p7: "Our operational efficiency resulted in a robust ROC
of 60% and a strong cash flow from operations of INR113 crores." Ravi Dusad,
Jun 2026, p5: "net debt to equity at 0.5x, ROCE at 19%, and ROE at 25%." No
reconciliation, no question. For a GARP transition screen this is the single
most consequential quality metric in the corpus and it moved 41 points.

**M7. Net cash of Rs206cr became gross borrowings of Rs835cr in twelve
months, against a stated posture of "strong financial discipline".**
Vikas Jain, Jun 2025, p7: "we have cash and cash equivalents of INR313 crores...
The net debt was negative to INR206 crores", inside a section describing
"strong financial discipline and industry-leading return ratios" (Manish
Gupta, p6). Twelve months later, borrowings of Rs835cr and net debt to equity
of 0.5x (Jun 2026, p5, p10). The swing is never framed as a change in
financial posture.

**M8. The Narmadapuram project scope changed three times while the capex
barely moved, and the MP module line disappeared without mention.**
Jun 2025, p5: "4-gigawatt solar module, 3-gigawatt solar cell and 54,000 metric
ton aluminium frame capacity expansion in Narmadapuram", at "INR1,300 crores
plus" (Manish Gupta, p14).
Feb 2026, p4: "4.5 GW TOPCon G12R cell manufacturing facility and an 18,000-ton
aluminum extrusion unit", still Rs1,300cr total (Ravi Dusad, p16).
Jun 2026, p3: "4.5 gigawatt TOPCon solar cell facility and 4.5 gigawatt of
aluminum frame manufacturing" — aluminium frame now quoted in gigawatts.
Cell capacity rose 50%, aluminium was specified three different ways in three
units, the 4 GW MP module line vanished with no comment, and the capex moved
from Rs1,300cr to Rs1,500cr with the increase attributed entirely to "working
capital margin and contingencies" (Ravi Dusad, Jun 2026, p12). The scope
change, not the Rs200cr, is the finding.

**M9. The cell ramp assumption is faster than the most experienced Indian
cell maker's, and it contradicts itself inside one call.**
Manish Gupta, Jun 2026, p7: full ramp "In Q1 FY28, definitely, that whole plant
should be ramp up", from a Q4 FY27 start, i.e. three to four months. Ravi
Dusad, same call, p9: "in next two quarters the capacity will be maximum 60%
to 70%." Both statements describe the same plant over the same window.
Peer benchmarks: "all new lines do take from 6 months to as long as 18 months
to fully stabilize" (Vinay Rustagi, Premier, Jan 2026, p15); "it takes two
years to build cell lines and to ramp them up and to stabilize them"
(Chiranjeev Saluja, Premier, Aug 2026, p18); Premier's own new lines take "4
to 6 months for stabilizing" with fifteen years of cell manufacturing behind
them (Saluja, May 2026, p9); and an Axis Mutual Fund analyst observes of new
Indian DCR cell capacity, "The utilization is not even going beyond 20%"
(Nitin Arora, Premier, Aug 2026, p5). This is INA's first cell plant, built in
a single 4.5 GW stretch with no pilot line, which management stated as a
deliberate choice: "Since we are not constructing this plant in phases, this
is a single location, single setup of 4.5 GW" (Vikas Jain, Feb 2026, p8).
Websol, the closest peer by size, does the opposite and says why: 4 GW "in
phase wise, in two phases of 2 gigawatts each... to take care of any technology
risk", preceded by converting an existing 600 MW line "to create a practical
experience of TOPCon before we are going for a greenfield expansion" (Amrit
Daga and Sohan Lal Agarwal, Aug 2026, p13).

**M10. INA's own cell output claim contradicts its own utilization norms,
inside two calls.**
Vikas Jain, Feb 2026, p8: "we hope to produce almost 3.8 to 4 GW of cells from
this plant", DERIVED 84-89% of 4.5 GW nameplate. Ravi Dusad, same call, p18:
50-55% is the utilizable norm. Manish Gupta, same call, p12: "all companies are

efficiency... 3.6 gigawatt". The low norm is used to argue away industry
overcapacity and INA's own low output; the high number is used for INA's cell
plant. Both cannot hold.

**M11. The Chairman gave two irreconcilable national cell-capacity figures two

Manish Gupta, Feb 2026, p12: "around 142 GW of total cell manufacturing

**M12. The CFO and the Chairman gave national cell-addition figures 2x apart

Manish Gupta, p9: "Next year, the addition of the cells will be in our country

**M13. Three calls, zero hedging disclosure, on the input that moved most.**
Premier discloses silver cost per watt rising "from about 1 cent to about 2.5

**M14. INA's Q4 FY26 margin held while every disclosed peer's compressed on
the same inputs, and INA offered no commodity explanation at all.**
Ravi Dusad attributes FY26 margin improvement to "operating leverage, improved
scale efficiencies, disciplined cost management" (Jun 2026, p5), with no
mention of silver, copper, freight or the war. In the same quarter, Waaree
reported a ~590 bps operating EBITDA margin decline and named the causes:
"the biggest impact which has taken up was the impact of silver pricing and
copper pricing... the cost of freight has gone through the roofs" (Abhishek
Pareek, May 2026, p9). Websol reported margin compression from silver and
softening cell prices (Sohan Lal Agarwal, Aug 2026, p13). Premier held margin
and an analyst attributed it explicitly to pre-bought stock, which management
confirmed: "building up stock ahead of the module line and also because of the
Middle East crisis that we've stocked up, we planned these inventory levels
and stocked up raw material" (Chiranjeev Saluja, May 2026, p8). INA's own raw
material cost rose ~190% against 61% revenue growth (analyst-stated,
uncorrected, Jun 2026, p10), and INA holds two to three months of raw
material. The plausible mechanism for a flat margin is the same one Premier
named, and it is a one-time buffer, not operating leverage. Management was
not asked and did not say.

**M15. The Feb 2026 "506 basis points" margin story is contradicted by INA's

Manish Gupta, Feb 2026, p3: "EBITDA margin expanded by more than 500 basis

**M16. The order book cannot simultaneously be 1.6-1.8 GW and 50% of FY27

Manish Gupta, Jun 2026, p5: "this year order book size is nearly 1.6 gigawatt

**M23. Backward integration into cells buys a policy spread, not a cost
advantage, and no stage says so.**

around USD0.04 to USD0.045 per watt peak basis in local markets, our

2026, p13). DERIVED: domestic cell manufacture costs 55-100% more than an
imported cell. The entire integration margin is the DCR price premium, which
exists only while ALMM-2 is enforced. B06 correctly found the ALMM-2 deferral
