## SECTION 1: MARKET DEFINITION

### 1A. Precise boundaries

- **Product scope**: condenser-graded transformer bushings — RIP/RIS (Resin
  Impregnated Paper/Synthetic, 83% of FY26 revenue), OIP (Oil Impregnated
  Paper, 10%), high-current bushings (4%), wall-through/oil-to-oil/other
  bushings (3%), plus retrofit, repair and testing services (6.8%,
  B04-bizmodel.yaml). Excludes bushing monitoring/sensor systems (a distinct
  market per web search — Transformer Bushing Monitoring System Market),
  full transformer manufacture, and non-condenser bushing types (simple
  porcelain/low-voltage types outside Yash's technology).
- **Geographic scope**: global market for TAM; India + emerging export
  markets (USA, LATAM, Europe, UK) for Yash's actual served footprint.
  Export today ~4-5% of revenue, target 20%+ in 2-3 years (Call-3, 05-concall.md
  section 1B).
- **Customer scope**: transformer OEMs (BHEL, CG Power, GE Vernova, Hitachi
  Energy, Siemens, Transformer and Rectifiers India, Toshiba — RHP p.150,
  "Key Transformer OEM Suppliers") plus utilities/DISCOMs and private EPCs
  directly for retrofit/repair services.
- **Channel scope**: direct B2B sale to OEMs/utilities; export via
  distribution partnerships (Weidmann — Europe/North Africa; Electrolink —
  UK/Ireland) and the new Yash HV USA subsidiary (05-concall.md, AR FY26).
- **Price segment**: engineered mid-to-premium component; RIP:OIP price
  ratio ~4-4.5x domestically, ~2x globally at similar kV (Call-1,
  05-concall.md section 3B). Bushing cost = 2-6% (~5% typical) of transformer
  value; every transformer needs 6-7 bushings (Call-3).
- **Voltage-range capability boundary**: current manufacturing range
  33kV-245kV; Vadodara greenfield extends to 550kV from H2FY27 (committed,
  not yet commercial — B07 capex figure, 05-concall.md).

## SECTION 2: TAM ESTIMATION, MULTIPLE METHODS

### Method 1 — Top-down

Global bushing market, all insulation types, CY2024 = **₹26,960 Cr**
(Ken Research/RHP p.144). Subtracting non-condenser types Yash does not
primarily serve (oil-communicating ₹1,959 Cr + others ₹2,400 Cr in CY2024,
RHP p.146 segmentation chart) leaves condenser-type (RIP ₹16,697 Cr + OIP
₹5,904 Cr = ₹22,601 Cr) as the more conservative, product-fit-adjusted
top-down TAM.

- **TAM conservative (condenser-type only, 2024)**: ₹22,601 Cr
- **TAM realistic (all bushing types, management/Ken Research headline, 2024)**: ₹26,960 Cr
- Forward: condenser-type grows to ₹40,242 Cr by CY2034F (RIP ₹32,807 Cr +
  OIP ₹7,435 Cr, RHP p.146), implying ~5.9% blended CAGR — consistent with
  the headline 5.6% figure.

### Method 2 — Bottom-up

Unit = bushing content as % of power transformer value. Management states
2-6% (typically ~5%) of transformer value is bushings, 6-7 bushings per
transformer (Call-A, Call-3, 05-concall.md).

- India: power transformer market FY24 = ₹33,400 Cr (RHP "334.0 hundred
  crores," p.163). At 2-6% bushing content: ₹668-2,004 Cr implied bushing
  market. RHP's own stated India bushing figure, ₹1,528.2 Cr FY24
  (line 11283), sits inside this range at an implied ~4.6% ratio — internal
  cross-check **passes**.
- Global: power transformer market 2024 = ₹6,23,350 Cr ("6,233.5 hundred
  crores," RHP p.163, line 10816). At 2-6%: ₹12,467-37,401 Cr implied;
  midpoint (5%) = ₹31,168 Cr. RHP's stated global bushing figure (₹26,960
  Cr) sits inside this range at an implied ~4.3% ratio — cross-check
  **passes**, and corroborates Method 1's order of magnitude independently.

### Method 3 — Peer revenue aggregation

Global bushing-segment revenue for the named majors (Hitachi, Trench
[Italy], GE — "~80% of global market" combined per Call-2) is **NOT
FOUND**: bushings are a sub-segment bundled inside much larger
transformer/electrical-equipment businesses and not separately disclosed.
Domestic peer Massa Izolyator/Mehru JV shows revenue of only ₹5.3 Cr
(FY22-23, RHP p.161) — immaterial in scale, confirming the "concentrated
among few players" read (RHP p.150) but offering no usable aggregation
base. No credible unorganised-sector estimate exists in the corpus (this is
a licence/qualification-gated engineered component, not a fragmented
unorganised-prone category) — **Confidence: LOW, data-constrained**. This
method cannot independently triangulate a number here; Methods 1, 2 and 4
carry the estimate.

### Method 4 — Import substitution

India's bushing imports (world, HS code 85049010) = ₹473.4 Cr in CY2023,
down from ₹840.1 Cr in CY2019 (-44%, RHP p.150-151, Trade Map/Ken Research).
Domestic content today: ~90% of OIP bushing content is domestically
produced vs only 20-50% of RIP content (RHP p.150). Against India's FY24
bushing market of ₹1,528.2 Cr, the ₹473.4 Cr import figure implies **~31%
of India bushing demand is still import-sourced** (calendar-year vs
fiscal-year basis mismatch noted; directionally sound, not exact) —
this is the direct answer to **LBF4**. No PLI-scheme-for-bushings target
number exists in the corpus (raised once in Call-A, dropped after — per
05-concall.md 1C, "DROPPED after Call-A") — **NOT FOUND**. This ₹473 Cr pool
is a *subset* of the India TAM already counted in Method 1, not additive;
it represents the substitutable revenue Vadodara's RIP-core localisation
targets from FY28 (05-concall.md 1B).

### Method 5 — Global benchmark

India = ₹1,528 Cr of the ₹26,960 Cr global bushing TAM (2024) = ~5.7% of
global bushing demand, despite India's outsized and faster-growing grid
build-out (CEA National Electricity Plan: transformation capacity to
expand from ~1,429 GVA to 2,345 GVA by 2032, +64%; ₹7.93 lakh Cr planned
transmission-sector capex 2026-27 to 2035-36 — web search, CEA/PIB, live
Sep-2026). This underpenetration is directional support for RHP's own
higher India CAGR assumption (7.0%) vs the global rate (5.6%); it is used
for direction only, per the method's design, not folded into the headline
TAM number.

## SECTION 3: SAM & SOM

### 3A. SAM

Applying the five filters (product fit, geography, channel, customer,
capability) to the realistic TAM, the binding filter is **capability**:
Yash's current manufacturing range tops out at 245kV. RHP's own Ken
Research SAM figure, computed the same way, is used directly: **global
bushing market up to 245kV, CY2024 = ₹15,638 Cr** (RHP p.167). This already
embeds the product-fit and capability filters; geography (global, since
Yash both serves India and exports), channel and customer filters do not
further narrow it materially at this stage (Yash sells to the same OEM/
utility customer set worldwide it already qualifies with).

- SAM (2024) = ₹15,638 Cr
- SAM (2026, grown at the 5.6% global bushing CAGR to the run date) =
  15,638 × 1.056² = **₹17,439 Cr**
- SAM as % of realistic TAM: 17,439 / 26,960 = **64.7%** (2024 ratio of
  15,638/26,960 = 58.0%; the run-date-grown figure is used below for the
  SOM/current-share calculations, so 64.7% is reported as sam_pct_of_tam;
  both are shown for transparency).

### 3B. SOM at 3 and 5 years, with arithmetic shown

Current SAM share (FY26 revenue ÷ SAM 2026): 235.2 / 17,439 = **1.35%**.
(RHP's own "~1%" claim uses FY24 revenue against the 2024 SAM: consistent
directionally.)

Share-gain path applied per the house rule (1-2pp/3yr normal; 3-5pp/3yr
aggressive with capacity+execution). Vadodara roughly doubles physical
capacity (7,272 units FY26 → combined 15,000-16,000 units/yr post-
greenfield) and the industry is genuinely supply-constrained (only 12-13
independent bushing makers globally vs 250+ transformer OEMs adding
capacity, per Call-2) — this supports using the upper end of "normal" to
lower end of "aggressive," not the top of "aggressive."

**3-year SOM (FY29, run-date + 3yr):**
- SAM(FY29) = 15,638 × 1.056⁵ = ₹20,540 Cr
- Share gain: +1.5pp (midpoint of normal band; capacity not yet fully
  ramped in year 1-2 of this window) → 1.35% + 1.5% = 2.85%
- **SOM(FY29) = 20,540 × 2.85% ≈ ₹585 Cr**
- Implied revenue CAGR, FY26 (₹235.2 Cr) → FY29 (₹585 Cr), 3 years:
  (585/235.2)^(1/3) − 1 = **~35.5%**

**5-year SOM (FY31, run-date + 5yr):**
- SAM(FY31) = 15,638 × 1.056⁷ = ₹22,900 Cr
- Share gain: +3.0pp (aggressive band, justified by capacity doubling,
  export ramp to 20%+, and Sukrut consolidation) → 1.35% + 3.0% = 4.35%
- **SOM(FY31) = 22,900 × 4.35% ≈ ₹996 Cr**
- Implied revenue CAGR, FY26 → FY31, 5 years: (996/235.2)^(1/5) − 1 =
  **~33.5%**

**Cross-check against management's own guide**: these bottom-up,
conservative-bias SOM CAGRs (35.5% at 3yr, 33.5% at 5yr) land close to but
below management's guided 38-42% forward CAGR (05-concall.md, Call-3) —
this is a **supportive**, non-circular cross-check: the SOM math was built
independently from a third-party market-size anchor, not from management's
growth guide, and still lands in the same order of magnitude, slightly
under it. **This is the formal handoff to Stage 11.**

For contrast, management's own 2029F claim (RHP, 5% of the *expanded*
440kV-inclusive SAM of ₹27,121 Cr) = ₹1,356 Cr — 2.3x this stage's more
conservative FY29 SOM of ₹585 Cr, because it (a) uses the bigger, not-yet-
commercial 440kV SAM and (b) assumes a faster 0.96%→5% share jump. Both
management's number and this stage's number point the same direction
(large multi-year re-rating of share), but this stage's figure is the more
defensible one for valuation purposes given the capacity timeline in 3C.

### 3C. Capacity cross-check

FY26 blended revenue-per-unit (crude, company-wide, not bushing-specific
since blended unit economics are NOT FOUND per B04): ₹235.2 Cr / 7,272
units = **₹32.3 lakh/unit**. Applying this flat (no price/mix uplift) to
the stated combined post-greenfield capacity of 15,000-16,000 units/yr
(B04, 05-concall.md) gives a **flat-price capacity ceiling of ₹485-517
Cr/yr**.

- **FY29 SOM (₹585 Cr) exceeds the ceiling by ₹68-100 Cr** — a modest gap,
  plausibly closed by price/mix improvement alone (export ramp to 20%+ at
  a stated 30-40% price premium; RIP mix shift toward higher-value product,
  Call-2/Call-1 pricing commentary).
- **FY31 SOM (₹996 Cr) exceeds the ceiling by ₹479-511 Cr** — a large gap.
  Adding Sukrut's own separate management target (₹150-160 Cr by ~FY30/31,
  a different JV entity/component line, additive rather than counted
  inside the 15,000-16,000 bushing-unit capacity) brings the combined
  capacity-implied ceiling to **~₹635-677 Cr**, still **₹319-361 Cr short**
  of the FY31 SOM.

**Which side is optimistic**: the FY29 gap is small and closeable by
price/mix alone, consistent with management's own stated levers. The FY31
gap of ₹319-361 Cr is large enough that the **5-year SOM revenue path is
the more optimistic side** unless (a) blended realisation per unit rises
materially beyond flat, or (b) a further capacity expansion beyond the
currently-announced Vadodara-plus-Sukrut plan is added — neither of which
is disclosed or quantified in the corpus today. **capacity_check: gap of
~₹320-360 Cr by FY31, SOM side is the more optimistic one at the 5-year
mark; FY29 gap (~₹70-100 Cr) is small and plausibly closed by price/mix.**

## SECTION 5: SUMMARY & RUNWAY

### 5A. Funnel

TAM (conservative, 2024) ₹22,601 Cr → TAM (realistic, 2024) ₹26,960 Cr →
SAM (2024) ₹15,638 Cr → SAM (2026, grown) ₹17,439 Cr → current SAM share
1.35% (FY26 revenue ₹235.2 Cr) → SOM 3yr (FY29) ₹585 Cr → SOM 5yr (FY31)
₹996 Cr.

### 5B. Runway assessment

- Revenue headroom = SAM(2026) ÷ current revenue = 17,439 / 235.2 =
  **74.1x**
- TAM growth rate = **5.6%** (global bushing CAGR, Ken Research)
- Company CAGR vs TAM: Yash's historical delivered CAGR (FY22-26, 29-57%
  across periods per RHP/concall) vastly exceeds the 5.6% TAM growth rate —
  the company is **gaining share, not riding the market** (necessarily
  true given SOM math requires share to rise from 1.35% toward 3-4%).
- Years to saturate SAM at a 35% company CAGR: ln(74.1)/ln(1.35) ≈ **14.4
  years** — no near-term saturation risk.

### 5C. Runway classification: **STRONG**

Reasoning: headroom (74.1x) alone would support MASSIVE, but the TAM
growth rate itself is moderate (5.6%, not explosive), and Section 3C shows
the binding constraint on near-term capture is **physical capacity and
price/mix**, not market size. The market is not the gating factor here;
execution and capital deployment are. STRONG (not MASSIVE) reflects that
the headroom is real but the company cannot access most of it without
further capacity investment beyond what is currently disclosed.
