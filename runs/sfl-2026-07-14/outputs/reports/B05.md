### 2B. Excuse pattern analysis

For each miss above, the reason given:
- **Showroom target miss**: attributed to Kurlon-driven "aberration" in the base year and net
  reclassification of MBO-to-EBO conversions — a mix of external factor (Kurlon integration
  scale) and internal planning revision. Classified **deflection-leaning** (the shortfall itself is
  never named as a miss; it is reframed as a natural normalisation).
- **Synergy tail delay**: attributed entirely to an overseas equipment supplier's delivery
  schedule. Classified **external-blame**, though plausible and specific (named the malleable-fibre
  machine each time).
- **Debt reduction pace**: no excuse offered; management simply restated updated numbers
  without acknowledging the original glide path was slower than delivered. Classified **silence**
  (the deceleration versus the original promise was never explicitly flagged by management;
  it surfaces only by comparing calls).
- **Growth target shortfall (11% vs 12-15%/15%+)**: framed positively as "halfway there" (Q3 FY26
  call) rather than as underperformance. Classified **deflection** via reframing, though the
  underlying numbers were disclosed transparently.
- **EBO count discrepancy**: management acknowledged the question both times it was raised
  and attempted a partial explanation (reclassification, format buckets) but never produced a
  reconciling number. Classified **deflection**, not stonewalling.

**Pattern check**: Management does credit itself for the positive numbers (record top line,
record EBITDA, first dividend, Furlenco turnaround) prominently and proactively in every
opening statement. It does **not** proactively call out the showroom-target miss, the synergy-tail
delay (each quarter's admission of delay is only volunteered when directly re-asked), or the
slower-than-guided debt paydown pace — these surface through cross-quarter reading, not
through management's own framing. On the positive side, management did say plainly, in
response to a direct question in Q4 FY26 ("what specifically... have we brought in... and the
sustenance of the same"), "we struggled with it for a year or two, and I think you all are
witness to that how we struggled" (Amit Gupta, Q4 FY26 call, p.15) — a genuine, unprompted
admission of past difficulty, which is a positive credibility signal. Hard topics (raw-material
spike, competitive intensity, Furlenco's earlier SleepX losses) are generally raised only when
an analyst pushes, not proactively, with the exception of the Middle East war risk, which
Rahul Gautam raised unprompted in his opening remarks in Q4 FY26 (p.2).

### 2D. What they are NOT saying

- **Kurlon-vs-Sleepwell segment profitability**: declined every time it is asked (Q2 FY26 call,
  Amit Gupta, p.12) — plausible commercial-sensitivity reason, but it removes the ability to
  verify whether Kurlon's margin catch-up (mid-single-digit pre-acquisition to ~10% blended) is
  real or masked by Sleepwell cross-subsidy.
- **Tarang/Aaram volume as % of total mattress volume**: asked in Q1 FY26 (cross-ref) and again
  in Q3 FY26 (Bhavin Rupani) — both times management gives revenue/value growth but declines
  the volume-mix number, citing a wish to avoid "circular" discussion (Q3 FY26 call, Amit Gupta,
  p.12). This is also a repeated-question item, see 2E.
- **Quantified market-share data**: management repeatedly asserts "the organized sector play is
  improving" (Q3 FY26 call, Rakesh Chahar, p.19) but cites no third-party tracking study — this
  needs peer verification (see Section 4B).
- **Full magnitude/duration of the Middle East RM shock on FY27 margins**: management gives
  directional reassurance (Q4 FY26 call, Amit Gupta/Rakesh Chahar, p.9) but does not quantify a
  worst-case scenario for Q1/Q2 FY27, despite RM having risen 25-35% and price hikes only
  "partly" passed through in some channels (online not yet repriced as of Q3 FY26, per Rakesh
  Chahar, p.17).
- **Depreciation policy change's P&L impact is not framed as profit-flattering**: management
  presents the useful-life extension purely as an accounting-accuracy correction (Q4 FY26 call,
  Amit Gupta, p.12) without acknowledging that it mechanically reduces FY27 depreciation by
  roughly Rs 35-40cr versus the prior ~Rs 180cr run-rate cited by an analyst in Q3 FY26 (Ravi
  Purohit, p.9), which flatters reported PAT precisely during the year the "turnaround" story is
  being sold to the market. This is flagged as a red flag below.

## SECTION 3: COMPETITIVE INTELLIGENCE FROM CONCALLS

### 3A. What management says about competitors (with credibility check)

- "A good part of our competition is looking to raise money [in] the equity markets... balance
  sheet will be pretty much pump-primed" (Ritesh Shah's framing, Q1 FY26 cross-ref, echoed by
  management's response that store expansion is the answer). By Q3 FY26, Rakesh Chahar
  states competitive discipline has **not** improved yet ("no change as such that they have
  reduced the competitive activity," p.19), hoping "some discipline... does come in" as more
  peers list publicly. **Credibility check**: directionally consistent with the known IPO/listing
  activity among mattress-adjacent D2C players; plausible and should be checked against
  Wakefit commentary at stage 6.
- Online price hikes: "on the online, we are operating at a similar level... online is the only
  one where the price increase has not been passed on" (Q3 FY26 call, Rakesh Chahar, p.17) —
  a specific, checkable claim about channel-level pricing behaviour.
- Offline price hikes: "most of the brands including the regional players have taken price
  hike" (Q3 FY26 call, Rakesh Chahar, p.17) — checkable against peer commentary.
- Australia: main competitor reportedly "wanting to exit," business "for sale about a year back
... did not find any takers," appears to be "shrinking" (Q3 FY26 call, Rahul Gautam, p.20-21;
  reiterated Q4 FY26 call, p.14) — a specific, repeatable claim, though not verifiable against the
  three named India peers (Centuryply/Greenlam/Wakefit are not Australia mattress-foam
  players); flagged as SFL-specific and likely not checkable at stage 6.
- Unorganised sector bearing the brunt of new entrant competition: "there is a sizable
  unorganized sector existing in this segment and most of the new people who are coming in...
  it is that market, the unorganized one, which will get impacted" (Q1 FY26 cross-ref, Rahul
  Gautam) — a self-serving framing that deserves scrutiny; if new, well-funded D2C entrants are
  actually taking organised share from SFL rather than from the unorganised base, this claim
  would not hold.

### 3B. Industry and market intelligence

- E-commerce mattress market (organised) sized at ~Rs 2,000cr, growing 15-20%, SFL targeting
  ~30% share "in coming few years" (Q2 FY26 call, Rahul Gautam, p.14) — growth-rate claim for
  peer check.
- India mattress market: ~85% still unorganised/traditional as of Q4 FY26 (Amit Gupta, p.6) —
  large addressable-market claim underpinning the U2O growth story.
- TDI/polyol price trajectory across the year (see 1B table) — directly checkable raw-material
  trend claim against any peer with foam/mattress or plywood/laminate RM exposure.
- Middle East war disrupting TDI supply: main domestic supplier GNFC had shutdowns
  (technical, then gas-related in March), forcing reliance on costlier imports/traders; SFL says it
  pre-emptively hedged with a new supplier partner (Korea/Japan/China mix) months before the
  disruption (Q4 FY26 call, Rakesh Chahar, p.16) — a claim of foresight that is checkable against
  peer RM-sourcing commentary.
- Market-share shift from unorganised to organised: asserted qualitatively, no third-party data
  cited (Q3 FY26 call, Rakesh Chahar, p.19; and Q4 FY26, Amit Gupta, p.6).
- Depreciation-policy peer context: SFL's foaming machine at Greater Noida cited as 22 years
  old and still productive, used to justify extending useful life to 40-50 years (Q4 FY26 call,
  Amit Gupta, p.11) — an industry-asset-life claim that could be sanity-checked against peer
  capex/depreciation disclosures.

### 3D. Customer and order book signals

- Kurlon showroom expansion specifically targeted at North and West India, where brand equity
