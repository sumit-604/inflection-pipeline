# Visaka Industries Ltd (VISAKAIND), Phase 1 evidence narrative
Run: runs/visakaind-2026-09-05 | Run date: 2026-09-05 | Phase 1 (evidence only)

Phase 1 carries no valuation. Stages 10 and 11 did not run, the Mental Model

## BUSINESS UNDERSTANDING NARRATIVE

Visaka Industries makes four products in factories and sells most of them
through dealers. Cement roofing sheets are 53% of revenue, pressed from
cement, fly ash and imported chrysotile fibre to roof village homes and farm
sheds. A rural buyer picks them because they cost less than a steel roof and
stay cooler under it. Fibre cement boards and panels, sold as V-Next, are 30%
of revenue and replace plywood and gypsum in walls, ceilings and facades.
Synthetic yarn is 15% of revenue, spun on twin air jet machines at one Nagpur
plant for fabric mills. ATUM Solar Roof is the fourth line, a roof panel that
generates power, and its revenue has never been disclosed.

The roofing customer is a rural household reached through 4,974 dealers and
stockists across 28 states, while the boards customer is a developer or
architect billed increasingly direct. The yarn customer is a named fabric
mill: Siyaram, Raymond, Arvind Mills, Grasim Bhiwani and D'Decor. No customer
takes more than 10% of revenue, so stickiness sits in specification and
certification, not in contracts.

Demand today runs on rural income and house building, and PMAY-Gramin and
PMAY-Urban physical progress sets the floor under roofing volumes. IMD monsoon
bulletins, MSP farm income data and chrysotile asbestos fibre import volumes
move the roofing line quarter to quarter. The domestic steel roofing price
index decides whether a buyer picks cement or metal, and BirlaNu quarterly
results read that same demand at a competitor.

Boards should grow faster than roofing, because BirlaNu puts boards industry
growth at 10% to 14% a year against 2% to 3% long run roofing growth. Green
building codes, FY27 government capex of Rs 12.2 lakh crore and PM Surya Ghar
enrolment are the external series to watch.

The moat sits in boards and nowhere else with confidence: 86% capacity
utilisation, GreenPro, TUV and ISI certification and architect specification
give partial pricing power. Roofing has 40 years of distribution reach but no
pricing power, and the company states it could not fully pass on cost benefits
to the market. Yarn has no moat, and ATUM patents in India, the United States
and South Africa carry no revenue after eight years. The emerging moat scan
scored MODEST at 14.5 of a 92 point ceiling, and Categories 21 and 22, talent
asymmetry and cannibalization barrier, both scored zero.

## Roofing cash funds boards growth, solar unproven

Visaka is four businesses inside one listed company. Cement roofing sheets are
53% of revenue and sell to rural India through a dealer network built over 40
years. Fibre cement boards and panels are 30% and sell to developers and
architects. Synthetic yarn is 15% and sells to fabric mills. ATUM Solar Roof
is the fourth, and after eight years since its 2018 launch the company has
never published a revenue figure for it.

The transition claim is a climb out of roofing. Non asbestos revenue rose from
43% of sales in FY25 to 47% in FY26. The mix moved from 56% roofing in FY23 to
53% in FY26, and boards moved from 25% to 30% over the same four years.
Boards grew 19% in FY26 and run at 86% of a 3.56 lakh tonne capacity, which is
the company's own evidence for pricing power in that line. The claim runs
ahead of the commitment. Management repeats a Rs 450 Cr boards capex plan
through 2030 three times in the annual report, while the audited capital
commitments note shows Rs 16.87 Cr actually contracted at 31 March 2026. The
50:50 legacy to new age mix promised for FY25 came in at about 58:42.

Gate 0 scores the company AVERAGE. Core score is 56 of 100, moat score 12 of
60, grand total 68 of 160, with two moats confirmed and one deal breaker
fired. The deal breaker is a real three year trough, not a data artefact:
return on capital employed ran 8.3% in FY23, 3.0% in FY24 and 3.5% in FY25,
and return on equity ran 7.3%, 0.3% and 0.02% across the same years.

FY26 looks like a recovery year, and part of it is. Reported profit before tax
was Rs 110 Cr, of which Rs 60 Cr is a one off gain on selling land in Ahmedabad
and land with a building in Kanchipuram. Strip that out and pre tax profit was
Rs 51 Cr, return on capital employed falls to roughly 6% to 7%, and interest
cover falls from a headline 4.3 times to 2.5 times. The operating recovery
underneath is genuine: consolidated operating cash flow was Rs 183 Cr, up 61%,
and the cash flow statement explicitly removes the land gain in its own
reconciliation. Two qualifiers sit against it. About Rs 46 Cr of the standalone
Rs 183 Cr operating cash flow is a working capital release rather than margin,
and it does not repeat if the receivables book has to expand again for growth.
Consolidated debt fell from Rs 534 Cr in FY24 to Rs 302 Cr in FY26, but roughly
Rs 70 Cr of land and building sale proceeds helped pay for that, not organic
cash alone. The trade receivables tail older than one year rose from 6.1% of
gross to 9.6% while the loss allowance stayed flat at Rs 8.4 Cr.

Management says more than it delivers. The delivery record scores 4 promises
met, 2 partial and 7 missed, and the credibility grade is D. The Midnapore
plant was guided at about Rs 100 Cr and capitalised at Rs 131 Cr, and it was
commissioned in December 2023 against a September 2023 date. Boards revenue
was guided to about Rs 450 Cr for FY24 and came in at Rs 389 Cr, down 4%. Yarn
margin was guided at 15% and above across two calls and collapsed to about
0.6% in FY25. Gross debt was guided down from Rs 410 Cr and rose to Rs 579 Cr
by the end of FY24. Ten ATUM Life stores were promised for FY23 and three exist
as of FY26. One caveat carries as much weight as the grade itself: the newest
transcript in the corpus is from August 2023, 37 months old, so this is a
prior on management style from 2022 and 2023, not a live signal. Whether calls
happened after August 2023 is not known from the corpus.

Governance is the stage that concerns most. The promoter verdict is CONCERN on
a scorecard of 2 clean, 3 caution and 5 red. An ED and Income Tax search under
FEMA and PMLA took place in November 2023 at the company's own Begumpet office
and at the Chairman's residences, over an Rs 8 Cr transfer from the Chairman's
personal account to Vigilance Security Services. The status of that matter as
of this run is not found in any primary source. The same entity has since
become an Rs 21 Cr lender to the company at 8% and was reclassified into the
promoter group with 4.83% of shares, an amount that reconciles almost exactly
with the unexplained 4.82 percentage point jump in the promoter category from
48.42% to 53.24%. The FY26 statutory remuneration annexure, signed by the
Chairman, states there was no increase in executive director pay, while the
same report's related party note shows the MD up 58% and the Joint MD up 107%
in the same year the land gain lifted the profit their commission is set on.
Foreign investors went the other way, from 0.45% of the register to 0.10%. No
transition evidence was found on the promoter file.

One peer with transcripts, BirlaNu, is available for triangulation, and it
complicates rather than confirms. BirlaNu names long run roofing growth at 2%
to 3% a year, below Visaka's own already reduced 3% to 5% floor. It puts boards
industry growth at 10% to 14%, well below the 25% to 30% Visaka guided for
V-Next. BirlaNu is adding boards capacity at Nellore and a newly approved
Hyderabad plant, funded debt first, at the same time Visaka narrates its own
Rs 450 Cr plan, which reads as an industry capacity race rather than a lone
expander. Neither company mentions chrysotile asbestos regulation, health or
litigation risk once across four peer calls and Visaka's whole corpus, even
though it underpins 53% of Visaka revenue. Two peer verdicts in this run are
themselves flagged as unsafe by the verifiers and are named in the gate
recommendation.

The market is a moderate pond, not a large one. Total addressable market across
roofing, boards and yarn triangulates to Rs 6,651 Cr to Rs 7,401 Cr, of which
Rs 6,429 Cr is servable, and Visaka already holds 25.6% of that servable
market. Three year capture of Rs 2,069 Cr and five year capture of Rs 2,441 Cr
imply revenue growth of 7.3% and 7.8% a year. Headroom is 3.8 times current
revenue and the runway class is MODERATE. The company's own annual report
cites a boards market of USD 4.8 bn, which is 26.7 times the Rs 1,500 Cr
figure triangulated from Visaka's own claimed share arithmetic and a direct
competitor's on record statement from August 2026, so the industry paragraphs
in the annual report are decorative rather than load bearing. ATUM sits
outside every one of those numbers by design, because eight years of zero
disclosed revenue gives nothing to size.
