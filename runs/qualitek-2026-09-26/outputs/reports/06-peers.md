## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

**2A DEMAND ENVIRONMENT.** All three peers describe steady-to-strong demand across FY26-FY27, driven by structural shifts (organized-vs-unorganized consolidation for Metropolis/Krsnaa; pharma/biologics R&D shift to India for Vimta), NOT a demand boom. Vimta and Metropolis both flag a temporary Q1 FY27 (Apr-Jun 2026) softness tied to the "Middle East crisis"/geopolitical disruption affecting food-testing import/export volumes (Vimta) and general seasonality (Metropolis notes weather-driven demand variability). None of the three peers describes an industry-wide demand boom of the kind that would corroborate an unstated Qualitek "demand surge" narrative; where growth outpaces the cited market CAGR (7-15% range across peers), management attributes it explicitly to market-SHARE gains from unorganized-to-organized migration or brand execution, not sector-wide acceleration. This is a mild caution against reading Qualitek's growth as riding an industry tailwind rather than execution/M&A.

**2B PRICING AND INPUT COSTS.** See Q6 above. Consistent theme across all three peers: modest, manageable input-cost inflation (reagents, chemicals, manpower), partially offset by GST relief (Metropolis, Krsnaa) and productivity/automation initiatives; none of the three shows a large gross-margin expansion. Pricing power is limited and calibrated: Metropolis explicitly delayed a routine annual price increase in FY26 to pass GST savings to customers ("we thought it's passing on to the consumer is the right thing to do"); Vimta states margins are being "maintained," not expanded, via pricing; Krsnaa has embedded 2-5% contractual rate escalations in PPP contracts but no discretionary pricing power.

**2C CAPEX CYCLE.** All three peers are EXPANDING capacity, not holding or cutting — this is an industry-wide capacity build, not a lone-expander situation. Vimta: ~INR100 Cr FY26 capex, ~INR80 Cr FY27 guided, new Genome Valley facility, biologics/CRADS build-out. Metropolis: INR55-65 Cr annual capex, network expansion to 750+ towns, "100 mini hubs" plan, genomics platform build. Krsnaa: cumulative ~INR5,000 million capex across Rajasthan PPP + MRI expansion, one of "the largest diagnostic infrastructure expansions undertaken in the country." This is an INDUSTRY-WIDE CAPACITY RACE, not a lone Qualitek build-out — Qualitek's FY27 Rs 63 Cr capex plan and new-lab strategy sits within a sector-wide pattern of aggressive capacity investment across testing/diagnostics peers, which somewhat de-risks the "is Qualitek overbuilding relative to demand" question, though all three peers ALSO report utilisation lag and margin drag from their own capacity builds (see Q3), so the capacity race itself does not guarantee near-term returns.

**2D COMPETITIVE MENTIONS.** No peer transcript mentions Qualitek by name or by unmistakable description. Vimta names its competitors as SGS, Eurofins, Intertek, Bureau Veritas, Covance, Charles River — all large multinational TIC/CRO players, none Indian small-caps. Metropolis and Krsnaa's competitive references are healthcare-diagnostics specific (Reliance's genomics rumor, other diagnostic chains) and do not touch industrial/TIC testing. This is a silence, not a finding either way; Qualitek is too small and in too different a sub-segment to appear in these peers' competitive framing.

**2E RISKS PEERS DISCUSS THAT QUALITEK DOES NOT.**
- Manpower attrition as an explicit, named, industry-wide risk: Vimta states "attrition is quite high across the industry... that's going to be the norm" (Feb-2026 call) — Qualitek's filings (per B04/B05) do not name attrition risk at all despite being a skilled-labour-dependent testing business.
- Leadership/key-person departures disclosed with specific framing and reassurance: Krsnaa lost its CFO, CBO mid-period and addressed it directly on-call with continuity framing (Feb-2026, May-2026 calls) — Qualitek has had company-secretary cessations (per B00) but no equivalent open discussion of the departures' business impact.
- Geopolitical/tariff pass-through risk to input costs and logistics named specifically and quantified in direction (Vimta, "war" impact on food-testing import/export and consumables lead times) — Qualitek's filings do not discuss any geopolitical or input-logistics risk.
- Explicit government-payment-system transition risk (SNA-SPARSH, RBI-link account rollouts) as the NAMED mechanical cause of receivable delays (Krsnaa) — Qualitek's own receivables disclosure (per B00 LBF2) does not name any comparable payment-system mechanism, leaving the debtor-day story less evidenced than Krsnaa's.
- Regulatory/pricing-benchmark risk from CGHS rate resets flowing through to competitor pricing (Metropolis, Krsnaa both discuss CGHS price-benchmark dynamics for existing and future tenders) — not applicable to Qualitek's segment but illustrates that peers routinely name specific regulatory pricing mechanisms as forward risks/opportunities, a level of specificity absent from Qualitek's disclosure.

## PART 4: TRIANGULATION SUMMARY

- Claims verified: 3 of 6 (Q1 debtor days, Q3 new-lab margin trajectory, Q4 industry growth-rate disclosure norm)
- Claims partially verified: 1 of 6 (Q6 input costs — directional match, magnitude mismatch)
- Claims contradicted: 2 of 6 (Q2 related-party disclosure norm — peers set a HIGHER bar than Qualitek meets; Q5 guidance walk-down acknowledgment — peers acknowledge misses explicitly, Qualitek does not)
- Claims unverifiable: 0 of 6

**Single most consequential contradiction.** Both Vimta and Krsnaa missed prior stated growth targets by wide margins in the exact same period Qualitek was missing its own (Vimta ~25% short of its INR500 Cr FY26 run-rate goal; Krsnaa FY26 growth of ~1% against a 15-16% guide), and in BOTH cases management acknowledged the miss on the call when asked, naming a cause. Qualitek's FY25 revenue miss (~22%) and EBITDA-margin dilution (30% to 25-27%) are never surfaced or reconciled in any Qualitek document. The behavioral norm in this peer set is "miss and explain, when asked"; Qualitek's pattern is "miss and stay silent, unasked." This should raise, not lower, the weight Role 2/3 place on the credibility grade.

**Single strongest independent confirmation.** Qualitek's debtor-day profile (124-150 days) is closely matched by Krsnaa's own PPP-sector receivables (124-155+ days across four consecutive quarters), explicitly attributed by Krsnaa to government payment-system mechanics (RBI link account rollout, SNA-SPARSH transition) rather than credit risk or collectability failure. This independently supports classifying Qualitek's cash-conversion weakness as channel-structural (tied to government/PPP-adjacent payer mechanics) rather than purely GROWTH-INDUCED mismanagement — though this reclassification cuts against Qualitek because "structural to a channel Qualitek chose to lean into" is not obviously more benign than "growth-induced and temporary."

**Overall: the peer set complicates the main company's narrative, more than it supports or undercuts it wholesale.** On operational mechanics — new-lab loss curves, capacity-race context, input-cost stability, debtor-day norms in government-adjacent channels — the peer set is broadly consistent with and corroborative of Qualitek's own disclosed figures, suggesting Qualitek's operating challenges are not unusual for the sector it has moved into. But on GOVERNANCE and DISCLOSURE BEHAVIOR — related-party acquisition transparency, and open acknowledgment of guidance misses — the peer set sets a visibly higher bar that Qualitek does not clear, based on direct comparison rather than mere absence of counter-evidence. The peer evidence is symmetric: it neither indicts Qualitek's business model nor exonerates its disclosure practice.

## PART 5: CROSS-PEER HYPOTHESIS

Across all three peers, a common pattern emerges that none of them states explicitly: the sector's margin story in FY26-27 is being told almost entirely through OPERATING LEVERAGE AND INTEGRATION MECHANICS (network densification, lab-to-center ratios, barcoding/vendor consolidation, "productivity" programs), NOT through gross-margin or pricing improvement. Vimta explicitly caps its own margin ambition ("we don't expect to see higher ones than these"); Metropolis attributes margin gains to "material productivity" and network ratios, not price; Krsnaa's margin recovery is entirely a function of new-project utilisation maturing, not price or gross-margin gain. Combined with the observation that ALL THREE peers are simultaneously in an active, large-scale capex/capacity-expansion phase (Q2C above) that is itself DEPRESSING near-term margins even as it is billed as the future margin driver — this suggests the testing/diagnostics services sector in India, as a whole, is currently in a phase where reported margins UNDERSTATE steady-state economics across nearly every listed name, because everyone is simultaneously building capacity ahead of utilisation. This is testable: if the hypothesis holds, sector-wide EBITDA margins across Vimta, Metropolis, and Krsnaa should show a visible step-up in FY28-29 (once FY26-27 capex cohorts mature to 3-4 year utilisation, per the Core Diagnostics and Rajasthan timelines both peers themselves name), even without further capex acceleration. If margins instead plateau or compress further in FY28-29 despite this capex build maturing, the hypothesis is falsified and the sector's capacity race would be better read as a structural feature (perpetual reinvestment need, not a temporary J-curve) — which would matter directly for how Qualitek's own new-lab drag (Rs 3.3 Cr H1 FY26 loss across 9 labs) should be read: as a temporary phase (bullish) or a permanent feature of the growth model (bearish, since Qualitek would then never "graduate" out of a state of margin-depressing new capacity).
