## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

**2A Demand environment.** All three peers describe demand as intact but uneven: PRAJIND's core ethanol/bioenergy domestic demand is described as slowing in greenfield ("first-generation ethanol domestic business continued to experience a slowdown in greenfield projects," Aug-2026 call) while export and CBG inquiry pipelines stay active with finalisation delays. SETL describes continued export growth ("FY26 our revenue is doubled, export revenue," May-2026 call) with occasional client-driven shipment timing slips. HLEGLAS describes a split market: "pharma and API sectors are not yet" deferring, but CDMO/agrochemical clients are deferring decisions since February 2026 due to the geopolitical situation (Jun-2026 call). None of the three describes an outright pharma-infrastructure demand boom matching Fabtech's framing of "unprecedented" opportunity; the peer picture is "resilient in pharma, softer in adjacent segments, geopolitically delayed at the margin" rather than a broad-based capex race. This complicates, rather than confirms, Fabtech's most bullish framing, even though it does not contradict the underlying delay-not-cancellation mechanic (Q3 above).

**2B Pricing and input costs.** Confirmed sector-wide cost pressure: SETL cites rising metal/steel cost and calls it an industry-wide, not company-specific, problem ("not only us, whole industry is facing," May-2026 call); HLEGLAS flags high-cost raw material affecting margin (Nov-2025 call); PRAJIND quantifies a smaller (1-1.5%) execution-cost escalation tied to project-delay-driven site costs (Jun-2026 call). No peer reports pricing power sufficient to fully offset this (SETL keeps freight in the customer's contractual scope rather than absorbing it; PRAJIND absorbs the escalation as a margin hit). This matches Fabtech's margin-pressure narrative in direction; magnitude (Fabtech's 33%/43%) is unconfirmed and looks large next to PRAJIND's 1-1.5% for an analogous mechanism.

**2C Capex cycle.** Mixed, not a uniform race. SETL is expanding aggressively (new Unit 5 capacity, C2C acquisition, GScale/data-centre diversification, targeting to become "the largest glass lining equipment manufacturer"). HLEGLAS made an inorganic move (Omeras GmbH, Aug-2025) explicitly to diversify beyond its "traditional chemical and API industry clientele" and is investing in Middle East sales presence ahead of demand. PRAJIND is holding/investing cautiously, citing "long gestation period" investments (Praj GenX) it must be "patient about," alongside acknowledged margin pressure. This is neither a lone-expander situation nor an industry-wide capacity race: it is a peer set with two expanders pursuing diversification/geography plays (SETL, HLEGLAS) and one (PRAJIND) holding capex steady while absorbing cost pressure. Fabtech's own acquisition-led Saudi/Italy expansion sits closer to the SETL/HLEGLAS pattern (inorganic, geography-driven diversification) than to a sector-wide capacity build.

**2D Competitive mentions.** No peer transcript names Fabtech Technologies or gives an unmistakable description of it (checked across all 12 transcripts; zero matches). This absence is itself informative given Fabtech's claimed positioning against only "unnamed European" players and its self-description as increasingly prominent in Saudi/GCC pharma-plant EPC — if Fabtech were a recognised competitive force in the segments SETL, HLEGLAS or PRAJIND touch, an analyst or management reference might be expected, though the segment overlap is genuinely partial (see Q6), so the silence is weak, not strong, evidence.

**2E Risks peers discuss that Fabtech's own calls do not.** (i) SETL and HLEGLAS both discuss customer-side inspection/decision postponement as a distinct risk channel separate from geopolitics ("customer inspections postponed," SETL Nov-2025 call; a ready shipment held because "client is asking to wait," SETL May-2026 call) — Fabtech's calls attribute delay only to war/donor-funding, never to routine client-side inspection or acceptance slippage, which peers show is also a live channel. (ii) PRAJIND explicitly quantifies and names "cost escalation... because the sites are open... not getting completely closed on the basis of the work" as a distinct margin-risk mechanism tied to project delay duration — Fabtech's own concalls describe RMC/execution cost inflation but never frame it as a function of how long a delayed order sits half-executed, which is a more precise, testable mechanism than "geopolitical disruption" alone. (iii) HLEGLAS names a specific bifurcation within its client base (pharma/API resilient, CDMO/agrochemical deferring) that Fabtech's own segment commentary (largely pharma/vaccine/veterinary, qualitative only) does not attempt, despite repeated analyst requests for a segment breakdown (per B05 2D).

## PART 4: TRIANGULATION SUMMARY

- Claims verified: 1 of 6 fully VERIFIED (Q3), 3 of 6 PARTIALLY VERIFIED (Q1, Q2 on direction, Q4), 1 UNVERIFIABLE (Q5), 1 mixed CONTRADICTED/UNVERIFIABLE (Q6).
- Claims contradicted: 1 (Q6, the "no Indian peer" framing; priority item for synthesis given it touches Fabtech's stated competitive moat).
- The single most consequential contradiction: Fabtech claims to compete only against unnamed European turnkey players, never Indian or Chinese ones. SETL and HLEGLAS both describe mixed Indian/international competitive sets in adjacent categories, undercutting the "no Indian peer" framing as a general industry claim, even though neither peer operates in Fabtech's specific full-scope pharma-plant design-build niche, so the contradiction is partial, not a clean kill of the claim.
- The single strongest independent confirmation: PRAJIND (Jun-2026 call) independently and unprompted describes the identical causal chain Fabtech uses to explain its own cost pressure — geopolitical/execution delay keeps project sites open longer, driving cost escalation and margin compression even as revenue stays flat — matching Fabtech's RMC/execution-cost inflation narrative in mechanism, though not in magnitude (PRAJIND: 1-1.5%; Fabtech: 33%/43%).
- Overall: the peer set complicates more than it either fully supports or fully undercuts Fabtech's narrative. The delay-not-cancellation framing for order finalisation (Q3) and the sector-wide cost-inflation direction (Q2) both hold up under triangulation, which is meaningful support for two of Fabtech's most consequential and most-doubted claims (feeding directly into LBF1). But the sectoral-boom framing (Q1) is only qualitatively echoed, the win-rate claim (Q5) sits in a disclosure vacuum across the whole peer set, and the "no Indian competitor" claim (Q6) reads as an overstated differentiation claim once set against how SETL and HLEGLAS describe their own competitive fields. Symmetrically: nothing in the peer set suggests Fabtech is fabricating its cost or delay narrative; equally, nothing in the peer set independently corroborates the specific magnitudes or the moat-like competitive claims management uses to justify the growth story's scale.

## PART 5: CROSS-PEER HYPOTHESIS (mandatory closing step)

Across all three peers, the businesses reporting the most acute cost pressure and margin drag (PRAJIND, most explicitly) are the ones with the largest share of construction/site-execution content mixed into their export contracts (Africa EPC-plus-supply orders explicitly flagged as margin-diluting versus supply-only international orders, Mar-2026 call), while the businesses that keep cost and schedule risk contractually with the customer (SETL: freight kept in customer's scope; HLEGLAS/Omeras: penalty-clause-bounded delivery) show comparatively less margin volatility from the same regional/geopolitical disruption. This suggests the sector is bifurcating not along "who is in MENA/Africa" (all three peers and Fabtech are), but along contract structure: turnkey/EPC-plus-construction exposure absorbs geopolitical and input-cost shocks directly into margin, while supply-only or customer-scoped-logistics contracts pass the same shocks through with less P&L damage. Fabtech, as a full turnkey design-build EPC player (the deepest construction-content exposure of any name discussed, including its own peers), sits at the high-absorption end of this spectrum — which would predict Fabtech's margin volatility should structurally exceed SETL's and HLEGLAS's over a full geopolitical-disruption cycle, not converge with them, even if the underlying demand and cost-inflation drivers are shared. This is testable: it predicts Fabtech's EBITDA margin swings (already seen: 37.6% to 46.7% contribution margin in one quarter, per B05) should remain structurally wider than SETL's or HLEGLAS's swings across the same macro period, and that any future Fabtech move toward percentage-of-completion billing (a live, deferred item per B05) would reduce this volatility only if it also changes the underlying contract risk allocation, not merely the revenue-recognition timing.
