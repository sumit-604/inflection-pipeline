## Business Understanding Narrative

**What does it do, and how does it make money?** QMS sits between a
pharmaceutical company and its patients. It does two things. It selects,
sources and distributes medical devices, some third-party and some under its
own brand. And it designs and runs patient-support programmes and screening
camps for drug makers, then reports the data back to them (Investor
Presentation FY2026 Q2, page 20). It works with over 50 pharmaceutical
companies including the top ten in India, names Pfizer, Sanofi, Abbott, Cipla,
Lupin, Sun, Novartis and Dr Reddy's among them, and began supplying hospitals
last fiscal year (Investor Presentation Q1 FY27, page 18).

The infrastructure is people, not plant: over 900 dieticians, 135 certified
medical laboratory technicians each covering a 35 kilometre radius, and more
than 5,000 addressable pincodes (Investor Presentation FY2026 Q2, page 20).

**What is changing?** The mix, and fast. Services were 24% of revenue in
Q1 FY26. They were 41% in Q1 FY27 (Investor Presentation Q1 FY27, page 7).
Product fell from 76% to 59% over the same year.

**Why now?** Because the two halves earn very different money and management
has said so on the record. Asked what margins each side carries, management
answered that services run at about 25% and products at "12% -- 10% to 12%
EBITDA level margins" (Earnings Call Q4 FY26, page 11). A business shifting
17 points of revenue mix from a 11% line to a 25% line is re-rating its own
margin without doing anything else.

**What must be true for the thesis to work?** The services contracts must
stick and must scale. Management says they are locked: "services side, we are
confident because our most of our costs and the contracts are locked in"
(Earnings Call Q4 FY26, page 11). The flagship programme, HUMRAHI, was
launched by Lupin and is managed entirely by QMS, with over 40,000 downloads
(Annual Report FY2025, page 67).

**What breaks it?** Customer power. The client list is every large pharma
company in India, and each programme is that client's programme. QMS runs
HUMRAHI; Lupin owns it. A services business with 25% margins and no contract
ownership invites its customers to insource once the volume is worth it.

## 2. Business model and archetype

Two reported segments, product and services, with very different economics.
Within product, the Q1 FY27 deck splits revenue by channel: pharma companies
and hospitals Rs 24 crore, point of care Rs 9 crore, e-commerce Rs 0.16 crore
(Investor Presentation Q1 FY27, page 18). Those three do not sum to the
Rs 56.9 crore quarter, so they are channels inside the business rather than the
whole of it. Read them as relative size, not as a reconciliation.

The services side runs patient-support programmes, health camps and screening.
The annual report states the economics plainly: "The profitability of the
service division far surpasses that of our other divisions" (Annual Report
FY2025, page 67). It also reports over 25,000 screening camps conducted and
more than 2 lakh patients screened.

**Archetype: outsourcing partner.** Client concentration, wallet share,
contract stickiness and price per programme are the variables. The device
distribution is a legacy converter-like business being out-grown.

## 3. Competitive advantages

Forming, in the services half. The asset is a national field force that a drug
maker cannot easily replicate for one brand: 900 dieticians, 135 technicians
with diagnostic devices, 5,000 pincodes, plus in-house customer service, health
educators, software development and programme managers (Investor Presentation
FY2026 Q2, page 20). Scale across many clients is what makes it affordable for
each client.

The own-brand device push is a second, weaker advantage. The company sells a
mesh nebuliser, blood pressure monitor, dual health monitor and personal
massager under its own label, which it says gives "control on quality and
supply chain, thereby offering avenues to expand margins" (Investor
Presentation FY2026 Q2, page 23).

Moat: forming in services, absent in products.

Read the two readings and name the separator. **Reading one: FY26 was a bad
year.** Revenue rose 11%, profit after tax fell 13%, and EBITDA margin fell
from 16.3% to 15.0%. **Reading two: FY26 was the year QMS paid to become a
services company.** Employee expenses rose 58%, from Rs 9.6 crore to Rs 15.2
crore, while other expenses were flat. Management's account matches reading
two: "because of the capabilities we had to build to meet those billing
requirements, we had to incur certain expenditure. That is the reason why the
margin took a hit in the quarter 4. The benefit of which will appear in quarter
1 onwards" (Earnings Call Q4 FY26, page 11).

**The observation that separates them is the services mix, and it is already
visible.** Services went from 24% of revenue to 41% in one year. Q1 FY27
delivered revenue up 22%, EBITDA up 27%, profit after tax up 25%, and gross
margin at 53.9% against 49.9% the prior quarter.

**Classification: margin-led, driven by a mix shift, after one year of paying
for it.**

## 6. The transition and the quality ladder

FROM R2 cost-advantaged converter, which is what device distribution is: buy,
warehouse, deliver, keep a spread. TO R3 value-added and spec'd supplier, and
arguably R4 if the programmes become the client's default.

The mechanism is arithmetic and management supplied both inputs. Services earn
about 25% EBITDA, products 10% to 12%. Mix moved 17 points in a year. Management
guided the blended FY27 margin to 18% to 19% (Earnings Call Q4 FY26, page 11).
FY26 actual was 15.0%, and Q3 FY26 already printed 18.1%.

Management's claim, quoted once: "our services business is emerging as an
increasingly important growth engine for QMS. The business has been driven by
deeper pharmaceutical partnerships, increasing adoption of patient support
programs and the growing scale of our health care camp operations" (Earnings
Call Q1 FY27, page 4).

One rung, inside the framework's base rate.

## 8. Proof check

The triggers claim that a services mix shift lifts the blended margin. The last
two quarters show it starting. Q4 FY26 was the trough: revenue flat year on
year, EBITDA margin 13.0%, profit after tax down 44%. Q1 FY27 was the turn:
revenue up 22%, EBITDA up 27%, gross margin 53.9%, profit after tax up 25%.

The proof point is present and it is one quarter old, with a second data point
behind it. Q3 FY26 printed an 18.1% EBITDA margin, which is inside the guided
FY27 range. That is useful: the margin the company guides has already been
achieved once, so the guide is not a fantasy.

The honest weakness: the full year FY26 went backwards on profit. One quarter
does not undo that.

## 9. Flags from the documents

- **Earnings per share does not reconcile.** FY26 profit after tax of Rs 11.9
  crore with EPS of Rs 5.4 implies about 2.20 crore shares. FY25 profit of
  Rs 13.7 crore with EPS of Rs 6.65 implies about 2.06 crore. Q1 FY26 profit of
  Rs 3.2 crore with EPS of Rs 2.20 implies about 1.45 crore. The three do not
  agree. Flag, and do not use EPS for anything until it is reconciled.
- **The two decks disagree on Q4 FY26.** The Q4 deck shows EBITDA Rs 5.8 crore,
  profit after tax Rs 2.0 crore and EPS Rs 0.89. The Q1 FY27 deck shows the
  same quarter as Rs 5.9 crore, Rs 1.9 crore and Rs 0.97. Small, but flag it.
- Finance cost rose from Rs 4.6 crore to Rs 6.6 crore in FY26 on revenue of
  Rs 172.9 crore, and was Rs 1.7 crore in Q1 FY27. This business carries debt
  against working capital. Flag.
- Employee expenses rose 58% in FY26 and then 3.5 times year on year in
  Q1 FY27, from Rs 3.2 crore to Rs 11.2 crore, while revenue rose 22%. That is
  the services build, and it is the single largest swing factor in the model.
  Flag it both ways.
- Product growth is exposed to shipping disruption, on management's own
  account. Flag.
- Customer concentration is unknown. The client list is impressive and it is
  also a concentration risk if two or three programmes carry the services book.
  Flag the absence.
- Promoter holding, pledge, receivable days and credit rating NOT FOUND.

## 11. Posture: the Transition Decision Matrix

- **Proof gate: FIRED.** The services mix moved from 24% to 41% of revenue in
  one year, and the quarter that followed delivered revenue up 22%, EBITDA up
  27% and gross margin at 53.9%. The mechanism and the result both show.
- **Ugliness: ARTIFACT-OF-CLIMB.** FY26 profit fell 13% while revenue rose 11%.
  The cause is a 58% rise in employee cost to build the services capability,
  which management described before the benefit appeared and which the mix data
  now corroborates.
- **Recognition gap: OPEN.** Price basis, not a corpus document: Bull AI
  company record, market capitalisation Rs 342.97 crore. FY26 profit after tax
  was Rs 11.9 crore, earned in the year the company paid for the transition.
  Q1 FY27 profit was Rs 4.0 crore. The market is priced against the year that
  absorbed the cost, not the mix that follows it.

**Posture: RE-RATING LIVE.** Proof fired, ugliness an artifact, gap open.

## 12. Verdict card

**PROCEED to `/step1`.**

This is the clearest transition mechanism in today's screen. Most cards ask a
reader to believe a margin will improve. This one hands over both segment
margins, the mix at two dates a year apart, and a quarter in which the guided
blended margin was already achieved. A Role 1 margin bridge can be built
straight from the company's own disclosure, which is rare.

The size is the caution, not the story. This is a small company selling a
service to very large customers.

**Load-bearing facts a `/step1` must verify first**
1. Promoter identity, holding and pledge. The corpus answers none of it.
2. The EPS reconciliation and the share count history. Three deck figures imply
   three different share counts.
3. Services revenue concentration: how much of the 41% comes from the largest
   two programmes, and who owns those contracts.
4. Contract tenor and renewal terms on the patient-support programmes.
   "Locked in" needs a length.
5. Receivable days against pharmaceutical customers, and the debt behind the
   Rs 6.6 crore finance cost.
6. Whether HUMRAHI-type programmes can be insourced by the client, and what
   has happened when one tried.

**What would change the view**
- A second quarter with the services mix above 40% and blended EBITDA margin
  at or above 18% confirms the guide and moves this to a full position case.
- A services mix that stalls near 40% while employee cost keeps rising turns
  the ugliness structural.
- Any disclosure that one client is more than a third of the services book.

No Role 1 valuation was run and no target price is set. This verdict decides only whether the
name earns a full `/step1`, and it is not a position.
