### 2C. Six mandatory diagnostics

1. **Did revenue grow YoY?** Consolidated yes, +51.3% (367.08 to 555.36). Standalone no, -22.2% (339.67 to 264.24). The group growth is entirely subsidiary/Energy-driven; the parent (telecom/ICT EPC) base contracted. Management's new FY27E guidance is Rs3,200-3,400 Cr (S24:L664); Q1 consolidated of Rs555.36 Cr is 16.8% of the Rs3,300 Cr midpoint, so the guidance requires heavy H2 back-loading (Q4FY26 alone was Rs1,096.78 Cr).
2. **Op EBITDA margin YoY?** Consolidated contracted: Q1FY27 15.50% vs Q1FY26 21.81% = **-631 bps**. Standalone 18.71% vs 21.48% = -277 bps. The consolidated contraction is driven by mix shift into lower-margin BESS manufacturing: consolidated cost of materials jumped from Rs39.66 Cr to Rs375.32 Cr (RL442, +846%) while EPC project expense fell from Rs224.87 Cr to Rs97.45 Cr (RL443).
3. **Core operating PBT (ex-OI) YoY?** **Fell on both bases** — consolidated -21.9% (68.24 to 53.29), standalone -39.1% (63.00 to 38.35). Reported PAT rose +14.3% consolidated. **The headline PAT growth is not core-operational; it is Other Income.** Named gap: core PBT -21.9% vs reported PAT +14.3% = a ~36-point wedge, entirely Other Income and a lower tax rate.
4. **What drove the gap between core PBT and reported PAT (consolidated)?** Other Income +Rs22.70 Cr (5.64 to 28.34); Finance costs -Rs18.62 Cr (a drag, 9.72 to 28.34); D&A -Rs2.35 Cr; tax +Rs0.06 Cr (near-flat). Other Income alone (+22.70) exceeds the entire reported PBT increase (+7.75). Strip Other Income back to the prior-year level and PAT would be roughly Rs45.1 Cr, ~17% below the prior-year Rs54.70 Cr.
5. **Are D&A and finance costs scaling faster than revenue?** Yes, far faster. Consolidated D&A +112.4% and finance costs +191.6% vs revenue +51.3%. This is the classic capex/leverage absorption gap: the BOO/manufacturing build (PREPL capex Rs486.0 Cr to date, RL236-238) is inflating the fixed-cost and interest base ahead of the revenue it will produce. ROCE compresses until volume catches up.
6. **Is Other Income concentration changing?** Sharply. Consolidated Other Income/PBT rose from 7.6% to 34.7%; standalone from 8.6% to 33.0%. The IPO net proceeds still parked (Rs146.90 Cr unutilised, RL228) are the likely treasury source. This is timing, not recurring operational income; strip it and the quarter is a core decline.

# PLAIN-LANGUAGE BRIEF (mandatory, final narrative)

## 1. Summary narrative

Pace Digitek reported group revenue of Rs555 crore for the June quarter, up 51 percent from a year ago. On the surface that looks like a good quarter. Underneath, the picture is mixed.

The growth is all coming from the energy subsidiaries, not the parent. The parent company's own revenue fell 22 percent. Group profit rose 14 percent to Rs62.5 crore, but that rise came from other income, not from the business. Strip out other income and the core operating profit actually fell about 22 percent. Earnings per share fell from 3.03 to 2.84 because the company issued a lot of new shares in its October 2025 IPO.

Profit margin dropped hard. Operating margin fell from 21.8 percent to 15.5 percent, a drop of 631 basis points. The reason is mix. The company is doing more battery manufacturing, which carries lower margins than its old telecom EPC work.

The most important number this quarter is missing. The company's cash flow number is the single thing the thesis hinges on, and a June quarter filing in India does not include a cash flow statement or a balance sheet. So the binding rule, which says exit if both the June and September quarters show negative cash flow, cannot be tested yet. Half the gate is open and unread. There is one warning sign in the numbers: inventory built up by Rs73 crore in the quarter, more than three times last year, which usually eats cash.

On execution the company delivered. Battery manufacturing capacity reached 5 GWh in early August, roughly on time. It commissioned 375 MWh at its MSEDCL project. It signed partnerships in Africa (NEC XON) and in AI data centre power (MEGMEET), and gave first-time revenue guidance of Rs3,200 to 3,400 crore for FY27. But those partnerships are not orders yet, the only new order this quarter was from a government buyer (BSNL, Rs264 crore), and management guided revenue and capacity while staying silent on margin and cash.

No exit rule has fired. The position stays HELD, a small starter. No add, no trim. The verdict is PROCEED WITH CAVEATS because we cannot see the cash number. The decision waits for the September quarter results, which by law must carry the cash flow statement and balance sheet. That filing resolves the gate.

## 2. Sector intelligence

The read-across is India's energy transition build-out: grid-scale battery storage (BESS), solar, and the telecom/fibre network rollout (BharatNet, Railways Kavach). Demand is policy-driven and real. The order book of about Rs10,800 crore, mostly from state utilities and central PSUs (SECI, NLC, DVC, NTPC, KPTCL, MSEDCL, BSNL), shows the pipeline is there. [Provenance: order book and client roster from this quarter's deck, S15/S16; sector framing from prior Notion thesis.]

The catch is who pays. About 96 percent of revenue is government or PSU (prior Notion estimate; this quarter's filing does not disclose a customer split, so the number is not confirmed here). Government demand is large but slow to pay, which is exactly why the cash conversion question matters so much for this company. The battery manufacturing shift also means the sector's margins are moving from higher-margin project work toward lower-margin factory output. This quarter's 631 basis point margin drop is the first clear sign of that in the numbers. [Provenance: margin from this quarter's filing; concentration from prior Notion, not confirmed this quarter.]

## 3. Business-model intelligence

The company makes money three ways: it manufactures BESS and telecom power gear (through subsidiary Lineage Power), it does EPC project work (design, supply, build), and it is starting to own some assets under long-term BOO contracts (build, own, operate) that pay an annuity for years. [Provenance: model from prior Notion + this quarter's deck slides 10-12.]

This quarter showed the model drifting toward manufacturing and subsidiaries. Group revenue is now about 52 percent subsidiary-sourced, up from 7.5 percent a year ago (this quarter's filing, RL438 vs RL158). The parent now runs large captive EPC work for its own subsidiaries: inter-company sales that get cancelled out were Rs251 crore, about half of the parent's gross revenue (this quarter's deck, S8). That raises a fair question about whether that captive work is priced at arm's length.

The unit economics are under strain in the build phase. Depreciation rose 112 percent and interest cost rose 192 percent year on year, far ahead of revenue at 51 percent (this quarter's filing). That is the cost of building factories and BOO assets before they earn. It is normal for a capex cycle, but it compresses returns until the volume shows up, and it makes the missing cash flow number even more important. [Provenance: all figures this quarter's filing.]

## 4. Competition intelligence

Named peers on file are VINDHYATEL, EXICOM, TEJASNET and GENUSPOWER, with Transrail Lighting flagged as scaling into the same space. [Provenance: prior Notion peer set.] EXICOM is the most direct read-across on grid-scale storage. No peer reported a concall inside the four-week window this run, so a proper side-by-side on demand and pricing could not be done this quarter and is flagged for the next cycle.

Where Pace wins: it is one of the few players offering the full chain from battery manufacturing through EPC to asset ownership, and it has a large, order-backed pipeline. [Provenance: this quarter's deck; prior Notion.] Where it is structurally weaker: heavy dependence on slow-paying government buyers, no proven private or non-government revenue yet (the Africa and AI data centre deals are partnerships, not orders), and a cash conversion record that has lagged profit (cumulative CFO/PAT was -1.07x at the prior analysis; prior Notion, not updated this quarter because the filing carries no cash flow). The competitive risk to watch is a well-capitalised competitor (Transrail, or a larger EPC major) winning the same PSU BESS tenders on price while Pace is mid-build and cash-tight. The next filing's cash flow statement will show whether that risk is biting. [Provenance: risk framing prior Notion; margin and leverage pressure this quarter's filing.]
