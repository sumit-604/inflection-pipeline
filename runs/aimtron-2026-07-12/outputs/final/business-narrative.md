Real EMS growth priced far above value

Aimtron is a Vadodara based electronics contract manufacturer that assembles circuit boards and builds finished box products for other companies across medical, automotive, aerospace and defence, industrial and telecom customers. The Vasani family controls it, Mukesh Vasani chairs it, and it listed in June 2024 and reports half yearly, not quarterly. FY26 standalone revenue was Rs 257 Cr, PAT Rs 39 Cr, diluted EPS Rs 18.5, and the shares trade near Rs 1,390, about 75 times earnings. Consolidated FY26, which folds in the US operation bought during the year, was Rs 301 Cr revenue and Rs 46 Cr PAT.

Growth is real and fast. Revenue and PAT both compounded above 65% a year across FY24 to FY26, ROCE held at 24% in FY26, and the balance sheet is net cash after the IPO cleared the debt. Box Build rose from 27% to 69% of standalone revenue in a single year while board assembly fell from 69% to 29%. The company is ramping a greenfield Vadodara plant aimed at Rs 500 Cr of added capacity and is lifting the acquired US unit from 11% EBITDA toward the group's 18 to 20%.

Cash does not yet back the profit. Standalone operating cash flow was minus Rs 18 Cr in FY25 in the same year PAT grew 89% to a record, and consolidated FY26 operating cash flow was minus Rs 40 Cr, a two year pattern. Cumulative operating cash flow to PAT ran at minus 0.13 times across FY24 to FY26. Trade receivables jumped 417% in FY25 against 70% revenue growth, and 46.3% of those receivables were owed by related parties, including one counterparty balance that flipped from a payable to a receivable while sales to it fell. The pipeline reads this cash conversion as INDETERMINATE, which means the record cannot yet say whether it is growth funded working capital or a structural leak.

Management delivered on the numbers and missed on disclosure. It beat its FY26 revenue target, held PAT margin in band, closed the AS9100D certification, and executed the named aerospace order, which earns a credibility grade of C rather than lower. It also broke a no fundraising promise, never issued the reclassification press release or moved to the quarterly reporting it committed to across a full year, and stated related party revenue was under 20% on a call against 28% to 32% documented in the accounts.

Related party exposure is the governance question. About 31.5% of FY25 standalone revenue flowed to non consolidated promoter controlled foreign entities, and Chairman Mukesh Vasani is externally disclosed as CEO of the largest of them, Aimtron Corporation USA. The FY26 AGM sought a Rs 120 Cr related party ceiling, about 76% of turnover, with no external valuation, and contingent liabilities equal to 81% of FY25 PAT sit unprovisioned. Against that sit a 0% promoter pledge, promoter open market buying, and the January 2026 sale of one related party vehicle. The promoter verdict is CONCERN.

Four listed EMS peers confirm the demand, not the specifics. Avalon, Centum, Cyient DLM and Vinyas all grew order books through FY26 on a broad industry upcycle, while Aimtron's own pipeline figure stayed static across three calls. Its roughly Rs 100 Cr of revenue per SMT line breaks against Vinyas at Rs 500 to 600 Cr per line, a five to six times gap that undercuts the capacity math. The addressable Indian EMS market runs to roughly Rs 2 lakh Cr and grows around 20% a year, a STRONG runway, but the company's five year revenue ambition of Rs 1,300 Cr sits exactly at its disclosed capacity ceiling with no margin.

Valuation decides the call. The four pillar destination multiple this business earns is only 8.9 times on the conservative required return track and 12.7 times on the additive track, against a 25 times sector cap and a current 75 times. Base fair value is Rs 314 on the first track and Rs 448 on the second, the bull ceiling is Rs 504, and every one of those sits far below Rs 1,390. The entry zone is Rs 129 to 161, the margin of safety price is Rs 129, and the hurdle test to clear 25% returns reads STOP because no earnings path bridges 75 times down to a low teens destination.

The decision is AVOID on valuation. The bear thesis was pressure tested and survives on all four dimensions, growth, moat, management and price, and the challenge hardened it rather than weakening it. The sharpest counter is that the 24% margin and 24% ROCE may themselves be a related party artifact, which would threaten even a buyer at the entry zone, not just the buyer at today's price. The one print that resolves it is H1 FY27 consolidated operating cash flow, and it counts only if it comes from third party collections, not from netting related party balances.
