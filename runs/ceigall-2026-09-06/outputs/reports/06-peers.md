## PART 1: CLAIM-BY-CLAIM VERIFICATION

### Q1. Consolidated operating cash flow trend and management tone (cash flow "not a problem" vs negative CFO)

**Claim (B05):** Ceigall says cash flow and equity funding are "not a problem" / "amazing" on
every call while consolidated CFO was negative in FY25 (Rs -515.6cr) and FY26 (Rs -91.3cr),
never reconciled.

**Verdict: CONTRADICTED** (the claim being tested is whether this framing is sector-wide)

**Peer evidence:**
- No peer transcript contains a blanket qualitative reassurance phrase equivalent to "cash flow
  is not a problem" or "amazing." Searches for "free cash flow," "cash rich," "comfortable cash,"
  "liquidity position" and "cash flow generation" returned zero hits across all 12 transcripts.
- Where leverage or funding gaps show up, each peer names the specific cause and the specific
  fix, not a general reassurance. HGINFRA, Q4 FY26: "the company secured additional working
  capital for temporarily increasing overall leverage" to bridge a named "temporary gap" pending
  a specific disbursement (HGINFRA-Concall_May_2026_Transcript.txt, p.6).
- KNRCON discloses consolidated debt rising quarter to quarter with a number each time (Rs
  1,847cr Mar-25 -> Rs 2,018cr Jun-25 -> Rs 2,443cr Dec-25) and ties it to named receivable
  delays (irrigation debtors, HAM debtors) rather than a cash-flow-is-fine framing (KNRCON-
  Concall_Aug_2025_Transcript.txt, p.5; KNRCON-Concall_Feb_2026_Transcript.txt, p.6).
- When analysts press on working-capital or debt movements, all three peers answer with hard
  debtor/payable/unbilled numbers by project or client (see Q6 below), not qualitative comfort
  language.

**Peers silent:** None; all three peers discuss cash/debt/working capital in every call, but
none uses Ceigall's reassurance framing.

**Net read:** The sector norm observed here is granular, named-driver disclosure of debt and
working-capital movements, not blanket qualitative reassurance. Ceigall's "not a problem" /
"amazing" framing, unreconciled against a negative consolidated CFO, is not matched by any peer
and reads as a company-specific communication choice rather than sector-standard tone.

### Q2. NHAI award pace (km and Rs value) when asked directly

**Claim (B05):** Ceigall deflects to the NHAI website on this repeatable question across
multiple calls; tests whether that is unusual or standard reticence.

**Verdict: PARTIALLY VERIFIED** (mixed: 2 of 3 peers disclose unprompted; 1 of 3 deflects like Ceigall)

**Peer evidence:**
- KNRCON and PNCINFRA proactively volunteer exact NHAI/MoRTH award figures in prepared opening
  remarks every quarter, unprompted by any analyst question:
  - KNRCON, Q3/9M FY26: "road project award during 9 months of FY '26 remained muted, with the
    Ministry of Road Transport and Highways and NHAI awarding 1,448 km and 712 km of the
    project respectively" (KNRCON-Concall_Feb_2026_Transcript.txt, p.3).
  - KNRCON, Q4/FY26 full year: "During FY '26, NHAI awarded 3,100 kilometres of project, lower
    by approximately 22% year-on-year and below the initial target of 7,500 kilometres"
    (KNRCON-Concall_Jun_2026_Transcript.txt, p.3).
  - PNCINFRA, Q2 FY26: "NHAI has set an ambitious target of 6,300 km length of new projects
    award for FY26" (PNCINFRA-Concall_Nov_2025_Transcript.txt, p.4).
  - PNCINFRA, Q3 FY26: "NHAI awarding only 377 km of new road projects in Q3 FY26, compared to
    around 504 km in Q3 FY25" (PNCINFRA-Concall_Feb_2026_Transcript.txt, p.3).
  - PNCINFRA, Q1 FY27: "NHAI's project awarding activity remained subdued in Q1 FY2027 also,
    with only 107 kilometers awarded" (PNCINFRA-Concall_Aug_2026_Transcript.txt, p.2).
- HGINFRA, by contrast, shares Ceigall's evasiveness when asked the same question directly. In
  the Q4 FY26 call, an analyst asks "how one can look at your own estimates from NHAI side...
  how much kilometer or value," and after a vague answer is pressed again ("roughly, how much
  kilometer do we think, 3,000 kilometer odd or?"), management replies: "Exactly, I'm not
  having a very exact number" (HGINFRA-Concall_May_2026_Transcript.txt, p.17-18).

**Peers silent:** None on the topic generally, but HGINFRA is effectively "silent" on the
precise number when asked directly, matching Ceigall's pattern.

**Net read:** Two of three peers show that proactive, precise NHAI/MoRTH award disclosure is
achievable and is in fact standard practice for them every quarter without being asked. One
peer (HGINFRA) shares Ceigall's deflection when the question is put directly. Ceigall's
deflection is not universal sector behaviour, but it is not unique either — it sits with the
weaker third of this three-peer set on this specific question.

### Q3. HAM-asset/InvIT monetization disclosure (sale price, realised equity IRR)

**Claim (B05):** Ceigall never discloses the Malout-Abohar-Sadhuwali sale price despite citing
the deal three times; tests whether this is industry-standard opacity or a specific red flag.

**Verdict: CONTRADICTED** on sale-price/consideration disclosure; **UNVERIFIABLE** on realised
equity IRR specifically (no peer states a numeric realised IRR)

**Peer evidence:**
- HGINFRA discloses a full, consistent consideration breakdown for its Tranche-2 HAM
  monetization (5 SPVs to Neo Infra Income Opportunities Fund) across three consecutive calls:
  "The enterprise value of the entire transaction is at INR3,584 crores and the total equity
  invested in these and the total debt obligation of all five assets are at INR767 crores and
  INR2,200 crores, respectively" (HGINFRA-Concall_Aug_2025_Transcript.txt, p.5-6), repeated
  with the same figures in the Nov-2025 call ("The enterprise value of the transaction is
  INR3,584 crores. Total investment, equity investment and total debt obligations... are INR767
  crores and INR2,200 crores, respectively," HGINFRA-Concall_Nov_2025_Transcript.txt, p.6) and
  again referenced in the Feb-2026 call as SPAs for all five projects were executed (HGINFRA-
  Concall_Feb_2026_Transcript.txt, p.5).
- PNCINFRA discloses the enterprise value for a completed HAM monetization when an analyst asks
  directly: "Enterprise value consideration is around Rs. 630 crore" (PNCINFRA-Concall_Nov_2025
  _Transcript.txt, p.10), with equity invested (Rs 114cr) given in the same exchange.
- KNRCON is the exception that shows the limit of this disclosure norm: when asked what proceeds
  are expected from a HAM monetization still under negotiation, management deflects: "so as far
  as HAM monetization is there, so we are discussing with that on forum, we'll let you know"
  (KNRCON-Concall_Aug_2025_Transcript.txt, p.20). This deflection is on a live, unsigned
  negotiation, not a completed transaction.
- No peer transcript states a numeric realised equity IRR for a completed monetization; "robust
  valuation" and similar qualitative language appears (HGINFRA, p.6) but never with an IRR
  figure.

**Peers silent:** None avoided the topic; all three discussed active monetization plans.

**Net read:** Two of three peers disclose hard consideration/enterprise-value figures once a
deal is binding or completed, and HGINFRA does so consistently across three calls for the same
transaction. Ceigall's total non-disclosure of the Malout-Abohar-Sadhuwali price despite the SPA
being signed and completed (per AR) is not matched by peer practice on completed deals — it
looks like a company-specific gap, not sector-standard opacity. Realised IRR is not disclosed by
any peer, so Ceigall's silence on IRR specifically is unverifiable against this peer set.

### Q4. HAM equity-commitment reconciliation quarter to quarter

**Claim (B05):** Ceigall's HAM/renewable/T&D equity-commitment totals are quoted on different,
unreconciled bases every call; tests whether this is a company-specific disclosure problem.

**Verdict: PARTIALLY VERIFIED** (2 of 3 peers reconcile cleanly with explicit scope
explanations; 1 of 3 shows a smaller, less-explained drift)

**Peer evidence — clean reconciliation:**
- KNRCON's HAM equity figures reconcile arithmetically quarter to quarter on a stated "revised"
  basis: Aug-25 call — "already invested INR676 crores out of INR990 crores of the revised
  equity requirement... additional requirement of INR314 crores... INR185 crores in FY26 and
  INR129 crores in FY27" (KNRCON-Concall_Aug_2025_Transcript.txt, p.4); Nov-25 call — "already
  invested INR698 crores out of the INR991 crores revised equity requirement... additional
  equity requirement of INR292 crores... INR175 crores and INR117 crores for FY26 and '27"
  (KNRCON-Concall_Nov_2025_Transcript.txt, p.4). The Rs 22cr rise in cumulative infusion (676 to
  698) and the matching Rs 22cr fall in the outstanding requirement (314 to 292) tie out exactly.
- PNCINFRA reconciles even through an explicit scope change: Nov-25 call — total requirement
  Rs 1,744cr, invested Rs 1,081cr (PNCINFRA-Concall_Nov_2025_Transcript.txt, p.5); Feb-26 call —
  same Rs 1,744cr total, invested Rs 1,110cr, remaining Rs 634cr, arithmetic exact (PNCINFRA-
  Concall_Feb_2026_Transcript.txt, p.13, "Page 13 of 18" printed); May-26 call — total
  requirement revised down to Rs 1,623cr "excluding 2 HAM projects, for which LOAs are yet to be
  received," invested Rs 1,081cr, remaining Rs 542cr, arithmetic exact and the reason for the
  total changing is stated explicitly (PNCINFRA-Concall_May_2026_Transcript.txt, p.6).

**Peer evidence — unexplained drift (partial echo of Ceigall's problem):**
- HGINFRA's "total equity requirement for 11 HAM projects" figure itself rises every quarter on
  the same declared project count, without an explicit reconciliation of why the total climbs:
  Aug-25 Rs 1,664cr -> Nov-25 Rs 1,709cr -> Feb-26 Rs 1,750cr -> May-26 Rs 1,903cr (HGINFRA-
  Concall_Aug_2025_Transcript.txt, p.6; -Nov_2025, p.6-7; -Feb_2026, p.6; -May_2026, p.6). The
  cumulative-infused figure climbs consistently in step (Rs 997cr -> Rs 1,165cr -> Rs 1,242cr ->
  Rs 1,210cr, the last a stated typo-corrected "as of March '26" restatement), so the mechanics
  are traceable, but no call explains why the total requirement itself keeps rising on an
  unchanged project count.

**Peers silent:** None; all three quantify HAM equity requirements every call.

**Net read:** Clean, scope-explained reconciliation is achievable and is what two of three peers
do. HGINFRA shows a milder, unexplained version of the same problem Ceigall has (a rising total
on a flat project count, never reconciled). This makes Ceigall's version of the problem more
severe in degree — its swings are described in B05 as larger and mixed across HAM, solar and T&D
scopes together — but not unique in kind; the failure mode exists elsewhere in the peer set too.

### Q6. Working-capital-day trends (receivables, unbilled revenue, payables)

**Claim (B05):** Tests whether Ceigall's working-capital stretch (payable days 79 to 138,
unbilled-revenue days 94 to 133) is sector-wide or company-specific, and ties it to the FY24
Atmanirbhar-scheme withdrawal.

**Verdict: PARTIALLY VERIFIED**

**Peer evidence:**
- No peer transcript mentions "Atmanirbhar" at all — the specific causal link Ceigall's calls
  imply is not corroborated or contradicted by any peer; that piece is unverifiable.
- Working-capital stretch itself is visibly sector-wide and can be severe: KNRCON's standalone
  working-capital days swung from 93 days (Mar-25) to 169 days (Jun-25) — nearly double —
  before easing to 82 days (Dec-25) (KNRCON-Concall_Aug_2025_Transcript.txt, p.5; KNRCON-
  Concall_Feb_2026_Transcript.txt, p.6). Management explained the spike with a named cause
  (irrigation debtors ~Rs 800cr and HAM debtors ~Rs 1,200cr pending from state governments,
  KNRCON-Concall_Aug_2025_Transcript.txt, p.8) and the subsequent recovery.
- PNCINFRA's net working-capital days sit around 110 (Q1 FY27, PNCINFRA-Concall_Aug_2026_
  Transcript.txt, p.9-10) with management naming the specific driver each quarter (a Rs 741cr
  Jal Jeevan Mission pending realisation, a Rs 416cr Andhra Pradesh irrigation invoice) and
  citing named improvements when a state releases payment.
- The pattern across peers is: working-capital stretch happens, is large in magnitude at times
  (KNRCON nearly doubling in one quarter), but each instance is tied to a specific named
  counterparty/cause and each is followed, within one to two quarters, by improvement or an
  explicit explanation of the expected reversal trigger.

**Peers silent:** None on working capital generally.

**Net read:** The direction (working-capital stretch under government-counterparty delay) is
sector-wide and can be sharper than Ceigall's numbers in a single quarter (KNRCON's spike).
What is not observed in this peer set is Ceigall's pattern of one-directional, multi-quarter
worsening (79->138 payable days, 94->133 unbilled days) without a named reversal catalyst on its
own calls. Peers show volatility with recovery and attribution; Ceigall (per B05) shows sustained
drift without either.

### Q7. Margin-guidance bands and treatment of one-off bonus/royalty income

**Claim (B05):** Tests whether Ceigall's practice of folding bonus/royalty income into some
periods' headline EBITDA margin without separate disclosure is standard sector practice.

**Verdict: CONTRADICTED**

**Peer evidence:**
- PNCINFRA discloses named, quantified early-completion bonuses project by project and states
  which quarter each will hit the P&L: "Company received Early Completion Bonus of Rs. 5.3
  crores for Mathura 1C project" (PNCINFRA-Concall_Nov_2025_Transcript.txt, p.4); a Rs 14cr
  Hardoi bonus tracked explicitly across two calls for timing of booking (PNCINFRA-Concall_Nov
  _2025_Transcript.txt, p.15; PNCINFRA-Concall_Feb_2026_Transcript.txt, p.15); and a >Rs 50cr
  MSRDC bonus explicitly attributed as the reason a prior year's turnover looked elevated: "we
  received INR 50 crores-plus bonus from one of the projects we completed for MSRDC... because
  that is reflected in the higher turnover in FY25" (PNCINFRA-Concall_May_2026_Transcript.txt,
  p.14).
- KNRCON tracks bonuses by named project and explicitly tells analysts when none will hit the
  P&L in a given half: "recently, we got bonus in one project, that is Cheyyur-Vandavasi, that
  is already accounted. But other 3 projects... not right now... nothing, nothing" for H2
  (KNRCON-Concall_Nov_2025_Transcript.txt, p.17).
- HGINFRA is asked about one-offs in margin on every single call in this set and answers with a
  specific attribution each time rather than a blended figure: a royalty/change-in-law item on
  the Ganga Expressway project quantified at "INR43 crores... this is the margin which we have
  taken for Ganga Expressway project, because of the one-off items, this is a change in law"
  (HGINFRA-Concall_Aug_2025_Transcript.txt, p.14); and in the Feb-2026 call, a direct denial that
  a clean quarter had any one-off: "For normal EPC margins, there's no additional exceptional
  item which has been added here" (HGINFRA-Concall_Feb_2026_Transcript.txt, p.7).

**Peers silent:** None; one-offs and bonuses are a standing line of analyst questioning on every
call across all three peers.

**Net read:** The observed sector practice is the opposite of what would make Ceigall's
treatment standard: peers name the specific bonus/royalty/one-off item, its rupee amount, the
project, and the quarter it will (or will not) hit the P&L, every time the topic comes up.
Folding such income into headline EBITDA margin without separate disclosure, as B05 describes
for Ceigall, is not supported as standard practice by this peer set.

## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

**2A. Demand environment.** All three peers describe FY26 as a below-peak, "muted" or "subdued"
NHAI/MoRTH award year, continuing into Q1 FY27: KNRCON's full-year FY26 NHAI award total of
3,100 km was 22% below the prior year and well under the initial 7,500 km target (KNRCON-
Concall_Jun_2026_Transcript.txt, p.3); PNCINFRA's Q1 FY27 update still shows only 107 km awarded
(PNCINFRA-Concall_Aug_2026_Transcript.txt, p.2). All three attribute the slowdown to land
acquisition and cabinet-approval delays, not to a demand-side problem — the pipeline (NHAI's
invited bids "aggregating to 1.5 trillion" per KNRCON, p.3 of the Feb-2026 call) is described as
large and improving. This is consensus across the peer set, not a split. Set against this, note
that Ceigall's own FY26 order inflow (Rs 11,332cr, beating its own Rs 5,000cr guide 2.3x, per
B05) grew sharply in a year the primary NHAI award market was at a multi-year low — worth
flagging to synthesis as a point needing its own explanation (state-level/non-NHAI wins, T&D/
renewables LOAs, or market-share gain), since the peer set's own framing makes strong FY26 order
growth look harder to achieve from NHAI alone than Ceigall's guidance beat implies.

**2B. Pricing and input costs.** All three peers explicitly name the same driver for elevated
input costs in FY26/Q4 FY26 and into Q1 FY27: the West Asia geopolitical conflict raising crude
oil and bitumen prices. HGINFRA: "headwinds emanating from the West Asian conflict... resulted
in a higher input cost for all metal, HSD, bitumen, cement, logistics" (HGINFRA-Concall_May_2026
_Transcript.txt, p.2), and later in the same call, "because of this war situation, because of
this geopolitical any turmoil, we cannot guarantee anything" (p.17). KNRCON: "the ongoing
geopolitical tension and conflict in the West Asia led to an increase in crude oil prices, which
consequently impacted bitumen prices" (KNRCON-Concall_Jun_2026_Transcript.txt, p.4), and notes a
policy response — MoRTH shortened the price-adjustment pass-through cycle from 3 months to 1
month specifically to help contractors absorb this (same page). PNCINFRA: "geopolitical tensions
in West Asia led to volatility in global crude oil prices and logistic costs, resulting in higher
input costs, particularly bitumen, fuel and logistics" (PNCINFRA-Concall_May_2026_Transcript.txt,
p.5). **This directly resolves the open item flagged in B05's analyst_note**: Ceigall's Aug-2026
call cites an unnamed "war situation" as a new reason for international caution without
identifying it. All three peers in the same period name the same event explicitly (the West Asia
conflict and its crude oil/bitumen/logistics transmission). This is very likely the same
reference; it is a real, sector-wide, explicitly-named macro factor in this window, not a
Ceigall-specific evasion, though Ceigall's own choice not to name it on its own call is still a
disclosure gap worth noting on its own terms.

**2C. Capex cycle.** All three peers are simultaneously diversifying beyond core road EPC into
solar, BESS, transmission, metro/rail, mining and water/irrigation during the exact period
covered — this is an industry-wide diversification race, not a lone-expander situation.
HGINFRA: "diversified into a critical infrastructure domain, such as railways, metro, renewable
energy and transmission projects... this is not just diversification, it's a targeted expansion
into future profit pools" (HGINFRA-Concall_Aug_2025_Transcript.txt, p.4; reiterated in near-
identical language in the May-2026 call, p.4). KNRCON: "as we are making the diversification in
other sector... the industry is entering a more diversified and sustainable growth phase"
(KNRCON-Concall_Jun_2026_Transcript.txt, p.3-4). PNCINFRA: entering mining, solar, BESS with a
stated non-road revenue target and guidance that diversification will structurally pressure the
core roads margin ("we are diversifying into the other sectors, which will impact the margins of
the roads and highways sector," PNCINFRA-Concall_May_2026_Transcript.txt, p.9). This is a strong
confirmation that Ceigall's renewables/T&D push (35% of FY26 order inflow per B05, trigger 3) is
consistent with a sector-wide capacity/diversification race rather than a company-specific
overreach — though PNCINFRA's own explicit caution that diversification will pressure margins is
a data point Ceigall's calls do not offer about its own renewables/T&D ramp.

**2D. Competitive mentions.** None of the 12 transcripts mention Ceigall by name or by an
unmistakable description. A targeted search for "Ceigall" across the full corpus returned zero
matches. Silence noted; no peer references Ceigall as a bidding competitor, benchmark, or
cautionary example in this period.

**2E. Risks peers discuss that Ceigall's calls do not.**
- The West Asia geopolitical conflict as an explicitly named, specific input-cost and demand
  risk (all three peers) — Ceigall's own calls reference an unnamed "war situation" without
  identifying it (per B05).
- NHAI potentially shifting preference toward BOT-toll structures over HAM, which would change
  the risk/funding profile of future order intake (HGINFRA-Concall_May_2026_Transcript.txt,
  p.17) — not discussed on any Ceigall call per B05.
- NHAI arbitration/dispute-settlement risk as a distinct, named government-counterparty risk
  line, including a Rs 235cr Vivad-se-Vishwas III settlement received by PNCINFRA (PNCINFRA-
  Concall_May_2026_Transcript.txt, p.6) and an NHAI show-cause notice leading to a one-month
  bidding suspension for KNRCON (KNRCON-Concall_Aug_2025_Transcript.txt, p.4) — Ceigall's own
  calls do not surface an equivalent government-counterparty dispute risk category.
- Land acquisition and cabinet-approval delay as the explicitly named primary driver of the
  sector-wide award slowdown (all three peers) — a systemic order-inflow risk not framed this
  way in Ceigall's own disclosure per B05.

## PART 4: TRIANGULATION SUMMARY

- Claims verified: 0 of 7 (none of the seven Ceigall-behaviour claims were corroborated as

- Claims partially verified: 3 of 7 (Q2 NHAI-deflection, Q4 equity-commitment reconciliation, Q6

- Claims unverifiable: 1 of 7 (Q5 governance-disclosure culture; no comparable peer event exists

**Single most consequential contradiction:** Q3. Two of three peers (HGINFRA consistently across
three calls, PNCINFRA once asked) disclose hard enterprise-value/consideration figures for HAM
monetizations, including for completed transactions. Ceigall's total non-disclosure of the
Malout-Abohar-Sadhuwali sale price, despite the SPA being signed and completed per the AR and the

matched by peer practice on completed deals. This sharpens the HAM-monetization thesis from "a
possible reticence" to "an anomalous silence even after deal completion," which should weigh

**Hypothesis:** Disclosure granularity in this peer set is not a topic-by-topic choice but a

not something any one peer or the Ceigall transcripts state directly.
