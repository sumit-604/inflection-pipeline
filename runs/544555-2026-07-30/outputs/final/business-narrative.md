RDSO rubber components maker riding railway capex

Ameenji Rubber makes RDSO and MoRTH approved rubber components and listed in September 2025. Railway track parts (sole plates, pads, UIC vestibules, level crossing pads) are about 40% of revenue. Higher value infrastructure bearings, POT-PTFE and expansion joints are about 59%. FY25 revenue was Rs 94 Cr. The shares trade at Rs 135 for a market cap of Rs 152 Cr.

The mix is shifting toward the higher value bearings segment, up 186% over two years. Blended EBITDA margin rose from 11% to 20% across FY23 to FY25. H1 FY26 revenue was Rs 43 Cr, up 8.47%, with EBITDA margin of 26.63% and PAT up 103.53%. New Railway orders of about Rs 68 Cr ex-GST came in across four zones between February and July 2026, equal to roughly 72% of FY25 revenue. About 89% of the order book sits with Indian Railways, so the growth reinforces single sector dependence rather than diversifying it.

Gate 0 scored 63 out of 160 with a core of 50 out of 100 and a classification of AVOID. The cash and working capital block scored 0 out of 20. Three of twelve moats were confirmed, moat class MODERATE. The forward emerging moat score was 21.9, MODEST. The depressor is working capital deterioration that is recent and ongoing, not a one time post-IPO rebase.

Working capital days rose from 49 in FY23 to a peak of 166 in FY25 and were still about 161 in FY26. Receivable days went from 48 to 66. Finished goods inventory grew 221% against 12.9% revenue growth with zero write-downs across three years, and inventory turnover fell from 2.99x to 1.29x. Free cash flow was negative in all three restated years. The FY25 operating cash recovery came from a Rs 805 lakh stretch in payables, not from inventory or receivable normalization. Borrowings rose 113% over two years, including NBFC debt at 10.75% to 14.51%, and debt to equity sits near 2.07x.

Management credibility grades C. The maiden post-IPO call had no CFO present and no promoter. Order book quantum was asked twice and refused twice. Two operating facts were stated wrong and left uncorrected, a capacity figure off by 100x and a competitor count given as three then eleven. Margin guidance was internally inconsistent, quoted as 20-25% and 23-24% on the same call against a 26.63% actual. Revenue growth of 8.47% ran well short of the guided 20-25% CAGR.

Four governance items sit unresolved. ABJ Rub Metalico traded with the company at up to 16.61% of revenue with no arm's length benchmark, yet the RHP concludes no group company exists. Nine years of unfiled cash flow statements were regularised through NCLT compounding filed the same day as the RHP. A FEMA and ODI reporting gap remains on the 90% owned US subsidiary. Interest on delayed MSME payments was not provided. The auditor resigned within three years, and the CFO resigned on 28 July 2026 with no named successor. Against that, the promoter has 0% pledge, took nothing off the table in a 100% fresh issue with Nil OFS, cut ABJ related party revenue from 16.61% to 16.04% to 4.45%, and added a qualified company secretary as an independent director in November 2024.

Peer corroboration of the rail thesis is thin. The only peer with transcripts, GRP Ltd, is a tyre and reclaim rubber maker with zero RDSO or Railways exposure, so it cannot confirm the railway demand story. GRP's own numeric capex and margin targets slipped in three of four consecutive quarters. Rubfila and Elgi Rubber were screening data only.

The realistic TAM is about Rs 475 Cr, SAM Rs 428 Cr, and the serviceable share Rs 140 Cr over three years rising to Rs 176 Cr over five, a runway class of GOOD with roughly 4.5x revenue headroom. The five year figure of Rs 176 Cr nearly exhausts the Rs 180 Cr core installed capacity ceiling, and the 60.4% capex embedded growth runs mostly through the unproven conveyor belting line rather than core expansion.
