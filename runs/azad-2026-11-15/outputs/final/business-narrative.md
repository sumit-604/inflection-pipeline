Airfoil revenue grows, cash still not converting

Azad Engineering Ltd (AZAD). Run 2026-11-15, Phase 1 lite: evidence stages only, no valuation. Units: ₹ Cr. The filings print INR million; ₹1 Cr = ₹10 Mn. Figures are rounded for reading.

## Business understanding narrative

Azad Engineering makes precision metal parts to its customers' drawings in Hyderabad, and most of them are airfoils, the blades that turn inside gas, steam and nuclear turbines and aircraft engines. Energy and oil and gas parts made about 81% of FY26 standalone revenue, and aerospace and defence parts about 17%. Job work, billed as a service, was about 20% of consolidated revenue and grew 72% in the year; the run did not establish who owns the material in that work. A turbine maker cannot easily replace these parts, because each one is qualified for one engine or turbine over 30 to 48 months, and a new supplier must repeat that process. The customers are a short list of global makers: GE Vernova, Siemens Energy, Mitsubishi Heavy Industries, Baker Hughes and BHEL in energy, and Honeywell, Eaton, Boeing and HAL in aerospace systems, with Rolls-Royce, Pratt & Whitney Canada and Safran still qualifying aero engine parts. The top customer took 18.6% of FY26 revenue and the top five about 47%, and four of these makers now have six plants built only for them, three of them for GE Vernova. They buy on supply contracts of 3 to 10 years, pay per part in foreign currency on 120 to 180 days of credit, and take about 93% of output abroad. Present demand comes from gas turbine orders, and the signal is GE Vernova's gas equipment backlog plus slot reservations, 122 GW at Q3 2026, read beside Siemens Energy Gas Services orders and backlog and Mitsubishi Heavy Industries gas turbine orders. The ₹6,500 Cr order book is management's own estimate, not audited, and in November management said the figure is not an order backlog. Demand should grow on four lines that outside sources can check: the MHI hot section facility, due about March to April 2027 under an 8 year single source contract; Rolls-Royce, Pratt & Whitney Canada and Safran qualification acceptance and production orders; a GTRE ATGG engine production order; and BHEL and domestic thermal and nuclear orders, which lifted the domestic share to 12% of H1 FY27 revenue. Howmet Engine Products revenue, up 33% to USD 1,451 Mn in Q3 2026, is the peer read on whether engine and turbine hardware stays short of capacity, and the USD/INR reference rate sets the rupee value of every export invoice. The moat sits in the energy airfoil line, where four filed agreements (MHI, Baker Hughes to 2030, GE Steam Power for about USD 53.5 Mn, and the Pratt & Whitney Canada master terms) support a strong score for qualification lock in. That lock in is strong per part and weak per customer, because management puts its wallet share under 2%, and the dedicated plants raise Azad's dependence on one maker as much as they raise that maker's cost of switching. The hot section and aero engine lines have no moat yet: the hot section facility is not built, and the Rolls-Royce qualification batch was still undelivered on 7 November 2026. Job work and oil and gas show no moat evidence in the run. The emerging moat scan scores 12, the floor of the MODEST band, with execution at zero, and the two structural asymmetry categories, talent asymmetry and the cannibalisation barrier, also score zero because the corpus names nothing a better funded rival such as Howmet or PCC would have to destroy to copy Azad.

## What the evidence stages found

What is changing. Azad is moving from compressor airfoils made on shared floor space to plants built for one customer each, hot section parts and complete engines. Six dedicated plants with 44,800 sq m are built, of eight planned. Capital work in progress fell from ₹257 Cr in March to ₹212 Cr in September 2026 as plant moved into service. Management says throughput starts in Q3 FY27, revenue ramps in Q4 FY27 and the full ramp lands in Q2 FY28. Its target is an annual run rate above ₹1,300 Cr by the end of FY28, against FY26 revenue of ₹603 Cr. That target needs about 9.3% sequential growth for six quarters; Q1 and Q2 FY27 delivered 8.3% and 10.1%. The ramp date moved on all three calls, and the plant description went from "full throttle" in August to spindles that had "just started" in November.

What the scorecard says. Gate 0 scores 64 of 160: core 53 of 100, moat 11 of 60, three of twelve moats confirmed, class AVERAGE. Growth earns full marks. Revenue compounded 37.5% a year from FY21 to FY26 and PAT 63.1%. Cash earns zero. Free cash flow was negative in all six years, and cumulative operating cash flow was negative ₹57 Cr against cumulative PAT of ₹328 Cr. Return on capital fell from 18.3% in FY24 to 8.7% in FY25 and 11.5% in FY26, because the ₹700 Cr QIP cash and unfinished plant sat in capital before they earned. Three deal breakers hold the class at AVERAGE.

Cash reality. FY26 operating cash flow was negative ₹119 Cr against positive ₹54 Cr the year before, on revenue up 32%. Inventory rose 74% to ₹329 Cr, receivables rose 39%, and balances with government authorities doubled to ₹110 Cr with no breakdown given. Free cash flow was negative ₹691 Cr. Net cash of ₹453 Cr became net debt of ₹272 Cr in one year. By September 2026 the run calculates net debt of ₹474 Cr, against management's ₹424 Cr, and nobody has explained the gap. H1 FY27 operating cash flow was negative ₹43.6 Cr, better than negative ₹75.9 Cr a year earlier, but the gain came from operating profit and lower tax, not from working capital. CARE put the working capital cycle at 344 days in March 2026, up from 287. Two readings stand. CARE ties the inventory build to development parts, bulk alloy buys and three new dedicated units, and expects it to normalise once serial production starts. Management's own annual report calls working capital "structural to the business rather than a function of the year's activity". The observation that separates them is FY27 operating cash flow, positive before the bill discounting lines, the OEM advance and any factoring, with inventory falling from ₹427 Cr.

Growth and margin. Revenue growth held above the 25% guide. Consolidated revenue rose 25.9% in Q1 FY27, 31.1% in Q2 and 28.6% in H1, to ₹363.5 Cr. EBITDA margin held near 37.5%. Below EBITDA the picture weakens. In H1, depreciation rose 89%, finance cost rose 80%, other income fell 63%, and profit before tax fell 8.9%. Each extra rupee of H1 revenue added 43 paise of EBITDA but only 19 paise of EBIT. Material consumption fell to 4.2% of revenue from 12.4%, with ₹72 Cr of work in progress waiting for customer approval, so part of the margin may sit in inventory; that is an inference. One verifier puts H1 constant currency growth near 23% to 24%, also an inference, because the run found no rupee rates. Other income was 24% of FY26 profit before tax, and a 5% rupee move changes profit before tax by about ₹10.4 Cr.

What management says and what it does. Credibility grades C. Of the promises tracked across three calls, 6 were delivered, 5 partly and 8 missed; the red flag verifier recounts 5, 6 and 7 and keeps the grade. Revenue, margin and plant promises held. Cash and timing promises did not. No H1 working capital days figure was reported against a target of about 200. The H2 target moved from 140 to 150 days, to 160 to 180, and then dropped out of the November call. The Rolls-Royce batch due within Q2 FY27 has not shipped. In November management refused to say how many customers remain in delta qualification, the step that gates the Q4 ramp. It gave two answers in one call on whether bill discounting had started, and hinted at another QIP while cash flow is negative. Governance reads CAUTION, not CONCERN. There is no pledge, the run found no SEBI or criminal record (the SEBI orders database was not reachable), and the MD, Murali Krishna Bhupatiraju, is an outside professional and a former president and CFO of Bharat Forge America. Against that, the promoter sold 31.7 lakh shares in a June 2025 block for about ₹514 Cr on media prices not yet verified, gave no reason, and saw his holding fall from 64.8% to 54.9%. His pay rose 10% in a year of negative cash flow. The lock in on 1.18 Cr promoter shares ends about December 2026.

What the peers confirm and contradict. No peer contradicted an Azad claim, and no peer mentions Azad. MTAR shows the cash fix can happen. Its working capital fell from 260 days to 50, and its H1 FY27 operating cash flow was positive ₹350 Cr, from customer credit terms and advances rather than discounting. MTAR itself says 50 days is not sustainable and targets about 100 by year end and 150 over two to three years, and it never states its basis. Unimech's working capital is rising from 110 days toward 150 to 160 as first article aerospace programmes grow, the stage Azad's new plants are in. Both peers slip commissioning dates too. The peer set holds no gas turbine OEM supplier. One 2023 PTC Industries meeting calls industrial gas turbines an evolving market with shorter approvals than aerospace, and that is all the gas turbine evidence the peers give.

Where the market opportunity sits. The market does not bind. The conservative addressable market is ₹82,653 Cr (realistic ₹85,383 Cr), the serviceable part is ₹26,741 Cr, Azad holds about 2.25% of it, and FY26 revenue has 44 times headroom; the runway class is STRONG. Conversion and capacity bind instead. A ₹1,300 Cr revenue line in FY29 sits ₹529 Cr above the ₹771 Cr that documented assets support at today's asset turn. Management's per plant arithmetic covers the gap at 82% to 93% utilisation, but that is a claim. Management's own market figure of ₹343,000 Cr is 4.15 times the conservative figure, because its aerospace leg counts five customers' revenue rather than spend on components. The market stage is partial: searches for an outsourced airfoil spend figure were skipped, and the corpus holds no sourced figure for machined aero engine component spend.
