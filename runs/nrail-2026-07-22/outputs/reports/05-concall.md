### 2B. Excuse pattern analysis
No analyst Q&A exists to observe live excuse behavior. From the **written** record only: the AR
is comparatively candid — it leads with a section titled "Seeking Opportunity in Adversity,"
puts the 85.93% PAT decline and 578bps EBITDA margin compression on the same page as the
revenue growth headline (AR p.4, "How we have grown over the years"), and attributes the
decline explicitly to "declining realizations and rising input costs" (external, verifiable
market factors — corroborated by the Rs41/kg→Rs37/kg realization decline disclosed on p.11) —
rather than concealing or downplaying it. It also credits the company for holding EBITDA margin
"in line with the industry average" during the downturn (self-credit), and for being "probably
the lowest cost paperboard manufacturer in India" — the latter is an unbenchmarked claim
(qualified by the company itself with "probably") that leans promotional. Net pattern:
**honest**, with a mild promotional tendency on unverifiable competitive-positioning claims.

## SECTION 3: COMPETITIVE INTELLIGENCE (from AR only; concall-only elements marked NOT FOUND)

### 3A. Competitor commentary, with credibility check
The Chairman's letter describes an industry consolidation dynamic: "small paperboard
manufacturing units are exiting the business, unable to match declining realizations, improve
end product quality or address debt repayment obligations," while "some of the larger
paperboard companies are acquiring large capacities" (AR p.7-8). This is directionally
plausible given the oversupply/import-pressure narrative corroborated by the third-party market
data cited elsewhere in the same AR (IMARC, Mordor Intelligence, Business Research Company), but
it is **self-serving framing** — no named competitor, no market-share number, and NRAIL
positions itself as a beneficiary/survivor. Credibility: **moderate, unverified** — this is
exactly the kind of claim Stage 6 should put to JKPAPER and KUANTUM.

### 3B. Industry and market intelligence disclosed in the AR
- Global paperboard packaging market: US$422.32bn (2024) → US$447.11bn (2025), 5.9% CAGR;
  global paper+paperboard consumption projected at 519 MT by 2035 (AR p.14-15).
- India paperboard packaging market: US$13.72bn (2025), 6.63% CAGR to US$18.92bn by 2030 (AR
  p.15).
- India packaging-paperboard consumption: 15 million tons in 2024, ~65% of the total
  paper/paperboard/newsprint market of 23 million tons (AR p.15).
- Paperboard realization: peak Rs41/kg, declined to Rs37/kg by close of FY24-25, attributed to
  "global paperboard oversupply and cheaper imports" (AR p.7).

### 3C. Toughest analyst questions
**NOT FOUND — no concalls exist.** No analyst Q&A record available in this mode.

### 3D. Customer and order-book signals
- Export mix: ~30% of output exported; 60% sold in western India near the Sarigam/Vapi plants
  (Chairman's letter, AR p.7).
- Demand absorption for the new capacity: "the Company had sold virtually all that it could
  produce" with finished-paperboard closing inventory equivalent to only 12 days of production
  by close of FY24-25 — a genuine, checkable operating signal of sell-through, not merely a
  claim (AR p.7).
- FY25 export earnings rose to Rs28,001.11 lakhs from Rs14,251.72 lakhs in FY24 (Note 30/37,
  Financial Statements) — nearly doubled, consistent with the stated export push.
