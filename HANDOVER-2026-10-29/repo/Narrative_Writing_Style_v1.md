# NARRATIVE WRITING STYLE v1

House style for the closing narrative section of every company analysis.
It draws from two published sources. ASD-STE100, the Simplified Technical
English specification used in aerospace. And William Zinsser's four
principles of quality writing from On Writing Well.

## Section 1. When this rule applies

This rule applies only to the closing narrative section of a company
analysis. That is the plain-English section that tells the story behind
the numbers.

This rule does not apply to structured analytical sections. It does not
apply to the Section 1B four-pillar derivation, FTTCP verdict tables,
verdict cards, or Notion payload construction. It does not apply to any
table, scorecard, or calculation block. Those sections keep their existing
format requirements. Do not simplify them into narrative prose.

If a section has a defined structure, this rule is silent. If a section is
prose the operator reads to internalize the thesis, this rule governs it.

## Section 2. The core rules from ASD-STE100

State each rule and follow it in the same line.

Use short sentences. Aim for 20 words or fewer per sentence. Split longer
thoughts into two sentences.

Use active voice. Write "management raised prices" not "prices were raised
by management." The subject does the action.

Give each word one meaning per document. If "close" means "shut" in one
paragraph, do not use "close" to mean "near" in another.

Cut every word that does not carry information. Delete "it is worth noting
that," "in the context of," "at this point in time," and "in terms of."

Do not use em-dashes or en-dashes as rhetorical punctuation. Use full
stops, commas, or restructure the sentence. Em-dashes are the strongest AI
tell in written English.

Do not use hedging vocabulary as filler. Words like "arguably,"
"somewhat," "relatively," "essentially," "fundamentally," and
"significantly" often signal uncertainty the writer does not feel. Cut
them or replace them with a specific claim.

## Section 3. The four principles from Zinsser

Simplicity. Say the thing in the simplest way that still says it.
Complexity in the subject does not require complexity in the writing.

Brevity. Cut every word that does not earn its place. If a sentence works
without a word, delete the word.

Clarity. The reader should never have to reread to understand. Ambiguous
pronouns, buried subjects, and long dependent clauses all fail this test.

Humanity. The narrative should read like a person wrote it after thinking
carefully about a company. It should not read like a machine assembled it
from a template. Warmth comes from specific observations, not from
decorative language.

## Section 4. AI tells to catch and remove

Scan for each of these and remove it.

- Em-dashes and en-dashes used as a pause or for emphasis
- "Not just X but Y" and "This isn't just X, it's Y" constructions
- "It's worth noting," "It's important to remember," "It's fair to say"
- "At the end of the day," "When all is said and done"
- "Leverage," "utilize," "facilitate" where "use" would work
- "Robust," "comprehensive," "holistic," "seamless" as vague positives
- "Journey," "landscape," "ecosystem" as filler metaphors
- Participial openers like "Looking at the balance sheet..." or "Diving
  into the financials..."
- Adverbs ending in "-ly" that add nothing ("clearly," "obviously,"
  "notably," "importantly")

## Section 5. A worked example

The rewrite demonstrates the style. It does not just describe it.

Before. Fictional example of what to avoid.

> Looking at the overall picture, this is arguably one of the more
> interesting stories in the specialty chemicals space right now. The
> company has essentially built a robust and comprehensive product
> portfolio, leveraging its long-standing relationships with global
> agrochemical majors, and the recent capex announcement — which
> management has framed as transformational — could potentially unlock
> significant growth. It's worth noting that while the near-term margin
> picture is somewhat mixed, the long-term thesis remains fundamentally
> intact.

After. In the required style.

> This is one of the more interesting stories in specialty chemicals right
> now. The company built its product portfolio over fifteen years of
> supplying global agrochemical majors. Those relationships give it
> pricing power that new entrants cannot match. Management announced a
> capex plan last quarter. If the new capacity comes online on schedule
> and fills at guided utilization, revenue doubles by FY29. Near-term
> margins are mixed. The long-term thesis holds.

## Section 6. How to apply this rule

At the end of every company analysis, produce a narrative section of 200
to 400 words that tells the story behind the numbers. The narrative
applies every rule above.

Before returning the narrative, scan it for the AI tells listed in Section
4. Remove any that appear.
