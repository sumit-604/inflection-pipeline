## 2A(ii). Accounting policy aggressiveness — extension beyond the triple-pass
- **Revenue recognition**: point-in-time, standard, not aggressive (Note 3A(a), p.160/227). One

- **Depreciation**: Schedule II lives, unchanged YoY, no revaluation of PPE/intangibles in current or
  prior year (Note 46 standalone p.206; confirmed no revaluation reserve exists in Other Equity,
  p.157). 🟢 not aggressive.
- **Borrowing-cost capitalisation**: no capitalisation threshold disclosed (a minor transparency gap,
  NOT FOUND IN DOCUMENT), but no evidence of aggressive capitalisation of interest into CWIP; capital
  commitments are separately, cleanly tabulated (Note 39/38).
- **Impairment assumptions**: no CGU-level growth/discount-rate disclosure for PPE (consistent with no
  impairment being recognised); investment-in-subsidiary impairment testing is "cost-less-impairment"
  under Ind AS 36 (Note 3B(i), p.166) — the POLICY is standard, the JUDGEMENT is where the problem
  sits (Phase 1B/2A(iii) above).
- **ECL matrix**: unchanged YoY both segments (Building products 1%/30%/45%/100% at 0-1/1-2/2-3/>3
  years; Synthetic yarn 0.01%/2%/10%/100%) — Building products' curve is materially harsher at the
  1-3 year buckets, consistent with a rougher receivables book there, but NOT recalibrated despite the
  ageing-tail deterioration (Note 35(B)(i) standalone p.195/196).
- **Ind AS 116**: immaterial (land lease only, standalone gross ₹0.97 Cr).
- **No policy change quantified anywhere** in the current year beyond routine, "no material impact"
  standard amendments (Note 1(iii)/(iv)).
- **Overall assessment**: accounting POLICY is conservative and unchanged; the weak point is
  JUDGEMENT APPLICATION (subsidiary investment, ICD impairment timing, ECL calibration versus the
  ageing tail), not policy choice.

## 6B. Strategic priorities — specific enough, capital allocated, execution evidence

## 6E. Quiet Abandonment Check (mandatory)
Reading the opening sections (Corporate Snapshot p.4-5, "growth architecture" p.7-12, dedicated ATUM
section p.53-54, Chairman-adjacent framing throughout) side by side with the operational sections
(segmental review p.25-29, CFO's overview p.27-31, Note 36/37 segment financials, AOC-1):

**Finding 1 — hedged retreat, MATERIAL.**
Opening claim: "ATUM integrated solar roofing redefines sustainable infrastructure, pioneering future-
ready, eco-friendly energy solutions" (Corporate Snapshot, p.4); "The Company is positioned to scale
its presence through its differentiated, integrated offering" (dedicated ATUM section outlook, p.54);
"[a] first-mover advantage in an emerging category," "limited direct competition," patents "across
India, the United States and South Africa" (p.53). Operational section where it should show up: the
segmental review (p.25) and the ATUM outlook paragraph ITSELF (p.54) walk this back without naming the
scale: "While volumes moderated in the near term, the Company expects a gradual acceleration in
adoption as awareness improves and the economics... become more compelling" and "recorded modest
offtake during the year" (p.29). No revenue, volume, or capacity number is ever given for ATUM/Atum
Life anywhere in 293 pages, and the wholly-owned subsidiary carrying the ATUM-adjacent retail business
(Atum Life Pvt Ltd) recorded a 71% single-year net-worth collapse and a second consecutive year of
cash losses (Phase 1D, 2A(iii)). Classification: **(c) hedged retreat** — the opening narrative retains
full confidence language ("positioned to scale," "first-mover advantage") while the operational
sections quietly downgrade to "modest," "moderated," with the actual financial deterioration visible
only in Notes the opening sections never reference. Materiality: **changes the thesis** — a reader
relying on the front matter alone would substantially overweight ATUM as a growth driver; the
financial reality (an ~₹0.19 Cr revenue line and a subsidiary near-wipeout) says it is currently
immaterial to the investment case and, on the subsidiary side, a value-destroying drag.

**Finding 2 — silent drop, MODERATE.**
Opening claim (Corporate Snapshot p.4, "growth architecture" p.7-12): the diversified-portfolio thesis
implicitly frames all four business lines (roofing, boards, ATUM, yarn) as comparably-weighted pillars
of a "collective counter-cyclical OTT effect." Operational section where it should show up: the
segmental review and CFO overview in fact show the recovery is driven overwhelmingly by TWO of the
four (boards +19% revenue/+37% profit; yarn +934% profit off a low base), with cement roofing flat and
ATUM/Atum Life effectively invisible in the numbers. The opening's "four equally-weighted pillars"
framing is never revisited or corrected against the actual, highly concentrated, two-segment nature of
the recovery. Classification: **(b) silent drop** — the opening's implied even-handed diversification
narrative is not contradicted, simply never reconciled against the segment note's numbers.
Materiality: **framework noise, leaning toward thesis-relevant** — worth naming because a GARP/
transition read should credit the boards segment specifically, not a generically "diversified"
portfolio.

**Finding 3 — implicit retraction, MODERATE.**
Opening claim (Corporate Snapshot p.4-5, "the boards and panels business... central pillar," "major
capex plan... a commitment that speaks to conviction"): a forward-leaning capital-allocation
commitment. Operational section: the CFO's own overview (p.29) states "Capital expenditure remained
limited and focused on maintenance, de-bottlenecking and selective capacity enhancements... the
Company invested ₹37 crore during the year" — a modest, maintenance-weighted capex figure (Capex/
Depreciation still only 0.57x per Phase 3A) that sits in some tension with the "major capex plan"
framing used elsewhere for the same fiscal year. Classification: **(a) implicit retraction** — the
front matter says "major capex," the CFO's own numbers section says "limited... maintenance... selective."
Materiality: **framework noise** — the capital-commitments note (+122% to ₹16.87 Cr) suggests the
"major" capex is genuinely FORWARD (FY27+), so this reads more as a timing/tense mismatch than a
substantive misrepresentation, but it is worth naming precisely because the two framings sit two pages
apart in the same document.

No other quiet abandonments of comparable scale were identified in the sections reviewed.

## Phase 6 summary + verdict
The front matter is unusually extensive, well-designed, and in places genuinely candid (the JMD's own
attribution of deleveraging partly to land-sale proceeds, the CFO's frank "capex remained limited"
line, the honest textile-segment framing). But it also contains: a reproducible, three-times-repeated
numerical inconsistency on interest coverage (Phase 3C); a plainly contradictory statement on executive
remuneration relative to the Notes three pages later (Phase 2B/5C); and the single most material Quiet
Abandonment in the document — a two-page, patent-and-certification-heavy ATUM narrative that is never
reconciled against the ATUM-adjacent subsidiary's near-total capital erosion, disclosed elsewhere in
the same Annual Report.

**Phase 6 verdict: 🔴 RED FLAG** (on narrative-accuracy grounds specifically — this is a flag on how
the story is told, not a verdict on the underlying business, which the earlier phases already treat on
its own, more mixed, merits).
