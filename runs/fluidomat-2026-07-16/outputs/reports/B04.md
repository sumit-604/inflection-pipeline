## SECTION 1: THE BUSINESS MODEL IN PLAIN ENGLISH

### 1A. One-line description
Fluidomat designs, casts, machines and tests "fluid couplings" — oil-filled hydraulic clutches that sit between a big industrial motor and the machine it drives — and sells them mostly one unit at a time, custom-built to order, to power plants, refineries, steel mills, cement plants, mines and ports (AR FY25 MD&A p.35; Inv. Pres. slide 5).

### 1B. Money-flow chain for each revenue stream
The company discloses revenue in three lines under "Revenue from Operations" (AR FY25 Note 27, p.98): "Sale of products (including spares & components)," "Income from services," and "Scrap sales." Spares are explicitly **commingled** inside the product-sale line and not broken out — this matters because it means the operator's "annuity-like aftermarket" thesis cannot be independently verified from the numbers (flagged below).

**Stream 1 — Sale of products (new couplings + spares/components, commingled), 99.1% of revenue from operations**
[Input: aluminium, cast iron & steel, bought-out components (AR FY25 Note 29.2, p.99)] → [Fluidomat's Dewas plant casts (Al foundry + CI foundry, 50 tonnes/month each), CNC-machines, fabricates, assembles and bench-tests each coupling to the customer's exact kW/RPM spec (Inv. Pres. slide 11)] → [delivered ex-works/to site, sometimes with installation support] → [industrial plant operators, EPC contractors and OEM pump/fan makers, e.g. NTPC, BHEL, BPCL, IOCL, Adani, Sulzer, KSB (Inv. Pres. slide 16)] → [paid per purchase order / milestone billing, standard capital-goods trade terms].

**Stream 2 — Income from services, 0.82% of revenue from operations**
[Input: engineering/commissioning expertise] → [on-site installation, commissioning or retrofit support] → [delivered at customer's plant] → [same industrial client base] → [paid a service fee, small relative to equipment value] (AR FY25 Note 27, p.98).

**Stream 3 — Scrap sales, 0.08% of revenue from operations**
[Input: foundry/machining metal scrap, a manufacturing by-product] → [sold as-is] → [collected by scrap dealer] → [scrap traders] → [pay prevailing scrap price] (AR FY25 Note 27, p.98). Immaterial, non-core.

**Not a separate disclosed stream but cross-cutting — Export sales**: ₹219.05 lakh in FY25, 3.03% of revenue from operations, down from ₹279.23 lakh / 5.03% in FY24 (AR FY25 Note 39, p.101; Note 38 confirms this is FOB export sales & services including Nepal sales realised in INR).

**Aftermarket spares/AMC split** (operator context: "annuity-like" revenue on 60,000+ installed base): **NOT FOUND, check investor presentation or concall.** Neither the AR nor the FY26 investor presentation breaks spares out from new-equipment sales; the AR's own product-sale line bundles them together (AR FY25 Note 27, p.98). This is the single biggest gap in verifying the aftermarket thesis quantitatively.

### 1D. Simplified business model canvas
- **What they sell**: Engineered fluid couplings across four product families — SC (on/off & scoop-controlled variable-speed), SM/HF (fixed-speed), WF/CBSF (hazardous-area, steel-body/water-filled), FNIC/FNCT (IC-engine drive) — up to 6,000 kW, plus spares and minor commissioning services (Inv. Pres. slides 5, 9).
- **Who buys**: Power generation, refinery/petroleum, cement, mining, steel, off-highway and material-handling companies; named clients include NTPC, BHEL, Coal India, ONGC, ADNOC, IOCL, BPCL, HPCL, Reliance, Adani, Jindal Power, L&T, Thermax, Tata, Vedanta, Jindal Steel, Sulzer, KSB, UltraTech, Ma'aden Gold, Petronas, thyssenkrupp, NCL, Etihad Rail (Inv. Pres. slide 16).
- **Why them**: India is "one of only 5 countries globally" able to independently design and manufacture fluid-coupling technology, with 55 years of accumulated engineering IP, ATEX certification for hazardous-area use, and case-study evidence that clients "don't switch fluid coupling vendors after successful installations" (Inv. Pres. slides 5, 12, 17).
- **How delivered**: Ex-works from the Dewas (MP) plant to customer site, with sales offices in Mumbai, Delhi, Chennai, Kolkata and export via 15+ countries (Inv. Pres. slides 15, 27).
- **Cost structure dominance**: Materials (aluminium, cast iron & steel, bought-out components) were ~30.7% of revenue from operations in FY25 (₹2,218.48 lakh / ₹7,218.29 lakh, AR Note 29) and employee costs ~17.9% (₹1,288.59 lakh, AR Note 31) — a materials-and-labour-heavy manufacturing cost base with near-zero finance cost (company is debt-free; Results FY26 Balance Sheet shows Borrowings = 0 in both years).
- **Scarce resource**: Fifty-five years of design know-how, ATEX/ISO-certified vertically integrated manufacturing (foundries + CNC + fabrication + 10 in-house test benches from 15 kW to 400 kW), and a trained GMAW-certified welding/foundry workforce (Inv. Pres. slides 5, 11).
- **Pricing power source or absence**: Likely moderate-to-strong on custom/high-kW/hazardous-duty orders (few certified global competitors) and weaker on standard low-kW couplings that face more competition; **NOT FOUND** — no product-tier gross margin is disclosed to confirm this split.
- **Asset intensity**: Medium. Net Property, Plant & Equipment was ₹1,044.44 lakh at 31-Mar-2026 (down from ₹1,174.21 lakh a year earlier as some plant/land was disposed) plus Capital Work-in-Progress of ₹421.76 lakh building toward the ₹35 crore expansion (Results FY26 Balance Sheet).
- **WC intensity**: Medium and **rising**. Trade receivables grew from ₹2,245.58 lakh to ₹2,963.00 lakh (+32.0%) between 31-Mar-2025 and 31-Mar-2026 while revenue from operations grew only ~0.4% (₹7,218.29 lakh → ₹7,246.14 lakh) over the same period (Results FY26 Balance Sheet & P&L).
- **Regulatory moat or burden**: ISO 9001:2015 / 14001:2015 / 45001:2018 and ATEX compliance function as a moat — an entry barrier for hazardous-area/refinery/mining work and described as "the global export passport" (Inv. Pres. slide 12, 17), not a compliance burden.

One-off note: the FY26 cash flow statement records a ₹76.32 lakh "Profit on Sale of Land," following a ₹169 lakh "Advance against sale of land (free hold)" booked at 31-Mar-2025 — the company monetised part of its freehold land during FY26. This is a one-time item, not part of the core coupling business (Results FY26 Cash Flow Statement; AR FY25 Note 24).

### 1E. The chai-stall-uncle version
Imagine every big factory has giant motors turning huge fans, pumps and crushers. Switching one of these on at full power instantly is like gunning a scooter from a standstill in top gear — it can shake the whole gearbox apart. A "fluid coupling" is like a hydraulic clutch, filled with oil, sitting between the motor and the machine, letting the motor spin up gently before the machine catches speed — this saves the equipment and the electricity bill. Fluidomat is one of only five companies in the world (and the only one in India) that knows how to design and cast these clutches from raw metal, in its own foundry in Dewas, Madhya Pradesh. It mostly sells one clutch at a time, custom-built, to power plants, refineries, steel mills, cement plants and mines — a big-ticket, made-to-order sale, not a subscription. It carries no bank debt, pays a steady dividend, and is now building a bigger factory because the current one is almost full.

### 3D. Unit economics — the physics of the business
- **One unit** = one engineered fluid coupling, built to a customer's specific kW/RPM requirement (ranging up to 6,000 kW per unit, Inv. Pres. slide 5).
- **Revenue per unit**: **NOT FOUND, check investor presentation or concall** — the company discloses only aggregate ₹ revenue and lakh-level order booking, never a per-unit average selling price or unit volume sold.
- **Margin per unit**: **NOT FOUND, check investor presentation or concall** — same reason; no per-unit or per-kW-tier margin breakdown is disclosed anywhere in the AR or presentation.
- **Volume drivers**: number of new orders won across capex-heavy end markets (power, refinery, cement, mining, steel, ports), plus the capacity expansion itself (target: raise annual capacity from 1,500 to 3,500 units over ~2-3 years, Inv. Pres. slide 21).
- **Price drivers**: kW rating/size of the coupling (larger kW commands a higher price), degree of customisation (ATEX/hazardous-area orders likely carry a premium, unconfirmed by disclosed margin data), and value-added engineering/retrofit design work.
- **Cost drivers**: raw materials (aluminium, cast iron & steel, bought-out components — ~30.7% of FY25 revenue from ops, AR Note 29), labour (~17.9% of FY25 revenue, AR Note 31), and foundry/CNC energy and consumables (AR Note 33).
- **Incremental margin / operating leverage**: Clearly high and mix-sensitive. EBITDA margin moved 27% (FY23) → 27% (FY24) → 40% (FY25) → 35% (FY26) even as revenue grew from ₹45 cr to ₹72 cr (Inv. Pres. slide 23). Management explicitly attributes the FY25 spike and the FY26 "normalisation" to "a shift in product and customer mix, alongside input cost pressures" (Inv. Pres. slide 21) — meaning the key lever is **order mix** (large-kW/custom/ATEX vs standard small-kW), not simply volume, plus fixed-cost absorption in the foundry/CNC shop as utilisation rises.

   Reassures: clearly explained transient factors with a credible path back toward 40%. Worries: a structural, permanent shift toward lower-margin standard products.

   Reassures: management confirms spares/aftermarket is a meaningful, growing, quantifiable share, validating the "annuity" thesis. Worries: management cannot or will not quantify it, suggesting the aftermarket narrative is more marketing than fact.

4. **What is the status and cost-to-date of the ₹35 crore modernisation/capacity program (86,988 sq ft civil construction, new CNC machines, foundry upgrades), and is it on schedule for commissioning?**
   Reassures: on-budget, on-schedule, subsidy application progressing. Worries: delays, cost overruns, or subsidy uncertainty.

5. **Why did trade receivables grow ~32% while revenue was nearly flat in FY26, and what is the ageing profile, particularly for PSU/EPC clients?**
   Reassures: growth tied to specific, collectible year-end billings with a strong collection track record. Worries: a genuine slowdown in collections or disputed billings.

6. **What is the succession plan for the Chairman & Managing Director (~age 76), and how is the transition to the next generation being structured?**
   Reassures: clear, gradual succession already underway. Worries: no formal plan communicated.

7. **What explains the decline in export revenue share (from ~5% to ~3% of sales) despite ATEX certification and a stated 15+ country footprint?**
   Reassures: a building export pipeline with large deals in progress. Worries: weakening overseas competitiveness or focus.
