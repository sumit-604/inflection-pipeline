## SECTION 1: THE BUSINESS MODEL IN PLAIN ENGLISH

### 1A. One-line description
SHYAMMETL takes iron ore, coal and bauxite-derived aluminium stock and converts them, in one
continuous "ore to metal" chain, into a widening basket of carbon steel, stainless steel,
ferro alloys and aluminium products — selling both half-finished ("intermediate") metal to
other steelmakers and finished, branded metal to construction, auto, packaging and
infrastructure buyers (Inv. Pres. p.4-7; AR p.66).

### 1B. Money flow chain, by revenue stream
(All flows share the same first two links — captive power + backward-integrated intermediates —
so those are described once, then each finished-product stream is traced onward.)

**Shared backbone:** Iron ore + coal/coke + ferro-alloy inputs (AR p.7, Inv. Pres. p.7) →
pelletised, reduced to sponge iron (DRI), and smelted into billets/pig iron at Sambalpur,
Jamuria, Mangalpur, Kharagpur plants, powered ~81-83% by captive power (Inv. Pres. p.37) →
either sold as-is (merchant intermediates) or rolled/drawn further in-house.

- **Intermediates (Pellets, Sponge Iron, Billets, Pig Iron):** iron ore/coal → rotary
  kiln/DRI/blast furnace → merchant-grade pellets/sponge iron/billets/pig iron → sold to
  other steelmakers, foundries and pipe-makers in West Bengal, Odisha, Chhattisgarh,
  Maharashtra, Jharkhand, Punjab, Nepal (AR p.24-25, 31) → paid on delivery/LC, cash-and-carry
  commodity terms (AR p.23).
- **Carbon steel long products (TMT, Structural, Wire Rod):** billets → rolling mills at
  Sambalpur/Jamuria (German Thermex technology, AR p.26) → SEL Tiger-branded TMT bars,
  structural steel, wire rods → construction contractors/distributors across West Bengal,
  Odisha, Bihar, Jharkhand, North-East states (AR p.26, Inv. Pres. p.54) → dealer network,
  advance/LC-based payment (AR p.23).
- **Ferro Alloys / Specialty Alloys:** manganese/chrome ore → ferro-alloy furnaces → ferro
  chrome, ferro manganese, silico manganese → sold to steel mills in India and exported (UK,
  Korea, Indonesia, Thailand, Japan, New Zealand; 52% of ferro-chrome/manganese/silico-manganese
  mix now exported vs 20% in FY21) (AR p.28; Inv. Pres. p.12, p.16).
- **Stainless Steel (billets, bright bars, wire rod, HR/CR):** acquired via Mittal Corp (Oct'23)
  + backward-integrated ferro-chrome feed → Pithampur/Sambalpur mills (Italian Danieli-built
  rolling mill) → Series 200/300/400 stainless products → kitchenware, automotive/white goods,
  construction/infrastructure buyers, aided by a GoI circular mandating stainless steel on
  national highway bridges in corrosive marine zones (Inv. Pres. p.19).
- **Flat steel (HR/CR coil, colour-coated/roofing sheets):** billets/HR coil → new Cold Rolling
  Mill (CRM) at Jamuria (₹603 Cr greenfield, PLI-approved, Phase 1 commissioned, Phase 2
  April'26) → CR coils, Galvalume/GI-GL coils, SEL Tiger-branded roofing sheets (Royale, Elite,
  Azure, Alfa) → construction/roofing distributors, EV and infrastructure sectors (Inv. Pres.
  p.21-22, p.54).
- **Aluminium (Foil, FRP, upcoming Battery Foil):** aluminium ingot/scrap → Achenbach
  (Germany)-built rolling mill at Pakuria/Giridih → food-grade foil (9-11 micron), flat rolled
  products, EV battery foil (0.005 MTPA, commissioned FY24-25 at ₹26 Cr, AR p.19) → food
  packaging, industrial laminate and EV battery-cell customers, >60% of foil output exported
  (Inv. Pres. p.23).

### 1E. The chai-stall-uncle version
Think of SHYAMMETL like a dhaba that doesn't just cook the final thali — it grows its own
vegetables, mills its own flour, and runs its own gas connection, so it can sell food cheaper
than the dhaba next door that buys everything ready-made. It digs up (well, buys) iron ore and
coal, turns them into half-cooked "sponge iron" and pellets, then finishes some of that into
rebar, wire, stainless steel and aluminium foil, and sells the rest half-cooked to other
dhabas (steelmakers). Because it makes its own electricity (fuel for the stove) and has its
own railway siding (its own delivery van), its cost per plate is lower than most rivals. But
the price of the thali itself — steel and alloy prices — is set by the whole market, not by
this one dhaba, so profits swing hard with the cycle even though the kitchen (backward
integration) stays efficient in good years and bad.

### 2D. Industry lifecycle stage and company position
Global steel is a **mature, contracting** commodity — world consumption fell 0.9% YoY in 2024
to 1.75 billion tonnes, China (54% of global crude steel output) saw a further ~1% demand
decline forecast for 2025 (AR p.63). Indian steel, by contrast, is in a **growth** phase:
demand grew ~8% in CY2024 even as global demand fell, production capacity rose to ~149 million
tonnes in FY24-25 (~6% above FY23-24), and industry demand is projected to grow 5-7.3% CAGR to
221-275 million tonnes by FY33-34 (AR p.63-64). SHYAMMETL's own positioning is a **mid-cycle
scale-up + mix-shift** story: management explicitly frames the company's history as moving
from "Commodity Focused Player" to "Integrated Multi-Metal, Value-Added Producer" (Inv. Pres.
p.4), targeting revenue growth from ₹18,552 Cr (FY26) to ~₹42,500+ Cr (FY31E), an ~18% CAGR,
with EBITDA growing faster at ~22% CAGR as the mix richens (Inv. Pres. p.46).

## SECTION 3: FINANCIAL METRICS THAT MATTER FOR THIS BUSINESS MODEL

### 3A. Ignore-these-track-these

| Commonly tracked ratio | Why misleading/irrelevant here |
|---|---|
| Net Profit Margin (in isolation) | Distorted by D&A on a fast-growing asset base and interest-cost timing; EBITDA margin and EBITDA/tonne are cleaner operating signals given heavy, lumpy capex |
| Trailing/Forward P/E (in isolation) | Earnings are highly cyclical — historical PAT margin has swung from ~3.5% (2012-16) to >20% (FY21-22) across cycles (Inv. Pres. p.5; AR p.14); an un-normalized P/E can badly misprice cycle position |
| YoY Revenue growth (without volume/price split) | Revenue growth conflates volume (company-controlled via capacity) with price (market-set commodity benchmark); must be decomposed into MT growth vs realization change |
| As-reported Gross Margin vs non-commodity peers | ~72% of revenue is raw material cost by design (Inv. Pres. p.57); comparing this "low" gross margin to asset-light businesses is a category error |
| Current Ratio (in isolation) | Dominated by mark-to-market commodity inventory (100-123 days); a "healthy" current ratio can still embed significant commodity price risk |
| ROE alone (without ROCE) | ROE mechanically declines during heavy capex phases (35% Mar-21 → 13% Mar-26, Inv. Pres. p.42) as equity is deployed ahead of earnings ramp; ROCE and incremental-capital returns are the better read during expansion |
