## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

**2A. Demand environment.** RAJOOENG's demand narrative across all four calls
is one of acceleration, not weakness: revenue growth of -7% (FY23, currency-
driven export delay), then +23.5% (FY24), then +27.1% (H1 FY25); order book
climbing from Rs140 Cr to Rs200+ Cr; a cited Rs1,000 Cr enquiry pipeline by
Oct-2024 (RAJOOENG, Q2 FY25, 22-Oct-2024, p.13/15). This is a clear divergence
from KABRAEXTRU's FY26 extrusion segment framing, subject to the 18-month
staleness caveat repeated throughout Part 1. HBLENGINE's AGM describes an
unrelated demand story (Kavach railway signalling order flood, defence fuse
demand surge) with no read-across to extrusion machinery or EV batteries.

**2B. Pricing and input costs.** RAJOOENG reports broadly stable raw material
prices through Oct-2024, protected by order-linked back-to-back procurement,
with margin expansion (EBITDA margin 8.76%→15.10%, PAT margin 6.16%→11.68%
across the four calls) driven mainly by capacity utilisation and a shift
toward higher-margin exports ("domestic is not really encouraging margin,
the margin comes from export," RAJOOENG, Q2 FY25, 22-Oct-2024, p.11; net
margin ~4-5% domestic vs "nearly 30% to 35% more" on exports, RAJOOENG,
Q4 FY23, 16-May-2023, p.20). This does not match a narrative of margin
compression from raw-material inflation for the peer group as of late 2024.

**2C. Capex cycle.** RAJOOENG is an active, continuous capacity expander in
every one of its four calls (see Claim 6). With only one directly comparable
peer in the corpus, this cannot be classified as an industry-wide capacity
race versus a lone expander — that classification needs at least two
comparable peers, and WINDMACHIN's absence (see coverage map) is exactly
the gap that prevents it. What can be said: the peer was expanding, not
retrenching, through the last observable quarter, the opposite of
KABRAEXTRU's FY26 capex trajectory.

**2D. Competitive mentions.** RAJOOENG never names KABRAEXTRU explicitly.
One tentative, unmistakable-only-if-corrected finding: asked directly "who
are our main peers in our segment," MD Khushboo Doshi named two domestic
players, "Windsor machines and carbon extrusion" (RAJOOENG, Q4 FY24,
18-Apr-2024, p.8). "Carbon extrusion" is not a known Indian extrusion-
machinery company; it reads as a plausible mistranscription of "Kabra
Extrusion." Flagged with appropriate caution (this is an inference, not a
confirmed quote) as the only candidate competitive mention of the main
company found in this peer set. If correct, it is weak, incidental
corroboration that a direct peer independently regards KABRAEXTRU as one of
only two domestic competitors worth naming unprompted — consistent with,
but not proof of, some form of market leadership; it says nothing about
magnitude and does not explain the AR FY26 disappearance of the ~40% figure.

**2E. Risks peers discuss that KABRAEXTRU does not.** (i) Red Sea shipping
crisis: import lead-time and inventory/payable-day distortion (RAJOOENG,
Q4 FY24, 18-Apr-2024, p.7) — not discussed in KABRAEXTRU's own AR MD&A per
the Stage 5 handoff. (ii) Shortening machine replacement cycle (20 years to
10 years) creating a refurbishment/replacement demand stream (RAJOOENG,
Q4 FY24, 18-Apr-2024, p.8) — a possible read-across for KABRAEXTRU's
installed base, undisclosed by KABRAEXTRU. (iii) Late-dispatch/space-
occupation risk: large custom machines held in the assembly bay delay other
orders and cost 2-3% of margin (RAJOOENG, Q4 FY23, 16-May-2023, p.8-9) — an
operational risk specific to large custom capital-goods manufacturing that
KABRAEXTRU's disclosures do not address. (iv) Conglomerate/portfolio
discount risk: HBL's chairman discusses investment bankers applying a
"consortium discount" to diversified companies and argues instead for a
"portfolio premium" (HBLENGINE AGM, 25-Sep-2025, p.5) — a live debate that
is directly relevant to KABRAEXTRU's own two-segment (extrusion + battery)
structure but is never addressed in KABRAEXTRU's own disclosures. (v)
Structural lithium-ion cell economics: HBL's explicit statement that
lithium-ion cell production is unprofitable outside China, and its citation
of failed large-scale bets by Reliance, Exide, Amara Raja, and Northvolt
(HBLENGINE AGM, 25-Sep-2025, p.4) — a structural industry risk in the
broader lithium-ion value chain that KABRAEXTRU's AR discussion of Geon
never raises, even as a risk factor to be distinguished from.

## PART 5: CROSS-PEER HYPOTHESIS (mandatory closing step)

**Hypothesis:** across this small peer set, margin trajectory in Indian
extrusion/battery capital-goods small-caps tracks EXPORT/PREMIUM-NICHE
ORIENTATION far more tightly than it tracks the domestic capex cycle — and
this is a specific, testable pattern that neither peer states directly.

RAJOOENG's EBITDA margin rose in lockstep with its export revenue share
across the four calls: export share 35% (FY23) → 45-46% (FY24) → 73-74%
(H1 FY25), while EBITDA margin rose 8.76% → 13.52% → 15.10% over the same
span, with management explicitly stating that "domestic is not really
encouraging margin, the margin comes from export" (RAJOOENG, Q2 FY25,
22-Oct-2024, p.11) and that export margins run "nearly 30% to 35% more"
than domestic (RAJOOENG, Q4 FY23, 16-May-2023, p.20). Independently, HBL
Engineering's chairman frames the company's entire strategy around
deliberately AVOIDING commoditised, price-competed, import-exposed
segments (mass lithium-ion cells, mass artillery-shell ammunition) in favor
of premium, defence-grade niches specifically because "when everybody jumps
on [a commoditised segment] and everybody can import [it], there is no
margin in it" (HBLENGINE AGM, 25-Sep-2025, p.17), citing failed large-scale

happens when a company chases the commoditised end instead.

No single peer states the general rule. But juxtaposed, RAJOOENG's revealed
margin-versus-export-mix correlation and HBL's explicit commoditised-versus-
niche strategic logic point to the same underlying mechanism: in this class
of Indian small-cap capital-goods/battery names, margin resilience now
depends on how much of the business sits in an export or technical niche
insulated from domestic, price-led competition, rather than on the strength
of the domestic capex cycle in general. Tested against KABRAEXTRU: its FY26
extrusion export revenue FELL 11.6%, moving against this cross-peer pattern
at exactly the point in the cycle when the pattern (per RAJOOENG) rewards
export mix most. If the pattern holds, KABRAEXTRU's FY26 problem is at
least partly a mix problem — losing the export lever that is protecting
margin elsewhere in the peer set — rather than, or in addition to, a purely
macro/JJM-driven one. This is testable: it predicts that KABRAEXTRU's
segment margin should correlate positively with its own export share in
future periods, and that any FY27+ recovery attributed to "JJM 2.0" without
an accompanying export-share recovery should be treated with added
skepticism.
