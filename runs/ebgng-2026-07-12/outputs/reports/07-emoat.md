## SECTION 2: CAPACITY & CAPEX PIPELINE

### 2A. Capex programme table

NO EVIDENCE FOUND for a committed capex programme in the traditional (₹Cr, funding source, commissioning date) sense. The company is explicitly asset-light:

- Historical capex (DRHP p.321, Restated Consolidated Financial Information): H1 FY25 ₹25.19mn; FY24 ₹247.27mn (of which ₹212.17mn was a "Leasing Asset – Computer" line, i.e. assets bought for the leasing business, not manufacturing capacity); FY23 ₹12.16mn; FY22 ₹6.40mn. Composition: office equipment, computers, furniture & fixtures, buildings (leasehold improvements), vehicles — 📄.
- Objects of the Offer (DRHP p.91-93): Fresh Issue net proceeds of ₹4,500mn are earmarked almost entirely for (i) prepayment/repayment of outstanding borrowings (up to ₹3,200mn, split ₹2,200mn Company / ₹1,000mn material subsidiary Electronics Bazaar FZC) and (ii) General Corporate Purposes (capped at 25% of gross proceeds). **No line item for new facility construction or manufacturing capex.** — 📄
- Capacity additions that did occur (UAE 3→8 facilities, Mumbai 1→2, monthly capacity 120k→150k units) were achieved through **leased space and headcount** (employees 1,194→2,148 per B05/Concall_May_2026), not owned fixed assets — 🎙️ (concall-only, not yet in any filed document; DRHP still shows the pre-expansion 5-facility, 58,127.82 sq ft footprint as of Sep-2024).

**Conclusion for 2A**: NO EVIDENCE FOUND of a disclosed forward capex programme. Growth capacity is being added via opex (rent, wages), consistent with management's own framing: "the key moat of this business... will be asset-light but working capital driven" (Concall_May_2026).

### 2B. Utilisation trajectory per facility

NOT FOUND at the individual-facility level. Aggregate volume trajectory (📄/🎙️ blended): FY22 195,651 units → FY23 248,135 → FY24 369,320 (all 📄, DRHP p.175) → FY26 727,000 (🎙️, Concall_May_2026). Against a stated current capacity of ~150,000 units/month (🎙️) = ~1.8mn units/year theoretical annualised capacity, implying utilisation of roughly 40% of the newly-expanded capacity base — i.e. the recent facility/headcount build-out was **run ahead of demand**, a genuine but unquantified (management declined specifics) utilisation slack. Flagged as a risk in Section 6, not credited as a moat.

### 2C. Growth embedded in capex (asset-light caveat)

The instructed method — total capex under execution × historical fixed-asset turnover = implied incremental revenue — **is not meaningfully applicable to this business**. Two reasons, both evidenced:

1. There is no disclosed "capex under execution" figure (see 2A) — the IPO proceeds fund debt repayment, not capacity. The arithmetic has no committed-capex input to multiply.
2. The disclosed fixed-asset turnover ratios are themselves extreme and unstable — 15.95x (H1 FY25, not annualised), 31.97x (FY24), 60.65x (FY23), 51.73x (FY22) (DRHP p.176) — reflecting a genuinely asset-light, working-capital-funded model where PP&E gross block is trivial relative to revenue. Applying, say, the FY24 historical capex of ₹247.27mn × a ~32-60x turnover ratio would mechanically imply ₹79-148cr of "embedded" revenue — but this would be **double-counting**: most of FY24's capex was the leasing-asset line already captured in the tiny leasing revenue stream in 1A, not a proxy for the labour/lease-driven capacity build that management says drove the FY26 volume jump. The method's premise (capex drives capacity drives revenue) doesn't hold when capacity is added via rented space and hiring instead.

**capex_embedded_growth_pct = NOT APPLICABLE (0)** — asset-light model, no committed capex pipeline disclosed; growth is opex/working-capital funded per management's own characterisation, not capex funded. Carried as 0 in the YAML with this note attached rather than a computed figure.

### 2D. New geography or market entries

- Netherlands (Electronics Bazaar B.V.) — 📄 subsidiary incorporated (DRHP p.183), for EU penetration.
- UAE facility expansion 3→8 — 🎙️ (Concall_May_2026), positioned as EMEA/APAC hub (existing strategy, not new geography per se).

No independent verification, no global manufacturer count, no qualification-timeline data. 🔍 (inference from a paid-for market report's descriptive claim). **Weak.**

**A2 — Patent and IP pipeline.** DRHP p.196: "We have registered our logo under class 9 of the Trademarks Act" — one trademark, zero patents disclosed, no licensing revenue, no lab partnerships. 📄 **NO EVIDENCE FOUND** (absence itself is documented).

**A3 — Process innovation.** 21-step refurbishing process and multiple data-sanitisation standards (R2V3, NIST 800, US-DoD 5200, Peter Gutmann, US Army AR 380 — DRHP p.179) are described, but no yield trend, no unit-cost-vs-quality disclosure, no automation capex. **NO EVIDENCE FOUND** for the specific "improving yield/declining unit cost" test.

**A4 — Product platform / modular architecture.** 4,996 SKUs as of Sep-2024 (DRHP p.175) — a single snapshot, no multi-year SKU growth trend or launch-frequency data disclosed. **NO EVIDENCE FOUND** (no trend to assess).

### FAMILY B — Supply Chain

**B1 — Backward integration and RM security.** Procurement is via a 447-partner network (corporates, recyclers, leasing cos, OEMs) as of Sep-2024, up from 157 in FY22 (DRHP p.175) — this is network breadth, not captive sourcing. No captive component manufacturing, no long-dated supply agreements disclosed. **NO EVIDENCE FOUND** for backward integration specifically.

**B2 — Qualification lock-in.** Strong and multi-part: certified refurbishment partner with Lenovo and HP (top-2 global laptop brands by 24%/21% share, DRHP p.175, p.179); India's largest Microsoft Authorised Refurbisher by refurbishing capability as of FY24 (DRHP p.167); facility audited by global Lenovo/HP teams (DRHP p.180); R2V3 (Responsible Recycling v3, SERI) certification, EPR registration with CPCB, ISO 9001/14001/27001/45001 (DRHP p.175-176). These are audited/certified facts, not aspirations. 📄 **Strong.**

**B3 — Supply chain network effect.** 447 procurement partners and 3,265 sales touchpoints across 35 countries (DRHP p.175) function as a hub-and-spoke aggregator of a fragmented used-device supply base. Documented counts, but no disclosed platform mechanics (matching algorithm, take-rate, network-effect flywheel data). 📄 (counts) but thin. **Weak.**

### FAMILY C — Customer

**C1 — Customer ecosystem / embedded relationships.** Buyback programmes run jointly with HP, Lenovo and Vijay Sales (9,247 units bought back as of DRHP date, DRHP p.179); new Ingram/Supertron distributor tie-ups (Feb 2026). But management explicitly characterises the Ingram/Supertron contribution as "minuscule" as of Q4 FY26 (Concall_May_2026; cross-ref B05). 🎙️ for the forward-looking piece. **Weak** (moved to optionality register rather than scored active — see below).

**C2 — Customer concentration improving.** Documented, multi-year trend: customers served grew 1,263 (FY22) → 1,833 (FY23) → 3,252 (FY24) → 3,265 (H1 FY25); procurement partners grew 157 → 265 → 356 → 447 over the same period (DRHP p.175, restated financials). This is a genuine, filed, multi-year base-diversification trend. 📄 **Strong.**

### FAMILY F — Talent & Organisational

**F1 — Talent density.** ESOS 2024 established (up to 5% dilution pool) but **zero options granted as of the DRHP date** (DRHP p.88-89, auditor-certified). 610 employees trained, 10,980 cumulative training hours disclosed (DRHP p.179) — an operational-training signal, not R&D/specialist density. 🔍/📄 mixed, thin. **Weak.**

**F2 — Execution moat.** Cross-referencing the injected B05 promise-delivery record: 4 delivered, 2 partial, 2 missed; credibility grade B; "honest" excuse pattern. Delivered: FY26 revenue growth of 34% vs 20-25% guided, and margin expansion of +166-209bps vs +75bps guided (Concall_May_2026); capacity build-out (120k→150k units/month, UAE 3→8 facilities, employees 1,194→2,148). **Missed**: H2 FY26 interest-cost savings (₹10-12cr guided, not delivered — finance cost actually rose per B05) and debtor days (guided 30-35, slipped to 40-45 — confirmed in Concall_May_2026: debtors "gone down to INR206 crore" at Mar-26 but management attributes elevated days to extending credit terms to new distributor partners). This is a genuine execution track record on growth/margin, but the two misses are both cash-conversion items — directly relevant to Gate 0's Block B (cash) score of 0/20 and its deteriorating WC trend (136→178 days). 🎙️/📄 blended. **Moderate** (not Strong — the misses are concentrated exactly where Gate 0 already flags weakness, so this is not credited as a clean execution moat).

### FAMILY H — Ecosystem & External

**H1 — Industry consolidation beneficiary.** The DRHP's own Industry Overview states: "The refurbishment industry in India is predominantly unorganized, creating significant opportunities for organized players... First-mover advantage for organized players" (DRHP p.164), and separately confirms no player holds >5% market share (DRHP p.177). Layered on top: the E-Waste (Management) Rules, 2022 phase in EPR collection targets from 60% (FY23-24/24-25) to 70% (FY25-26/26-27) to 80% (FY27-28) (DRHP p.165-166) — a documented, tightening compliance regime that raises the bar for informal/unorganised competitors and favours certified players like GNG. The regulatory fact is 📄; the consolidation-beneficiary conclusion is 🔍 (analyst inference layered on the documented regulation and fragmentation data, since the DRHP's "first-mover" framing is itself sourced to the company-paid 1Lattice Report). **Moderate.**

**H2 — Strategic partnerships.** Existing Lenovo/HP/Microsoft certified-partner relationships are already-established moat elements (per Gate 0's M3/M4), not emerging. The incrementally new items are the Ingram Micro and Supertron distributor partnerships (signed Feb 2026) — 🎙️, contribution explicitly "minuscule" to date. **Weak** (kept off the active list; see optionality register).

**H3 — ESG moat.** R2V3 (SERI), EPR/CPCB registration, ISO 14001 (environmental management), ISO 45001 (occupational health/safety) all held (DRHP p.175-176). External policy tailwinds cited: France mandates 20% of organisational IT purchases be refurbished (rising to 40% by 2040, and 50% by 2025 in the public sector); Ireland's "Buying Greener" strategy targets 80% of newly-procured ICT products being refurbished or environmentally compliant by 2025 (DRHP p.183, sourced to 1Lattice) — these are documented foreign-government policies, independent of GNG, that structurally favour certified refurbishers exporting into those markets (GNG already sells into Europe). 📄 (certifications + cited government policy). **Strong.**

### 4C. Regulatory moat assessment

**Active vs emerging**: the EPR/R2V3/ISO certification stack is already active (B2/H3 above); what is *emerging* is the escalation schedule — EPR targets step from 60% to 80% collection through FY27-28, which should progressively raise the compliance floor for informal competitors while GNG is already compliant. **Time to kick in**: incremental, staged through FY26-28 (already partially active). **Sustainability**: durable as long as the underlying rules aren't repealed, but **not exclusive to GNG** — the same certifications are attainable by any competitor willing to invest in compliance, so this is a widening-moat-for-the-organised-category story more than a GNG-specific moat. Scored R1 = Strong on the strength of the documented, already-legislated schedule, with the caveat (carried into top_moat_risks) that it is shared, not proprietary.
