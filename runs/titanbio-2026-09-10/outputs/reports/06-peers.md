## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

### 2A Demand environment
Across the two current-cycle peers, FY26 reads as a mixed-to-cautious year, not a boom. ADVENZYMES' own management repeatedly refuses to characterise a trend quarter to quarter ("let's not get into the QoQ... I would say some quarters here and there," Mukund Kabra, Feb-04-2026 call) and named US tariff-driven order softness explicitly (inquiries "used to be, let's say, about hundred in a month dropped down to four, five, six," Mukund Kabra, Nov-13-2025 call). VIDHIING's opening remarks in both its Nov-2025 and May-2026 calls describe "significant global economic uncertainty, geopolitical tensions, tariff pressures and a noticeable slowdown in demand across several international markets" (Bipin Manek, May-14-2026 call) and give a specific, sector-wide capacity-utilisation figure for the wider Maharashtra dyestuff/chemical belt (30-35%, one shift). Titan's own filings offer no demand narrative at all for its FY26 rebound (Stage 5 finding); the peer set does not supply one either — if anything it argues the opposite of an industry tailwind.

### 2B Pricing and input costs
Peers show more active and quantified cost/pricing management language than Titan's boilerplate "risk of raw-material price increase" (unchanged every year, unquantified). ADVENZYMES quantifies its worst-case US tariff hit ("INR 10 crore to INR 11 crore is impact on EBITDA," Beni Rauka, Nov-13-2025 call) and later reports active price increases ("pricing is better in this particular quarter because we have increased some prices," Beni Rauka, Aug-12-2026 call). VIDHIING states explicitly that it held US pricing flat despite a 50% tariff as a demonstration of pricing power from switching costs ("we have not reduced a penny on our pricing to the US market... that is the kind of pricing power that we have," Mihir Manek, Nov-13-2025 call), and separately treats rupee depreciation as a net positive given a 95%-export revenue base. Titan discloses none of this: no tariff exposure quantified (despite a growing, 39%-of-revenue export book), no pricing-power discussion, no currency-sensitivity discussion.

### 2C Capex cycle
This is a moderate, utilisation-triggered industry-wide expansion phase, not a capacity race — each peer times its next capex tranche to a disclosed utilisation threshold (ADVENZYMES ~70-75%, VIDHIING ~65-70% approaching full), and each attaches an expected revenue outcome to the capex number. Titan is a "lone expander" only in the narrow sense that it is the one name in the set that discloses neither a utilisation number nor a stated revenue-per-rupee-of-capex logic — its Rs 34.41cr FY26 investing outflow is smaller in absolute terms than ADVENZYMES' Rs 123cr FY27 plan or VIDHIING's Rs 100cr FY27 plan, and unlike both peers, 77% of it did not go into physical capacity at all (Stage 3/5 finding). The industry pattern is "expand transparently against a stated utilisation trigger"; Titan does not fit that pattern, not because it is expanding faster or slower, but because it does not disclose the trigger.

### 2D Competitive mentions
No peer transcript mentions Titan Biotech by name or by unmistakable description, in any of the 11 calls. This is unsurprising given the imperfect segment overlap (enzymes, vitamin D3, and food colours are adjacent but distinct end-markets from peptones/culture media), and it is recorded here as an explicit finding of absence, not a gap in extraction.

### 2E Risks peers discuss that Titan does not
- Quantified US tariff/EBITDA exposure (ADVENZYMES: Rs 10-11cr EBITDA, ~1-2%; VIDHIING: ~10-20% of quarterly revenue impact in specific quarters) — Titan names no tariff exposure despite a growing export book.
- Currency/FX sensitivity as a named, discussed factor (VIDHIING) — Titan is a large net FX earner (Rs 80.33cr exports FY26) and discusses none.
- Customer-concentration and key-account margin trade-offs (FERMENTA, stale, but structurally recurring: "key accounts... prices, the margins are a little lower because of long-term relationships," Satish Varma, Nov-17-2021 call) — Titan discloses no customer concentration data at all.
- Quantified regulatory/customer-approval-cycle risk (VIDHIING's 4-10 years) as a named pipeline risk — Titan never frames its own export/customer pipeline this way.
- Named competitor counts and market-share percentages in specific product lines (ADVENZYMES: "three, four players," 15% US nutraceutical bulk share) — Titan's own market-share language stays permanently hedged and unquantified across all three ARs (Stage 5 finding).
- Freight/logistics cost volatility as an explicitly named risk category (VIDHIING: "volatile freight markets") — Titan discloses a freight gross-up practice in its numbers but never names freight or logistics as a risk factor in its MD&A.
- Live, adversarial capital-allocation scrutiny of a non-core asset programme (FERMENTA's real-estate deal, challenged directly on a call) — a governance discipline mechanism Titan's FVTPL portfolio has never been subjected to, because no analyst call exists for Titan.

## PART 5: CROSS-PEER HYPOTHESIS

**Hypothesis.** Across this three-name peer set, FY26 margin trajectory has decoupled from FY26 revenue trajectory, and the decoupling mechanism is the same in each case: management is defending or expanding EBITDA margin through an explicit shift toward higher-value/niche product mix, not through volume growth, while headline revenue growth has been flat or erratic. ADVENZYMES held EBITDA margin around 30-32% through quarters where YoY revenue growth swung from 26% down to 2%. VIDHIING's FY26 revenue fell slightly (Rs 380cr vs Rs 382.30cr) while EBITDA margin rose sharply (17.91% to 20.52%) purely from a stated mix shift toward higher-value colours, even though the mix-shift target itself slipped badly (guided at "around 50%" high-value-product contribution in the Nov-2025 call, delivered "around 5%" by the May-2026 call). FERMENTA (stale, but structurally the same pattern) showed margin resilience tracking the human-nutrition (higher-value) share of D3 revenue, independent of the more commoditised animal-feed volume line. No single peer states this as a sector-wide pattern; each describes only its own mix shift. The combined read is that FY26 margin expansion in this specialty bio-ingredient/specialty-chemical export segment should be treated as a MIX story, not a VOLUME story, until proven otherwise by a name in this space.

**Test.** This is directly testable against Titan's own FY26 numbers, and the test cuts against the peer-consistent, favourable reading of Titan's margin recovery. If Titan's OPM improvement (16% FY25 to 19% FY26) were a genuine value-add mix climb — analogous to VIDHIING's manufacturing-mix-driven margin gain — the cost-of-materials ratio should have DECLINED as higher-value lines (e.g., the collagen-peptide brands) gained revenue share, the way VIDHIING's manufacturing gross margin moved as trading revenue was retired. Instead, Titan's cost-of-materials ratio was flat at 48% of revenue between FY25 and FY26 (Stage 1/2 finding). A flat raw-material ratio alongside a rising OPM is more consistent with an operating-leverage/volume-recovery explanation (or a freight-accounting effect, per the Load-Bearing Fact #1 gross-up question) than with the value-add mix-climb pattern this peer set otherwise exhibits when margin expands. This does not resolve the Titan thesis question, but it gives Stage 11/FTTCP a specific, sourced cross-check to run: ask whether Titan's FY26-FY27 margin gain is genuinely mix-driven (bullish, supports the "value-added mix climb" reading in the Company Memory thesis) or base/volume/accounting-driven (bearish, supports the "delayed restock" reading) — and note that the peer-consistent MIX signature (a declining raw-material ratio) is currently ABSENT from Titan's own numbers.
