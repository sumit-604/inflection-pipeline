# Entero Healthcare Solutions — Business Narrative (Phase 1)

## Business understanding narrative

Entero Healthcare buys medicines and medical devices from makers and sells them on to pharmacies and hospitals; sale of traded goods is 99% of revenue. The company stocks about 97,500 product lines from roughly 3,300 manufacturers and ships from about 138 warehouses across 19 states. A single pharmacy cannot hold thousands of lines from thousands of makers, nor fund the credit that stocking them needs, so Entero aggregates the catalogue, carries the receivable, and fulfils the order. The customers are retail pharmacies and hospitals, and they stay because switching means rebuilding a catalogue and a credit line, though that lock-in is modest. MedTech customers are hospitals buying diagnostics, cardiology, orthopaedics, and consumables.

Present demand tracks India's pharma consumption, which an outside reader follows through IQVIA and AIOCD monthly data; price controlled lines cut the distributor margin, so NPPA and DPCO notifications on NLEM products are the second signal. Demand should grow for three reasons: organised distributors hold only 8 to 10% of the market, MedTech is scaling toward a Rs 1,000 Cr run rate at higher margin, and hospital capacity keeps expanding. Each has an outside check: Apollo HealthCo and Keimed disclosures for consolidation pace, hospital chain bed and capex releases for MedTech pull, GST e-way-bill pharma volumes for the formalisation shift, and Sun Pharma, Cipla, and Alkem commentary for underlying formulation growth.

The competitive advantage is uneven. The distribution network is the moat management names, and it reads as a moderate supply chain network effect, but every reach metric fell 28 to 36% in the June 2026 quarter, unreconciled, while warehouse count rose. Until that is explained the core moat is a claim, not a proven barrier. The digital platforms, Entero Direct, Teqtic, HealthEdge, and the Enteropreneur programme, exist but disclose no adoption numbers, so their lock-in is unproven. The one durable edge is external: Entero gains from sector consolidation, a tailwind that helps every organised distributor. No structural barrier stops a funded rival from copying the roll-up.

Fast scaling pharma distributor built by acquisition

Entero is a business to business distributor of pharma and MedTech products, listed in February 2024, now worth about Rs 5,537 Cr. Revenue grew 29% in FY26 to roughly Rs 6,600 Cr. Net margin is thin at about 2%, normal for a distributor that earns an 8 to 15% gross spread, not a maker's markup. The company has grown by buying regional distributors, so more than 45 acquisitions sit inside the group.

What is changing is the mix and the cash. MedTech, the higher margin leg, is guided to cross Rs 1,000 Cr in FY27 and to rise from about 15% of sales toward 20% over two to three years. EBITDA margin moved from 4.0% in FY26 toward the guided 5.0%, and hit 5.0% in the June 2026 quarter. Operating cash flow turned positive at Rs 96 Cr in FY26, the first positive year after six negative ones. That cash turn is the transition the thesis rests on.

Gate 0 scored the company AVOID, core 35 of 100. Read that as provisional. About half the score is genuine weakness: cumulative operating cash flow was negative Rs 203 Cr across FY20 to FY26 against positive cumulative profit of Rs 193 Cr, and ROCE sits at 10 to 12% on the two years that can be computed. The other half is missing data: the shareholding block scored zero for want of a filed pattern, four of twelve moat tests needed peer figures the corpus did not hold, and the return and working capital trends rest on two usable years.

The cash reality is unresolved, and honestly so. Free cash flow cannot be computed at all, because no source gives a consolidated capex line. FY26 operating cash flow turned positive, but trade receivables grew 50.6% against revenue growth of 29.4%, running 1.7 times faster. About 85% of the jump in the expected loss provision came in with acquisitions rather than from the existing book. Management refused to disclose receivables ageing when an analyst asked directly on the Q3 FY26 call, and refused quarterly debt and cash flow detail on the Q1 FY27 call.

Management says what it will do and mostly does it. The FY26 triple guidance of 30% growth, 4% margin, and about Rs 100 Cr of operating cash flow was essentially met. The MedTech Rs 1,000 Cr target landed on schedule. That earns a B credibility grade, not an A: the same management will not disclose receivables ageing, and it left the 28 to 36% drop in network reach metrics unexplained in the quarter it reported record margins.

Peers neither confirm nor clear the story. The three available peers, MedPlus, Redington, and Redington Technology, sit in adjacent industries, so five of the pharma specific claims could not be tested. Where peers can speak, the read is mixed. Redington's same store gross margin supports the direction of Entero's margin mix story but not its size. Neither MedPlus nor Redington Technology showed any network contraction in the same quarter Entero's reach fell, which undercuts the idea that the drop was industry wide.

The market is large and the runway is long. The distributor revenue pool Entero fishes in is about Rs 3.7 to 5.0 lakh Cr, and Entero holds only about 2.5% of its served market. Revenue headroom is roughly 40 times. The constraint is not the market; it is funding. Reaching the three year revenue path needs about Rs 1,000 to 1,100 Cr of extra working capital, on top of debt that rose 92% year on year and about Rs 351 Cr of near term put option and deferred acquisition payments.
