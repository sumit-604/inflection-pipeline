# SFL business summary (post FTTCP, plain language)

Sheela Foam makes mattresses and polyurethane foam, which is the soft
cushioning inside a mattress, under two brands, Sleepwell and Kurlon. It also
owns Furlenco, a furniture rental business, and foam plants in Australia and
Spain. FY26 revenue was Rs 3,821 crore. It sells through about 13,000 dealers
and 1,800 own stores, plus online and a growing small town dealer network. Its
two main raw materials are TDI and polyol, chemicals whose prices swing a lot.

The business is coming out of a rough patch. Profit fell to Rs 89 crore in FY25
and bounced back to Rs 161 crore in FY26, up 78 percent. Debt fell from Rs
1,493 crore to Rs 908 crore as the company digested its 2024 Kurlon buyout, and
it paid its first ever dividend. Cash is the strong point. The company brought
in Rs 409 crore of operating cash on Rs 161 crore of profit.

Opportunities. The organised mattress market is about Rs 7,000 crore and Sheela
Foam already holds close to 30 percent of it. The growth engines are the small
town dealer network, where sales more than doubled in FY26, online sales up 39
percent, the Gulf, and Furlenco.

Strengths. Two of the largest mattress brands in India, the widest dealer
network, cash coming in faster than profit, falling debt, and a plant base that
can make two to two and a half times more without big new spending.

Weaknesses. Margins are thin at about 10 to 11 percent, below plywood and
laminate peers. Reported return on capital is under 7 percent because the
Kurlon deal loaded the books with goodwill, the extra paid above book value.
Management keeps missing its own smaller targets. It cut the store opening plan
three times and has delayed one cost saving machine four quarters in a row.

Risks. Raw material prices jumped 25 to 35 percent and management admits it
cannot predict them. Competition is rising, with Wakefit and Duroflex
discounting hard, so pricing power is not proven. Much of the FY26 profit came
in the second half and was helped by an accounting change, so the recovery may
look better than it is. Big investors have been selling, not buying.
