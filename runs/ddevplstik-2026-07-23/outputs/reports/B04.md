## SECTION 1: THE BUSINESS MODEL IN PLAIN ENGLISH

### 1A. One-line description
DDev Plastiks buys raw plastic resin (PVC and polyethylene) and petrochemical
additives, melts and chemically re-engineers them into custom "compounds" —
essentially recipe-grade plastic — that cable and appliance makers use as the
insulation, jacketing and structural material in their finished products; it now
also assembles imported lithium battery cells into containerised grid-storage
units (BESS) as a second, mechanically different business bolted onto the same
balance sheet.

### 1E. The chai-stall-uncle version
Think of DDev like the person at a roadside stall who does not sell you raw
sugar or raw tea leaves — they buy those and mix, boil and blend them into the
exact chai recipe each customer wants, then sell that finished mix by the kilo.
DDev buys plain plastic resin, and through heating, mixing and some clever
chemistry (cross-linking) turns it into hundreds of specific "recipes" of
plastic that cable companies like Polycab or Havells need to coat their wires
so they do not catch fire or leak electricity. Because DDev's price is
basically "raw material cost plus a fixed fee per kilo," when plastic prices
spike, they pass most of it straight through to the cable companies within
days — so their profit per kilo stays roughly steady even when the news is full
of oil-price shocks. And now they have started a completely different side
business: instead of cooking a recipe, they are importing ready-made battery
cells (like buying pre-made biscuits) and just assembling and packaging them
into big storage boxes for solar/grid projects — a snap-together business, not
a cook-from-scratch one, sitting inside the same company.

### 2D. Industry lifecycle stage
The core polymer-compounding-for-cables industry is in a mature-but-growing
stage in India — the Indian wire and cable market is projected to grow from
USD 21.22 Bn (2025) to USD 32.85 Bn (2030) (AR p.83, sourcing Mordor
Intelligence), and India's cable compounding market itself is roughly ₹2.5 lakh
tonne p.a. with DDev holding about one-third share (Inv. Pres. p.19) — DDev
sits as a scaled incumbent riding an expanding underlying market. BESS, by
contrast, is at the earliest formative/nascent stage — the presentation itself
describes India's BESS sector as "nascent... poised for explosive growth,"
citing a 22% global CAGR to 2030 (Inv. Pres. p.9) — and DDev is a first-mover
entrant with zero operating history in that specific vertical.

## SECTION 3: FINANCIAL METRICS THAT MATTER FOR THIS BUSINESS MODEL

### 3A. Ignore-these, track-these

| Commonly tracked ratio | Why it MISLEADS or is IRRELEVANT here |
|---|---|
| Gross margin % (as a standalone trend line) | Mechanically compresses/expands with the absolute level of RM prices even when the underlying ₹/kg spread is unchanged (cost-plus pricing model, Concall May-2026 p.13); track EBITDA/kg (spread) instead of GM% |
| Standalone trailing P/E | Understates optionality — BESS carries zero FY26 earnings but real capex/capacity commitments (₹150-200 Cr Phase-1, Inv. Pres. p.11); a blended P/E prices two businesses with very different growth/margin trajectories as one |
| Order book / backlog | Not a project/EPC business model — sales are short-cycle spot/repeat orders capped at ~90 days even for exports (Concall May-2026 p.15); an order-book lens borrowed from EPC/construction peers does not apply |
| Dividend yield | Company is in a multi-year reinvestment phase (₹350-400 Cr core capex plus ₹150-200 Cr BESS capex over 2-3 years, funded from internal accruals, Inv. Pres. p.9, p.11); not an income story |
| Debt/Equity as the primary leverage-risk lens | Company has been net debt-free since Q4FY24 with Net Debt/Equity near 0.0x for FY24-26 (Inv. Pres. p.25); the standard "leverage funds growth" risk framework does not apply — growth is being funded from accruals, so capex execution risk (not balance-sheet risk) is the metric to watch |
| Same-store-sales style comparisons | Not a retail/distribution business; not applicable |

| Cost drivers | Resin/petrochemical input price, freight (mitigated by coastal plant siting), power & fuel (₹4,269.79 lakh FY25 vs ₹3,663.20 lakh FY24, AR Note on other expenses) |
| Incremental margin / operating leverage | Core: EBITDA/tonne has stayed roughly flat (₹14-16/kg) even as revenue/tonne rose, meaning the pass-through model caps organic margin expansion from RM moves — incremental margin instead comes from mix-shift toward higher-margin lines (XLPE/HFFR) and from utilization gains on largely fixed manufacturing overhead (utilization went from 52% FY21 to 75% FY26 on total installed capacity, Inv. Pres. p.27) — classic operating leverage on the fixed-cost base, not on RM spread. BESS: EBITDA margin explicitly guided to ramp from 5-8% initial to >10% at scale (Inv. Pres. p.11-12) — a scale/learning-curve leverage story, structurally different from the core business's utilization-driven leverage |

## SECTION 4: RISKS, VALUATION APPROACH & MONITORING

### 4A. Business-model-specific risks

| Category | Risk | First financial line item that would deteriorate |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue model | Loss of a top qualification-based customer (top client ~9%, top-5 ~25% of FY26 revenue, Inv. Pres. p.30-31) | Trade receivables/revenue growth by segment; top-5 customer revenue share (if disclosed going forward) |
| Margin | Pass-through lag failing to fully offset a violent RM spike (as happened Israel-Iran, Feb-2026) | EBITDA/tonne (spread) falling below the ~₹14-16k/tonne band; EBITDA margin % |
| Balance sheet | BESS capex overrun or funding shift away from internal accruals toward debt | Net Debt/Equity moving off ~0.0x; capex vs. guided ₹150-200 Cr (Phase-1) |
| Execution | BESS Phase-1 commissioning slippage past Q3FY27, or utilization/customer-contract shortfall | BESS segment revenue vs. the guided ₹300-500 Cr (FY28 window) / ₹200-250 Cr (FY27) ranges once disclosed |
| Structural | R&D spend (0.06% of turnover, AR R&D section) insufficient to sustain the certification/innovation-based moat as global rivals (Dow, LG, Borealis) invest more heavily | R&D expenditure as % of turnover; rate of new certification/approval wins per Inv. Pres. p.43-style disclosures |

### 4B. Valuation method applicability — formal handoff to Role 1 (Stage 11)

| Method | Applicability | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| EV/EBITDA (core compounding business) | Applicable — cross-check | Best fits a capacity/utilization-driven manufacturer where EBITDA/tonne is the cleanest unit-economics signal; needs adjustment to strip out pre-revenue BESS capex drag |
| Sum-of-the-parts (SOTP): core compounding (EV/EBITDA) + BESS (EV/Sales or capacity-based, ₹/GWh) | Applicable — PRIMARY | The two businesses have genuinely different margin profiles (core ~11% EBITDA margin at scale vs BESS guided 5-8% ramping to >10%), different capital intensity (compounding capex ~₹80 Cr for 48,000 MTPA vs BESS ₹150-200 Cr for 5 GWh phase-1), and different growth trajectories (core: 13-15% volume growth guided; BESS: 0-to-₹2,000-2,500 Cr incremental by FY30 per operator context, non-anchored — cross-check against future filings). Blending them into one multiple misprices either. |
| EV/Sales (BESS segment specifically, pre-earnings visibility) | Applicable — SECONDARY, BESS component only | Appropriate for the early-stage, pre-EBITDA-visibility ramp phase of BESS (2HFY27 revenue start, margins only 5-8% initially, Inv. Pres. p.11) |
| P/E (blended, standalone) | Not primary — misleading if used alone | Understates BESS optionality pre-earnings; usable only as a sanity cross-check on the core business once SOTP is built |
| DCF | Tertiary at best, use with caution | High assumption-sensitivity given BESS has zero operating history to anchor terminal-value or margin-ramp assumptions; more defensible for the core compounding business alone than for the combined entity |
| Dividend Discount Model | Not applicable | Company is in a multi-year reinvestment phase funded by accruals (₹350-400 Cr core + ₹150-200 Cr BESS capex, Inv. Pres. p.9, p.11); not a distribution story |
| Asset-based / Book value (P/B, NAV) | Not applicable | ROE has run 20-28% in recent years (Inv. Pres. p.24); book value materially understates a business generating this return on equity, and the BESS vertical has no comparable asset base to value on a replacement-cost basis yet |
| Order-book-based valuation (EPC-style) | Not applicable | Sales cycle is short (max ~90-day export contracts, Concall May-2026 p.15); no multi-year order book exists in the core business (BESS may develop one, but not yet disclosed) |

**Stated handoff: PRIMARY = SOTP (core EV/EBITDA + BESS EV/Sales-or-capacity-based). SECONDARY = EV/Sales for the BESS segment as a stand-alone cross-check. TERTIARY = DCF on the core compounding business only (exclude BESS from DCF until it has at least 2-3 quarters of revenue history).**

Cycle stage that matters for valuation: the core business is mid-cycle on a
structural growth curve (14% revenue CAGR FY21-26, Inv. Pres. p.6, with FY30
target of ₹5,000 Cr ex-BESS per operator context), while BESS is pre-cycle
(commissioning phase) — valuation multiples should be applied on the core at a
mature-manufacturer-with-growth-tailwind basis, and on BESS at an
early-stage/pre-profitability basis with a wide execution-risk discount until
Phase-1 commissions and reports its first full quarter of revenue.

### 4C. Quarterly monitoring checklist (10-15 items)

1. Capacity utilization by product line, especially PVC and HFFR (both stuck near ~49-50% FY26, Inv. Pres. p.27) — good: rising toward 70%+; trouble: flat or falling
2. EBITDA per tonne/kg (spread) — good: holding near ₹14-16k/tonne; trouble: sustained drop below ~₹10-12k/tonne
3. Volume growth (MT), core compounding — good: on track for FY27's guided 15%/2,31,000 MT (Inv. Pres. p.7); trouble: sub-5% for two straight quarters
4. Export revenue and volume growth, and any transit/geopolitical disruption commentary — good: double-digit growth resuming; trouble: renewed disruption without a stated mitigation
5. Bhiwadi XLPE (48,000 MTPA) ramp-up and revenue contribution (~₹500 Cr potential, Inv. Pres. p.8) — good: on/ahead of the 1QFY27-onward recognition schedule; trouble: utilization stuck low multiple quarters post-commissioning
6. HFFR capacity ramp to 20,000 MTPA by FY27 (Inv. Pres. p.32) — good: on schedule; trouble: capex delay
7. BESS Phase-1 (5 GWh Ahmedabad) commissioning milestone vs. the Q3FY27 target (Inv. Pres. p.11) — good: on track; trouble: any public slippage
8. BESS revenue recognition start (2HFY27) and first reported quarterly revenue vs. the ₹200-250 Cr / ₹300-500 Cr FY27 guided range (Inv. Pres. p.13, operator context) — good: within/above range; trouble: materially below with no explanation
9. Net working-capital days (receivables + inventory - payables) — good: holding near the ~65-73 day range; trouble: stretching past ~90 days
10. Net Debt/Equity and Net Debt/EBITDA — good: staying near 0.0x; trouble: any move toward term debt to fund BESS capex (contradicts the stated internal-accruals-only plan, Inv. Pres. p.11)
11. Top-5/top-10 customer revenue concentration trend — good: stable or diversifying; trouble: rising concentration into named accounts
12. Debtors turnover ratio — good: holding near/above ~6x; trouble: falling meaningfully below 6x (already declined 6.39x -> 6.02x FY24->FY25, AR p.84)
13. R&D spend as % of turnover — good: rising from the current 0.06% base if the innovation-moat narrative is to hold; trouble: staying flat/negligible while competitors invest more
14. New product certifications/approvals won (132kV progress, any 220kV milestone) — good: incremental wins reported; trouble: silence on the stated roadmap (Inv. Pres. p.32)
15. Any new named BESS customer/contract disclosure (EPC/integrator wins) — good: named contracts emerging; trouble: continued absence of named customers as commissioning approaches

### 4D. Highest-value questions for management

1. Worrying: refusal to disaggregate, or evidence that the blended spread is propped up by one line while others compress.
3. Q: "Who are the named EPC/integrator customers or LOIs secured for BESS ahead of the Q3FY27 commissioning, beyond the general SECI/NTPC/POWERGRID 'radar' list?" — Reassuring: specific signed contracts or LOIs with volume commitments. Worrying: no named customers this close to commissioning.
4. Q: "Why is R&D expenditure only 0.06% of turnover (AR FY25) despite the innovation-led positioning (WTR XLPE, 132kV/220kV roadmap)?" — Reassuring: most product development is embedded in plant trial-run/production costs (not separately capitalised) with a credible explanation of where the real innovation spend sits. Worrying: R&D genuinely is this thin, and future voltage-class/certification wins are at execution risk.
5. Q: "What happens to PVC and HFFR utilization (currently ~49-50%) if the guided FY27 volume growth of 15% does not materialise — is that capacity a stranded-asset risk or a demand-timing issue?" — Reassuring: specific named demand pipeline (e.g., Adani/Ultratech cable capacity coming online, Inv. Pres. p.8) explaining the utilization gap as timing. Worrying: generic "demand will come" answers with no named driver.
6. Q: "Is the ₹350-400 Cr core capex and ₹150-200 Cr BESS capex genuinely fully funded by internal accruals through the full multi-year build, or is a term-loan drawdown contemplated if BESS ramp is slower than planned?" — Reassuring: explicit accrual-funding math shown. Worrying: hedging language that leaves debt-funding on the table.
7. Q: "What is the pass-through lag and fixed-price contract exposure specifically for exports, given the ~90-day cycle and back-to-back import bookings — how much RM-price risk genuinely sits on DDev's books during that window?" — Reassuring: minimal open exposure, hedged via back-to-back bookings (as already partly stated, Concall May-2026 p.15). Worrying: material unhedged inventory/price exposure during the 90-day export cycle, especially visible after the Feb-2026 Israel-Iran shock.
