## 5. Write-off policy history: the 90 / 120 / 150 DPD question

FINDING: no numeric write-off trigger exists in either filing. Both documents
carry only a qualitative test, in identical words.

QUOTE (AR PDF p.103, consolidated accounting policy; the standalone text at AR
PDF p.70 is word for word the same):

> "Write-off
> The Company writes off financial assets, either partially or in full, when there
> is no reasonable expectation of recovery based on past experience and assessment
> of the borrower's financial condition.
> Amounts written off that exceed the accumulated loss allowance are recognised as
> an expense in the Statement of Profit and Loss in the period in which the
> write-off occurs."

QUOTE (RHP PDF p.277 / printed p.271, restated financial information accounting
policy): the same two sentences appear, prefaced by "A write-off of a financial
asset is considered a derecognition event."

What the filings DO say about days past due:

- 90 DPD is the default and Stage 3 threshold. QUOTE (RHP PDF p.16 / printed p.10,
  definitions): "Gross Stage 3 loans A loan is categorised as Stage 3 when a
  borrower fails to make contractual payments within 90 days".
- 90 DPD is also the legal and recovery trigger. QUOTE (RHP PDF p.207 / printed
  p.201): "Legal and recovery processes are typically initiated when customer
  accounts reach 90 DPD."
- 180 DPD appears, but only inside the AUM formula, not as a write-off rule. QUOTE
  (RHP PDF p.166 / printed p.160): "Note(s): Formula - AUM doesn't include - 180
  and above DPD, restructured assets, write off assets". Repeated at RHP PDF p.170
  / printed p.164: "Formula - AUM excludes 180+ DPD, restructured assets and write
  off assets".
- A board-approved write-off policy exists as a document. QUOTE (RHP PDF p.41 /
  printed p.35): "...management policy, outsourcing policy, asset classification and
  write off policy, expected credit losses policy, privacy policy,". The policy
  itself is not in the corpus.

COMMENT.
- 120 DPD and 150 DPD appear NOWHERE in the RHP or the AR. The only source in the
  whole corpus for the move from 120 to 150 DPD is the Q4 FY26 call, where the CFO
  states it. No filed document corroborates it, dates it, or quantifies its effect.
- Effective dates: NOT DISCLOSED, for any threshold.
- Note the structural point this raises. AUM excludes 180+ DPD assets by formula,
  while Stage 3 starts at 90 DPD. If write-off happens at 150 DPD, then the
  window in which a loan can sit in reported Gross NPA is roughly 90 to 150 days,
  and the AUM denominator stops counting it at 180 days in any case. The filings
  state each threshold separately and never set them side by side.
- Where a numeric trigger would sit if disclosed: the "Write-off" accounting policy
  note in either statement, or Si Creva's own annual report, which is not in the
  corpus and is not public.

## 12. The deck's AUC chart: which model

QUOTE (Investor_Presentation_1.pdf p.23, Q1 FY27 deck):

> "FRAUD DETECTION MODEL - AUC PERFORMANCE
> Latest AUC
> ...
> 2023 66% AUC Baseline
> 2024 68% AUC +2pp
> 2025 70% AUC +2pp
> ... 74% AUC +4pp"

QUOTE (AR PDF p.15):

> "400+ UNDERWRITING VARIABLES
> 40 AI/ML MODELS DEPLOYED
> 2.5x BETTER RISK SEPARATION THAN TRADITIONAL BUREAU SCORES
> 74% LATEST MODEL AUC
> Our fraud detection framework integrates over 200 fraud triggers across identity
> verification, device intelligence, transaction monitoring, documentation checks,
> geolocation, and payment behaviour to strengthen portfolio quality."

ANSWER.
- The RHP never uses the term AUC, or "area under". Zero occurrences in 464 pages.
  So no SEBI-filed document defines what the 66% to 74% series measures.
- The deck labels the chart "FRAUD DETECTION MODEL". The AR puts "74% LATEST MODEL
  AUC" in a block whose surrounding text describes the fraud detection framework,
  which is consistent with the deck.
- The AR's own layout is ambiguous in one way worth flagging: the same block carries
  "400+ UNDERWRITING VARIABLES" and "40 AI/ML MODELS DEPLOYED" next to "74% LATEST
  MODEL AUC", so a reader can take the 74% as an underwriting-model statistic. The
  deck's heading says fraud detection. Nothing in either document states the AUC of
  any credit underwriting or probability-of-default model.
- The nearest filed underwriting performance statistics are different metrics
  entirely: "Our 90 DPD risk for first EMI, a critical early delinquency metric, has
  declined from 6.83% to 0.83% between" (RHP PDF p.200 / printed p.194) and "an
  underwriting approval rate of 11.20% for new applicants and 73.54% for repeat
  customers in the nine months ended December 31, 2025" (RHP PDF p.199 / printed
  p.193).
- Conclusion for the web side: the 66% to 74% series is a fraud-model statistic on
  the documents' own labelling, and phase 1 treated it as evidence of credit
  underwriting improvement. That reading is not supported by any filed text.
