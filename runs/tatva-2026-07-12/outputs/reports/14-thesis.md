## SECTION 3: THE BULL CASE (Why Buy)

Presented in full because the framework demands a symmetric bull-bear read. None of it changes the AVOID at CMP; it sizes what a buyer in the Rs 97-121 zone would be buying.

**3A. Quality Evidence.** The forward numbers are recovering from a genuine trough: FY25 EPS Rs 2.44 rebounded to FY26 Rs 17.98 (B11 line 52); EBITDA margin climbed 18.0% -> 19.4% -> 20.9% across Q2-Q4 FY26 (B07 line 31); FY26 revenue crossed the Rs 500 Cr milestone at Rs 505.86 Cr (B11 line 19). The balance sheet carries only Rs 114.6 Cr net debt (B11 line 17) and zero promoter pledge (B08 line 29). But the quality case is thin: ROCE is 6.6% (B11 line 28), FCF negative in all 8 computable years FY19-FY26 (cumulative FCF/PAT -1.32, B01 line 17), and Gate 0 Block B scores 2/20 (B01 line 20). This is a recovery story, not a quality story.

**3B. Growth Triggers (ranked by conviction, B05 lines 15-22).**
1. SDA / Euro 7 tailwind (conviction H). New-customer invoicing ~Aug 2026; FY27 SDA guided >=20% reaching Rs 250-300 Cr (B05 line 43). Confirmed by: on-schedule invoicing. Killed by: EV/SDA demand reversal or Euro7 delay. Caveat: the 15%->70% share claim vs Sarchem is UNVERIFIABLE against peers (B06 line 22).
2. EBITDA margin sustaining 20-22% (conviction H). Three-quarter delivery track record (B07 line 31). Killed by: RM cost shocks not passed through.
3. PASC agro block on new Dahej (conviction H) and Pharma intermediates ~Rs 70-75 Cr FY27 (conviction M) (B05 lines 19-20).
4. Electrolyte Salts ramp to 8-10% of revenue (conviction M); Q4 FY26 delivered +1,378% YoY off a tiny base (B05 line 54).
5. Jolva greenfield (Rs 400-500 Cr potential) and semiconductor optionality (up to Rs 2,000 Cr speculative) — both conviction L, both long-dated and slip-prone (B05 lines 21-22; B07 line 34).

**3C. Moat Assessment.** Existing moats are real but shallow: switching costs HIGH durability (multi-year requalification), all others MODERATE or lower (B04 lines 28-35); only 1 of 12 Gate 0 moats confirmed (B01 line 23). **3D. Management Quality.** Promoter verdict CAUTION, zero red flags / zero deal-breakers but a four-item FY24-25 amber cluster (B08 lines 19-25): GPCB Ankleshwar plant-closure order Sept 2024 (revoked after remediation, root cause unconfirmed); promoter-executive pay +27.89% while standalone PAT fell 98.9% and dividend was cut Rs 2 -> Rs 1; two CRISIL downgrades in under a year to BBB+/Stable (June 2025); 16 unrelated promoter-group entities disclosed as related parties (no RPT value). Credibility grade B (B05 line 65): core financial guidance delivered on schedule, but long-cycle capex commitments repeatedly slip (Jolva 3x, B05 line 76) and ROCE questions were deflected twice (B05 line 62). But SOM-implied revenue CAGR is only 14.3% 3yr / 13.9% 5yr (B09 line 24), below the 20% that would earn a growth premium, and management's own aggregate TAM claim ($141.78bn) is INFLATED ~79.7x the product-specific estimate (B09 lines 13, 30-31). **3F. Peer Positioning.** Middle of the pack, and the demand narrative is contradicted. Two of four listed peers (Clean Science, Neogen) describe persistent or reversing soft demand across the exact FY26 window Tatva frames as destocking-to-restocking recovery (B06 lines 8, 19). The "sole India supplier" of hybrid-battery electrolytes framing is contradicted by Neogen's own India electrolyte-salt platform (B06 lines 9, 20). ## NARRATIVE (plain English)

Tatva Chintan is a genuinely improving business at an impossible price. Earnings have rebounded hard off an FY25 trough, margins have climbed three quarters running, and the SDA / Euro 7 and Dahej stories give it real near-term triggers. But the market already pays 73.75 times earnings for it, and the four-pillar framework says the quality of those earnings — a 6.6% return on capital that sits below the cost of that capital, and cash that has not converted in eight straight years — is worth only about nine times. That gap, roughly an eight-to-one de-rating, swamps any plausible earnings growth. On the base case the stock is worth about Rs 237 in three years against Rs 1,326 today, a 44% annual loss. The Hurdle Ratio says STOP, and three other independent tests (AVERAGE quality, CAUTION promoter, inverted risk-reward) say the same thing.

None of this makes it un-ownable forever. It makes it un-ownable at Rs 1,326. The price that would give a 25% return is Rs 97-121, roughly a tenth of today's. The catch, and it is the important one, is that the stock will most likely only reach that zone by breaking the very thing that would make it worth buying: the cash print turning structurally bad. So the discipline is exact — watch the cash and the ROCE every quarter, and only re-open the file if the price comes to the zone AND the cash has turned good, not because it turned bad.
