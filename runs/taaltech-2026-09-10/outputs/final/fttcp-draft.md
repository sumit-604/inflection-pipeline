## 3. THE CATALYST STORY IN PLAIN WORDS

**Revenue.** Something changed in the December 2025 quarter and got bigger in June. The best
supported explanation is mechanical: the company hired through FY26 and those people reached full
billing. Group employee cost rose 27% across FY26, from Rs25.1cr to Rs31.8cr a quarter, ahead of
revenue; then in Q1 FY27 revenue rose 13.6% on the quarter while employee cost rose 3.6%. That is
what a filled bench looks like. Management described exactly this practice at the August 2025
annual meeting: they add people when customers ask. Beyond that the evidence thins. Six new
clients were mentioned at that meeting. A company case study of 18-Aug-2026 describes a 28 person
team on a US data centre programme, with the client unnamed. The top customer went from 11.9% of
revenue in FY23 to 24.05% in FY26 and accounts for roughly 70% of the growth over that span, so
the honest reading is that one account has carried this company and may be carrying it still.
**What confirms it:** Q2 FY27 revenue above Rs60cr with employee cost flat to up. **What kills
it:** revenue back into the Rs44cr to Rs49cr band the company held from March 2024 to
September 2025.

**Margin.** There is no margin catalyst that belongs to the company. The lift is the rupee and
operating leverage on people already hired. The structure that produces a 28% parent margin is a
transfer pricing arrangement that is ordinary and, per the dossier, sits inside the arm's length
range at about 9.5% operating profit on total cost for the US arm. It is not a moat and it will
not expand. **What confirms sustainability:** Q2 FY27 margin above 30% at a rupee below 92.
**What kills it:** Bangalore wage inflation showing up as employee cost rising faster than
revenue for two quarters.

**Cash conversion. NONE FOUND.** This is the significant finding of the run. In 164 annual report
pages, three results filings and one corrigendum, there is no working capital programme, no
factoring facility, no debtor day target, no receivables discounting arrangement and no sentence
from management about the billing cycle. The company does not hold earnings calls, so there is no
other channel where it could have been said. The absence is not an oversight in one document; it
is the entire disclosure record. Under this protocol a transition going the wrong way with no
observable catalyst is the loudest signal available, and it caps the verdict. **What would
create a catalyst:** a disclosed milestone certification schedule, a factoring facility with an
amount and an operational date, or a stated debtor day target. **What confirms the decline:**
H1 FY27 CFO/PAT below 0.5x with unbilled revenue still above 20% of the quarter.

**ROCE.** The operating business needs almost no capital, so its return is structurally high and
the only real question is whether the company keeps burying that return under a growing
investment book. Between FY22 and FY26 the treasury book went from Rs5cr to Rs143.89cr while the
operating business stayed asset light. Every rupee added to the book lowers the headline return
without touching the operating one. **What confirms the operating read:** the next annual report
showing operating capital employed roughly flat while EBIT grows. **What breaks it:** the
Section 186 limit, raised to Rs350cr on 1-Sep-2026, being used, which would take the book toward
half the market capitalisation and make the headline number the honest one.

## 4. THE VERDICT

The composite is **+2 out of 8**, which places the name in the **DEEP WATCH leaning AVOID** band.
The **Kernex cap engages independently**, because cash conversion is DECLINING with no observable
catalyst, and that caps the verdict at DEEP WATCH on its own. The band is the harder of the two

The whole call turns on one print. **H1 FY27, due around November 2026**: does quarterly revenue
hold above Rs57cr to Rs60cr and above about $6.3m, while CFO/PAT recovers above 0.5x and unbilled
revenue falls back under 20% of the quarter. Two legs out of three is not proof. If revenue holds
and cash does not, this is a company converting its own growth into receivables it cannot collect
on a schedule it will not describe. If both hold, the cash verdict re-scores and the composite
moves.
