### 2B. Excuse pattern analysis

For every miss above, management's explanation, classified:
- #1 (FY26 growth 8%→6%): external-blame (weather, SBI RFP delay by the bank/process, AGS-linked offsite ATM contraction) — quantified at Rs150 Cr, but the FY26 capex over-investment "ahead of the SBI contract" was explicitly self-owned: "In hindsight, we invested ahead of this contract and our anticipation on things becoming normal was very aggressive... this has been a big learning for us" (Rajiv Kaul, Q3 FY26 call) and later, more bluntly, "that was with hindsight a terrible call to make, and we are paying for that" (Rajiv Kaul, Q4 FY26 call). This is a genuine self-admission, not deflection — a meaningfully positive tone signal, not the sector norm.
- #3 (ATM count miss): silence — no explanation, no acknowledgment. Classified silence.
- #5 (DSO miss): silence — no explanation, no acknowledgment, and no analyst follow-up either. Classified silence.
- #2 (FY27 revenue cut): external-blame, but unusually well-quantified and paired with an honest reframing ("we have trimmed the revenue number affected by an external supply issue, but we have raised the guidance on a metric which reflects the quality of the business") — this is closer to honest-admission-plus-external-attribution than pure deflection, because the offsetting margin claim is independently verifiable against the printed Q1 number.
- #6 (SBI scope halving): deflection by omission in Q2 FY26 (not proactively disclosed), followed by a direct, non-evasive answer once asked in Q3 FY26 ("Yes... rest going to other industry participants").
- #7 (margin miss vs FY25): external-blame (wage settlements, revenue flow-through) with some honest framing of the trade-off management chose ("we defended market and revenue and therefore, has had some impact on margins possibly" — Rajiv Kaul, Q3 FY26).
- #8 (retail recovery softer than promised): a mix — externally partly explained (GST 2.0 timing) but also self-owned as a deliberate choice ("we took an attempt to see where we can increase prices and where we think the prices just don't make any sense" — pruning was a management decision, not an external shock).

Pattern check: management does say "we made a mistake" on at least two occasions in specific, named terms (SBI capacity over-investment, called "terrible" by the CEO himself), which is unusual and a positive credibility marker. But two other misses (ATM count, DSO) were never surfaced, addressed, or admitted at all — a genuine and repeated silence pattern on inconvenient numbers that nobody, including analysts, followed up on. Hard topics are sometimes raised proactively (the Q4 FY26 warning about a "dip in currency supply" months before it hit, later realised almost exactly as flagged) and sometimes only surface under direct analyst pressure (SBI scope, cash-management segment decline). Overall pattern: **balanced**, tilted toward candour on big, headline numbers and silence on smaller operational metrics that would complicate the narrative.

## Analyst note (carried forward, not duplicated in narrative above)

The strongest single piece of evidence in this corpus is LBF-3 (guidance cut on revenue, raised on margin, in the same sentence, backed by an already-printed 27.2% quarter). It is also worth weighing against two much quieter facts the calls reveal: a DSO promise made once and never revisited, and an ATM-count target that vanished from every subsequent call without comment. Both point the same direction — management is highly disciplined and transparent about the numbers it chooses to defend, and comfortably silent on the ones that would complicate the story. That asymmetry, not any single quarter's miss, is the credibility read that should travel forward into stage 6 and stage 11.
