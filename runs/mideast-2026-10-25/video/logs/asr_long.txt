An oil price near $117, two blockades at sea, and a possible new strike on Iran within weeks. Kiti Sharma's research explains it all in plain words. It began with uranium. The same machines that make fuel for power plants can also make fuel for a bomb. Power plants need about 5%. Iran reached 60%. Bombs need 90. America and Israel do not trust Iran to stop. Iran says enriching uranium is its legal right. In 2015, Iran signed a deal that capped its enrichment. In 2018, America walked out of that deal. Iran then pushed enrichment higher. In June 2025 came a 12 -day war. On the 28th of February 2026, America and Israel struck Iran again, and its supreme leader was killed. Iran answered by blocking the Strait of Hormuz. The official reason is nuclear, but analysts point to more. Before the war, China bought almost all of Iran's oil. Hitting Iran also hits China's cheap supply. Control of Hormuz gives huge power over world energy. And Israel had wanted this war for years. Critics and supporters still argue over which reason mattered most. Today there are two blockades. Iran attacks and mines tankers in the Strait of Hormuz. America stops Iran's own oil ships. In September, Iran sold no oil at all from its ports. Oil prices have jumped to their highest level since April. The fight has also moved to Saudi Arabia. Yemen's Houthis, backed by Iran, are firing missiles at Saudi airports and oil sites. Their main target is a pipeline that let Saudi oil skip Hormuz. Iran cannot beat the American Navy, so it makes the Gulf pay. There is a hidden risk under the sea. Iran has threatened internet cables in Hormuz if it is attacked again. The bigger danger is the Red Sea, which carries over 90 % of the data between Europe and Asia. About 60 % of India's internet traffic goes that way. The world would not go dark, but internet could get slower and costlier. Inside Iran, the economy is breaking. One diller now costs about 27 lakh reals. Prices have almost doubled in a year. Petrol queues are long before winter, but the streets are quiet. Last winter's protests were crushed by force. The economy is breaking. The regime is not. America votes on the 3rd of November. If Trump's party loses Congress, he still stays president until 2029. Removing him would need 67 senators, which is very unlikely. But after the vote, the pressure of oil prices on his party eases. That opens a window for a new strike before the new Congress sits in January. So what happens next? Qitih Sharma's research sees a new strike within a year as likely. About 70%.
