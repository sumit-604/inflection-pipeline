### Q3. How do peers describe aluminium/copper input-price pass-through

| Field | Content |
|---|---|

| Verdict | VERIFIED |
| Peer evidence | DYCL: "we have two kinds of contracts with our customers, one is the fixed price contract and one is the variable price contract... whatever is the increase or decrease in input cost... we pass it on to our customers... in the fixed price contract, whenever we take an order, we typically try to book the raw material at the same time so that our margins are not impacted. ## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

**2A. Demand environment.** All three peers describe a healthy-to-strong
demand backdrop for T&D cable/conductor products across Oct-2025 through
Aug-2026. DIACABS is the most bullish, citing an order book that grew from
roughly INR1,900 crore to INR3,688 crore (DIACABS, Q1 FY27 call, p.4) and
INR400 crore of fresh orders "in the last six to seven days" alone (same
call, p.4), underwritten by a INR22,000 crore multi-state disaster-management
tender pipeline (p.14). DYCL reports occasional order-booking softness
(flattish order book across three quarters, Rs.721cr to Rs.787cr to Rs.808cr,
Oct-2025 through May-2026 calls) attributed to monsoon-driven EPC execution
delays and, later, customer reluctance to lock in orders during a sharp
aluminium/PVC price spike in March-April 2026 (DYCL, Q4 FY26 call p.5-6; Q1
FY27 call p.5) — management frames this as timing, not demand destruction, in
both cases. VIDYAWIRES reports steady growth (H1 FY26 revenue +5.1%, FY26
+24.2%) with power/transmission as its largest single segment (~51% of
revenue, VIDYAWIRES Q4/FY26 call, p.7) and no discussion of demand weakness.
Consensus: demand is real and broadly consistent with Susan's framing of a
healthy discom/EPC order environment, though none of the three peers
corroborate Susan's specific bid-to-award conversion assumption (see Q7).

**2B. Pricing and input costs.** Uniform across all three peers: complete or
near-complete pass-through of aluminium/copper price moves via named
contractual mechanisms (price-variation clauses, back-to-back LME booking
with hedged forex) — see Q3 above for quotes. No peer describes absorbing
metal-price volatility into margin on a structural basis; DIACABS explicitly
attributes its own Q1 FY27 gross-margin compression to a timing lag in
pass-through, not to an inability to pass costs through (DIACABS, Q1 FY27
call, p.3-4). This matches Susan's implied protection but exposes Susan's own
disclosure as less specific than the sector norm.

**2C. Capex cycle.** This is an industry-wide capacity race, not a lone
expander. DYCL, DIACABS and VIDYAWIRES are all expanding simultaneously
during the exact window Susan is expanding: DYCL's delayed greenfield plant
(now targeted September 2026), DIACABS' multi-line MV/EHV expansion funded by
a INR1,640 crore QIP (DIACABS, Q1 FY27 call, p.4), and VIDYAWIRES' near-doubling
of capacity via its ALCU subsidiary (19,680 to 37,680 tonnes/year). DYCL
states this explicitly: "everybody in the industry, including us" is
continuing capex to keep pace with growth (DYCL, Q4 FY26 call, p.6). The
capacity-race framing supports Susan's own expansion logic directionally, but
DYCL's own ~12-15 month guided slippage (see Q5) is a live cautionary
data point on execution risk that applies industry-wide, not just to Susan.

**2D. Competitive mentions.** No peer, across any of the seven transcripts,
mentions Susan Electricals India Ltd by name or by an unmistakable
description.

**2E. Risks peers discuss that Susan's call does not.** (1) Raw-material
price spikes causing customer order deferment/booking slowdowns — DYCL names
this explicitly for two consecutive quarters (DYCL, Q4 FY26 call p.5-6; Q1
FY27 call p.5-6); Susan's B05 transcript never raises order-deferment risk
tied to input-price spikes. (2) Deliberate exit from competitively-squeezed,
low-margin sub-segments as an active strategy (DYCL discontinuing railway
signalling and low-voltage conductors — "we have temporarily held back that
segment," DYCL Q1 FY27 call p.10) — Susan's call frames its winding-wire
deprioritisation as a mix-shift ambition, not as a response to margin
compression the way DYCL frames its exits. (3) Single-customer/group order-book
concentration named as an explicit governance risk with a board mandate to
reduce it — DIACABS: "the present order book from Adani is around 40% plus.
Our Board mandate is to bring it down to 20%" (DIACABS, Q1 FY27 call, p.16) —
Susan's own repeat-customer concentration collapse (89.47% to 45.09%, per B04)
is a related but differently-shaped risk that its own call does not address
head-on. (4) Weather/monsoon seasonality as a structural, named risk to a
specific quarter's numbers (DIACABS: "this year India's monsoon arrived early
and arrived hard... a large number of our customer's installation sites were
flooded," DIACABS Q1 FY27 call, p.3) — Susan's single transcript does not
discuss weather risk to execution at all. (5) Analyst-driven scrutiny of OCF
conversion against EBITDA, met with a specific numeric improvement plan
(VIDYAWIRES: receivable days targeted down from 41 to 35, inventory to <20
days, VIDYAWIRES Q4/FY26 call p.17) — Susan's two-year negative CFO history
was never raised on its own call (per B05 red flags), a contrast this peer
set makes more visible, not less.

## PART 4: TRIANGULATION SUMMARY

- Claims verified: 2 of 8 (Q3 pass-through mechanism; Q5 capex lead-time/delay
  pattern, direction confirmed with an added magnitude caution for Susan)
- Claims partially verified: 2 of 8 (Q1 LT margin — direction yes, magnitude
  Susan slightly low; Q2 HT/MVCC margin — direction yes, magnitude closer to
  EHV than true MV)
- Claims contradicted: 1 of 8 (Q6 winding-wire margin — priority item for
  synthesis)
- Claims unverifiable: 3 of 8 (Q4 discom DSO; Q7 conversion rate; Q8 trading
  line — the last two carry informative context or silence even without a
  clean verdict)

**Single most consequential contradiction:** Susan's disclosed ~24.39%
winding-wire EBITDA margin sits 5-6x above the only directly comparable
listed peer, VIDYAWIRES, which reports 4.3% (H1 FY26) and 4.66% (FY26 full
year) across two separate quarters. Even allowing for VIDYAWIRES' broader,
more commoditised product mix, a gap this large cannot be explained by scale
or mix alone and should be treated as a high-severity, unresolved question
mark over Susan's highest-margin disclosed segment.

**Single strongest independent confirmation:** All three peers, unprompted
and independently, describe a specific, named raw-material pass-through or
hedging mechanism (price-variation clauses or back-to-back LME booking),
confirming this is standard sector practice. This makes Susan's own vague
"monitored closely" answer look like a disclosure gap relative to the norm,
even if the underlying protection may exist.

**Overall:** The peer set complicates Susan's narrative more than it
supports it. It corroborates the broad shape of Susan's story — higher
voltage bands carry higher margins, raw-material pass-through is standard,
RDSS-adjacent demand is real and large — but it directly contradicts the
single most load-bearing margin number in the LBF1 set (winding wire), it
casts doubt on the magnitude (not the direction) of both the LT and HT/MVCC
margin claims, and it supplies concrete peer-sourced evidence (DYCL's own
~12-15 month capex slippage) that Susan's Feb-2027 commissioning target,
against only 11.7% capex utilisation as of Q1 FY27, is running an aggressive
rather than conservative timeline. No peer mentions Susan by name in either
direction.

## PART 5: CROSS-PEER HYPOTHESIS

All three peers, at very different scales, describe the identical structural
move: full commodity pass-through plus a deliberate rotation away from
commoditised low-voltage/legacy SKUs toward medium/high-voltage or
higher-value-add categories, each funded by a capex cycle running on similar
12-18 month lead times with real slippage risk (DYCL's own guidance moved by
roughly a year). None of the three frames its coming margin expansion as a
volume or scale story; each frames it purely as a mix-shift story executed on
a near-identical playbook and timeline.

Hypothesis: the "margin expansion via mix-shift to higher voltage/value
product" narrative that Susan is telling investors may not be company-specific
alpha. It may be a sector-wide, near-simultaneous rotation that at least three
other listed peers are executing on the same 12-18 month capex clock, for the
same structural reason (commoditised low-voltage segments are being vacated
industry-wide). If that is right, the market may already be pricing this
mix-shift into small-cap cable-sector valuations broadly, which would narrow
Susan's specific recognition gap faster than a single-company reading would
suggest, unless Susan's execution pace or magnitude of shift genuinely
outpaces this peer set. This is testable: track whether DYCL's, DIACABS' and
VIDYAWIRES' trading multiples move in step with their respective MV/HV
revenue-mix percentages over the next few quarters, and whether Susan's own
multiple is already reflecting a comparable re-rating or still sits at a
discount to peers with equivalent mix-shift claims.

## Analyst note

The most load-bearing new evidence this stage produced is the VIDYAWIRES
winding-wire margin contradiction (Q6): two quarters, one peer, a 5-6x gap
against Susan's disclosed 24.39% figure. This should be flagged for
resolution before any Section 1B margin-bridge or destination-mix input keys
off Susan's winding-wire number as a reliable, sector-typical figure. The
capex lead-time evidence (Q5, from both DYCL and DIACABS) independently
reinforces the B05 kill-signal on the Feb-2027 capacity trigger; downstream
stages should treat that trigger's conviction as no higher than "M" absent
new evidence. The HT/MVCC margin question (Q2) is genuinely mixed: DIACABS'
own numbers put Susan's ~20% claim closer to an extra-high-voltage band than
a true medium-voltage band, which is a magnitude concern worth carrying
forward rather than a clean confirmation or a clean contradiction.
