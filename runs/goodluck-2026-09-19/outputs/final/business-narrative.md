## BUSINESS UNDERSTANDING NARRATIVE

Goodluck India buys steel and turns it into five product families. Cold rolled coils, pipes and hollow sections made up 34% of FY26 sales; they are priced on a formula that follows the hot rolled coil price, so buyers pay for tonnage and not for a name. Precision pipes and auto tubes made up 27%; they are drawn to carmaker tolerances, and a buyer who switches supplier must qualify the new part again under the IATF 16949 automotive standard. Engineering structures made up 24%; these are bridges, girders, and solar and transmission structures built against project orders. Forgings made up 15%; these are flanges, shafts and gear rings of up to 14 tonnes in one piece for oil and gas, industrial and defence users. Since October 2025 a subsidiary, Goodluck Defence and Aerospace, has made 105mm to 155mm artillery shell bodies under an Arms Act licence and a DGQA quality certificate; it was 1% of FY26 revenue and about 6% in the June 2026 quarter.

Volkswagen, Audi, BMW, Mercedes-Benz, TVS, Bajaj, Toyota and Tata Motors buy the tubes, while Indian Railways, L&T and NTPC buy the structures and Reliance, L&T Defence, HAL and Indian Oil buy the forgings. The Government of India buys the shells, and the annual report finds no significant credit concentration across 600 customers in more than 100 countries.

Present demand tracks six outside signals: the India National Infrastructure Pipeline and Union Budget capex, MNRE/CEA solar capacity addition data, global automotive OEM production volumes, Indian Railways and NHSRCL high speed rail milestones, oil and gas E&P and refinery capex, and the Union Budget defence capital acquisition allocation. Growth rests on two documented facts: India reserves 75% of its FY27 defence capital acquisition budget for domestic suppliers, and India crossed 500 GW of renewable capacity in FY26. NATO/EU rearmament procurement could open a shell export channel, but no shell export order is disclosed, and the CMD's claim that India needs 4.8 million shells a year runs about 24 times the run's own market estimate.

Only the defence line has a documented moat: the licence and the DGQA certificate, which CRISIL says face "negligible competition", counted once after a verifier removed a double credit. Tubes and forgings hold a moderate qualification barrier, and the export line holds a moderate position on filed FY26 export growth of about 10%. The cold rolled base has no moat, since it passes the coil price through and earns 3% to 5%, and the structures business shows none in the evidence. Execution and working capital scored negative, and talent, categories 21 and 22, scored zero. The licence does not stop rival supply either: Balu Forge has commissioned a 360,000 shell line, more than twice Goodluck's 150,000.

Steel converter with a shell licence attached

Goodluck is a steel processor with nearly four decades of history and 500,000 tonnes of capacity across seven plants in Uttar Pradesh and Gujarat. It sells commodity steel products, specified tubes, forgings and fabricated structures. It now also makes artillery shells through a subsidiary it owned 79% of at the FY26 annual report, and about 69% after the August 2026 raise. FY26 revenue was Rs 4,100 Cr, up 4%. The standalone plants ran at 98% of capacity in the June 2026 quarter, so volume growth in the core now needs new capacity.

The transition is a mix shift away from the 3% to 5% margin commodity base toward tubes, forgings and shells. The value added share rose from 64% to 66% of sales in FY26. EBITDA grew 26% on 4% more income, and consolidated EBITDA margin rose to 10% from 8%. Gross margin jumped from about 27% to 33%, and management calls that sustainable without giving a reason. The defence plant delivered Rs 46 Cr in FY26 against a Rs 100 Cr guide. It then delivered about Rs 81 Cr at a 36% to 38% margin in the June 2026 quarter. In that same quarter the standalone margin fell 57 basis points to 9.2%, although the Chairman had said the input cost shock would have no margin impact. The climb shows up in the subsidiary but not yet in the core plants.

The numbers split two ways. Growth is real: over ten years profit compounded at 28% a year against 16% for revenue, and it was positive every year. Cash is the weak side. Free cash flow was negative in each of the last three years, Rs 722 Cr out in total against Rs 479 Cr of reported profit. Borrowings rose from Rs 612 Cr to Rs 1,119 Cr, and the company closed FY26 with under Rs 1 Cr of consolidated cash. FY26 operating cash flow was Rs 200 Cr against Rs 418 Cr of EBITDA. Inventory rose 29% on 3% more revenue, and India Ratings puts the working capital cycle at 111 days, up from 86 days two years earlier. Net debt sits at 2.7 times EBITDA and interest cover at 3.3 times, both close to the 3 times line the framework treats as an avoid trigger. The Gate 0 score is 46 of 100, not the 48 first stated, after a verifier recomputed the ten year median ROE at 11.8%. The class stays AVERAGE. ROE has fallen four years in a row. On the other side, CRISIL upgraded the company to AA- in June 2026, India Ratings assigned AA- in July, and the auditor opinions carry no qualification.

Management's word has not held. Over four calls the credibility grade is D. The FY26 growth guide of 15% to 20% was reaffirmed twice and delivered at 4%. The 400,000 shell expansion, first promised within a year, now targets commercial output in Q4 FY28. That is a slip of 10 to 17 months, not the two years first reported. Hydraulic tube use was due to reach 70% by March 2026; it was 50% then and 60% in June. The one promise first scored as delivered, the 30% to 35% defence margin, rests on one clean quarter, and the verifier reclassified it as too early to call. In four calls management never gave an order book number, while the claimed horizon moved from no order book to eight months, one year and two years. It also refused per shell pricing in all four calls. On the other side, the subsidiary dilution arithmetic ties across quarters, and the June quarter defence margin roughly matches consolidated minus standalone.

Governance is the second weak side. The FY26 annual report calls the defence subsidiary "wholly-owned" twice, while its own AOC-1 form shows 79.43%. The outside investors behind the Rs 137 Cr raise in December 2023 and the Rs 285 Cr raise in August 2026 are never named. On management's own FY27 guide, the August raise priced the subsidiary at about 15 to 20 times forward EBITDA, not the 9 times first reported. A promoter family company, Goodluck Green Energy, appears in no annual report, yet the board approved its merger into the listed company in July 2026 with no terms set. A company run by promoter group relatives routes Rs 11 Cr to Rs 33 Cr a year of advances through the defence subsidiary. The promoter group sold 2.45% on 29 June 2026, five weeks before the subsidiary raise. The COO and his HUF sold about 45% of those shares, and the Chairman's family did not sell. Only the August call came after the sale, and nobody raised it there. Against this, the promoter directors cut their pay by 30% to 54% in a year of profit growth and put in Rs 22.5 Cr through warrants. Pledge is nil, an outside group CFO joined in September 2026, and five of nine directors are independent. The promoter read is CONCERN.

The peers confirm the input story and contradict the revenue excuse. Hi-Tech Pipes and Ratnamani both confirm the coil price path, from a five year low near Rs 47,000 a tonne in October 2025 to Rs 58,000 by May 2026. Ratnamani raised the West Asia gas cost shock without being asked. But Hi-Tech grew its March 2026 quarter volume 27% while Goodluck's standalone revenue for that quarter fell 3.9%, so the FY26 miss was not an industry condition. A verifier corrected the peer stage on this reading. Balu Forge's 360,000 shell line is competing supply, and it weakens the scarcity pricing claim behind the defence margin. Exports are 27% to 29% of revenue; the 53% figure from the August call is export growth, not export share. No call has mentioned the EU carbon border tax, which Hi-Tech treats as a live risk.

The market is large, but the realistic capture is slower than the guide. The run sizes the total market at Rs 1.06 lakh Cr to Rs 1.74 lakh Cr and the serviceable part at Rs 44,120 Cr. Goodluck holds about 9% of the serviceable market, which leaves 11 times headroom, and the runway class is STRONG. The obtainable share implies only 9% revenue growth a year over three to five years, against the 15% to 20% guide. The defence sizing chain rests on an assumed 30% to 40% share for 155mm shells in the Army ammunition budget, so it carries low to medium confidence. The CMD claims a 30% share in solar structures, but the filed numbers imply 3% to 5%. The run found no reliable market size for precision tubes, so the total understates that line. The emerging moat score is 14 to 15 after verifier corrections, down from 19, and stays in the MODEST band.
