## 3. VALUE TRAP TEST

**3A. What makes it LOOK cheap / safe.** Headline PE on filed FY26 EPS Rs 5.75 looks like ~26x but the filed EPS is on a pre-IPO weighted share count that understates the true multiple (B10). "Net cash Rs 31/share," "asset-light," "Rs 900 Cr order book 2.19x revenue," and "operational ROCE 16.2%" all read as quality markers. On the operator-approved 13x forward, base fair value Rs 150.2 sits right at CMP, so it superficially looks fairly-valued rather than expensive.

**3B. What is actually hiding — rebuild on OPERATING earnings only (mandatory stress test).**

FY26 reported (B10): PBT before exceptional ≈ Rs 48.26 Cr (other income Rs 20.56 Cr = 42.6% of PBT ⟹ PBT = 20.56 ÷ 0.426). Effective tax = (48.26 − 38.36) ÷ 48.26 = 20.5%.
- **Operating PBT FY26 = 48.26 − 20.56 = Rs 27.70 Cr.** Operating PAT ≈ 27.70 × 0.795 = **Rs 22.02 Cr**; operating EPS (4.445 Cr diluted) = **Rs 4.95** (vs reported Rs 5.75).

Forward FY27 base (B10/B11): PAT Rs 51.3 Cr, EPS Rs 11.55. Strip other income held roughly steady at ~Rs 20.56 Cr pre-tax (justified: only 22.2% of proceeds deployed, so most IPO-deposit interest persists), = Rs 16.35 Cr post-tax.
- **Operating PAT FY27 ≈ 51.3 − 16.35 = Rs 34.95 Cr; operating EPS ≈ Rs 7.86.**
- **13x × operating EPS 7.86 = Rs 102.2** (bull operating EPS Rs 8.42 → **Rs 109.5**).

**What this does to the Rs 150.2 base fair value and the Hurdle.** The reported-basis Rs 150.2 capitalises Rs 20.56 Cr of forex + parked-cash interest at 13x. That is worth ~Rs 48/share of the fair value — about one third of it. A clean sum-of-parts is more honest: operating business Rs 102 + net cash. But the distributable net cash is not Rs 31.25/share; after stripping ~Rs 110 Cr customer advances and ~Rs 16 Cr guarantee encumbrance it is ~Rs 3/share. **Sum-of-parts fair value ≈ Rs 102 + Rs 3 = ~Rs 105** (and no more than ~Rs 133 even crediting the full Rs 31.25 net cash at par). Either way, **base fair value Rs 150.2 is generous by Rs 17-45 because it capitalises interest income at 13x (Rs 48/sh) instead of valuing at par the cash that throws it off (Rs 3-31/sh).** The valuation's own "no separate cash add" caveat (B11 Section 1A) avoided ONE double-count but left this one standing.

Corollary — **the entry zone is also too high.** On an honest base fair value of ~Rs 105-133, the 25% entry (÷1.953) is **Rs 54-68**, not Rs 62.9-80.6. The stated Rs 80.6 ceiling is too generous; the honest entry ceiling sits around Rs 68, and the RRM-track floor of Rs 50.3 (B11) looks like the right neighbourhood.

**3C. Melting ice cube check.** Not a melting ice cube on revenue — the ~28% FY23-26 revenue CAGR is genuine (B03 strengths). It is a melting ice cube on CASH and EARNINGS QUALITY: CFO/PAT collapsed to 1.25% FY26 (cumulative 8.1%), FCF negative Rs 4.23 Cr, and profit is increasingly propped by interest on a cash pile the company cannot deploy. Growth that does not convert to cash, funded by IPO money earning interest that is then booked as profit, is the classic transition-alpha false positive. **Verdict: value trap confirmed on cash and earnings quality; the AVOID is correct and its fair value is soft.**

## 4. VARIANT PERCEPTION CHECK

Role 2 holds no ownership variant — the stance is AVOID. The variant worth testing is the OPERATOR OVERRIDE embedded in the valuation: that 13x forward on FY27 EPS is the right destination. **Argue the market is right and the override is too kind.** The framework's own derived tracks say additive mid 12.1x and RRM 8.5x, a 30% divergence, with the rule that the MORE CONSERVATIVE track sets the entry zone (B11 Section 1B). The market pricing FABTECH at ~Rs 152 is not "seeing value the AVOID misses"; the market is paying up for the order-book narrative and the cash line, and it is the AVOID's Rs 150 fair value that is too close to that market price. **The market is not right that the stock is worth Rs 152; but the market IS a useful reminder that 13x is generous — the conservative framework read is Rs 98.**

## 5. CATALYST DEPENDENCY — SHARED CATALYST single point of failure (mandatory)

The implicit catalyst is IPO-proceeds deployment. It carries BOTH legs of the valuation:
- **Pillar 1:** operational ROCE 16.2% (base 15.6x) exists only after stripping Rs 179.22 Cr idle cash and assumes it redeploys at operating returns.
- **Pillar 3a:** the +2x growth premium leans on the capex-embedded / order-book growth that the same proceeds are meant to fund.

**Stress test — three failure branches, all adverse:**
1. **Deployment stalls** (base-rate outcome given 22.2% deployed / 0% inorganic a year in): statutory ROCE stays ~11.25%, Pillar 1 base falls to 0.5×11.25+7.5 = ~13.1x, AND Pillar 3a weakens. Double hit from one event. Fair value compresses toward the RRM Rs 98 track.
2. **Deployment into low-return M&A** (JBN Europe, repeatedly slipped since Q3 FY26; B05): blended ROCE can fall BELOW the 16.2% operating base, so even a "firing" catalyst destroys value, while Pillar 3a is not earned because the acquired growth is not organic/visible.
3. **The interest-income paradox.** Deploying the cash is exactly what removes the Rs 20.56 Cr other income (42.6% of PBT) that props up forward EPS. So the branch that fixes ROCE (deploy) simultaneously guts the EPS that the 13x is applied to (Section 3B). The base fair value Rs 150.2 quietly assumes cash is deployed enough to justify 16.2% ROCE yet still parked enough to throw off Rs 20.56 Cr of interest. That is internally inconsistent and cannot both be true.

**What happens to the price while you wait:** if deployment is delayed 2-3 years, the stock has no re-rating headroom (current forward PE 13.16x ≈ destination 13x; B11), so it can only track mid-teens EPS growth AT BEST, i.e. dead money versus a 25% hurdle, with the downside branches above open the whole time. **The SHARED CATALYST is not a call option; it is a straddle where both legs are short.**

This is genuine adverse asymmetry, not the benign mandate-exclusion kind. Per the framework, low FII+DII (2.47%) is NOT counted here — mandate exclusion, not informed avoidance. The asymmetry that matters is on receivables, revenue recognition, and litigation, and it points one way.

## 7. PRE-MORTEM (it is 2028; a buyer at Rs 152 is down 40%, at ~Rs 91)

The receivables were the tell all along. In FY27 the Rs 58.9 Cr of >365-day overdue balances kept climbing while management stayed silent on the KAM, and by the FY27 annual report the Rs 37.18 Cr reconciliation gap had not closed — it had grown. When the auditor finally forced expected-credit-loss provisioning against the concentrated Saudi book (Rs 72.57 Cr on a single Rs 120 Cr contract), a chunk of prior "profit" reversed. Reported PAT, already only Rs 22 Cr of genuine operating income against Rs 38 Cr headline, was exposed as thinner still, and the market re-based the stock from 13x on a padded number to a low-teens multiple on operating earnings of ~Rs 5-8 a share — roughly Rs 100 — before the provisions pushed it lower.

The IPO cash never became a return engine. Two and a half years after listing the inorganic tranche was still barely touched, or worse, was spent late and expensively on the long-promised European platform that did not integrate. Operational ROCE never converged on the 16.2% the valuation assumed; it drifted toward the 11.25% statutory reality. And the interest income that had been quietly carrying 42.6% of pre-tax profit shrank as the deposits were finally drawn down, so the very act of "putting the money to work" cut reported earnings instead of lifting them.

Governance was the accelerant. The stayed criminal complaint against the lead promoter moved, or a fresh related-party disclosure landed — the sister listed company with no non-compete, the reciprocal cross-guarantees, the AOC-2 contradiction the board never reconciled. None of it was new information; all of it was in the FY26 annual report and the concall silences. The 40% drawdown was not a bolt from the blue. It was the market slowly agreeing with the Gate 0 AVOID, the promoter CONCERN, and the structural cash flag that were all present, and all ignored, on the day of purchase at Rs 152.
