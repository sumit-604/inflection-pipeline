### 2E. Civil litigation — the central legal fact of this promoter profile

The unresolved Priyamvada Devi Birla estate/probate dispute is material and long-running:
- Origin: Priyamvada Devi Birla died in 2004; a 1994 will left her ~₹5,000-25,000 crore (sources vary, 📰 MEDIA REPORTED) estate to advisor/CA R.S. Lodha, contested by the Birla family since. R.S. Lodha (Harsh V. Lodha's father) ran the group as Administrator until his 2008 death, after which Harsh V. Lodha assumed chairmanship — succession within a still-disputed estate, not a clean-title inheritance.
- 18 September 2020: Calcutta HC single judge ordered Lodha's removal from all MP Birla Group company boards (📰 MEDIA REPORTED, Business Standard).
- Two of the joint Administrators Pendente Lite (APL) subsequently filed DIR-12 forms with MCA purporting to record his cessation as director of five investment companies (Punjab Produce Holdings, Baroda Agents & Trading, East India Investment, Gwalior Webbing, Punjab Produce & Trading) — the company discloses this was done "without his knowledge, consent and without compliance with the provisions of law," and legally challenged by him (AR p.46, VERIFIED, company's own disclosure).
- 14 December 2023: Calcutta HC Division Bench (CJ T.S. Sivagnanam and Justice Sabyasachi Bhattacharyya) ruled a probate/testamentary court cannot interfere in company management, effectively restoring Lodha's position and nullifying the two APLs' actions (AR p.46, VERIFIED company disclosure; corroborated 📰 MEDIA REPORTED, LiveLaw, Bar & Bench, India Legal Live).
- 22 March 2024: Supreme Court declined interim relief in SLPs filed against the December 2023 order (AR p.46, VERIFIED).
- The dispute remains unresolved: web search (📰 MEDIA REPORTED, LawChakra) indicates the Supreme Court has since admitted appeals against the ruling allowing Lodha to continue as chairman, and Calcutta HC testamentary proceedings (T.S. No. 6 of 2004) were still active as of March 2026 (indiankanoon.org case record, dated 12 March 2026). No final resolution exists as of the run date.

Net effect: Harsh V. Lodha's chairmanship rests on litigation outcomes that remain open after 22 years. This is not a fresh allegation but it is a live, unresolved title/control dispute over the estate that ultimately backs the promoter shareholding structure.

No family-partition suit is found to directly attach BCL's own assets; no JV disputes or defamation suits found.
