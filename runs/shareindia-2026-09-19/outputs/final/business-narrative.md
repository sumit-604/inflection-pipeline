# SHAREINDIA: business narrative (Phase 1 lite, run 2026-09-19)

## BUSINESS UNDERSTANDING NARRATIVE

Share India is a Noida capital markets group, and its largest income line is trading its own money in equity, derivatives and commodity markets. That proprietary book produced 58% of FY26 income as fair value gains. A further 9% came from gross sales of securities held as stock in trade, which is the same trading economics reported gross. Neither line has a customer behind it; the buyer is the market on the other side of each trade. Interest income, 18% of FY26 income, comes from margin trading loans against clients' shares and from the Share India Fincap NBFC, which lends to retail and small business borrowers. Fees and commissions, 11% of income, pay for trade execution, depository accounts, mutual fund and insurance distribution, merchant banking and portfolio management. The customers are retail, HNI and institutional traders, margin and NBFC borrowers, and companies that raise capital, and institutional clients rose from 154 to 212 over three quarters. No filing shows switching costs or network effects, so a trader can move to another broker at will. Present demand follows exchange activity, which the NSE and BSE monthly cash and F&O average daily turnover and options premium turnover series track, together with the SEBI active derivatives trader count. SEBI F&O circulars and the RBI intraday funding and bank guarantee circular set how much of that activity, and how much prop desk capital, the rules allow. Demand for the client lines should grow with NSDL and CDSL demat account additions, AMFI mutual fund AUM and SIP flows, and SEBI DRHP and SME platform IPO listing counts, all public series. The company's own growth plan rests on margin lending, 25 to 30 new Tier 3 branches over two years, PMS and AIF fees and institutional broking, and peer disclosed MTF book sizes and interest rates are the outside check on the lending bet, while Infomerics rating cooperation status is the check on the debt that funds it. The prop trading line has no moat, because it takes the price the market sets. Broking has weak pricing power, and fee income fell 13% standalone and 19% consolidated in FY26. Margin lending and the NBFC price on credit risk, not brand, and the NBFC's revenue and branch count both fell in FY26. The SEBI and RBI licences are a real entry barrier, but every licensed peer shares them. The emerging moat scan found two moderate signals, rising institutional client counts and uTrade algo platform subscriptions at 6,543 paid users, but neither has a disclosed rupee revenue figure, and the scan scored about 10, below the lowest band of 12, so the run found no meaningful emerging moat.

## Prop trading house, client story unproven

Share India made ₹1,470 Cr of revenue and ₹324 Cr of profit in FY26. 58% of income was gains on its own trading book. Its sustainability report puts 73% of turnover in trading securities and 15% in broking. One reported segment, broking and trading, carries 93% of revenue and 93% of segment profit, so the accounts cannot split prop profit from client profit. Revenue is back to its FY24 level: ₹1,483 Cr then, ₹1,470 Cr now. Three year profit growth is zero.

Management says the business is moving from prop to client and fee income. It set a target of 55% client and 45% prop profit by the end of FY27, and 70 to 30 within three years. Prop was about 52% of profit in the Q4 FY26 call and about 52% again in Q1 FY27. Fee and commission income fell 13% standalone and 19% consolidated in FY26, the same year the annual report claimed less reliance on prop trading. The parent's share of group profit rose from 75% to 92%, and Algoplus profit fell 65%. The new lines are still small: an MTF book of ₹470 Cr in Q1 FY27 against a ₹650 Cr FY27 target, PMS money of ₹150 Cr that now includes advisory, and 6,543 paid uTrade users. Institutional clients are the one clean rising series, 154 to 212 in three quarters.

The backward score is 69 of 160, which is AVERAGE, with a core score of 46 of 100. Two deal breakers set that cap, cash and pledge, and both are current. Operating cash flow was an outflow of ₹170 Cr in FY23, ₹310 Cr in FY24 and ₹182 Cr in FY26. Over eleven years operating cash came to 0.085 times profit. In FY26 the standalone MTF book grew 70%, and new NCDs and bank loans paid for it. Bank guarantees, mostly margin to the NSE and MCX clearing houses, rose 48% to ₹3,233 Cr, or 123% of net worth, backed by ₹1,527 Cr of pledged deposits. Q1 FY27 profit of ₹124 Cr was a record, up 114% on the quarter, but management conceded that valuation gains helped and never sized them. On the other side, debt is 0.25 times equity, both audit opinions are clean, and 99% of trade receivables are under six months old.

Management earns a C for credibility. Of ten tracked promises, five were delivered, three partly and two missed. A second read of the transcripts is harsher: the MTF target was restated every quarter while the book grew 3% in six months, and all five dated milestones set in Q3 FY26 slipped. The uTrade revenue question went unanswered in two calls. The prop share of revenue moved from about 70% to 58 to 60% between two calls 68 days apart, with no explanation. No call mentions the pledge, the negative cash flow, the guarantees or the NCD early redemption.

The promoter verdict is CONCERN. Pledged shares were 52% of promoter holding in September 2025, 43% in March 2026 and 58% in June 2026, and nothing in the record explains the jump. The company borrowed ₹50 Cr in FY26 against shares of promoters and relatives. A SEBI show cause notice alleging ₹1.91 Cr of abnormal profit from NSE co-location access is still open. MCX fined the company ₹1.09 Cr for a position limit breach after adding two related entities' positions to its prop book, and the company is appealing. The annual report says there were no significant orders. Infomerics has rated the company under Issuer Not Cooperating since November 2024, through four downgrades from A1+ to A4. Against this, the board added two independent directors with outside listed board seats and appointed a dedicated insider trading compliance officer.

Peers confirm every checkable industry fact: the commodity led turnover recovery in Q3 FY26, the RBI intraday funding curb, and MTF pricing pressure. They weaken the edge claims. SMC Global grew its MTF book from ₹760 Cr to more than ₹900 Cr in one quarter. SMC has also launched an AI based algo platform, against Share India's claim that nobody offers this product. Choice International runs 210 to 220 branches and targets 800; Share India plans 30. SMC and Choice report NBFC bad loan ratios every quarter, and Share India gives no such figure for its margin book.

The client pool Share India serves earns it ₹442 Cr, 30% of FY26 revenue. The serviceable market is ₹3,650 Cr, and Share India holds 12% of it inside a conservative total of ₹21,600 Cr drawn from six named brokers. If the branch, MTF and AIF plan works, client revenue reaches ₹749 Cr in three years and ₹1,101 Cr in five, about 19 to 20% a year, and the runway class is STRONG. That math assumes share gains in the same year fee income lost share. The prop pool has no rupee size, because no source gives a margin capture rate. Funding is the binding limit: growth runs on debt, and the rating agency cooperation has lapsed.
