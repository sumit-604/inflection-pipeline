TITLE: India Ratings Affirms Premier Explosives's Bank Loan Facilities at ‘IND A-’/Stable; Rates Additional Limits
DATE: May 20, 2026
ISSUER: Premier Explosives Limited

== OVERVIEW ==
India Ratings and Research (Ind-Ra) has taken the following rating actions on Premier Explosives Limited’s (PEL) bank loan  facilities:

 Bank loan facilities | - | - | - | INR700 | IND A-/Stable/IND A2+ | Assigned | 
Bank loan facilities | - | - | - | INR3,270 | IND A-/Stable/IND A2+ | Affirmed |

== ANALYTICAL APPROACH ==
While arriving at the ratings, Ind-Ra continues to fully consolidate the financials of PEL and its subsidiaries, Premier Wires Products Limited (PEL holds 80% stake) and PELNEXT Defence Systems Private Limited (100% subsidiary of PEL).

== DETAILED RATIONALE ==
The rating reflects Ind-Ra’s expectation of a stable revenue trajectory and improved EBITDA margins over FY27–FY28, while sustaining credit metrics and liquidity at levels comparable to 9MFY26. Furthermore, PEL has two strategically located sites spread across approximately 200 acres each, offering substantial capacity for expansion and operational efficiency.

The company holds various licenses, including from Petroleum and Explosives Safety Organisation (PESO), and it has strong technical expertise along with substantial vintage, creating significant entry barriers and ensuring minimal competition. Its proximity to key defence and industrial entities such as the Defence Research and Development Organisation (DRDO), Bharat Dynamics Ltd (BDL), and Singareni Collieries Company Ltd (SCCL; rated at IND AA+/Stable) enhances its strategic positioning.

The ratings also factor in the extensive experience of the promoters in the explosives and defence manufacturing sector, ensuring strong regulatory compliance and sectoral expertise.

== KEY RATING DRIVERS ==
Strategic Position In Explosives Manufacturing: PEL operates in India's industrial and defence sector, specialising in high-energy materials such as explosives and pyrotechnics for aerospace and missile systems. It benefits from being an approved supplier for key defence programs and has a joint venture with Global Munition Ltd, enhancing its capabilities. The company operates in a niche segment that is characterised by high entry barriers due to regulatory norms and the need for technical expertise. The presence of experienced promoters with industry knowledge strengthens its operations. Furthermore, government initiatives in domestic defence would continue to support PEL’s growth. The company has substantial land at two plants and holds critical licenses, positioning it well for future expansion.

Improving Scale Of Operations, Supported By Unexecuted Orderbook, Provides Medium-Term Revenue Visibility: Ind-Ra expects PEL’s revenue to improve by at least 10%-15% yoy in FY27, backed by a robust order book of nearly INR12.71 billion at end-4QFY26. The anticipated revenue growth is subject to the timely receipt and commencement of the defence segment’s orders. The country’s rising defence budget, with 75% of the defence capital outlay  being allocated for domestic players, , augurs well for the order inflows during the medium term. 

In FY26, Ind-Ra expects the revenue to have remained stable, ranging between INR4,000 million - 4,200 million (FY25: INR4,175 million; FY24: INR2,717 million), due to slower approval receipts in defence contracts during 9MFY26. Subsequently, the revenue booking has been shifted to FY27.  The defence segment contributed 83% to the revenue in 9MFY26  (FY25: 81%; FY24: 68%), while exports accounted for 17% (FY25: 19%; FY24: 26%). In 9MFY26, PEL reported revenue of INR2,992 million and it had an unexecuted order book of INR12,711 million at March 2026, equivalent to 3.04x of FY25 sales, offering medium-term visibility. The order book remains defence-heavy (91%), with exports contributing 4%. In April 2026, the company received a new export order of INR3,502 million, which is scheduled to be executed over a period of two years,  leading to an increase in the export contribution. 

The EBITDA margins are likely to remain stable in FY27, ranging between 13%-15%, due to healthy orders in hand and a likely improvement in export orders. The EBITDA margin stood at 13% in 9MFY26 (FY25: 13.9%; FY24: 21.5%); in FY24, the margins had been boosted by a higher share of export revenue, which typically carries better profitability. The margins are likely to have remained rangebound between 13%-15% in FY26, largely due to the liquidated damages factored into one of the orders. 

Reputed Clientele Across Defence, Space, And Industrial Sectors: PEL has long-standing relationships with marquee clients in India’s defence and space sectors such as Bharat Dynamics Limited (BDL), Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO), Advanced Systems Laboratory (ASL), Defence Research and Development Organisation (DRDO) and Bharat Electronics Limited (BEL). These partnerships span several years and involve supplying critical components such as solid propellants, pyrotechnics, and explosive devices for strategic programs such as Akash, Astra, and long range surface-to-air missiles.

The company also supplies to major mining entities such a Northern Coalfields, Singareni Collieries, and Neyveli Lignite Corporation. PEL’s technical reliability and regulatory compliance have enabled it to secure repeat orders and have led to strong visibility in future defence contracts. In FY25, a single customer accounted for over 57% of the company’s total revenue, while the top five customers collectively contributed 87%. In FY26, Ind-Ra expects the company’s revenue to have remained highly concentrated. The Ministry of Defence is likely to remain a key contributor to the company’s revenue. The high dependence on a limited customer base exposes the company to significant concentration risk, wherein any disruption due to regulatory or policy changes could materially impact revenue stability and business continuity. 

Comfortable Credit Metrics: Ind Ra expects the credit metrics of the company to remain comfortable in the medium term, with an interest coverage (EBITDA/gross interest cost) of 6.0x-7.0x and a continued net cash position. In FY26, the interest coverage is likely to have improved to approximately 14x (FY25: 5.26x; FY24: 5.56x), driven by lower interest expenses due to reduced utilisation of working capital limits. The interest coverage is likely to return to previous years’ levels over the medium term due to an increase in debt levels to support the capex for capacity enhancements. The net leverage (net debt/EBITDA) is likely to have to remained negative in FY26 (FY25: net cash; FY24: 0.71x). The leverage improved in FY25 due to a reduction in debt levels and increased cash and cash equivalents at end-March 2025. Ind-Ra expects total outside liabilities to EBITDA (TOL/EBITDA) to have remained below 4.0x in FY26 (FY25: 4.85x; FY24: 3.77x; FY23: 6.17x) and expects it to remain below these levels in the near term. In FY25, TOL/EBITDA had increased due to an increase in payables.

Intensive Working Capital Cycle: PEL’s working capital levels, which were at exceptionally low levels in FY25,  are likely to have normalised in FY26 and are projected to stabilise at 130–140 days over the medium term. In FY26, the net working capital is estimated to have been at approximately 16% of revenue, while gross working capital is likely to have been around 46%. At FYE25, the gross working capital cycle of the company as percentage of revenue had improved to 49% (FY24: 73%; FY23: 71%) and net working capital cycle as percentage of revenue had improved to 4% (31%; 47%). The improvement was a led by significant reduction in the debtors, unbilled revenue, advances given to the suppliers, and an increase in the trade payables at end-March 2025. In FY25, the receivables days (including the advances and deposits) improved to 44 days (FY24: 147days; FY23: 114days), while inventory days improved to 231 days (286 days; 329 days). The improvement in working capital in FY25 was also led by an extension in payable days (including advances from customers) to 163days (FY24: 152days; FY23: 85days). 

Environmental and Social Risk Exposure: PEL faces environmental risks due to its operations involving hazardous materials, which require strict compliance with evolving regulations. Social risks include potential community impact and reputational damage from any safety lapses. However, PEL has maintained a strong safety record and regulatory compliance, mitigating these risks effectively. Its alignment with national defence initiatives further supports its operational resilience. Furthermore, the company has been taking steps to reduce its carbon footprint by enhancing its reliance on renewable sources for power.

Raw Material Price Volatility: PEL is exposed to fluctuations in the prices of key raw materials, particularly specialised chemicals, which are influenced by global commodity trends, regulatory constraints, and geopolitical developments. These price movements can impact production costs and margins. To mitigate this risk, the company employs strategic sourcing, maintains buffer inventories, and enters into long-term supply agreements where feasible. The company sources about 20% of its raw materials from Israel, exposing it to geopolitical risks associated with the Middle East. Supply disruptions, such as those experienced in 4QFY26, though now resolved, could delay order execution and impact operations. To mitigate this risk, the company is exploring alternative sourcing options to ensure continuity of operations.

== LIQUIDITY ==
Adequate: As on 31 March 2026, the company had unencumbered cash and cash equivalents amounting to INR512 million, reflecting a healthy liquidity position. It has access to fund-based working capital limits of INR900 million and non-fund-based limits of INR1,931 million, with average utilisation levels of 4% and 59%, respectively, over the 12 months ended 31 March 2026. The company has modest scheduled debt repayments of INR78.51million, INR180.14million and INR 155.14million in FY27, FY28 and FY29, respectively. PEL plans to undertake capex of  nearly INR700 million over FY26-FY27 for the expansion of its existing plant. The company has indicated that the same will be funded through debt of INR500 million and the balance through internal accruals, and it does not intend to raise any additional debt, indicating a stable capital structure.

== RATING SENSITIVITIES ==
Positive: A significant expansion in the scale of operations, driven by sustained revenue visibility, while maintaining the current levels of profitability, credit metrics, and liquidity, could lead to a positive rating action. 

Negative: A decline in the scale of operations or profitability or any substantial debt-funded capex, all leading to an interest coverage ratio of below 3.0x on a sustained basis, could lead to a negative rating action.

== ABOUT THE COMPANY ==
PEL manufactures explosives, propellants, and related products primarily for defence, aerospace, and industrial applications. Established in 1980, it operates facilities approved by government agencies and serves both domestic and export markets. It operates two key defence and propellant units in Peddakundukur and Katepally (Telangana), along with six bulk explosive manufacturing facilities spread across Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Telangana, and Tamil Nadu.
