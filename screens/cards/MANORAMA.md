## Business Understanding Narrative

Manorama turns forest seeds into chocolate fat. Tribal collectors in India gather sal,
mango, kokum, mowrah and other tree-borne seeds from the forest floor; village networks
pre-clean and deshell them; the seeds go to the Birkoni plant in Chhattisgarh (IP p.11).
Shea nuts and shea butter come from West Africa through wholly owned subsidiaries in ten
regions. The plant extracts, refines and fractionates the oil into stearin and olein
fractions, and the hard stearin becomes MILCOA cocoa butter equivalent, the vegetable fat
chocolate makers blend with cocoa butter (IP p.6, p.11). Customers are Fortune 500 food,
confectionery and cosmetics companies across 39-plus countries (IP p.5, p.6). Stearin and
CBE were 71.4% of FY26 revenue, CBE alone 30% (IP p.6; Concall p.7). Export to domestic
mix is 60:40 (IP p.5).

The economics are unusual for a processor. FY26 revenue Rs 1,358 crore on average fixed
assets of Rs 184 crore, an asset turnover of 7.4x, with EBITDA margin 26.4%, PAT margin
16.5%, ROE 39.4% and ROCE 46.2% (IP p.5, p.13). Revenue compounded 48.5% and PAT 76.2%
over FY22 to FY26.

What is changing is scale and integration, not the business. Fractionation capacity went
from 15,000 TPA in FY22 to 47,500 TPA in FY26, and a proposed Rs 460 crore capex over two
to three years adds a 75,000 TPA third fractionation plant, a 90,000 TPA refinery, new
CBA and ESOS capacity in India, and a processing factory in Burkina Faso (IP p.12, p.14).

Why now: the capacity is full. Utilisation ran about 80% in Q1FY27 and is guided at 80%
to 85% for the year (Concall p.8).

What must be true: the Rs 460 crore must earn something close to the current return.

What breaks it: arithmetic. The 46% ROCE rests on a Rs 184 crore asset base. Adding
Rs 460 crore is a 2.5x increase. Even excellent execution will pull ROCE down hard while
the new plants fill. Everything else here is working.

## 2. Business model and archetype
Manorama buys a raw material almost nobody else can buy at scale, and sells a formulated
ingredient into recipes it helped design. Revenue is tonnes times a price set by
formulation, not by a commodity screen: "our products are more value-added product, which
is formulated and based on certain specifications and recipes of the confectionery
chocolate or cosmetics. So our pricing model has remained stable largely" (Concall p.6).
Archetype: licence and scarcity business, in the library's sense, dressed as a processor.
The scarce asset is the collection network. Sal seeds lie scattered on a forest bed and
are gathered by tribal women through village aggregation centres (IP p.11). That network
took two decades to build and cannot be bought.
The one economic engine is asset turnover. A Rs 184 crore fixed asset base produced
Rs 1,358 crore of revenue in FY26 (IP p.13). Working capital, not plant, is where the
money sits, because seeds are bought in a season and processed through the year.
By-product de-oiled cake is monetised (IP p.11).

## 3. Competitive advantages
Strong, and rare in Indian small caps.
Sourcing is the moat. Ten wholly owned African subsidiaries plus a new one in Chad, an
Indian tribal collection network, and a Burkina Faso processing plant coming (Concall
p.4, p.7, p.8). When Nigeria banned shea nut exports, the company could say Nigeria is
one of 22 shea-growing countries and the ban "doesn't materially impact our sourcing
strategy" (Concall p.8). That is what supply diversification is for.
Qualification is the second barrier. Products are formulated to a customer's recipe, with
co-creation between R&D teams, in a DSIR-accredited centre (IP p.15). Certifications run
FSSC 22000, ISO, RSPO, Fairtrade, Kosher, Halal, Sedex SMETA (IP p.1, p.7). Food companies
do not change a chocolate fat casually.
Regulation is a tailwind, not a threat: the EU and FSSAI allow up to 5% CBE in a product
still labelled chocolate (IP p.9).
The weakness is contract tenor. Contracts run 9 to 12 months and roll continuously
(Concall p.7). There is no multi-year lock. Moat: present and durable in sourcing,
qualification-based in product, short-dated on paper.

## 5. Financial trajectory
FY22 to FY26 revenue: Rs 279, 351, 457, 771, 1,358 crore. Average fixed assets: Rs 56, 76,
113, 152, 184 crore. Asset turnover: 5.0, 4.6, 4.0, 5.1, 7.4x (IP p.13). Revenue CAGR
48.5%, EBITDA CAGR 75.3%, PAT CAGR 76.2% (IP p.7).
FY26 ratios: EBITDA margin 26.4%, PAT margin 16.5%, ROE 39.4%, ROCE 46.2% (IP p.5).
Q1FY27: revenue Rs 404 crore, up 39.5%; EBITDA Rs 106 crore, up 42.2%, margin 26.3%, up 49
basis points; PAT Rs 79 crore, up 67.6%, margin 19.5%, up 326 basis points (Concall p.4,
p.5).
Three details matter more than the headline.
First, the growth is volume, not price. About 85% of the 39.5% year-on-year growth came
from volume (Concall p.10). That is the opposite of Laxmi Organic in the same quarter.
Second, PAT grew far faster than EBITDA, 67.6% against 42.2%. Other income was about
Rs 16 crore in the quarter, from fixed deposit income and forex gains, and the QIP money
landed in the same quarter (Concall p.12). So part of the PAT margin expansion is
non-operating and will normalise.
Third, gross margin fell this quarter. Management put it to freight timing and by-product
realisation, and gave a 45% to 50% band, saying gross margin "will be best tracked
together with EBITDA margin rather than in isolation" (Concall p.10).
Consolidated subsidiaries lost Rs 3 crore in the quarter against Rs 8 crore the prior
quarter, on essentially no revenue: "West African entities are mostly for cost
procurement vehicles with no standalone revenue" (Concall p.11).
Debt, cash flow and the working capital cycle are NOT FOUND.
Inflection classification: earnings-led and volume-led, sustained over four years. This
is the cleanest trajectory of the nine names screened.

## 6. The transition and the quality ladder
This is where the card must be careful, because Manorama is not a transition candidate in
the usual sense. It is already at the top of the ladder.
Management's claim, quoted once: "we are well-positioned to deliver profitable growth and
strengthen our global market presence... building a globally integrated specialty
ingredients platform with a scalable and resilient supply chain" (Concall p.4). That is a
scale claim, not a rung claim.
FROM R4 to R5 already, on the ladder's own test. Pricing power is evidenced ("our pricing
model has remained stable largely" through a volatile input year, Concall p.6). ROCE is
46.2%, far above the R5 threshold of 30% (IP p.5). Structural scarcity is the sourcing
network. It sits at R5 today.
TO: nowhere higher. There is no rung above R5.
The consequence for the strategy is direct. The re-rating engine that transition alpha
hunts, a destination-PE delta from a rung migration, does not exist here. There is no rung
to migrate to. Any return has to come from earnings growth and from the recognition gap
being open today, not from a climb.
The forward integration into CBA and ECBE (enzymatic cocoa butter equivalent, converting
olein liquid fractions into hard fractions using enzymes, Concall p.9) deepens the moat and
raises value capture. It does not move a rung.

## 8. Proof check
The quarters move exactly the way the triggers claim, which is unusual.
What fired: revenue up 39.5% with 85% of it volume, on capacity that was expanded in July
2025 and debottlenecked in March 2026 (IP p.12; Concall p.10). Utilisation about 80%.
EBITDA margin held and expanded 49 basis points through a quarter of higher freight and
container costs (Concall p.5, p.10). Capacity went 15,000 to 47,500 TPA over four years
while asset turnover rose from 5.0x to 7.4x, which is the signature of expansion that
fills. CARE upgraded the credit from A to A+ (IP p.7). The QIP closed. Subsidiary losses
halved.
What has not fired: nothing yet, because the Rs 460 crore programme has barely started.
Fractionation Plant-3 and the refinery commission in Q3FY28. Burkina Faso awaits
regulatory approval. Brazil is at trial production.
Named proof point: asset turnover and ROCE through FY28 and FY29 as the new capacity
capitalises. The company's own execution priority is 85% to 90% utilisation of the
expanded 52,000 TPA (IP p.14). Watch whether the same discipline holds on a base 2.5x
larger.

## 9. Flags from the documents
- **Return dilution is coming by construction.** ROCE 46.2% sits on average fixed assets

  in FY27 alone (IP p.13, p.14; Concall p.12). Commissioning is FY28. Any model that
  carries 46% ROCE forward is wrong.
- Other income of about Rs 16 crore in a quarter with Rs 79 crore of PAT (Concall p.12).
  Roughly a fifth of profit is fixed deposit income and forex gains, and the QIP cash
  arrived this quarter. PAT margin expansion of 326 basis points is not all operating.
- Sourcing sits in politically fragile states. Burkina Faso and Chad are the two newest
  sourcing investments, both awaiting or subject to regulatory approval (Concall p.4).

- Contracts are 9 to 12 months and roll (Concall p.7). No multi-year revenue lock.
- Consolidated subsidiaries carry cost with almost no revenue: Rs 3 crore loss this

- Disclosure is selective. The company declines to give per-tonne realisation or

- Two numbers for FY26 revenue in the same deck: Rs 13,667 Mn on p.5 and Rs 1,358 Cr on

## 11. Posture: Transition Decision Matrix
- Proof gate: FIRED. Four years of 48.5% revenue CAGR, capacity 15,000 to 47,500 TPA
  filled to 80%, asset turnover up from 5.0x to 7.4x, ROCE 46.2%, and an external credit
  upgrade. This is as clean a proof set as a shallow read can produce.
- Ugliness: ABSENT. There is no ugly optic to classify. Margins are high, growth is
  volume-led, the balance sheet was just strengthened by a QIP, and the credit was
  upgraded. The matrix assumes a transition thesis carries an ugly number; this one does
  not.
- Recognition gap: INDETERMINATE from the corpus, and the honest prior is CLOSED. A
  business printing 46% ROCE, 26% EBITDA margin and 48% revenue CAGR, with a QIP just
  placed with institutions, is not usually a name the market has failed to notice.
Proof FIRED plus no ugliness plus a probably-closed gap maps to **EARNINGS-ONLY**. The
climb is real and already priced; the re-rating engine is spent because there is no rung
above R5. Returns from here ride EPS growth alone. Under the matrix that is actionable
only if growth clears the 25% CAGR hurdle without help from re-rating, which for this
company is a live question rather than a dismissal: revenue has compounded at 48.5%.

## 12. Verdict card
**PROCEED to `/step1`, classified EARNINGS-ONLY, not RE-RATING LIVE.** Manorama is the
highest-quality business of the nine screened. It has a sourcing moat that took two
decades to build and cannot be bought, formulated products qualified into Fortune 500
recipes, a regulatory tailwind, 46.2% ROCE, 7.4x asset turnover, four years of 48.5%
revenue CAGR with growth that is 85% volume, and a credit upgrade from CARE.
It is worth a full run for one reason and one reason only: whether earnings growth alone
clears the 25% hurdle from whatever the price is. Do not run it looking for a rung climb.
There is no rung above where it already sits, and the deep run should be built to test
compounding, not re-rating.
Load-bearing facts a deep run must verify first:
1. The price. Establish CMP, market cap and the trailing multiple before any other work.
   This name's entire case turns on whether the gap is open, and the prior is that it is
   not.
2. ROCE after the Rs 460 crore capex. Model the asset base at Rs 184 crore going to
   roughly Rs 650 crore and ask what return the incremental capital earns. Do not carry
   46% forward.
3. The CARE rating rationale. It is the one document that would give debt, working
   capital and an outside view on the capex.
4. Promoter holding, pledge and the QIP terms: size, price, allottees, and post-issue
   promoter stake.
5. Other income quality: how much of the Rs 16 crore quarterly run rate is QIP cash
   parked in deposits, and what PAT looks like without it.
6. Africa political and regulatory risk on Burkina Faso and Chad, and the working capital
   tied up in a seasonal seed purchase.
What would change the view: a price that leaves the recognition gap open would move this
to RE-RATING LIVE and make it the strongest name of the nine. Evidence that the
incremental Rs 460 crore earns 25% plus would keep the compounding case intact even at a
full price. A commissioning slip on Fractionation Plant-3, or a Sahel sourcing disruption
that the ten-country spread cannot absorb, would move it to WATCH. No price here.
