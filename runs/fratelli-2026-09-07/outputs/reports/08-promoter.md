**The consolidation itself (WHY):** on the evidence assembled, this
reads as an intra-family estate/succession restructuring rather than a
governance red flag in the SEBI-conduct sense. Bhupinder Kumar Sekhri
(the patriarch, no operating role) receives shares from his son (Gaurav,
the sitting MD), his son's wife (Aarti), his own wife (Shobha), and his
late son Kapil's widow (Puja) — the four sellers are precisely the four
women/men one generation below him plus his own spouse, each reduced to a
near-identical 1.00% residual (except Puja, left at 3.93%, the largest of
the four). This pattern is more consistent with equalising or
consolidating family branches' holdings under the elder generation
(plausibly ahead of estate planning, given a sibling has already died
young) than with an operational power grab, since Bhupinder Kumar Sekhri
takes no board seat and Gaurav Sekhri was simultaneously reappointed MD
for five years the same year (01-Apr-2026). It nonetheless **does**
create a governance fact the operator should track: the single largest
individual FVL shareholder (21.32%) now has no board seat, no disclosed
board nomination right, and no stated succession or voting arrangement
with the operating MD. Economic and voting control (Bhupinder Kumar
Sekhri) and operational control (Gaurav Sekhri, holding only 1.00% after
the transfer) have visibly separated within the same family. Whether this
is stable (a quiet elder-generation shareholder) or a precursor to a
future board or ownership change is not answerable from the corpus and is
named as a monitorable, not resolved as a verdict input.
