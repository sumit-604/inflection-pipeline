## PART 1: CLAIM-BY-CLAIM VERIFICATION

### Q1. ER&D demand conditions through FY26 and into Q1 FY27, in Plant Engineering, A&C and Aerospace verticals

**Claim tested:** TAAL Tech's 24.6% then 13.6% sequential quarterly revenue
jump reflects a sector-wide ER&D demand step-up (B05 trigger #2).

  previous quarter... largely volume-led" — and framed as catch-up from a

  still there, from an industry perspective... Times are still tough out

  Healthcare de-grew 13.1% QoQ; management revised its FY27 aspiration DOWN
  from double-digit to "high-single digit exit" for automotive, citing
  "current geopolitical and all the war" uncertainty (TATAELXSI Q4 FY26
  call, p.2, p.6).
- Tata Elxsi Q1 FY27: overall growth 1.3% QoQ / 6.5% YoY constant currency;
  Europe automotive described as "a little moderated," Germany "soft";
  growth described as "led by our major verticals" with no claim of a
  sector-wide acceleration (TATAELXSI Q1 FY27 call, p.2-4).
- Cyient Q4 FY26: DET revenue DEGREW 2.4% QoQ constant currency; "a shift

  "some slowness in awarding of discretionary projects as customers across

- Onward Technologies is the one bright spot on demand LANGUAGE (not

**Peers silent:** none of the three checked peers was silent on demand;
all three narrate it at length every quarter. No peer discussed TAAL

**Net read:** No peer, across 11 transcripts spanning January to August
2026, describes anything close to a 24.6%-then-13.6% sequential jump.
Aggregate peer growth in this window is low-single-digit to negative at
the quarterly level, with one genuine bright spot (aerospace volume at
Cyient) and one genuine soft spot (plant-engineering-adjacent energy work
at Cyient, and transportation at Onward). This sharpens rather than
resolves B05's FLAG-SILENCE: TAAL Tech's acceleration is not visible
anywhere in the listed ER&D peer set in the same window, which makes it

### Q2. Pricing power / utilisation / billing-rate improvement

**Verdict: PARTIALLY VERIFIED**

**Peer evidence:**
- Tata Elxsi: utilisation ran 73% (Q4 FY26) to "just above 75%" (Q1 FY27),
  well below the company's own historical peak of 85-86% (Q3 FY26 call,
  p.15; Q4 FY26 call, p.7). Margin improvement in this window is
  explicitly attributed to currency, operating leverage from utilisation,
  fixed-price mix, and cost discipline — not broad price increases.
  Management: "we are not seeing irrationality" on competitor pricing,
  "there are a few cases here and there" of aggressive AI-priced
  competition (Q4 FY26 call, p.11-12).
- Cyient: the one peer with an explicit price-increase disclosure: "Some
  of our existing customers have also backed us on price increases this
  year. That is one of the clearest markers of strength" (Q4 FY26 call,
  p.5). Price hikes are also named as one FY27 margin lever alongside
  automation savings and G&A cuts (Q1 FY27 call, p.11).
- Onward Technologies: margin improvement "predominantly driven by
  utilization and productivity" (Q2 FY26 call, p.4); explicit bill-rate
  disclosure — India client work bills at roughly Rs500/hour versus
  roughly $18/hour for export work, and the company has been shifting mix
  toward the higher export rate (Q2 FY26 call, p.11). Revenue per employee
  rose from roughly Rs8-9 lakh to Rs22 lakh over four years on flat
  headcount (Q4 FY26 call, p.3, p.13) — a real productivity/mix story, but
  driven by geographic-mix shift and offshore transition, not a broad
  price hike.

**Peers silent:** none of the three offer a single, clean "we raised
rates by X%" number; all frame pricing as customer-by-customer and
qualitative.

**Net read:** Utilisation- and mix-driven margin improvement is real and
disclosed at two of three peers; one peer (Cyient) discloses explicit
realised price increases. None of this evidence approaches the magnitude
that would explain TAAL Tech's reported ~27.8% genuine EBIT margin (see
Margin Benchmark below), and nothing describes a sudden, broad billing-
rate jump that could explain a 24.6%/13.6% sequential revenue jump on its
own.

### Q4. Client concentration levels, rising or falling industry-wide

**Verdict: VERIFIED (direction: peer concentration is typically higher
than TAAL Tech's, and management frames high concentration as strategy,
not risk)**

**Peer evidence:**
- Onward Technologies discloses the cleanest numbers: top 25 customers
  rose from 80% of revenue (when the company was ~Rs250cr) to 88% of
  revenue at Rs550cr FY26 revenue (Q4 FY26 call, p.4, p.16-17), 87% in Q1

  "any company with more than $1 billion market cap up to $10 billion has

- Tata Elxsi discloses no numeric top-client percentage but discusses
  concentration as an active strategic concern: "we are constantly trying
  to diversify our concentration risk... diversify our geography risk"

- Cyient discloses no top-client percentage either, but several of its
  quarterly swings (Strategic Units degrowth, connectivity delays) are
  explicitly tied to "two-three key customers" delaying program starts
  (CYIENT Q4 FY26 call, p.4), implying meaningful account-level dependency
  even without a disclosed number.

**Peers silent:** none give a number below top-10 or top-25 aggregation;
no peer discloses a true single-largest-customer percentage comparable to
TAAL Tech's 24.05% figure.

**Net read:** The one peer with disclosed numbers (Onward) runs concentration
far higher than TAAL Tech — 87-88% in its top 25 clients, against 24.05%
in TAAL Tech's single largest customer — and its own management frames
concentration in the 90%+ range as the normal, even deliberate, shape of
a scaled ER&D services business, not a red flag. This does not clear
TAAL Tech's concentration trend of scrutiny (it is still rising, and the
MD&A still omits it, per B04/B05), but it reframes the SEVERITY: 24.05% in
a single customer is comparatively modest next to what "normal" looks like
in this peer set, and Tata Elxsi's active language about "diversifying
concentration risk" is the closer read-across for what TAAL Tech's MD&A
should be saying and is not.

## THE SECTOR DEMAND READ

Did ER&D demand step up in the January-June 2026 window, in verticals
TAAL Tech serves? The peer answer is NO, not as a broad step-up, with one
narrow exception.

Aggregated across all eight quarterly earnings calls: Tata Elxsi's total
revenue moved +3.2% (Q3 FY26), +0.9% (Q4 FY26), +1.3% (Q1 FY27) quarter
on quarter in constant currency — modest, decelerating into Q4, then
recovering only slightly. Cyient's DET segment moved -2.4% (Q4 FY26),
-0.5% (Q1 FY27) — DEGROWTH in both of the two quarters read. Onward
Technologies, the most bullish of the three on demand language, still
posted company-wide revenue growth of only 10.5% for the FULL YEAR FY26
and 11.5% year-on-year in Q1 FY27 — an order of magnitude below TAAL
Tech's 24.6%-then-13.6% SEQUENTIAL jumps.

The one place a genuine acceleration shows up in the peer set is Cyient's
Transportation & Mobility cluster, which includes aerospace: +13.2% YoY
constant currency for FY26, +14.8% YoY in Q1 FY27, explicitly attributed
to "the current volume surge" in aerospace maintenance and production
volumes (CYIENT Q4 FY26 call, p.6; Q1 FY27 call, p.6). This maps directly
onto one of TAAL Tech's stated verticals (Aerospace) and is the single
most plausible sector read-across this run can offer: IF TAAL Tech's
unnamed "multiple strategic accounts" sit inside aerospace, an aerospace-
volume tailwind is a real, peer-confirmed phenomenon in this window. But
this is inference, not confirmation — B04/B05 record no vertical-level
revenue split for TAAL Tech that would let this run test the hypothesis
against filed evidence.

Where TAAL Tech's other named verticals map to peer evidence, the read is
weaker or negative: Cyient's energy/plant-engineering-adjacent Strategic
Units segment degrew -12.4% (Q4 FY26) and -8.2% (Q1 FY27) quarter on
quarter, driven by West Asia project deferrals and "customers across the
globe were cautious." Onward's Transportation vertical (auto+rail)
DE-GREW 1% for FY26. Tata Elxsi's transportation vertical, the closest
peer proxy to plant/industrial engineering demand, ran flat to soft
through the window, with Europe specifically called out as "moderated"
and "soft" in Q1 FY27.

**The finding, stated plainly per the B05 brief: peers describe flat, soft,
or narrowly-concentrated demand (aerospace volume being the one exception)
across the January-June 2026 window, while TAAL Tech's revenue rose 24.6%
then 13.6% sequentially. That gap is real. It makes TAAL Tech's
acceleration look company-specific, not sectoral, and it remains
unexplained by any filed document in this run's corpus (per B05
FLAG-SILENCE).**

## THE MARGIN BENCHMARK

TAAL Tech earns roughly 27.8% genuine EBIT margin (Other Income excluded)
at Rs197cr revenue (per B04). What do the three peers actually earn, in
their own words, and what would a Rs200cr Indian ER&D firm plausibly need
to do to reach that number?

**Tata Elxsi — the acknowledged listed design-led margin ceiling:**
EBITDA margin ran 23.3% (Q3 FY26) to 24.6% (Q4 FY26) to 21.2% (Q1 FY27,
depressed by one-off transition and provisioning costs). Management's own
stated AMBITION, repeated across two calls, is to exit Q4 FY27 at "27%
kind of margin... at the PBT level" (Q4 FY26 call, p.9-10) — an ASPIRATION
for a future quarter, not a current achieved number, and one the company
had not yet reached as of the Q1 FY27 call (21.2% EBITDA, well below even
the EBITDA-basis equivalent of 27% PBT). Utilisation across the window ran
73-75%, well below the company's own historical peak of 85-86%. Even Tata
Elxsi — larger, older, and explicitly brand-premium in the listed ER&D
set — has never, in any of the three quarters read, ACHIEVED a genuine
margin near TAAL Tech's current 27.8%, at roughly 5x TAAL Tech's revenue
scale.

**Cyient:** DET segment EBIT margin ran 12.2% (FY26) to 13.2% (Q1 FY27),
working toward a stated 15% EBIT exit target that management pushed out
from Q4 FY27 to "H1 FY28" during the Q1 FY27 call (p.11) because "revenue
ramp up... is running a little behind." Group-level EBIT margin (including
loss-making semiconductor investment) is 9.5-9.7%.

**Onward Technologies:** EBITDA margin rose from roughly 9% (prior year)
to 13.2% (FY26) to 12.3% (Q1 FY27, sequentially up 113bps). An analyst on
the Q3 FY26 call directly benchmarked Onward against "our peers, I believe
[who] are somewhere around in the range of 20%" (Q3 FY26 call, p.11) —
management did not dispute the 20% peer figure, only its own trajectory
toward it. Onward's own stated mechanism for margin expansion is offshore-
mix shift (India client bill rate ~Rs500/hour vs. export ~$18/hour) and
headcount discipline (2,500 employees flat for five years against 5x
revenue growth).

**Conclusion.** No peer, across eleven transcripts spanning January to
August 2026, describes ACHIEVING a genuine EBIT or EBITDA margin near
TAAL Tech's 27.8%, at anything like TAAL Tech's Rs197cr scale. The closest
comparator, Tata Elxsi, treats 27% as a forward aspiration requiring
utilisation above 80% (versus its actual 73-75%) and years of platform
investment — TAAL Tech's disclosed utilisation is NOT FOUND (per B04).
The three peer-demonstrated margin mechanisms, in the peers' own words,
are: (1) high utilisation (80%+; none of the three peers is currently
there), (2) an optimised, heavily-offshore delivery pyramid (Onward's
explicit mechanism), and (3) realised price increases on a sticky, tenured
client base (Cyient's explicit mechanism). TAAL Tech discloses none of
utilisation, offshore/delivery mix, or realised pricing data (per B04),
so none of these three peer-demonstrated mechanisms can be confirmed or
ruled out from filed evidence. Peer evidence sharpens B04's FLAG-MARGIN-
SOURCE finding — it establishes that TAAL Tech's margin is genuinely
anomalous against the best comparable evidence this run can obtain — but
it cannot resolve the mechanism question; that remains the single most
important open item for this run, as B04 already flagged.

## THE WORKING-CAPITAL BENCHMARK

TAAL Tech's unbilled revenue nearly tripled (Rs4.64cr to Rs13.75cr) and
CFO/PAT fell to 0.33x while revenue grew 6.6% (per B01/B02). Every peer
statement on DSO, collections, or cash conversion in this window is
collected in Part 1, Q3 above. Restated here as the direct benchmark:

- Onward Technologies (the only peer disclosing DSO): 73 days improving to
  70 days across the two quarters it reported the metric — IMPROVING, the
  opposite direction. The metric then disappears from disclosure in the
  two most recent calls (Q4 FY26, Q1 FY27), which is itself worth noting:
  even a peer with a track record of transparent DSO disclosure stopped
  reporting it in exactly the window this run needed it most, so the
  Apr-Jun 2026 comparison point does not exist in this peer set.
- Cyient: FCF/PAT conversion 163% (Q4 FY26, explicitly attributed to
  "sustained focus on working capital efficiency and collections
  discipline") falling to 80.5% (Q1 FY27, explicitly explained by payment
  timing and called "consistent with prior years"). Cyient actively
  narrates every cash-conversion swing on the call; nothing resembling
  TAAL Tech's magnitude of deterioration appears.
- Tata Elxsi: no DSO metric; the sole receivables event is a discrete,
  named customer Chapter 11 bankruptcy prompting a one-off conservative
  provision — a credit event, not a billing-cycle trend.

**Is a lengthening billing cycle a sector condition or a TAAL Tech
condition? Based on the evidence available in this peer set, it reads as a
TAAL Tech condition.** Every peer disclosure on this topic points stable,
improving, or explained; none describes a pattern resembling TAAL Tech's.
This does not by itself prove TAAL Tech's cash-conversion fall is
abnormal in absolute terms — none of the peers is a close enough business
model match (fixed-price mix, geography, contract structure all differ)
to serve as a precise control — but it removes the most charitable
explanation (that this is simply what a growth-phase T&M services
business looks like) from the table. Combined with B05's finding that
management never once addresses the cash-conversion fall in any filed
document, this triangulation makes the FLAG-CASH finding (B01/B04) more,
not less, concerning.

## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

### 2A. Demand environment

decelerating-to-flat quarterly growth across Tata Elxsi and Cyient in this
window, steady but unaccelerating annual growth at Onward, with one real

### 2B. Pricing and input costs
Cyient is the one peer with an explicit realised-price-increase
disclosure this year. Tata Elxsi notes occasional aggressive AI-priced
competition but calls it isolated, not systemic. Onward's margin gains
trace to offshore-mix shift and productivity, not broad rate increases.
The main "input cost" all three discuss is wage inflation: Tata Elxsi ran
two staggered wage-hike cycles in this window (junior staff Q4 FY26,
company-wide Q2 FY27) each costing 90-110bps of margin per cycle; Cyient
deferred its wage-hike decision to "beginning of H2"; Onward ties

**Read:** sentiment and forward investment across the peer set run ahead

**The single most consequential contradiction:** Q1, the sector demand

should close (and has not) remains exactly as wide as it was before this
stage, now with an external benchmark confirming it is not an industry-
wide gap.

## PART 5: CROSS-PEER HYPOTHESIS

Reading the three peer sets together, rather than one at a time, a pattern
emerges that no single peer states: the pockets of real demand strength in
this window are not spread evenly across ER&D, they cluster specifically
around end markets that are themselves capex beneficiaries of the AI/
data-center buildout, while traditional discrete-manufacturing and
automotive-OEM ER&D demand stays soft almost everywhere.

The evidence, combined: Cyient's one genuine growth engine is aerospace
volume plus telecom fiber/connectivity build-out explicitly tied to
"hundreds of billions of dollars of network expansion" and AI-driven data
demand (CYIENT Q4 FY26 call, p.6). Onward Technologies' strongest demand
language ties directly to "the massive scale that we are seeing across
North America and Europe in data centres, AI, and energy" (ONWARDTEC Q1
FY27 call, p.13) and its one named large win in this window is with "a
leading global power management company" serving that exact buildout. Tata
Elxsi's growth, by contrast, is concentrated in automotive OEM ramp-ups of
PREVIOUSLY-WON deals (not fresh demand) and is explicitly soft in Europe
automotive and flat-to-negative in Healthcare. Cyient's own energy/plant-
engineering vertical, superficially similar to the power-management theme,
is WEAK — because it serves traditional oil-and-gas/industrial energy
projects (West Asia-exposed), not the AI-power-infrastructure theme.

No single peer frames this as a unifying pattern; each describes only its
own vertical's dynamics. The testable hypothesis this comparison
surfaces: **ER&D demand in the January-June 2026 window is not broadly
cyclical-recovering. It is narrowly concentrated in accounts whose end
customers are themselves capex beneficiaries of the AI/data-center
buildout (power management, semiconductors, telecom/connectivity
infrastructure, aerospace MRO tied to flying-hour growth), while
traditional discrete-manufacturing and automotive-OEM ER&D demand remains
structurally soft in the same window.** This is falsifiable: it predicts
that any Indian ER&D company showing outsized, unexplained growth in this
specific window is more likely to be riding a small number of accounts
adjacent to AI-infrastructure capex than a broad sector recovery. Applied
to TAAL Tech, this reframes the open mgmt_question from B04 ("what caused
the Q1 FY27 revenue jump") into a sharper, checkable form for a future
stage with access to live data: do TAAL Tech's unnamed "strategic
accounts" sit in power/data-center/connectivity/aerospace-MRO-adjacent end
markets, or in traditional discrete manufacturing? The former would be
consistent with this cross-peer pattern; the latter would leave TAAL
Tech's acceleration unexplained by ANY mechanism visible in this peer set.
