**Transition 2, Margin: backward SUSTAINED, forward STAGNANT (0).** EBITDA margin sits near 22% for FY26, above every peer (NRB 19.5%, HARSHA 15 to 17%, PRECAM 8.9%, BIMETAL 6.2%), and moved only +100 bps over two years, so it is sustained at a peer leading level, not expanding. The bear reading is DECLINING: the Q3 promise to cut the cycle from 180 days to 30 was already contradicted in Q4, the large DDP customers refused to convert, and DSO is still rising. The draft uses STAGNANT because the deterioration is growth linked and a weak catalyst exists, which also means the Kernex cap does NOT engage. The single fact that flips it to DECLINING and drops the composite to AVOID: standalone receivables again growing faster than revenue with DSO above 114 days in the next print.

**Transition 4, ROCE: backward SUSTAINED at a premium, forward STAGNANT (0).** ROCE dipped to about 20% in FY24 then rose to 23.72% in FY25 and 29.80% in FY26 (AR Note 33), a premium level well above the 18% asset heavy bar. ## THE CATALYST STORY IN PLAIN WORDS

Revenue has the strongest forward case. The capacity is built, not promised, and the export orders are being invoiced, not pitched. The China plus one shift is confirmed by two peers independently, and Menon supplies engine bearings to John Deere, Tata, International Tractors and Allison as sole or major supplier. What confirms it firing is two or three more quarters above 25% YoY. What kills it is a US tariff shock or a tractor and commercial vehicle down cycle, since 80 to 90% of revenue is auto and the bulk is cyclical CV and tractor.

Margin has little forward room. It is already the best in the peer set, and the lever that could lift it, a richer export and machining mix, is offset by copper and aluminium inflation that resets into prices with a lag. What confirms expansion is two quarters above 24%. What kills it is another input spike before the escalation clauses catch up.

Cash is the swing. The only real catalyst is the ex-works conversion, which would shorten the export receivable cycle, plus PCFC financing that lowers interest but does nothing for the receivable itself. Both are weak. The large customers who drive the receivable are refusing ex-works, and there is no factoring facility and no delivered debtor day target. What confirms it firing is a printed drop in DSO back toward 90 days with CFO to PAT above 0.70. What kills the growth induced defence is one more print of receivables outrunning revenue, which reclassifies the flag to structural.

ROCE has no forward catalyst that scores, because it is already premium. The near term risk is mild dilution as the new lines sit idle before they ramp. What would firing look like is ROCE holding near 28 to 30% while revenue scales on the new capacity. What kills it is a capacity build that does not fill, leaving fresh capital earning nothing.

A NONE FOUND is worth stating plainly: there is no documented catalyst that would push margin or ROCE through a band over the window, and the only cash catalyst is failing in the transcripts. That absence is the finding.

## STEP 4: VERDICT

Composite score is +1 out of a range of minus 4 to plus 8, which lands in the DEEP WATCH leaning AVOID band. The Kernex cap does NOT engage, because the weakest transition, cash, is STAGNANT with a weak catalyst rather than DECLINING with no catalyst. The TRIM rule does not apply, because all four transitions are not firing backward. This is a good business where only one of the four transitions is firing forward, the two quality transitions are already at their peak with no further transition to price, and the one broken transition is cash. The whole call turns on a single print: whether standalone receivables keep outrunning revenue. If they do, cash goes to DECLINING and the composite falls to AVOID. If ex-works actually collapses the cycle, cash goes to STARTING and the composite rises toward a small buy on dips.
