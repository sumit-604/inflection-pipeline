# PLAIN-LANGUAGE BRIEF (standing deliverable)

## 1. SUMMARY NARRATIVE
Man Industries showed two very different pictures this year. The standalone company had a strong FY26:
revenue up 10.8% and clean operating profit (before other income) up 60%. The group as a whole did
far less: consolidated revenue rose only 1.7% and clean operating profit rose 10.6% [this quarter's
deck]. The gap matters. In FY25 the subsidiaries added Rs 16 Cr to profit; in FY26 they subtracted
Rs 25 Cr, so consolidated profit (Rs 171 Cr) came in below standalone profit (Rs 196 Cr). That flip
lit the first Notion tripwire. The second tripwire also lit: the group's operating margin without
other income was 12.3%, below the 13% floor. The reported 13.0% only clears the floor because the
deck counts other income inside EBITDA [deck footnote]. Depreciation and interest jumped 50-75% while
the plants funding them (Jammu stainless, Dammam coating) will not produce until March 2027, so ROE
sat at just 9.2%. The company also raised about Rs 300 Cr of fresh equity (share count up ~15.7%) to
fund the Saudi NPC deal, but the deck never shows EPS or the dilution. NPC itself looks good on its
own numbers (24.8% margin, low tax), but it only joined the group for 40 days and its debt (USD 70 Mn)
appears to sit in current liabilities, which pushes the net-cash-versus-net-debt reading toward roughly
Rs 480 Cr of net debt. There is no cash-flow statement, so cash quality is unverifiable. The price,
Rs 714, is above the buy zone of Rs 412-589 [prior Notion]. Verdict: hold on the watchlist, no buy,
and re-decide at the Q2 FY27 result, the first quarter that shows the full group with NPC inside it.

## 2. SECTOR INTELLIGENCE
Man makes large-diameter welded line pipe (HSAW/LSAW) for oil, gas, and water infrastructure. Demand
is a project-and-tender cycle driven by pipeline capex from national oil companies, city-gas networks,
and water authorities [business-model context, prior Notion]. The Saudi angle is the story: NPC's
buyers are Aramco, KOC, Qatar Energy, and Bapco [this deck, p22/p25], so group demand now leans on
Gulf energy capex, which has been firm. The India side is exposed to domestic gas-grid and water-mission
tenders, which are lumpy. Steel (HR coil) is the main input; a falling steel price lifts gross margin,
which likely explains part of the FY26 gross-margin jump to 38% [this deck, p31], though that jump did
not carry down to EBITDA. Regulation and payer mix are not the swing factors here; the swing factor is
the global energy-pipeline capex cycle and Aramco's approved-vendor gate, which NPC already holds.

## 3. BUSINESS-MODEL INTELLIGENCE
The company converts steel coil into coated welded pipe and earns a spread over input cost plus a value
-added layer from coating and, soon, stainless-steel seamless pipe. It is capital-heavy and working
-capital-heavy: inventory ~179 days, receivables ~103 days, payables ~172 days, so cash is tied up for
about 110 days and worsened ~9 days this year [this deck, p30]. Long-dated (non-current) receivables
jumped 2.5x, a sign that some revenue is booked well ahead of cash. The model is mid-teens-ROCE at best
today (headline 18.4%, but flattered by other-income-in-EBITDA and by plants still in Capital-WIP).
The transition claim is a climb up the value ladder: from commodity line pipe toward spec'd, higher
-margin stainless and coated product, plus a Saudi manufacturing base. This quarter says the climb is
funded and underway (Jammu 58% spent, NPC closed) but not yet visible in group profit. Model drift to
watch: the fresh equity and new debt have enlarged the capital base faster than earnings, so returns
fell before they can rise. Metrics the deck did NOT disclose: EPS, CFO, India order book, segment mix.

## 4. COMPETITION INTELLIGENCE
Man competes with Indian large-diameter pipe makers (Welspun Corp, Jindal SAW, Ratnamani for stainless)
[peer context, prior Notion; not named in this deck]. Where Man wins: an inherited Aramco approval via
NPC that greenfield rivals would need 1-2 years to earn [this deck, p24], a Saudi manufacturing footprint,
and 40+ year GCC relationships. Where it is structurally weaker: scale and balance-sheet strength versus
Welspun and Jindal SAW, single-digit ROE this year, and a working-capital cycle that stretches on
long-dated receivables. The clearest competitive risk to watch is margin durability at NPC: the deck
models the acquired route at 15-18% EBITDA yet shows NPC actually at 24.8% [this deck, p24 vs p26], so
either the underwriting was conservative or 24.8% is a cyclical Gulf peak that normalises toward peer
levels as regional pipe capacity competes for the same Aramco tenders. The next result, with NPC fully
inside the group, is the first honest test of whether Man is climbing the ladder or just buying revenue.
