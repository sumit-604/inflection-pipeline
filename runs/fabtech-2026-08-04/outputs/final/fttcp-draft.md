## MY RULINGS

Every call below is made. Each carries a confidence tag and the single fact that would overturn it.

**1. Forward window: 3 months primary, 6 months secondary, 12 months for ROCE.** The company reports quarterly, so the default stands unchanged. `sure`. Overturned by: the company moving to semi annual reporting.

**2. Business type: standard operating business, so the standard four transitions run.** B04 labels it hybrid, but that describes a revenue mix of 75.5% turnkey project execution and 24.5% equipment supply, not lender status. No lending, no AUM, no NIM. `sure`. Overturned by: evidence that a financing arm carries material assets.

**3. Workup intent: first workup, not a refresh.** No companies/FABTECH.md, manifest run_type full, listed 7 October 2025 per CRISIL. There is no prior decision status to defend, which removes the confirmation bias risk the protocol warns about. `sure`. Overturned by: a prior Notion page or company memory file surfacing.

**4. Sector cap row: CORRECTED from "Pharma / CDMO" 38x to "EPC / Civil construction" 20x.** The manifest auto picked the pharma row and flagged it "verify". Fabtech does not make drugs. It builds the factories that make drugs. Three quarters of revenue is milestone billed turnkey project execution carrying retention money, Q4 loaded billing and fixed price cost overrun risk, which is EPC economics. CRISIL rates it under its manufacturing, trading and corporate services criteria, not pharma. I considered "Consulting / Engineering services" at 25x and rejected it: that row fits a design house that sells drawings and hours, while Fabtech carries execution risk, retention money and inventory. **Phase 3 must inherit 20x.** `fairly sure`. Overturned by: evidence that equipment supply and design fees, not project execution, carry the majority of revenue and margin.

**5. Cash conversion is STRUCTURAL, not growth induced, and not INDETERMINATE.** Phase 1 left this open because the rating rationale had not been read. I read it. CRISIL, 15 October 2025, states that operations "continue to remain working capital intensive in nature", that high debtor days are "primary on account of major billing being done in Q4 and retention money held with the customer which is released within 2 years depending upon the contract", and that "WC operation is expected continue on similar lines over medium term". Both named causes are contractual features of the model, not artifacts of growth. Debtor days rose 157 to 169 to 182 across FY24, FY25 and FY26 while growth swung wildly, which is the pattern of a structural cycle, not a growth bulge. Section 1B says the rating agency assessment takes precedence. **This changes the Phase 1 gate flag from INDETERMINATE to STRUCTURAL and sets Pillar 2 at 0.65x with no growth offset.** `sure`. Overturned by: a sanctioned and operational factoring or receivables discounting facility with an amount and a date, or two consecutive quarters of debtor days below 150.

The framework names IPO equity bloat as a qualifying temporary cause and that is exactly what happened: Rs 179.22 Cr of the Rs 230.30 Cr raised sat undeployed at year end, which is 40.1% of capital employed. I record the harder half plainly: even stripped, 16.2% is about 1,000 basis points below the FY23 and FY24 level, and that residual gap is margin compression that began before the IPO. So the recovery on offer is to the mid teens, not to the high twenties. `fairly sure`. Overturned by: the FY27 print showing statutory ROCE still at or below 11% with the cash deployed.

I skipped the blend rather than invent an FY28 operational ROCE, so operational ROCE feeds the formula alone. Blend basis: blend skipped. `fairly sure`. ## STEP 2 — THE CATALYST STORY IN PLAIN WORDS

### Revenue: the one place the evidence is real

Fabtech is sitting on an order book of about Rs 900 Cr against FY26 revenue of Rs 410.77 Cr. That is 2.19 years of work already signed, and it is the strongest documented fact in this file. It has held near that level across three quarters, Rs 926 Cr in January, above Rs 900 Cr in March, about Rs 920 Cr adjusted for currency in June.

Underneath it, three things are genuinely moving. Saudi Arabia is the big one: the company set up a local entity to qualify under Vision 2030 local content rules and has won a first civil order through it, and the Q1 FY27 results notes record board approval of up to Rs 2,400 lakh into the UAE arm and a shareholders agreement for a Saudi step down subsidiary. Africa is the second, with vaccine plant work in Botswana, Morocco, Kenya and Djibouti and three more projects said to be in discussion. Ticket sizes are the third: typical orders have moved from about 1.5 to 3 million dollars a few years ago to 7 to 10 million now, with the largest quoted near 70 million.

What would confirm it: two consecutive quarters above 20% year on year growth with the order book holding above Rs 900 Cr. What would kill it: a third guidance cut, or the order book slipping below Rs 800 Cr.

The catch is that none of this has yet shown up as steady growth. Q1 FY27 grew 10.3%. Guidance for the year has been cut twice, from 30% to 40%, to about 25%, to 20% to 25%, all before the year had reported a single quarter. And Fabtech's own market sizing work supports about 13% to 14% a year over five years, not 20% plus.

| Catalyst | Evidence | Confirms | Kills |
|---|---|---|---|
| Order book Rs 900 Cr, 2.19x revenue | Documented, three quarters | Conversion at guided pace | Book below Rs 800 Cr |
| Saudi local entity and step down subsidiary | Documented, Q1 FY27 results note 6 | Second and third Saudi order | No order beyond the first civil job |
| Africa vaccine pipeline | Management claim, three projects in talks | Signed letters of credit | Talks stay talks past FY27 |
| Europe acquisition in Italy | Management claim, slipped two quarters | Definitive agreement signed | Abandoned or slips past FY27 |

**Forward: STARTING, probability about 65%, catalyst strength Moderate to Strong. Score +1.**

### Margin: talk without machinery

Management wants contribution margin near 45% and EBITDA margin of 13% to 14%. There is nothing documented behind either number. The levers named are operating leverage on second half volumes, a richer engineering mix, and a project cycle cut from 24 to 27 months down to 18 to 24 months. All of it is spoken, none of it is filed.

Two things argue against paying for it. The credibility grade is C, and the promise record is three delivered, two partial, five missed, which the protocol turns into a further discount on anything management promises. And the cost inflation story used to explain FY26, raw material up 33% and execution cost up 43%, could not be corroborated at anything like that scale by any of the four peers, the nearest being Praj at about 3% raw material inflation. Either Fabtech was hit far harder than its peers, which is a company specific problem rather than a sector one, or the number needs revisiting.

Against that, the honest bull point: Q1 FY27 operating margin of 5.66% follows a Q1 FY26 that was negative, so the year on year direction is genuinely up, and Q1 is the seasonally weakest quarter in a business that bills in the second half.

**Forward: STAGNANT, probability about 25%, catalyst strength Weak. Score 0.**

### Cash conversion: nothing is coming

This is the transition that decides the file, and the catalyst search comes back empty.

There is no factoring facility. There is no receivables discounting line. There is no debtor day target that has been set and hit. The move from shipment based revenue recognition to percentage of completion, which is what peers already do and what would align billing with work done, has been open across three consecutive calls and the horizon has moved out to "the next few years" rather than in. The auditor's single key audit matter is Rs 58.9 Cr of receivables more than 365 days overdue, which is 27% of gross receivables, and management has not addressed it once across three calls.

CRISIL is explicit that this is how the model works, not a phase it is passing through. High debtor days come from billing concentrated in the fourth quarter and from retention money the customer holds for up to two years. Working capital operations are expected to continue on similar lines over the medium term.

Ion Exchange and Praj, running the same kind of project business, both treat percentage of completion billing as settled practice, and Ion Exchange discloses project level detail on stuck contracts that Fabtech does not offer on its own overdue book.

**Catalyst: NONE FOUND. The absence is the finding.**

**Forward: DECLINING, probability below 20%, catalyst strength None. Score -1.**

### ROCE: a mechanical recovery, to the mid teens

Return on capital fell from 29.32% to 11.25% in three years. Most of the last leg is arithmetic rather than decay: Rs 230.30 Cr came in from the IPO in October 2025 and Rs 179.22 Cr of it was still sitting undeployed at year end, which is 40.1% of capital employed earning deposit rates. Take that out and the business earned 16.2% on the capital it actually used.

So there is a real recovery available and it does not need anything clever to happen. Cash gets deployed, or gets absorbed into working capital as the order book converts, and the reported number moves up toward the mid teens on its own.

What it will not do, on this evidence, is return to the high twenties. That gap is margin, and margin compression started before the IPO, falling from 15.41% in FY24 to 11.87% in FY25 while the company was still private. Two counter forces also push the other way: the Saudi build out is fresh capital that will not earn for a while, and any acquisition adds capital before it adds profit.

**Forward: RECOVERING, probability about 50%, catalyst strength Moderate. Score +1.**

## THE VERDICT

The composite is +1 out of 8, which lands in the DEEP WATCH leaning AVOID band, and the Kernex cap engages on top because cash conversion is going backwards with no catalyst at all. The cap holds any upside at DEEP WATCH, though the score already sits below it, so the cap removes headroom rather than providing a floor. The TRIM rule does not apply, since nothing is firing backward.

What this business has is a real order book, 2.19 years of signed work, and a genuine Saudi and Africa opening. What it does not have is any mechanism, named or filed, for turning that work into cash, and the rating agency says the working capital pattern continues over the medium term.

The whole call turns on one print: whether Q2 or Q3 FY27 shows operating cash flow above 30% of profit with debtor days below 175. If that lands, cash moves off DECLINING, the cap lifts, and this becomes a different conversation. If it does not, the order book is just a bigger receivable.

### THE EARNINGS BASIS QUESTION, FOR THE OPERATOR TO DECIDE

The multiple has to be applied to an earnings figure, and the choice changes the answer materially.

**One year forward.** Management guides 20% to 25% revenue growth and a 9.9% to 10.5% profit margin, which would put FY27 profit near Rs 51 Cr and earnings per share near Rs 11.50. Applying 12.1x to that gives a very different picture from the trailing view.
