## SECTION 1: MARKET DEFINITION

### 1A — Precise boundaries

**Product scope (in):** Flexible Intermediate Bulk Containers (FIBC / jumbo bags, all types A/B/C/D including food- and pharma-grade), small PP woven sacks, AD Star bags, and PP woven fabric/yarn — i.e. the entire Woven Sacks Division, 99.46% of HPBL's consolidated revenue (source: outputs/blocks/04-bizmodel.yaml `revenue_streams`). This is a rigid/semi-rigid bulk industrial-packaging product line, not flexible film/pouch packaging.

**Product scope (out):** Woven Label Division (Jacquard labels, 0.54% of revenue) — different product, not sized here. General flexible packaging (films, laminates, pouches for FMCG) — a much larger, different product category (India flexible-packaging market ~US$20bn 2025, WebSearch synthesis, not HPBL's business). Paper sacks, jute bags, rigid drums/IBC totes — substitute products, treated as a risk (Section 4B), not part of TAM.

**Geographic scope:** Global demand for FIBC and PP woven sacks, since HPBL both sells domestically in India (46.3% of FY25 revenue) and exports directly (53.70% of FY25 revenue — RATING p.6), with stated target export markets of the EU and US (AR p.57: "The Company's target markets are the EU and the US, which have stringent quality requirements") and a new Malaysia subsidiary for further international exposure (AR p.57).

**Customer scope:** B2B industrial/agri/chemical/food/pharma end-users who buy FIBC and PP woven bags for bulk material handling — cement, sugar, food grain, fertiliser, chemicals (AR p.57 "OUR BUSINESS OVERVIEW"). Rating agency separately characterises HPBL as positioned in the "specialised and customised segment... food and pharma grade FIBC... rather than the commoditised segment of cement and fertiliser sacks" (RATING p.4) — this is a point of tension with the AR's own product-application list (flagged, not resolved with a numeric split; see input_gaps).

**Channel scope:** Direct export sales and domestic direct sales under annual rate contracts or routine orders (RATING p.5); historically also job-work for group subsidiaries (now a minority of the model since direct exports commenced FY25 — RATING p.2).

**Price/quality segment:** Mid-to-premium, export-quality-certified segment (customer approvals required for EU/US food-pharma-grade FIBC), not the bottom-of-market commodity cement/fertiliser sack segment where EBITDA margins run 3-5% (RATING p.6-7) vs HPBL's 5-8% (RATING p.7).

**Explicit exclusions:** Global "flexible packaging" market (films/pouches, ~US$20bn+ India alone) — wrong product. Rigid IBC totes, steel/plastic drums, bulk tankers — substitute/competing formats, not counted in TAM (noted as a disruption risk, Section 4B). India's broader "packaging industry" (₹7.36 lakh cr / US$84bn in 2024, WebSearch synthesis) — far too broad; not used.

### 1B — Management's own TAM claim

HPBL/its AR does **not** state a company-specific TAM, SAM, or addressable-opportunity figure anywhere in the extracted MD&A. What the AR does reproduce, unattributed to a named publisher, in its "FIBC Space" industry-commentary section (AR p.56) is:

- "The global FIBC market is anticipated to touch a valuation of US $12.6 billion by 2033, with a CAGR of 5.4%." (AR p.56)
- "The global polypropylene woven sacks market is expected to surpass US $6.1 billion by 2034 growing at a CAGR of 4.2% from 2024 to 2034." (AR p.56)
- "India holds... a 75% share in the European FIBC imports and a 72% share in the US import market" (AR p.56, attributed only to "an industry expert")

**Definition used:** global, industry-level, unsegmented by company capability — not the company's own addressable opportunity.
**Date:** underlying research vintage not stated in the AR; the AR itself is the FY25 annual report.
**Credibility read: BROAD.** This is pure industry-report reproduction with no translation into HPBL's own product mix, capacity, or geographic reach — exactly the kind of undifferentiated "market is huge" framing the operating rules warn against. It is used below only as a cross-check anchor (Section 2), not as the company's forward-looking claim on itself.

## SECTION 3: SAM & SOM

### 3A — SAM: five filters applied to conservative TAM (₹1,04,170 Cr)

1. **Product fit filter:** Already embedded in the TAM definition (FIBC + PP woven sacks only, both HPBL product lines) — no further cut. Caveat: neither sourced report explicitly excludes sub-categories HPBL doesn't make (e.g., laminated BOPP woven sacks, geotextiles within the PP-woven-sacks report scope) — TAM may be marginally overstated by an unquantifiable, unsourced margin; flagged, not corrected with a fabricated %.
2. **Geography filter:** HPBL's stated served geography is India domestic + EU + US (AR p.57), with a new Malaysia subsidiary as an emerging fourth leg (AR p.57). Regional-share sources found are **materially inconsistent** (Mordor Intelligence: North America 38.25% of 2025 FIBC market; Global Growth Insights: Asia-Pacific >45% of global *production*, Europe ~20%; a third source's absolute-dollar figures were internally inconsistent with market scope and discarded). Synthesising North America (~30-38%) + Europe (~20-25%) + India's own domestic slice (embedded within the Asia-Pacific figure, not separable) gives an approximate **55%** geography-reachable share, applied conservatively (rounded down from a ~58% midpoint). **This is the single weakest, least-sourced number in this stage — flagged explicitly, not smoothed over.**
3. **Channel filter:** No further cut — HPBL's direct-export + domestic-direct model (post job-work transition, RATING p.2) matches the standard B2B channel structure assumed in the underlying reports; no evidence of a materially different (e.g., retail) channel segment to exclude.
4. **Customer/segment filter:** No sourced numeric split exists between HPBL's targeted specialised (food/pharma/chemical-grade) segment and the commodity cement/fertiliser segment it says it avoids (RATING p.4) — despite AR's own product-application list including cement and fertiliser (AR p.57), a contradiction already flagged in Stage 4 (input_gaps). **NOT FOUND, no cut applied; carried as a downside caveat that SAM may be modestly overstated.**
5. **Capability filter:** Not applied to SAM (capability constrains SOM, not addressable market) — tested explicitly in 3C.

**SAM = ₹1,04,170 Cr × 55% = ₹57,294 Cr. SAM as % of TAM = 55%.**

### 3B — SOM at 3 and 5 years

**Current share of SAM:** HPBL FY25 consolidated revenue ₹463.44 Cr ÷ SAM ₹57,294 Cr = **0.81%**.

**Share-gain trajectory (per the standard rules — 1-2pp normal in 3yr; 3-5pp aggressive with capacity+execution; >5pp only on competitor exit/consolidation):**

- **3-year SOM:** Conservative-bias 1.0pp gain (bottom of the "normal" 1-2pp band, per operating rule 6) → share 0.81% + 1.00% = 1.81% of SAM = **₹1,037 Cr**. Cross-checked against the capacity-based unit-economics anchor (Method 2b): ₹1,037 Cr ÷ ₹0.0817 Cr/MT = 12,693 MT = **52% utilisation of the 24,300 MTPA total capacity** — comfortably feasible and consistent with the rating agency's own expectation that "capacity utilisation is expected to improve over the medium term" (RATING p.5). The two methods converge closely (₹1,037 Cr vs. an independently-computed ₹1,032 Cr at 52% utilisation), a useful internal-consistency check.
- **5-year SOM:** A full "aggressive" 3-5pp share-gain scenario (→3.81-5.81% of SAM = ₹2,183-3,329 Cr) is **not capacity-feasible** — see 3C. **SOM is instead set at the capacity ceiling: 85% utilisation of 24,300 MTPA = 20,655 MT × ₹0.0817 Cr/MT = ₹1,688 Cr**, implying a share of SAM of 2.95% (a 2.14pp gain from the 0.81% base — placed at the high end of "normal"/entering "aggressive," justified by the capacity expansion (+6,000 MTPA from 1 Apr 2025) and export-model transition already executed, not speculative).

**Implied revenue CAGR (arithmetic shown):**
- 3-year: (1,037 / 463.44)^(1/3) − 1 = (2.2374)^0.3333 − 1 = **30.7%**
- 5-year: (1,688 / 463.44)^(1/5) − 1 = (3.6427)^0.20 − 1 = **29.5%**

Both figures cluster near HPBL's own recent trajectory (FY24→FY25 consolidated revenue growth 57.3%, 9mFY26 YoY growth 31.01% — RATING p.3), which lends the ~30% SOM-implied CAGR plausibility as a tapering-but-still-strong continuation, not an outlier assumption.

### 3C — Capacity cross-check

Installed capacity: 24,300 MTPA total (18,300 MTPA pre-expansion + 6,000 MTPA operational from 1 Apr 2025, RATING p.3/p.5). No committed further capex is guided for FY27-FY28 (RATING p.5: "the company does not have any major capital expenditure planned during the projected period of FY27–FY28").

- **3-year SOM (₹1,037 Cr) requires 52% utilisation — physically producible within existing capacity, no gap.**
- **5-year SOM (₹1,688 Cr) is deliberately capped at an assumed 85% practical-maximum utilisation of the existing 24,300 MTPA base — this is itself the ceiling, not a target with headroom beyond it.**
- If instead the "aggressive" 3pp share-gain scenario from 3B (₹2,183 Cr) were pursued as the 5-year SOM: required volume = 26,720 MT = **110% of installed capacity — physically impossible without new capex.** Gap = **₹495 Cr** beyond the 85%-utilisation ceiling (₹2,183 Cr − ₹1,688 Cr).
- **Read: on this scenario, SOM (not the "no major capex planned FY27-28" guidance) is the optimistic side.** The rating agency's own capex-light guidance is internally consistent with a demand/execution outlook that does *not* assume aggressive (3pp+) SAM-share capture — it assumes exactly the kind of utilisation-ramp-within-existing-capacity trajectory this stage's capacity-bound 5-year SOM reflects. Any equity-research assumption of faster share gains than ~2.1-2.2pp over 5 years should be treated as requiring an unguided capex event to be credible.

## SECTION 5: SUMMARY & RUNWAY

### 5A — Funnel

```
Global FIBC + PP woven sacks TAM (2025):     Conservative ₹1,04,170 Cr | Realistic ₹1,22,474 Cr
   × Geography filter (~55%, India+EU+US+Malaysia-reachable)
SAM:                                          ₹57,294 Cr   (55% of conservative TAM)
   × current share 0.81% → 3yr share 1.81% → 5yr share 2.95% (capacity-capped)
SOM 3-year:                                   ₹1,037 Cr   (52% capacity utilisation)
SOM 5-year:                                   ₹1,688 Cr   (85% capacity utilisation — ceiling)
Current HPBL FY25 revenue:                    ₹463.44 Cr
```

### 5B — Runway assessment

- **Revenue headroom (raw, SAM ÷ current revenue):** 57,294 / 463.44 = **123.6x** — but this is a theoretical, not a practically achievable, number: it assumes unconstrained capacity, which does not exist (Section 3C). Flagged explicitly rather than used as the headline runway read.
- **TAM growth rate (blended, volume-weighted by conservative-scenario mix):** FIBC 5.4% CAGR (61% weight) + PP woven sacks 4.2% CAGR (39% weight) = **≈4.9%** (AR p.56, both components).
- **Company CAGR vs. TAM:** SOM-implied company CAGR ~29.5-30.7% vs. TAM growth ~4.9% — HPBL is overwhelmingly a **share-gainer**, not a market-rider; almost all of its projected growth comes from utilisation ramp and the job-work-to-direct-export transition, not underlying market expansion.
- **Years to saturate SAM at current growth:** At a ~30% company CAGR against a 0.81% current SAM share, SAM saturation is not a near-term constraint — **capacity is the binding constraint, not market size** (Section 3C). Practically, the 24,300 MTPA base saturates (at 85% utilisation) at ~5 years under the SOM trajectory modelled here; a further multi-decade runway theoretically exists in the SAM itself but is inaccessible without new capex that is not currently guided.

### 5C — Runway classification: **STRONG**

Not classified MASSIVE despite the nominal 123.6x SAM headroom, because that headroom is not capacity-achievable within the 3-5 year horizon this stage is built to inform — the operative, capacity-bound growth path is a ~3.6x revenue increase over 5 years at a ~30% CAGR, which is a strong, credible, execution-driven runway, not an open-ended one. This reading follows directly from the CLAUDE.md/prompt principle that TAM ≠ SAM ≠ achievable runway — treating the raw SAM headroom as the runway classification would be exactly the kind of management-style TAM-inflation this analysis is built to avoid.
