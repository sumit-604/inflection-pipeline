## NEW FINDING 1 — 🔴 CONSOLIDATED PAT ESSENTIALLY FLAT/DOWN YoY WHILE STANDALONE PAT GREW 22.3%, ENTIRELY EXPLAINED BY PRAGATI FINSERV SWINGING FROM PROFIT TO A LARGE LOSS

This was explicitly flagged as a gap in Pass 1 ("no loss-making-subsidiary quantification in standalone notes... flagged for Pass 2/3") and is the single most important finding missed in Pass 1.

- **Consolidated Profit for the year (total, before NCI split)**: ₹301.32 Cr (30,131.81L) FY25 vs ₹317.69 Cr (31,769.27L) FY24 — **DOWN 5.2% YoY**, even as standalone PAT rose +22.3% to ₹342.62 Cr. (Consolidated P&L statement, PDF p.353-354, line ref "Profit for the year (A)")
- **Profit attributable to Owners of the holding company**: ₹304.55 Cr (30,454.79L) FY25 vs ₹308.33 Cr (30,833.39L) FY24 — down 1.2% YoY. Standalone PAT (₹342.62 Cr) exceeds consolidated owners'-share PAT (₹304.55 Cr) by ~₹38 Cr — the gap is consolidation elimination (intercompany dividend/fee income from loss-making subsidiaries eliminated on consolidation) plus net subsidiary losses.
- **Pragati Finserv Private Limited (90.10%-owned rural/microfinance subsidiary) — profit-to-loss swing**: Group's share of Pragati's result went from **+₹19.55 Cr (1,954.63L) profit in FY24 (6.15% of consolidated profit)** to **−₹29.39 Cr (2,939.40L) LOSS in FY25 (−9.76% of consolidated profit)** — a swing of **−₹48.94 Cr** in one year. Pragati's net assets (Group's share) collapsed from ₹34.65 Cr (3,465.00L) to **₹4.96 Cr (496.47L)**, an 85.7% net-worth erosion. Pragati's total comprehensive income share went from +₹20.31 Cr to **−₹29.68 Cr**. (Note 22, "Additional information as required under Schedule III... of entities consolidated," PDF p.353)
- This directly undermines the reasonableness of the goodwill impairment test cited in Pass 1 (Note 81, Pragati CGU, 30% discount rate/4% terminal growth, recoverable amount ₹55.20 Cr vs ₹20.85 Cr goodwill carrying value) — that test's projected cash flows should be revisited against an entity that just posted a large loss and 85.7% net-worth erosion in the current year. Not flagged by the Company as a triggering event in the notes.
- **Northern Arc Investment Managers Pvt Ltd (NAIM, subsidiary)** also swung to loss: −₹5.13 Cr (−512.90L, −1.70% of consolidated profit) FY25 vs +₹7.18 Cr (718.00L, +2.26%) FY24 — swing of −₹12.31 Cr.
- **Non-controlling interest (NCI) profit share** swung from +₹9.36 Cr (935.88L) FY24 to **−₹3.23 Cr (−322.98L)** FY25 — consistent with, and driven by, the Pragati loss (9.9% NCI in Pragati).
- **IFMR Fimpact Long Term Credit Fund**, a one-time associate-fund contributor of +₹6.71 Cr (671.35L, 2.11% of consolidated profit) in FY24 (held only 22-Nov-2023 to 12-Jan-2024, i.e., a short-window exit gain), is **entirely absent from the FY25 table** — a non-recurring FY24 profit source that did not repeat, a headwind to FY25 vs FY24 comparability not mentioned in Pass 1.
- **Associate-level losses continue**: Finreach Solutions Pvt Ltd posted a loss of −₹8.59 Cr (−859.13L) FY25 vs −₹8.71 Cr (−871.24L) FY24 (roughly flat, chronically loss-making, consistent with the post-year-end stake reduction from 24.55% to 11.16% already flagged in Pass 1). Group's "Share of loss from associates" (combined Finreach + Northern Arc Emerging Corporates Bond Fund line in the consolidated P&L) worsened to −₹2.02 Cr (−201.96L) FY25 from −₹0.97 Cr (−97.21L) FY24.

## NEW FINDING 2 — 🟡 ECL COVERAGE ON *PERFORMING* (STAGE 1 AND STAGE 2) LOANS ROSE SHARPLY, NOT JUST STAGE 3 — A BROADER MODEL/RISK-APPETITE SHIFT THAN PASS 1 CAPTURED

Pass 1 covered the Stage 3/GNPA doubling and the aggregate ₹378.53 Cr P&L impairment charge, but the notes show the provisioning build was much broader than just stage-3 migration:

- **Stage 1 (performing, current/1-30 DPD) provision**: ₹160.52 Cr (16,051.79L) FY25 vs ₹53.66 Cr (5,365.90L) FY24 — **+199% YoY**, against Stage 1 gross loans growth of only +13.5% (₹10,492.52 Cr vs ₹9,244.28 Cr). Implied Stage 1 coverage ratio: **1.53% FY25 vs 0.58% FY24** — nearly tripled.
- **Stage 2 (watchlist) provision**: ₹43.50 Cr (4,350.30L) FY25 vs ₹11.42 Cr (1,141.93L) FY24 — +281% YoY, against Stage 2 gross loans growth of +96.1% (₹239.20 Cr vs ₹121.996 Cr). Implied Stage 2 coverage ratio: **18.19% FY25 vs 9.36% FY24** — roughly doubled.
- Combined with the already-flagged Stage 3 coverage compression (~82-84% to ~68%), this shows the Company built provisions conservatively on the *performing* book even as Stage 3 coverage nominally loosened — read together this looks like a genuine PD/macro-overlay recalibration across the whole book (a materially more conservative FY25 ECL model), not merely mechanical Stage 3 catch-up. Worth weighing against Pass 1's flag #7 (flat 65% LGD, "not empirically modelled") — the Stage 1/2 coverage build is a mitigating, not aggravating, signal on overall provisioning conservatism. (Note 36(i)A, ECL stage-wise provision table and gross/ECL rollforward, PDF p.215-216)
🟡 (new; nuances/partially offsets Pass 1 finding #7)

## NEW FINDING 3 — 🟡 LEVEL 3 FAIR VALUE OF THE AMORTISED-COST LOAN BOOK WAS MATERIALLY BELOW CARRYING VALUE, WITH THE DISCOUNT NARROWING SHARPLY YoY

Not covered in Pass 1 at all. Note 35(A) fair-value-hierarchy disclosure for financial instruments not measured at fair value shows:
- **FY25**: Loans carrying value ₹8,661.21 Cr (8,66,120.89L) vs disclosed Level 3 fair value **₹7,980.05 Cr (7,98,004.71L)** — fair value is **92.1%** of carrying value (7.9% discount, ₹681.16 Cr gap).
- **FY24**: Loans carrying value ₹6,801.47 Cr (6,80,147.43L) vs Level 3 fair value **₹5,152.11 Cr (5,15,211.25L)** — fair value was only **75.7%** of carrying value (24.3% discount, ₹1,649.36 Cr gap).
- The discount rate used for this DCF-based fair value (unobservable, Level 3) is not separately disclosed in the note, but the sharp narrowing of the fair-value-to-carrying-value gap (24.3% discount → 7.9% discount) in one year is a large swing that merits a question to management: is it a discount-rate assumption change (e.g., reflecting the post-IPO reduction in cost of funds/risk premium — consistent with the gearing de-leveraging and ratings context already in Pass 1) or a genuine credit-quality-driven re-rating of the loan book's economic value? Either explanation is plausible and non-alarming, but the magnitude (nearly 3x reduction in the discount) is unusual and undisclosed. (Note 35(A), PDF p.209-210)
🟡 (new; question for management)

## PASS 2 NEW FINDINGS SUMMARY

The most material miss from Pass 1 is **Finding 1**: standalone PAT growth of +22.3% masks a consolidated picture where total Group PAT fell 5.2% (owners'-share PAT fell 1.2%), driven almost entirely by Pragati Finserv's swing from a ₹19.55 Cr profit to a ₹29.39 Cr loss (an 85.7% net-worth erosion at that subsidiary), with a secondary contribution from Northern Arc Investment Managers also swinging to loss and a one-off FY24 fund-exit gain not repeating. This should be elevated to Pass 3/consolidated-analysis prominence alongside the FLDG and complaints findings already ranked top-3 in Pass 1, and should inform the goodwill-impairment-headroom read (Pragati CGU) and any consolidated-vs-standalone earnings-quality commentary downstream.

Secondary new findings of note: broader Stage 1/Stage 2 ECL coverage build (Finding 2, a mitigant to Pass 1's LGD-conservatism flag); a large and unexplained narrowing in the Level 3 fair-value discount on the loan book (Finding 3); cash-flow-hedge-reserve deterioration (Finding 4); a strongly positive liquidity signal in undrawn facilities alongside a less-clear near-doubling of pledged margin money (Finding 5); unexplained "other receivables" spike and originator-partner-advance runoff (Finding 6); fast-growing real estate sector exposure (Finding 7); the investment book's first-ever Stage 3 classification (Finding 8); a fraud disclosure that triangulates with the already-flagged complaints/attrition operational-strain theme (Finding 9); and a "Staff Interaction/Collection related" complaints category that directly implicates collections conduct, subject to a table-labelling ambiguity flagged for verification (Finding 10).
