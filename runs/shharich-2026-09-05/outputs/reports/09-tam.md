## SECTION 1: MARKET DEFINITION

### 1A Precise boundaries

- **Product scope**: H-Acid (1-amino-8-naphthol-3,6-disulfonic acid), the
  dye-intermediate compound itself, manufactured or bought-and-resold.
  Excludes: finished reactive/acid/direct dyes (SHHARICH lists these as
  end-use applications of H-Acid, not products it makes — AR FY26 p.10);
  by-products (gypsum, iron sludge, globular salt) sold to the cement
  industry, a wholly different demand pool (AR FY26 p.8); any future
  Shakambhari Dyechem Pvt Ltd (SDPL) product line, since SDPL has zero
  turnover and no disclosed product mix (AOC-1, AR FY26 p.74) and its
  eventual chemistry is unknown.
- **Geographic scope**: global. H-Acid is a globally traded commodity
  intermediate; SHHARICH itself sold 53.6% domestically and 46.4% via
  export in FY26 (Note 35.9, AR FY26 p.130), and the AR states exports
  reach "4 countries" without naming them (AR FY26 p.5).
- **Customer scope**: dye and dyestuff manufacturers (reactive, acid and
  direct dye makers) who consume H-Acid as a key input. This is close to
  the entire demand base for H-Acid — it has no other significant
  industrial use identified in the corpus or in web search.
- **Channel scope**: direct sale and sale through agents, both in India
  and overseas (AR FY26 p.5, "in India/overseas directly and through
  agents"). No e-commerce or large third-party distributor network is
  described.
- **Price segment**: undifferentiated commodity. B04 already tags
  SHHARICH's pricing power as "price-taker" (B04-bizmodel.yaml); no
  purity/brand premium segment is evidenced anywhere in the AR or in web
  search.
- **Inclusions**: H-Acid manufacture (Note 27, "Sale of Manufactured
  Goods" less the by-product sub-line = Rs 144.76 cr, AR FY26 p.122/p.126)
  and H-Acid trading/resale (Note 27 "Sale of Traded Goods" Rs 33.50 cr,
  AR FY26 p.122, matched by Note 30 "Purchase of Stock-in-Trade — H-Acid"
  Rs 32.99 cr, AR FY26 p.123, confirming the traded line is H-Acid itself
  bought and resold, not a different product).
- **Exclusions**: by-product cement-industry sales (Rs 2.26 cr, immaterial
  to this market but a different market entirely); "other operating
  revenue" (lifting charges, scrap, subsidy, export benefit — Rs 3.98 cr,
  Note 27, AR FY26 p.122), which is incentive/ancillary income, not H-Acid
  market revenue; the MOA-broadening object clause into cranes/EPC/
  infrastructure (AGM Notice Item 15, AR FY26 pp.17-19) is entirely
  outside this market and is not sized here.

**Own-revenue figure used**: per the run brief's instruction, this report
uses **Rs 147.02 cr** ("Sale of Manufactured Goods," Note 27, AR FY26
p.122 = Rs 14,702.27 lakh) as SHHARICH's own H-Acid-market revenue
wherever a ratio calls for it. This includes Rs 2.26 cr of by-product
sales (1.5% of the figure, immaterial to the ratios) and excludes the
Rs 33.50 cr traded-goods line and the Rs 3.98 cr "other operating
revenue" line. Where the tonnage cross-check in Section 3C needs the
purest H-Acid-only figure, Rs 144.76 cr (manufactured goods less
by-products) is used and flagged as such.

### Method 4 — Import substitution / anti-dumping

No H-Acid-specific anti-dumping duty was found in India. DGTR does carry
an active anti-dumping case on **4,4'-Diamino Stilbene 2,2'-Disulphonic
Acid (DASDA)** from China (dgtr.gov.in case listing, via WebSearch
2026-09-05) — DASDA is a related but chemically distinct dye
intermediate (used mainly for direct dyes/optical brighteners, not the
reactive-dye chain H-Acid feeds). This is **not** a tariff protection for
H-Acid and should not be read as one. The AR's own MD&A instead names
Chinese price competition as a live **threat**, not a protected
opportunity: "Price competition from Chinese manufacturers remains a
significant threat, especially for export-oriented units" (AR FY26 p.76).
Import substitution is therefore currently running in **reverse** for
this specific chemical — the opposite of the standard "import
substitution as tailwind" pattern this method usually finds.

### Method 5 — Global benchmark (price cross-check)

234,500 tonnes (2025 global volume, Method 1) x USD 4,724/MT (China spot,
September 2025, chemanalyst.com, via WebSearch) = **USD 1,107.8 million**
= **Rs 10,524 cr** — 61% higher than the Method 1 aggregator-value
estimate of USD 689.9 million. This is a **material divergence between
methods**, not averaged away per the pipeline's own instruction. Two
explanations are plausible and neither can be confirmed without primary
report access (blocked): (a) the aggregator "market value" reports use a
lower blended annual-average price that predates the 2025 China price
recovery, or (b) the 234,500-tonne "market" figure double-counts captive
H-Acid that integrated Chinese/Indian dye producers consume in-house and
never sell at merchant prices, while the price benchmark reflects only
merchant/export-grade transactions. **Realistic global TAM taken as the
midpoint of the two methods: (USD 689.9M + USD 1,107.8M)/2 = USD 898.9M x
Rs 95 = Rs 8,539 cr.**

## SECTION 3: SAM & SOM

### 3A SAM

TAM (Section 1A) is already product-scoped to H-Acid only, so the
**product filter is 100% retained** at the SAM stage (no further cut).

| Filter | Retained % | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Product fit | 100% | TAM already scoped to H-Acid only in Section 1A |
| Geography | ≈25% (**analyst inference**, not a sourced statistic) | India's collective share of global H-Acid capacity is not disclosed as a single number, but corroborating data points anchor a working estimate: Kiri Industries alone holds >15% of global capacity (WebSearch, 2026-09-05); "India and China together account for over 75% of Asia-Pacific's H-Acid production capacity" (WebSearch, 2026-09-05), and Asia-Pacific is understood to hold the overwhelming majority of global H-Acid supply given the near-total absence of production elsewhere. Several further sizable Indian producers (AksharChem, Bodal, Atul, Shree Pushkar, SHHARICH) sit alongside Kiri. 25% is used as a working mid-point of a plausible 20-30% Indian-industry share range; it is NOT independently sourced as a single figure and should be treated as the weakest link in this section |
| Channel | 90% | SHHARICH sells only direct + through agents (AR FY26 p.5), with no broader distributor/e-commerce network; a 10% cut is applied for this constraint, though B2B bulk-chemical sales in this industry are typically direct/agent-based anyway, so the cut is modest |
| Customer | 100% | H-Acid's demand base is already almost entirely dye/dyestuff manufacturers; no further customer-type cut applies |

Filtered SAM (top-down cross-check) = Rs 6,554 cr (conservative global
TAM) x 25% x 90% x 100% = **Rs 1,475 cr**.

This converges reasonably well with the **Method 3 bottom-up peer
aggregation (Rs 1,183 - 1,540 cr, Section 2)** — two independently built
figures landing in the same broad range is the strongest piece of
triangulation this report can offer, though both ultimately rest on the
same shaky underlying inputs (peer capacity disclosures of uneven vintage
and an unsourced geography-share inference).

**CONSERVATIVE BIAS applied: SAM = Rs 1,183 cr** (the lower, more
directly evidenced Method-3 named-player sum, before the unorganised
uplift).

**SAM as % of conservative global TAM: 1,183 / 6,554 = 18.1%.**

### 3B SOM at 3 and 5 years

**Current share of SAM**: Rs 147.02 cr / Rs 1,183 cr = **12.43%**.

**Share-gain assumption**: the standard rules give a "1-2pp in 3 years
normal" band. This report takes the **low end of, and in fact below, the
normal band** for three evidenced reasons: (1) B07 scores SHHARICH's
forward pipeline 8/92 (em_classification NONE) with a combined_assessment
of AVOID, citing SDPL's 2+ year stall at nil turnover and a D-grade
execution-credibility record (B07-emoat.yaml); (2) FY26's own organic
manufactured H-Acid growth was only ~4.79% (Rs 144.76 cr vs Rs 138.14 cr
prior year — computed from Note 35.2 and Note 27, AR FY26 pp.122/126),
which is close to, not clearly ahead of, the global H-Acid demand CAGR of
3.57% used below, and this modest growth arrived alongside an EBITDA
**decline** (Rs 1,147.66 lakh to Rs 980.64 lakh, MD&A p.77 — B04's
FLAG-NEGATIVE-OPERATING-LEVERAGE), meaning the growth was not
margin-accretive; (3) no capacity-addition tonnage is disclosed for
either warrant-funded expansion object or for SDPL (B07
capex_embedded_growth_pct: NOT FOUND), so there is no physical basis for
assuming even a "normal" share gain. **Assumption used: +0.5pp by year 3,
+1.0pp by year 5** (12.43% -> 12.93% -> 13.43%).

**TAM/SAM forward growth rate used: 3.57%/year**, the **demand-side**
global CAGR (Method 1), deliberately **not** the faster ~8.5%/year
India-capacity-growth figure that also surfaced in search (CARE-sourced,
via WebSearch 2026-09-05, "India's H acid capacity has been expanding at
approximately 8.5% annually since 2021"). Using the capacity-growth
figure would overstate revenue opportunity: more capacity without matched
demand growth compresses price/spread, which is exactly what B04 already
evidences in FY26 (revenue up, EBITDA down). This is an explicit
analytical choice, shown for transparency.

- SAM at year 3 = Rs 1,183 cr x (1.0357)^3 = **Rs 1,314 cr**
- SAM at year 5 = Rs 1,183 cr x (1.0357)^5 = **Rs 1,410 cr**
- **SOM year 3 = 12.93% x Rs 1,314 cr = Rs 170 cr**
- **SOM year 5 = 13.43% x Rs 1,410 cr = Rs 189 cr**

**Implied revenue CAGR** (arithmetic, from Rs 147.02 cr current base):
- Year 3: (170/147.02)^(1/3) - 1 = **4.9%**
- Year 5: (189/147.02)^(1/5) - 1 = **5.2%**

This is the **formal handoff to Stage 11**: the market-sizing-implied
revenue growth ceiling sits at roughly **5% CAGR**, far below the
strategy's 25% CAGR transition-alpha hurdle. If Stage 11/FTTCP assumes
revenue growth materially above ~5%, that assumption is not supported by
this market-sizing exercise and must rest on share-gain evidence (proof
of the transition thesis) that Sections above do not find, or on a TAM
growth rate faster than the demand-side figure used here.

### 3C Capacity cross-check

`B07.capex_embedded_growth_pct` = **NOT FOUND** (B07-emoat.yaml,
FLAG-EMOAT-CAPACITY-GAP: "No tonnage, MTPA, or capacity-addition
percentage is disclosed anywhere in the AR for either warrant-funded
expansion object or the SDPL facility"). This alone means Section 3C
**cannot be closed to a clean "sufficient" or "gap of ₹X cr"** — there is
no disclosed physical-capacity figure to check the SOM revenue against.

An analyst-built cross-check (not a disclosed fact, shown as arithmetic)
narrows the gap partially. Using the same price range as Method 3/5
(Rs 2.857 - 4.488 lakh/tonne):
- FY26 pure manufactured H-Acid revenue (Rs 144.76 cr, excl. by-products)
  implies **3,225 - 5,067 tonnes** of actual FY26 production.
- The AR's stated "270 TONNES production capacity" (AR FY26 p.5, period
  unstated) is inconsistent with this if read as an *annual* figure
  (production cannot exceed 100% of annual capacity, and 270 tonnes/year
  is 12-19x smaller than the implied production). It is much more
  internally consistent read as a **monthly** figure: 270 t/month x 12 =
  3,240 t/year, which sits almost exactly at the high-price end of the
  implied-production range (3,225 tonnes). This is the report's own
  inference, offered as a plausible reading, not a confirmed fact — B07
  and B04 both leave the period genuinely unstated and this run does not
  resolve it either. **This ambiguity itself should be a named
  verification target at Halt 1.**
- SOM year 3 (Rs 170 cr, using the 100%-H-Acid Rs 147.02 cr-based
  proportion, i.e. roughly Rs 167 cr of pure H-Acid) implies **~3,730 -
  5,850 tonnes**, i.e. **roughly +500 to +800 tonnes/year** of
  incremental production above the FY26 implied run-rate, regardless of
  which end of the price range is used (the percentage increase, ~15.6%,
  is price-independent).

**capacity_check conclusion**: gap **not quantifiable in ₹ Cr** because
no capacity-addition tonnage exists to price against (B07
capex_embedded_growth_pct NOT FOUND). In tonnage terms, the SOM
year-3 assumption requires roughly 500-800 tonnes/year of incremental
output with **no disclosed capacity-addition figure behind either the
Rs 10.24 cr direct-expansion warrant object or SDPL** (AGM Notice Item
16, AR FY26 p.45; AOC-1, AR FY26 p.74). **The SOM is the optimistic side
of this check**: it assumes execution the company has not yet
demonstrated (B07: em_score 8/92, combined_assessment AVOID, SDPL nil
turnover for 2+ years past an undated "near term" Chairman's claim).

### 5B Runway assessment

- **Revenue headroom**: SAM / current revenue = Rs 1,183 cr / Rs 147.02
  cr = **8.05x**.
- **TAM growth rate**: 3.57%/year (demand-side, Method 1; the
  supply-side India-capacity growth figure of ~8.5%/year is deliberately
  not used here, see Section 3B).
- **Company CAGR vs TAM**: FY26 organic manufactured growth (~4.79%)
  marginally exceeds the 3.57% demand-side TAM growth rate, but arrived
  with an EBITDA **decline** (B04 FLAG-NEGATIVE-OPERATING-LEVERAGE) —
  this is volume/trading growth, not evidenced margin-accretive
  share-gain. The company is at best **riding the market**, not
  demonstrably **gaining share** in a value-accretive way.
- **Years to saturate SAM at current growth**: at ~4.79% company growth
