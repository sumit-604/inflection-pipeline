Micro enterprise lender growth outpacing earnings quality

Aye Finance lends small sums to India's smallest businesses. It serves about 6.7 lakh micro enterprises through 571 branches across 18 states and three union territories, with an average loan of Rs 1.5 to 4.9 lakh. It underwrites shopkeepers and workshop owners who carry no formal accounts by reading 70 plus business clusters built over 12 years, then earns the spread between a roughly 10 to 11% cost of borrowing and a 22% lending yield. It listed in February 2026 and has no identifiable promoter: the register is held by private equity and venture backers, Elevation Capital at 12.6% and LGT at 10.1%, run by founder MD Sanjay Sharma.

FY26 revenue was Rs 1,815 Cr and PAT Rs 194 Cr, on a loan book near Rs 7,300 Cr. AUM grew about 26%, short of the 29 to 30% management guided for the year. The shares trade at Rs 183, about 19 times trailing earnings and 1.83 times book.

The transition is a credit cost recovery, not a new business line. Gross Stage III bad loans rose from 3.2% in March 2024 to a peak 4.85% in September 2025, then eased for two quarters to 4.49% by June 2026. Return on equity fell from 17.3% in FY24 to 7.6% annualised in the first half of FY26 as credit cost roughly doubled to above 5%, then began recovering: FY26 came in at 9.3% and the June 2026 quarter annualised near 16% on the post IPO capital base. The February 2026 IPO put CRAR at 42%, so the balance sheet has years of growth capital before gearing bites.

Reported profit leans on a line that is not pure lending. Gain on derecognition, the upfront profit Aye books when it sells seasoned loan pools to banks, rose from 1.9% of income in FY23 to 3.65% in FY26 and is still climbing. Add treasury and fair value gains and roughly 9% of FY26 income sits outside recurring spread. Cash flow from operations is negative every year, which is normal for a lender funding its book, but the earnings quality question inside it stays open: the pipeline could not resolve normalized profit stripped of the securitisation gain, and that keeps the gate at proceed with caveats rather than clean.

Management does about half of what it says, cleanly. It missed the headline AUM growth number but delivered the asset quality and profit improvement it promised for the March 2026 quarter, including a Rs 86 Cr fourth quarter profit that doubled the prior quarter. Governance reads trustworthy: eight clean checks, two cautions, no red. The cautions are real. The register restated profit in four of five reported periods, cutting FY23 profit 25.9%, with no risk factor written for it. Covenant breaches touched 23.6% of borrowings at September 2025, most unwaived, and the prospectus described them only as technical delays. The CFO seat changed twice in four months around listing.

Peers complicate the story rather than confirm it. On the closest analogue to Aye's Bihar exposure, the Karnataka MFI ordinance, SBFC and Northern Arc report collection damage that ran deeper and less contained than Aye's minimal spillover framing implies, and SBFC says the stress tracked ticket size, not geography. SBFC also flags that borrower level over leverage across multiple lenders stays invisible to bureau checks, which matters for a book where 99% of FY26 growth came from repeat borrowers and deepening existing branches. On the one point Aye leans on, sub 2% mortgage credit cost, SBFC's comparable secured book carries higher loss and recovery timelines almost twice its book average. The funding tailwind does check out: all three peers report cost of borrowing falling over the same window.

The runway is genuine and large. The addressable micro enterprise credit market runs Rs 4.1 lakh crore on a disciplined read and higher on looser ones, against Aye's roughly Rs 7,300 Cr book, so headroom is not the constraint. The framework disciplined three year revenue path implied by realistic share gains is about 18% a year, well below management's own high 20s aspiration, so the debate is pace, not direction. Management's own Rs 34 lakh crore market claim runs about 8 times the disciplined figure and reads inflated.

On price the stock is fair to rich, and the call is watch, not buy. The primary lender lens, price to book against cost of equity, puts fair value at Rs 81 to 90 versus 1.83 times book today, so the no growth floor sits near the since listing low of Rs 88. The exit multiple work runs two tracks: the operator approved destination is 15 times forward earnings, which the additive pillar build supports at 15.4 to 16 times and which sets a base fair value of Rs 324 and an entry ceiling of Rs 166; the mechanical required return track sits at 10.4 times, about 31% lower, and on that track the stock is a sell at today's price. The hurdle clears only on the growth capped bull case, so it is conditional, and probability weighted three year return at Rs 183 is 19.7%, under the 25% bar. The plan is to accumulate Rs 133 to 166, margin of safety at Rs 133, a small 2 to 3% position; the devil's advocate case survives weakened, its sharpest point being that the entry rests on the most generous of three lenses while the primary lens and the required return track both point to Rs 86 to 115. The single print that breaks the thesis is Gross Stage III turning back up from 4.49% in the next reported quarter, because asset quality, the return recovery and the forward earnings all turn on that one number.
