## SECTION 1: MARKET DEFINITION

### 1A — Precise boundaries

MPS is NOT a publisher, NOT an LMS-software vendor, and NOT a generic BPO. It sells three distinct outsourced knowledge-services lines. The market definition must sit at the SERVICE-LAYER MPS actually invoices for, not at the size of the end-markets its customers operate in (publishing, education, corporate training, healthcare). Getting this wrong is exactly the "TAM=SAM" dishonesty the framework warns against, and management's own claim (Section 1B) makes precisely that error.

- **Product scope**: (i) Research Solutions — journal/book production and copyediting/typesetting/XML, platform hosting (HighWire-class), peer-review/integrity screening tooling, author services (AJE); (ii) Education Solutions — K-12/higher-ed content development, assessment/test-item authoring, accessibility remediation, and — new in FY26 — Unbound Medicine's point-of-care clinical/nursing reference subscription; (iii) Corporate Learning — managed/outsourced corporate L&D content design and delivery, including AI-led/immersive learning (Liberate Global, BridgeAI).
- **Geographic scope**: Global demand, India-and-Tier-2-city delivery. FY26/Q1 FY27 revenue mix: North America 49-52%, Europe 29-33%, Rest of World 15-20% (Investor Presentation, Q1 FY27, slide 11; AR FY25-26). India itself is a DELIVERY location, not a demand market, for this business — there is no separate "India TAM" to size; the Section 4 India note below records this explicitly instead of forcing an India split the business model does not have.
- **Customer scope**: STM/scholarly publishers, university presses, ed-tech firms, K-12/higher-ed institutions, hospitals/nursing/healthcare institutions (new, via Unbound Medicine), and corporate/government L&D buyers. Excludes individual consumers, solo/self-published authors (AJE serves individual researchers but as a paid author service, not a mass-consumer product), and small school districts that buy direct-to-consumer edtech apps.
- **Channel scope**: Direct enterprise/institutional contracts (B2B), increasingly platform/SaaS-hosted (DigiCorePro, THINK365) and, via Unbound Medicine, a direct institutional-subscription model. No retail/reseller channel.
- **Price segment**: Premium/specialised knowledge-services, not price-competitive commodity BPO — revenue per employee ~₹22.9 lakh/year (₹768.37 Cr FY26 revenue ÷ ~3,352 employees, Investor Presentation Q1 FY27 slide 5 headcount base), consistent with skilled-services pricing, not lowest-cost seat-based BPO.
- **Explicit exclusions**: MPS does not own IP/journals it services, does not sell LMS software or hardware, does not do general-purpose translation/localization outside its knowledge niche, and does not compete for pure seat-based data-entry BPO work.

## SECTION 2: TAM ESTIMATION, MULTIPLE METHODS

### Method 1 — Top-down, per segment (service-layer market reports)

**Research Solutions.** Two comparable reports exist at very different scope:
- Total STM (Scientific/Technical/Medical) Publishing market (includes publisher-retained subscription/APC revenue, not addressable by a services vendor): **USD 30.2 bn (2025)**, MarketIntelo, verified June 2026. A second source: USD 34.19 bn (2026), businessresearchinsights.com.
- Publication Support Services market (the vendor-services layer — copyediting, typesetting, XML conversion, editorial workflow — MPS's actual business): **USD 9.48 bn (2025) → USD 15.0 bn by 2035, 4.7% CAGR**, wiseguyreports.com.
- Conservative bias: use the narrower, more accurate figure → **Research TAM (conservative) = USD 9.48 bn**. Realistic estimate widens toward the total-industry ceiling to capture MPS's growing direct-to-researcher (AJE) and platform-hosting SaaS revenue, which sit outside pure back-office production: **Research TAM (realistic) = USD 15.0 bn** (WiseGuyReports' own 2035 figure, used here as a present-scope proxy for the wider addressable slice).

**Education Solutions (incl. Unbound Medicine).**
- Assessment/testing content services: **Global Assessment Services Market, USD 12.03 bn (2025) → USD 25.24 bn by 2033, 9.7% CAGR**, SkyQuest.
- Healthcare knowledge (Unbound Medicine's actual product category — point-of-care clinical/drug reference, NOT the broad "AI in healthcare" category management cites): **Medical Reference Apps Market, USD 1.34 bn (2024) → USD 3.10 bn by 2030, 14.9% CAGR**, Grand View Research. A second estimate: USD 3.5 bn (~2025), Verified Market Reports/InsightAce Analytic → USD 8 bn by 2033, 12.5% CAGR.

(A separate, much larger "global corporate e-learning market" range of USD 129.5 bn–530.83 bn also turned up in search — this reflects the TOTAL corporate-training-spend category, including trainer salaries, facilities and in-person delivery that a managed-services vendor like Liberate Global cannot capture; excluded from the TAM for that reason, and flagged as the likely source of management's USD 353 bn "corporate e-learning" claim.)

### Method 2 — Bottom-up (Research Solutions sanity check)

Addressable unit: a scholarly article requiring production services. Global output: **~3.5 million science & engineering articles in 2024** (AR FY25-26 MD&A, p.27, citing global research-output data). Per-article production cost found via web search (F1000Research industry analysis, and multiple editing-service benchmarks): typesetting/XML conversion **USD 100-180/article**; full editorial service (copyediting, formatting) **USD 150-1,000+/article** depending on depth.

- Low case (typesetting only, USD 150/article): 3.5m × $150 = **USD 0.525 bn/year**.
- High case (fuller editorial service, USD 600/article midpoint): 3.5m × $600 = **USD 2.10 bn/year**.

This bottom-up range (USD 0.5-2.1 bn) sits well BELOW the Method 1 top-down figure (USD 9.48 bn). Explained, not averaged: the per-article unit model only prices NEW-article production flow. It structurally excludes book/monograph production (MPS won a 900-title multi-year scholarly-books mandate in FY26, AR p.28), platform-hosting SaaS fees, direct-to-author paid services (AJE), and compliance/integrity-screening subscriptions — all real MPS revenue lines outside the per-article model. **Conclusion: bottom-up is not used as the TAM number (it understates by construction), but it confirms the top-down USD 9.48 bn figure is not absurd — it is 4.5-19x the narrowest unit-cost floor, a normal multiple for a market that also monetises hosting, subscriptions and author services on top of raw production. This is the opposite conclusion from management's USD 800 bn claim, which is 380-1,600x the same unit-cost floor.**

## SECTION 3: SAM & SOM

### 3A — SAM (five filters applied to conservative TAM, USD 26.48 bn / ₹249,706 Cr)

The Method-1 TAM was already constructed at the narrow service-layer, so most of the "product fit" filtering is built in. Remaining filters:

| Filter | Retention | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Product fit | 90% | Minor residual overlap loss where "Managed Learning Services"/"Assessment Services" reports bundle some software-licensing revenue MPS does not sell |
| Geography | 95% | MPS already has genuine 9-country, 3-continent reach (AR "Leaving a Global Footprint," p.24-25); minor gaps in LatAm/Middle East corporate-learning delivery |
| Channel | 95% | Nearly all of the narrow TAM is already B2B/enterprise-channel; small consumer-facing slice (e.g. some medical-reference-app retail subscriptions) excluded |
| Customer | 70% | Largest haircut: much of Assessment Services and Medical Reference Apps demand is served direct-to-small-school-district or direct-to-consumer, which MPS's enterprise/publisher-channel model does not reach |
| Capability | 80% | ~3,352 employees / 15 delivery centres (Investor Presentation, AR "Leaving a Global Footprint") cannot simultaneously serve every large publisher/enterprise/hospital system at once; capacity is real even though it is people-based, not capex-based |

Compound retention = 0.90 × 0.95 × 0.95 × 0.70 × 0.80 = **0.455 (45.5%)**

**SAM = 0.455 × USD 26.48 bn = USD 12.05 bn = ₹113,617 Cr (45.5% of TAM).**

### 3B — SOM at 3 and 5 years

**Current SAM share**: MPS FY26 revenue ₹768.37 Cr ÷ SAM ₹113,617 Cr = **0.68%** (computed). Management self-reports "~0.5% serviceable-market share" (task-brief concall citation). The two are the same order of magnitude — a rare instance where an independently-built SAM roughly cross-checks a management self-report, even though the underlying TAM claim (Section 2) does not. Blended baseline used going forward: **~0.6%**.

Share-gain trajectory, per the framework's rules (1-2pp/3yr normal; 3-5pp/3yr aggressive with capacity+execution; >5pp only on competitor exit/acquisition), cross-checked against management's own runway narrative (0.5%→1%→2%):

- **Year 3 target share: 1.0%** — a +0.4pp move from the 0.6% baseline over 3 years. Falls WITHIN the "normal" 1-2pp band; not even aggressive. Matches management's own near-term "→1%" claim.
- **Year 5 target share: 2.0%** — a further +1.0pp move over years 4-5 (2 years), i.e. ~0.5pp/year sustained. This pace sits at the boundary of the "aggressive" 3-5pp/3yr band and is only credible given MPS's demonstrated M&A cadence of roughly one acquisition per year (AJE FY24, Liberate Global cluster FY25, Unbound Medicine FY26). **Classified AGGRESSIVE/M&A-dependent, not normal.**

SAM itself grows at the blended TAM CAGR. Weighted by conservative TAM $-share: Research (9.48bn @ 4.7%) + Education (13.37bn @ blended 10.22% [Assessment Services 9.7% and Medical Reference Apps 14.9%, weighted]) + Corporate Learning (3.63bn @ 10.3%) = **8.25% blended CAGR** (arithmetic: (9.48×4.7 + 13.37×10.22 + 3.63×10.3) ÷ 26.48 = 218.59 ÷ 26.48 = 8.25%, rounded to 8.2%).

- SAM at year 3 = ₹113,617 Cr × (1.082)³ = ₹113,617 × 1.2672 = **₹143,983 Cr**
- SAM at year 5 = ₹113,617 Cr × (1.082)⁵ = ₹113,617 × 1.4830 = **₹168,504 Cr**

- **SOM year 3 = 1.0% × ₹143,983 Cr = ₹1,440 Cr**
- **SOM year 5 = 2.0% × ₹168,504 Cr = ₹3,370 Cr**

**Implied revenue CAGR, shown arithmetic:**
- Year 3: (1,440 ÷ 768.37)^(1/3) − 1 = (1.8741)^0.333 − 1 = 1.2329 − 1 = **23.3%**
- Year 5: (3,370 ÷ 768.37)^(1/5) − 1 = (4.3841)^0.20 − 1 = 1.3439 − 1 = **34.4%**

The year-5 figure is a CAGR-TO-DATE number, not a marginal year-4/5 growth rate; it is higher than the year-3 figure because the SHARE gain itself accelerates in years 4-5 (0.4pp in years 1-3 vs. 1.0pp in years 4-5) — an analyst artefact worth flagging, not a contradiction (see `analyst_note`).

### 3C — Capacity cross-check

Injected B07 capex-embedded-growth figure = **0%** (asset-light services model; no capacity-expansion capex programme disclosed). This means the standard "can installed + committed capacity physically produce the SOM revenue" question does not apply in its capex form — MPS's growth model is explicitly NOT capex/capacity-bound. Instead:

- Growth to date has come from AI-driven productivity (Q1 FY27 revenue +20.4% YoY vs. headcount +2.7% YoY, per B04) and from acquisitions (AJE, Liberate Global cluster, Unbound Medicine), not from adding floor space or machines.
- **Capacity check: sufficient — with a named caveat.** The SOM's back-half (years 3-5, share 1.0%→2.0%) is not capacity-constrained in the physical sense; it is CONSTRAINED BY M&A APPETITE AND BALANCE-SHEET CAPACITY, since that pace of share gain has historically required roughly one acquisition per year. MPS drew its **first-ever external borrowing (₹40.25 Cr) in FY26** to fund the Unbound Medicine acquisition (per B04), and its current ratio fell from 1.97x to 1.45x over the same year. **The optimistic side of this SOM is therefore the year-5 share target (2.0%), not any capex/capacity plan** — capex_embedded_growth of 0% confirms the physical side is not the constraint; the financing side of continued M&A is.

### 4C — Market structure

- **Fragmented, largely un-mapped.** No named competitors or market-share data exist in either corpus document (confirmed, consistent with B04's flag). The competitive landscape had to be reconstructed from adjacent-sector proxies (Section 2, Method 3).
- **Known scale peer**: Straive/SPi Global (~USD 1-2.5 bn revenue, unaudited estimate) is 12-30x MPS's own USD 81.5mn (₹768.37 Cr) revenue — MPS is a small player even within its own niche.
- **Consolidating, not fragmenting**: AR explicitly states customers are moving toward scaled, full-service partners — a tailwind for an incumbent of MPS's scale, assuming it stays ahead of smaller/local vendors.
- **Shifting toward differentiation, away from pure price competition**: AR MD&A p.26-27 frames the entire investment case around "the rising premium on trust as AI floods the world with content" — i.e., management's own framing is that undifferentiated/price-competed work is exactly what AI is commoditising, and MPS's bet is on the trusted/verified layer above it.

### 5B — Runway assessment

- **Revenue headroom** = SAM ÷ current revenue = ₹113,617 Cr ÷ ₹768.37 Cr = **147.9x**.
- **TAM growth rate** (blended, Section 3B) = **8.2%/year**.
- **Company CAGR vs TAM**: MPS's headline FY26 revenue growth was 5.71% (distorted by a deliberate AJE reset), but organic growth ex-AJE/Unbound was ~13.3% (per B04), and segment-level organic growth ran 17% (Research) to 28.6% (Education) (AR "FY26 at a glance," p.3). Company growth (13-29% organic) is running well ABOVE the 8.2% blended TAM growth rate — **MPS is gaining share, not merely riding the market** — consistent with the SOM trajectory in 3B, though tempered by the Section 4C peer-growth caution.
- **Years to saturate SAM at current growth**: solving 768.37 × (1.20)ⁿ = 113,617 × (1.082)ⁿ using an illustrative ~20% sustained company CAGR (mid-point of the organic segment range) → (1.20/1.082)ⁿ = 147.9 → n = ln(147.9) ÷ ln(1.1091) = 4.997 ÷ 0.1036 ≈ **48 years**. Even at a materially slower sustained pace, saturation remains multi-decade.

### 5C — Runway classification

**MASSIVE.** ~148x revenue headroom and a ~48-year saturation horizon at even an aggressive sustained growth rate is far beyond the STRONG/GOOD thresholds. Importantly, this classification holds using the CONSERVATIVE, heavily-filtered, service-layer-only TAM — it does not require, and should not be confused with, management's inflated USD 800 bn claim. The size of the opportunity is real; management's description of its size is not.

### 5D — SAM expansion levers actually being pursued

- **Healthcare knowledge adjacency (executed, FY26)**: Unbound Medicine extends Education Solutions into the Medical Reference Apps market (USD 1.34-3.5 bn). Potential addition to SAM at even a modest 45.5%-equivalent capture ratio: order of USD 0.6-1.6 bn (₹5,700-15,000 Cr) of incremental addressable opportunity — a real but modest expansion relative to the ₹113,617 Cr base SAM.
- **AI/immersive corporate learning (executed, ongoing)**: Liberate Global's BridgeAI and immersive-learning push targets a growing sub-slice of the Managed Learning Services market rather than expanding its outer boundary; more a share-capture tool within the existing Corporate Learning SAM than a new SAM addition.
- **Geographic mix shift**: Rest-of-World revenue share (15-20% currently, Section 1A) is the most underweighted geography relative to global R&D-output and corporate-training-spend distribution; no specific expansion plan is disclosed, so this is named as a lever not yet actively pursued with disclosed investment, not a quantified addition.

### 5E — Final output card

- **SOM-implied revenue CAGR**: 23.3% (year 3) to 34.4% (year 5, CAGR-to-date, back-half M&A-dependent).
- **Margin trajectory**: FY26 consolidated EBITDA margin 30.7% (AR "Financial Review," p.29), management guidance "comfortably surpass INR 300 Cr of EBITDA in FY27" against a Vision-2027 target of ~₹1,500 Cr revenue by FY28 at "margins similar to 30 percent" (AR "Vision 2027," p.2) — i.e., a broadly STABLE ~30% EBITDA margin trajectory, not a margin-expansion story on top of the volume story.
- **Earnings growth embedded**: at a stable ~30% EBITDA margin and roughly proportionate PAT conversion (FY26 PAT margin 22.5%, Q1 FY27 Investor Presentation slide 5), earnings growth should track revenue growth closely, i.e. **~23-34% PAT CAGR embedded in the SOM**, before any multiple re-rating.
- **Current valuation**: CMP ₹2,635 (per B00-inputs spear-gate line) ÷ FY26 basic EPS ₹102.11 (AR "Financial Review," p.29) = **trailing P/E ≈ 25.8x**.

> **At 23-34% revenue CAGR implied by SOM, with a margin trajectory of ~30% EBITDA / ~22-23% PAT, the earnings growth embedded here is ~23-34% CAGR, which SUPPORTS the current valuation of ~26x P/E** — provided the year-5 end of that range, which depends on sustaining the FY24-26 acquisition cadence (Section 3C), actually materialises. The year-3 end of the range (23% CAGR) is the more conservative, largely-organic-plus-already-announced-M&A figure and is the one that should anchor Stage 11's base case; the year-5 figure (34%) should be treated as an upside case contingent on continued M&A, not a base-case input.
