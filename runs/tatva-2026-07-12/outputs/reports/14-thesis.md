## NARRATIVE (plain English)

Tatva Chintan is a genuinely improving business at an impossible price. Earnings have rebounded hard off an FY25 trough, margins have climbed three quarters running, and the SDA / Euro 7 and Dahej stories give it real near-term triggers. The Amendment 4.5 refresh does the improving business justice: instead of pricing the 6.6% trough return on capital as if it were permanent, the framework now credits the sustainable earning power of this asset, blending the pre-depression 22.45% ROCE the business earned in FY18-21 with the recovering forward numbers, for a 13.51% anchor. That lifts the fair multiple from about nine times to about eleven times, the base three-year fair value from Rs 237 to Rs 287, and the entry zone from Rs 97-121 to Rs 118-147.

None of it changes the answer. The market still pays 73.75 times earnings, and even the corrected framework says those earnings are worth only about nine times on the governing track. That gap, roughly eight to one, swamps any plausible earnings growth. On the base case the stock is worth about Rs 287 in three years against Rs 1,326 today, a 40% annual loss. The Hurdle Ratio says STOP, and three other independent tests, AVERAGE quality, CAUTION promoter, inverted risk-reward, say the same thing.

The refresh matters for one reason: it fixes the re-open zone so the process will not miss a genuine capital-cycle recovery. But it does not turn an overvalued price into a buy, and the anchor is self-withdrawing. If the recovery fails to print, the normalized credit is removed, not grandfathered, and the fair value falls back toward the prior run. The stock will most likely only reach the Rs 118-147 zone by breaking the very thing that would make it worth buying: the cash print turning structurally bad or the ROCE recovery stalling. So the discipline is exact and it is now written into the re-open rule. Watch the cash and the ROCE every quarter, and only re-open the file if the evidence turns good, ROCE reverting toward the pre-depression band and cash conversion turning, not because the price fell.
