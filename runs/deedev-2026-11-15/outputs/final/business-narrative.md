## Business understanding narrative

DEE cuts, bends and welds steel pipe to each customer's drawing in its own plants in India and Thailand. The piping division made 88% of FY26 revenue from pipe spools, induction bends, fittings, pressure vessels and skids. These parts carry high pressure steam and process fluid in power plants and refineries, to a certified specification. A builder cannot switch to an unapproved fabricator, because NTPC, NPCIL, ASME and PED approvals take time to earn. Since March 2026 a 7,000 tonne Anjar plant also makes heavy wall seamless pipe for supercritical coal boilers. The buyers are boiler and turbine makers such as BHEL, L&T, GE, Siemens, Doosan and Thermax, and refiners such as Numaligarh. They bid out each project at a fixed price with no escalation clause and pay on milestones. The top ten customers gave about 80% of FY26 income, against 59% a year earlier. Numaligarh Refinery alone gave 15% of revenue and Dow Chemical Canada 10%. Power plant work is about 65% of the order book, and refinery and chemical projects make most of the rest. Present demand tracks BHEL thermal boiler and pressure part tender awards, Numaligarh Refinery expansion capex, and Dow and chemical sector capex. Forward domestic demand rests on the 80 GW thermal plan to FY32, which CEA thermal project monitoring tracks each quarter. Abroad, the signals are a GE Vernova purchase order for 15 to 16 HRSG units (the boilers behind gas turbines) and Siemens Energy gas turbine backlog. NPCIL and DAE fleet mode reactor awards are an option under a 16 Oct 2026 framework agreement, not an order. Only the piping line has a moat, a narrow one built on vendor approvals and port side scale. The emerging moat scan scored 10.1 against a 12 point line; only seamless integration and HRSG qualification lock-in are active. Categories 21 and 22, talent asymmetry and the cannibalisation barrier, scored zero, because a rival gives up nothing to copy DEE. Heavy fabrication and biomass power, together 12% of revenue, have no moat.

Order book doubled, cash has not followed

DEE Development Engineers listed in June 2024 and has 93,500 tonnes a year of rated piping capacity. FY26 revenue was Rs 1,142 cr, up 38%. EBITDA rose 55% and PAT rose 77% to Rs 77 cr. Exports were 40.5% of FY26 revenue in the filed segment data. On the August call management said exports run above half. The order book stood at Rs 2,586 cr on 31 Oct 2026, more than twice FY26 revenue.

The transition is from a half used fabricator to a larger, power led supplier that makes some of its own pipe. The 30,000 tonne Anjar fabrication plant came on stream in September 2025, on time. The Rs 90 cr seamless plant started on 19 Mar 2026, about two and a half months late. The order book rose from Rs 1,228 cr in March 2025 to Rs 2,531 cr in September 2026. In July 2026 the company raised Rs 300 cr of equity at Rs 502 a share and repaid Rs 225 cr of short term debt. CARE upgraded the long term rating to A Stable on 9 Nov 2026. Management guides FY27 revenue of Rs 1,500 cr plus at an EBITDA margin above 19%, and Rs 2,500 cr by FY30. H1 FY27 delivered Rs 587 cr, so H2 must deliver Rs 913 cr, 55% more than H1. The Q2 operating margin of 20.1%, worked out from the filing, is the first quarter in the guided zone. H1 as a whole was 18.5%. Q2 revenue was Rs 293 cr, below Q1's Rs 294 cr, though management had said Rs 25 cr of Q1 dispatches moved into Q2.

The Gate 0 screen scored 51 of 160 and classified the company AVOID. Leverage set that call: FY26 net debt was 3.5 times EBITDA and interest cover was 2.6 times. Return on capital sat under 10% in every year from FY20 to FY26 on the proxy basis, with a median of 7.7%. On the strict basis FY26 reads 14.9%, because Rs 553 cr of short term debt sits in current liabilities. A verifier ruled that the strict formula applies where inputs exist. That lifts the core score from 39 to 41, and leverage still holds the AVOID classification. The FY26 margin gain came from overheads, not price. Employee cost fell from 19.5% to 16.4% of revenue while gross margin fell from 65.0% to 58.1%. One reading says operating leverage persists and margin reaches 19%. The other says material heavy power jobs keep gross margin low and overheads grow back. H2 FY27 margin and gross profit in rupees separate the two.

FY26 group operating cash flow was Rs 20 cr against PAT of Rs 77 cr and EBITDA of Rs 191 cr. That is 10 paise of cash per rupee of EBITDA. The parent company's operating cash flow was an outflow of Rs 45 cr in FY26 and Rs 154 cr in FY25. Over the two years, free cash flow was negative by about Rs 452 cr. H1 FY27 operating cash flow was an outflow of Rs 7 cr on PBT of Rs 50 cr, against a promise of positive H1 cash. Parent receivables rose 69% while revenue rose 44%. The company also sold Rs 159 cr of receivables without recourse, 3.8 times the FY25 amount. The share overdue beyond six months rose from 5.3% to 9.2%, and no loss allowance is booked. The growth reading: the order book doubled, inventory days fell from 227 to 174, and CARE expects the cycle to reach 190 to 200 days from FY27. The structural reading: the company buys about half of each order's value in steel at order receipt on a fixed price. Creditor days fell from 122 to 34, and the Q1 FY27 cycle of 263 days is only 13 days shorter than a year earlier. Peers run about 120 to 165 days, though one ran 190 to 200 days in a stressed FY26. H2 FY27 operating cash flow, with Anjar utilisation disclosed above 60%, separates the two readings.

The accounts carry three disputes that need sizing before any position. The auditor qualified the FY26 group opinion, because management could not test Rs 48 cr of Malwa Power assets for impairment after the power purchase agreement expired. The Q2 FY27 review still carries Rs 51 cr of those assets untested. At Abohar, a retrospective tariff cut implies Rs 13.85 cr payable to PSPCL. Nothing is provided, because a High Court stay holds, and the auditor added an emphasis of matter. The Directors' report states that the audit report has no qualification. Listed contingent liabilities, with Abohar and a customs demand added, total Rs 255 cr, 28.7% of net worth. Reported gearing of 42.9% leaves out Rs 66 cr of trade credits that the company's own policy calls financing; with them it is 45.2%. The notes review graded accounting quality 5 of 10. On the other side, the record shows no going concern doubt, no loan default, no covenant waiver and no SEBI order.

Management delivered the plant and order items and missed the cash items. Over the last four quarters, 8 promises were delivered, 7 partly delivered and 5 missed, for a credibility grade of B. Delivered items are the seamless plant, the Malwa tariff order, the order wins and the use of equity proceeds. Missed items are positive H1 cash, no new term loans, FY27 capex of Rs 10 to 15 cr, near full Anjar use in FY27, and Rs 80 cr of FY27 biomass revenue. That last figure fell to Rs 48 cr in a filing. H1 brought Rs 58 cr of new long term loans against Rs 21 cr repaid, and Rs 58 cr of capex. The red flag verifier rates credibility lower, at the B and C border. It found a "three years booked" claim for Thailand beside a Thailand book of about one year's work. It also found a Rs 5 cr one time tariff credit in Q4 FY26 that management did not flag. The promoter verdict is CAUTION. At the September 2026 AGM, 71% voted for a pay rise for Whole Time Director Shikha Bansal, short of the 75% needed, with public institutions 50% against. The group owes her Rs 7.6 cr, mostly through a subsidiary, with the split undisclosed. A tax demand of about Rs 10 cr on her was under appeal at the IPO, and no later status was found. The CFO resigned in January 2026. Against this, the promoter pledge is nil, and the founder put Rs 20 cr into the July 2026 issue at Rs 502.

Peers confirm the direction of demand and little else. The run put seven questions to twelve transcripts from Anup, ISGEC and Ratnamani. None was verified, three were partly verified and four could not be tested. No peer contradicted DEE outright. Anup and Ratnamani both report strong thermal, nuclear and export oil and gas demand. Peers also report rising steel, gas and freight costs on fixed price orders, and Anup cut its FY27 EBITDA guide to about 15%. DEE still guides above 19% and did not mention input costs on its May and August calls. Ratnamani says its own plant is the only Indian facility NPCIL has approved for nuclear work. DEE says it is in advanced talks with NPCIL. ISGEC cancelled two orders worth about Rs 550 cr in FY26 and is building piping shops. Ratnamani plans thermal and oil and gas spools, against DEE's claim of practically no competition in India.

The market leaves room, and capacity is the binding limit. The D&B study in the prospectus puts India process piping at Rs 25,400 cr in FY23, growing 6.1% a year. The end uses DEE serves are 63% of that, about Rs 19,100 cr on an FY26 basis, so DEE holds about 2.8%. The runway class is GOOD on that reading. A Grand View Research figure puts the market about ten times smaller, near Rs 2,100 to 2,700 cr at an exchange rate the run did not source. On that reading DEE already holds 15 to 20% of its market. Piping revenue of named peers would separate the two, and the run did not find it. The run's market sizing implies revenue growth of 15.6% a year for three years from FY26. Installed and committed capacity supports about Rs 1,300 to 1,470 cr of revenue at FY26 asset turns. The Rs 2,500 cr FY30 plan needs asset turns of 3.5 times against 1.83 today, or about Rs 656 cr more fixed assets, against a capex guide of Rs 20 to 30 cr. Power is 65% of the order book but 15% of the D&B market, and D&B calls it the slowest growing end use.
