## 1. SIGNED MENTAL MODEL (operator-approved, supersedes the 09b draft)

**Archetype.** Build-to-spec component maker. Secondary traits of an outsourcing partner (few customers, wallet share, capacity fill). Not a commodity converter, so Amendment 17 converter restrictions do not bind. Applies to all lines; one reportable segment (Note 39).

**Analogy.** A specialist tailor sewing for two big fashion houses. Designed into the collection years ahead, rarely replaced mid-season because re-fitting takes months. Profit comes from volume over a fixed cost base.

**FROM to TO.** FROM: R2 cost-advantaged converter. Margin driven by fixed-cost absorption and India cost position, weak pricing power, cyclical operating ROCE (4.2% FY25, 12.7% FY26 ex-Other Income). TO: R3 value-added spec'd supplier, partial pricing power from spec-in and switching costs, ROCE durably 20-25%.

**AMENDMENT 1 (operator, 29-Aug-2026) — THE TRANSITION SPLITS IN TWO.** The 09b draft treats this as a single climb. That is too tidy. Two distinct transitions are running at different speeds:
- **EARNINGS TRANSITION — RUNNING.** Exports and Indonesia. Both are the existing mechanical business scaling: same products, same machining skill, more volume and new geographies. Raises margins through operating leverage. Does NOT change the category of the company.
- **QUALITY TRANSITION — NOT STARTED.** EV transmission and automatic transmission. Only these change the archetype from parts maker to design-owning supplier. Both stalled (Section 3).

Valuation consequence, and this is the operative instruction: **the destination PE may be sized only on what exists.** The earnings engine is real and should be priced. The re-rating engine depends on the quality transition, which has not begun. Do not credit an R3 destination multiple on the strength of the earnings transition alone.

**Engine.** (1) Product mix must shift toward IP-owned content. (2) Customer and geography base must diversify beyond Mahindra and Tata. Evidence the engine turns: B2 qualification lock-in and E2 China+1 both STRONG in the forward moat scan. Evidence it does not: EV never launched, export share fell 18% to 16%, no automatic-transmission contract exists.

**AMENDMENT 2 (operator) — TWO-PART PROOF GATE**, replacing the 09b EV-only gate. Rationale: the EV line is 8% of revenue and has never launched; near-term earnings come from Transfer Case and exports, so an EV-only gate tests the wrong thing first.
- Part 1: Q2 FY27 Transfer Case revenue at or above Rs 70 Cr AND Op EBITDA margin at or above 24%.
- Part 2: Sigma EV transmission reaches confirmed SOP and holds Rs 10-12 Cr per quarter for two consecutive quarters.
- Failing either is a downgrade. Failing both kills the transition claim.

**AMENDMENT 3 (operator) — EV HARD DEADLINE.** No SOP announced by the Q3 FY27 call (approx. Feb 2027) and the EV leg is **dead for valuation, not delayed**. Zero weight, no option value. Justification: eighteen months of slippage plus a customer that has built its own alternative (Section 3).

**AMENDMENT 4 (operator) — MAYFLOWER AND AUTOMATIC TRANSMISSION ARE TWO LINKED OPTIONS, BOTH AT ZERO.** Not one merged option, as an earlier Claude web framing had it. Mayflower has an independent justification (localising components for existing US customers) that stands without any transmission contract. The transmission cannot happen without an American footprint, but the footprint can happen without the transmission. Both valued at zero in every scenario including bull. The South Carolina state announcement is the shared early signal.

**Recognition gap.** Open, resolved at Stage 11. On record: sell-side moved Buy to Hold on 04-May-2026 on valuation grounds (P/B ~3.3x, ~61x trailing); the stock then rose further. At CMP ~Rs 1,175-1,190 the trailing PE is ~57x against a peer set at ~30-68x. Claude web's spear arithmetic (rough, not a Role 1 substitute): probability-weighted FY29 value ~Rs 1,310 giving ~3% CAGR from CMP; entry for a 25% hurdle ~Rs 630-715.

**Ugliness test — ARTIFACT-OF-CLIMB, confirmed.** Gate 0 AVERAGE (47/160) is driven by median ROCE 7.68%, mechanically depressed by a ~Rs 293 Cr post-IPO cash pile in the denominator. Operating ROCE ex-treasury rose 4.2% to 12.7%; CWIP up 1,043% on the Shirwal ramp. The cash is a legacy IPO asset being deployed, not a decaying business. **Carried separately and NOT waved away by this call:** the disclosure-contradiction cluster (Section 5), which is a governance-quality item, not a climb artefact.

**Transition falsifier.** A fourth consecutive quarter of EV SOP slippage, plus still no signed automatic-transmission contract, plus Indonesia plateauing post-batch with no confirmed follow-on. That combination shows the IP-differentiated and export legs are not converting and FY26-27 was a single-programme event.

**Business falsifier** (kills the FROM business, distinct from the above). EBITDA margin below ~20% for two quarters without a named one-off; OR a second instance of a key OEM in-sourcing Transfer Case work (Mahindra already in-sourced the 3-wheeler EV assignment); OR unremedied recurrence of the disclosure-integrity cluster into the FY27 AR, which moves promoter classification beyond CONCERN.

**Dominant variables (4).** (1) Sigma SOP and run-rate. (2) Indonesia execution and the organic-versus-programme split. (3) Export mix trajectory in rupees. (4) Automatic-transmission contract status.

**What the model rejects as noise.** All market-sizing. Global transfer-case estimates diverge ($14.0-18.82bn); no India-specific value sourceable. The "40 million installed base" in the MD's letter is one unnamed North American OEM's legacy fleet already on the road, NOT an addressable market — it must not enter any TAM or revenue derivation. The dateless "Rs 2,000+ Cr" slide is Divgi's own aspiration, driven mainly by the non-contracted automatic transmission, and was itself softened to "addressable potential" on the Q1 FY27 call. Binding constraint is programme execution, not market size (SOM headroom 6.12x, STRONG runway).

## 2. SUPERSESSIONS — where the live layer overrode the corpus

Each carries its evidence. Corpus-derived views listed here are superseded for FTTCP purposes.

**S1. Indonesia programme size: 35,000 units → 70,000 pickups. CORPUS VIEW SUPERSEDED.**
B09 corrected management's 70,000 figure down to 35,000 verified and flagged the Tata half as unconfirmed; B12a logged this as a CRITICAL. Live verification of both customers' own releases confirms management was right and the correction was wrong: Mahindra announced 35,000 Scorpio Pik Up units to PT Agrinas Pangan Nusantara for 2026 (mahindra.com, 04-Feb-2026); Tata announced 35,000 Yodha pickups plus 35,000 Ultra T.7 light trucks to the same buyer. Divgi supplies 4x4 driveline hardware, so the Ultra T.7 light trucks do NOT count; the correct pickup figure is 70,000. **Use 70,000 for capacity and revenue derivation, not 35,000.**

**S2. Character of the Indonesia order — new material qualifier, not in the corpus.**
This is a single government fleet purchase, not a market opening. Buyer is an Indonesian state enterprise under the Koperasi Desa/Kelurahan Merah Putih village-cooperative programme; total programme ~160,000 vehicles including Japanese and Chinese makers. Agrinas' managing director stated publicly that 4x4-specification vehicles are not available from local production. The programme drew domestic political criticism for not sourcing locally, and Tata was required to answer BSE and NSE surveillance queries on 02-Mar-2026 confirming the order stands. **Implication for FTTCP: treat Indonesia revenue as a dated batch with no contracted follow-on, not a recurring export channel.** Management's own framing moved from "specific, time-bound one-off, concludes by FY27 end" (Q3 FY26) to ">50% recurring" (Q1 FY27) — a cross-quarter contradiction B12b flagged as only partially caught.

**S3. Management's "flat EV market" excuse — REFUTED by public data.**
Cited across all three calls as the cause of Sigma slippage. Vahan/FADA: Indian electric passenger vehicle registrations 82,737 units in Apr-Jun 2026 vs 43,710 a year earlier (+89.3%), following +83.6% in FY26 (199,923 vs 108,873). The customer specifically: Tata registered 32,283 EVs in Q1 FY27 vs 15,794, more than double, with 39% share. B06 already recorded SONACOMS contradicting the same claim. **The delay is not market-caused. It is either Divgi product readiness or Tata deprioritisation.** Treat as a management-credibility input to the FTTCP credibility split, not as a market variable.

**S4. Competitive threat to the EV leg — NEW, absent from the corpus entirely.**
March 2026: Bosch Limited and Tata AutoComp Systems announced an equally-owned JV to engineer, manufacture and sell e-axle systems and electric motors in India, operations targeted from mid-2026 (bosch-press.in). Tata AutoComp is Tata Group's own component arm. **Divgi's largest EV customer now has a house supplier backed by Bosch technology.** Wider field crowding: Sona BLW (180,000+ traction motors, Chennai), ZF, Schaeffler, Nidec, Valeo, Dana, Bharat Forge all adding India EV driveline capacity. Divgi's claim to be "the only supplier in India with in-house capability to conceive, design, develop, manufacture and supply ground-up EV transmission" (Inv. Pres. slide 15) was defensible in 2023; it is being surrounded in 2026. Precedent inside the company: Mahindra already in-sourced the 3-wheeler EV assignment. **This reframes the EV leg from "delayed" to "competitively threatened."**

**S5. The automatic-transmission market gap is closing — REFUTES a claim repeated on all three calls.**
Management states on every call that India has no local Tier-1 for automatic transmissions; B06 recorded this as unverifiable against the peer set. On 04-Feb-2026 Aisin announced ~JPY 32 bn (~Rs 1,800 Cr) to localise automatic transmissions and CVTs in India: Rohtak expansion plus a new ~86,000 sq m plant at Sambhajinagar, both operational 2029. Aisin's six-speed automatic is already fitted to 22 cars across 7 carmakers in India. **The claim was arguably true when first made and has been on its way to being false since 04-Feb-2026; management repeated it on two subsequent calls.** Divgi's own AT SOP is "earliest, second half of CY2028" with no contract and no named customer. Timeline comparison: an unfunded, uncontracted 2028 versus a funded, customer-backed 2029.

**S6. Project Mayflower — no American conversion markers exist.**
Searched: South Carolina governor's office announcements, Greenville Area Development Corporation investment lists, state filings. **Nothing for Divgi.** Every comparable automotive arrival in that county is announced with name, dollar figure and job count (Magna $200mn/200 jobs; Isuzu $280mn/700 jobs; JTEKT $48.4mn/87 jobs; MP Husky $11.1mn/35 jobs). What exists: Delaware incorporation 04-Jun-2026, board approval for up to Rs 3 Cr, an address at CU-ICAR — a 250-acre research campus, the correct address for engineering and sales, not for machining. The "$5 million phase one" is call commentary, explicitly caveated, not a board resolution. **Value at zero; the state announcement is the conversion signal, and it would precede revenue by 12+ months.**

**S7. The Mayflower tariff rationale weakened BEFORE the board approved it — new context.**
Management cited US local-content rules on the Q1 FY27 call. That mechanism is real and independently corroborated by two peers (Sansera, Sona Comstar describe a 65% threshold rising toward 70-75%, extended five years, recently widened to medium and heavy vehicles) — B06 already has this. **What the corpus does not have:** in February 2026 the India-US interim trade framework gave India a tariff rate quota under which roughly half of auto-part exports enter the US at zero duty, against ~25% under Section 232 before. The board approved the subsidiary in May, three months later; management talked up the tariff rationale in August, six months later, without noting the change. Both sides are true — the quota covers only about half of exports, and local-content rules are a separate mechanism from tariffs — but **the single sharpest reason to spend $5 mn weakened and the pitch did not adjust.** Same pattern as S3 and S5.

**S8. Promoter shareholding discrepancy — NOT settled.**
AR Note 13(f) shows 56.47% at 31-Mar-2026; every market tracker shows 60.56%, flat across four quarters. Most likely the same holding on two definitions (promoter individuals vs full promoter group). No selling on either basis. The filed Reg 31 pattern could not be retrieved (BSE blocks automated access). **Carry as unresolved, low risk.** No pledge found in the AR or any web source; treat as nil until a filing says otherwise. Oman India Joint Investment Fund II steady at ~12.19% since the March 2023 IPO — an independent institutional check that survives.

**S9. No credit rating exists.**
Confirmed by search: the only ICRA document is the IPO Monitoring Agency Report, a different instrument. **This is a finding, not merely a gap** — the company is functionally debt-free (total borrowings Rs 1.41 Mn, a single vehicle loan) and appears never to have commissioned a rating. Consequence: the rating-agency working-capital commentary the framework requires to certify GROWTH-INDUCED cash drag cannot be supplied, so **FLAG-CASH stays INDETERMINATE and Pillar 2 defaults conservative.** Do not clear it on the operating evidence alone.

**S10. IPO capex delay — corroborating external evidence.**
The ICRA Monitoring Agency Report for the quarter ended 31-Mar-2026 records revised net proceeds Rs 169.662 Cr, Rs 96.919 Cr cumulatively utilised, Rs 72.743 Cr unutilised in fixed deposits, and states capital expenditure timelines delayed by up to 24 months on supplier lead times and macroeconomic factors, with the balance to be spent in FY27.
