## 1. REVENUE MIX SHIFT — CASH MANAGEMENT SERVICES DECLINING (Note 19, biggest Pass 2 miss)

Pass 1 gave only the headline revenue growth figure and a Board's Report service-line proxy. The audited Note 19 disaggregation (SA p.91/147; CON p.124/147) was not extracted and shows a materially different picture than "flat revenue":

**SA (Note 19, p.91/147), ₹ million:**
| Line | FY26 | FY25 | YoY |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sale of ATM and ATM Sites | 1,750.40 | 2,393.38 | -26.9% |
| Sale of products | 869.27 | 1,024.69 | -15.2% |
| Sale of services — Cash Management services | 12,185.68 | 12,758.74 | **-4.5%** |
| Sale of services — Managed Services | 7,353.53 | 5,548.80 | **+32.5%** |
| Sale of services — Card Personalisation | 563.04 | 503.42 | +11.8% |
| Revenue from operations | 22,721.92 | 22,229.03 | +2.2% |

**CON (Note 19, p.124/147), ₹ million:**
| Line | FY26 | FY25 | YoY |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sale of ATM and ATM Sites | 1,750.40 | 2,393.38 | -26.9% |
| Cash Management services | 13,701.18 | 14,670.91 | **-6.6%** |
| Managed Services | 7,861.92 | 5,652.92 | **+39.1%** |
| Card Personalisation | 563.03 | 503.42 | +11.8% |

🔴 **Red Flag / significant new finding.** The company's core, legacy Cash Management services line (ATM cash replenishment, retail cash management, cash-in-transit) is *shrinking* in absolute terms (-4.5% SA, -6.6% CON), fully masked at the headline (+2.2%/+2.6%) by Managed Services growth (+32.5%/+39.1%) and by the acquired Securens contribution. Hardware sale (ATM/ATM Sites) fell 26.9%, consistent with fewer new ATM deployments. This directly corroborates the segment-profit finding below and reframes the "flat revenue, falling PAT" story Pass 1 flagged (Finding 6): it is not flat-revenue-with-margin-pressure across the business, it is a genuine contraction in the highest-quality, highest-margin legacy segment offset by a lower-margin newer one. Material for the GARP transition thesis and the Quality Ladder classification.

## 2. SEGMENT NOTE — MARGIN COMPRESSION CONCENTRATED IN CASH MANAGEMENT (Note 38, CON p.133/147)

Pass 1 stated the granular segment P&L "was not fully captured." It is now extracted:

| Segment result before interest/tax, ₹m | FY26 | FY25 | YoY |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cash Management services | 3,012.14 | 4,013.79 | **-25.0%** |
| Managed Services | 1,365.44 | 1,365.17 | flat |
| Cards | 182.05 | 205.49 | -11.4% |
| Total segment result | 4,559.63 | 5,584.45 | -18.4% |

Segment assets: Managed Services assets grew 39.4% (₹9,437.78m → ₹13,158.42m, reflecting the Securens acquisition and FSS-related capital advances); unallocated corporate assets fell 22.7% (₹10,930.68m → ₹8,449.45m, largely the cash-balance drawdown, see Section 5). Segment liabilities: Managed Services liabilities fell 17.3% (₹4,647.60m → ₹3,841.08m) while Cash Management liabilities rose 11.4% (₹3,229.63m → ₹3,597.99m).

🔴 **Red Flag.** A 25% profit decline in the segment carrying nearly two-thirds of Group segment result, on revenue that itself fell (Section 1), is the clearest single piece of evidence in the notes that the core business is under margin AND volume pressure simultaneously — not merely absorbing input-cost inflation on flat volumes. This should be read together with Pass 1's Finding 1 (receivables ageing) and Finding 6 (PAT decline): all three point to the same segment.

## 4. PENDING FSS ATM MANAGED SERVICES ACQUISITION — ₹1,150m, NOT IN THE CAPITAL COMMITMENTS NOTE (Note 10 Other Assets footnote, SA p.89/147; CON p.121/147)

Not found anywhere in Pass 1. Note 10 (Capital advances) footnote, identical wording SA and CON: "The Company/Group has entered into an agreement to acquire the ATM Managed Services assets of FSS for ₹1,150 million... With this asset purchase, CMS's ATM managed services portfolio grows to approximately 33,000 ATMs... payment process in 2 (Two) tranches. ₹600 million paid on March 30, 2026 as Advance Purchase Consideration and the balance amount of ₹550 million Balance Purchase Consideration, shall be payable upon completion of transfer of the Assets (including the novation of all the Commercial Contracts)."

Capital advances (non-current) rose to ₹677.47m (FY26) from ₹124.30m (FY25), driven mainly by this ₹600m FSS advance (SA Note 10, p.89/147; CON equivalent p.121/147).

🟡 Watch — disclosure-completeness flag. **Note 31(b) Capital Commitments (SA p.96/147; CON p.128/147) shows the estimated amount of contracts remaining to be executed on capital account as ₹0 (FY26) vs ₹93.63m (FY25)** — i.e. the ₹550m remaining FSS payment obligation, evidenced by a signed agreement and a ₹600m advance already paid, does NOT appear in the standard capital-commitments disclosure. This is a second material acquisition (after Securens) in progress at year end, funded partly from the cash pile (Section 5), and an investor reading only the commitments note would not see the ₹550m forward obligation. Flag for Halt 1: confirm total FSS consideration, expected completion date, and whether the ₹550m is provided for in FY27 cash planning.

Not flagged in Pass 1, which anchored LBF-1 (CFO vs PAT) but did not follow through to the closing cash position.

|---|---|---|---|
| SA (Note 13 / Cash Flow) | 904.26 | 2,166.19 | **-58.3%** |
| CON (Cash Flow Statement, p.110/147) | 1,122.56 | 2,308.52 | **-51.4%** |

Bank-balance mix also shifted: "Funds held relating to cash management activity" fell from ₹346.45m to ₹33.91m (-90.2%); Margin money deposits fell from ₹133.85m to ₹50.81m; a new ₹300m "deposit with original maturity of less than three months" and a new ₹255m "deposit 3-12 months" appeared, suggesting some of the drawn-down cash was parked in short-term deposits rather than sitting as pure cash-in-hand (SA Note 13, p.89/147).

The consumption is explained by the combination already partly visible in Pass 1 (capex ₹4,092.68m) plus two items Pass 1 did not connect to cash: the ₹1,479.58m dividend paid (Section 6 below, +39.2% YoY) and the ₹600m FSS advance (Section 4). With the ₹550m FSS balance and the ₹1,679.30m buyback both falling due after this already-depleted year-end cash position, FY27 liquidity headroom is tighter than the strong CFO headline alone suggests. Relevant to Halt 1's capital-allocation review alongside Pass 1's Finding 10 (buyback pricing).

## 6. DIVIDEND NOTE — UNUSUAL SPECIAL INTERIM DIVIDEND INFLATED FY26 CASH OUTFLOW (Note 46, SA p.103/147; CON equivalent p.136/147)

Not extracted in Pass 1 (Pass 1's Section 12 covered the buyback but not the dividend note).

"Dividends declared and paid during the year ended March 31, 2026 include an amount of ₹3.00 per equity share Special Interim Dividend and ₹3.25 per equity share towards final dividend for the year ended March 31, 2025, and an amount of ₹2.75 (PY: ₹3.25) per equity share towards interim dividends for the year ended March 31, 2026. The Board... has recommended a final dividend of ₹2.50/- per Equity share... for FY 2025-26" (subject to AGM approval).

Total cash dividend paid, per Statement of Changes in Equity (identical SA and CON): ₹1,479.58m (FY26) vs ₹1,063.16m (FY25), **+39.2%**, against an 18.6% PAT decline (Pass 1 Finding 6).

🟡 Watch. The aggregate increase is driven substantially by an unusual ₹3.00/share Special Interim Dividend that relates to FY25 but was paid during FY26 (a timing/classification item, not a recurring payout increase) — the FY26 proposed final dividend itself (₹2.50/share) is actually *lower* than FY25's final (₹3.25/share), consistent with softer earnings. Still, the cash-paid payout ratio rose materially (from ~28.5% to ~48.8% of CON PAT) in a year when receivables quality deteriorated (Pass 1 Finding 1) and cash fell by half (Section 5) — a capital-allocation question for Halt 1, additive to the buyback pricing concern already flagged.

## 7. SA LOSS ALLOWANCE ON RECEIVABLES ACTUALLY FELL WHILE AGEING WORSENED 16x (Note 12/37, SA p.99/147)

Pass 1 gave the CON loss-allowance movement (rose 7.7%, ₹766.82m→₹825.71m) but not the SA-level movement, which moves in the opposite direction and is the more striking number given the deterioration originates almost entirely at SA (per Pass 1 Finding 1):

| SA loss allowance movement, ₹m | FY26 | FY25 |
|---|---|---|
| Opening balance | 533.94 | 1,014.60 |
| Transfer to cash-loss provision (reclassification) | - | (577.49) |
| Amounts written off (net) | - | (78.47) |
| Net re-measurement of loss allowances | (75.14) | 175.28 |
| **Closing balance** | **458.80** | **533.94** |

🔴 **Red Flag — amplifies Pass 1 Finding 1.** SA loss allowance fell 14.1% YoY (a net ₹75.14m *release*, not an addition) in the exact same year the SA 1-2 year overdue bucket grew 16.2x (₹92.14m → ₹1,490.59m, Pass 1 Finding 1). Loss-allowance coverage as a percentage of gross overdue receivables therefore compressed sharply at the standalone level — the entity where the ageing deterioration is concentrated. This is a provisioning-adequacy question the CON-level "allowance rose 7.7%" framing in Pass 1 does not surface, because the CON increase is driven by the Securens opening-balance addition (₹74.55m), not by fresh Group-level conservatism on the ageing receivables. Direct question for Halt 1 and the auditors: what changed in the SA impairment model to justify a net release in a year the ageing profile worsened this severely.

🟡 Watch. A double-digit forfeiture rate in the newer, larger 2023 scheme in the same year the CEO's own award vested and was fully exercised is not itself wrongdoing, but is a fair question for Halt 1 on broader employee attrition/retention at a time revenue and margin softened in the core segment (Sections 1-2).
