## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

### 2A. Demand environment
Peer commentary across all four names converges on a domestic-demand-led recovery through FY26, with the GST 2.0 rate cuts (effective ~September 2025) repeatedly cited as the single largest driver of H2 FY26 strength — not export/tariff dynamics. (TALBROAUTO, Q3 FY26 call, Feb 2026): "India's automobile industry delivered a strong performance in Q3 FY26... driven by...supportive policy measures, including GST rate cut." (RACLGEAR, Q4/FY26 call, Jun 2026): consolidated revenue crossed Rs500cr milestone, "despite... global turmoil." (DIVGIITTS): four consecutive record quarters through FY26, but driven primarily by domestic OEM volume (Mahindra/Tata) and only secondarily by exports. (PRECAM): explicitly the outlier — Indian demand is described as offsetting a genuinely weak/insolvent European market (MFT GmbH went into insolvency, Dec 2025 call), i.e., domestic strength is masking real export-side deterioration for this peer. Net: consensus that FY26 demand strength is real and broad-based, but management across peers attributes it predominantly to domestic policy tailwinds (GST cut) rather than to the export/tariff narrative OBSC's questions foreground — a framing gap worth noting for synthesis.

### 2B. Pricing and input costs
Consensus across PCL, RACL and Talbros: raw material inflation (steel, aluminum, LPG, freight) intensified from roughly April-June 2026, attributed by name to the "Iran war" / "West Asia war" / "Middle East issues." All three describe a pass-through mechanism to OEM customers with a lag of one to three quarters, and all three explicitly frame the pressure as temporary rather than structural. None report a currency-based (INR/Yuan) pricing dynamic; Talbros and RACL both discuss rupee depreciation as a modest export-margin tailwind, not the China-related mechanism OBSC's Q1 posits. Divgi is the outlier with no RM-cost commentary at all in the reviewed window, and its margins are the most stable of the four (24.3%-24.9% band throughout FY26).

### 2C. Capex cycle
This is an industry-wide capacity race, not a lone-expander situation. All four peers are mid-cycle on meaningful capex: Divgi (US beachhead office, EV/automatic-transmission R&D, modest scale ~Rs3cr initial); PRECAM (~Rs120cr Solapur facility plus a further ~Rs100cr multi-year program, commissioning April 2027); RACL (Rs50-77cr annual capex, heat-treatment plant replacement, BMW-linked Venus/Titan lines); Talbros (Rs150cr+ planned for FY27, new Gujarat facility, forging press upgrades). All four peers explicitly tie capex to already-secured order visibility rather than speculative build-ahead (RACL, Gursharan Singh, repeatedly: "we never invest until there is a business"). This corroborates OBSC's Supa/Sanand buildout as consistent with a genuine sector-wide capacity expansion cycle rather than an outlier bet — call this a "capacity-race" situation, not a "lone expander."

### 2D. Competitive mentions
No peer transcript (16 of 16 read) mentions OBSC Perfection Ltd by name or by unmistakable description at any point. Complete silence on the main company across the peer set.

### 2E. Risks peers discuss that OBSC's management does not (per the injected claim set)
- **European subsidiary insolvency risk**: PCL's German step-down subsidiary MFT GmbH entered insolvency proceedings (PRECAM, Q2 FY26 call, Dec 2025), a Rs48.8cr full write-off — illustrates the real tail risk of thin-margin European auto-component exposure during a genuine regional recession, distinct from tariff-driven softness.
- **Cyberattack / operational disruption risk in the export supply chain**: Talbros' large European client (JLR) suffered a cyberattack that caused zero deliveries for over a month and a ~Rs10cr one-time business loss (TALBROAUTO, Q2 FY26 call, Nov 2025) — a supply-chain risk category not typically flagged by management teams pre-event.
- **Export incentive/policy risk**: Government of India halved a road-tax-linked export benefit for RACL, an estimated Rs1cr annual hit (RACLGEAR, Q3 FY26 call, Feb 2026) — a specific policy-reversal risk to exporters.
- **EV demand reversal among global OEMs**: PCL explicitly describes "a nearly reversal of strategy from going all-electric to now looking at ICE and hybrid powertrains" among its OEM customers (PRECAM, Q3 FY26 call, Mar 2026) — a demand-side risk to any peer with EV-linked capex plans.
- **Customer concentration materializing as risk**: RACL's largest two-wheeler customer (KTM, under Bajaj) went through insolvency and a 6-8 month production halt in 2024-25, directly denting RACL's growth that year (RACLGEAR, multiple calls) — a live illustration of single-customer concentration risk crystallizing, relevant background for any concentration analysis of OBSC.
- **Labour code cost impact**: both Divgi (Rs76.5 lakh, 9M FY26) and PCL flag incremental costs from newly notified labour codes — a small but real, broadly shared cost item.

## PART 4: TRIANGULATION SUMMARY

- **Claims verified**: 0 of 7 fully VERIFIED (two or more peers corroborating both direction and magnitude). 3 of 7 PARTIALLY VERIFIED (Q1, Q2, Q5-cost-pressure-component). 1 of 7 CONTRADICTED on its core assertion (Q5-guidance-cut-component). 4 of 7 UNVERIFIABLE (Q3, Q4, Q6, Q7).
- **Claims contradicted**: 1 (Q5) — peers facing comparable tariff/Iran-war/shipping cost pressure explicitly reaffirm, rather than cut, medium-term margin-expansion guidance. This is a priority item for synthesis: if OBSC management is using macro cost pressure to justify a guidance cut, three independent peers under the same macro conditions are not doing so.
- **The single most consequential contradiction**: Q5. Precision Camshafts, RACL Geartech, and Talbros Auto all name the same input-cost driver (Iran/West Asia war-linked steel, aluminum, LPG, freight inflation) in the same quarter (roughly Apr-Jun 2026) that OBSC would be citing, and every one of them reaffirms unchanged guidance in the same breath, framing the pressure as temporary and substantially pass-through eligible. If OBSC cuts medium-term margin-expansion guidance citing this same driver, the peer set suggests either OBSC's pass-through mechanism is structurally weaker than peers', or the cut reflects OBSC-specific factors being attributed to a macro cause.
- **The single strongest independent confirmation**: Q1/China+1. Divgi, Talbros, and RACL each independently and explicitly frame FY26 export order wins in China-displacement terms, with Talbros' MD using the exact phrase "China Plus One" as a named right-to-win driver and RACL's COO describing the same dynamic for Eastern-Europe-then-India mechanical component sourcing. This is genuine, broad-based, multi-peer corroboration of the direction (if not the specific INR/Yuan currency magnitude) of OBSC's central export thesis.
- **Overall**: the peer set complicates rather than cleanly supports or undercuts OBSC's narrative. The broad macro tailwinds OBSC cites — China+1 sourcing shift, strong CV volumes, an industry-wide capex/capacity race — are corroborated by multiple independent peers and are not OBSC-specific. But several of OBSC's more specific or more convenient framings do not survive contact with peer evidence: no peer corroborates a forward-looking CV softening call (peers are bullish into FY27), no peer describes a tariff squeeze-then-restoration cycle, no peer confirms BEL/BDL-specific defense order wins at pace (the closest analog, RACL's BHEL relationship, is slow and unquantified), no peer reports capacity-driven margin dilution during capex ramps (the opposite framing, in fact), and — most consequentially — peers facing the same input-cost driver OBSC would cite are reaffirming rather than cutting guidance. Symmetrically: this is not evidence that OBSC's specific claims are false, only that they are not independently corroborated by, and in the guidance-cut case run directly against, what its closest listed peers are telling their own analysts in the same period.
