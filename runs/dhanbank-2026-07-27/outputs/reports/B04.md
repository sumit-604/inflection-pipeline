## SECTION 1: THE BUSINESS MODEL IN PLAIN ENGLISH

### 1A. One-line description
Dhanlaxmi Bank borrows money from depositors in Kerala and South India, lends most of it back out as gold-backed and retail/MSME loans, and keeps the difference between what it pays depositors and what it earns from borrowers, topped up with fee income and treasury gains on its government-bond portfolio.

### 1B. Money flow chains (one per revenue stream)

|---|---|---|---|---|---|

### 1E. The chai-stall-uncle version
Think of Dhanlaxmi Bank like a small, old, well-known moneylender-cum-post-office in a Kerala town. People trust it because it has been there for generations and because it is the bank that even the local temple trust uses. Most of its customers walk in with gold jewellery and walk out with cash the same day, because gold is easy to value and hard to run away with. It pays savers a certain rate to keep money with it, and charges borrowers a higher rate — the gap between the two is how it eats. The problem is, it is a small shop competing against much bigger, shinier shops (larger banks) and specialist gold-pawn chains (Muthoot, Manappuram) for the same customers, so it has to run leaner and smarter just to survive, and for years a hole in its accounts (an old accumulated loss) has meant it cannot even hand out sweets (dividends) to its shareholders no matter how well it does this year.

### 2D. Industry lifecycle stage
Indian scheduled commercial banking overall is a mature, consolidating industry. Dhanlaxmi Bank itself sits in a post-restructuring recovery phase: it exited the RBI's Prompt Corrective Action framework in February 2019 (ICRA p.1) and has only recently re-accelerated growth (loan book CAGR of 12.48% March 2019-December 2025, ICRA p.1; FY25 loan growth 18.39% up from 6.88% in FY24, ICRA p.3), aided heavily by a cyclical, gold-price-driven expansion of its gold-loan book (31% to 38% of the loan book between Mar-25 and Dec-25, ICRA p.1). The bank's growth engine is therefore currently riding a gold-price up-cycle layered on top of an otherwise mature, low-growth franchise.

## SECTION 3: FINANCIAL METRICS THAT MATTER FOR THIS BUSINESS MODEL

### 3A. Ignore-these / track-these table

| Commonly tracked ratio | Status for this business | Why |
|---|---|---|
| EV/EBITDA | IRRELEVANT | A bank's interest income/expense IS its core operating activity, not a financing item sitting "below EBITDA"; there is no meaningful EBITDA line |
| EBITDA margin | IRRELEVANT | Same reason — interest expense is operating, not financing, for a bank |
| ROCE (return on capital employed) | IRRELEVANT | Banks do not have a distinct "capital employed" separate from their entire balance sheet; use RoA/RoE instead |
| EV/Sales | IRRELEVANT | "Sales" (interest income) scales with balance-sheet leverage, which differs bank to bank; not comparable to non-financial company revenue multiples |
| Working capital days (inventory/receivable/payable) | IRRELEVANT / N/A | No inventory, no trade receivables/payables in the manufacturing sense; the loan book and deposit base are the entire "working capital" |
| Current ratio | IRRELEVANT | Bank balance-sheet liquidity is governed by LCR/NSFR, not a current-assets-to-current-liabilities ratio |
| Fixed asset turnover | IRRELEVANT | Fixed assets are ~1.4% of the balance sheet (Results p.10); irrelevant to how the bank actually earns money |

### 4B. Valuation method applicability (handoff to Role 1 / stage 11)

| Method | Applicable? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Price/Book (P/B) or Price/Adjusted Book Value (P/ABV) vs sustainable RoE relative to cost of equity | **PRIMARY** | Standard bank valuation framework. Book value here must be adjusted for the ₹711.25 Cr accumulated loss already embedded in net worth (AR p.81) and for net NPA drag; sustainable RoE (currently 4.78%, AR p.12, well below likely cost of equity) is the key input that will determine whether the stock deserves to trade at, above, or below adjusted book |
| P/E on normalized earnings | **SECONDARY** | Cross-check only — current EPS (₹2.37 FY25, ₹2.60 FY26, AR p.12/Results p.8) reflects a still-subscale, high-cost-to-income earnings base; normalize for the credit-cost cycle and the gold-loan-driven revenue spike before applying any multiple |
| Residual income / excess-return model (RoE minus cost of equity, applied to book) | **TERTIARY** | Useful cross-check precisely because current RoE is likely below cost of equity — this model explicitly prices the value-destruction/creation gap that a flat P/B multiple can obscure |
| Dividend Discount Model (DDM) | NOT APPLICABLE currently | No dividend has been declared (FY25, AR p.13), constrained by the accumulated loss; DDM has no cash flow to discount until the deficit clears and a payout policy resumes |
| EV/EBITDA | NOT APPLICABLE | No meaningful EBITDA construct for a bank (Section 3A) |
| EV/Sales | NOT APPLICABLE | Interest income scales with leverage, not comparable across capital structures |

**Cycle stage that matters for valuation:** two overlapping cycles must be priced together — (1) the credit/asset-quality cycle, where Dhanlaxmi is in a post-PCA recovery phase with improving but not yet clean asset quality (GNPA 1.89% FY26 headline vs 7.45% including write-offs, ICRA p.3, Results p.8) and a falling PCR on one basis (53.63% ex-write-off, ICRA p.3) even as it rises on another (92.46% incl. technical write-off, Results p.14); and (2) the gold-price cycle, since the bank's recent growth reacceleration is substantially a gold-price effect rather than a franchise-share-gain effect. A valuation that extrapolates the current growth rate without normalizing for both cycles will overstate sustainable earnings power.
