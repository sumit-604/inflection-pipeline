## SECTION 1: THE BUSINESS MODEL IN PLAIN ENGLISH

### 1A. One-line description
Divgi TorqTransfer Systems designs, engineers and manufactures precision automotive
driveline and transmission hardware — 4WD/AWD transfer cases, EV transmissions, manual
transmissions/synchronizers and precision-machined components — supplied on a
build-to-spec, purchase-order basis to passenger-vehicle, commercial-vehicle and
off-highway OEMs and global Tier-1 suppliers in India and export markets (AR p.7, Inv.
Pres. slide 2).

### 1B. Money flow chain, by revenue stream

1. **Transfer Case (TC)**: [steel/aluminium castings, forgings, bearings, gears] →
   [Divgi machines, assembles and tests the 4WD/AWD transfer case to OEM spec at
   Bhosari/Shivare/Sirsi plants] → [delivers the assembled unit to the OEM's vehicle
   assembly line — e.g. Mahindra Scorpio/Thar, Tata pickups, the Indonesia 4x4 Pik-up
   program] → [OEM pays] → [purchase order/supply agreement, invoiced on delivery,
   OEM payment terms] (AR p.7, p.32; Inv. Pres. slides 6, 10, 12).
2. **E-Gear Drive (EV transmission)**: [motor-side gear sets, differential, casing] →
   [Divgi designs and builds the in-house EV transmission, 10-150kW range, at the
   Shirwal EV-dedicated plant] → [delivers to an EV OEM's line, currently ramping on
   the "Sigma" platform] → [OEM pays on SOP-linked volumes] (AR p.24, p.32; Inv. Pres.
   slides 7, 10, 17).
3. **Manual Transmission & Synchronizers**: [gear blanks, synchronizer rings] →
   [machining/assembly of synchronizer systems] → [delivered to OEM/off-highway
   customers, e.g. Force Motors, JCB] → [OEM pays] (AR p.7, p.45).
4. **Components / precision machining, incl. exports**: [forgings/castings] →
   [precision grinding, honing, machining of gears/shafts] → [delivered to Tier-1
   customers, e.g. BorgWarner's North American plants, partly via overseas warehouses
   in Mexico/USA] → [Tier-1 pays] (AR p.68; Inv. Pres. slides 13, 19, 33).
5. **Automatic Locking Hub (ALH)**: small legacy line, same build-to-spec flow, minor
   revenue (AR p.73 revenue-mix table).
6. **Other Income (non-operating)**: IPO-proceeds cash placed in bank fixed deposits →
   interest earned (Note 26, AR p.232). Not a customer-facing revenue stream; flagged
   separately because it is currently ~6% of Total Income and inflates naive return
   ratios (see Section 3A).

### 1D. Simplified business-model canvas

- **What they sell**: Engineered driveline/transmission hardware (mechanical + emerging
  EV) qualified into an OEM's bill of materials.
- **Who buys**: Passenger-vehicle, commercial-vehicle and off-highway OEMs, and global
  Tier-1 suppliers — Mahindra & Mahindra (50+ yrs), Tata Motors (50+ yrs), BorgWarner
  (30+ yrs), Toyota Kirloskar (10+ yrs), Force Motors (10+ yrs), Ashok Leyland (10+ yrs),
  JCB, MG Motors (AR p.9, p.27).
- **Why them**: 60-year engineering heritage; in-house EV transmission design
  capability the company describes as unique among Indian suppliers; IATF16949 quality
  certification; cost competitive versus global peers (AR p.31, p.42).
- **How delivered**: Direct plant-to-OEM-assembly-line delivery; overseas warehouse
  stocking for export customers in Mexico and the USA (AR p.45, p.68).
- **Cost structure dominance**: Fixed manufacturing cost (4 plants, depreciation Rs29.2
  Cr FY26) + raw material (~39% of revenue) + a fast-growing royalty/tech-license line
  (Rs7.58 Cr FY26, +183% YoY, Note 32A) — capital- and engineering-intensive, moderate
  variable cost.
- **Scarce resource**: In-house EV-transmission design/engineering talent; decades-long
  OEM qualification and trust (relational scarcity, not physical).
- **Pricing power source or absence**: Weak-to-moderate. OEM buyer power dominates —
  auto-industry contracts are typically multi-year, fixed-price with cost-down clauses.
  Any pricing upside comes from technology-led mix (NexTrac, in-house EV transmission)
  rather than list-price increases.
- **Asset intensity**: High — gross block ~Rs421.6 Cr (AR Value-Creation Report),
  4 plants, FY26 capex Rs44.3 Cr, fixed-asset turnover 0.92x (Inv. Pres. slide 35).
- **WC intensity**: Medium, improving — net working capital 68 days FY26 vs 96 days
  FY25 (inventory 50 days, receivables 70 days, payables 52 days; Inv. Pres. slide 35).
- **Regulatory moat or burden**: Neutral-to-burden. IATF16949/ISO certifications are a
  cost of doing business, not a moat. No licence/quota-style regulatory protection.

### 1E. The chai-stall-uncle version
Think of Divgi as the specialist who makes the gearbox that lets your Mahindra Thar
switch into four-wheel-drive when the road turns bad. Big carmakers like Mahindra and
Tata do not make this part themselves — they trust Divgi to design it, build it in
their Pune-area factories, and hand it over just in time for the car's assembly line.
Divgi does not sell to you, the car buyer; it sells to the carmaker, one truckload of
parts at a time, at a price fixed years in advance. Divgi is now also learning to make
the equivalent part for electric SUVs, and shipping parts to America. It earns money
the old-fashioned way — buy steel, machine it precisely, ship it on time, get paid in
60-90 days — and because the factory and machines are already paid for, every extra
rupee of sales beyond the break-even point drops disproportionately to profit.

### 2D. Industry lifecycle stage
The global and Indian auto-components industry is mature overall (India auto
components market ~14.8% CAGR to 2030, MD&A p.143), but the EV/electrification
sub-segment is in a growth phase (Indian EV passenger-vehicle sales +80%+ YoY in
FY26, MD&A p.141-142). Divgi sits mid-transition: a mature ICE mechanical-driveline
base being extended into growth EV and export segments.

## SECTION 3: FINANCIAL METRICS THAT MATTER FOR THIS BUSINESS MODEL

### Archetype fit
**Best-fit archetype: Build-to-spec component maker** (customer capex/platform cycle,
design-win pipeline, content per unit, input-cost pass-through), with secondary
**Outsourcing-partner/Tier-1-supplier** characteristics (client concentration, wallet
share via NexTrac/PTU/export programs, capacity fill). This is explicitly NOT a
commodity converter (no spread/utilisation-based economics) and NOT a lender — the
Section 1B converter-multiplier and Amendment-17 CONVERTER restrictions do not apply
to this name.

### 3A. Ignore-these / track-these

| Commonly tracked ratio | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Same-quarter YoY revenue growth, unadjusted | Misleading | A single large program ramp (Indonesia 4x4) can double reported YoY growth without signalling broad-based demand (Q1FY27 Transfer Case +93% YoY, Inv. Pres. slide 10) |
| Gross margin trend, in isolation | Misleading | Gross margin has stayed flat at 62-64% across the cycle (Note 27/28) while EBITDA margin swung 24.4%-31%; the real profitability driver is fixed-cost absorption, not raw-material spread |
| RoE / RoCE inclusive of Other Income | Misleading | Other Income is treasury interest on IPO cash (~6% of Total Income), not operating income; reported RoCE 9.9% FY26 vs operating RoCE ex-Other-Income 12.7% (AR CFO overview, p.153) |
| Receivable days vs consumer-company benchmarks | Irrelevant framing | 70-day receivables (FY26) is normal for OEM Tier-1 supply terms, not a red flag by consumer-retail standards |
| P/E vs branded-FMCG or platform peers | Irrelevant | Apply Indian auto-ancillary/driveline Tier-1 peer multiples instead |

### 3D. Unit economics — the physics of the business

- **Unit**: one Transfer Case unit (or the equivalent one vehicle-set of driveline/
  transmission components supplied to an OEM).
- **Revenue per unit**: **NOT FOUND** — per-unit ASP and unit volumes are not disclosed
  in the AR or investor presentation.
- **Cost per unit**: **NOT FOUND** — no per-unit cost breakdown disclosed.
- **Volume drivers**: OEM platform production volumes (Scorpio/Thar/XUV700, Tata
  pickups, Indonesia 4x4 Pik-up), EV OEM volume ramp (Sigma platform), export program
  volumes (Tier-1 replenishment, ~Rs10-12 Cr/month additional export potential cited,
  Inv. Pres. slide 19).
- **Price drivers**: product-mix richness (NexTrac vs base mechanical TC), content
  per vehicle, export realisation uplift.
- **Cost drivers**: raw material (steel/aluminium castings & forgings, ~39% of
  revenue); machining/development charges paid to related party Divgi Transmission
  Systems & Technologies Pvt Ltd (Rs4.50 Cr FY26, Note 34); employee cost (Rs41.5 Cr,
  11.8% of revenue); royalty to technology partners (Rs7.58 Cr FY26, up from Rs2.68 Cr
  FY25 — more than doubled, a cost line to watch, Note 32A); power & fuel (Rs9.41 Cr).
- **Incremental margin / operating leverage**: Strongly positive and explicitly
  evidenced. FY26 revenue +61.2% YoY drove PAT +92.2% YoY (AR CFO overview, p.153);
  Q1FY27 revenue +85% YoY drove EBITDA +118% and PAT +183% (Inv. Pres. slide 5, 11).
  The physics: a largely fixed manufacturing and employee cost base means volume
  growth above breakeven utilisation flows disproportionately to the bottom line.

## SECTION 4: RISKS, VALUATION APPROACH & MONITORING

### 4A. Business-model-specific risks

| Category | Risk | First financial line item to deteriorate |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue model | Customer/program concentration — heavy reliance on M&M/Tata plus single-program dependence (Indonesia 4x4) | Transfer Case % of Total Income, or single-program revenue, plateauing/declining |
| Margin | Raw-material (steel/aluminium) cost inflation outrunning pass-through | Gross margin (Note 27) falling below ~60% |
| Balance sheet | Working-capital deterioration as export/US operations scale (inventory build, longer receivable cycles) | Net working capital days rising back above ~90-100 |
| Execution | EV Transmission (Sigma platform) delays — already occurred once (Q1FY27) | E-Gear Drive segment revenue declining QoQ/YoY (already -20% YoY in Q1FY27, Inv. Pres. slide 10) |
| Structural | Substitution of mechanical 4WD/transfer-case architecture by electric AWD in the EV transition | New OEM RFQ mix shifting away from mechanical transfer case toward integrated e-axle solutions (qualitative signal) |

### 4B. Valuation method applicability

| Method | Applicability |
|---|---|
| Relative valuation (EV/EBITDA, P/E vs Indian auto-ancillary/driveline peers) | **PRIMARY** — profitable, EBITDA-generative business in a cyclical recovery from an FY24-25 trough; peer comps capture sector-appropriate multiples |
| DCF / scenario-based residual earnings | **SECONDARY** — cross-check that captures platform-ramp optionality (Indonesia TC program, EV Sigma SOP, export scale-up) not visible in trailing multiples |
| Asset/replacement-cost cross-check | **TERTIARY** — sanity-check floor given the high fixed-asset base (~Rs422 Cr gross block) and ongoing capex cycle |
| Sum-of-the-parts | Not applicable — single reportable business segment per Note 39 |
| Dividend discount model | Not applicable — low, reinvestment-focused payout |
| NAV/asset-holding valuation | Not applicable — not a holding/asset company |

**Cycle stage that matters**: Early-recovery / re-rating phase. The company just
emerged from a two-year auto-industry slowdown (FY24-25) into a FY26 recovery, plus
a structural push into EV and exports — valuation should weight the forward path
(FY27 targeted revenue ~Rs500 Cr, FY29-30 aspiration ~Rs1,000 Cr, AR MD's letter p.35)
more heavily than trailing multiples alone.

1. Revenue from Operations, QoQ/YoY, ex-Other Income

5. EBITDA margin %
6. PAT margin %
7. Gross margin % (raw-material pass-through)
8. Net working capital days
9. Capex spend vs plan / any capacity-utilisation commentary
10. New platform/program wins or delays (RFQ-to-SOP news flow)
11. Royalty/tech-licence expense trend
12. Cash & bank balance / debt level (should remain net-cash)
13. Related-party transaction quantum (machining charges to Divgi Transmission Systems
    & Technologies Pvt Ltd; rent to Divgi Holdings Pvt Ltd)
14. US subsidiary (Project Mayflower) cost burn and RFQ traction

### 4D. Highest-value questions for management

1. What is customer-wise revenue concentration (top 1/top 3 as % of revenue), and how
   will management reduce it? *Reassuring*: quantified declining concentration.
   *Worrying*: refusal to disclose, or admission of rising concentration.
2. What caused the E-Gear Drive revenue decline in Q1FY27, and what specific
   milestones now de-risk the Sigma SOP timeline? *Reassuring*: SOP started on
   schedule with confirmed volumes. *Worrying*: further slippage or vague "on track"
   language without dates.
3. What is current capacity utilisation at each of the 4 plants, and how much
   incremental capex is needed for the FY27 Rs500 Cr revenue target? *Reassuring*:
   utilisation data with clear headroom. *Worrying*: evasive answer, or utilisation
   already near ceiling with no capex plan.
4. How is the royalty/licensing expense (more than doubled YoY) structured under the
   renegotiated BorgWarner scope — is it capped as % of revenue? *Reassuring*: capped
   or declining with broader IP ownership. *Worrying*: open-ended and rising with no
   offsetting IP transfer.
5. What is the committed capital/opex burn for the US subsidiary (Project Mayflower)
   over the next 2-3 years, and what milestones justify continued investment?
   *Reassuring*: capital-light, milestone-gated plan. *Worrying*: open-ended spend
   absent firm customer commitments.
6. Given single-program dependence on the Indonesia 4x4 contract for Transfer Case
   growth, what is the contract tenure, and are follow-on volumes confirmed beyond
   FY27? *Reassuring*: multi-year confirmed volumes plus a pipeline of follow-on
   programs. *Worrying*: contract-specific with no confirmed extension.
7. What is the realistic timeline and probability-weighted revenue contribution of the
   Automatic Transmission technology-transfer opportunity (40mn+ installed base cited
   in the MD's letter)? *Reassuring*: term-sheet/MOU stage with a named counterparty
   and dates. *Worrying*: still "under evaluation" after repeated mentions with no
   binding commitment.
