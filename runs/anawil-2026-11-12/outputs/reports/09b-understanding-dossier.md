**A2. The simple analogy.** Anawil is a heavy-metal contract workshop for the wind industry. A turbine maker orders a steel tower. In some jobs the customer sends the steel plate, and Anawil rolls, welds and coats it for a fee. In other jobs Anawil also procures the steel and the fittings and supplies the finished tower. The workshop has two sites and room for 612 towers a year (B05). It made 210 towers in FY26 and 107 in the half year to September 2026 (B04; B05). A tower is large and costly to move, so a workshop near the wind states has a real edge, but a rival can build a similar workshop (B04; B07). Today the workshop serves a few customers, and one customer holds 36.6% of the order book (B04; B07).

## SECTION 3: BUSINESS UNDERSTANDING NARRATIVE (draft from B01 to B09)

Anawil makes tubular steel towers for onshore wind turbines at two plants. Koppal in Karnataka holds 420 towers a year of capacity, and Kutch in Gujarat holds 192 (B05). A turbine cannot ship without its tower, and the towers reach hub heights of 140 metres (B05). In Model 1 the customer supplies the steel, and Anawil earns a conversion fee of about Rs 50 to 60 lakh per tower. In Model 2 Anawil adds its own fittings at Rs 1.0 to 1.1 Cr per tower. In Model 3 Anawil also procures the steel and supplies the finished tower at Rs 3.0 to 3.5 Cr (B04). Model 3 was 64.3% of H1 FY27 revenue, Model 1 21.2% and Model 2 10.8% (B04). The customers are wind turbine makers and renewable developers, with eight names in the order book; Sany holds 111 towers and Envision 49 (B09). Customer A holds 36.6% of the book, and the largest customer was 29.3% of FY26 revenue (B04; B07). Orders come project by project with no long-term agreements, and a customer can cancel (B07; B05). Demand exists because India installed a record 6.05 GW of wind in FY26, and the downstream signals are the MNRE monthly installed wind capacity, Envision India wind installations and tower sourcing, Sany Renewable Energy India installations and tower sourcing, and wind and RTC/FDRE tender awards (B09). Demand should grow with the industry roadmap of 8, 9, 10, 12 and 13.2 GW a year to FY31, which implies tower volume growth of 22.5% a year over three years (B09). Peers temper this, because turbine makers say cleared sites and grid, not factories, limit installations (B06). The signals to track are Suzlon and Inox Wind quarterly deliveries, order books and tower sourcing choices, and any ALMM or localisation rule for towers (B09). Models 1 and 2 draw their advantage from plant location near the top wind states and from customer approvals, and both have low to moderate durability because a rival can build a similar plant (B04). Model 3 has no moat the run could confirm, and its margin hinges on an undisclosed steel clause (B04). The Emerging Moat scan scores the company NONE and Gate 0 records a THIN moat, and no peer supports the claim of a limited number of qualified suppliers, since Inox Wind is building its own tower plant and KP Green is entering tubular towers (B07; B01; B06). The run did not establish customer-site geography, FY26 revenue by contract model, or the outsourced share of OEM towers (B04; B09).
