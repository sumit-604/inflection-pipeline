# Defence fabric profits wait on cash collection

Kusumgar Ltd (KUSUMGAR), Phase 1 evidence run of 2026-11-12. Phase 1 lite: no valuation has run, so this file carries no entry zone, no decision and no exit multiple.

## Business understanding

Kusumgar weaves, dyes, coats and laminates nylon and polyester cloth to tight customer specifications, and since FY25 it also sews some of that cloth into finished military systems. Aerospace and defence fabric was 32% of FY26 contract revenue: parachute, tactical gear and camouflage cloth sold in rolls of 25 to 500 GSM, made to the buyer's specification after years of trials. Aerospace and defence solutions was 23%: parachutes, camouflage nets, decoys and shelters cut, sewn and tested to designs licensed from a US parachute partner, plus maintenance work. Industrial and automotive fabric was 24%, cloth for wire harness tapes, rubber goods reinforcement and inflatables, and outdoor and lifestyle fabric was 19%, cloth woven, dyed and laminated to a global brand's specification. A buyer keeps a qualified supplier because approvals take 2 to 10 years, and one automotive tape customer ran two years of type qualification before its first order. Government owned buyers took 15% of FY26 revenue, and the company names Gliders India and Troop Comforts as direct buyers that turn its fabric into military goods. The rest goes to private defence integrators, export buyers, tier 3 and tier 4 automotive suppliers and the fabricators who make garments for Decathlon, a chain worth Rs 700 mn of FY26 sales. Every sale is a purchase order with no long term agreement, and the largest customer fell from 47% of contract revenue in FY24 to 11% in FY26 after two large FY25 orders did not repeat. Present demand comes from the Ministry of Defence capital acquisition budget, which reserves 75% for domestic industry, and from the Defence Acquisition Council approvals and the parachute and camouflage tenders that trigger each solutions order. Exports were 40% of FY26 revenue, so the monthly technical textile export data, SIAM vehicle production for the tape business and US tariff actions on Indian engineered fabrics track the other demand legs. Growth should come from defence indigenisation, brand by brand approvals in outdoor fabric and export buyers adding a supplier outside China, and DAC releases, GeM bids and the Gliders India filings give outside checks on the defence leg. The company's commissioned market report puts growth in its domestic pools at 13.1% a year, but the company paid for every market size in that report, and the run found no independent size for the Indian parachute market. The qualification barrier gives the defence fabric and industrial lines a moderate moat, documented by AS9100D and IATF 16949 certificates and the two year tape qualification. The solutions line rests on a moderate partnership position that the company does not own, because the US partner keeps the design IP. Exports hold a moderate China+1 position, documented by revenue from outside India rising from Rs 1,167 mn in FY24 to Rs 2,698 mn in FY26. The outdoor line has no moat the run could establish: the brand sets the price and the contract sits with the fabricator. The company holds no registered IP, talent asymmetry and cannibalisation barrier score zero, and the emerging moat scan totals 15.5 to 17.5, a modest reading with no category scored strong.

## The story in eight paragraphs

Kusumgar is a 36 year old textile business that listed on 15 July 2026 and runs six plants in Gujarat and one fabrication unit in Agra. FY26 revenue was Rs 692 Cr, down 11% on FY25, and FY26 profit after tax was Rs 98 Cr. H1 FY27 revenue was Rs 498 Cr, up 91% on H1 FY26, and H1 profit before tax was Rs 1,107 mn against Rs 223 mn. The listing was a 100% offer for sale, so the company received none of the money.

The change underway is a move from selling cloth to selling finished defence systems. Aerospace and defence end use was 69% of contract revenue in FY24, 77% in FY25 and 55% in FY26. The solutions line went from almost nothing to 29% of revenue in FY25 on one order, and a second order of Rs 2,372 mn was only 24% executed at March 2026. Final output capacity rose from 47 mn to 128 mn metres, and the plants ran at 49.5% in FY26 against 94.3% in FY24. Management said 55% to 60% in August 2026, and only Rs 101 mn of capital work in progress sits on the books, so growth has to come from filling the plants. One reading says H1 growth is broad, since at least 64% of H1 revenue came from work outside the big order. The other says the overflow of that one order carries the half. H2 FY27 revenue without the order separates them, but the company stopped reporting its four segments after listing, so the mix stays hidden until the FY27 annual report.

The screen scores Kusumgar 72 of 160, with 59 of 100 on the core and 13 of 60 on moats, three moats confirmed and a moderate moat class. A core of 59 is AVERAGE, and four years of history adds a one tier downgrade to AVOID; both facts stand. The cash block scored zero: cumulative cash from operations was 0.28 times profit over FY23 to FY26, and free cash flow was negative in two of three years as Rs 2,544 mn of capex outran operating cash. ROCE fell from 55.9% in FY24 to 24.8% in FY26, but a liability spike that reversed in FY25 inflated the FY24 figure. Against that, restatement adjustments were nil in all three years, net debt is 0.93 times EBITDA and debt is 0.44 times equity. A verifier recount of one moat test lifts the moat score to 14 or 16 without changing the class.

Cash is the central fact. Receivables rose from Rs 561 mn to Rs 2,333 mn in FY26 while revenue fell 11%, and debtor days went from 26 to 123. FY26 cash from operations was Rs 283 mn, 15% of EBITDA of Rs 1,879 mn. Foreign currency accounts made up 77% of the build, sit at 226 days, carry an implied rate of 123 rupees per dollar against 92.8 and 95.3 elsewhere in the same note, and only 8.6% of them are hedged. The bad debt allowance fell from Rs 69 mn to Rs 62 mn as gross receivables almost quadrupled. Three year operating cash was 25% of profit, or 63% if the Rs 1,119 mn paid to the promoters for ECFPL moves out of operating cash flow. The timing reading has support: Q4 FY26 carried Rs 1,151 mn of bulk and service revenue, only 1.2% of March receivables were over six months old, and September receivables fell to Rs 2,204 mn, about 81 days of H1 sales. The structural reading has support too: H1 FY27 cash from operations was Rs 249 mn on the consolidated basis and Rs 341 mn standalone, against Rs 394 mn in H1 FY26, inventories absorbed Rs 740 mn, management now names a 100 to 120 day cycle, and it booked a Rs 67 mn credit loss charge on government receivables. Full year FY27 cash conversion and March 2027 receivable days separate the two.

Management's record grades C, the default for a company with one call on file, and the red flag verifier argued for C minus. Two promises were kept: Q2 FY27 revenue held 1% above Q1, and the margin moved 2.7 points, inside the "few percentage points" management promised. Five were partial, among them positive but lower operating cash and debtors that are not back at the 26 to 33 day history management pointed to. Management withdrew the segment split and the order book after listing, replaced the Q2 call with a written Q&A it drafted itself, and labelled a margin series as excluding other income when it includes it. The prospectus says the company hedges a significant portion of its currency exposure, and forward cover is 8.6%. The CEO called the business sticky and recurring, and the prospectus says there are no long term sale agreements. The Rs 2,000 Cr revenue goal for FY30 comes from a TV interview and appears in no filed document.

The promoter verdict is CAUTION. The family sold 15.52 mn shares for Rs 6,500 mn in the offer, a count printed as 15,521,350 in the Q2 FY27 results and 15,521,698 in the prospectus. Its stake fell from 89.44% fully diluted, or 89.79% on the basic count, to 75.01% in September 2026 on a screener print. The company bought ECFPL from the same promoters for Rs 1,119 mn, 10.1 times net assets, in a year when operating cash was negative. CRISIL rated Rs 10 Cr of bank lines B and A4 on 21 January 2026 and revised them to BB minus and A4 plus on 22 October 2026, both as issuer not cooperating; the company says it never gave CRISIL a mandate, and CARE reaffirmed A Stable in April 2026. The CFO changed 27 days after listing, and public institutions cast 87% of their votes against the ESOP ratification, which the promoter block carried. On the other side, no regulator, court or auditor has found against the promoters, no shares are pledged, the promoters kept personal guarantees on company debt, and the record shows Rs 2,544 mn of capex, Rs 1,245 mn of preference capital raised and DRDO technology transfers. The promoter stage closed partial: the CFO exit reason and the lender behind the CRISIL lines are still open.

The peers confirm direction and nothing more. Two of three peers had call transcripts, nothing was contradicted, nothing was fully verified, and seven claims were partly verified. Mayur Uniquoters runs a 25% to 30% margin band on an export OEM mix and called part of its own peak margin a currency effect, so Kusumgar's 27% to 31% band on the deck basis is plausible for coated fabric but may carry the same currency help. Arvind reports lumpy defence orders and a 14% to 15% margin on a different mix. No peer gives a cash conversion or defence receivable anchor, and Kusumgar alone sits at 55% to 60% utilisation after an expansion while both peers add capacity in small steps.

The market is large on paper and capacity binds first. The serviceable pool is Rs 63,445 Cr, Kusumgar holds 0.64% of it, and the run classes the runway MASSIVE, but every size comes from the report the company paid for. That report's Indian defence fabric pool of Rs 5,850 Cr is 3.87 times a benchmark built from India's share of global defence spend. One point of share equals about Rs 634 Cr of revenue, more than the Rs 582 Cr of headroom left in the plants at FY26 mix. The central three year market path of Rs 984 Cr sits below the H1 FY27 run rate of Rs 996 Cr, so the run carries a second path anchored to that run rate at 22.9% a year for three years against 13.4% on the central path. The FY31 path of Rs 1,273 Cr needs revenue per metre to rise with defence mix, from the Rs 98 of FY26 toward the Rs 166 of FY25.
