### Q1 — Saudi LSAW/HSAW demand-supply gap (~2mn tons demand vs ~0.8-0.9mn tons domestic supply,
~6-8% CAGR) — the rationale for NPC + Dammam coating

**Claim (verbatim from handoff):** "Is MANINDS's claimed Saudi LSAW/HSAW demand-supply gap (~2mn
tons LSAW demand vs ~0.8-0.9mn tons domestic supply, ~6-8% CAGR) consistent with peers?"

**Verdict: PARTIALLY VERIFIED**

**Peer evidence:**
- Jindal SAW COO Vinay Kumar, Q2 FY26 call: "Currently the domestic ductile capacity in Saudi is
  not much. Okay? So, there are only two haling [helical] plants." (JINDALSAW Oct 2025, p.16-17)
  He also confirms Saudi has opened an anti-dumping investigation on Indian DI pipe imports,
  evidence the Kingdom is actively protecting an under-supplied local market (JINDALSAW Oct 2025,
  p.16).
- Welspun Corp CEO Vipul Mathur, Q2 FY26 call: "the Saudi market, there is a huge demand which is
  currently being serviced, mostly from imports. Almost two-third of the DI requirements seems to
  be serviced through imports." (WELCORP Nov 2025, p.6-7)
- Both Jindal SAW and Welspun independently cite Saudi Aramco's ~$10bn/year, 10-year capex
  programme and the Vision 2030 water-security/desalination build-out as structural demand
  drivers behind their own Saudi capacity decisions (WELCORP Nov 2025 p.6-7; WELCORP Feb 2026
  p.126-137; WELCORP Jul 2026 p.21 on the "East-West pipeline" and multiple Aramco pipeline
  augmentations).

**Peers silent:** No peer (Jindal SAW, Welspun, or Ratnamani) states a specific Saudi LSAW
tonnage-demand figure, an 0.8-0.9mn ton domestic-supply figure, or a 6-8% CAGR. The peer
commentary that exists is largely about DI (ductile iron) pipe import-dependency, not
specifically LSAW/HSAW segmentation.

**Net read:** Direction confirmed by two independent peers — a genuine, import-dependent Saudi
supply gap exists and is capex-driven (Aramco, Vision 2030). But the specific tonnage and CAGR
figures MANINDS cites are unverifiable against these transcripts; the peer evidence speaks mostly
to DI pipe, not the LSAW/HSAW segment the NPC/Dammam thesis rests on. Treat the qualitative gap as
corroborated, the quantitative claim as untested.

### Q2 — Do Welspun (East Pipes ~350ktpa LSAW) and Jindal SAW (~300-400ktpa spiral) confirm the
Saudi capacity-addition timeline/tonnage MANINDS cited?

**Claim:** peer capacity-addition tonnage/timeline consistency test for oversupply-risk
understatement.

**Verdict: PARTIALLY VERIFIED (tonnage confirmed for Jindal SAW; Welspun tonnage not disclosed in
transcripts; timeline evidence suggests oversupply risk may be understated, not overstated)**

**Peer evidence:**
- Jindal SAW, Q1 FY27 call (Jul 2026): "we had announced seamless project in Abu Dhabi and SAW
  pipe project in Saudi Arabia... The JV is dedicated to establishing advanced LSAW and HSAW pipe
  mills of 300,000 metric tons per annum EACH to serve regional water infrastructure and energy
  demand," a 51%/49% JV with "Buhur of KSA." (JINDALSAW Jul 2026, p.5-6) This directly confirms
  the ~300,000tpa-class magnitude in the handoff question, and clarifies it is 300k LSAW + 300k
  HSAW (600k combined), not a single 300-400k line.
- Jindal SAW's own earlier calls put commercial start even later than MANINDS's Dammam target:
  "starting '28-29 we can have some operations in the seamless facility as well as in the helical
  facility" (JINDALSAW Oct 2025, p.15-16), later firmed to FY29 (JINDALSAW Jul 2026, p.5).
- Welspun confirms its own Saudi LSAW plant is "absolutely on track" and targeted to "come in
  stream in FY 2027" (WELCORP May 2026, p.6-7; also WELCORP Feb 2026, p.6-7 lists "our LSAW project
  in Saudi" among projects moving on track). No transcript discloses Welspun's Saudi LSAW capacity
  in tonnes; the ~350,000tpa figure in the handoff question is not independently sourced from
  these calls.

**Peers silent:** Welspun never states a tonnage figure for its Saudi LSAW plant or for East Pipe
in these 4 transcripts (peers silent on that specific number). Ratnamani has no LSAW/HSAW exposure
and does not speak to this question at all (peer silent, different sub-segment — Ratnamani's
Saudi capex is a stainless-steel cold-finishing line).

**Net read:** The competitive capacity build is real and larger than a single-peer read would
suggest: Jindal SAW alone is committing 600,000 MTPA of new Saudi LSAW/HSAW capacity via a local
JV, and Welspun is simultaneously bringing its own Saudi LSAW plant onstream in FY2027 — the same
window MANINDS's Dammam coating facility targets. Taken together, oversupply risk into Saudi
LSAW/HSAW over FY27-29 looks understated in MANINDS's own framing, not overstated.

### Q3 — Jal Jeevan Mission revival + Rs.67,000cr allocation as FY27 domestic demand driver, same
"green shoots" framing

**Verdict: VERIFIED (fact of the allocation and initial framing), with a material complication
peers raise but MANINDS's calls (per B05) do not resolve**

**Peer evidence:**
- Jindal SAW CFO/Treasury head, Q3 FY26 call (Jan 2026), on the pending budget: "They have — even
  though they have done INR67,000 crores to INR17,000 crores, but at least INR17,000 crores is
  likely to be disbursed... Even if they would confirm INR50,000 crores allocation for the next
  year, that's a very welcoming statement... the whole supply chain, which has virtually stopped
  for the Jal Jeevan Mission will start." (JINDALSAW Jan 2026, p.9-10) — states the identical
  Rs.67,000cr figure MANINDS cites.
- Welspun Corp, Q3 FY26 call (Feb 2026), post-budget: "the Honorable Finance Minister announced
  almost INR70,000 crores allocation to JJM. Once that happens... I'm sure the moment that starts
  trickling in, which would be in the very first quarter of the next year, I think so there we
  will see a complete resurgence of the projects." (WELCORP Feb 2026, p.5-6) — same order of
  magnitude, same "green shoots"/resurgence framing MANINDS uses.
- **Complication:** by the following two quarters, both peers report the hoped-for revival has
  NOT materialized on the ground. Jindal SAW, Q4 FY26 call (May 2026): "Despite positive policy
  announcements under the Jal Jeevan Mission, project execution on..." remains constrained
  (JINDALSAW May 2026, p.3), and Q1 FY27 call (Jul 2026): "the water segment remained weak as Jal
  Jeevan mission-linked project execution continued to be affected by delayed release of central
  funds and title scrutiny of the state projects. Multiple states reported pending dues and slowed
  project timelines." (JINDALSAW Jul 2026, p.4) Welspun's Jul 2026 call likewise: "We see that
  fundamentally, the things under Jal Jeevan Mission are slow" (WELCORP Jul 2026, p.2) and later,
  "under this Jal Jeevan mission impacting the domestic marketing demand" (WELCORP Jul 2026,
  p.18).

**Peers silent:** Ratnamani references JJM funding stress only briefly ("I'm guessing the Jal
Jeevan Nigam is under stress," RATNAMANI May 2026, p.6) without independently confirming the
Rs.67,000cr figure.

**Net read:** The allocation number and the initial optimistic ("green shoots") framing are a
genuine, sector-wide fact independently confirmed by two peers, not company-specific PR — VERIFIED
on that narrow point. But peer commentary running two quarters past the announcement shows the
disbursement-to-execution lag is real and ongoing; if MANINDS's own "green shoots" language is
carried into its FY27 guidance without acknowledging this lag, it is repeating an optimism peers
themselves later walked back.

### Q4 — All input costs fully hedged at order-booking stage (margin-stability narrative)

**Verdict: PARTIALLY VERIFIED**

**Peer evidence:**
- Welspun Corp CEO, Q1 FY27 call (Jul 2026), on why the company avoids quarterly guidance but
  gives annual EBITDA guidance: "with the steel completely hedged and the margin profile what we
  have, we are able to give a clear cut guidance on the EBITDA number." (WELCORP Jul 2026, p.20) —
  direct, explicit corroboration using near-identical language ("completely hedged") for the
  primary raw material.
- Jindal SAW COO, Q2 FY26 call: "we don't accumulate or hold steel in anticipation of getting the
  order... we book the steel the moment we get the order for the pipe. So eventually, the
  volatility in the raw material prices is [minimised]... this situation normally does not arise
  that the steel price going up and down is impacting the pipe player very significantly."
  (JINDALSAW Oct 2025, p.16) — functionally the same order-linked procurement mechanism MANINDS
  describes.
- **Caveat peers add that MANINDS's blanket claim does not:** Jindal SAW, Q4 FY26 call (May 2026):
  on fuel/freight cost inflation on delivered-basis contracts, "we would try our level best to
  pass on some of the cost, but there is no guarantee that this cost would eventually be passed on
  to the buyer." (JINDALSAW May 2026, p.14) Similarly, escalation-clause pass-through for DI pipe
  contracts is capped at "30% to 35%" of the contract, not 100% (JINDALSAW May 2026, p.14).

**Peers silent:** Ratnamani never characterises its raw-material practice as "hedged"; its
commentary (RATNAMANI May 2025, p.9) attributes margin variance partly to raw-material price
moves, implying some residual exposure rather than full insulation.

**Net read:** Two peers corroborate the core mechanism — steel is procured against a firm order,
which is the same practice MANINDS describes and functions as an effective hedge on the primary
input. But peers explicitly disclose gaps MANINDS's "all input costs fully hedged" framing glosses
over: freight/fuel cost pass-through is not guaranteed, and escalation-clause coverage on
non-steel inputs tops out well short of 100%. Downgraded from VERIFIED to PARTIALLY VERIFIED on
that basis.

### Q5 — NPC 1.5x EV/EBITDA vs claimed peer 7-10x multiple — any peer Saudi/GCC benchmark?

**Verdict: UNVERIFIABLE on the specific multiple — but a highly material qualitative peer
disclosure exists and should be treated as a priority item**

**Peer evidence:** No peer discloses an EV/EBITDA multiple for any Saudi/GCC pipe-sector

> [Moderator, seeking clarification]: "...which asset are you referring to, please?"

The timing (Jul 2026, post-NPC-announcement), the "another Indian company had also acquired"

company is never named and this identification cannot be treated as certain.

acquisition target.

**Net read:** The specific 1.5x vs 7-10x multiple claim is UNVERIFIABLE against these transcripts
— no peer gives a number. But Welspun's disclosure that it scouted and passed on what is very
likely the same asset, explicitly on quality/reputation grounds rather than price, is a
consequential independent data point: it complicates the "cheap because the market missed it"
framing of the 1.5x multiple and raises the alternative reading that the multiple is cheap because
a discerning peer already flagged quality risk. Carried forward as a flag, not folded into a
verified/contradicted bucket, because it does not address the numeric claim itself.

## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

**2A Demand environment:** Peers uniformly describe FY26 as operationally "challenging" or
"muted" — EPC payment delays, JJM fund-release lag, and (from ~March 2026) Middle East
geopolitical disruption (Strait of Hormuz blockade amid a US-Iran conflict) halting MENA
shipments for months. Simultaneously, all three peers report strong-to-record order books
(Section Q6 above), driven substantially by export/Saudi/US demand (Aramco capex, US LNG/data
centre pipeline, Saudi water infrastructure). This is broadly consistent with MANINDS's own
framing of near-term execution headwinds plus a longer-run demand tailwind, except that MANINDS's
booking performance diverges sharply from the peer set (Q6).

**2B Pricing and input costs:** Peers describe raw-material risk management as order-linked steel
procurement (Jindal SAW, Welspun) plus, for DI pipe, partial escalation clauses capped at
30-35% pass-through tied to pig-iron indexation (Jindal SAW). None describe a blanket "all costs
fully hedged" position; freight/fuel inflation pass-through is explicitly described as
not guaranteed (Jindal SAW). This partially supports but does not fully match MANINDS's
margin-stability narrative (see Q4).

**2C Capex cycle:** This is an industry-wide capacity race into Saudi/GCC, not a lone expander.
Jindal SAW (Abu Dhabi seamless 300,000tpa ~$300mn + Saudi LSAW/HSAW JV 300,000+300,000tpa,
~$400-425mn combined three-project capex), Welspun Corp (Saudi LSAW plant + Saudi DI plant, both
targeted FY2027 stream, plus two US projects), and Ratnamani (Saudi stainless-steel cold-finishing
line) are all simultaneously building Saudi/GCC capacity in the FY27-29 window that MANINDS's
NPC acquisition and Dammam coating facility also target. Name: capacity race, not lone expander —
this raises oversupply risk beyond what MANINDS's own disclosure acknowledges (see Q1/Q2).

**2D Competitive mentions:** Welspun Corp's Jul 2026 call contains the single clearest peer
mention in this set — an analyst asking about "an asset available [in Saudi Arabia]... The one
which another Indian company had also acquired," almost certainly a reference to NPC given the
timing (two months post-announcement) and context. Welspun's management confirms it evaluated and
passed on the target, citing quality/reputation standards rather than price (full quote and
analysis under Q5). No peer names MANINDS or NPC directly. No other unmistakable competitive
mention was found in any of the 12 transcripts.

**2E Risks peers discuss that MANINDS's own concalls (per B05) do not:**
- Saudi anti-dumping investigation into Indian DI pipe imports (Jindal SAW, Oct 2025, p.16) —
  signals Saudi protectionist tilt against India-sourced product that MANINDS's NPC/local
  manufacturing narrative does not address for its own non-LSAW/HSAW product lines.
- Domestic Indian DI pipe sector overcapacity, built in anticipation of JJM demand that has not
  materialized: "there may be some oversupply... in ductile iron pipes... major capacity
  expansions... were in anticipation of demand from Jal Jeevan Mission... this position seems to
  be remaining as it is for quite some time." (JINDALSAW May 2026, p.10-11) — a sector-level
  capacity-discipline caution not raised in MANINDS's own calls.
- Middle East shipping blockade (Strait of Hormuz closed from March 2026) halting all MENA exports
  for months, with "all outward shipments... suspended since March 2026" for Jindal SAW
  (JINDALSAW Jul 2026, p.4) — a live, ongoing execution risk for anyone with Saudi/GCC export
  exposure that is more severe and more currently disclosed by peers than anything flagged in
  MANINDS's own transcripts per B05.
- API license suspension risk for seamless/oil-and-gas certified product (Jindal SAW, Jul 2026,
  p.4) — a certification/regulatory risk class relevant to MANINDS's own seamless pipe ambitions
  (Jammu SS plant) that is not discussed in MANINDS's calls.

## PART 4: TRIANGULATION SUMMARY

- Claims verified: 1 of 6 (Q3, on the narrow allocation-figure/framing point)
- Claims partially verified: 3 of 6 (Q1, Q2, Q4)
- Claims contradicted: 1 of 6 (Q6) — priority item for synthesis
- Claims unverifiable (numeric): 1 of 6 (Q5) — but carries a material qualitative flag

**Single most consequential contradiction:** Q6, order-book trajectory. All three peers report
stable-to-record order books through FY26 despite acknowledging the same demand headwinds MANINDS
cites. MANINDS's order book fell 37% (Rs.4,750cr to Rs.3,000cr) over the same three quarters. The
peer set makes it very hard to attribute this decline to sector conditions; it reads as
company-specific.

**Single strongest independent confirmation:** Q3, the Rs.67,000cr (Jindal SAW) / Rs.70,000cr
(Welspun) Jal Jeevan Mission allocation figure and initial "resurgence" framing, cited
independently by two peers within weeks of the same Union Budget event MANINDS references. This is
a genuine, verifiable, sector-wide fact — not a company-specific talking point — though both peers
later (Q1 FY27 calls) report the anticipated execution pickup had not yet materialized.

**Overall:** the peer set complicates MANINDS's narrative more than it supports it. The macro
demand thesis behind the NPC/Dammam investment (Saudi import-dependency, Aramco capex, Vision
2030, JJM allocation) is directionally real and independently corroborated. But three specific
elements of MANINDS's framing look weaker once tested against peers: the order-book decline
appears company-specific rather than sector-wide (Q6, contradicted); the Saudi LSAW/HSAW capacity
race is larger and more imminent than MANINDS's own disclosure implies, given Jindal SAW's
confirmed 600,000tpa JV and Welspun's parallel Saudi LSAW plant on the same FY27-29 timeline (Q2);
and a credible peer (Welspun) scouted and passed on what is very likely the same NPC asset
explicitly on quality grounds, not price (Q5) — a fact that complicates, without disproving, the
"cheap acquisition" framing of the deal's 1.5x multiple.

## PART 5: CROSS-PEER HYPOTHESIS

Across all three peers, Saudi/GCC market entry is structured as a minority or joint local-partner
arrangement, never a wholly-owned acquisition: Jindal SAW holds 51% in its Saudi LSAW/HSAW JV
with Buhur of KSA holding 49% (JINDALSAW Jul 2026, p.5); Welspun's long-standing Saudi water
business runs through its associate East Pipe rather than a wholly-owned subsidiary (WELCORP Nov
2025, p.6); Ratnamani frames its Saudi stainless-steel entry around being present locally ahead of
a named local competitor rather than acquiring outright (RATNAMANI Nov 2025, p.6-7). Separately,
Jindal SAW flags that Saudi Arabia has opened an anti-dumping investigation against Indian pipe
imports and is actively favouring in-country, locally-partnered manufacturing (JINDALSAW Oct 2025,
p.16-17). No single peer states this as an industry pattern, but combined, the data suggest Saudi
market-access economics increasingly reward local co-ownership structures over wholly foreign-owned
entry — a protectionist tilt that may be pricing MANINDS's 100%-acquired, wholly-owned NPC
structure differently than peers' minority-JV structures, either as a competitive advantage (NPC
is already Saudi-domiciled with an existing Aramco relationship, sidestepping the JV requirement
peers face) or as a latent regulatory-eligibility risk if Saudi in-country value-addition rules
tighten further around ownership. This is testable: watch whether NPC retains full local-content
and Aramco-approved-vendor status as a wholly Indian-owned subsidiary, or whether MANINDS is later
required to bring in a Saudi equity partner to preserve the eligibility peers are already securing
through JV structures.
