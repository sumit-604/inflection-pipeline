## 1. ACCOUNTING POLICIES & CHANGES

- **Going concern**: standalone Note 2.3(i) explicitly ties the going-concern assertion to "a turnover of Rs 11,808.53
  Lakhs during fiscal year 2024-2025" (Annual_Report.pdf p.85) — correctly current-year. The **consolidated** Note 3(a)
  going-concern paragraph instead states "a turnover of Rs 29,455.55 Lakhs during the fiscal year 2023-2024"
  (Annual_Report.pdf p.130) — this is the **prior year's** consolidated revenue, not FY25's ₹46,343.54 lakh. Boilerplate
  not updated. 🟡 Watch — disclosure-quality/copy-paste error, not a substantive going-concern doubt (auditor gave
  unmodified opinion and explicit going-concern conclusion in the audit report), but it is the kind of "stale figure"
  error the operator should be aware of when the notes are otherwise this information-dense.
- **CIRP disclosure buried in a classification policy, not a stand-alone note**: standalone Note 2.3(ii) "Current
  versus non-current classification" ends with: "However as CIRP process has been initiated all liabilities towards
  Banking Facilities have been converted in to Current Demands and hence shown under Current Liabilities."
  (Annual_Report.pdf p.85-86). Identical language in consolidated Note 2(b) (Annual_Report.pdf p.138). This is the
  **only place in the current-year notes that names the CIRP directly as a policy driver**; the substance of the
  event (resolution plan approval, share cancellation of erstwhile promoters, capital infusion) is scattered across
  Note 14 (share capital), Note 15 (securities premium jump), Note 34 (contingent liability "As per Approved
  Resolution Plan..."), and Note 30/Exceptional Items template line "CIRP Cost" (₹NIL both years — cost already fully
  expensed pre-FY24). 🔴 Red Flag for disclosure organisation, not for the underlying event: an investor reading only
  the current two years of notes would not easily reconstruct that this is a post-insolvency-resolution entity; the
  event itself (positive — company emerged from CIRP with a new promoter group and capital infusion in FY23) is
  structurally important context that belongs in a dedicated note, not a subordinate clause.
- **Impairment testing not performed for 5 years**: policy note on fair value measurement (standalone p.88-89 and
  consolidated p.141-142, identical wording) states: "such fair value report is not available for all assets except
  equity investment as on 31st March, 2020, Hence Impairment Loss not booked for immoveable properties." Repeated
  verbatim from FY2020 onward. 🟡 Watch — stale valuation base for PPE/land & buildings; five years without a fresh
  impairment assessment on immovable property is unusual disclosure to keep repeating unchanged year after year.
- **Depreciation useful lives**: standalone/consolidated Note 2.3(c) / 3(i) — Plant & Machinery split into
  "Continuous process" (8 years) and "Normal process" (15 years), both **shorter than Schedule II defaults**
  (typically 25 years for continuous process, 15 years normal), i.e. management has adopted **more conservative
  (shorter) useful lives** than the statutory default (Annual_Report.pdf p.90, p.142). 🟢 Clean — conservative choice,
  faster depreciation, not aggressive.
- **Revenue recognition**: point-in-time on dispatch/delivery, net of estimated sales returns/rebates (Ind AS 115);
  flagged as the sole Key Audit Matter by the auditor, standalone and consolidated (see §0). No contract assets/
  liabilities or unsatisfied performance obligations disclosed anywhere in the notes — either genuinely absent given
  the short-cycle nature of the business, or simply not disclosed; treated as NOT FOUND IN DOCUMENT rather than
  assumed zero. 🟡
- **ESOP / Ind AS 102 disclosure is thin**: Note 41 standalone (Annual_Report.pdf p.114 approx.) and Note 43
  consolidated read, in full: "The Company has issued Employee Stock Options (ESOP) to the employees of the Company
  and its Subsidiary. ESOP entitles its holder to equity shares to be considered Potential Equity Shares." There is a
  Share Based Payment Reserve of ₹87.80 lakh on the balance sheet (Note 15/16.1) and an annual P&L charge (₹33.35 lakh
  standalone FY25, ₹38.17 lakh consolidated) but **no grant date, exercise price, number of options outstanding,
  vesting schedule, or valuation-model assumptions are disclosed** anywhere in the notes reviewed. 🟡 Watch — Ind AS
  102 minimum disclosure requirements appear materially short of what is required.
- **Ind AS 116 leases**: policy is fully described (ROU assets, lease liabilities) but the balance sheet shows
  Right-of-use Assets = NIL and Lease Liabilities = NIL both years, consolidated (Note on balance sheet, p.123).
  Rent expense of ₹175.51 lakh (consolidated, Note 29) and ₹3.31 lakh (standalone, Note 28) is instead run through
  P&L as an operating cost — consistent with the short-term/low-value lease exemption, but this is inferred, not
  stated; NOT FOUND IN DOCUMENT is the explicit reconciliation. 🟡
- **No revaluation of PPE or borrowings**: standalone Note 3A explicitly states borrowings and preference shares were
  not revalued and interest not amortised per Ind AS during the year (consolidated Note 3A, Annual_Report.pdf p.135) —
  a rare admission of a compliance gap in the accounting policy note itself. 🟡 Watch.

## 2. RELATED PARTY TRANSACTIONS (standalone Note 35, consolidated Note 37 — Annual_Report.pdf p.112-114 / p.171-172)

- **Related-party revenue concentration is extreme.** Standalone: Sale of Mfg Goods/Stores & Spares to Subsidiary
  (₹1,563.92 lakh) + Promoter Group (₹3,710.19 lakh) + Associate (₹230.32 lakh) = **₹5,504.43 lakh, 46.6% of FY25
  standalone revenue (₹11,808.53 lakh)**. Consolidated: Promoter Group (₹14,776.49 lakh) + Associate (₹2,623.94 lakh)
  = **₹17,400.43 lakh, 37.5% of FY25 consolidated revenue (₹46,343.54 lakh)**, before adding job-work service sales.
  Single-customer disclosure (Note 38.2(c) standalone / 40.2(c) consolidated) confirms **Plastene India Limited alone
  = 34.7% of standalone revenue and 32.96% of consolidated revenue** — both figures for one related entity.
  🔴 Red Flag — this is not incidental related-party trading, it is the dominant channel for the business.
- **Related-party purchases (COGS) concentration is even higher.** Standalone: Subsidiary (₹1,393.54 lakh) + Promoter
  Group (₹3,882.27 lakh) + Associate (₹609.16 lakh) = ₹5,884.97 lakh = **62.6% of Cost of Materials Consumed
  (₹9,406.43 lakh)**. Consolidated: Promoter Group (₹20,172.37 lakh) + Associate (₹4,471.22 lakh) = ₹24,643.59 lakh =
  **70.4% of Consolidated Cost of Materials Consumed (₹35,028.61 lakh)**. 🔴 Red Flag — the group is functioning as an
  intercompany manufacturing/trading ecosystem on both the sales and purchase side; arm's-length assertion (Note 35.2A
  states all RPT are "on terms equivalent to arm's length") cannot be independently verified from the disclosures
  given, and margin comparability with unrelated-party pricing is NOT FOUND IN DOCUMENT.
- **Related-party receivables are a large share of total receivables.** Standalone Note 10: Receivable from Related
  Party ₹846.97 lakh of ₹2,927.62 lakh total (28.9%). Consolidated Note 11: ₹4,814.77 lakh of ₹9,888.95 lakh total
  (48.7%). Consolidated Note 40.2(b) shows Plastene India Limited alone at ₹4,980.82 lakh — **49.2% of total group
  receivables in one related party.** 🔴 Red Flag — combined with the CARO/bank-reconciliation finding above (banks
  exclude "non-allowable" — i.e. largely related-party — receivables from drawing power), this concentration is a
  genuine cash-conversion and collateral-quality risk, not just a disclosure curiosity.
- **Possible cross-note figure inconsistency (flagged for verification, not asserted as error):** standalone Note 10
  states Receivable from Related Party = ₹846.97 lakh (FY25), but the RPT balances table (Note 35, part 3(b)) shows
  "Other receivable" for the Subsidiary Company alone at ₹646.97 lakh with no other related-party receivable balance
  shown in that table. The two numbers differ by exactly ₹200 lakh / one leading digit (846.97 vs 646.97) — could be a
  transcription/OCR artefact in the source document or a genuine gap between the two notes' scope. Needs a management
  question. 🟡 Watch.
- **Unsecured loans from related parties are the company's only long-term debt.** Standalone Note 16: all long-term
  bank term loans are ₹NIL in both FY25 and FY24 (were ₹633.00 lakh FY23, ₹2,082.50 lakh FY22); the entire long-term
  borrowing balance of ₹1,800.44 lakh is an unsecured loan from HCP Enterprise Limited (promoter-group entity) at 9%
  p.a. (Note 31, p.108). 🟡 Watch — the parent's only long-term financing is now intercompany.
- **Cross-guarantees between parent and subsidiary.** Standalone/consolidated Note 34/36: Corporate Guarantee given
  BY the Company (parent) TO Bank of Baroda FOR loans of subsidiary K.P. Woven Pvt Ltd = ₹5,500.00 lakh (both years).
  Separately, Corporate Guarantee given BY the subsidiary TO Bank of Maharashtra FOR the parent's working capital
  facility = ₹6,922.00 lakh (both years) (Annual_Report.pdf p.108, p.111, p.169). Combined ₹12,422 lakh of
  cross-guaranteed exposure against a consolidated net worth of only ₹5,957.15 lakh — **more than 2x net worth.**
  🔴 Red Flag — legitimate group-financing structure, but creates real contagion risk: financial distress at either
  entity has direct recourse to the other via personal/corporate guarantees (personal guarantees of Mr Prakash Parekh
  and Mrs Madhu Parekh also stand behind the facilities, Note 31).
- KMP turnover was heavy in the year to 16 Dec 2023 / 12 Aug 2023 (Executive Director, CFO, Company Secretary,
  one Independent Director all changed within months of each other) (Note 35, p.112) — consistent with a
  post-resolution-plan management reconstitution, not flagged as a current-year (FY25) event but useful context.

## 9. PROVISIONS

- Gratuity (defined benefit, funded status): standalone net liability grew from ₹2.26 lakh to ₹5.98 lakh (Note 30,
  p.106-107); consolidated (parent + subsidiary combined) grew from ₹43.45 lakh to ₹61.49 lakh (Note 32, p.163).
  Plan is unfunded (fair value of plan assets = NIL both entities, both years) — a small but consistent gap between
  "funded" as described in the accounting policy and "unfunded" as shown in the numbers. 🟡 Watch — policy text says
  "Post Employment Defined Benefit Plan-Gratuity (Funded)" but Fair Value of Plan Assets is ₹NIL every year shown.
- **Employee turnover assumption (parent/standalone) = 56.00% p.a., unchanged FY24 and FY25** (Note 30.1, p.107;
  Note 32.1, p.164). This is an extremely high actuarial turnover assumption for a manufacturing workforce and is a
  genuine HR/organisational-stability signal, not just an actuarial technicality — it directly reduces the gratuity
  liability recognised (higher assumed attrition = lower expected payout = lower provision), so it is also

  service, 20% for 3-4 years, 15% for 5+ years) — a materially different (lower) turnover assumption for the same
  employee-benefit obligation within the same group. 🔴 Red Flag — both for what it implies about parent-entity
  workforce stability, and for the inconsistency in actuarial assumptions between the two group entities.
- No warranty provisions, no decommissioning provisions, no onerous-contract provisions disclosed. NOT FOUND IN
  DOCUMENT (plausible nil for this business).
