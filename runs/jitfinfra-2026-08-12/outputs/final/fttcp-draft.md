## THE CATALYST STORY IN PLAIN WORDS

**Revenue has the strongest evidence in the file.** The order book is Rs 11,352 Cr against Rs 2,808 Cr of annual revenue, split Rs 8,767 Cr of construction work and Rs 2,585 Cr of operations and maintenance. Both roughly doubled in the year. This is an audited disclosure, not a claim. What confirms it: quarterly revenue holding above Rs 800 Cr and the eleven project completions management targets for FY27 actually landing, against three delivered in FY26. What kills it: the EMS precedent, where a peer in the same government funded water business lost 36% of its revenue in one year to payment delays, elections and weather.

**Margin has a real lever but it is slow and small.** The mix shift toward operations and maintenance and hybrid annuity work is supposed to carry 18% to 25% margins against roughly 12.7% on plain construction. The evidence is management commentary, not contract level disclosure, so it is discounted. The one dated near catalyst is Jodhpur, 10 MW, commercial operation planned on or before October 2026, civil work 70% complete with major equipment already at site. Ten megawatts against 110 already running will not move a blended margin much. What kills it: commodity cost, which already forced EIEL to cut FY27 guidance.

**Cash conversion is where the catalyst search comes up close to empty, and that is the finding.** There is no factoring facility, no disclosed debtor day target, no renegotiated payment terms, and no customer advance programme anywhere in the annual report or the results filings. Management does list tightening working capital as an FY27 priority, but that is words, and the annual report's own risk section does not name working capital, going concern, or the related party funding concentration at all. Two documented items could release cash: the Bathinda arbitral award of 21 May 2025, which the municipal counterparty has not honoured, and the Jalandhar and Ferozepur awards of about Rs 294.7 Cr, at the Supreme Court since February 2026 after four years unresolved. Both are real and both have a poor conversion record. The unused credit line growing to Rs 589.84 Cr is the wrong kind of good news: it is more borrowing capacity to fund the gap, not a plan to close it. What confirms a turn: a sanctioned and dated receivables facility, or overdue receivables falling below 8%. What kills it: overdue receivables above 15% with the provision still near 0.12%.

**ROCE depends on whether commissioning outruns the new capital.** Capital already sunk at Jodhpur, Kakinada, Nellore, Bawana and the Okhla expansion is sitting in capital work in progress earning nothing. As each plant commissions, earnings arrive on capital already counted, which lifts ROCE mechanically. Against that, the group keeps starting new projects, and each start puts fresh dead capital into the denominator. What confirms it: FY27 ROCE printing above 15%, which would be the first time in eleven years. What kills it: FY27 ROCE flat or below 14% while capital work in progress keeps climbing.

### THE EARNINGS BASIS QUESTION, for you to decide

Both bases have a problem here, and you should know that before choosing.

**Trailing P/E does not currently work.** FY26 profit attributable to owners was minus Rs 48.13 Cr, so trailing earnings per share is minus Rs 18.73 and a trailing multiple has no meaning. Adding back the one off charge does not fix it: the Rs 45.92 Cr ESOP charge sits at JWIL, which is 54.6% owned, so the owners' share of the addback is about Rs 25 Cr and owners' profit stays negative at roughly minus Rs 23 Cr.

**One year forward P/E works only on a modelled number.** There is no management earnings guidance anywhere in the filings, so an FY27 owners' earnings per share would be built, not quoted. It also has to clear a real hurdle: owners' profit has to cross from negative to positive, which requires the ESOP charge not repeating and interest, currently Rs 401.29 Cr against Rs 604.43 Cr of EBITDA, not rising further.

**The honest note on fit:** B04 already ruled that P/E is not the right primary tool for this company, because consolidated book value is negative and owners' profit diverges sharply from group profit through the minority interest. It named sum of the parts primary and EV/EBITDA secondary. On EV/EBITDA the company currently trades at 7.7 times, being Rs 892.83 Cr of market value plus Rs 3,745.06 Cr of audited net debt over Rs 604.43 Cr of EBITDA. One structural fact matters for that route: JWIL on its own had net worth of Rs 713.1 Cr at 31 March 2026 while the consolidated group was minus Rs 513 Cr, so the value sits in the water arm and the accumulated deficit sits above it.

My recommendation is that you treat the destination PE as a cross check on the water EPC slice and let Phase 3 lead with sum of the parts. But the earnings basis choice is yours, and I have not made it.
