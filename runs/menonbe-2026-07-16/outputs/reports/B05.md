**Pattern check:** Management is candid and specific on near-term operational misses (RM timing, GST/tariff softness, Africa disruption) and will directly concede when a strategic KPI (Alkop growth) has undershot. However, on the SINGLE most-repeated, highest-stakes miss — the Brakes dynamometer/railway approval — the excuse degrades in quality over four quarters, ending in an explicit refusal to explain in the most recent call. This is the one area where the pattern tips toward deflection rather than honest accounting. Hard topics (Alkop underperformance, margin guide-downs) are mostly raised only when an analyst asks (reactive), not proactively volunteered in the opening remarks, which stay uniformly upbeat across all four calls.

**Excuse pattern classification: balanced-with-one-notable-deflection** (not purely external-blame-heavy, not purely honest — the dynamometer thread is the exception that drags the overall rating down).

### 2D. What they are NOT saying

- **Menon Alkop's negative reserves.** Per stage B02, Menon Alkop Ltd (the subsidiary management repeatedly frames as the second growth engine, targeted for ₹95-120cr of FY27/2-year revenue) carries negative Reserves & Surplus of ₹(206.98) lakh against the parent's unimpaired carrying cost of ₹2,823.50 lakh — a ~4.6x gap with no impairment recognised (AOC-1, AR pp.41-42). This is never mentioned, referenced, or explained on any of the four calls, even while Alkop's slower-than-guided growth is being discussed openly. Likely reason for silence: it is a going-concern-adjacent, governance-sensitive disclosure that would undercut the growth narrative being built around Alkop.
- **The related-party advance to Menon Brakes** (grew 44.9% YoY per B02, interest-free/unsecured, undisclosed terms) is never addressed on any call, even as Brakes is discussed as an emerging profit center.
- **A reconciled Alkop revenue number.** Three different figures (₹85-90cr FY26 / ₹120cr FY27 / ₹95cr FY27-from-₹40cr-base / ₹50-60cr incremental-in-2-years) are given across four calls and one presentation, never reconciled against each other even when analysts (Madhur Rathi, Q3 FY26 call) ask for the bridge.
- **Order-book methodology.** "Order book" is used interchangeably with forward revenue guidance throughout (e.g. Q2 FY26 call: "current order book position is for this year it is ₹290cr and for the next year it is ₹350cr" — a full-year revenue figure, not a contracted backlog). No cancellation rate, no aging, no definition of what counts as "in the order book" is ever volunteered.
- **Closure on three dropped initiatives** (Canada OEM, US competitor's 800-part aftermarket offer, Menon Ventures EV charging, distributor-network target) — none is explicitly withdrawn; they simply stop appearing in the transcript with no analyst follow-up forcing a status update.
- **Quantified customer concentration by name.** Management asserts "no customer above 15% of revenue" (Q4/FY26 call) but never provides a concentration table; given Alkop's growth being tied heavily to John Deere entities (albeit described as multiple "vendor codes"), this claim is asserted rather than evidenced.
