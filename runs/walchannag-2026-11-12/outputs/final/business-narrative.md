# Walchandnagar Industries (WALCHANNAG): business narrative, phase 1

Phase 1 lite. Evidence stages only. No valuation has run, so this file carries no entry zone, no decision and no exit multiple. Units: Rs Cr throughout (the annual report and results print in Rs Lakhs; 100 Lakhs = Rs 1 Cr).

## Business understanding

Walchandnagar Industries is a 117 year old heavy engineering company that builds large steel equipment to its customers' drawings at plants in Walchandnagar, Satara and Dharwad. About 31% of FY26 revenue came from defence, nuclear, aerospace and missile work: rocket motor casings and segments for ISRO launch vehicles including Gaganyaan, missile casings, and nuclear reactor parts such as calandria and end shields. About 55% came from product lines the company designs itself, mainly industrial and marine gearboxes, sugar centrifugals, crushing and grinding machines, and process equipment such as heat exchangers, columns and kilns. The last 14% came from iron castings, machining and instruments. A customer needs these parts because they are large, made to a qualified specification, and must be welded, heat treated and pressure tested by a shop the programme has approved; the claim that only two to four Indian shops can do this is the company's own.

The strategic work goes to the state: ISRO and VSSC, DRDO, the Department of Atomic Energy, NPCIL, BARC and the defence ministry, in one off orders won by tender after long qualification. The product lines sell to sugar mills, cement and chemical plants, shipyards and export buyers, and regular customers pay in about 75 days. The company names no customer and says none holds more than 10% of receivables, and its Gaganyaan order pays no tooling money and supplies the raw material free, which are the terms a buyer gives a fabricator it can replace.

Present demand comes from government programmes, so the signals that carry it are ISRO/VSSC procurement and Gaganyaan programme milestones, MoD DAC acceptance of necessity and DRDO missile contracts, and the NPCIL tender and award of calandria and end shield packages. On the product side, sugar mill capex and ethanol policy drive centrifugal and gearbox orders, and the capital goods output index tracks the cement, steel and power spending behind industrial gearboxes.

The forward case rests on nuclear. The SHANTI Act rules notification and private nuclear plant announcements could open private orders that management expects within 12 months of November 2026, and an End Shield tender is open now; neither has turned into an order. The market the run could size, nuclear qualified equipment plus organised gearboxes, grows about 8% a year, while the defence, aerospace, sugar, process and foundry pools could not be sized at all. Competitor nuclear island awards to L&T, Godrej and BHEL, and the Elecon gear division order book and margin, are the outside checks on how much of this demand the company wins.

The emerging moat scan found one moderate category out of 22: rare manufacturing capability in the strategic line, resting on programme qualification and a run of aerospace awards. That position earns fabricator margins on the filed record, about 8 to 9% before other income against 23 to 24% at MTAR and Elecon, so the moat is a qualification gate and gives no evidenced pricing power. MTAR, which has qualified for this scope, now competes for the same End Shield and calandria packages. The product lines carry no moat the run could document: no cost advantage, no network effect, and no spares revenue figure for an installed base the company puts at 4,500 centrifugals and 6,500 gearboxes. The foundry has no moat. The last two categories, talent asymmetry and what a rival would have to give up to copy the position, both scored zero.

Defence hopes, legacy revenue, stretched balance sheet

Walchandnagar sold Rs 275 Cr of equipment in FY26. Legacy product, foundry and instrument lines made 69% of it. Defence, nuclear, aerospace and missile work made about 31%. The company files standalone accounts only. The Walchand promoter group holds about 31.6% of the shares.

The transition the company claims is a climb from industrial product maker to strategic supplier. Some of the record supports it. H1 FY27 revenue was Rs 184 Cr, up 82% on H1 FY26. EBITDA before other income moved from a loss of Rs 5 Cr to a profit of Rs 16 Cr over the same halves. Q2 FY27 brought Rs 38 Cr of aerospace orders, and VSSC placed a Rs 31 Cr Gaganyaan motor case order in August 2026. The rest of the record cuts the other way. The strategic share of revenue fell from 39% in FY25 to about 31% in FY26, and the FY26 annual report stopped printing it. FY26 growth came from product lines and exports, while India revenue fell 13.5%. The foundry rebound after its 2025 lockout supplied 40% of the Q2 FY27 rise.

The order book is the load bearing gap. The annual report shows Rs 872 Cr of orders on hand at 31 March 2026. The rating agency shows Rs 375 Cr of unexecuted orders at 30 June 2026. No filing defines either figure or bridges the Rs 497 Cr between them. Order inflow is filed for one recent quarter only: Rs 99 Cr in Q2 FY27, 1.06 times that quarter's revenue. The FY26 book to bill works out at 0.87 times. Without one stated definition, nobody can say whether the backlog is refilling.

The ten year numbers are poor. Gate 0 scores the company 10 of 160 and classifies it AVOID, with no moat confirmed. Nine of the last ten years were loss years. Median ROCE (return on capital employed) is about 4%. FY26 net debt of Rs 144 Cr was 9.75 times EBITDA, and interest cover was 0.63 times. Other income made 59% of FY26 EBITDA and between 17% and 68% of quarterly EBITDA over the last four quarters. Profit before tax excluding other income was negative in each of the last three quarters, and the Q1 FY27 profit includes a Rs 3.3 Cr gain on an asset sale. The headline FY26 profit swing was Rs 71 Cr. Like for like, after the FY25 TNEB loss provision of Rs 54 Cr and other income are taken out, it was about Rs 31 Cr. The counterweight is the run rate: since Q3 FY26 the company has held Rs 81 to 94 Cr of revenue and Rs 7 to 15 Cr of operating profit a quarter. Four quarters is a short run.

Cash has not followed the revenue. Operating cash flow was minus Rs 11 Cr in FY26 and minus Rs 6 Cr in H1 FY27. Of Rs 201 Cr gross receivables, 56% are over three years old, mostly long pending TNEB dues. Unbilled revenue adds Rs 121 Cr with no ageing given. The rating agency calls the working capital cycle intensive, at 640 gross current asset days, and expects it to stay at similar levels over the medium term. Borrowings rose from Rs 219 Cr in March 2026 to Rs 246 Cr in September 2026, and 85% of the March figure was due within a year. A 17.5% NCD is secured by a pledge of promoter shares equal to 15.63% of the company, which is about half the promoter holding. Rs 19 Cr of NCD falls due in FY27, to be paid from land sales that have sat unsold for more than a year. Acuite rates the company BB Stable and calls liquidity stretched. Against this, cumulative operating cash flow over FY17 to FY26 was a positive Rs 508 Cr while cumulative profit was negative, and investment property carried at Rs 1.5 Cr has a stated fair value of Rs 32 Cr.

Management says more than it shows. There has been no earnings call since May 2019, so the credibility read rests on press releases, filings and the annual report. The grade is C: three promises delivered, two partly delivered, five missed. Verifier B would grade it D. EBITDA stayed positive and sales picked up, as promised. The order book did not grow, the strategic share was dropped from disclosure, operating cash flow reversed, and debt rose while the release spoke of a stronger balance sheet. A Rs 400 Cr wind tunnel opportunity flagged in May 2025 has produced no order. A planned preferential share issue by FY2027 appears only in the rating rationale. Rs 21 Cr of issue money moved from capex to debt and working capital while filings said "no deviation" for five quarters. The CFO left after 15 months. The promoter verdict is CAUTION: the pledge, a flat promoter share count through the warrant conversions, and a 2015 SEBI order against nine group entities still pending in the Supreme Court, with no evidenced integrity breach. The statutory record is clean: the audit opinion is unmodified and there is no default.

Peers confirm the setting and widen the gap. MTAR, ISGEC and Elecon all quantify order inflow and define their order books; Walchandnagar's 1.06 times sits at the bottom of the peer range. MTAR and Elecon printed EBITDA margins of 24% and 23% in Q2 FY27, against 8 to 9% here before other income and the 25 to 40% the company's own presentation implies for strategic work. All three peers cite rising steel and component costs. All three also run late on capex, so the company's two quarter slip is normal for the group. MTAR confirms the nuclear pipeline is real and puts End Shield work at Rs 350 to 400 Cr per reactor, and it is bidding for the same packages for the first time. Peers report defence order flow slipping and cite no space or missile boom.

The market opportunity is real but only partly sized. The two pools the run could measure, nuclear qualified equipment and organised gearboxes, average Rs 5,315 Cr a year on the conservative read and Rs 12,538 Cr on the realistic read over FY27 to FY31. The serviceable market is Rs 5,315 Cr, an upper bound, where the company holds about 5.8%. Headroom is about 17 times current revenue, and the runway class is GOOD. Management's Rs 1 lakh Cr opportunity is 3.8 times the sized market over five years and mixes scope. Capacity is the binding limit: the reachable share runs Rs 170 Cr above capex supported revenue by year three. New capex would need funding the balance sheet does not have today.
