## SECTION 1: THE BUSINESS MODEL IN PLAIN ENGLISH

### 1A. One-line description
CMS is India's largest outsourced cash-and-ATM operations partner for banks. It runs the trucks, the vaults, the machines and, increasingly, the software and cameras that watch all of it, so banks do not have to (AR pp.2-3, "Three Platforms" AR pp.6-7).

### 1B. Money flow chain, one per revenue stream

**Stream 1 — ATM Management Solutions (~58% of services revenue, AR p.56-57, MD&A p.51)**
[Bank signs a multi-year outsourcing contract] → [CMS forecasts cash need, replenishes 70,000+ ATMs, runs Brown Label ATMs (BLA) under its own brand, and does the hardware/software upkeep] → [CMS delivers uptime, cash availability and reconciliation data] → [the bank (fixed-fee contracts) or the ATM's own transaction stream (BLA, volume-linked contracts) pays] → [payment via monthly service fee (bank contracts) or per-transaction interchange/fee revenue (BLA)] (AR pp.22-23; MD&A p.51 "Since Brown Label ATM contracts are volume-linked, revenues were hit directly").

**Stream 2 — Retail Solutions and Currency Logistics (~26% of services revenue, AR p.56-57)**
[Retailer, e-commerce firm, NBFC or bank branch generates cash that must move safely] → [CMS picks up, transports (cash-in-transit), digitises and reconciles the cash through Retail 360 and roughly 1,400 CIT routes across 20,000 bank branches] → [CMS delivers audited, banked cash plus working-capital analytics] → [the client pays] → [mostly per-route / per-service fixed-fee arrangements] (AR pp.24-25).

**Stream 3 — Technology and Payment Solutions (~16% of services revenue, up from 12% FY25 and ~7% FY22, AR p.57)**
[Bank or non-BFSI client wants remote monitoring, ATM software, or card issuance] → [CMS layers HAWKAI (Vision AI on existing cameras), ALGO MVS (ATM software) or its card-personalisation facility onto its own or the client's infrastructure] → [CMS delivers alerts, uptime software, or issued cards] → [client pays licence/subscription-like or per-unit fees] (AR pp.26-27).

**Stream 4 — Product / hardware sales (implied ~7% of total revenue, "Purchase Cost" note, MD&A p.52)**
[Bank wants to buy ATMs, cash recyclers or kiosks outright] → [CMS resells hardware sourced from OEMs] → [CMS delivers installed machines] → [bank pays a one-time capital price]. This is a trading, not a services, line and it is volatile: Rs10 Cr in Q1 FY27 versus Rs56 Cr a year earlier, described by management as "a cyclical trough" (Inv. Pres. slide 7).

### 1E. The chai-stall-uncle version
Imagine the local kirana shop's cash box, but for the whole country's ATMs and bank branches. Every day, someone has to drive money from a vault to a machine, make sure the machine has the right amount, and watch that nobody steals it on the way. CMS is the company banks hire to do exactly that, for 70,000-plus machines. Now imagine that same delivery guy also carries a camera and a laptop: he is starting to watch the branch for trouble and run the software that tells the bank how the machine is doing. That second job pays better and grows faster than the cash-driving job. The bet on CMS is that the camera-and-laptop business (Tech & Payments) keeps growing faster than the truck business (Cash), and the market starts paying CMS software-company prices instead of truck-company prices.

### 2D. Industry lifecycle stage
Cash logistics (the ATM Management Solutions core) sits in a mature, consolidating industry: total India ATM count is contracting (per company memory, ~4% YoY) even as currency in circulation grows, and the sector just lost its #2 listed player to insolvency. Technology & Payment Solutions sits in an early-growth stage, expanding from 7% to 16% of services revenue in four years (AR p.57) with a stated TAM of Rs8,000 Cr against current HAWKAI-only revenue of roughly Rs200 Cr (Inv. Pres. slide 20) — see the HAWKAI figure discrepancy flagged below. CMS's position: incumbent consolidator in the mature segment, early leader in the growth segment.

### 4B. Valuation method applicability (handoff to Role 1)

| Method | Applicability | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Sum-of-the-parts (SOTP) | High — three platforms have genuinely different growth (Tech ~25-42% YoY vs Retail -7.8% YoY FY26), margin, and predictability profiles that a single blended multiple would distort | **PRIMARY**. Why: MD&A itself separates and quantifies three distinct economics (AR p.56-57); valuing them separately and summing respects the transition thesis directly (rising Tech share = re-rating engine) |
| Normalised / through-cycle P-E, peer-referenced | High, as a cross-check | **SECONDARY**. Why: single-year P/E is distorted by the FY26 cash-supply shock and elevated capex; a peer set exists in company memory (Radiant, SIS, Quess, global anchors Brink's/Loomis/Euronet) though none of it is AR-sourced |
| DCF | Moderate | **TERTIARY**. Why: long-dated contracts (SBI 10yr) and historically strong cash conversion (OCF/EBITDA 70%+ through FY25) support a cash-flow approach, but FY26's conversion dip (65.9%) and rising DSO argue for treating DCF as a cross-check, not the anchor, until WC trend is confirmed as transitory |
| EV/EBITDA (blended, single multiple) | NOT APPLICABLE as a standalone primary | Blends three platforms with different growth/margin profiles into one number; useful only as a sanity check on the SOTP sum |
| EV/Sales or ARR-style SaaS multiples | NOT APPLICABLE | Only ~16% of the business has SaaS-like economics; applying this to the whole company is the exact mis-classification this stage was asked to correct |
| Asset-based / replacement cost | NOT APPLICABLE | Value sits in the contract book, data, and relationships, not in the depreciated fleet/hardware |

Cycle stage that matters for valuation: the ATM Management Solutions core is mid-consolidation (post-AGS-collapse share gains still being absorbed) while Technology & Payment Solutions is early-growth. A valuation anchored only to the current blended (post-shock) earnings base risks under- or over-crediting the mix shift; Role 1 should weight the SOTP split explicitly rather than trend-line the consolidated P&L.
