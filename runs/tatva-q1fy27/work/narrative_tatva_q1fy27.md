## What unfolded this quarter

The quarter looked great on the surface. Revenue rose 43% over the same
quarter last year to Rs 167 Cr, the highest in any quarter shown. Reported
profit jumped 140% to Rs 16 Cr. The investor deck led with "EBITDA up 86%." If
you read only the headline, this was a blowout.

But most of the profit growth did not come from the core business. When you
split the Rs 9.3 Cr of profit growth into its real parts, only about a third
(Rs 3.3 Cr) came from Tatva's own factories earning more. About 28% came from a
one time jump in other income at the parent, helped further by the parent
paying almost no cash tax this quarter. And about 43% came from two small
overseas arms in the USA and Europe that have almost no staff, no factory and
no equipment, yet booked a third of the group's entire profit. Those arms look
like resale desks, not real operations, and the auditor gave no separate
comment on them. So the durability of nearly half the profit growth is an open
question.

Margins actually slipped, quarter on quarter. Operating margin fell from 21% in
the previous quarter to 19% this quarter, below the 20% to 22% level management
had targeted for the year. The deck did not restate that target. The year on
year "up 86%" headline hid this sideways to down move.

The company is quietly taking on more debt. Finance costs were five times
higher than a year ago, borrowings climbed, and the board raised the borrowing
ceiling more than three times over to Rs 1,000 Cr. Debt relative to earnings is
already above the level the thesis wanted to see.

The big growth story got board approval but not yet a shovel in the ground. A
new plant, Dahej-III, was approved: Rs 200 Cr, roughly 21 months to build. The
three founders were locked into their roles through January 2030, which lines
up with that build. But there was no groundbreaking, and the semiconductor
chemical shipment that was supposed to be a this quarter milestone was simply
not mentioned anywhere.

One number we most need was missing by rule. A June quarter filing does not
include a cash flow statement in India, so we cannot yet see whether the
reported profit is turning into real cash. That single missing number is the
one that most cleanly decides whether this business is healthy or whether cash
keeps leaking into receivables and inventory.

## What the next 10 to 11 months will decide

Around October 2026 (Q2 half year result): the make or break read. This is when
the first cash flow statement of the year arrives. Bull fork: cash generation
is positive and customers pay on time even as revenue grows, and the biggest
worry eases. Bear fork: cash is negative on rising revenue with collection days
stretching past 185, a pre set tripwire fires, and the name moves toward a hard
avoid.

Across the year, three things to watch. First, the overseas arms: bull if they
keep contributing at arm's length prices, bear if this was a one off markup.
Second, Dahej-III and the semiconductor shipment: bull if the plant breaks
ground and the first dispatch finally happens, bear if the silence continues.
Third, margin and SDA: bull if margin climbs back to 20% to 22% and the SDA
product line, running near Rs 231 Cr annualised, reaches its Rs 250 Cr to
300 Cr goal, bear if margin stays under 20% and SDA misses.

## Bottom line

The operating business is genuinely recovering, and that part is real. The
price today, around Rs 1,194, is roughly ten times higher than the Rs 97 to
121 zone where this becomes a buy, so the stock stays on the watch list as an
avoid. A strong quarter does not close a ten times valuation gap, and a profit
beat that leans this heavily on non core arms and one off items does not change
the verdict. The decision stays where it was. The real test comes in October.
The pipeline flags; the operator decides.
