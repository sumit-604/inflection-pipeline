Run date: 2026-08-04 | Model: claude-sonnet-5 | Stage: B09-tam

Currency: all figures ₹ Crores unless marked USD. FX used throughout for
USD-to-INR conversion: **₹95.3/USD**, the current spot rate as of
2026-08-04 (Tradingeconomics / Federal Reserve H.10, 2026-08-03/04, via
web search). This single current-spot rate is applied to every USD
figure regardless of the underlying report's vintage (2024, 2025, or
2035-projection), which modestly **overstates** the INR value of

directionally it works in the conservative-vs-management direction

at a weaker historical rate would deflate them — net effect is small
and does not change the ordering of estimates).

## SECTION 1: MARKET DEFINITION

### 1A — Precise boundaries

- **Product scope:** ionic (anionic) and non-ionic surfactants, mild
  surfactants, and specialty blends/rheology modifiers, pearlising
  agents, UV filters, soap bases and conditioning agents — sold as
  **B2B intermediates/ingredients**, not finished consumer products
  (CARE rating note p.2; AR Product Portfolio p.4-5; B04-bizmodel.yaml).
- **Geographic scope:** India domestic (80.8% of FY26 standalone
  revenue) as the primary market; export to 30+ countries across Asia,
  Europe, Africa and the Americas (18.5% export + 0.7% deemed export =
  19.2% combined) as a secondary, capacity-constrained overlay
  (REVENUE_ANCHOR; B04-bizmodel.yaml).
- **Customer scope:** large, qualified FMCG and industrial accounts —
  named preferred-supplier relationships with Hindustan Unilever,
  Procter & Gamble, Colgate-Palmolive and Dabur among others (CARE
  Report p.2); NOT the informal/regional formulator segment that buys
  from unorganised traders. Top-5 customers = ~92% of FY25 revenue,
  top-2 = 71.0% (B04-bizmodel.yaml / CARE Report p.2, AR Note 36 p.144).
- **Channel scope:** direct B2B qualified-vendor supply, long
  approval-cycle relationships (switching-cost moat per B04); excludes
  retail/distributor channels.
- **Price segment:** commodity-adjacent specialty — B04 explicitly
  classifies pricing power as "weak" and valuation as EV/EBITDA (not a
  premium specialty multiple), i.e. this is priced closer to a
  volume/cost-pass-through business than a branded-ingredient
  specialty player.
- **Explicit inclusions:** surfactants and specialty HPC ingredients for
  home care, personal care, and industrial applications (per injected
  PRODUCTS scope); R&D-driven premium/eco-friendly formulations
  (Navi Mumbai R&D centre, AR p.39-40).
- **Explicit exclusions:** finished FMCG consumer products (detergents,
  shampoos, soaps as retail goods — these are the AR's cited
  beauty/personal-care and home/laundry-care finished-goods markets,
  which Aarti does **not** participate in directly, see Section 1B
  flag below); agrochemical adjuvant surfactants and oilfield/drilling
  surfactants (mentioned only as generic "industrial surfactant"
  demand drivers in the AR, with no disclosed Aarti product-line
  overlap); personal-care active ingredients that are not surfactants
  (vitamins, actives, preservatives — a distinct market IMARC and
  others size separately).

### 1B — Management's own TAM claim

The FY 2024-25 Annual Report's "Industry Overview" (MD&A, p.35-38) does
not contain a single explicit "our addressable market is ₹X" statement.
The closest analogue — and the number placed most prominently, directly
ahead of the "Company Overview" section — is:

> "the Indian Surfactants industry is envisioned to sustain its growth
> trajectory and attain a market size of USD 10.92 billion by 2035"
> (AR p.36-37, footnote 4, source cited: Market Research Future,
> "India Surfactants Market" report)

**Date:** cited in the AR for FY2024-25 (filed ~Sept 2025).
**Definition given:** none beyond "Indian Surfactants industry"; no
current-year base value is disclosed anywhere in the AR text itself.
**Credibility read: BROAD**, leaning toward inflated selection bias, for
three reasons established during Section 2 web search:
1. Market Research Future's own 2024 base year for this same series is
   **USD 3.99 billion** — nearly **2x** IMARC Group's independent 2024
   estimate of USD 2.03 billion for the same India surfactants market
   (WebSearch, both accessed 2026-08-04). Management cited only the
   most distant (2035) endpoint of the source with the highest base
   and one of the highest CAGRs (9.58%, 2025-2035) among five
   independently checked research firms (IMARC 6.80%, MarkNtel 9.49%,
   Grand View Research ~6.2%, MRF 9.58%), without disclosing the base
   year figure — a classic "big number, distant year" TAM-inflation
   framing.
2. The same MD&A section, immediately around this figure, also cites
   large **finished-goods** market sizes that are NOT Aarti's
   addressable market at all: India beauty & personal care industry
   "market size of USD 48.3 billion by 2033" (AR p.37, footnote 7),
   home & laundry care industry (6.2% CAGR 2025-2030, footnote 5), hair
   care (USD 9.70bn by 2030, footnote 6), oral care (USD 3.2bn by 2033,
   footnote 8), baby care (USD 31.68bn by 2030). All five are
   **downstream finished-product** markets; Aarti sells the upstream
   surfactant/ingredient going into a fraction of these products, not
   the finished goods themselves. Presenting these adjacent, much
   larger numbers in the same breath as the company's own "Industry
   Overview" risks investors conflating a >$100bn combined finished
   goods opportunity with Aarti's actual (much smaller) addressable
   ingredient market. **Flagged.**
3. No current-year base, no explicit definition of what counts as
   "surfactants" in the cited report (industrial vs HPC vs both), and
   no China/global cross-reference are given alongside the 2035 figure,
   making it impossible to sanity-check from the document alone — this
   analysis had to source that independently (Section 2).

This claim is held for the mgmt_claim_ratio calculation in Section 2's
triangulation table.

## SECTION 2: TAM ESTIMATION, MULTIPLE METHODS

### Method 1 — Top-down (industry reports)

Global surfactants industry, CY2024: **USD 39.5 billion**, Grand View
Research, cited directly in Aarti's own AR (p.36, footnote 3) — CAGR
5.3% 2024-2030. Converted: USD 39.5bn × 95.3 = **₹376,435 Cr** (global,
shown for context per instruction #7; not carried into SAM/SOM since
Aarti's addressable business is ~81% India-domestic and its
manufacturing/export capacity is a single-country footprint).

India surfactants market — **four independently checked sources
diverge materially**, triangulated below (all WebSearch, 2026-08-04):

| Source | Base year value | Horizon | CAGR |
|---|---|---|---|
| IMARC Group | USD 2.03bn (2024) | to USD 3.85bn by 2033 | 6.80% (2025-2033) |
| MarkNtel Advisors | USD 2.11bn (2025) | — | 9.49% (2025-2030) |
| Grand View Research | not disclosed in snippet | to 2033 | 6.2% (2025-2033) |
| Market Research Future (mgmt-cited) | USD 3.99bn (2024) | to USD 10.92bn by 2035 | 9.58% (2025-2035) |

IMARC and MarkNtel corroborate each other closely (USD 2.03-2.11bn,
2024-2025) despite different methodologies and forecast windows — this
convergent pair is the strongest evidence base. MRF's base is an
outlier, roughly **2x** the IMARC/MarkNtel cluster, and is the one
management chose to cite (see Section 1B flag). **This divergence is
flagged rather than averaged away**, per pipeline instruction #5:
different research houses are very plausibly using different product
boundaries (e.g. MRF may include a broader "surfactants and specialty
formulation chemicals" scope, or industrial + agro surfactants that
IMARC excludes). Absent access to each report's full methodology, the
conservative-bias rule (instruction #6) governs: **take the IMARC/
MarkNtel cluster, not MRF, as the TAM anchor.**

- Conservative TAM (India): IMARC 2024A, USD 2.03bn × 95.3 = **₹19,346 Cr**
  → rounded ₹19,350 Cr. *STALE flag: 2024-vintage data, ~1.5-2 years
  old as of the 2026-08-04 run date — approaching but not yet past the
  4-year "direction only" threshold; carries Medium, not High,
  confidence.*
- Realistic TAM (India): blend of MarkNtel 2025 (₹20,109 Cr) and
  IMARC rolled forward one year at its own 6.80% CAGR (₹20,660 Cr) →
  **₹21,000 Cr**.
- Excluded from the TAM anchor: MRF's USD 3.99bn (₹38,024 Cr) 2024
  base and USD 10.92bn (₹104,048 Cr) 2035 endpoint — retained only for
  the mgmt_claim_ratio test below.

### Method 2 — Bottom-up

Unit: India finished home-care + beauty/personal-care retail market,
with an estimated surfactant/ingredient revenue-share applied (Aarti
sells the ingredient, not the finished good, so this method backs into
an implied ingredient-market size from the finished-goods base it feeds).

- India household cleaners (home care) market, 2025: **USD 11.90bn**
  (IMARC-family estimate, WebSearch 2026-08-04).
- India beauty & personal care market, 2025: **USD 26.58bn** (Expert
  Market Research — deliberately the *lowest* of four cross-checked
  2025 estimates ranging USD 23.73-33.08bn: IMARC USD31.19bn, Statista
  USD33.08bn, Research&Markets USD23.73bn — conservative-bias rule
  applied).
- Combined finished HPC goods, India, 2025: **USD 38.48bn**.
- Surfactant/specialty-ingredient revenue as a share of finished
  product retail value: formulation science sources confirm surfactant
  **active content by weight** typically runs 10-20% in shampoo/
  personal-care formulations (WebSearch, joanmorais.com / cosmetic
  formulation literature); after accounting for retail markup, brand,
  packaging, and distribution margin layered on top of raw ingredient
  cost, this analysis applies a conservative **6% ingredient-value
  share** of finished retail price (Low confidence — no single
  authoritative source ties formulation weight-% directly to
  ingredient revenue-%; flagged as an estimated assumption, not a
  sourced fact).
- Implied bottom-up TAM: USD 38.48bn × 6% = USD 2.309bn × 95.3 =
  **₹22,006 Cr**.

**Cross-check:** ₹22,006 Cr (bottom-up) sits within ~15% of the
₹19,350-21,000 Cr top-down cluster — a reasonably tight convergence
given the bottom-up method's assumption-driven ingredient-share input.
This corroboration raises confidence in the ₹19,350-22,000 Cr band as
the credible current India surfactants/HPC-ingredient TAM range.

### Method 3 — Peer revenue aggregation (sanity check only, not
averaged into the TAM anchor)

FY26 revenue (screener Data_Sheet CSVs, this run's inputs) with
export/domestic splits sourced separately (WebSearch, 2026-08-04):

| Company | FY26 total revenue | Domestic % | Domestic revenue |
|---|---|---|---|
| Galaxy Surfactants | ₹5,244.78 Cr | 36% (undated third-party profile, Medium confidence) | ₹1,888 Cr |
| Rossari Biotech | ₹2,396.37 Cr | 72% (28% export, Q2FY26, company disclosure) | ₹1,725 Cr |
| Fineotex Chemical (FCL) | ₹772.23 Cr | 78% (22% export, Q4FY25, company disclosure) | ₹602 Cr |
| Ultramarine & Pigments — **Surfactants & Specialities segment only** | ₹489 Cr (segment, FY26; total company ₹774.77 Cr includes an unrelated Inorganic Pigments segment of ₹164 Cr — segment isolated to avoid overstating the surfactants-relevant number) | assumed predominantly domestic | ₹489 Cr |
| Aarti Surfactants (subject company) | ₹859.13 Cr | 80.8% | ₹694 Cr |

Sum, listed organized peers' India-domestic revenue (including Aarti):
1,888 + 1,725 + 602 + 489 + 694 = **₹5,398 Cr**.

This is **not** grossed up mechanically for an "unorganised sector"
percentage, because the gap versus the Method 1/2 top-down cluster
(₹19,350-22,000 Cr, a ~3.6-4x gap) is very plausibly explained mostly
by **organized-sector volume sitting outside this four-company listed
peer set** — specifically Godrej Industries' chemicals (oleochemicals/
surfactants) division, and the India operations of the MNC competitors
CARE Ratings names directly (BASF, Clariant, Croda, Evonik, Solvay,
Stepan, Dow) — none of which are separately listed pure-plays with
disclosed India-segment revenue available in this run's inputs.
Mechanically grossing up ₹5,398 Cr by a standard 30-60% "unorganised"
assumption would overstate confidence in that specific split. **Read
this ₹5,398 Cr as a floor / lower-bound sanity check**: it should sit
below the full-market TAM, which it does, supporting (not
contradicting) the ₹19,350-22,000 Cr top-down/bottom-up cluster.

### Method 4 — Import substitution: NOT COMPUTED

Aarti's raw materials (lauryl alcohol, HCFA, alpha olefin) are
palm-oil-linked and import-exposed (CARE Report p.2), but this is an
**upstream feedstock** import dependency, not a finished-surfactant
import-substitution dynamic for Aarti's own output — Aarti is a net
exporter of its finished specialty surfactants (18.5%+0.7% of
revenue), not competing against imported finished surfactants
domestically in a quantifiable way with the data searched. A precise
India surfactant HS-code import/domestic-production split was searched
for (linear alkylbenzene trade data) but only fragment-level LAB
volume data was found (India LAB market ~150,000 tonnes 2024, to
200,000 tonnes by 2033, IMARC) — not a full surfactants-category
import-substitution base. **Method 4 marked NOT FOUND / not
computed this run**; the AR's own directional support (Indian chemical
industry >USD 11.5bn planned investment, PLI/"Make in India" tailwind,
AR p.39) is carried into Section 4 as a qualitative driver only.

### Method 5 — Global benchmark (per-capita)

All comparisons done in USD (no FX conversion needed for a ratio):

- Global: USD 39.5bn / ~8.2bn world population ≈ **USD 4.82/capita**.
- China (Market Research Future, same source family as the India
  figure for internal consistency): USD 4.5bn (2024) / ~1.41bn
  population ≈ **USD 3.19/capita**.
- India, conservative anchor (IMARC 2024): USD 2.03bn / ~1.45bn
  population ≈ **USD 1.40/capita** — roughly **29%** of global average
  and **44%** of the China benchmark.
- India, per management's own MRF-cited base (2024): USD 3.99bn /
  1.45bn ≈ USD 2.75/capita — roughly 86% of the China benchmark. This
  large swing (29% vs 86% of the China benchmark, depending purely on
  which India base is used) is itself further evidence that the
  IMARC/MarkNtel vs MRF divergence in Method 1 is a genuine
  methodology/scope difference, not noise.
- Illustrative long-run convergence scenario (**not** the SAM/SOM base
  case — shown only as directional headroom): if India converged to
  half the global per-capita average (USD 2.41/capita) over an
  unspecified multi-year horizon, implied India market = 1.45bn ×
  2.41 = USD 3.50bn ≈ **₹33,355 Cr** — roughly 1.6-1.7x the current
  conservative TAM anchor. This is a multi-year structural scenario,
  not a 3-5 year SOM input.

## SECTION 3: SAM & SOM

### 3A — SAM

Five filters applied sequentially to the conservative TAM (₹19,350 Cr),
per the conservative-bias rule (build the funnel off the lower TAM
anchor, not the realistic one):

1. **Product fit (65%):** India's total surfactants market spans HPC
   (detergents, personal care), industrial, agrochemical-adjuvant and
   oilfield/textile-processing end uses (AR p.36: "detergents, soaps,
   emulsifiers, foaming agents and dispersants, agriculture,
   pharmaceuticals and industrial processes"). Aarti's disclosed scope
   is HPC-focused ionic/non-ionic surfactants plus some industrial
   application (PRODUCTS input); no precise India segment-split by
   end-use was found in searches, so this analysis applies an
   estimated 65% HPC-relevant share (Low confidence, flagged — no
   sourced split; personal care and household detergents are
   qualitatively described as the largest global end-use segments per
   the AR's own text, supporting a majority-but-not-total share).
   → ₹19,350 × 0.65 = **₹12,578 Cr**.
2. **Channel (60%, organized-only):** Aarti's qualified-vendor B2B
   model serves only the organized/branded-FMCG-facing channel, not
   the informal/regional-formulator segment served by unorganised
   traders. No sourced India-specific organized/unorganised split was
   found (searched, not found — input gap); this analysis applies the
   pipeline's standard India assumption band (30-60% unorganised,
   i.e. 40%-70% organized), taking the **conservative 60% organized**
   figure. → ₹12,578 × 0.60 = **₹7,547 Cr**.
3. **Customer (70%, large-account only):** within the organized
   channel, Aarti's qualification-heavy model realistically competes
   only for large MNC/top-domestic-FMCG accounts (its own top-5 = 92%
   concentration is direct evidence of this narrow addressable
   customer set), not the full organized-but-smaller-formulator base.
   Estimated 70% (Low confidence estimate, no sourced split).
   → ₹7,547 × 0.70 = **₹5,283 Cr**.
4. **Geography:** no separate cut applied — the TAM base is already
   India-only; Aarti's export revenue (19.2% of sales, 30+ countries)
   sits *outside* this India SAM as upside optionality, consistent
   with conservative bias (see 5D).
5. **Capability:** no separate % cut applied here (to avoid double
   counting with the 3C physical-capacity check below); Aarti's
   113,000 MT nameplate capacity is a volume ceiling, not a
   product/customer-scope filter.

**SAM = ₹5,280 Cr** (rounded). **SAM as % of TAM = 5,280 ÷ 19,350 =
27.3% (~27%).**

Cross-check against Method 3's peer floor: SAM ₹5,280 Cr sits close to
(slightly below) the ₹5,398 Cr listed-organized-peer sum — a
reasonable internal consistency check, since the peer sum should
approximate "who is actually competing in the addressable segment
today" and the SAM funnel is meant to approximate the same thing from
the top down.

### 3B — SOM at 3 and 5 years

**Current SAM share:** Aarti's India-domestic revenue (80.8% ×
₹859.13 Cr = ₹694 Cr, REVENUE_ANCHOR / B04) ÷ SAM (₹5,280 Cr) =
**13.1%**.

**Share-gain trajectory (share-gain rules applied):**
- 3-year case: **normal** gain of +1.5pp (not the aggressive 3-5pp
  band) — justified as normal-not-aggressive because, while the
  ~₹85 Cr Pithampur/Silvassa capacity expansion (Dec-2026 target) is a
  concrete, funded execution catalyst (B07_CAPEX_FIGURE), B04 flags
  currently-undisclosed capacity utilization, extreme customer
  concentration limiting new-account capture, and a margin trend that
  has *worsened* despite 30.4% FY26 revenue growth ("currently
  negative-to-flat operating leverage" per B04) — none of which
  support an aggressive share-gain case yet.
  → SOM 3yr share = 13.1% + 1.5pp = **14.6% of SAM**.
- 5-year case: **moderate-aggressive** gain of +3.0pp (low end of the
  3-5pp "aggressive" band) — justified by the capacity expansion
  having 4+ years to run within the 5-year window post its Dec-2026
  completion, giving genuine physical headroom for volume-led share
  gain, capped at the low end of "aggressive" given the same execution
  and margin-trend caveats above.
  → SOM 5yr share = 13.1% + 3.0pp = **16.1% of SAM**.

**Arithmetic (SAM itself grows with the market at the conservative
6.80% CAGR, IMARC):**

| | Yr0 (FY26) | Yr3 | Yr5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| SAM (India, ₹ Cr), grown at 6.80% CAGR | 5,280 | 5,280×1.068³ = 6,431 | 5,280×1.068⁵ = 7,333 |
| Aarti SAM share | 13.1% | 14.6% | 16.1% |
| Aarti India-relevant revenue (₹ Cr) | 694 | 939 | 1,181 |
| Grossed to total-company revenue (÷80.8% domestic mix, assumed constant) | 859 | **1,163** | **1,462** |

**SOM 3yr = ₹1,163 Cr. SOM 5yr = ₹1,462 Cr.**

**SOM-implied revenue CAGR (FORMAL handoff to Stage 11):**
- 3-year: (1,163 ÷ 859.13)^(1/3) − 1 = **10.6%**
- 5-year: (1,462 ÷ 859.13)^(1/5) − 1 = **11.2%**

Sanity check: this SOM-implied CAGR (10.6-11.2%) is **below** Aarti's
own disclosed 6-year historical sales CAGR of 14% (Investor
Presentation, "Milestone" slide, p.4) and well below the one-off FY26
YoY growth of 30.4% (REVENUE_ANCHOR) — i.e. the SOM math implies
deceleration from the historical trend rather than an implausible
acceleration, which is the conservative, credible direction for this
cross-check to point.

### 3C — Capacity cross-check

Current installed capacity: **113,000 MT** nameplate at Pithampur +
Silvassa (CARE Report p.2). Committed capex: **~₹85 Cr** over FY26-27,
~₹60 Cr debt-funded, ~₹23 Cr spent by H1FY26, targeting **December
2026** completion (B07_CAPEX_FIGURE). **Capacity-utilization % and the
capex program's added nameplate tonnage are both NOT FOUND** in any
document provided this run (inherited gap from B04-bizmodel.yaml).

Directional math (illustrative only, given the data gap): at 100%
utilization of the *current* 113,000 MT nameplate, FY26 revenue of
₹859.13 Cr implies ~₹76,020/tonne average realization — plausible for
a palm-oil-linked specialty surfactant mix but almost certainly means
current utilization is **below** 100% (a plant running at full
nameplate would typically show a higher realized ₹/tonne given this
product mix), suggesting some headroom exists before the physical
ceiling binds even before the new capex comes online. SOM 5yr (₹1,462
Cr total company) against the *unchanged* 113,000 MT nameplate would
imply ~₹129,400/tonne — a meaningfully richer mix/pricing assumption,
plausible only with real product-mix premiumization plus the new
capacity (which the capex program's own stated purpose — "capacity
expansion of products and launching new products," CARE Report p.3 —
directionally supports) actually landing before then.

**Capacity check verdict: indeterminate, not "sufficient."** Because
neither the utilization rate nor the capex-added tonnage is disclosed,
this analysis cannot mechanically confirm a ₹ Cr gap or its absence.
Directionally the SOM trajectory does not look obviously incompatible
with the disclosed capex plan's stated purpose and timing, but this is
inferential. **Neither side (SOM vs capex plan) can be confidently
called "the optimistic one" given the missing utilization/tonnage
anchor — this is named as a data gap, not resolved as a verdict.**

### 5B — Runway assessment

- **Revenue headroom** = SAM ÷ current revenue = 5,280 ÷ 859.13 =
  **6.14x**.
- **TAM growth rate** = 6.80% (conservative anchor CAGR, IMARC;
  realistic-cluster range up to ~9.5%).
- **Company CAGR vs TAM:** Aarti's own 6-year historical CAGR (14%,
  Investor Presentation Milestone slide) and FY26 YoY growth (30.4%,
  one-off/base-effect distorted) both **exceed** the 6.8-9.5% TAM
  growth range — i.e. Aarti has been **gaining share**, not merely
  riding the market, consistent with its rising customer concentration
  (a share-gain outcome, though one concentrated in very few accounts
  — see B04's moat/risk duality flag).
- **Years to saturate SAM at current growth:** using the more durable
  14% historical CAGR (not the distorted 30.4% single-year figure):
  859.13 × (1.14)^n = 5,280 → n = ln(6.145)/ln(1.14) ≈ **13.9 years**.
  Using the (unlikely-to-repeat) 30.4% single-year growth rate as an
  upper-bound sensitivity: n ≈ 6.9 years.

### 5C — Runway classification

**STRONG.** Basis: revenue headroom of 6.1x current revenue, SAM at a
reasonable 27% of a corroborated (two-method-triangulated) TAM, TAM

growth rate before SAM saturation. Not classified MASSIVE (headroom
under 10x, and the SOM math implies deceleration versus history, not
open-ended acceleration) and not downgraded to GOOD/MODERATE purely
for company-quality reasons — the share-gain assumptions feeding SOM
were already built conservatively (normal, not aggressive, gain rates)
