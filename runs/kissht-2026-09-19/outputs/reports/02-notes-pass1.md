**But the rollforward (p.128) shows why:** "Assets written off during the year" = **₹4,725.95 mn**, entirely

new defaults enter it.

**This is the single most important finding of Pass 1 and speaks directly to LBF-1**: the reported low and
improving GNPA-equivalent ratio (2.12-2.25%) is achieved through an aggressive and *increasing* write-off
policy (~15-16% of the book annually), not through improving underlying credit quality — new defaults
into Stage 3 grew nearly 29% year on year. The 150-DPD write-off threshold cited in company context could
not be verified against this AR (see B1); this makes independent verification of the exact write-off trigger
via the RHP/results filings a priority live-verification item.

The **management overlay (₹1,359.53 mn, FY26)** sits on top of the model ECL and is nearly 59% the size
of the base model's own output (₹2,309.56 mn) — a very large discretionary layer for a young public
company, essentially flat year on year (₹1,349.89 mn FY25) even as the base model ECL grew 27%. 🟡 Watch.
