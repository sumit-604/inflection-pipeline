## SECTION 1: MARKET DEFINITION

### 1A. Precise boundaries

Awfis runs two distinct revenue engines (B04, Note 31 FY26 AR): co-working
space on rent and allied services (82.8% of FY26 revenue, Rs1,236.85 Cr)
and construction and fit-out projects / Transform, design-and-build (17.2%,
Rs256.63 Cr, of which third-party work is Rs152 Cr, AR p.34/B04). These sit
in genuinely different markets and are sized separately below.

**(A) Co-working / managed office ("flexible workspace")**
- Product scope: shared desks, private cabins, premium Gold/Elite formats,
  managed offices for enterprises/GCCs, allied services (meeting rooms,
  events, F&B). Includes both "managed aggregation" (co-invested with
  landlord) and pure operator-funded straight-lease/partial-MO formats.
- Geography: India only. Awfis itself is present in 18 cities (9 Tier 1 +
  9 Tier 2, Investor_Presentation_1 p.2/deck line 334); the underlying
  market is measured across "Tier 1" metro India (Bengaluru, Delhi-NCR,
  Mumbai, Pune, Hyderabad, Chennai, Kolkata) plus a smaller, fast-growing
  Tier 2 layer (Prospectus p.199).
- Customer scope: enterprises, GCCs, SMEs, start-ups, freelancers — B2B/
  B2SME, not residential.
- Channel scope: direct operator-to-occupier licence agreements (not
  brokerage). Excludes owner-occupied/purchased corporate real estate.
- Price segment: organized/branded operators at standard-to-premium price
  points. Excludes the large unbranded/informal "Grade B/strata" shared-desk
  layer that the CBRE Report itself calls out as outside the "flexible
  workspace" category (Prospectus p.167, line 12569-12570).
- Explicit exclusion: co-living, industrial/warehouse flex, retail flex,
  and flex markets outside India.

**(B) Design & build / office fit-out contracting ("Transform")**
- Product scope: space planning, interior design and project execution for
  workplace fit-outs, for both Awfis's own centres (captive/internal) and
  external corporate clients (third-party, the stated growth engine, AR
  p.14: "positions Transform to capture a much larger addressable market of
  corporate fit-out spend in India" — qualitative claim, no number given).
- Geography: pan-India, 20+ cities in FY26 (deck line 634) — broader
  reach than the coworking business since fit-out does not require Awfis to
  hold the lease.
- Customer scope: "marquee enterprises" for multi-city national fit-outs
  (deck line 634) — skews toward larger corporate/MNC mandates, not the
  full universe of small-tenant fit-outs typically done by local
  contractors.
- Excludes: residential, retail, hospitality and industrial fit-out (the
  bulk of the broader "India Interior Design Market," a USD35.5bn 2026
  figure that is not comparable — see Method note below).

## SECTION 2: TAM ESTIMATION, MULTIPLE METHODS

### 2A. Co-working / managed office

**Method 1 — Top-down (two convergent CBRE-linked reports)**

*Independent report (primary, fresher):* CBRE and FICCI, "Flex-plosion:
India's Flexible Workspaces Era," 24-Mar-2026 (not commissioned by Awfis).
Key figures (web search, cross-cited by Business Standard, FICCI press
release, CBRE India site, 24-Mar-2026):
- India flex stock: 110-114 million sq ft (2025-26), tripled since 2020,
  23-25% CAGR over the past five years.
- Market value: USD6 billion (2025), projected to exceed USD11 billion by
  2030 (~14% CAGR).
- Structure: 500+ operators, ~2,600 centres nationwide.
- Listed flex players' combined market cap (20-Mar-2026): ~USD2-2.2bn
  (~Rs18,300 Cr) — this gives the report's own implied FX rate: Rs18,300
  Cr / USD2.1bn = **Rs87.1/USD**, dated ~20-Mar-2026. I use this rate
  (not the 19-Sep-2026 spot of Rs95.8/USD, tradingeconomics.com, which
  reflects nine months of subsequent rupee depreciation, not market
  growth) to convert the report's own USD figures.
  - USD6bn x Rs87 = **Rs52,200 Cr** (2025)
  - USD11bn x Rs87 = **Rs95,700 Cr** (2030 projection)

*Company-commissioned cross-check:* the CBRE Report cited in the 2024
Prospectus (Section 1B above): Rs47,400-59,200 Cr "by 2026." The two
reports, issued two years apart by related CBRE research teams, land in
the same band (Rs47,400-59,200 Cr vs Rs52,200 Cr) despite one being
company-paid and the other independent. This is a genuine, meaningful
cross-validation.

**Realistic estimate (Method 1): Rs52,200 Cr (2025-26), growing toward
~Rs95,700 Cr by 2030 (~14% CAGR).** Confidence: M-H (two independent
vintages of the same data family converge; FX-conversion introduces
modest additional uncertainty).

**Method 2 — Bottom-up (seats x realized pricing)**

Addressable unit: one occupied seat (desk/cabin equivalent).
- Total units: the Prospectus (CBRE Report, Dec-2023 data, p.175) gives
  Tier-1 flex stock of 62 million sq ft against ~950,000 workstations —
  an implied density of **65.3 sq ft/seat**. Applying this density (the
  only sourced density ratio in the corpus) to the 2025-26 national stock
  of 110-114 million sq ft (Method 1): **1.68-1.75 million seats**
  nationally. This density figure is itself Dec-2023 vintage (STALE,
  >2yr), used here only as a ratio, not a headline number.
- Revenue per unit: market-survey pricing (secondary sources, lower
  confidence): coworking Rs5,000-25,000/seat/month; managed office
  Rs8,000-20,000/seat/month (LinkedIn/stylework.city coworking pricing
  guides, 2026). Blended annualized range: Rs60,000-300,000/seat/year.
- TAM range: 1.68-1.75M seats x Rs60,000-300,000/seat/year =
  **Rs10,100-52,500 Cr**, midpoint **~Rs26,000-30,000 Cr**.

Confidence: L-M (seat density is a stale ratio; pricing is from secondary
aggregator sources, not a primary research house).

**Method 3 — Peer revenue aggregation + unorganized estimate**

Named FY26 revenue of the six largest organized/listed-or-listing flex
operators (all figures FY26, web search of company results releases,
Apr-Jun 2026):

| Operator | FY26 revenue (Rs Cr) | Source |
|---|---|---|
| WeWork India | 2,477 | company FY26 results release, May-2026 |
| Table Space | 2,262 | Entrackr/Inc42, FY26 results, 2026 |
| Smartworks | 1,796 | company FY26 results release, 2026 |
| Awfis | 1,493 | this run, results 20260525 p.18 (audited) |
| Indiqube | 1,469 | company FY26 results / DRHP, 2026 |
| DevX | 226 | company FY26 results release, 2026 |
| **Total (6 named)** | **9,723** | |

Per CBRE-FICCI, this is 6 of 500+ national operators across ~2,600
centres — the named set almost certainly excludes several other
Rs100-1,000 Cr-scale organized chains (91Springboard, Cowrks, BHIVE,
Skootr, IWG/Regus India and others) that could not be individually
sourced within this stage's time budget (see SEARCH LOG). Applying the
instruction's stated "often 30-60%" unorganized-sector uplift to the
named-6 total as if it were the full organized segment:
- At 40% unorganized share: Rs9,723 Cr / 0.60 = **Rs16,200 Cr**
- At 50% unorganized share: Rs9,723 Cr / 0.50 = **Rs19,400 Cr**

**Conservative estimate (Method 3): Rs16,200 Cr.** Confidence: L — this
is a data-availability floor, not a confirmed market ceiling, because the
"named-6" base already omits several known organized competitors before
any unorganized-sector adjustment is even applied.

**Method 5 — Global benchmark (qualitative only)**

APAC flex penetration averaged ~4% of Grade A office stock as of Mar-2023
(Prospectus p.167). India's own penetration (12.5% of occupied non-SEZ
stock, Dec-2023, same report) already sits well above this APAC average —
though the two figures use different denominators (Grade A stock vs
occupied non-SEZ stock, a basis mismatch the Prospectus itself footnotes,
p.12850-12851), so this is directional evidence only, not a numeric
TAM input. It weighs against the standard "India is under-penetrated vs
global peers, so runway is huge" framing: on a comparable basis India may
already be ahead of the regional average, not behind it.

**Method 4 (import substitution): not applicable** — a domestic services
market with no import/export dynamic.

**Triangulation table — co-working / managed office**

| Method | Estimate (Rs Cr) | Confidence | Staleness |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Top-down (CBRE-FICCI 2026 + CBRE 2024 cross-check) | 52,200 (2025-26) | M-H | Fresh (Mar-2026) + STALE cross-check (Apr-2024) |
| 2. Bottom-up (seats x pricing) | 26,000-30,000 (midpoint) | L-M | Density ratio STALE (Dec-2023) |
| 3. Peer aggregation + unorganized est. | 16,200-19,400 | L | Fresh (peer FY26 results, 2026) but incomplete peer set |
| 5. Global benchmark | qualitative only | — | STALE (Mar-2023) |

**Conservative estimate: Rs16,200 Cr. Realistic estimate: Rs52,200 Cr.**

The methods diverge materially (~3.2x between the conservative floor and
the top-down figure). The divergence is explained, not averaged away: the
top-down figure is corroborated twice, independently, by the same
research house family (CBRE, 2024 and 2026); the peer-aggregation floor
is mechanically capped by how many of the 500+ national operators could
be individually priced in this stage (6). Per this stage's fixed
methodology (rule 6, conservative bias), the Rs16,200 Cr figure is carried
forward as the formal "conservative" TAM for the SAM/SOM funnel below,
but the realistic Rs52,200 Cr figure is the better-evidenced number and
should be weighted more heavily by Stage 11 (see analyst note).

**Management claim vs conservative estimate:** Rs53,300 Cr (midpoint of
the CBRE 2024 claim) / Rs16,200 Cr (conservative) = **3.29x → "inflated"**
by the mechanical >2x rule. Against the realistic estimate (Rs52,200 Cr),
the same claim is 1.02x — "reasonable," in fact almost exactly matched.
**The most evidenced reading: management's number is not inflated; the
"inflated" read is an artifact of the conservative estimate's incomplete
peer coverage, not evidence that CBRE (or Awfis) overstated the market.**
The formal YAML field below still reports the mechanical 3.29x/"inflated"
result, as the rule instructs, with this caveat carried in the analyst
note.

### 2B. Design & build / office fit-out

No named India-specific "office fit-out market size" report was found
(JLL, Knight Frank, Anarock, Mordor/Ken/RedSeer style reports were not
individually pulled in the time available — see SEARCH LOG). This is a
data gap, named per rule 2, and the estimate below is analyst-constructed
from two anchored primary inputs, not a directly reported market-size
figure. Confidence: **L** throughout this sub-section.

**Method 2 — Bottom-up (only method used for D&B; Methods 1/3/4/5 skipped,
no sourced peer revenue set exists for pure-play India fit-out
contractors)**

- Addressable unit: one sq ft of newly leased office space requiring a
  fit-out.
- Total units: India's record CY2025 gross office leasing, 82.6 million
  sq ft (AR FY26 p.4; independently corroborated by Smartworks and
  Indiqube calls per B06). Assumption: essentially all newly leased Grade
  A commercial space requires some interior fit-out before occupancy.
- Revenue per unit: Cushman & Wakefield "APAC Office Fit Out Cost Guide
  2026" (24-Mar-2026): Rs5,847-6,567/sq ft for a "collaborative hybrid
  workplace" build, across Mumbai (highest, Rs6,567), Delhi (Rs6,207),
  Bengaluru (Rs6,027) and Ahmedabad/Chennai/Hyderabad/Kolkata/Pune
  (Rs5,847). Blended mid-point used: Rs6,000/sq ft.
- **Realistic TAM (100% of leased space, professional-grade fit-out
  cost):** 82.6M sq ft x Rs6,000/sq ft = **Rs49,600 Cr/year**.
- **Conservative TAM:** not all leased space uses an organized/branded
  D&B contractor like Transform — much flows through landlord-provided
  interior teams, informal local contractors, or is bundled into the
  lease. Applying a 35% "addressable via an organized D&B vendor" haircut
  (assumption, stated explicitly, not sourced): Rs49,600 Cr x 0.35 =
  **Rs17,300 Cr/year**.

Sanity ceiling (not used as an estimate): the "India Interior Design
Market," USD35.5bn in 2026 (Mordor Intelligence, 2026), is far larger but
spans residential, retail and hospitality fit-out well outside Transform's
scope — cited only to show the office-only figure above is a small,
plausible slice of a much broader design/build economy, not to anchor a
number.

**Conservative estimate: Rs17,300 Cr/year. Realistic estimate:
Rs49,600 Cr/year.**

## SECTION 3: SAM & SOM

Both tracks (conservative and realistic) are shown; the formal YAML
handoff uses the **conservative track** per rule 6, with the realistic
track carried as the named alternative.

### 3A. SAM — co-working / managed office

Five filters applied to TAM, each shown as a percentage subtraction:

1. **Product fit: 100%.** TAM is already defined at the flexible-
   workspace-operator level; no further product subtraction needed.
2. **Geography: 92%.** Awfis covers all major Tier-1 metros plus 9 of the
   national Tier-2 set (18 cities total); Tier-1 is ~90-91% of national
   stock (Prospectus p.199: Tier-2 is 9-9.5% of the pan-India total), and
   Awfis does not yet reach every Tier-1 micro-market or every growing
   Tier-2 city.
3. **Customer fit: 95%.** Awfis serves the full SME-to-GCC spectrum; the
   discount reflects the largest single-tenant, fully dedicated campus
   deals that sometimes go to pure real-estate developers rather than
   flex operators.
4. **Channel/capability fit: 97%.** Awfis's MA/straight-lease/partial-MO
   models can pursue most of the market structurally; a small discount
   for niche sub-scale/franchise-only regional formats it does not run.
5. **Price segment: 98%.** Standard-to-premium positioning already
   excludes the informal/unbranded tier (already stripped from the
   organized TAM above); minor residual discount only.

Combined factor: 0.92 x 0.95 x 0.97 x 0.98 = **0.831**

- **Conservative SAM = Rs16,200 Cr x 0.831 = Rs13,500 Cr (83% of TAM)**
- Realistic SAM = Rs52,200 Cr x 0.831 = Rs43,400 Cr (83% of TAM)

### 3B. SOM — co-working / managed office

Current base: FY26 co-working segment revenue **Rs1,236.85 Cr**
(results 20260525 p.18, audited, Note 31).

- Current SAM share (conservative): Rs1,236.85 Cr / Rs13,500 Cr = **9.2%**
- Current SAM share (realistic): Rs1,236.85 Cr / Rs43,400 Cr = **2.9%**

Share-gain assumption: given the credibility flags carried from B05/B06
(FY27 revenue guidance cut within one quarter with no acknowledgment;
occupancy plateau at 75-76% blended, contradicted by all three peers
running 80-92%; cash EBITDA margin 10.1% vs peer band 16-21%; credibility
grade C), the **normal** share-gain band (1-2pp over 3 years) is applied
rather than the aggressive band, even though the unorganized share implied
by the conservative track (~40%) would technically qualify for a faster
allowance. This is the most evidenced path given demonstrated execution,
not the most favourable one.

- 3-year: +1.5pp. 5-year: +2.5pp.

**Conservative track:**
- SAM share 3yr: 9.2% + 1.5pp = 10.7% -> SOM_3yr = 10.7% x Rs13,500 Cr =
  **Rs1,445 Cr**
- SAM share 5yr: 9.2% + 2.5pp = 11.7% -> SOM_5yr = 11.7% x Rs13,500 Cr =
  **Rs1,580 Cr**
- Implied CAGR 3yr: (1,445/1,236.85)^(1/3) - 1 = **5.3%**
- Implied CAGR 5yr: (1,580/1,236.85)^(1/5) - 1 = **5.0%**

**Realistic track (for comparison, not the formal handoff):**
- SAM share 3yr: 2.9% + 1.5pp = 4.4% -> SOM_3yr = 4.4% x Rs43,400 Cr =
  Rs1,910 Cr -> CAGR 3yr = **16.1%**
- SAM share 5yr: 2.9% + 2.5pp = 5.4% -> SOM_5yr = 5.4% x Rs43,400 Cr =
  Rs2,344 Cr -> CAGR 5yr = **13.6%**

The two tracks diverge sharply: ~5% CAGR (conservative) vs ~14-16%
(realistic), both well below management's own FY27 coworking growth guide
of 23-25% (B05) and FY26 delivered coworking growth of 35% YoY (B05/this
run's segment table). This is a material finding, not smoothed over: see
Section 5 and 3C.

### 3B (continued) — Design & build

Current base: **third-party** D&B revenue only, Rs152 Cr (AR p.34/B04);
internal/captive fit-out for Awfis's own centres is not market-facing
revenue and is excluded from the share calculation.

- Conservative SAM: five filters applied to the Rs17,300 Cr conservative
  TAM — product fit 90% (workplace-only, not retail/hospitality/
  residential), geography 95% (pan-India reach), customer fit 50%
  (Transform explicitly targets large "marquee enterprise" multi-city
  mandates, not the SME/small-tenant volume that dominates leasing by
  space count), channel/capability 95%, price segment 90% (premium/
  Grade A+ positioning). Combined factor: 0.90 x 0.95 x 0.50 x 0.95 x
  0.90 = 0.3655. **Conservative SAM = Rs17,300 Cr x 0.3655 = Rs6,320 Cr.**
  Realistic SAM = Rs49,600 Cr x 0.3655 = Rs18,130 Cr.
- Current SAM share: Rs152 Cr / Rs6,320 Cr = 2.4%
- Share gain: +1.5pp (3yr), +2.5pp (5yr), same normal band as above.
  - SOM_3yr = 3.9% x Rs6,320 Cr = Rs247 Cr -> CAGR 3yr = **17.5%**
  - SOM_5yr = 4.9% x Rs6,320 Cr = Rs310 Cr -> CAGR 5yr = **15.3%**

(These D&B growth rates sit below Transform's own delivered 27% CAGR
FY24-26 third-party growth, B04 — a conservative reading is intentional
given B04's FLAG-TRANSFORM-QUIET-RETREAT: total segment revenue actually
fell 7.8% YoY in FY26 even as the third-party slice grew.)

### 3C. Capacity cross-check

Injected capex figure: FY27 capex guidance Rs200-210 Cr, self-funded
(B07/Concall_Aug_2026 p.13, reiterated Concall_May_2026 p.15). FY27 gross
seat-addition guide: 22,000-25,000 seats (~1.25 million sq ft, Concall
May-2026). Revenue per seat: Rs74,000-97,400/year (B04, derived from
AR/deck).

- Capacity implied by the FY27 guide alone: 22,000-25,000 seats x
  ~Rs85,000/seat/year (mid-point) = **Rs187-212 Cr of incremental
  annualized revenue capacity, in one fiscal year.**
- Conservative combined SOM growth over the FULL 3 years (co-working +
  D&B): SOM_3yr (Rs1,445 + Rs247 = Rs1,692 Cr) minus current combined
  base (Rs1,236.85 + Rs152 = Rs1,388.85 Cr) = **Rs303 Cr over three
  years.**

**Finding: Awfis's single next fiscal year's capex-funded seat pipeline
(Rs187-212 Cr of new annualized revenue capacity) is nearly equal to the
ENTIRE 3-year conservative-track SOM growth (Rs303 Cr).** A gap of
roughly Rs180-190 Cr exists between what one year of guided capacity
addition could generate and what the conservative market-share path says
the company can profitably fill over three years.

**Which side is optimistic:** under the conservative track, the capex
plan is the optimistic side — Awfis is building capacity for a
market-share gain path materially larger than the conservative SAM/SOM
analysis supports, which is consistent with the already-flagged occupancy
plateau (75-76% blended vs peers' 80-92%, B06) and the twice-cut FY26
seat-addition guidance (40,000 -> 32,000-33,000 -> 30,000 gross, B05).
Under the realistic track (~14-16% combined CAGR), the capex plan is
roughly right-sized, or still conservative relative to the addressable
opportunity. The company's own execution record (credibility grade C,
B05) is the reason this stage does not resolve the gap in Awfis's favour
by default.

## SECTION 4: GROWTH DRIVERS, RISKS & STRUCTURE

### 4A. TAM growth drivers

- **Penetration (H, strong evidence):** flex penetration of gross office
  leasing: 18-21% (Q1 CY2026, AR p.4) -> "21% today" (Concall Aug-2026
  p.14) -> path to ~25% by 2027 (AR p.29; Concall Aug-2026 p.14; also
  independently the CBRE-FICCI Mar-2026 report frames the same
  trajectory). Multiple independent sources agree on direction and a
  similar 2027 destination.
- **New applications / GCC demand (H, strong evidence):** India GCC
  ecosystem crossed 2,100+ centres generating ~USD98.4bn revenue FY26E
  (AR p.29, sourced Nasscom-Zinnov/Colliers), up from USD61bn FY21 — a
  ~10% CAGR; GCCs now >40% of India CRE leasing (AR p.29); mid-market GCC
  segment (25-200 seats) named as "among the most underpenetrated"
  categories (AR p.29), directly aligned to Awfis's stated client-entry
  model (25-50 seats -> 100-300 seats -> managed office, B06 claim 8,
  UNVERIFIABLE against peers but internally consistent with the sector
  data).
- **Premiumisation (M, moderate evidence):** Gold/Elite footprint grew to
  35 centres (27 Gold + 8 Elite) by Mar-2026 (AR p.8); Awfis's own
  premium capex (Rs1,950-2,250/sq ft) runs above peers' blended capex
  (Smartworks Rs1,350, Indiqube Rs1,517-1,650/sq ft, B06 claim 6) — a
  real premium-tier build-out, though B05 flags the realization premium
  itself as not yet proven in margins across three quarters of questions.
- **Tier-2 expansion (M, moderate evidence):** Tier-2 flex stock grew
  65% CAGR 2019-2023 (0.76msf -> 5.7msf) and is projected to reach
  8.5-9msf by 2026 (~16% CAGR, Prospectus p.199) — a small absolute base
  (9-9.5% of national stock) growing fast.
- **Formalisation (M, moderate evidence):** the organized flex segment's
  own scale-up (four IPOs in ~18 months: Awfis May-2024, Smartworks and
  Indiqube Jul-2025, DevX Sep-2025) signals capital-market-backed
  consolidation of what remains a fragmented 500+-operator market.
- **Regulatory tailwind: NOT FOUND.** No positive regulatory driver is
  named in the corpus; GST/taxation change is instead named as a risk
  (AR p.30, see 4B).
- **Technology enablement / demographics (L, weak/qualitative evidence
  only):** hybrid-work culture and India's young, English-speaking talent
  pool are cited generically (Prospectus p.148 section) without a
  quantified driver specific to flex demand.

### 4B. TAM risks

- **Substitution / graduation risk (evidence: AR p.29, B04's own client
  lifecycle language):** the GCC client lifecycle Awfis itself describes
  (25-50 seats -> 100-300 seats -> managed office) implies the largest,
  most mature GCCs eventually graduate to owned campuses, capping the
  addressable life of Awfis's best clients. Monitoring signal: net seat
  growth from existing top-tier GCC clients vs new client-count growth.
- **Cyclical downturn / macro (evidence: AR p.30, "Opportunities and
  Challenges"):** interest-rate and real-estate-cycle movements,
  construction-cost inflation and global trade-policy shifts are named
  as challenges by the company itself. Monitoring signal: quarterly
  gross office absorption (CBRE/JLL/Colliers trackers).
- **Regulatory headwind (evidence: AR p.30; Indiqube Q4 FY26 call p.16-17,
  B06):** GST/taxation and workplace-norm changes named as a risk by
  Awfis; a peer (Indiqube) already names a 5-7% material-cost/tariff bump
  no Awfis call has attributed any part of its own cost story to (B06) —
  a disclosure gap worth monitoring.
- **Saturation in specific micro-markets (evidence: CBRE-FICCI Mar-2026):**
  Bengaluru alone holds 30-32 million sq ft of the 110-114 million sq ft
  national stock (~27-29% concentration) — a single-city concentration
  risk for the sector broadly, not Awfis-specific.
- **AI-driven hiring impact on office demand (evidence: B06, peers
  DevX and Indiqube address this on calls; Awfis does not):** a risk to
  the GCC-hiring-led demand thesis that Awfis's own calls do not
  substantively address despite GCC demand being its largest cited
  trigger.
- **Environmental restriction / import competition: NOT FOUND** — not
  applicable to a domestic services market.

### 4C. Market structure

- Competitor count: 500+ operators, ~2,600 centres nationwide
  (CBRE-FICCI, Mar-2026).
- Top concentration: the six largest named organized players (WeWork
  India, Table Space, Smartworks, Awfis, Indiqube, DevX) generate a
  combined Rs9,723 Cr FY26 revenue — against the conservative Rs16,200 Cr
  TAM this implies ~60% concentration, but this figure is almost
  certainly overstated given several other organized chains
  (91Springboard, Cowrks, BHIVE, Skootr and others) were not individually

  riding the market, consistent with its ~1-3% current SAM share

- Years to saturate SAM at current (Awfis) growth pace (~24% blended,
  FY27 guide): conservative track, ln(14.3)/ln(1.24) = **~12.3 years**;
  realistic track, ln(44.3)/ln(1.24) = **~17.5 years**.

## Analyst note

Two things a downstream stage cannot reconstruct from the fields alone.
First, the conservative/realistic TAM divergence (Rs16,200 Cr vs
Rs52,200 Cr for co-working) is not a genuine range of opinion about market
size — it is the gap between a data-availability floor (only 6 of 500+
national operators could be individually priced within this stage's
budget) and a figure cross-validated twice by the same research house
family two years apart. Stage 11 should not treat the "conservative"
number as more defensible simply because it is lower; it is lower because
fewer competitors could be named, not because the underlying market is
smaller. Second, the capacity-vs-SOM gap in 3C (Rs180-190 Cr) reads
differently depending on which track is trusted: on the conservative
track it says Awfis is over-building; on the realistic track it says the
capex plan is appropriately sized. Given Awfis's own credibility grade C
(B05) and its occupancy plateau contradicted by all three peers (B06),
this stage recommends Stage 11 treat the realistic-track SOM CAGR
(~14-16% combined) as the primary cross-check on revenue growth
assumptions, while still naming the execution risk that the capex plan
could be sized for a market larger than Awfis is currently proving it can
fill.
