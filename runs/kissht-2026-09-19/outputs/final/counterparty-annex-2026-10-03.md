## 1. Piramal Finance, Q1 FY27 earnings call transcript

File `01-PIRAMALFIN-Q1FY27-transcript.pdf`. Call 16-Jul-2026, filed 22-Jul-2026.

### The co-lending PL and FLDG passage, in full

QUOTE (PDF p.15-16; analyst Avinash Singh, then Jairam Sridharan of Piramal Finance):

> Avinash Singh: "Yeah, hi. good evening. Thanks for the opportunity. One question on
> your co-lending arrangement, particularly if I see from the disclosures, it's mostly
> on the personal loan side. And there if I see, I mean, you know, the ticket size would
> be slightly on the lower side as per kind of your PL mix. So nearly 2,700 odd crores
> worth, 99% in kind of a standard asset. Generally I would expect this to be typically
> high yielding and slightly higher credit cost and maybe a bit worse kind of a, you
> know, the asset quality matrix here. So can you just help us in terms of what kind of
> partner they are where you have co-lending arrangement, FLDG arrangement kind of a
> thing and how sort of you know, really the accounting works. I mean, is there
> something that typically you write off immediately or something because 99% plus
> kind of a, you know, the number in this co-lending PL, it looks quite impressive."
>
> Jairam Sridharan: "Yeah, no, you've answered yourself. What you're saying is
> absolutely correct. That's exactly what's happening. See, basically the way our
> co-lending business works is with mostly it's smaller ticket, short duration, and it
> is mostly on an FLDG basis, 80 plus percent of it is on an FLDG basis. When an account
> gets to 90 plus, within 30 days, we recover the money from our partner and hence the
> delinquency goes back to zero, right? And if any amount is remaining at the end of that
> period, by 150 days we make the 100% provision on that case. So basically, not much
> remains in the account, immediately either the money collects within 30 days. Because
> that's the FLDG norm that you have to collect from your partner, which we do, or we
> provide for it pretty quickly and that's why you don't see the delinquency build up."

COMMENT.
- The Rs 2,700 Cr and "99% standard" figures are the ANALYST's, read off a
  disclosure the analyst does not name. Management endorses the description ("you've
  answered yourself ... absolutely correct") but does not restate either number. Tier:
  MGMT endorsement of an analyst figure, not a management disclosure.
- The mechanism matters for Kissht directly. On the lender's side, a 90+ DPD account on
  an FLDG book is recovered "from our partner" within 30 days, and anything left is
  fully provided by 150 days. So a partner's reported delinquency on such a book reads
  near zero by design, and the loss lands on the fintech that gave the FLDG. This is
  consistent with Kissht's standalone "First loan default guarantee cost" of Rs 100.95 Cr
  in FY26 being a cash-settled cost rather than a provision build (extraction annex 2,
  item 4). It does not prove Piramal is a Kissht partner.
- The 150-day point in this passage is PIRAMAL's provisioning point on its co-lending
  book. It is not Kissht's write-off point, though it coincides with the 150 DPD figure
  Kissht's CFO gave on the Q4 FY26 call.
- Partner identity: NOT DISCLOSED. The analyst asks "what kind of partner they are";
  the answer describes the structure and names no partner.

### The test: could MAS be Kissht's Partner 4?

Kissht's RHP gives Partner 4 at Rs 715.53 Cr (FY23), Rs 828.03 Cr (FY24) and Rs 96.38 Cr
(FY25), as off-book AUM held on that partner's books (extraction annex 3).

What the document supports:
- MAS runs exactly the kind of channel a fintech partner could sit in: lending through
  182 NBFC partners in which "the NBFC partner is responsible for both sourcing the
  end-customer and managing the risk of default". Si Creva is an NBFC. That is
  STRUCTURALLY CONSISTENT with a 100-0 or BC arrangement backed by a first-loss
  guarantee. It is not evidence that Si Creva was one of the 182.
- MAS's co-lending in this report runs the OTHER way: MAS originates and sells down
  ("assignment of portfolio/co-lending ... 2,242.21 crore", "without recourse to the
  Company"). That is MAS as originator, not as the funding partner of a fintech.

What the document rules out:
- Partner 4 cannot have been a pool MAS bought by direct assignment. MAS's entire
  outstanding book of loans acquired through assignment was Rs 167.94 Cr at FY23 and
  Rs 322.75 Cr at FY24. Partner 4's book was Rs 715.53 Cr and Rs 828.03 Cr at the same
  dates, larger than MAS's total acquired-assignment book in both years.

What the document does not support:
- Any naming of Si Creva, OnEMI or Kissht: NOT FOUND in the 313-page report.
- Any partner-wise exposure, FLDG or DLG disclosure: NOT DISCLOSED. No "default loss
  guarantee" disclosure appears anywhere in the FY24 report.

VERDICT: NOT DETERMINABLE. The direct-assignment route is excluded on size. The
NBFC-partner or BC route is neither confirmed nor excluded by this document. The one
filed document that could settle it is MAS's own Si Creva disclosure
(mas.co.in/documents/SiCrevaCapitalServicesPrivateLimited-Kissht.pdf), which was not
