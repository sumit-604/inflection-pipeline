## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

### 2A. Demand environment
All three peers describe FY26 as a year of real allocation friction (tender
shortfalls, an OMC waterfall/methodology shift, and — critically — litigation)
rather than either a demand boom or a demand collapse. TRIVENI is the most
explicit: Cycle 1 nationally near-fully subscribed (1,048/1,050cr L) in Nov-2025,
but "the ethanol tender this year has been a huge disappointment... we haven't
even had R2" by Jun-2026, and by Jul-2026 the entire national tender program was
frozen behind a Supreme Court stay (see 2E). This matches TruAlt's own narrative
of an allocation shock disclosed only after the fact, and dates the mechanism
(litigation, not demand weakness) consistently with TruAlt's own excuse.

### 2B. Pricing and input costs
Consistent across all three: the FCI rice mandate (40% floor) materially
softened maize and broken-rice prices through most of FY26, and grain-based
ethanol margins for GULPOLY and TRIVENI held or improved through the year, not
compressed. Separately, BALRAMCHIN's cane-route ethanol (B-heavy, juice) prices
have not been revised for three years despite a record UP cane-price (SAP)
hike — a real margin-pressure story, but on a different feedstock route than
TruAlt's grain-based business, so it does not directly cross-check TruAlt's
margin claim; it is included here because it is the closest sector analogue to
"margin squeezed by an input-cost/output-price mismatch," and BALRAMCHIN's
version of that story is real and disclosed with dates, unlike GULPOLY's and
TRIVENI's grain economics, which moved the other way.

### 2C. Capex cycle
None of the three peers is expanding core ethanol distillation capacity in the
period reviewed. GULPOLY has explicitly paused all new capex until FY28,
prioritising cash generation and deleveraging (repeated in three of four calls).
BALRAMCHIN's capex is concentrated in PLA/bioplastics (Rs 3,080cr, a Rs 230cr
cost overrun, funded by a Rs 450cr preferential equity raise) — a diversification
away from, not into, core cane/ethanol capacity. TRIVENI's incremental capex
(Rs 340cr) is in its power-transmission/defence gearbox business, now demerged
into TPTL; its distillery growth this year is entirely from de-bottlenecking and
reactivating an idle acquired unit (Shamli), not new-build ethanol capacity.
TruAlt, by contrast, is the lone aggressive expander in ethanol-adjacent capex
in this peer set (CBG Sumitomo ~Rs 940-980cr, CBG GAIL Rs 425-650cr, SAF
Rs 2,000-2,250cr) at a point where three peers are each independently
de-risking away from core ethanol capacity exposure. This is a lone-expander
situation, not an industry-wide capacity race — see Part 5.

### 2D. Competitive mentions
No peer transcript names TruAlt or gives an unmistakable description of it.

### 2E. Risks peers discuss that TruAlt's own calls do not
- **Supreme Court stay on the Karnataka HC ethanol-allocation ruling.**
  TRIVENI (TRIVENI-Concall_Aug_2026_Transcript.pdf, p.6-7, Tarun Sawhney):
  "the Supreme Court has stayed any alteration to the existing ethanol
  allocation framework and ordered a status quo as it examines BPCL's
  challenge to the Karnataka High Court ruling... The AG yesterday has said
  that 100 crore litres should be allocated and is seeking the Supreme
  Court's permission... Triveni will be a significant beneficiary if that
  does get allocated." This is very likely the same underlying litigation
  TruAlt's own 15cr L court-ordered allocation sits inside (a Karnataka HC
  order that TruAlt's Q1FY27 call blames on "a parallel LTOA-vs-OMC Supreme
  Court case" without naming it). TruAlt's own four calls never name BPCL,
  never describe the Supreme Court stay mechanism, and never mention the
  Attorney General's 100cr L proposal — all disclosed in far more legal
  and procedural detail by TRIVENI. This is a missing-risk-disclosure gap on
  TruAlt's side, not a fabrication: the underlying case is real and dated,
  but TruAlt's own calls under-disclose it relative to a peer directly
  exposed to the same litigation.
- **Standalone-distillery solvency risk as PSU-bank interest moratoriums
  expire.** TRIVENI (TRIVENI-Concall_Aug_2026_Transcript.pdf, p.10-11):
  "the interest moratorium for a lot of the standalone distilleries...
  will expire next year... the survival of that and the operations of that
  comes under huge question." TruAlt's own calls never frame TruAlt's own
  balance sheet in these terms, despite carrying the CFO/DSCR profile B02/B03
  flag as its most severe audited-financial-statement finding.
- **Ethanol price non-revision (cane route) for three years despite record
  cane-price hikes.** A real, dated BALRAMCHIN-specific pain point on a
  feedstock route TruAlt does not use, but it evidences that pricing power in
  this sector sits with the OMCs across feedstock routes, not just the grain
  route — a structural read that supports, rather than undercuts, TruAlt's
  own "OMCs have become like an oligarchy" framing from its Q4FY26 call.

## PART 4: TRIANGULATION SUMMARY

- Claims verified: 1 of 6 (Q6, FCI-rice realisation price point)
- Claims partially verified: 2 of 6 (Q1 allocation shock direction; Q5
  government-scheme dependency as a sector-wide qualitative pattern)
- Claims contradicted: 2 of 6 (Q3 maize-margin compression; Q4 balanced
  demand-supply framing) — these go to synthesis as priority items
- Claims unverifiable: 1 of 6 (Q2 private-OMC reneging)

**The single most consequential contradiction:** GULPOLY and TRIVENI, two
independent grain-exposed peers, both report ethanol EBITDA/litre holding or
improving through FY26 at maize prices in the same Rs 18-25/kg range TruAlt
cites for its own claimed collapse from Rs 15-16/litre to Rs 6-7/litre. Because
company memory's load-bearing fact 1 rests on this exact margin-compression
claim, this contradiction should be treated as the top verification priority
carried into Halt 1 and any live-web follow-up: either TruAlt's dual-feed cost
structure is genuinely worse-positioned than GULPOLY's and TRIVENI's (a
company-specific finding worth investigating), or the claimed compression
overstates the pressure TruAlt actually faces.

**The single strongest independent confirmation:** TRIVENI's Q1FY27 disclosure
of the Supreme Court stay on the Karnataka HC ethanol-allocation ruling and
BPCL's specific challenge to it corroborates, in far more procedural detail
than TruAlt's own calls provide, that TruAlt's most persistent, longest-running
excuse (the unimplemented 15cr L court order, blamed since Q3FY26 on "a
parallel LTOA-vs-OMC Supreme Court case") is a real, dated, industry-wide legal
overhang and not a fabricated deflection. This is also, symmetrically, a
disclosure-quality finding against TruAlt: a peer describes the same
litigation with more names, dates and mechanics than TruAlt itself has
disclosed across four calls on the exact matter costing it 15cr L of revenue.

**Overall: the peer set complicates, rather than cleanly supports or undercuts,
TruAlt's narrative.** It corroborates the allocation-shock direction and the
underlying litigation as real and sector-wide (supporting TruAlt's excuse
pattern as evidenced, not fabricated), and it corroborates the FCI-rice
realisation price point almost exactly. But it directly contradicts TruAlt's
central margin-compression claim with two independent, better-margined
comparators, and it contradicts TruAlt's "roughly balanced" demand-supply
framing with two peers who independently call the same market a structural
overcapacity carrying a forward solvency risk for grain distillers — a risk
TruAlt's own calls never apply to itself, even as TruAlt is the lone aggressive
ethanol-adjacent capex expander among the four names reviewed.

## PART 5: CROSS-PEER HYPOTHESIS

All three established peers are independently rotating capital AWAY from core
grain/cane-ethanol capacity at the same point TruAlt is committing several
hundred crore to ethanol-adjacent capex (CBG, SAF): GULPOLY has frozen all new
capex until FY28 to build cash and deleverage; BALRAMCHIN is diverting its
distillery-generation cash flow into PLA/bioplastics, a business explicitly
designed to reduce reliance on the ethanol/sugar cycle; TRIVENI is demerging
its highest-margin, highest-growth business (power transmission/defence) away
from the sugar-and-distillery parent entirely, and its own management states
outright that standalone grain-distillery solvency "comes under huge question"
once interest moratoriums lapse next year. Read together, this is not simply
"the sector has overcapacity" (each peer says that individually); it is a
specific, testable pattern that sector insiders are reading the current
overcapacity and price-freeze window as STRUCTURAL rather than cyclical, and
are visibly de-risking their own capital structures away from pure ethanol
exposure in response — a read TruAlt's own capex program (raising leverage to
build MORE ethanol-adjacent capacity right into this window) implicitly
disputes. Testable: if peer ethanol/distillery capex resumes and expands once
their diversification capex matures (FY28-29), the hypothesis fails; if peer
ethanol capex stays flat or falls further while TruAlt's own utilisation
struggles to clear 50-60% through FY27-28, the hypothesis holds, and TruAlt's
CBG/SAF diversification should be read as the same defensive instinct arriving
later and more leveraged than its peers'.

## Analyst note

The two contradicted claims (Q3 margin compression, Q4 balanced demand-supply)
and the one strong confirmation (the Supreme Court/BPCL litigation) should each
carry forward into Halt 1 as named, dated, peer-anchored items — not general
skepticism. The margin contradiction in particular sits directly against
company memory's load-bearing fact 1 and deserves a live-web check of TruAlt's
own maize-cost and DDGS-yield assumptions against GULPOLY's and TRIVENI's
disclosed unit economics before that fact is treated as settled. The missing
BPCL/Supreme Court disclosure is a transparency finding, not a fabrication
finding: TruAlt's underlying excuse is real, but its own calls tell that story
with materially less procedural detail than a peer facing the identical
litigation.
