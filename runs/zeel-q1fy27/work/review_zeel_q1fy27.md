### 2C. Six mandatory diagnostics

1. **Did revenue grow YoY?** Yes but weakly. Standalone +5.88% (Rs 1,682.0 to Rs 1,780.9 Cr);
   consolidated +4.52% (Rs 1,824.8 to Rs 1,907.3 Cr). The mix is the story: consolidated
   subscription revenue rose +15.81% (Rs 981.7 to Rs 1,136.9 Cr, L962) while advertisement revenue
   FELL -11.48% (Rs 758.5 to Rs 671.4 Cr, L961). Growth is carried entirely by subscription; the
   advertising engine is shrinking. This is below the 25% CAGR portfolio bar on every line.

2. **Did operating EBITDA margin expand, contract, or stay flat YoY?** Contracted hard, both
   books. Standalone 5.32% vs 11.44% = -612 bps. Consolidated 5.22% vs 13.10% = -788 bps. Both
   now sit near the ~6.9% adjusted-EBITDA trough flagged in the Notion baseline, far below the
   withdrawn 18-20% original guidance.

3. **Did core operating PBT (ex-Other Income) grow YoY?** No, it collapsed. Standalone -66.5%
   (Rs 136.0 to Rs 45.6 Cr); consolidated -75.1% (Rs 172.2 to Rs 42.9 Cr). The core is
   deteriorating faster than the headline PBT, so reported PBT overstates operational health.
   Consolidated Other Income now funds 42.1% of PBT (Rs 31.2 Cr of Rs 74.1 Cr).

4. **What drove the gap between core-operating-PBT growth and reported PAT growth?** On the clean
   consolidated book (Step 4 bridge): reported PBT fell Rs 123.1 Cr YoY, of which the dominant
   driver is a +Rs 171.6 Cr surge in advertisement & publicity expense (Rs 275.2 to Rs 446.8 Cr,
   +62.4%) plus +Rs 60.7 Cr higher operational (content) cost, against only +Rs 82.5 Cr revenue.
   PAT then fell less than PBT (-48.3% vs -62.4%) purely because the tax charge went to near-zero:
   consolidated tax swung from Rs 53.5 Cr (ETR 27.1%) to Rs (0.2) Cr (ETR -0.3%), carried by a
   non-repeatable Rs 28.9 Cr earlier-years credit (L984). Strip that and PAT would be materially
   lower. The reported-PAT softness understates the operating deterioration.

5. **Are D&A and finance costs scaling faster than revenue?** No on D&A (falling -26.4% both
   books, consistent with lower content amortisation after the Q4 policy reset). Finance costs are
   rising fast in percentage terms (+79.2% standalone, +71.4% consolidated) but off a tiny base
   (Rs 7-13 Cr), so absolutely immaterial. No capex-absorption signal; this is not a capex story.

6. **Is Other Income concentration changing?** Consolidated Other Income is small in absolute
   terms (Rs 31.2 Cr) but now covers 42.1% of PBT (vs 12.7% a year ago) because PBT itself
   collapsed. It is not a treasury-timing spike inflating the quarter; rather, thin core profit has
   made a small Other Income line proportionally large. Stripping it (core PBT ex-OI Rs 42.9 Cr,
   -75.1%) gives the honest read.

  while subscription/digital holds up. This quarter's filing shows exactly that split, ad revenue
  -11.5% and subscription +15.8% YoY (L961-962, this quarter's filing). (Prior Notion work flagged
  FY26 ad revenue -12% and a broad ad-market weakness; this quarter confirms it.)
- Regulation and enforcement are an active sector headwind for Zee specifically: SEBI's securities-
  market ban and SCN cluster (this quarter's filing, Note 7) sit on top of an MCA Section 206(5)
  inspection. This is a company-specific overlay, not a sector-wide event, but it shows how
  regulatory risk can gate a media company's access to capital markets.
- Payer mix: the company operates a single reported segment, "Content and Broadcasting"
  (Consol Note 14, L1423, this quarter), so a sector-style advertiser-versus-subscriber margin
  split is NOT disclosed; that granularity is a filing gap.
- Structural read: content-cost inflation and marketing intensity are compressing sector margins;
  Zee's own operating EBITDA margin near 5% versus an original 18-20% ambition (Notion baseline,
  memory) illustrates the squeeze.

### 3. Business-model intelligence

- How Zee makes money: two engines, advertising (Rs 671 Cr this quarter, L961) and subscription
  (Rs 1,137 Cr, L962), plus small other services (Rs 99 Cr, L963). Subscription is now the larger
  and the only growing leg (this quarter's filing).
- Unit economics this quarter: content/operational cost Rs 1,032 Cr and marketing Rs 447 Cr consumed
  most of Rs 1,907 Cr revenue, leaving ~5% operating margin (this quarter's filing). The model's
  profitability is highly sensitive to marketing intensity and content amortisation policy, which
  the company reset in Q4 FY26 (Rs 302 Cr catch-up charge, Note 10).
- Model drift signals this quarter: capital is being redeployed into VFX (Phantom associate,
  Rs 116 Cr) and experiential ventures (Culture of Real Experiences JV, Rs 10 Cr), and the
  syndication business was slump-sold to a subsidiary (Rs 490 Cr, Note 6). New associate held at
  fair value, not equity method, is an unusual accounting election (this quarter's filing).
- Tax and geography: the group's profit is increasingly foreign-subsidiary driven, with the India
  standalone entity a shrinking Rs 47 Cr contributor and the group ETR at ~0% this quarter versus
  30% standalone (this quarter's filing). Not a durable structure.
- Balance-sheet dependency: the model currently needs a Rs 3,143 Cr promoter warrant infusion to
  de-risk, and that raise is regulatory-gated by the SEBI ban (this quarter's filing, Notes 7 and
  11). Cash conversion is unverifiable at Q1 (no cash-flow statement filed).

### 4. Competition intelligence

- Where Zee is structurally weaker: against JioStar (Reliance-Disney), the very counterparty in the
  USD 1,097M LCIA arbitration (this quarter's filing, Note 9). JioStar controls the ICC cricket
  rights that were the subject of the terminated Alliance Agreement; Zee lost that access and now
  faces a claim worth ~88% of its market value. Scale and balance-sheet depth versus JioStar is a
  structural disadvantage. (Peer identity from Notion/prior work; the claim escalation to USD
  1,097M and hearing completion are this quarter's filing.)
- Where Zee still competes: subscription/content libraries, where subscription revenue grew 15.8%
  this quarter (L962), suggests the catalogue and distribution still monetise. This is the one leg
  that is holding against streaming and larger-network competition.
- Competitive risk to watch: continued advertising-share loss (ad revenue -11.5% this quarter) to
  larger digital-plus-broadcast rivals, and the possibility that the LCIA award and SEBI overhang
  distract management and constrain content investment while better-capitalised peers keep spending.
  (Ad-share erosion is this quarter's filing; the capital-constraint risk is inferred from the
