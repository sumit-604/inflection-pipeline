The previous brief covered Friday, 4 September. This one covers one new

buy or sell anything. The operator decides.

## The picture in one paragraph

The average stock is drifting lower while the best stocks keep making new
highs. Over the last five sessions, six stocks fell for every four that rose.
Yet more stocks sit near a one-year high tonight than at any point in this
data. Both facts are true at once. The large companies fell again today and
the small ones held, so the gap between the two ends of the market widened
for another session. The middle of the market keeps emptying into the two
ends. Nothing here says the market is about to break, and nothing here says a
new leg has started. It says the index will pay you very little and the right
stock will pay you a lot.

## Where the market stands

Think of the market in three layers again. The largest companies sit below
their long-term trend line. The Nifty closed at 23,779, down half a percent,
and it sits about two percent under that line. The mid-sized companies sit a
little above theirs. The small and very small companies sit well above
theirs, close to nine percent up. Today the microcap index rose while the
Nifty fell. That is the same shape as last week, one more session deep.

Eight of thirty-five indices rose today. Pharma and healthcare led. Defence
and the small-cap group followed. Media fell almost three percent, IT fell
more than two, and realty fell nearly two. Nothing collapsed. The tape was a
slow drift down with a loud few names going up.

The base is still there, and it is thinner than it was. Since early July, the
share of stocks above their long-term average has stayed between about
forty-five and fifty-two percent. That is nine weeks in a narrow band.
Tonight it is 48.8 percent. But the raw count above the 200-day line peaked
near 1,370 on 7 August and sits at 1,230 now. About 140 stocks have dropped
out of the long-term uptrend in a month. In the same month the list of stocks
near their yearly high grew. The top of the market is holding while the floor
under it thins. That is the single most important line in tonight's data.

The short-term layers are weak. Only four in ten stocks sit above their
twenty-day line, 919 against 1,338. Under half sit above their fifty-day
line. The weekly momentum reading has now slipped under half for the first
time in this stretch, with 47.8 percent of stocks holding a rising weekly
trend. Among the larger Nifty 500 companies, seven in ten are still above
their long-term line, down from nearly three-quarters a week ago. The good
end of the market is still the good end. It is being nibbled at.

The five-day picture and the one-day picture disagree, and that disagreement
is the story. Over five sessions, about six in ten stocks fell. Today, 115
stocks jumped four and a half percent or more, while only 42 fell that hard.
So the crowd drifts down and the movers move up. A market that behaves this
way is rewarding a small list and punishing the rest quietly.

There has still been no thrust day. In this history, a real new leg announces
itself when three hundred or more stocks jump four and a half percent in one
session. The last time that happened was 12 June, with 344. Before that, 1
April with 1,140 and 8 April with 895, the pair that ended the March crash.
The last washout was 12 May, when 505 stocks fell that hard in a day. Almost
four months have passed with neither event. The market is in the quiet
middle.

Fear rose today and it is still cheap. The India VIX gained four and a half
percent to 11.16. That sounds like a jump. It is a jump from a very low base.
The VIX is about nineteen percent below its own thirty-week average and down
eighteen percent over three months. Meanwhile gold is up about six percent
over the last month while the Nifty is slightly down. A calm stock market and
a rising gold price still disagree. That disagreement has now run for several
weeks.

One last number on money. Turnover is about 1.6 times what it was a year ago.
But the spread of that money is narrow. For every stock trading on unusually
heavy volume, roughly two are trading on unusually light volume. More money
is arriving and it is going to fewer names.

## What the sectors say

Healthcare is the strongest sector in the market and it stopped resting
today. About six in ten healthcare stocks sit above the four-week line and
seven in ten sit above every longer weekly line. The pharma index led the
market today. Nine healthcare names sit on the fresh weekly momentum list.
Nineteen sit on the near-breakout list. This is the one sector where the index and the breadth agree.

Aerospace and defence is second and it is holding. Almost six in ten defence
stocks hold their short-term line, and just under seven in ten hold the long
one. The index rose today. The gap between short and long is smaller here
than in most sectors, which means the strength is spread across names rather
than carried by two or three.

Banks are strong over the long run and tired now. Seven in ten bank stocks
sit above their year-long trend line. Only a third sit above the four-week
one. The PSU bank index fell one percent today, the private bank index fell
slightly. This is the same shape as a fortnight ago and it has not resolved.

IT broke, and it broke exactly where Friday's brief said to watch. The IT
index fell 2.3 percent today. It has now slipped about two percent below its
thirty-week line after being the best index of the quarter, up fourteen
percent over three months. Sector breadth is weak on every horizon, with a
third of IT stocks above the short line and a third above the long one. The
rally lived in a few big names and the few big names have stopped. There is a
second signal underneath: IT is the second-largest group on the daily
oversold-reversal list, with eight names turning up from a low. Falling index,
turning tail. That is early and unproven.

Media is the worst index of the day, down 2.9 percent, and among the weakest
sectors on every horizon. It is also the largest single group on the weekly
oversold-reversal list, with four names. A sector this beaten that starts
turning is worth watching. It is not worth trusting yet.

Realty repeated its trap. The index fell 1.7 percent today, and over three
months it is up more than seven percent. Only about a third of realty stocks
are in an uptrend on any horizon. The index rides the big developers. The
scans fill with small ones. Do not read one as a sign for the other.

Metals gave back. The sector fell 1.2 percent today after a good quarter.
Friday's read said metals had room because breadth was only middling. Today
it did not pay. Sector breadth is still around half, so the case is not dead.
It is unproven.

Auto is still a spread, not a sector. The auto index was flat today. Only a
third of auto stocks hold their short-term line, while nearly six in ten hold
the long one. And the parts makers keep filling the scans. Rane Madras traded
at almost nineteen times its usual volume and sits on six separate screens.
Talbros and Steel Strips Wheels sit on six screens each. The suppliers lead. The assemblers do not.

Plastic products is the clearest turn from the bottom in the whole sector
table. Nearly six in ten plastic stocks are above their four-week line, while
only about a third are above the forty-week one. That shape, short strong and
long weak, is what an early sector turn looks like. Texmo Pipes rose
seventeen percent today on more than forty times its usual volume. The
sector is small and the move is real.

Telecom services shows the same early shape from lower down. About a third of
its stocks are above the four-week line against fewer than a fifth above the
twenty-week one. Vodafone Idea is the largest company on the fresh weekly
momentum list. Reliance Communications turned up from oversold on the weekly
scan. A sector left for dead is stirring.

Power and utilities is the weakest sector in the market on every horizon.
Only one stock in eight is above its four-week line. About one in five is
above its forty-week line. FMCG is the worst index of the quarter, down six
and a half percent, and it sits seven percent below its thirty-week line. Yet
Peria Karamalai Tea rose twenty percent today and Mukka Proteins sits on the
highs list. The tail of a sinking sector can still run.

One number covers the whole table. In 22 of 26 sectors, the short-term
breadth reading sits below the medium-term one. Across all sectors, about
forty-eight percent of stocks are above their forty-week line but only
thirty-nine percent are above their four-week line. The market is losing its
short-term grip almost everywhere while the long-term grip holds.

## What is leading

About 115 stocks closed at a one-year high. Two in three are small companies.
The share of the market making a new yearly high sits at the very top of its
own history. The share of stocks within fifteen percent of a yearly high, at
28.6 percent, is the highest reading in this entire data set. Those two records are the strong
half of the barbell, and they are why the index looks so much duller than the
market feels.

Several groups moved as groups, not as single names. Jewellers ran hardest.
Manoj Vaibhav, PC Jeweller, Uday Jewellery and Radhika Jeweltech all sit
among the day's biggest movers, and Tribhovandas Bhimji Zaveri is up more
than two and a half times over three months. Gold is up about six percent in
a month. The jewellers are a gold trade wearing a retail shopfront.

Dye and pigment chemical makers are the other clear group. Bodal Chemicals is
the single strongest name in the data, up about eighty percent in a week and
more than one and a half times in a month, and it appears in the top thirty
of five different return horizons. Alkali Metals rose twenty percent today.
Bhageria, Fineotex and Shree Pushkar sit on the same lists. When a whole
sub-industry moves in one week, something changed for the sub-industry. That
something is not in these files.

Pipes and tubes moved together. Venus Pipes, Texmo Pipes and MMP all rose ten
percent or more today and all sit on several screens at once. Textiles keep
showing up too. Jindal Worldwide is up nearly sixty percent in a week and
Mittal rose fifteen percent today.

The names on the most screens at once are the strongest single facts in the
file. Centum Electronics and Kabra Extrusion each sit on nine separate
screens. Bodal Chemicals and Syrma sit on eight. Two hundred and eleven names
sit on three or more. Industrials is the largest sector across all these
screens with 59 names, healthcare next with 50, then chemicals with 36.

One screen is worth naming for its narrowness. The modified relative strength
scan, which needs a weekly momentum cross and a price at least seventy-five
percent off its low, holds only nine names tonight. Their median week is up
about six percent. When a demanding screen returns nine names, the market is
not broadly strong. It is narrowly strong.

Large companies are not absent. The long-term breakout screen is 28 percent
large cap, more than any other scan. CG Power, MCX, SBI Cards and Sundaram
Finance all crossed into fresh daily momentum today. Among the biggest
momentum readings in the market, power equipment and pharma dominate: Hitachi
Energy, Apar, Solar Industries, PTC Industries, Bosch and Neuland.

There is froth and it grew. Twenty-seven stocks rose ten percent or more
today and two rose twenty percent or more. Twenty-eight stocks are up more
than twenty percent over five days, against five that are down that much.
Three percent of the market now trades more than ten percent above its
ten-day line, the most in a month. None of that calls a top. It says money
has reached the fast end of the market and is staying there.

## Who is buying

One caveat first, in plain words. These shareholding files include many rows
where the older quarters read zero. A zero usually means a new listing or a
gap in the record, not a real jump from nothing. Only the names with a real
three-quarter history are counted below. For foreign money that is 778 rows
of 1,000. For domestic institutions it is only 210 rows of 918, so the
domestic list is the thinnest evidence of the three.

Foreign money has two themes. The first is Indian banks and lenders. Yes
Bank, ICICI Bank and IDFC First Bank all show large three-quarter increases.
The second is the equipment and electronics complex: MTAR Technologies in
defence electronics, Adani Green in power, Shriram Pistons in auto parts, and
Coforge in IT services. The typical increase across the whole list is about
half a percentage point. The large increases are concentrated and they point
at hard assets and at banks.

Domestic institutions are buying two different things. One is the recently
listed platform companies: Swiggy, Eternal, Delhivery and PolicyBazaar all
show large three-quarter increases, and Delhivery also had the largest single
quarter rise. The other is banks that have gone nowhere: Ujjivan, Bandhan,
City Union, and Kotak in the most recent quarter alone. Domestic buying into
flat bank prices is the same pattern flagged last week. It still has not paid
off.

Forty-four names appear on both the foreign and domestic lists. Deepak
Nitrite, Neuland, Sona Comstar, Data Patterns, Talbros, ICICI Bank and Kronox
are among them. Two independent buyers adding to the same name over three
quarters is a stronger fact than either list alone.

Promoters raised their stake in 510 of the 973 names with real history. The
typical rise is small, about a third of a percentage point. The largest rises
are in very small companies, several above twenty percentage points. A jump
that size is rarely open-market buying. It is usually a preferential issue or
a reclassification. Each of those means something different, and the
difference is not in this file.

Retail holding rose in every single one of the 995 names with a real history.
The typical rise is about one percentage point. That is a structural fact
about who owns the Indian market now, not a signal about any one company. The
sharper point is where retail crowded most: IRB, Gensol, Kaynes, Allcargo and
Shrenik all show double-digit increases in retail ownership. The framework
does not treat low institutional holding as a risk, and that rule stands.
This is a different point. Retail that arrives after a big move is the float
that sells first when the mood turns.

## Thirty-six points

### For the trader

**1. There is still no thrust.** Three months have passed without a session
where three hundred stocks jumped four and a half percent. Today's 115 is a
good day, not a launch day. Until that number arrives, every up move is a
bounce inside a range. The read changes on the day it arrives.

**2. The five-day tape is negative under a positive day.** Six in ten stocks
fell over the last five sessions. Yet today's hard risers beat hard fallers
by more than two to one. Trade the movers, not the market. That gap is the
whole condition right now.

**3. The twenty-day line is the swing vote and it is losing.** Only 919
stocks of about 2,257 sit above it, down 88 in a day. When that count crosses
back over half the market, the bounce has become a trend. Under 800, the
range has broken.

**4. Weekly momentum has slipped under half.** Fewer than forty-eight percent
of stocks hold a rising weekly trend, down from just over half a week ago.
This is the medium-term layer starting to give. It matters more than any
single day's index move.

**5. Shorting still does not pay, but it pays more than it did.** Hard
fallers rose from 26 to 42 in a session while hard risers fell. The ratio is
still nearly three to one in favour of the risers. A day where fallers
outnumber risers would be the first real change since May.

**6. The parabolic tail is fuller.** Twenty-eight stocks are up more than
twenty percent in five days. Three percent of the market trades more than ten
percent above its ten-day line, the widest in a month. In this data those
names populate next week's list of twenty percent five-day losers.

**7. Jewellery is still a gold trade.** Four jewellers sit in today's biggest
movers and gold is up about six percent in a month while the Nifty is flat.
The group moves with the metal. If gold turns, the group turns.

**8. Metals did not pay.** Friday's case was that metals led with breadth
only middling. Today the sector fell more than one percent. Sector breadth is
still around half, so the setup is unresolved rather than wrong.

**9. IT confirmed the failure.** The best index of the quarter fell 2.3
percent and is now below its thirty-week line with a third of its stocks in
uptrend. That is the exact condition Friday's brief named. A leader that
loses its long line on thin breadth is no longer a leader.

**10. But watch the IT tail.** Eight IT names crossed up from oversold on the
daily scan, the second-largest group on that list. The index breaks while the
small names turn. Both can be true and only one lasts.

**11. Media is the deepest oversold turn.** The worst index of the day is
also the biggest group on the weekly reversal list. Four media names crossed
up from a low. That is a very early signal in a sector with the weakest
breadth in the table.

**12. Auto is still a spread.** Parts makers dominate the momentum screens
while the auto index sits flat and only a third of auto stocks hold their
short-term line. The gap between suppliers and assemblers has not closed in
two weeks. It closes when one side moves to the other.

**13. Plastic products is the widest short-term turn.** Nearly six in ten
plastic stocks are above their four-week line against a third above the
forty-week one. That is the strongest short-over-long reading in the sector
table. It is a small sector, so position size does the work here.

**14. Pipes moved as a block.** Venus Pipes, Texmo Pipes and MMP each rose
ten percent or more today and each sits on multiple screens. A sub-industry
that moves together on one day is a sector event. The reason is not in these
files.

**15. Fresh momentum is a small-cap event.** Seventy percent of the names
crossing into fresh daily momentum are small caps, and seventy-three percent
of the weekly ones. The large caps are not driving this. Size the trade for
the liquidity you actually have.

**16. Large caps are showing up in one place.** The long-term breakout screen
is 28 percent large cap, well above every other screen. CG Power, MCX, SBI
Cards and Sundaram Finance crossed into fresh momentum today. If a large-cap
leg starts, this is the screen it shows up on first.

**17. The demanding screen returned nine names.** The modified relative
strength scan needs both a weekly momentum cross and a big move off the low.
Nine names cleared it. Narrow strength like this concentrates the reward and
concentrates the risk.

**18. Volume is arriving but not spreading.** Turnover is about 1.6 times a
year ago, while heavy-volume stocks are outnumbered roughly two to one by
light-volume ones. Concentrated money in a narrow list produces sharp
reversals. That is a size instruction, not a direction one.

### For the investor

**19. The base is holding and thinning at once.** Nine weeks in a narrow band
around half the market above its long-term average. But the raw count has
fallen about 140 from its 7 August peak. Range intact, floor slowly eroding.
Which one resolves decides the next six months.

**20. The record top sits over a shrinking base.** The share of stocks near a
yearly high is the highest in this data. The share above the long-term
average is falling. That combination is either healthy rotation or quiet
distribution, and the two look identical until they do not.

**21. The barbell hardened again.** Both ends grew. More stocks near yearly
highs, more stocks near yearly lows, fewer stocks in between. An index bet
gets you the average of two crowds moving apart. Stock selection is the whole
return.

**22. The quality end is being nibbled.** Seven in ten Nifty 500 companies
are still above their long-term line. That was nearly three-quarters a
week ago. The healing at the good end has paused. It has not reversed.

**23. Healthcare is the one sector where index and breadth agree.** Seven in
ten healthcare stocks are above every weekly line, and the index led today.
Nineteen healthcare names sit on the near-breakout screen. For a long holder
this is the least contradictory place in the table.

**24. Defence strength is spread, not carried.** Almost six in ten defence
stocks hold the short line and just under seven in ten hold the long one. A
narrow gap means many names doing the work. That is a healthier leadership
shape than banks or IT show.

**25. Banks are the unresolved large-cap case.** Seven in ten bank stocks are
above the year line and only a third above the four-week line. Domestic funds
have added for three quarters into flat prices. The setup is intact and the
payoff has not come.

**26. Long-run weakness sits in power, media, telecom services, realty and
IT.** These are the low rungs. A company climbing the quality ladder from a
low rung starts here, in a sector the market has stopped paying for. The
strong list, healthcare and defence and industrials, is where the market
already pays up.

**27. Two sectors are turning from the bottom with real numbers.** Plastic
products shows six in ten above the short line against a third above the long
one. Telecom services shows a third against under a fifth. Transition names
tend to appear in sectors with this shape before the market names them.

**28. The whole market is losing its short-term grip.** In 22 of 26 sectors,
short-term breadth sits below medium-term breadth. Across sectors the average
gap is about nine points. This is not one sector rolling over. It is a
market-wide loss of near-term momentum under a stable long-term picture.

**29. Foreign money is buying banks and hard assets.** Lenders on one side,
defence electronics and power and auto parts on the other. Three quarters of
steady adding, with price confirming in most cases. It is a concentrated bet,
not a scattered one.

**30. Domestic money is buying the new listings.** Swiggy, Eternal,
Delhivery, PolicyBazaar. Alongside that, small banks and Kotak. Only 210
names carry a real three-quarter record here, so read the direction and not
the size.

**31. Forty-four names have both hands buying.** Deepak Nitrite, Neuland,
Sona Comstar, Data Patterns, Talbros and Kronox are on both the foreign and
domestic lists. Two independent institutions adding to the same name over
three quarters is the strongest ownership fact in the file.

**32. Promoter increases need their mechanism checked.** Five hundred and ten
promoters raised their stake, with a typical rise of a third of a percentage
point. The very large rises are in tiny companies. A twenty-point jump is
almost never open-market buying, and the file does not say which it was.

**33. Retail ownership rose everywhere.** Every one of the 995 names with a
real record shows a higher retail share. That is a fact about the market, not
about a company. The point that bites is retail arriving after a large move,
as in IRB, Gensol, Kaynes and Allcargo.

**34. Gold beating stocks by six points is still a warning.** Gold is up
about six percent in a month while the Nifty is slightly down. Fear in stocks
is near the bottom of its range. Two markets disagree and one of them is
wrong.

**35. Protection is cheap and the calendar says nothing.** The VIX at 11.16
is about nineteen percent below its own thirty-week average. Low fear does
not predict a shock. The May shock came days after breadth peaked. Cheap
insurance is a condition, not a forecast.

**36. Nine pipeline names show up tonight.** Kabra Extrusion is on nine
screens with retail rising. Man Industries is on six with both foreign and
promoter buying. Pitti Engineering made a yearly high on twenty-five times
usual volume. Entero is on five screens. Kronox has both institutions buying.
Shyam Metalics, Amagi, Indegene and Aurum hold above their volume-weighted
average with a positive trend stack. Venus Remedies and UFBL sit in the
long-horizon return leaders. Borana and Fratelli appear on no list
tonight. This is memory to weigh, never evidence.

## What would change this read

The constructive view fails if the count above the twenty-day line keeps
falling toward 800, or if the count above the 200-day line breaks under about
1,200 while the yearly-high list stays full, or if a session comes where hard
fallers outnumber hard risers. It also fails if healthcare loses its
medium-term breadth, since healthcare is the only sector where the index and
the breadth still agree.

The cautious view fails if a real thrust day arrives, with three hundred or
more stocks jumping four and a half percent in one session. It also fails if
more than half the market reclaims the twenty-day line on rising volume, or
if the weekly momentum reading climbs back over half, or if the Nifty closes
back above its thirty-week line and the large-cap breakout screen keeps
filling with big companies.
