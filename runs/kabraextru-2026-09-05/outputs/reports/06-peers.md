## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

### 2A. Demand environment

Across the RAJOOENG window (FY23-H1 FY25), Rajoo's own demand signal is uniformly positive after FY23 (revenue +23.51% FY24, +27.07% YoY H1 FY25; order book Rs 185 Cr → "new heights" → Rs 140 Cr → Rs 200 Cr+). No peer anywhere in this corpus describes a demand slowdown resembling KABRAEXTRU's stated FY26 extrusion decline, but — as flagged repeatedly in Part 1 — none of this data reaches into the FY26 window and Rajoo's product mix under-indexes to the PVC/pipe-extrusion sub-segment JJM actually funds. **Read as silence, not confirmation:** if a genuine, sector-wide JJM/infra-spending slowdown had already been visible in H1 FY25 (Apr-Sep 2024, which does overlap slightly with the tail of KABRAEXTRU's own FY25), no Rajoo call mentions it — but Rajoo's JJM exposure via its Bausano JV is too small and unreported to make this a strong test.

### 2B. Pricing and input costs

RAJOOENG reports raw-material prices as stable through FY24 and H1 FY25 (Claim 4 above), with margin driven instead by product mix, capacity utilisation, and logistics lead-times (Red Sea crisis). Rajoo also describes a structural domestic-vs-export margin gap: "the net margin in domestic... extremely price sensitive market... you can say a 4% to 5%, where... export it is nearly 30% to 35% more" (RAJOOENG, Q4 FY23 call, 16-May-2023, p.19-20). KABRAEXTRU's AR MD&A never discusses raw-material trends and its FY26 revenue mix shifted toward domestic (exports fell 11.6% while domestic fell a smaller 4.5%) — if a similar domestic-price-sensitivity dynamic applies to KABRAEXTRU, a domestic-heavier mix would itself be a margin headwind independent of any raw-material story, though this is inference from an adjacent peer, not direct evidence about KABRAEXTRU.

### 2C. Capex cycle — lone expander, not an industry-wide race

Only one peer (RAJOOENG) provides usable capex data (Windsor's transcript is absent). Rajoo is a **continuous, phased capacity expander** across all four available calls (Rs 20-25 Cr FY24-25 plan, Rs 9.33 Cr FY24 actual plus new facility, further FY25 tooling capex, 30% capacity addition completed by Oct-2024, land bank held for the next phase). Given n=1 usable peer, this cannot be characterised as either "industry-wide capacity race" or definitively "lone expander in a cautious peer set" — there is no second or third peer data point to compare against. What can be said: the one extrusion-machinery peer this corpus can observe was adding capacity steadily through the most recent available data (Sep-2024), which is inconsistent with a demand-side rationale for capex retrenchment industry-wide in that window, though it says nothing about FY26 specifically, when KABRAEXTRU's own capex nearly halved.

### 2D. Competitive mentions

The single most consequential finding in this stage: **RAJOOENG, Q4 FY24 call (18-Apr-2024, p.8)**, in response to "who are our main peers in our segment?", Khushboo Doshi names "Windsor machines and carbon extrusion" as the two big domestic players. "Carbon extrusion" is almost certainly a transcription artifact for "Kabra Extrusion" — flagged here with appropriate uncertainty, not asserted as fact, because the transcript literally reads "carbon," not "Kabra." If the identification is correct, this is an unprompted, independent peer statement placing KABRAEXTRU in a two-player domestic leadership tier alongside Windsor Machines, and framing both as older, more established incumbents ("these two competitors are double in the age of Rajoo Engineers," same page) that Rajoo competes against on technology rather than price. No other peer mention of KABRAEXTRU, by name or description, appears anywhere else in this corpus.

### 2E. Risks peers discuss that KABRAEXTRU does not

- **Red Sea shipping crisis** driving import lead-time extension and a temporary net working-capital-cycle stretch to 165 days (inventory days 130→190, payable days +10-15 days), expected to normalise within two quarters (RAJOOENG, Q4 FY24 call, 18-Apr-2024, p.6-7). KABRAEXTRU's ARs never mention Red Sea/shipping-lane disruption as a supply-chain risk.
- **Manpower/skills scarcity in capital-goods manufacturing**, named as a "real problem" affecting both Rajoo and its customers (RAJOOENG, Q4 FY23 call, 16-May-2023, p.10). Not discussed by KABRAEXTRU in either AR.
- **Domestic market price-sensitivity as a structural margin drag** distinct from raw-material cost (see 2B above). KABRAEXTRU's AR never frames domestic-vs-export as a margin-mix issue.
- **Chinese cost dominance foreclosing profitable mass lithium-ion cell manufacturing outside China** — HBL's chairman states this explicitly and describes it as the reason HBL deliberately avoided the general lithium-ion market: "I knew six years ago that no one outside China can make a profit from the production of lithium ion cells... we chose a niche for defense" (HBLENGINE, FY25 AGM, 25-Sep-2025, p.3-4). KABRAEXTRU's ARs describe Geon's competitive risk only in generic terms ("increasing competition across both the extrusion and energy segments," per B05) and never frame the risk in these China-cost-structure terms, despite Geon assembling lithium-ion PACKS (an adjacent, not identical, activity to cell manufacture) in a segment reporting widening losses on rising revenue.

### Working-capital / advances / inventory norms in extrusion machinery (company-memory verification priority)

RAJOOENG describes an advance-payment-heavy, order-linked working-capital model typical of the extrusion-machinery capital-goods business: "we normally receive 35% to 40% advance whenever we finalize any order" and raw material is procured back-to-back against the order (RAJOOENG, Q2 FY24 call, 6-Nov-2023, p.9-10); in a later call, "in 95% cases there are always 100% payment before we dispatch and in some cases there are LCs" (RAJOOENG, Q2 FY24 call, 6-Nov-2023, p.12). Receivable days at Rajoo are correspondingly low and fluctuate mainly with quarter-end LC timing (22-71 days across the observed calls). This establishes a sector norm of advance-funded, low-receivable-risk working capital for extrusion machinery MAKERS. It is not directly transferable to KABRAEXTRU's stale HEVPL receivable, which sits in the Geon battery segment (a different customer-payment model, selling to EV OEMs rather than pre-paying industrial customers), so this norm does not test that specific exposure, but it is a useful baseline for KABRAEXTRU's extrusion-segment working capital, on which this corpus found no adverse signal from the peer.

## PART 5: CROSS-PEER HYPOTHESIS

**Hypothesis:** Both peers, operating in unrelated industries, converge on the same
structural answer to "how does an Indian small/micro-cap hardware manufacturer
protect margin," and neither answer describes how Geon is built — which may be a
sharper, more testable explanation for Geon's negative operating leverage than
"scaling pains."

Rajoo protects margin and cash by making the CUSTOMER fund working capital before
execution risk transfers: 35-40%+ advances against every order, back-to-back
raw-material procurement locked to the order, and (per its own account) 100% payment
before dispatch in 95% of cases (RAJOOENG, Q2 FY24 call, 6-Nov-2023, p.9-10, 12). HBL
protects margin by refusing to compete in any product line where Chinese
manufacturing scale drives cost to the point that "no one outside China can make a
