**FINDING 3 (MAJOR) — VERIFIED verdict rests on a single peer.** Rule 4 as written is explicit:
"any VERIFIED resting on one peer is MAJOR (should be PARTIALLY VERIFIED)." B06's Q6 verdict
is VERIFIED, anchored entirely to GRP Ltd — the only peer with transcripts in this run — across
five citations spanning its four quarterly calls. Applying the rule literally, this should have
been labelled PARTIALLY VERIFIED rather than VERIFIED, regardless of how many quarters/anchors
support it, because "independent" in the rubric's ordinary sense means independent *companies*,
not independent *time periods* from the same company.

Mitigating context I want to be explicit about: this claim is structurally different from Q1-Q5.
Q1-Q5 ask "does GRP corroborate an Ameenji claim" (a cross-company triangulation, for which the
≥2-peer bar is clearly the right one). Q6 instead asks a peer-benchmark question purely about
GRP's own behaviour ("does GRP itself give numeric targets, and does it hit them") — used to
calibrate whether Ameenji's guidance silence is sector-normal. Multiple independent *instances*
of the same company's guidance-and-delivery behaviour across four separate quarters is a
different, and in this narrow case arguably reasonable, form of internal corroboration for a
claim that is inherently about a single company's track record. That said, the rubric's plain
text does not carve out this exception, and with a peer set that structurally contains only one
usable company, the rule as written would forbid any VERIFIED verdict for this run at all —
which is itself worth flagging as a rubric/input-mismatch rather than silently accepting or
silently overriding. I am flagging FINDING 3 as MAJOR per the letter of the rule, and noting
the mitigating structural circumstance for the orchestrator/synthesis stage to weigh; I am not
resolving the tension myself.
