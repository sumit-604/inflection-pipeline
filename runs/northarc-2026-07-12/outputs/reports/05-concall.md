### 1C. Trigger evolution across the three quarters

- **D2C mix shift** — STRENGTHENING. 54% → 56% → 59%, on track toward the stated 65-70% target, consistently reaffirmed each call.
- **Cost of funds decline** — REVERSING. Framed as a strong tailwind in Q2 and Q3 FY26 calls (declining 40bps and 90bps YoY respectively); by the Q4 FY26 call management explicitly said the rate cycle has "bottomed out" and flagged rising G-Sec yields and hedge costs (Atul, p.11) — a new headwind not present in the earlier two calls.
- **Rural/MFI branch network** — VOLATILE, then STRENGTHENING. 285 branches (Q2 FY26, p.3) → 278 branches, described as deliberate "optimization" (Q3 FY26, p.9) → 342 branches, +64 added in a single quarter (Q4 FY26, p.4). The reversal from calibration to aggressive scale-up happened without a call-by-call bridge explaining the change in risk appetite.
- **New credit fund launches** — DROPPED. Named specifically in the Q2 FY26 call ("climate... MSME... MSME... women entrepreneurs," Ashish, p.17) as "in pipeline... expect to launch in the coming quarters." Never named again in Q3 or Q4 FY26 calls. Credit fund AUM actually fell from ₹3,207cr (Q3 FY26, p.4) to ₹3,092cr (Q4 FY26, p.5) — the opposite of what a new-fund-launch narrative would produce.
- **RoA/RoE long-term targets** — WEAKENING/SLIPPING (see 2A for full promise-delivery detail). Both the absolute level and the timeline moved across all three calls, always in the direction of a softer near-term number pushed further into the future.
- **FY27 credit cost guidance** — WEAKENING. Guided progressively worse across three consecutive calls: 2.3%-2.5% (Q2 FY26) → 2.7%-3.0% (Q3 FY26) → 2.7%-2.8% (Q4 FY26).
- **HFC NCLT exposure** — DROPPED FROM DISCLOSURE. Actively discussed with specific near-term timelines in Q2 and Q3 FY26 calls; completely absent from the Q4 FY26 call, with no analyst prompting either. See Section 2D and 4D.
- **Affordable housing / vehicle finance products** — NEW trigger, appeared only in Q4 FY26 call (p.9), framed as board-approved but deliberately not yet launched ("my learning... is the time you open focus on affordable housing... the focus on business loans comes down").

## SECTION 3: COMPETITIVE INTELLIGENCE FROM CONCALLS

### 3A. Statements about competitors/peers, with credibility check

- "Unlike our peers, we are pretty conscious that we will keep [assignment] around 5% to 7% of our overall assets under management" (Ashish, Q3 FY26 call, p.7). Implies peers sell down more aggressively. No hard peer data given — self-serving framing, plausible but unverified.
- "It's skin in the game for Northern Arc... we are not like other arrangers" (placement business) (Ashish, Q2 FY26 call, p.8). Positions NORTHARC as differentiated from generic placement arrangers because it tracks risk on placed pools; unverified against peer practice.
- Analyst-sourced comparison: "one of the other peers on the lower ticket side (₹2-5 lakh) was saying that the behavior was the problem" for MSME borrowers (Prit Nagersheth, Q3 FY26 call, p.13). Management's response distinguished NORTHARC's ticket size (₹12-15L) as a different, less-stressed segment — a credible, falsifiable distinction that peer verification should test.
- Industry MFI market described as having shrunk "from ₹4,16,000 crores to about ₹3,23,000 crores," roughly 30% (Ashish, Q3 FY26 call, p.15) — a specific, checkable sector statistic.
- Kaushik Agarwal (Q4 FY26 call, p.14) noted that system-wide bank lending to NBFCs had risen even as NORTHARC's own bank-borrowing share fell (65%→52%, Mar25→Mar26); management characterized this as a deliberate funding-mix choice (shift to fixed-rate NCD/ECB) rather than a bank-access constraint — plausible but worth testing against peer funding-mix disclosures.

### 3B. Industry/market intelligence dropped in the calls

- MSME credit gap: "50% of India's MSME does not have access to formal credit" (Q2 FY26 call, Ashish, p.18); refined in Q4 FY26 call to "an addressable credit gap of nearly 24%, estimated to be about ₹30 lakh crores" (p.4).
- Rural credit access: "only 10% have access to formal credit" (Q2 FY26 call, Ashish, p.18).
- MFI sector growth guided at 15%-17% sector-wide (Q3 FY26 call, Ashish, p.3).
- Retail securitization volume down ~7% YoY on a YTD basis as of Q3 FY26 (Ashish, p.2), alongside "muted" offshore funding participation.
- Household consumption in India: $2.4tn in 2024 (from Q4 FY26 call, p.3), projected to reach $3.4tn by 2030 — cited to support the consumer-finance growth thesis.
- Interest rate regime turn: repo cut to 5.25% by Q3 FY26 (p.2); by Q4 FY26, management explicitly flagged the cycle "bottoming out" with a risk that "RBI [will be] increasing rates" in coming quarters given inflation (Atul, p.11) — a reversal of the rate-cut tailwind narrative present in the first two calls.
- Karnataka MFI ordinance (Feb-2025) cited as the specific trigger for a geography-level collection-efficiency dip, since fully recovered (94.5%→99.5%, Q4 FY26 call, p.4).
- West Asia geopolitical tension flagged as a new risk factor in the Q4 FY26 call, cited as the reason for a fresh ₹66cr prudential overlay and for rising hedge costs/G-Sec yields (+50bps) affecting funding cost.
