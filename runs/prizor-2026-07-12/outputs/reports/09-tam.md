## SECTION 2: TAM ESTIMATION — MULTIPLE METHODS

All figures in ₹ Crore unless stated; FX conversions (used only for excluded/context figures) at an indicative ~₹87/USD, not independently verified for this run — flagged.

### Method 1 — Top-down (India, video surveillance hardware)

- **Frost & Sullivan (cited in Aditya Infotech / CP Plus RHP, via secondary press reporting — not independently fetched, paywalled primary report):** India video surveillance market valued at ₹106.2bn (₹10,620 Cr) in FY25, projected to grow at **16.46% CAGR** to ₹227.4bn (₹22,740 Cr) by FY30. [Source: WebSearch, secondary reporting of Aditya Infotech RHP disclosure, corroborated by a second WebSearch pull citing the same F&S figure independently as "₹9,670 Cr FY24 → ₹19,980 Cr FY29 @15.6% CAGR" — the two F&S citations differ slightly in vintage/base year but agree on scope and are in the same ~15.6–16.5% CAGR band.] **Not stale** (FY25 base, <2 yrs from run date).
- **Prizor's own domestic chart (Sec 1B, corrected reading):** FY25 = ₹110bn (₹11,000 Cr); FY26 = ₹128bn (₹12,800 Cr); FY29 = ₹200bn (₹20,000 Cr). Implied CAGR ~14.4%. **Corroborates** the F&S figure within ~4% at the FY25 point — strong independent cross-check given Prizor's slide cites no source.
- **Grown to FY26 (current fiscal year, matching company revenue base):** ₹10,620 Cr × 1.1646 = **₹12,368 Cr** (F&S basis) vs **₹12,800 Cr** (Prizor chart basis). These two are close; **₹12,370 Cr is taken as the top-down conservative TAM.**
- Confidence: **H** (two independently-sourced figures converge within ~4%).

### Method 2 — Bottom-up (unit economics)

Using Prizor's own chart: FY25 domestic unit volume ≈ 41 Mn cameras, revenue ₹110bn → implied blended ASP ≈ ₹2,683/unit. FY26: 48 Mn units, ₹128bn → ASP ≈ ₹2,667/unit. ASP is stable at **~₹2,650–2,700 per camera-system-equivalent** (blended across IP/analog/entry-AI cameras and attached NVR/DVR revenue).

Total addressable units (FY26) ≈ 48 Mn units × ₹2,667 ASP = ₹12,800 Cr — **this reproduces Method 1 by construction** (same source chart), so it is not a fully independent method; its value here is as a **plausibility check on ASP**: ₹2,650–2,700 per unit is consistent with India's entry/mid-segment camera pricing (a Prizor camera retails materially below this on a per-camera basis, but the blended figure includes NVR/DVR attach, which raises the average). Confidence: **M** (not independent, but internally consistent).

### Method 3 — Peer revenue aggregation

- **CP Plus / Aditya Infotech Ltd (direct listed hardware peer, RHP disclosure):** FY25 revenue ₹3,112 Cr (up 11.8% YoY from ₹2,782 Cr), with a **disclosed 20.8% market share in FY25** (per RHP). Implied total market = ₹3,112 Cr ÷ 0.208 = **₹14,962 Cr (FY25 basis)**.
- **Discrepancy flag:** this is ~41% higher than the Frost & Sullivan ₹10,620 Cr FY25 figure cited above (Method 1), even though both trace to Frost & Sullivan-style sourcing. This likely reflects a different vintage or slightly broader scope in the RHP's commissioned market study versus the publicly-reported F&S figure used in Method 1. **Not silently averaged** — carried forward as the "realistic" (higher) top-down anchor precisely because it derives from a direct hardware peer's own disclosed numerator/denominator, which is at least internally consistent for that single data point.
- Grown to FY26 at 16.46%: ₹14,962 Cr × 1.1646 = **₹17,425 Cr**.
- **Unorganised-sector context:** post the April 2026 STQC/BIS-ER mandate barring non-certified (largely Chinese) brands, Indian/organised brands now hold **>80% of the market**, up from ~two-thirds previously (Chinese firms held "about one third of total sales" before the ban) — Business Standard, Feb 2026 (WebSearch). This is a very recent, structural formalisation event, not the steady-state "30–60% unorganised" default in the standard method; flagged as a genuine and dated **competitor-exit** event (relevant to SOM share-gain rules in Sec 3B).
- Other named organised peers (Godrej Security Solutions, Zicom, Matrix Comsec, Prama/Hikvision-JV entities, Honeywell) were not individually revenue-sourced within this run's search budget — **input gap**, noted below. Peer aggregation here rests on CP Plus alone as the single largest, most comparable data point.
- Confidence: **M** (single-peer denominator, scope discrepancy vs Method 1 flagged, not resolved).

### Method 4 — Import substitution

- MeitY's Essential Requirements (ER)/STQC certification mandate (April 2024 rule, effective for internet-connected CCTV cameras) has, as of Feb 2026, pushed Hikvision and Dahua out of India's internet-connected camera segment; Dahua has "scaled back to analogue cameras only," and Xiaomi/Realme exited the smart-camera segment entirely for non-compliance (Business Standard, sqmagazine.co.uk, digimro.com — WebSearch, Mar–Feb 2026 reporting). Indian/organised brands moved from ~67% to >80% share in this window.
- This is a **quantifiable, dated, structural import-substitution event** — the vacated ~13–33 percentage points of share (depending on which pre-ban baseline is used) is the single largest identifiable near-term TAM-capture opportunity for BIS-ER-certified domestic players including Prizor and CP Plus. Not separately re-sized here (would double count Methods 1/3) but used directly in the SOM share-gain justification (Sec 3B) and growth drivers (Sec 4A).
- Confidence: **H** for the directional/qualitative claim (multiple independent, dated news sources); **not quantified into a standalone ₹ TAM figure** to avoid double-counting.

### Method 5 — Global benchmark (context only, not used in TAM headline)

- Global video surveillance market estimates diverge enormously by scope: Memoori ($35.5bn 2025, ~6% CAGR) is closest to management's own $35.9bn/10.4% claim; Grand View Research ($83.5bn 2025), Markets&Markets ($56.1bn 2025), and Technavio all run 1.5–2.3x higher, reflecting inclusion of VMS software, VSaaS, and services. Management's own figure sits at the **low, hardware-weighted end** of the range it could have chosen — a mild positive credibility signal (they did not cherry-pick the largest number).
- Per-capita camera density (Prizor's own deck, p.20, "Source: Comparitech," vintage undated on the slide — **flagged, unknown vintage, used qualitatively only**): India's most-surveilled city, Hyderabad, has 79.4 cameras/1,000 people; Mumbai only 3.7; Ahmedabad 2.3. China is reported at ~494 cameras/1,000 people (SCMP/aithority, vintage unclear, likely 2020–21 — **STALE, direction-only per the >4-year rule**). The India intra-city dispersion (2.3 to 79.4 per 1,000, a 34x range within India alone) is the more decision-useful data point: it shows most Indian cities are 10–30x under-penetrated relative to India's own leading city, independent of any China comparison. Used only to support the penetration growth driver in Sec 4A, not to size TAM.

## SECTION 3: SAM & SOM

### 3A — SAM (five filters applied to conservative TAM, ₹12,370 Cr, FY26)

| Filter | Retained % | Rationale | Cumulative |
|---|---|---|---|
| Product fit | 90% | Prizor covers the full IP/analog/AI camera + NVR/DVR hardware category; excludes thermal/PTZ-specialist/enterprise-only sub-segments not in its 1,281+ SKU range (Investor Presentation p.5) | 90.0% |
| Geography | 72% | Present in 21 of 28 states/UTs (Investor Presentation p.12), 3,000+ tehsils, but concentration skewed to Maharashtra/Gujarat/TN/Delhi/A&N/J&K; meaningful gaps in depth across remaining states | 64.8% |
| Channel | 70% | Dealer/retail/installer-led (11,000+ dealers); thin-to-absent presence in large government/systems-integrator tender channel where big Safe-City/Smart-City contracts are typically won | 45.4% |
| Customer | 75% | Retail/SMB/light-institutional customer base; limited large-enterprise/national-government direct relationships (BIS-ER certification only obtained 2026, per company timeline p.7) | 34.0% |
| Capability | 85% | Backward-integration capacity (16 lakh cameras + 50 lakh PCB p.a., scalable to 40 lakh) is real but "unproven at scale" per B04; modest discount for execution/ramp risk | 28.9% |

**SAM = ₹12,370 Cr × 28.9% = ₹3,577 Cr ≈ ₹3,580 Cr.**
**SAM as % of TAM = 28.9%.**

### 3B — SOM at 3 and 5 years

Current revenue (FY26, unaudited, Investor Presentation p.25): **₹148 Cr**. (Audited FY25 base is ₹70.98 Cr — see note at end of 3B.)

**Current SAM share = 148 ÷ 3,580 = 4.13%.**

Share-gain trajectory: the standard band is 1–2pp (normal, 3yr) / 3–5pp (aggressive, with capacity+execution) / >5pp (only on competitor exit or acquisition), with faster gains possible where unorganised share is formalising. Here, unorganised/Chinese share is already down to <20% post-ban (Method 3/4) — the formalisation event has *already occurred* structurally (a genuine, dated competitor-exit event: Hikvision/Dahua's ~one-third historical share vacated by regulation, not by Prizor's own execution). This justifies sitting toward the **top of the "aggressive" 3–5pp band** rather than the ">5pp, competitor-exit-only" band, because the exit is real but the *capture* of that vacated share among many domestic claimants (CP Plus, Prizor, and others) is not guaranteed to accrue disproportionately to Prizor — tempered further by execution risk (backward integration unproven at scale, negative cumulative CFO, short-term borrowings up ₹2.76 Cr→₹31.82 Cr per B07).

- **3yr share gain: +2.5pp** → SAM share = 4.13% + 2.5% = **6.63%** → SOM_3yr = ₹3,580 Cr × 6.63% = **₹237 Cr**
- **5yr share gain: +4.5pp cumulative** → SAM share = 4.13% + 4.5% = **8.63%** → SOM_5yr = ₹3,580 Cr × 8.63% = **₹309 Cr**

**Implied revenue CAGR (arithmetic):**
- Yr3: (237 ÷ 148)^(1/3) − 1 = (1.601)^0.333 − 1 = **~17.0% CAGR**
- Yr5: (309 ÷ 148)^(1/5) − 1 = (2.088)^0.20 − 1 = **~15.9% CAGR**

**Note on revenue base:** if the audited FY25 figure (₹70.98 Cr) is used instead as the "current" base (more conservative/compliance-grade, but one year stale relative to the FY26 TAM figures used above), current SAM share falls to 70.98/3,580 = 1.98%, and headroom widens further — reinforcing, not weakening, the runway conclusion in Sec 5. FY26 (₹148 Cr) is used as the primary base here because it is time-matched to the FY26 TAM/SAM figures; the unaudited status is flagged.

### 3C — Capacity cross-check (against B07 capex-embedded growth)

Installed camera-assembly capacity: **16 lakh (1.6 Mn) units p.a.** (2 lines × 8 lakh), scalable to **40 lakh (4.0 Mn) units p.a.** (Investor Presentation p.13), built largely in FY26 and substantially **debt-funded** (short-term borrowings ₹2.76 Cr → ₹31.82 Cr against negative cumulative CFO, per B07).

At the Method-2 blended ASP of ~₹2,700/unit:
- Installed capacity (16 lakh units) → potential CCTV-segment revenue ceiling ≈ **₹4,320 Cr**
- Scalable ceiling (40 lakh units) → potential CCTV-segment revenue ceiling ≈ **₹10,800 Cr**

Against this, the CCTV-segment component of SOM_5yr (77% of ₹309 Cr) ≈ **₹238 Cr** — implying only **~5.5% utilisation** of the already-installed 16-lakh-unit capacity.

**Finding: capacity is not the constraint — it is a large surplus.** Installed and near-term scalable capacity is built for a revenue base 14–45x larger than what a realistic, share-gain-rule-bound SOM_5yr implies. **The capex plan (B07: ~116% of current revenue, debt-funded) is the optimistic side, not the SOM.** This is a utilisation/ROCE-dilution/depreciation-drag risk (idle capacity carrying debt service cost) that stage 11 should weigh explicitly against the company's currently very high disclosed ROCE (47.4% FY26) — a metric likely to compress mechanically as fixed-asset base grows into an under-utilised plant, independent of any demand-side deterioration.

`capacity_check: "surplus — installed 16 lakh unit capacity (~₹4,320 Cr revenue ceiling) vs SOM_5yr CCTV requirement of ~₹238 Cr (~5.5% utilisation); capex plan is the optimistic side, not SOM"`

### 4B — TAM Risks

| Risk | Monitoring signal |
|---|---|
| Regulatory reversal / dilution of STQC enforcement | Any relaxation of the April 2026 ER mandate, re-entry of Chinese brands via re-badging/local-assembly workarounds |
| Price competition among the now-larger pool of domestic-certified brands (CP Plus, Prizor, and others all chasing the same vacated share) | Gross margin trend across domestic peers; Prizor's own pricing_power is already flagged "weak" in B04 |
| Capacity overbuild / execution risk | Utilisation rate of the 16→40 lakh unit facility; ROCE trend (currently 47.4%, likely to compress mechanically per Sec 3C) |

| Substitution (VSaaS/software-only models bypassing hardware refresh cycles) | Enterprise/urban shift toward cloud-managed, longer-refresh-cycle systems could slow unit replacement demand over time |
| Saturation of the "easy" formalisation gains | Once >90% organised share is reached, the import-substitution tailwind (4A) is largely spent; growth reverts to organic TAM CAGR (~15–16%) |

### 4C — Market Structure

- **Top player:** CP Plus (Aditya Infotech) — 20.8% disclosed FY25 share, ₹3,112 Cr revenue, recently IPO'd (2025).
- **Organised vs unorganised:** >80% organised as of Feb 2026, up sharply from ~two-thirds pre-ban — **consolidating/formalising rapidly**, a structural one-time shift, not a steady-state.
- **Import share trend:** Declining sharply (Chinese brands ~33%→near-zero in the internet-connected camera segment specifically, per the STQC rule; analog-only residual for Dahua).
- **Competition basis:** Price and distribution reach dominate over deep technical differentiation at Prizor's price point (weak pricing power per B04); BIS-ER/STQC certification is now a baseline requirement, not a differentiator, since all surviving organised players must hold it.
- **Entries/exits:** Notable forced exits (Hikvision, Dahua-internet-segment, Xiaomi, Realme); notable new entrant/scale-up activity (CP Plus IPO, Prizor's own FY23–26 120% revenue CAGR from a small base) — a genuinely fluid, share-up-for-grabs period, time-bound.

## SECTION 5: SUMMARY & RUNWAY

### 5A — Funnel

```
Global video surveillance market (context only, not operative): $35.9bn FY25 (~₹3,08,000 Cr indicative), 10.4% CAGR to FY30
        │  (geography filter: India only)
        ▼
India video surveillance hardware TAM (FY26): ₹12,370 Cr (conservative) / ₹17,425 Cr (realistic)
        │  (5 SAM filters: product 90% × geography 72% × channel 70% × customer 75% × capability 85% = 28.9%)
        ▼
SAM (FY26): ₹3,580 Cr  (28.9% of conservative TAM)
        │  (current share 4.13%; +2.5pp @3yr, +4.5pp cumulative @5yr)
        ▼
SOM 3yr: ₹237 Cr (17.0% implied revenue CAGR)
SOM 5yr: ₹309 Cr (15.9% implied revenue CAGR)
```

### 5B — Runway Assessment

- **Revenue headroom (SAM ÷ current revenue):** ₹3,580 Cr ÷ ₹148 Cr = **24.2x**
- **TAM growth rate:** ~16.46% (Frost & Sullivan CAGR, corroborated by Prizor's own chart at ~14.4%; midpoint ~15–16%)
- **Company CAGR vs TAM:** Prizor's disclosed FY23–FY26 revenue CAGR is 120.1% — vastly ahead of the ~16% TAM CAGR, i.e. the company has been **gaining share aggressively**, not merely riding the market (consistent with the small-base/import-substitution dynamic in 4A/4C). This historical rate is not sustainable off a larger base and is not used in the SOM CAGR calc (Sec 3B uses the share-gain-rule-bound 15.9–17.0% instead).
- **Years to saturate SAM at a sustainable growth rate:** using the SOM-implied ~17% CAGR, years to fully saturate SAM = ln(24.2) ÷ ln(1.17) ≈ **20+ years**. Even at CP Plus's own realistic-TAM-implied SAM (larger), saturation is decades away.

### 5C — Runway Classification: **MASSIVE**

Basis: >20x revenue headroom combined with a still-growing (mid-teens %) underlying TAM and a genuine, dated, structural competitor-exit event (STQC-driven import substitution) still being distributed across domestic players. This classification holds even under the conservative TAM estimate; it does not depend on the excluded, larger-scope aggregator figures (Method 5). The caveat is entirely on the **capture side** (execution, funding, capacity utilisation — Sec 3C, 4B), not the market-size side.

### 5D — SAM Expansion Levers Actually Being Pursued

- **Tier-2/Tier-3 geographic deepening** (explicit stated strategy, Investor Presentation p.11) — would raise the "geography" filter from 72% toward higher; potential SAM addition of several hundred ₹ Cr if state-depth filter moves from 72%→85% (illustrative: SAM would rise from ₹3,580 Cr to ~₹4,225 Cr at TAM ₹12,370 Cr, holding other filters constant).
- **AI camera range / SoC backward integration (IndieSemic MoU, in pipeline)** — could raise "product fit" and enable entry into higher-ASP AI-analytics segment, though this is a distinct, not-yet-realised product category; no independent TAM data available for India's AI-camera-specific sub-segment — **NOT FOUND**, flagged as input gap.
- **Display products segment (~16% of revenue)** — genuine SAM-expansion optionality but not sized here (Sec 1A); if pursued deliberately (own manufacturing rather than third-party sourcing) it could add a material, currently unquantified TAM slice — flagged for a future stage/run to size properly with Prizor-specific channel data.

### 5E — Final Output Card

- **TAM (India, conservative / realistic):** ₹12,370 Cr / ₹17,425 Cr (FY26)
- **SAM:** ₹3,580 Cr (28.9% of conservative TAM)
- **SOM 3yr / 5yr:** ₹237 Cr / ₹309 Cr
- **SOM-implied revenue CAGR:** 17.0% (yr3) / 15.9% (yr5)
- **Runway class:** MASSIVE (24.2x headroom, ~16% TAM CAGR, structural competitor-exit tailwind, capacity is a surplus not a constraint)
- **Management claim credibility:** REASONABLE (1.03x vs conservative estimate — not inflated, once the chart is read correctly)

**Valuation implication line:** "At 17.0% (yr3) to 15.9% (yr5) revenue CAGR implied by SOM, with a margin trajectory of ~21–23% EBITDA / ~14.0% PAT (FY26 actual, no independently-forecast multi-year margin trajectory available — NOT FOUND beyond current disclosed level), the earnings growth embedded here is approximately **16–17% CAGR** (PAT: ₹20.8 Cr FY26 actual → ~₹33.2 Cr at SOM_3yr, ₹148 Cr → ₹237 Cr revenue, margin held flat), which [supports / does not support] the current valuation of **NOT FOUND x P/E** (no market price/valuation multiple was provided as an input to this stage)."

**Critical cross-check for stage 11:** the market-size-bound SOM CAGR (15.9–17.0%) is dramatically below the company's own FY23–26 track record (120.1%) and below the FY26 YoY print (108.1%). Any stage-11 valuation model that extrapolates recent growth rates forward for 3–5 years should be tested explicitly against this ceiling — a persistent gap between assumed and SOM-implied growth is itself a valuation red flag, independent of multiple choice.
