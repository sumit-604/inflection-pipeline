# KISSHT (OnEMI Technology Solutions Ltd): business narrative

Phase 1 lite. Run 2026-09-19. Evidence stages only. No valuation has run, so this file carries no entry zone, no decision and no destination multiple.

## Business understanding narrative

Kissht sells small unsecured personal loans through an app, and these make up 92.3% of its Rs 8,001 Cr loan book. A newer loan against property product, sold through a branch network, makes up the other 7.7%. The listed parent, OnEMI, does not lend. The loans sit in its wholly owned NBFC, Si Creva, and the parent earns sourcing fees and a guarantee fee. Interest on loans held on the books brings 58% of revenue, fees for sourcing and servicing loans that partner lenders hold bring 27%, and late payment and foreclosure charges bring 11%.

The borrower is a mass market or mass affluent Indian who wants quick credit without a branch visit. These borrowers are not locked in. The repeat customer share of the loan book fell from 73% to 49% in one year, and management says about 45% of its customers carry another personal loan elsewhere. The second customer is the partner lender: 8 partners hold 53.6% of the book off Kissht's balance sheet, and Kissht covers their first losses up to a capped guarantee.

Present demand is personal credit for the mass market. Across all lenders the personal loan and loan against property market is about Rs 26 to 27 lakh crore, and the new age digital lender slice was Rs 60,000 Cr in FY25. Bureau reports from CRIF High Mark, TransUnion CIBIL and Equifax, and the RBI Financial Stability Report, are the outside checks on whether this demand stays healthy.

Demand should grow as formal jobs and tax filing widen the pool of borrowers who qualify for credit. EPFO net payroll additions, GST collections and ITR filing counts show that month by month. The digital lender slice is forecast to reach Rs 4.1 lakh Cr by FY30, but that forecast comes from a report the company paid for, and only the personal loan leg has independent support. Partner money also sets the pace: the RBI Master Directions on digital lending and co lending, and the appetite of the off book lending partner banks and NBFCs, decide how much of the book can sit outside the balance sheet.

The one line with a real edge is personal loan underwriting, built on 400 plus variables and 40 models, with the model accuracy score (AUC) up from 66% to 74% between 2023 and 2026. That edge is an execution lead, not a barrier. No competitor would have to give anything up to copy it, and the talent asymmetry and cannibalisation barrier tests both scored zero. The accuracy trend also rests on decks and calls, not audited filings. Brand, cost position, scale and customer lock in show no moat, and the loan against property line has no moat evidence at all. The named new age peers KreditBee, Navi, Fibe and Moneyview are the share test for the underwriting edge.

## Fast growing lender with unproven loss math

Kissht listed on 8 May 2026. FY26 revenue was Rs 2,209 Cr and profit Rs 281 Cr. The loan book reached Rs 8,001 Cr in June 2026, up 61% in a year and 13% in the quarter. Profit runs at about Rs 5 for every Rs 100 of average loans, and return on equity was 24% in FY26. The two founders hold 24.8% with no pledge and have sold no shares.

The change underway is a move to longer loans, a small secured property book, more partner held loans and cheaper borrowing after a CRISIL upgrade to A minus in February 2026. Credit cost fell from 8.85% to 6.80% of average loans in a year. Other parts of the change lag. The branch rollout added 3 branches in Q1 FY27 against a promise of at least 80 more this year. Cost to income worsened from 46% in FY24 to 57% in FY26, which runs against management's own claim that costs fall with scale.

The backward scorecard lands at AVERAGE, 51 out of 100 on the core score. Operating cash flow was minus Rs 1,648 Cr over FY21 to FY26 against Rs 671 Cr of profit. For a lender that is mostly loan growth passing through the cash flow statement, and the FY26 outflow shrank to Rs 461 Cr from Rs 661 Cr. The verifier found the moat half scored too harshly under the written rules, but the AVERAGE class stands either way.

The loss math is the open question. Kissht writes off about 15.7% of its average book a year, while new bad loans grew 28.9% in FY26. That write off pace, not better lending alone, explains most of the fall in gross bad loans from 2.89% to 2.12%. Management said on its first call that the write off point moved from 120 to 150 days overdue, but neither the annual report nor the prospectus states any numeric trigger. The cost of first loss guarantees on partner loans more than tripled to about Rs 250 Cr in FY26. Asked where that cost sits, management gave three answers on one call that do not agree. In Q1 FY27 gross bad loans rose to 2.25%, loans 30 to 90 days late rose from 2.4% to 3.15%, and collection efficiency slipped.

Management delivered on the numbers it can move fast. Loan growth, credit cost and return on equity all printed at or above guidance in Q1 FY27, and the IPO money reached Si Creva as promised, with the monitoring agency reporting no deviation. On the other side, guidance was restated upward within one quarter, disbursements fell from Rs 3,954 Cr to Rs 3,812 Cr while the book grew on longer tenures, and revenue as a share of loans fell 249 basis points in one quarter. The parent guarantees Si Creva's lenders for 228% of its own net worth, backing 99.8% of the subsidiary's bonds, and this goes unmentioned on both calls. A Rs 832 Cr share sale at Rs 314 came four months after the IPO with capital adequacy already at 40.2%, and no filing gives a number for why. Credibility grades C. The promoter review reads CAUTION: two founder tax disputes of Rs 25 Cr, an Enforcement Directorate summons to Si Creva from March 2023 with no closure on record, and a board with no investor nominee left.

Peers cut both ways. In the same quarter SBI Cards and Poonawalla reported better asset quality, which undercuts Kissht's claim that its Q1 weakness is seasonal. Poonawalla raised equity at 16.8% capital adequacy and Ugro near 18%, far below Kissht's 40.2%. The peer stage first called Kissht's cheaper borrowing story contradicted, but the verifier corrected that: Ugro's borrowing cost fell for 7 straight quarters, SBI Cards guided flat, and only Poonawalla rose. The 150 day write off point sits inside the industry range, with Poonawalla at 180 days.

Market size is not the constraint. Kissht holds about 6.2% of its segment, leaving 23 times revenue headroom, and the run grades the runway MASSIVE. The capacity to fill it depends on the Rs 832 Cr raise clearing its 14 October 2026 shareholder vote and on return on equity holding near the guided 19 to 21%.
