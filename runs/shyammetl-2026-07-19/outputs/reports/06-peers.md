## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

### 2A. Demand environment

Three of four peers (GPIL, SARDAEN, GALLANTT) independently and consistently
describe a structurally strong, infrastructure-led Indian steel demand
environment through FY26, with India recording double-digit domestic
production/crude-steel growth even as global markets (especially China)
contracted. The standout, independently-verifiable new fact: **India became
a net exporter of steel for the first time in six consecutive quarters**
(SARDAEN Q3 FY26, Feb 2026, p.3; corroborated by GALLANTT Q4 FY26, May 2026,
p.2, "India has also, for the first time, become a net exporter of steel").
This is a genuinely strong, independent confirmation of the demand/trade
narrative that Shyam's own concalls did not specifically cite in this form.
JAIBALAJI is a partial dissenting voice: its FY24-FY25 calls flag government
infrastructure order slowdowns pressuring its DI pipe business — but these
calls predate the FY26 safeguard-duty recovery window, so this is not a
direct contradiction of the current-period demand story, more a reminder
that the "structural, not cyclical" framing (Gallantt's phrase) has not
always held for every sub-segment.

### 2B. Pricing and input costs

Coal and iron ore costs were broadly benign-to-declining across FY26 per
GPIL and SARDAEN (Indian Coal Index down 13% YoY per SARDAEN Q1 FY26, p.4;
iron ore range-bound $95-110/ton throughout per GPIL's four calls). The
dominant pricing story is the safeguard duty's lapse-and-return cycle
detailed in Q2 above: weak/pressured pricing through much of H1 FY26,
followed by a genuine, peer-corroborated 5-15% sector-wide price recovery
concentrated in Q3-Q4 FY26. This is broadly consistent with, but not
identical in magnitude to, Shyam's own margin narrative (see Q2 verdict).
Nickel, stainless, and aluminium input-cost commentary is entirely absent
from this peer set — a structural blind spot, not a finding.

### 2C. Capex cycle: industry-wide capacity race, not a lone expander

This is unambiguously an **industry-wide capacity race**, not a lone
expander in a cautious peer set. All four peers are simultaneously
committing large capex programs during the same window Shyam is expanding:
GPIL (INR1,600cr CRM complex + BESS project announced Q1 FY26; a further
~INR4,500-5,000cr greenfield 1 MT steel plant under evaluation; Ari Dongri
mining capacity expansion 2.35 MT → 6 MT; captive solar 165 MW → 540 MW).
SARDAEN (INR500-700cr/year capex program through FY26-FY28; coal mine
capacity expansions; hydro and solar project pipeline; evaluating 600 MW
IPP thermal expansion). GALLANTT (INR3,000cr capex program: steel 1.0 MT →
1.3 MT, mine development, solar). JAIBALAJI's earlier calls (FY24-FY25)
likewise discuss DI pipe and ferro-alloy capacity additions. Every peer in
this set is deploying safeguard-duty-era cash flow into further capacity,
in parallel with Shyam's own aluminium/stainless/HR-mill program — this is
a sector-wide bet on the same demand and trade-policy tailwind, which
somewhat de-risks (via consensus) but also somewhat concentrates (via
correlated capacity coming online together) the volume-growth thesis.

### 2D. Competitive mentions of Shyam Metalics

**None.** Across all 13 transcripts and all four peers, Shyam Metalics is
never named or unmistakably described. This is itself informative: these
four peers' core businesses (commodity long steel, ferro-alloys, DRI/pellet,
merchant power) overlap only partially with Shyam's product mix, and none
touch Shyam's growth segments (stainless, aluminium) at all — consistent
with the coverage gaps identified in Part 1.

### 2E. Risks peers discuss that Shyam's own concalls do not

- **Fatal industrial accident risk**: GPIL Q2 FY26 (Nov 17, 2025) opens by
  disclosing a September 26, 2025 incident at one of its pellet plants that
  killed six employees and injured six others (p.2) — a safety/operational
  risk category not present in Shyam's own B05 disclosure review.
- **M&A/litigation integrity risk**: SARDAEN's SKS Power acquisition (via
  NCLT/IBC) remains under active Supreme Court appeal by unsuccessful
  resolution applicants across all four SARDAEN calls in this set, "reserved
  for order" as of the Q3 FY26 call (Feb 9, 2026, p.3) — a live legal
  overhang on a marquee capital allocation decision, a risk category (M&A
  integration/legal challenge) not present in Shyam's own reviewed disclosures.
- **Commodity-grade HR coil margin compression despite the safeguard duty**:
  GPIL Q1 FY26 explicitly notes that even with the duty active, "the premium
  which sellers were getting per HR coil earlier is not same anymore. HR
  coil has become more of a commodity" (p.7) — directly relevant context for
  Shyam's own planned HR mill economics if its output competes in
  commodity-grade HR rather than differentiated value-added grades; this
  specific risk framing was not found in Shyam's own concalls.
- **A "war" effect on freight/diesel/pricing**: GPIL's Q4 FY26 and
  post-quarter (Q1 FY27) commentary repeatedly cites an unnamed war as
  driving diesel escalation, freight cost spikes (sea freight "crossed $22"),
  and a ~10% post-April-2026 price softening across the supply chain (May
  20, 2026 call, p.6-7) — a live geopolitical risk to the pricing recovery
  that Shyam's own Q4 FY26 call frames differently (citing a Middle East
  conflict as a demand-redirector rather than a direct freight/cost shock).
- **High-grade iron ore import dependency**: SARDAEN Q3 FY26 notes Indian
  iron ore imports reached a seven-year high of 12+ MT in FY26 despite
  higher domestic production, "highlighting robust consumption trend" but
  also a persistent high-grade ore supply gap for the sector (implicit in
  GPIL's May 2026 call, p.4-5) — a raw-material security risk theme absent
  from Shyam's own disclosures.

## PART 5: CROSS-PEER HYPOTHESIS

**Hypothesis**: the entire mid-cap Indian secondary-steel peer set
(GPIL, SARDAEN, GALLANTT, and by extension Shyam) is using the current
safeguard-duty-driven cash-flow windfall as a funding window to diversify
away from commodity steel exposure, precisely because each of them
independently recognizes — GPIL most explicitly — that commodity-grade
long/flat steel margins are structurally compressing even with the duty
active, and that the duty itself is not a permanent fixture (it has already
lapsed once within this analysis window). This is not stated by any single
peer as a strategic thesis, but the pattern is visible only when the four
transcripts are read together: GPIL is funding a BESS/battery-storage
business (10 GW planned) and a value-added CRM/color-coated complex
specifically because, in its own words, "HR coil has become more of a
commodity" even under the safeguard duty; SARDAEN is funding hydropower,
solar, coal-mine, and a mineral-fibre/insulation diversification well beyond
its core ferro-alloys business; GALLANTT, the most steel-pure-play of the
four, is nonetheless funding a de-bottlenecking and mining-integration push
explicitly framed around defending EBITDA/ton against realization softness
rather than betting on further duty-driven price gains; and Shyam itself
(per B05) is deploying its current cash flow into stainless and aluminium,
the two segments furthest from commodity long/flat steel. Read together,
this suggests the safeguard duty is functioning less as a permanent
re-rating of Indian steel margins and more as a temporary capital-raising
window that every mid-cap player in this set is racing to convert into
non-commodity or non-steel optionality before it narrows again.

**Test**: if this hypothesis is correct, each peer's FY27 capex guidance
(and Shyam's own FY27-29 board-approved capex) should show an accelerating,
not flat, share of spend directed at non-commodity-steel or non-steel
diversification (BESS, renewables, specialty/value-added products, mining
integration for margin defense) relative to FY26. If the safeguard duty
instead proves durable and commodity steel margins hold up on their own
merits through FY27, this diversification share should plateau or the
peer set should redirect capex back toward core commodity capacity instead.
