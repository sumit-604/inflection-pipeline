### A2. The simple analogy
Think of a village blacksmith who grew very big and very old (B04). He makes huge steel parts to the customer's drawing. For most of his life he made machines for sugar mills and cement plants. Now he wants government customers: rocket makers, the nuclear power board and the army labs (B04). Those jobs bring fewer rivals, but they come in big lumps with long gaps. He pays his steel suppliers late and gets paid late, so he borrows to keep the shop running (B02, B04). The test is whether he can keep the shop full of government jobs while the old customers still pay, so the fixed cost of the big shop spreads over more sales (B04).

# SECTION 3: BUSINESS UNDERSTANDING NARRATIVE (draft from B01 to B09; Stage 13 holds the final)

Walchandnagar Industries makes heavy steel equipment to the customer's drawing (B04). The defence, nuclear, aerospace and missile line supplies launch-vehicle motor casings and segments, missile casings and canisters, and nuclear reactor parts such as the calandria and end shield (B04). The product lines supply gearboxes, sugar centrifugals, crushing and grinding machines and process equipment, and a small foundry and instrument line adds castings and gauges (B04). In FY26 the defence, nuclear, aerospace and missile line was about 31% of revenue, the product lines about 55% and the foundry and instrument lines about 14%, on the Acuite split, because the audited accounts do not report the strategic share (B04). The strategic parts matter to the customer because only two or three makers hold programme approval per missile and the company says it is one of four in nuclear island equipment, which is a company claim the run could not test (B04, B06). The customer cannot easily switch mid-programme, but each new tender is bid again (B04).

The customers for the strategic line are government bodies: ISRO and VSSC, DRDO, DAE, NPCIL, BARC and the Ministry of Defence (B04). They order by tender, qualify suppliers at programme level, and pay on milestones with retention and bank guarantees held back (B04). The product customers are sugar mills, cement and process plants, shipyards and export customers, and they order on capital spending cycles (B04). No single customer holds more than 10% of receivables and unbilled revenue, but no customer name is given and the oldest receivables sit with a state utility (B02, B04).

Demand today comes from Indian space and defence programmes and from export orders (B04, B03). The Gaganyaan motor case order for 30.53 Cr and 38.13 Cr of aerospace inflow in Q2 FY27 are the visible cases (B09, B05). The signals that track this demand are the ISRO and VSSC procurement and Gaganyaan milestones and the MoD acceptance and DRDO missile contract candidates (B09). In FY26 growth came from products and exports, not from the strategic line: exports rose from 973 to 5,936 Lakhs and India revenue fell 13.5% (B03).

Demand should grow if nuclear orders flow. The End Shield tender is released, the company expects private nuclear orders within 12 months, and the signals are the NPCIL tender and award candidate, the SHANTI Act rules candidate and the competitor award candidate (B05, B09). Sugar mill capex and the capital goods output index track the product base (B09). The run could not size five of the revenue pools in rupees, so the market size is a subset figure (B09). The run did not establish the order book by line or the capacity utilisation (B07, B09).

The advantage sits narrowly in the strategic line, where programme approvals and security clearance give a medium-durability moat at programme level (B04). The Emerging Moat scan scored it as one moderate category, EM 5.2, none (B07). The product and foundry lines have no moat: no cost advantage, a 65-year-old plant in places, and zero funded R&D in FY26 (B04). One filed observation would settle the question: a DNAM against Product margin split, which the corpus does not hold (B07).

# SECTION 5: PLAIN-LANGUAGE SUMMARY

1. Walchandnagar Industries is a 117-year-old maker of heavy steel equipment built to the customer's drawing (B04).
2. It makes rocket motor casings, nuclear reactor parts, missile casings, gearboxes, sugar centrifugal machines and castings (B04).
3. FY26 revenue from operations was 27,519 Lakhs. About 31% came from defence, nuclear, aerospace and missiles, 55% from product lines and 14% from foundry and instruments (B04, B03).
4. The strategic customers are government bodies such as ISRO, DRDO, NPCIL and the Ministry of Defence. They order in big lumps through tenders (B04).
5. The product customers are sugar mills, cement and process plants, shipyards and export customers. They order on capital spending cycles (B04).
6. Demand today comes from space and defence programmes and from exports. Q2 FY27 inflow was 99.46 Cr, including 38.13 Cr of aerospace (B05).
7. In FY26 growth came from products and exports, not from the strategic line. Exports rose from 973 to 5,936 Lakhs and India revenue fell 13.5% (B03).
8. Demand could grow if nuclear orders flow. The End Shield tender is released and the company expects private nuclear orders within 12 months (B05). The run could not confirm either (B09).
9. The moat is narrow. Programme approvals give a medium-durability edge in the strategic line only (B04). The product and foundry lines have no moat (B04, B07).
10. The mental model, in draft: WIL claims a climb from a price-taking fabricator toward a value-added defence supplier. It needs a margin before other income above 8% to prove it (B04). That claim is not yet proven (B03, B05).
11. The fragility verdict is FRAGILE. Seven outside variables must go right and 3 of them are company-narrated (B09, B05). One failure alone can break it: the NCD instalments with 84.7% of borrowings due within a year (B02).
12. The profit picture is thin. Other income was 59.4% of FY26 EBITDA and 17% to 68% of quarterly EBITDA (B04, B12a). The company lost money in 9 of 10 years (B01).
13. The corpus could not establish the order book on one definition. 871.77 Cr and 374.56 Cr are both filed and not reconciled (B04). It also lacks any call transcript since May 2019, the SEBI order text, and the exchange shareholding filing (B00).
14. The biggest open question: is the margin step-up a lasting climb, or a base effect from the Foundry lockout and the TNEB provision (B04, B07)? One filed DNAM against Product margin split would answer it (B07).
15. The second open question: do the NCD instalments of 500 Lakhs each on 31 Dec 2026 and 31 Mar 2027 get paid on time, and from what source (B03, B08)?
