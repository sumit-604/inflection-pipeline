# Vinyas Innovative Technologies (VINYAS) — Business Narrative
Run date: 2026-09-01 | Phase 1 Lite (evidence gate, no valuation)

## Business Understanding Narrative

Vinyas builds electronics for other companies. It makes printed circuit board assemblies, wire harnesses, sub-systems, and full box-builds. These parts sit inside defence radios, avionics, missiles, and medical devices. The customer sends a design or a specification. Vinyas buys the components, assembles the boards, tests them, and ships. Two work types run side by side: build to print, where the customer owns the design, and build to spec, where Vinyas adds engineering. Products and services together are the whole business, and services are about 1% of sales.

The customers are defence public sector units, defence primes, and aerospace and medical OEMs. HAL Korwa signed a five year agreement for the electronics inside head up displays and IFF units. Fresenius Kabi anchors a small medical line. Buying runs through long qualification cycles, so a customer cannot move a running defence program to another vendor part way through its life. That lock in is the customer relationship, though customer concentration is not disclosed for FY23 to FY26.

Demand today rests on an order book of about Rs 1,309 Cr, which is 2.5 times revenue, and 85% to 90% of it is defence. The forward drivers tie to named downstream signals: Positive Indigenisation List updates from the MoD and DDP, the Union Budget defence capital outlay, and NADCAP and PRI accreditation status for Vinyas and its rivals. Growth beyond that leans on the Global A&D Tier 1 OEMs and the Israeli JV partner, on medical device OEM localisation of the Fresenius Kabi type, and on independent EMS and A&D market trackers. Each is externally checkable where a filing or a tracker exists.

The competitive advantage is not spread evenly. The moat sits on the qualified defence and aerospace programs, where the NADCAP AC7120, AS9100-D, ISO 13485, IATF 16949, and Defence Industrial License bundle plus program qualification create switching costs. The general build to print contract work, where the customer owns the design IP, carries little pricing power. The emerging moat scan reads the forward moat as real but shallow, MODEST, and rests mostly on management claims rather than filed evidence. The run did not establish an independent, filed basis for the NADCAP, JV, and US subsidiary claims.

## Defence electronics maker grows faster than cash

Vinyas is a defence led electronics manufacturing services maker, listed on NSE SME Emerge since 2023. FY26 revenue was Rs 514 Cr, up about 30%, PAT was Rs 30.87 Cr, and the EBITDA margin was 12.5%. The order book was Rs 1,309 Cr at March 2026, 2.5 times revenue, and 85% to 90% defence. The shares trade near Rs 1,574, about 64 times trailing earnings.

The claimed transition is a climb from a commodity contract assembler toward a spec qualified defence and aerospace supplier. The proof so far is thin. The FY26 EBITDA margin lift from 11.1% to 12.5% traces mostly to a work in progress inventory change swing in the accounts, not to a cost or pricing advantage, and gross margin fell from 27% to 21%. ROCE can be computed for only two years, FY25 at 24.3% and FY26 at 22.9%, because the balance sheet liability split is absent before FY25.

Gate 0 scores the company AVERAGE, 76 of 160, capped by a cash deal breaker. The moat count is STRONG at 4 of 12, but the cash quality block is the weakest by far. The emerging moat scan reads MODEST at 22.1. Combined, the read is AVERAGE. The gate does not halt on quality; it flags and moves on.

The cash reality is the central problem. FY26 standalone operating cash flow was negative Rs 32.30 Cr against PAT of positive Rs 30.87 Cr. The three year cumulative operating cash flow to PAT is negative. The growth was funded by Rs 61.25 Cr of warrant proceeds and fresh short term debt, not by operations. Receivable days ran near 161 at year end, four times the FY23 level. Two facts point to a timing artifact rather than bad debt: 96.4% of receivables sit under six months of their due date, consistent with milestone billing, and peers across the sector run a similar inventory buffer against chip shortage and supply risk. Two facts cut the other way: the expected credit loss allowance was cut 83.6% in the same year that the over six month overdue bucket rose 603%, with the reversal booked to Other Income, and named foreign exchange risk is left unquantified despite 70% to 75% imported inputs.

Management credibility grades C. Revenue, margin, and order inflow guidance were beaten. The order book target and the CFO's own under four months receivable target were missed and never revisited. The capacity ceiling was stated three ways across three calls, from Rs 1,200 Cr to Rs 2,100 Cr peak, without reconciliation. The most material FY26 fact, negative operating cash flow under a record profit headline, was never raised on the call. Governance reads CAUTION. The record is clean on SEBI, pledges, and promoter selling, and three external independent directors joined at listing. Against that, two associates, Nexus at 26% and UVT at 49%, are absent from the FY26 audited related party note, yet the AGM proposes a combined Rs 550 Cr related party ceiling that exceeds FY26 revenue. A second US subsidiary and a 99.99% shell subsidiary are absent from the audited notes, and the FY26 report dropped the CARO and KAM sections that FY25 carried.

Peers confirm the shape of the business and complicate parts of the story. The 9% to 11% EMS margin band is peer confirmed across CyientDLM, Centum, and Avalon, so the 12.5% margin sits at or above the sector, not below. Peers also confirm the sector trades receivables discipline for a deliberate inventory buffer, so the 161 day working capital needs splitting into inventory and receivables before judging. CyientDLM's matching 161 day figure is inventory driven, with true DSO of 77 days. Two claims fail peer support. The roughly 50% export share is uncorroborated by any filed segment note, and the structurally closest peer, Data Patterns, runs near 1.5% export. The capex to capacity claim, once read correctly, is revenue per line not capex per line, and the true capex is about Rs 30 Cr, so the peer contradiction narrows to a timeline question.

The market opportunity is MODERATE, not open ended. The addressable slice for India defence and aerospace led EMS is about Rs 2,873 Cr, against a total pool of Rs 6,000 Cr to Rs 9,500 Cr. The three year serviceable share implies about Rs 629 Cr of revenue, a headroom of 5.6 times. The share implied CAGR of about 7% sits far below the FY26 actual of 30% and the guidance of 30% to 35%. That gap depends on the unverified 50% export claim. Confirm the export mix from a filed segment note and the true addressable market moves toward the global A&D EMS pool; leave it unconfirmed and 7% is the anchored floor with 30% to 35% the claim under test.
