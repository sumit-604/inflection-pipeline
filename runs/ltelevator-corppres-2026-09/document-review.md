## 11. Plain-language brief

### (1) Summary narrative
L.T. Elevator used the Alpha Ideas platform to widen its story from two engines
to three. The deck is a growth pitch. It leads with reported profit and a clean
compounding chart. FY26 revenue nearly doubled to ₹111 Cr. PAT reached ₹17 Cr.
Revenue has compounded 48% a year since FY23.

The new fact is DYPC. In August 2026 the company signed a deal to buy DYPC Inc,
a Korean automated-parking firm with 12 patents and a global track record. This
takes the company into the US market and lifts parking from a small India line
to a global ambition. The May thesis never saw this. It changes the scope of the
business and needs a fresh look at cost, funding, and dilution. The deck names
no price for the deal.

Two engines track the thesis well. Ricardo, the D2C home-lift brand, hit a
₹100 Cr order milestone six months early. The new plant is on schedule for a Q4
FY27 start at 2.5 times the current capacity. Both match what the thesis
expected.

The gap is cash. The deck shows profit and hides cash flow. It carries no
cash-flow statement and no balance sheet. The thesis records FY26 operating cash
at minus 0.98 times profit, and "cash flow negative through FY27" is a
thesis-break line. A deck that omits the one statement that tests its own profit
is telling you where to look. Two tripwires stay open: the Ricardo merger
closure and the cash turn. Neither the deck confirms.

Verdict: the growth case is intact and now larger. The quality case is
unproven. Treat every forward number as a management claim until a filing or a
concall confirms the cash and the DYPC terms.

### (2) Sector intelligence
- India elevator market: deck ₹35,000 Cr+, 12-15% CAGR. The house TAM study put
  the total Indian elevator market at ₹10,500-15,000 Cr. The deck figure is
  more than double. Treat ₹35,000 Cr as an unverified MGMT claim.
- Home elevator (D2C) sub-market: ~₹3,000 Cr, fragmented, no MNCs. Nibav is the
  pioneer at ~₹350-400 Cr.
- Global mechanical parking: $5 Bn+; DYPC holds ~5% with 739+ units.

### (3) Business-model intelligence
- Three engines on one plant. Engine 1 core B2B/B2G elevators, GM ~50%, NM
  13-15%, plus a recurring AMC annuity. Engine 2 Ricardo B2C, GM 58-60%, NM
  ~20% long term, cash-collected before dispatch (the thesis WC-fix mechanism).
  Engine 3 automated parking, NM 13-16%, now global via DYPC.
- The bet: shift mix toward high-margin, cash-up-front B2C and IP-led parking,
  while a 2.5x plant absorbs the combined ₹289 Cr order book.
- Weakness: heavy working capital in the B2B/B2G core, unproven cash conversion,
  and an untested overseas acquisition.

### (4) Competition intelligence
- Positioned as the only listed Indian SME spanning B2B elevators + D2C home
  lifts + automated parking with global IP.
- Scale is small: ₹111 Cr vs Nibav ~₹350-400 Cr, Johnson/Omega ₹1,000+ Cr, and
  MNCs (Otis, Kone) ₹3,000+ Cr. The edge claimed is breadth and margin, not size.
- D2C threat: Nibav leads the digital home-lift channel. MNC threat: Otis/Kone
  hold scale and, per the deck, only "limited" parking. The DYPC IP is the claim
  to a durable parking edge; it rests on the deal closing.
