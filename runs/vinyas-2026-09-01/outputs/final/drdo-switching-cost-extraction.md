## BLOCK A — DRDO

**1. Every DRDO mention in the corpus.** Only ONE, and it is generic policy, not company-specific. Quote (Vinyas_PROSP.pdf p123): "...procedure for acquisition of systems designed and developed by the DRDO/DPSUs/OFB..." Comment: this is a description of the Defence Acquisition Procedure categories, in the industry/regulatory-overview section — not a statement that Vinyas is a DRDO vendor. "DRDO" appears nowhere else: not in the FY24, FY25 or FY26 Annual Reports, not in any of the three concalls, not in the FY26 or H1 FY26 or H2 FY25 presentations, and not in the results filings.

**2. Vinyas as registered/approved/empanelled DRDO vendor, on a DRDO approved-vendor list, holding line-item approvals, or supplying DRDO-developed programmes.** NOT DISCLOSED. Three "approved vendor" phrases exist, none of which is this:
- Quote (FY25 AR p15, under "Supply Chain Management"): "...mitigate component obsolescence, validate approved vendor..." Comment: this is Vinyas managing ITS OWN supplier/approved-vendor list for components — the opposite direction from being a DRDO-approved vendor.
- Quote (Vinyas_PROSP.pdf p117): "Approved vendor list for efficient supply chain management." Comment: again Vinyas's own supplier management, not a customer/DRDO approval of Vinyas.
- Quote (Concall_Nov_2025_Transcript.pdf p7, Sumukh Narendra): "We are a part of lots of defense programs where there is a repeat requirement that would come. And in a lot of cases, we are the only approved vendor. So, we would be participating as a single tender opportunity in a lot of cases." Comment: this is the strongest switching-cost statement in the corpus — a management claim of sole-approved-vendor / single-tender status on many programmes. But it is an unqualified management claim (tier: management, 🎙️), it names no programme and no approving authority, and it does NOT say DRDO or line-item DRDO approval. It supports the weaker "single-tender in many cases" claim, not the DRDO-line-item mechanism.

**3. SAMAR, DRDO technology transfer, or a DRDO licensing agreement.** NOT DISCLOSED. No mention of SAMAR, DRDO ToT, or any DRDO licence anywhere in the corpus.

**4. ISRO / the 2017 industry-built satellite consortium.** NOT DISCLOSED / not corroborated. "ISRO" appears nowhere in the corpus. Quote (Vinyas_PROSP.pdf p101): "satellites, robots that can roam in moon & Mars or swarm robots for defence applications..." Comment: this is technology/market context, not a Vinyas programme. No filed document places "Vinyas Technologies, Mysuru" in a six-company satellite consortium with Alpha Design, Newtech, Aidin, DCX Cables and Avantel; the blog claim is not corroborated by any filed document (the PROSP p12 "consortium thereof" refers to the share offer, not a satellite build).

## BLOCK E — VERDICT

**16. Does the corpus support, refute, or fail to address DRDO line-item approvals?** It CANNOT ADDRESS the claim, and it leans against it on the filed record. The only DRDO reference is generic policy (PROSP p123). Every "approved vendor" phrase is either Vinyas's own supplier management (FY25 AR p15, PROSP p117) or a management sole-source claim with no authority named (Concall_Nov_2025 p7). The corpus supports the WEAKER claims — Vinyas supplies BEL and HAL, works largely under defence offset, and management asserts it is "the only approved vendor" on many programmes — but it does NOT establish a DRDO-approved-vendor status or line-item approvals. Per the rule, DRDO approval is not inferred from the Defence Licence, AS9100, or the BEL/HAL relationship.

**17. The single document that would settle it, and where it would be filed.** A DRDO / BEL configuration or LATOT approved-vendor list naming Vinyas at the line-item level, or a DRDO vendor-registration / SAMAR rating certificate for Vinyas. These are held by DRDO and the DPSU (BEL), not by Vinyas as a public filing; the nearest public traces would be a BEL tender/purchase-order document naming Vinyas as the approved source, or a DRDO empanelment/SAMAR notice. None is in the company's own filings, and none can be produced from this corpus.

**18. Does Block C change the "no long-term contracts, therefore no switching costs" reading in the signed mental model?** Partially, and only on the commercial axis. The prospectus confirms the no-long-term-agreement / purchase-order model at IPO (PROSP p25). Post-listing the corpus shows a genuine shift toward agreements — a medical-OEM manufacturing agreement (Jun-2025 concall) and, per the ferried note, a HAL-Korwa 5-year defence LTA (Apr-2026). If the HAL-Korwa LTA is genuine and multi-year, it is the company's first defence long-term agreement and softens the "no contract-based switching cost" reading — but its terms are undisclosed, so the switching cost it creates cannot be sized, and it is a COMMERCIAL agreement, not the DRDO REGULATORY lock the BEL Q3 FY26 mechanism describes. On filed evidence the switching-cost case still rests on (a) ~1.5-2 year facility/line qualification (an industry barrier shared with Avalon/Centum/Kaynes/Cyient DLM) and (b) one undocumented management sole-source claim. The corpus does not reverse the "no firm-level advantage" reading; it leaves exactly one management claim that only DRDO/BEL line-item documentation could confirm.
