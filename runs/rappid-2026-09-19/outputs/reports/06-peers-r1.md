# STAGE 6 — PEER CONCALL VERIFICATION: Rappid Valves (India) Ltd (RAPPID)
Run date: 2026-09-19. Protocol v1.1.

Main company claims under test come from B05 (01-Jun-2026 earnings call, NO-CONCALL MODE, single company transcript). Peer set: 8 of 12 transcripts (KSB Ltd x4 quarters, Atam Valves Ltd x3 quarters, Quest Flow Controls/Meson Valves India Ltd x1 quarter). Marine Electricals dropped (files no transcripts); no listed pure-play marine valve maker exists in India.

Note on naming: the BSE-SME filer named "Quest Flow Controls" in the task brief files its transcript under the corporate name "Meson Valves India Limited" (CIN U29299GA2016PLC012972, BSE 543982) — the transcript header confirms this is the same SME valve/actuator/remote-control-valve-system maker referenced. Cited below as QUESTFLOW (Meson Valves India Ltd).

## PART 1: CLAIM-BY-CLAIM VERIFICATION

### Claim 1 — PSU shipyard payment terms / receivable cycle / price-escalation clause
**Claim (verbatim):** "What are typical PSU shipyard (Mazagon Dock, GRSE, Cochin Shipyard, HSL) payment terms and receivable-cycle length for naval valve orders, and do PSU contracts carry any price-escalation clause?"
**Verdict: PARTIALLY VERIFIED**
**Peer evidence:**
- Atam Valves states its industry-standard credit period is "90 to 120 days" against the new MSME 45-day payment rule, which it says is disrupting order flow because customers "want to be sure about certain things" before committing under the shorter law (ATAM-Concall_Apr_2024_Transcript.txt, p.6, Amit Jain). Atam has zero PSU exposure: "No PSUs, no L1 bidding and so on" (ATAM-Concall_Apr_2024_Transcript.txt, p.14, Amit Jain).
- KSB's own PSU-type receivable problem is on the solar (PM-KUSUM, state-government) book, not naval: "your net working capital more than 120 days?" / "Yes, today... I feel it is not right to take today's example... we expect it to come down below 120 days" (KSB-Concall_Sep_2025_Transcript.txt, p.11, Rajeev Jain). One year later the issue persists: "receivable days kind of inched up... this is meant to do to the solar business... this depends on the fund's availability and documentation. Typically this goes to state governments" (KSB-Concall_Aug_2026_Transcript.txt, p.29-30, Mahesh Bhave). KSB Mar 2026 names the mechanism explicitly: "payment becomes receivable only after you have uploaded all the things report, installation, satisfactory performance in that portal. The portal acknowledges it. That day it starts" (KSB-Concall_Mar_2026_Transcript.txt, p.28-29, Rajeev Jain).
**Peers silent:** No peer discusses Mazagon Dock, GRSE, Cochin Shipyard or HSL by name, naval-valve-specific payment terms, or any price-escalation clause in a defence/PSU contract.
**Net read:** No like-for-like naval-shipyard benchmark exists in the peer set. But the pattern that emerges — a government/PSU-linked counterparty with a portal-driven, documentation-gated payment process running 120+ days — is independently confirmed at KSB for its PM-KUSUM state-government book, and Atam's plain industrial credit norm already sits at 90-120 days before any PSU bureaucracy is added. Rappid's ~170-day cycle is therefore not obviously anomalous against the PSU-adjacent norm elsewhere in the sector, but no peer confirms 170 days specifically or the presence/absence of a price-escalation clause.

### Claim 2 — Naval/marine margin differential and non-ferrous raw-material pass-through
**Claim (verbatim):** "What EBITDA margin differential, if any, do naval/marine valve orders carry versus general industrial valve orders, and how has non-ferrous raw-material price volatility (nickel-aluminium bronze, copper) been passed through in the last 12-18 months? Tests the 'nobody likes to be at the 12% margin point' comment..."
**Verdict: CONTRADICTED (on the margin-ceiling framing); PARTIALLY VERIFIED (on imperfect cost pass-through)**
**Peer evidence:**
- Quest Flow Controls (Meson Valves India Ltd) — the closest peer by product mix (nonferrous valves, defence/marine/offshore customer base) — reports EBITDA margin of 24.96% and PAT margin of 14.85% for H2 FY24, and 23.61% EBITDA / 14.3% PAT for full FY24 (QUESTFLOW-Concall_Jun_2024_Transcript.txt, p.3-4, Brijesh Madhav Manerikar). This directly contradicts the idea that ~12% is an unavoidable floor for a nonferrous, defence/marine-facing valve maker.
- KSB confirms imperfect input-cost pass-through at the group level: "We have not been able to pass on the 100%. Let's say we have been given foundries maybe in the range also of 12% to 15%. Our price hike to our standard markets would be still single-digit so far" (KSB-Concall_Aug_2026_Transcript.txt, p.24, Rajeev Jain). Direction matches Rappid's claim (input cost up, price realisation lags), but magnitude is much smaller than Rappid's cited ~32% NAB/copper shock.
- KSB's own consolidated EBITDA sits at 12-14% despite gross margin compression from 54% to 43-44% over 2017-2024, which management attributes to product mix and strategic entry pricing into new segments, not input-cost pass-through failure (KSB-Concall_Nov_2025_Transcript.txt, p.31-32, Mahesh Bhave / Rajeev Jain).
**Peers silent:** No peer explicitly splits margin by naval/marine versus general industrial order within its own book, so a like-for-like naval-vs-industrial margin delta is not directly available from any single peer.
**Net read:** The evidence cuts against Rappid's framing more than it supports it. A peer specialising in the same nonferrous, defence/marine niche runs materially higher margins (mid-20s% EBITDA) than Rappid's ~12%, which weakens the "nobody likes to be at 12%, it's structural" reading and strengthens a company-specific-execution reading. Separately, and consistent with Rappid's claim, KSB independently confirms that raw-material cost increases are not being fully passed through anywhere in this sector right now — a real, sector-wide mechanism, just smaller in magnitude than Rappid's NAB shock.

### Claim 3 — Working capital cycle typical for a valve maker serving PSU shipyards and private industry
**Claim (verbatim):** "What working-capital cycle (debtor days, inventory days, supplier-advance practice) is typical for a valve maker serving PSU shipyards and private industrial customers?"
**Verdict: PARTIALLY VERIFIED**
**Peer evidence:** Same Atam 90-120 day industry credit norm and KSB's 120+ day, portal-gated PSU/state-government receivable cycle as cited under Claim 1. No peer discloses inventory days or a supplier-advance practice.
**Peers silent:** Quest Flow Controls gives no working-capital-day figures at all in its single filed transcript; it discusses capacity and demand, not the balance sheet.
**Net read:** Elongated working capital (100+ days) tied to PSU/government-linked customers is a genuine, peer-corroborated feature of this industry, not a Rappid-specific anomaly. But no peer quantifies inventory days or confirms/denies an advance-to-supplier practice, so the claim is only partially triangulated.

### Claim 4 — Size of the Indian marine/naval valve addressable market and per-vessel valve cost share
**Claim (verbatim):** "How large is the Indian marine/naval valve addressable market, and what share of a shipyard's per-vessel cost typically goes to valves? Tests the CMD's own admission that no shipyard could give him a consistent answer..."
**Verdict: UNVERIFIABLE**
**Peer evidence:** Quest Flow Controls (Meson Valves India Ltd) sizes only the broad industrial valve market: "the global industrial valve market is projected to grow from USD 80.4 billion in 2023 to USD 99.8 billion by 2028... In India, the industrial valves market was valued at USD 2.6 billion in 2021 and is expected to reach USD 3.7 billion by 2028," alongside a defence-export target of "USD 5 billion by 2024-25" (QUESTFLOW-Concall_Jun_2024_Transcript.txt, p.3, Brijesh Madhav Manerikar). None of this isolates marine/naval valves or per-vessel cost share. KSB, the named check-peer, discusses entering the marine segment across three of its four transcripts (Sep 2025, Nov 2025, Aug 2026) purely in qualitative terms — "one sector which we see will be definitely propelling the growth strategy," "our entry into an export... for a nuclear plant in Europe" — without ever attaching a rupee or dollar figure to the marine/naval opportunity specifically.
**Peers silent:** No peer gives a marine/naval-valve-specific TAM or a per-vessel valve cost percentage.
**Net read:** This silence is itself informative. KSB — a far larger, more analyst-covered company with an active marine push — is just as unable to size its own marine opportunity in rupee terms as Rappid's CMD admits he is. This corroborates, rather than resolves, the CMD's admission: the naval/marine valve TAM appears to be a genuinely unquantified pocket across the whole listed peer set, not a gap unique to Rappid's disclosure.

### Claim 5 — Capacity-utilization-vs-realized-revenue reconciliation
**Claim (verbatim):** "How does a claimed capacity-utilization figure... normally track against realized revenue for a build-to-order valve maker, given that valve size and pressure rating, not unit count, drive revenue?"
**Verdict: UNVERIFIABLE**
**Peer evidence:** Atam Valves discloses capacity in physical units, not rupees: "a valve manufacturing capacity of 96,000 pieces per month across all products categories and for FY'24, our capability utilization was 85%" (ATAM-Concall_Apr_2024_Transcript.txt, p.2), against FY24 revenue of Rs52.6 crore. KSB discloses utilization purely as a percentage — "close to 85% kind of utilization" (KSB-Concall_Nov_2025_Transcript.txt, p.39), later "already at 80% to 90% capacity utilization" (KSB-Concall_Aug_2026_Transcript.txt, p.9) — without ever pairing it to an explicit rupee-crore capacity ceiling.
**Peers silent:** Neither named peer frames capacity as "well-equipped up to Rs X crore of revenue at Y% utilization" the way Rappid does, so no peer offers a like-for-like reconciliation check.
**Net read:** No peer uses Rappid's revenue-ceiling framing, so whether that framing is normal for the business model cannot be tested against these peers. It is mildly notable that both named peers describe capacity in units or bare percentage terms, sidestepping the very reconciliation problem (unit count vs. size/pressure-rating-driven revenue) that Rappid's disclosure now has to answer for.

### Claim 6 — 50% advance payment to foundry/raw-material suppliers to lock pricing
**Claim (verbatim):** "How common is a practice of paying foundry/raw-material suppliers 50% advance to lock pricing, and how much does it widen the buyer's own working-capital gap?"
**Verdict: UNVERIFIABLE**
**Peer evidence:** None. Neither Atam Valves (three transcripts) nor Quest Flow Controls (one transcript) discusses any supplier-advance-payment practice, at 50% or any other level.
**Peers silent:** Both named check-peers (Atam, Quest Flow) are silent on this topic entirely.
**Net read:** No basis to confirm or deny whether a 50% foundry advance is standard industry practice or a Rappid-specific working-capital choice.

## PART 2: UNPROMPTED CROSS-READ

**2A Demand environment.** Across all eight transcripts, no peer describes a demand slump. KSB reports a sustained, broad-based order book across nuclear (~Rs1,300 crore on hand across four quarters), non-nuclear growth of 12-16% ex-nuclear/ex-solar, and fresh entries into marine, mining and green hydrogen (KSB-Concall_Sep_2025/Nov_2025/Mar_2026/Aug_2026). Atam Valves targets 35-40% growth over the next two years, hiccups attributed to the MSME 45-day law and one election-quarter pause, not weak demand (ATAM-Concall_Jul_2024_Transcript.txt, p.4). Quest Flow Controls describes running at "almost 100% capacity utilization" purely on domestic PSU/defence/marine demand, citing "Amrit Kaal 2047" and a "huge surge in the local domestic shipbuilding projects, naval projects" (QUESTFLOW-Concall_Jun_2024_Transcript.txt, p.6). This collectively supports, rather than contradicts, Rappid's framing that PSU/naval-adjacent demand is real and structurally growing — the one point of daylight is that no peer, including KSB with its own new marine push, can size that specific pocket in rupees (see Claim 4).

**2B Pricing and input costs.** KSB is the only peer that quantifies pass-through failure: foundry cost increases of 12-15% against single-digit realised price hikes, done "in a phased way" to protect customers (KSB-Concall_Aug_2026_Transcript.txt, p.24). This matches the direction of Rappid's margin narrative (input costs outrunning realised pricing) at roughly half to a third of Rappid's cited ~32% NAB/copper magnitude. Against that, Quest Flow Controls' 23-25% EBITDA margin on a nonferrous, defence/marine mix (see Claim 2) is the one clean data point that complicates Rappid's "12% is where everybody in this niche ends up" framing.

**2C Capex cycle.** Every peer that discusses capex is expanding, not holding or cutting: KSB is adding a fourth shed at Sinnar and an extra plot at Shirwal (KSB-Concall_Sep_2025_Transcript.txt, p.20-21); Atam Valves is funding a ~Rs30 crore new plant for larger valve sizes plus a bathroom-faucet import line (ATAM-Concall_Nov_2023_Transcript.txt, p.6-9); Quest Flow Controls is adding CNC machines and backward-integrating into its own foundry (QUESTFLOW-Concall_Jun_2024_Transcript.txt, p.7-8). This is an industry-wide capacity race, not a lone-expander situation — Rappid's own capex and capacity build sit inside a sector-wide investment cycle rather than standing out as an outlier commitment.

**2D Competitive mentions.** No peer names Rappid or gives an unmistakable description of it (grepped for "Rappid," "per vessel," "vessel cost" across all eight files; no hits). KSB does discuss a named competitive threat in its own nuclear-pump business — "one of our competitors has a development order" (KSB-Concall_Sep_2025_Transcript.txt, p.16, Nitin Patil) — which is a useful analogue for how a leading peer frames competitive risk that Rappid's B05 report does not address for the naval-valve space at all.

**2E Risks peers discuss that Rappid does not.**
- The MSME 45-day payment law actively holding up order execution for a small valve maker (Atam Valves: "most of the projects were put on hold" — ATAM-Concall_Apr_2024_Transcript.txt, p.5) — a live regulatory risk to receivable timing that Rappid's B05 report never names.
- Skilled-manpower attrition and retention named as an explicit, actively managed strategic risk at KSB even in a critical nuclear-qualified workforce (KSB-Concall_Sep_2025_Transcript.txt, p.20-21) — Rappid's report does not discuss labour/skills risk at all.
- A named, tracked competitor "development order" in a qualification-gated segment (KSB's nuclear pumps) — Rappid's report never frames naval-valve qualification as a race against a specific, tracked rival.
- The documentation-portal payment-delay mechanism KSB names explicitly for its PSU-adjacent solar book (upload-then-acknowledge, state-by-state variation) is a more granular mechanical explanation for long PSU receivables than anything Rappid's B05 report offers for its own ~170-day cycle.

## PART 4: TRIANGULATION SUMMARY

- Claims verified: 0 of 6
- Claims partially verified: 2 of 6 (Claim 1 — PSU payment-cycle plausibility; Claim 3 — working-capital elongation)
- Claims contradicted: 1 of 6 (Claim 2 — the "12% is where everybody in this niche ends up" framing, contradicted by Quest Flow Controls' 23-25% EBITDA margin in the same nonferrous/defence-marine niche)
- Claims unverifiable: 3 of 6 (Claim 4 — marine TAM sizing; Claim 5 — capacity-vs-revenue reconciliation method; Claim 6 — 50% supplier advance practice)

**Most consequential contradiction:** Quest Flow Controls (Meson Valves India Ltd), a small nonferrous valve maker selling into the same defence/marine/offshore customer base Rappid targets, posts 23-25% EBITDA margin and 14-15% PAT margin — roughly double Rappid's ~12% margin point. This undercuts the framing that 12% is simply where this niche settles; it points toward Rappid's margin outcome reflecting company-specific pricing, bidding, or NAB-timing decisions more than an unavoidable sector floor.

**Strongest independent confirmation:** The PSU/defence/marine demand tailwind Rappid's management cites is corroborated across the whole peer set without exception — KSB's nuclear and marine order book growth, Quest Flow Controls' 100% capacity utilization on domestic PSU/defence/marine orders, and Atam Valves' unrelated-but-confident growth guidance all describe an expanding, not contracting, demand environment for valve makers serving this customer base in this period.

**Overall:** the peer set complicates the main company's margin narrative more than it supports it, while it independently supports the main company's demand narrative. On working capital, peers make Rappid's long receivable cycle more plausible as an industry feature (PSU/government-linked customers run 90-120+ days elsewhere too) without confirming the specific ~170-day figure. On the two capacity/advance-payment mechanics questions (Claims 5 and 6) and the marine-TAM question (Claim 4), the peer set simply has nothing to say either way — these remain open, unresolved by any independent source available to this run.

## PART 5: CROSS-PEER HYPOTHESIS (mandatory closing step)

Reading the three peers together rather than individually: Atam Valves (broad ferrous/nonferrous industrial mix, 13-18% EBITDA), KSB (large diversified pumps-and-valves major, 12-14% consolidated EBITDA despite gross-margin compression), and Quest Flow Controls (narrow nonferrous defence/marine specialist, 23-25% EBITDA) sit on an inverse relationship between segment breadth and margin, not between "naval" and "industrial" per se. Every peer that names a qualification gate — Atam's API approval, KSB's PTR/EIL/GeM tendering for BP&CL and PSU business, Quest Flow's own type-testing and government-agency approvals for marine pumps — describes that gate as slow and capital-intensive, and the peers pursuing the most simultaneous gates (KSB: nuclear + solar + marine + mining + BP&CL; Atam: API + plumbing + export + bathroom-faucet import) show the most margin dilution, while the peer holding a single, narrow, deeply qualified niche (Quest Flow's nonferrous defence/marine book) shows the highest margin.

This is testable and specific: it predicts that Rappid's margin will not close toward Quest Flow Controls' ~24% band by adding more qualification-gated verticals (US export, CBG, mining-adjacent products) at the same time it is still building out its naval PSU book — the same simultaneous-breadth pattern that appears to be diluting KSB's and Atam's blended margins today. It predicts the opposite path — narrowing toward the highest-margin, most-qualified PSU/naval niche and letting that mix rise as a share of revenue — is the one the peer set shows actually working. No single peer states this trade-off; each only names its own qualification bottleneck.
