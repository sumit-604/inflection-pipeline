## 3. "ULTIMATE BENEFICIARIES" LAYERED FUNDING DISCLOSURE (Note 49(b), AR
p.854–855, STANDALONE — first-time disclosure under the amended Schedule
III intermediary/ultimate-beneficiary rule)

🟡 **New for FY26: the parent funded a subsidiary — Clean Max Osaka Gas
Renewable Energy Pvt Ltd ("CORE", formerly Clean Max Yamuna Pvt Ltd) —
with ₹216.04 Cr total (₹196.32 Cr in Oct-25 + ₹19.72 Cr in Dec-25, split
across equity, Optionally Convertible Debentures, and a loan), and CORE
then used these funds to acquire equity stakes in and/or lend to SEVEN
further step-down subsidiaries** (Scorpius ₹35.87 Cr, Mirage ₹23.84 Cr,
Eliora ₹69.05 Cr, BIAL Renewable Energy ₹28.46 Cr, Uranus ₹18.18 Cr,
Aurora ₹5.80 Cr, Beta ₹10.96 Cr — summing to ₹192.15 Cr of the ₹216.04
Cr received). This two-layer parent → intermediate-subsidiary → seven-
further-subsidiaries fund flow is disclosed specifically because it
triggers the Companies Act "Ultimate Beneficiaries" anti-layering
disclosure (the rule exists to flag exactly this kind of pass-through
structure). The Company separately confirms it has "complied with the
number of layers prescribed" under Section 2(87) and has not itself
received any Ultimate-Beneficiary-triggering funds from outside parties
(Note 49(c)). Not inherently improper — the CORE entity name ("Osaka
Gas") suggests a strategic JV co-investment vehicle — but the layering
itself, and the commercial rationale for routing capital through an
intermediate entity rather than direct investment, is a fair management
question given the size (₹216 Cr) relative to the group's ₹94.13 Cr FY26
consolidated PAT.

## 4. FIRST-YEAR INTEREST RATE SWAP PROGRAMME — MOSTLY UNHEDGED FLOATING
BOOK (Note 37.4.3C interest rate risk, AR p.439–441, CONSOLIDATED)

🔴 **New finding entirely missed by Pass 1, which covered FX/USD hedging
gaps but did not address interest-rate hedging at all.** The Group
entered interest rate swaps for the first time in FY26, notional
₹1,026.78 Cr (10,267.80M), swapping floating-rate foreign-currency
borrowings to fixed, 1:1 hedge ratio, cash-flow-hedge accounting applied.
Against this: **total variable-rate financial liabilities = ₹8,651.29 Cr
(86,512.87M) FY26, up from ₹6,914.03 Cr (69,140.25M) FY25** — i.e. only
**≈11.9% of the Group's floating-rate debt book is interest-rate hedged**
even after the new swap programme; the remaining **₹7,624.51 Cr sits
fully exposed to rate movements**. Company-disclosed sensitivity: a 50bp
rate increase reduces pre-tax profit by ₹38.12 Cr (381.22M) net of the
swap benefit (₹43.26 Cr gross exposure less ₹5.13 Cr swap offset).
Fixed-rate financial liabilities also grew, ₹2,222.79 Cr FY25 → ₹3,952.39
Cr FY26 (39,523.89M), but variable-rate debt still dominates the ₹12,410
Cr total debt stack and grew in both absolute and relative terms. 🔴
Watch — this compounds Pass 1's finding #7 (unhedged USD principal
exposure ₹1,431.37 Cr): the Group carries simultaneous unhedged FX
principal risk AND unhedged interest-rate risk on the large majority of a
rapidly growing, still-net-building debt stack, in the same listing year.

## 5. OTHER INCOME QUALITY — TWO CONSECUTIVE YEARS OF LARGE ONE-OFF /
NON-CASH ITEMS (Note 31, AR p.428–429, CONSOLIDATED — pattern not
connected in Pass 1)

🟡 Pass 1 separately flagged the FY25 ₹27.50 Cr step-acquisition gain
(Section 6 of Pass 1) and the FY26 VPPA hedge-ineffectiveness gain
(Section 12 of Pass 1) but did not connect them as a two-year pattern in
the same P&L line. Reading Note 31 in full:
- **FY25 Other Income = ₹114.64 Cr (1,146.41M)**, of which ₹27.50 Cr
  (24.0%) is the non-recurring Clean Max Alpha LeaseCo step-acquisition
  gain, plus a further ₹24.14 Cr (241.36M) "gain on modification of
  borrowing terms" (also one-off, Nil in FY26) — together **≈45% of FY25
  Other Income is non-recurring**.
- **FY26 Other Income = ₹162.34 Cr (1,623.41M)**, of which ₹71.54 Cr
  (715.35M, 44.1%) is the "cash flow hedges — ineffective portion of
  changes in fair value" line tied to the newly recognised, internally
  modelled VPPA derivative (Note 37.3) — a model-driven, non-cash,
  Level-3 fair-value gain, not a cash item.
Both years, roughly 45% of a P&L line that is smaller than but not
trivial relative to segment EBITDA is either non-recurring or non-cash.
An investor building a "clean" earnings bridge for the transition thesis
should strip both years' Other Income of these components, not just the
FY26 VPPA item Pass 1 flagged in isolation.

## 7. SECURITY PACKAGE — PLEDGE PERCENTAGE HIGHER DURING CONSTRUCTION
THAN PASS 1'S GENERALISED "51%" FIGURE (Note 54, sampled, AR ~p.510–522)

🟡 Pass 1 described the recurring security pattern as "pledge of 30% on
a pari-passu basis plus a non-disposal undertaking over a further 21%
(51% total)". At least one sampled facility (AR p.510–522 range) instead
specifies **pledge of 74% of the parent's fully paid-up equity in the
borrowing SPV during construction/stabilisation, stepping down to 51%
only after the Project Stabilisation Date and two successful annual
covenant tests (based on the FY25 and FY26 audited balance sheets)**.
This means the pledge intensity is HIGHER than the 51% baseline during
exactly the phase when a project SPV is least proven — newly
commissioned assets, pre-stabilisation, the same population that Pass
1's CARO clause (xvii) finding shows is disproportionately running cash
losses. Not a new risk in kind, but a refinement: the encumbrance on
parent equity in the riskiest-stage SPVs is greater than the headline
51% figure suggests.

## 8. NCI PROFIT SHARE IS NEGATIVE BOTH YEARS — DIRECT P&L CORROBORATION
OF THE CARO CASH-LOSS FINDING (Note 20(B), AR p.422–423, CONSOLIDATED)

🔴 **"Profit for the year attributable to NCI" = ₹(8.56) Cr [(85.55M)]
FY26 and ₹(8.41) Cr [(84.14M)] FY25 — the minority-interest pool as a
whole is being allocated a LOSS in both years**, even as fresh minority
capital keeps being injected: "Change in NCI due to additional
investments" = ₹349.29 Cr (3,492.86M) FY26 and ₹326.40 Cr (3,264.02M)
FY25. This is the P&L-level confirmation of Pass 1's #1 finding (103
group entities with CARO qualifications, ~90+ for cash losses): the
newly-funded project SPV cohort is not merely running individual cash
losses per the auditor's CARO note, it is, in aggregate, loss-making at
the consolidated minority-interest level too, in both FY25 and FY26. The
"annuity" archetype assumes each commissioned SPV becomes a net
contributor; two straight years of aggregate negative NCI profit share
says the portfolio as a whole has not crossed that line yet, even as new
capital is deployed to expand it further.

**Partial answer to Pass 1's open item on the NCI put-option quantum**:
Note 20(B) shows a "Transfer to other financial liabilities" line of
₹8.52 Cr (85.23M) FY26 and ₹9.11 Cr (91.06M) FY25 — a reclassification
out of NCI equity into financial liabilities, consistent with Ind AS 32
treatment of written put options over NCI shares (present-valuing the
option liability). Pass 1 flagged the 26%-NCI buyout put options in Note
38(B) as "no quantum disclosed" — these transfer amounts are the closest
quantification available in the notes, though they are the incremental
annual movement, not the cumulative put-option liability balance, which
remains NOT FOUND as a standalone disclosed figure.
