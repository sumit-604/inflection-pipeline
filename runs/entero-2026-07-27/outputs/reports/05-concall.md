### 2D. What they are NOT saying

- **Receivables quality**: the downstream flag that receivables grew +50.6% against +29.4% revenue growth is never addressed in any of the three calls. The one time an analyst asked for the aging profile directly (Q3 FY26 call, Sumit from Finserv Analytics), management declined to disclose it. This is a live, unresolved gap given the receivables growth flag.
- **Network reach contraction**: retail pharmacy count fell from >100,000 (Q4 FY26 call) to 72,000 (Q1 FY27 call), hospitals from 3,600 to 2,300, SKUs from 97,500 to 83,400, districts from 523 to 475 — all in one quarter. No analyst raised this in the Q1 FY27 transcript provided, and management's only related comment (a ~2.5% revenue drag from exiting low-margin sub-distribution accounts) does not obviously scale to a 28-36% drop in customer and hospital counts. Given "reach" is management's own core moat argument, this silence is itself a finding.
- **CARO-qualified cash-loss subsidiaries** (40 per downstream flag) and **goodwill at ~44% of net worth**: neither figure is mentioned in any call. The only adjacent disclosure is GS Pharma's subsidiary-level losses (Q3 FY26) and the flattish FY26 net worth explained by first-time put-option-liability recognition under IndAS (Q4 FY26, Balakrishnan Kaushik) — the latter is a genuine, if narrow, explanation for one piece of the net-worth-quality picture, but does not address the wider goodwill or subsidiary-loss count.
- **A forward view on the outperformance multiple**: management explains why it fell (denominator effects, deliberate low-margin exits) but never commits to where it settles going forward (1.4x as the new normal, or a return toward 1.7-1.9x).

## SECTION 3: COMPETITIVE INTELLIGENCE FROM CONCALLS

### 3A. What management says about competitors

- **PharmEasy** (Q3 FY26 call, Chintan Sheth exchange, p.6): management downplays a PharmEasy return to acquisitive competition for distributor targets, arguing buyers are structurally scarce ("the set of distributors available for acquisitions are huge, while the buyers are only 2 or 3"). Credibility check: plausible directionally but unverifiable from the transcript; worth checking against RPTECH/REDINGTON commentary on the buyer landscape.
- **Amazon partnership** (Q3 FY26 call, Naman Bagrecha exchange, p.5-6): an unnamed "listed peer" is said to have discontinued its Amazon partnership, and management confirms this benefits Entero ("Yes, it does") without elaboration on the mechanism or scale. Peer identity and mechanism unverified in-transcript.
- **Retail chain consolidation** (Q4 FY26 call, Bharat Shah exchange, p.17-18): management names Apollo and MedPlus as the two largest organised pharmacy chains (MedPlus ~5,500 stores, Apollo+MedPlus together ~10,000-12,000, adding 500-600 stores/year), against >1 million total pharmacies in India, to argue organised retail will not meaningfully disintermediate the distributor model for years. Verifiable against MEDPLUS's own disclosed store count.

### 3B. Industry and market intelligence

- IPM (Indian Pharmaceutical Market) growth rate: described as historically cyclical, "10% to 12%, then we went down to 7% to 9%," now "again come back to 10% to 12%" over the last two quarters (Q1 FY27 call, Prabhat Agrawal, p.13); management "not able to isolate any specific factor" behind the recent acceleration (Q1 FY27, Alok Dalal exchange, p.9).
- MedTech India market sized at "more than INR3 lakh crores" (Q3 FY26 call, p.7).
- Healthcare supply chain industry sized "over USD 33 billion," "extremely fragmented" (Q4 FY26 call, opening remarks, p.2).
- GLP-1 category: management estimates Entero handles ~10% of GLP-1 distribution value in Q3 FY26 (p.12), refined to "approximately 5% of all GLP value" and Rs 10-12 Cr/month of GLP sales by Q1 FY27 (p.9) — the two figures are not fully reconciled across calls (10% vs 5% of "GLP value") and should be checked.
- MedTech distributor pricing dynamics: distributors "not only play... the demand fulfillment part, but they also play an active role in demand creation," earning better margin terms than in pharma distribution (Q3 FY26, p.4).
- Global trade deals (US/EU) and Middle East conflict: management states Entero is "largely insulated" given very low direct import exposure and a domestic-only revenue base (Q3 FY26, p.9; Q4 FY26, p.10).

### 3D. Customer and order book signals

- Deliberate, ongoing exit from low-margin sub-distribution and hospital-supply accounts to free up working capital for higher-return deployment; stated to have cut ~2.5% off reported Q1 FY27 revenue growth (Q1 FY27 call, p.3, p.14).
- Wallet-share expansion at existing customers, not new customer acquisition, is repeatedly named as the primary growth lever (Q3 FY26, p.14-15; Q1 FY27, p.13-14).
- New global MedTech partnerships being built across imaging, neuropsychiatry, orthopedics and cardiology segments (Q1 FY27, p.4).
- Small bolt-on acquisition: Sai Pharma BTA in Andhra Pradesh, surgical consumables, immaterial revenue (Q1 FY27, p.10).
- Episodic government-contract revenue timing: a diagnostic PPP contract's billing was preponed into Q4 FY26 from what would have been Q1 FY27 (Q1 FY27 call, p.8), and separately a "significant preponement" of a partially-owned subsidiary's government-project sales into Q4 FY26 drove an abnormal minority-interest print that quarter (Q4 FY26 call, p.4-5, p.12). Flag: government-linked revenue in this business appears lumpy across quarter-ends.
