ABOUT 
THIS REPORT
One company. One year. A closer look at how we engineer, 
manufacture and deliver flow-control solutions.
This is our Annual Report for the financial year 2025–26. We have brought together our business, products, 
manufacturing capabilities, engineering strengths, markets and customers, people, financial performance 
and strategic priorities to provide a complete view of Rappid Valves during the year.
The report is intended to be read as one connected account of our business — from the valves we manufacture 
and the critical applications they serve, to the capabilities and relationships that support our growth.
It sets out our business and operational performance during the year, the progress we made in strengthening 
our engineering and manufacturing capabilities, developments across our industrial and marine businesses, 
and our financial performance for FY2026.
Rappid Valves (India) Limited, formerly Rappid Valves (India) Private Limited, is the reporting entity for FY26. 
Incorporated in 2002, the Company operates from its registered office and manufacturing facility in Palghar, 
Maharashtra. Its equity shares are listed on the SME Platform of the National Stock Exchange of India

MANUFACTURING
CAPACITY & FACILITY 
Where Engineering Becomes Reality
An integrated manufacturing ecosystem built to deliver 
precision, consistency and reliability.
Every valve begins long before it reaches the customer. It begins with 
engineering intent, material selection and manufacturing discipline. 
At Rappid Valves, our integrated manufacturing facility brings these 
capabilities together under one roof, enabling us to transform 
engineering designs into high-performance flow-control solutions 
for critical industrial and marine applications.
Located at Genesis Industrial Complex, Palghar, Maharashtra, our 
manufacturing facility spans over 45,000 sq. ft. and is equipped 
with modern machining, assembly and testing infrastructure. 
The integrated layout enables efficient workflow across design, 
manufacturing, inspection and dispatch while maintaining stringent 
quality standards throughout the production process.
With an installed manufacturing capacity of 33,500 industrial and 
marine valves and 85% capacity utilisation, the facility supports 
both current production requirements and future growth. Continued 
investments in advanced machinery, testing infrastructure and 
automation are strengthening our manufacturing capabilities, 
enhancing productivity and improving delivery responsiveness.
FROM RAW MATERIAL TO RELIABLE PERFORMANCE
A disciplined manufacturing process designed to ensure precision, quality and 
reliability at every stage.
The manufacturing process begins with the procurement of high-quality metal in 
the form of billets, castings and bars, including stainless steel, carbon steel, brass, 
bronze, duplex steel and Inconel, selected to meet specific application requirements. 
The quality of raw materials forms the foundation of every valve we manufacture.
Raw Material Procurement
01
Using advanced CNC (Computer Numerical Control) and Vertical Machining Centres 
(VMCs), raw materials are precision-machined into critical valve components. 
Computer-controlled machining ensures dimensional accuracy, consistency and 
repeatability, forming the core of our manufacturing process.
Machining
02
Precision-machined components are assembled by skilled technicians in accordance 
with defined engineering procedures. Every assembly stage is carefully executed to 
ensure proper alignment, leak-tightness and dependable operational performance.
Assembly
03
Every valve undergoes multiple levels of inspection and testing using specialised 
equipment such as PMI machines, X-ray systems and test benches to verify pressure 
ratings, material integrity and other critical technical parameters before approval.
 Testing
04
Following successful testing, each valve is assigned traceability numbers, supported 
by Material Test Certificates (MTCs) and third-party inspection reports, wherever 
required by customer specifications. The products are then painted, packed and 
prepared for dispatch.
Certification & Dispatch
05
To support increasing demand and expand export capabilities, the Company has continued to strengthen 
its manufacturing infrastructure. Recent initiatives include the operationalization of a 6,000 sq. ft. export-
dedicated facility, the addition of a 9,000 sq. ft. inventory management facility, and planned deployment 
of four automated test benches and a new VMC machine to improve production efficiency and delivery 
timelines.
Strengthening Manufacturing Capabilities
Our manufacturing facility is more than a production unit—it is an 
integrated engineering platform that enables us to deliver precision, 
quality and reliability across every valve we manufacture.

2. Global Economic Overview
FY2026 was a year of disciplined execution for 
Rappid Valves (India) Limited. While the business 
environment remained supportive for long-term 
growth across industrial, marine and defence 
applications, the second half of the financial year 
witnessed heightened volatility i n commodity prices 
and temporary disruptions in global supply chains. 
Against this backdrop, the Company remained 
focused on preserving profitability, strengthening 
its engineering and manufacturing capabilities, 
expanding market access through product approvals, 
and positioning itself for sustainable long-term 
growth rather than pursuing volume growth at any 
cost.
The 
financial 
and 
operational 
performance 
discussed in this Management Discussion and 
Analysis (“MD&A”) should be viewed in the context 
of the Company’s disciplined approach towards 
project selection, cost management and execution 
throughout FY2026. While the Company reports its 
performance on a full-year basis, FY2026 is best 
understood in two distinct phases. The first half 
of the year benefited from favourable operating 
conditions and healthy project execution, whereas 
the second half was characterised by heightened 
raw material volatility, supply chain disruptions and 
geopolitical uncertainty. The Company’s strategic 
response to these changing market conditions 
shaped its financial and operational performance 
during the year and is discussed in greater detail in 
the subsequent sections of this MD&A.
This MD&A should be read together with the audited

4. Industry Structure and Developments
Engineering the Infrastructure of Industrial 
Growth
Structural Growth Drivers
Industrial valves are fundamental components of 
modern infrastructure. Whether transporting crude 
oil through pipelines, regulating steam in power 
plants, controlling chemicals in process industries 
or managing critical systems aboard naval vessels, 
valves perform an essential function by enabling 
the safe, reliable and efficient movement of liquids, 
gases and steam. As economies continue to invest 
in industrial capacity, energy infrastructure, water 
management and defence manufacturing, demand 
for engineered flow-control solutions is expected to 
grow alongside these investments.
According to IMARC, the global valves market is 
estimated at USD 82.9 billion in 2025 and is projected 
to reach USD 136.0 billion by 2034, representing a 
5.7% CAGR.
Several long-term structural trends continue to 
support demand across the industrial valve industry:
•	
Continued investments in oil & gas infrastructure 
and pipeline networks.
•	
Expansion of water treatment and distribution 
projects under initiatives such as the Jal Jeevan 
Mission.
•	
Increasing adoption of industrial automation 
across manufacturing facilities.
•	
Expansion of natural gas infrastructure, including 
LNG terminals, CGD networks and pipeline 
projects.
The Indian industrial valves market is expected 
to expand from USD 3.4 billion to USD 6.3 billion 
during the same period, reflecting a faster growth 
trajectory of 6.64% CAGR, supported by increasing 
industrialisation and infrastructure investment.

2.6 Depreciation & Amortisation 
 
 
Tangible Assets
 
Depreciation on Property, Plant and Equipment

(WDV) Method as per the useful life prescribed 
in Schedule II of the Companies Act 2013. 
Depreciation shall be charged on a pro-rata

year of acquisition.    During the current year, 
the Company carried out a detailed technical 
assessment of certain items of Property, Plant

(scrap) value of identified assets. The revision

2.7 Impairment of assets 
 
 
 
The Company assesses at each Balance Sheet

asset may be impaired. If any such indication 
exists, the carrying value of such assets is

impairment loss is recognised in the statement 
of profit and loss. If at the Balance Sheet date 
there is any indication that a previously assessed 
impairment loss no longer exists, then such loss 
is reversed and the asset is restated to that 
extent.

2.9 Taxation  
 
 
 
Current Tax  
 
 
 
Provision for current tax is made on the basis

Deferred Tax

being the differences between the taxable 
income and the accounting income that originate 
in one period and are capable of reversal in 
one or more subsequent periods. Deferred tax

tax assets are recognised and carried forward 
only to the extent that there is reasonable

unabsorbed depreciation which are recognised 
based on virtual certainty that the difference 
will reverse in future. 
 
 
 
 
2.10 Provisions, 
Contingent 
Liabilities 
and 
Contingent Assets 
 
 
A provision is recognised when the company has 
a present obligation as a result of past event; it 
is probable that an outflow of resources will be 
required to settle the obligation, in respect of 
which a reliable estimate can be made. Provisions 
are not discounted to their present value and are 
determined based on best estimate required to 
settle the obligation 
 
 
 
 
 
Where no reliable estimates can be made, 
a disclosure is made as contingent liability. 
A disclosure for a contingent liability is also 
made when there a possible obligations or 
present obligation that may, or probably will not, 
require an outflow of resources. When there is 
a possible obligation or a present obligation in 
respect of which the likelihood of outflow of 
resources is remote, no provision or disclosure is 
made Contingent assets are neither recognised 
nor disclosed in the financial statements. These 
are reviewed at each Balance Sheet date and 
adjusted to reflect the current best estimates
