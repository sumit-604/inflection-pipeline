# KISSHT (OnEMI Technology Solutions Ltd): business narrative

Phase 3 finalize. Run 2026-09-19, finalized 04-Oct-2026. This file supersedes the phase 1 lite narrative of the same name. Every value is priced under the unmerged Section 1B v3.11 draft (open item I5).

## Business understanding narrative

Kissht makes small unsecured personal loans through an app, and these are 92.3% of its loan book; loans against property, sold through branches, are the other 7.7%. The listed parent, OnEMI, does not lend: the loans sit in its NBFC subsidiary Si Creva, and the parent earns sourcing fees and a guarantee fee. Interest on loans held on the book brings 58% of revenue, fees for sourcing and servicing partner loans bring 27%, and late payment and foreclosure charges bring 11%. Pricing power is weak, and the run did not establish what a borrower would lose by moving to another lender. The borrowers are mass market and mass affluent Indians who want credit without a branch visit. They are not locked in, since the repeat customer share of the loan book fell from 73% to 49% in FY26. The second customer is the partner lender. Eight or more off book lending partner banks and NBFCs carry 53.6% of the book, and Kissht covers their first losses under a first loss default guarantee. Present demand is personal credit for the mass market, about Rs 26 to 27 lakh crore across all lenders, and the new age digital lender slice was Rs 60,000 Cr in FY25. Bureau delinquency reports from CRIF High Mark, TransUnion CIBIL and Equifax, and the RBI Financial Stability Report, are the outside checks on whether that demand stays healthy. Demand should grow as formal jobs and tax filing widen the pool of borrowers who qualify, which EPFO net payroll additions, GST collections and ITR filing counts track each month. The digital lender slice is projected at Rs 4.1 lakh Cr by FY30, but that projection comes from a study the company paid for, and only the personal loan leg has independent support. How much of the book can sit off balance sheet depends on the RBI Master Directions on digital lending and co lending, and on the appetite of the off book lending partner banks and NBFCs. Prints from the named new age peers KreditBee, Navi, Fibe and Moneyview show whether Kissht gains or loses share. Personal loan underwriting is the one line with a scored edge: the proprietary data asset, built on 400 plus variables and 40 models, rates Strong. That edge is an execution lead, not a barrier, because the talent asymmetry and cannibalisation tests both scored zero. The digital platform and partner integration rate Moderate, brand, cost position and scale show no moat, and the loan against property line has no moat evidence at all.

## Fast growing lender, true losses still hidden

Kissht listed on 8 May 2026. FY26 revenue from operations was Rs 2,179 Cr and profit Rs 281 Cr. The loan book reached Rs 8,001 Cr in June 2026, up 13% in the quarter and 61% in a year. About 46% of it sits on Si Creva's books. The other 54% sits with partner lenders, and Kissht pays the first 5% of losses on each partner pool. Profit runs at about Rs 5 for every Rs 100 of average loans.

The operator signed a one rung climb as the evidenced reading: from short checkout credit to a well run high yield lender, with return on equity near 17 to 18% after the new capital. Management claims more, a data edge that keeps cutting losses, which would be a two rung leap. The engine is the all in loss rate, meaning on book impairment plus partner book losses plus excess collection spend. It fell from 11.6% to about 11.0% of average loans in FY26. New defaults on the own book fell by a third, and first instalment defaults fell from 6.8% to 0.8%. The proof gate needs all in losses below 10.5% in the second half of FY27, with the partner book loss rate not rising. It has not fired. Cheaper funding helps less than it looks: a 100 to 150 basis point cut is worth Rs 23 to 35 Cr pre tax, while one point of all in loss costs about Rs 87 Cr.

The backward scorecard lands at AVERAGE, 51 out of 100 on the core score. Operating cash flow was minus Rs 1,648 Cr over FY21 to FY26 against Rs 671 Cr of profit, on a screener basis that includes a disputed FY23 figure. The operator ruled this growth induced, because a lender's disbursals run through operating cash flow. The loss content of the book is still not certified. Partner book losses sit in other expenses, not in credit cost, and no filed note states the write off trigger in days past due. The partner loss number arrives only once or twice a year, in the annual report or a rating note.

Management delivered the numbers it can move fast. Loan growth, headline credit cost and return on loans printed at or above guidance, and the IPO money reached Si Creva with no deviation. It withheld the numbers that matter most. Guidance drifted upward within one quarter. Disbursement fell from Rs 3,954 Cr to Rs 3,812 Cr while the book grew on longer tenures. The partner loss cost drew three different answers on one call and was never quantified. The parent guaranteed Si Creva's lenders for 228% of its own net worth in FY26. A Rs 832 Cr share sale at Rs 314 came four months after listing, with capital adequacy already at 40.2% and no number given for the need. Credibility grades C. The promoter review reads CAUTION, and the operator ruled an integrity concern with a Small size ceiling until the company names the years of its only RBI inspection.

Peers cut both ways. In the same quarter SBI Cards and Poonawalla reported better asset quality while Kissht's 30 to 90 day bucket rose from 2.4% to 3.15%, though Kissht's own pattern a year earlier supports its seasonal reading. Poonawalla raised equity at 16.8% capital adequacy and Ugro near 18%, far below Kissht's 40.2%. Funding cost evidence is mixed: Ugro's fell for seven straight quarters. Market size is not the constraint. Kissht holds about 6% of its segment and the runway grades MASSIVE. The constraint is the partner side: two lenders held 95% of the parent's guarantee pools at 31 August 2026, on the company's own disclosure portal as read live by Claude web.

The valuation is a sum of parts at 31 March 2027. The own book sits at 0.8x to 1.2x book. The partner half sits at 18.6x FY28 profit on Track 1 and 22.7x on Track 2, and the 22.7x cap is an operator blend that Section 1B does not carry. The whole company cross check gives 12.2x on Track 1 and 16.0x on Track 2. Fair value is Rs 414 a share on Track 1 and Rs 531 on Track 2 at the valuation date, rising to Rs 738 and Rs 937 by March 2030. Fair value compounds at 21.2% a year on Track 1, a COMPOUNDER by 1.2 points; if return on loans slides 15 basis points a year it is 17.9%, a HYBRID. The entry zone of about Rs 350 is an operator override. The formula entry is Rs 344, so the override sits above it, and the price of Rs 369.55 sits 5.6% above the zone. Bought at Rs 350, the return to the Track 1 value is 23.8% a year, under the 25% hurdle. The hurdle ratio passes at 2.04 on the midpoint and fails at 1.79 on Track 1.

The decision is AVOID. The thesis stage applied the rule that a Gate 0 AVERAGE name is never a buy, and the framework verifier concurs. Stage 11 had written WATCHLIST. The FTTCP band, in the operator's words, is "Composite +2 of 8. DEEP WATCH leaning AVOID. Posture VALUE-TRAP RISK." The zone can become live only through an operator ruling on a Gate 0 lender variant, a clean Q2 FY27 asset quality test, and a fired proof gate. The verifier asks the operator to strike the thesis stage's 0.8x upside to downside trigger and its Rs 347 reopen price, and to set the size field to none.

The thesis was built to depth with seven second order chains and a filled Entrepreneur Ledger. The devil's advocate came back WEAKENED BUT ALIVE on all four dimensions. Its sharpest counters: the partner half is 73% of Track 1 value and rests on two lenders; a partner exit sized at half, not a third, gives Rs 264 a share; the partner half's asset quality multiple is read from on book bad loans that exclude partner loans by definition; and the Rs 350 zone sits above every formula entry, from Rs 344 down to Rs 291. The conviction test said no to three times the size. Ten major verifier findings, the unmerged framework draft, the Q2 FY27 update and the disbursement split by book stay open for the operator.
