### 2B. Utilisation trajectory per facility

- **Electrical cables (overall)**: ~mid-60s% as of end-March FY26 (Q4 FY26 call); auto/solar/

  FY26. Building/construction wire utilization is lower and was the laggard through FY26.
- **Solar cables**: 80-85% of built capacity as of Q3 FY26 call ("nearing capacity utilization"
  per Q4 FY26 call) — the highest-utilization new line, driving the FY27 doubling decision.
- **Communication/OFC draw capacity**: reported inconsistently across quarters — "factory
  booking is probably around 60% of the capacity" (Q2 FY26 call, Nov-2025) vs "close to three
  and a quarter [3.2mn km], out of 4[mn km]" i.e. ~80% (Q4 FY26 call, May-2026). This could
  reflect genuine utilization improvement or a change in the basis of measurement; the
  discrepancy is flagged rather than resolved.
- **EHV JV**: key machinery utilization >80% (Q4 FY26 call); overall plant utilization targeted
  to rise to 70-75% (base level not disclosed).
- **Conduits (FMEG)**: ~85% utilization (Q4 FY26 call) — best-performing FMEG line.
- **Fans (FMEG)**: flat-to-down in volume FY26 (unseasonal monsoon + BIS-norm-driven channel
  destocking, Q2/Q4 FY26 calls) — no utilization figure given, but this is the weak line.

### 2C. Growth embedded in capex — arithmetic shown

Capex under execution (as of run date, per Q4 FY26 call): ~₹300cr for FY27 (₹200cr new
capacity expansion, including the solar doubling and general electrical-cable additions, +
₹100cr balance to complete the fibre-draw/preform program).

Historical fixed asset turnover (company-wide, blended): Revenue from Operations FY26
₹6,321.01cr ÷ Net Property, Plant & Equipment at 31-Mar-2026 ₹831.42cr (Inv. Pres. slides 4, 9)
= **~7.6x**.

Mechanical calculation: ₹300cr × 7.6x ≈ **₹2,280cr** implied incremental revenue, or **~36%**
of FY26 revenue (₹6,321cr).

**Caveat (required by the "be skeptical" operating rule)**: this blended, company-wide FAT
figure almost certainly overstates near-term impact. It mixes mature, fast-turning wire/cable
assets with newer, slower-to-ramp OFC/preform/EHV assets. Management's own bottom-up guidance
for the single largest identified capex line — the OFC/fibre program — points to a full-
capacity revenue potential of only ~₹750cr versus ~₹500cr today, i.e. an incremental **~₹250cr
(~4% of FY26 revenue)**, not the ~36% the mechanical calc implies. The ₹200cr "new capacity
expansion" line (solar + general electrical cable) has no bottom-up revenue guidance at all.
The mechanical 36% figure is reported per the Section 2C instruction and carried in the YAML
field below, but should be read as a mechanical upper bound, not a forecast; the honest
embedded-growth read is closer to single digits based on management's own numbers.

## SECTION 4: REGULATORY & POLICY TAILWINDS (R1)

**4A. Regulatory approvals in pipeline**: switchgear/switch variants require regulatory
certification with a 6-8 month cycle per product (🎙️, Q2 FY26 call) — routine, not a moat.

**4B. Government policy tailwinds**:
- BharatNet Phase 3 (national optical-fibre network, originally announced 2010 for completion
  by 2013-14, now on its third phase — management's own characterisation of chronic delay, Q1
  FY26 call). 16 BSNL tenders floated; 12 awarded to other suppliers, 4 going to retender;
  Finolex "not successful in any of the tenders" directly but is pursuing subcontract supply
  deals with the tender winners who lack cabling/fibre capacity (🎙️, Q2/Q3 FY26 calls) — this
  remains unrealised for Finolex specifically.
- Seven state-level BharatNet tenders expected; only Gujarat had floated as of Q2 FY26 call.
- Anti-dumping duty imposed by the Government of India on Chinese fibre dumping (📄 regulatory
  action, Q1 FY26 call) — benefits all domestic OFC players equally, not a Finolex-specific
  differentiator.
- New labour code (effective 21-Nov-2025) increased gratuity obligations; Finolex booked a ₹6cr
  provision in Q3 FY26 — a regulatory **cost**, not a tailwind (📄, Q3 FY26 call).
- Revised BIS norms for fans drove channel destocking and hurt FY26 fan volumes (📄 negative
  regulatory impact, Q4 FY26 call) — also a headwind, not a tailwind.

**4C. Regulatory moat assessment**: what tailwind exists (BharatNet, anti-dumping duty) is
industry-wide, not company-specific, and BharatNet in particular carries a well-documented
15-year history of announcement-to-execution lag per management's own words. Classification:
**emerging, not active**; sustainability is low as a differentiator because competitors share
the same benefit; time to kick in for Finolex specifically is unclear (management: "hopeful
that there will be adequate business for us over the next two years," not a committed number).

## SECTION 5: EMERGING MOAT SCORECARD

## SECTION 6: TIMELINE, RISKS & COMBINED ASSESSMENT

### 6A. Moat evolution timeline

- **Next 12 months (through mid-2027)**: fibre-draw capacity ramp toward 8mn km (management's
  own timeline has already slipped three times — watch for a fourth); communication-cable EBIT
  margin trajectory toward the guided 8-9%; solar capacity doubling; Sumitomo JV execution
  against its ~₹380cr opening order book; possible first BharatNet subcontract orders.
- **12-24 months**: state-level BharatNet tender outcomes; FMEG in-house-manufacturing go/no-go
  decision; export ramp visible in quarterly numbers; new-entrant (cement-group) wire/cable
  production start expected ~Q2-Q3 CY2027, first real test of pricing/share impact.
- **24-36 months**: Sumitomo EHV JV capacity expansion if the market grows toward the higher end
  of management's TAM claim; FMEG ₹500cr-by-2028 target tested; data-center demand either
  converts to a real order book or remains aspirational.
- **3-5 years**: possible mix shift if communication cables + EHV JV scale into a larger,
  structurally higher-margin share of the business; alternatively, a shakeout among the new
  wire/cable entrants (Birla-linked, Adani) that pressures industry-wide margins, Finolex
  included, given wires remain ~87% of revenue.

### 6B. Risks to the top-scoring emerging moats, with early warning signs

- **Preform/fibre-draw execution risk**: both flagship backward-integration projects have
  slipped repeatedly (preform ~4-6 months against the original Aug-2025 promise; fibre draw
  pushed from March 2026 to July/Sept 2026 across three consecutive calls). Early warning:
  any further push in the next concall.
- **Raw material supply chain**: germanium (preform input) sourced from China/Europe under
  export restrictions tied to defense-use concerns; management: "that is the challenge... a
  challenge that we need to overcome" (Q4 FY26 call). Early warning: commentary on months-of-
  cover remaining, or further sourcing disruption.
- **Fibre/preform price reversal**: current pricing is at a multi-year high ($5/meter vs a
  2-year low near $2) driven by data-center and defense demand — the very tailwind funding the
  margin-recovery story could reverse. Early warning: fibre price commentary turning in future
  calls.
- **New entrant competition**: a cement-group-linked entrant (widely understood in context to
  be UltraTech) targets wire/cable production start ~Q2-Q3 CY2027; Adani has announced intent
  but given no specifics. Both target the 87%-of-revenue wires segment that funds the emerging-
  moat capex program. Management: "competition will intensify... we should be able to handle
  those pressures" — an assertion, not evidence.
- **EHV JV durability**: one profitable year (~4.6% PBT margin) after "several years" of
  challenges is thin evidence of a durable turnaround.
- **Working capital deterioration**: WC days rose from 22 to 33 (H1 FY25→H1 FY26); a ~₹300cr
  inventory build in Q4 FY26 (Middle-East-driven prebuying) could reverse the 9M FY26 CFO
  recovery and echoes the Gate-0-flagged CFO/PAT deterioration (see 6C).
- **BharatNet program risk**: the company's own characterisation of the program as chronically
  delayed since 2010 applies with full force to the Phase 3 orders Finolex is counting on.
