## SECTION 1: THE BUSINESS MODEL IN PLAIN ENGLISH

### 1A. One line
NGL crushes animal bones near Jabalpur and turns them into gelatine (the jelly protein that makes pharma capsules) and animal-feed phosphate (DCP). Both are sold at market prices. Profit comes from the gap between what it pays for bones and fuel and what these two products fetch (AR p.11-12, AR p.68, CARE p.3).

### 1E. Chai-stall-uncle version
Think of a mill that buys sacks of raw grain from a rough market and sells two things, flour and bran. The mill owner does not set the price of flour or bran. The mill earns well when grain is cheap and fuel is cheap. NGL does the same with bones. It sells gelatine, about 71 rupees of every 100, and feed phosphate, about 29 of every 100. In FY26 bones and coal got cheaper and the feed price went up, so profit per 100 rupees of sales jumped from about 13 to about 19. Nobody has shown yet that the cheap-grain year will repeat.
