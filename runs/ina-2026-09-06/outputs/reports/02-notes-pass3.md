## PASS 3: PATTERN FINDINGS

Sequential re-reads (Pass 1, Pass 2) are done. This pass reads across notes instead of
through them, testing for contradiction, mismatch, vague disclosure, restatement, subsequent
events and going-concern language, and testing the five leads named in the task brief.

### Standard pattern categories

- **Contradictions between notes**: two are already on file and are confirmed genuine on
  re-check, not extraction artefacts: (1) Note 4 (p.120, "Title Deeds of all Immovable
  properties are held in the name of Company") versus Note 18A (p.126/169, SBI cash-credit
  security described as an equitable mortgage over factory land "IN THE NAME OF Sh. Manish
  Gupta and Sh. Vikas Jain Director of the Company"). (2) Note 8's LLP investment table
  (Rs 5,00,00,000 fixed capital, p.121) versus its own narrative sentence on the same page
  (Rs 40,000,000). Both are internal, same-document, same-note-or-adjacent-note conflicts on
  hard numbers or hard facts, not something an investor can resolve from the AR alone.
- **Numbers not matching primary statements**: none additional found beyond the two above and
  the cash reconciliation Pass 2 already resolved (Section 1 below). The Note 53 self-disclosed
  ratio table (p.150-151) is internally consistent with the primary statements on the numbers
  themselves; the anomaly there is presentational (parentheses convention), covered below, not
  a numeric mismatch.
- **Vague disclosure relative to other notes**: the RPT note (Note 41/40) gives no arm's-length
  pricing statement anywhere, against a company that otherwise discloses granular per-instrument
  borrowing terms. The contingent liability note (Note 42) gives no probability assessment
  ("remote/possible/probable") for any item, against a company that otherwise itemises
  guarantees down to the individual bank facility. Both read as disclosure being detailed where
  routine and thin where judgment-laden.
- **Restated or reclassified prior-year numbers**: only the standard boilerplate found in both
  passes ("Regrouped, Recast, Reclassified... to ensure comparability," Note 52 consolidated /
  Note 54 standalone, p.130/177), with no quantified restatement line identified. Separately,
  FY24 (1-Apr-2024) is the Ind AS 101 transition date; PPE opening balances use the deemed-cost
  exemption (Note 4 footnote, p.120), so FY24 figures are IGAAP-derived, not independently
  Ind-AS-measured. This is a comparability caveat, not a restatement in the ordinary sense.
- **Events after balance sheet date**: no dedicated subsequent-events note surfaced in either
  pass despite both passes reading note-by-note through Note 59/58. NOT FOUND IN DOCUMENT (per
  the extraction). The one date-adjacent anomaly on file is the ESOP note's internally
  inconsistent "14/11/2026" issue date (Note 15(h), p.124), which post-dates both FY26 year-end
  and the 25-May-2026 audit signing date — almost certainly a typo for 14/11/2025, but it means
  no genuine subsequent-events disclosure was verified as present or absent with confidence.
- **Going concern language**: NONE found anywhere in the 200-page extraction, in either the
  Board's assessment or either auditor's opinion (consolidated or standalone).

### The four leads named in the task brief

**Lead 1 — one coherent group-structure story.** CONFIRMED. Every metric Pass 1 and Pass 2
localised points the same direction: parent standalone revenue FELL 13.3% (Rs 113.02 cr to
Rs 98.03 cr, Standalone P&L p.160) while consolidated revenue rose 60.9% (Rs 1,333.77 cr to
Rs 2,146.02 cr); parent cash capex was Rs 28.43 cr against consolidated Rs 448.05 cr (~94% of
group capex sits outside the parent, Consolidated CFS p.117 / Standalone CFS p.160-161); parent
standalone total assets are Rs 544.04 cr against consolidated Rs 2,155.13 cr (Balance Sheets,
p.116/159); parent's own receivables ageing is clean (93.9% not due, Note 12 standalone, p.166)
while the consolidated book is 39.5% aged beyond 6 months. The connecting fact: the parent
(the LISTED entity) guarantees Rs 1,654.01 cr of subsidiary debt (Note 42(i), p.137) — a sum
**3.0x the parent's own total assets** (Rs 1,654.01 cr / Rs 544.04 cr) and 205% of consolidated
net worth. This is one story, not several: a legacy module-manufacturing parent, flat to
shrinking on its own book, has become the guarantee-and-listing vehicle for a fast-scaling,
debt-funded, working-capital-hungry EPC/generation subsidiary (Insolation Green Energy Private
Limited). The market prices the consolidated numbers; the parent's own balance sheet could not
absorb the guaranteed exposure if called. Whoever holds INA equity is underwriting subsidiary
execution and collection risk through a corporate structure where the risk-bearing entity
(parent, thin balance sheet) and the risk-generating entity (subsidiary, most of the debt, most
of the receivables) are different legal persons consolidated into one reported number.

**Lead 2 — the FY2026 cash inversion: which reading does the evidence support.** MIXED, cannot
be fully settled from the notes alone; both a working-capital-ramp reading and a
revenue-quality-concern reading have partial support, and they are not mutually exclusive.
Supporting the ramp reading: inventory growth (Rs 76.98 cr to Rs 379.05 cr) is concentrated in
Raw Materials (Rs 56.62 cr to Rs 138.02 cr) and Finished Goods (Rs 12.46 cr to Rs 183.13 cr),
consistent with a manufacturer scaling capacity ahead of shipment, and tracks the Rs 448.05 cr
consolidated capex and Rs 901.43 cr capital-commitment programme (Note 42(b), p.138); the
Power & Fuel expense line rose 3.2x (Note 33, p.131) tracking the same ramp. Supporting the
revenue-quality reading: the receivables growth is not a uniform scale-up — "Unbilled" revenue
is Rs 135.98 cr, 48% of the FY26 consolidated receivables book (Note 13, p.122), against a
stated revenue-recognition policy of 100% point-in-time recognition with NO over-time /
percentage-of-completion component (Note 51, p.149); a large unbilled balance sitting inside a
point-in-time-only policy is not self-explanatory from what the notes disclose, and the
governing revenue-recognition policy text (Notes 1-3) is the one document that could resolve
whether this is normal EPC billing-cycle lag or something closer to revenue booked ahead of
invoicing rights. Layered on that: the >6-month-aged bucket grew from Rs 13.36 cr to Rs 111.35
cr in one year with a Rs NIL ECL provision throughout (Note 13), and the new Rs 265.19 cr
Solarworld Energy Solutions customer concentration (12.4% of revenue, Note 46, p.144) has no
disclosed ageing-by-customer split to confirm whether this specific receivable is current or
aged. **What would settle it**: (a) the missing Notes 1-3 revenue-recognition and ECL policy
text; (b) an ageing-by-customer breakdown, specifically for Solarworld; (c) a subsequent-events
disclosure of post-year-end collections against the Rs 111.35 cr aged bucket, which was NOT
FOUND in this extraction; (d) the ECL methodology that produces a Rs NIL result on this book.
Absent those, the honest read is: genuine capacity-driven working-capital growth is real and
evidenced, but it does not by itself explain why nearly half of a fast-growing receivables book
is unbilled and why none of the aged >6-month balance carries any provision. FLAG-CASH stands.

**Lead 3 — are the governance items independent or a cluster.** PARTLY A CLUSTER, with two
distinct sub-clusters rather than one. Sub-cluster A (title/property): the Note 4 vs Note 18A
title-deed contradiction and the factory land mortgaged in the promoter-directors' personal
names are not two separate items — they are the SAME underlying fact (the land is not
demonstrably in the company's name) surfacing twice with conflicting labels; treat as one
finding, not two, in the top findings list. Sub-cluster B (capital allocation and scrutiny): the
Rs 32.5-34.5 cr LLP investment in promoter-family vehicles, the pervasive personal guarantees by
both promoter-directors across nearly every group facility, the zero-KAM audit opinions in the
same year the group scaled 5x+ on several balance-sheet metrics, the auditor's replacement at
this AGM, and the ~76 SPVs of which only 5 carry a formal Note 59 business-combination
disclosure with immaterial NCI despite several being held at only 49% voting interest — these DO
connect. The common thread is that the group's legal and financial complexity (SPV count,
guarantee web, promoter-controlled capital vehicles) has grown far faster than the audit and
disclosure apparatus visibly tracking it: an auditor reporting zero KAMs on this scale of change,
being replaced the same year, is the single fact that ties the rest together as an "audit
scrutiny gap" rather than a set of unrelated oddities. Sub-cluster A and Sub-cluster B are
related (both involve the same two promoter-directors) but analytically distinct: A is a
property-title/security risk; B is a governance-and-oversight risk. Both belong in the top
findings; neither should be diluted by merging into a single vaguer line.

**Lead 4 — Note 53's self-disclosed ratios.** CONFIRMED as an internal presentation anomaly, not
independently provable as intentional. Every ratio that genuinely worsened (Current Ratio,
Debt/Equity +481%, Inventory/Receivable/Payable Turnover, ROCE) is shown as an unsigned positive
variance percentage; the only two ratios that genuinely IMPROVED (DSCR 8.68x→9.35x, ROE
20.63%→24.82%) are the only two shown with a parenthetical variance, "(7.74)%" and "(20.29)%"
respectively — the opposite of where a bracket-for-adverse convention would apply. This does not
change the underlying numbers, which tie to the primary statements, but it means a reader
scanning only the sign convention, rather than the ratio values themselves, could misread
direction on exactly the two metrics (debt coverage and equity returns) most likely to be quoted
standalone by a lender or in an investor deck. Separately and more substantively: the "improving"
DSCR of 9.35x is built on an accrual measure ("earnings available for debt service") that sits
against an actual consolidated operating cash outflow of Rs (73.13) cr in the same year (Note 53
p.150-151; Consolidated CFS p.117). A DSCR read in isolation overstates debt-servicing comfort
relative to the cash reality. This is a genuine consistency-and-transparency problem in the
company's own voluntary disclosure, additive to, not a restatement of, Lead 2's receivables
finding.

## B. CROSS-STAGE CORRECTION CARRIED FORWARD

Stage 1 (Gate 0) reported an unexplained ~Rs 232 cr cash reconciliation gap and an implied
closing cash of ~Rs 753 cr. This is RESOLVED against the AR (Pass 2, confirmed on this pass):
Consolidated CFO Rs (73.13) cr + CFI Rs (185.57) cr + CFF Rs 697.80 cr = net increase in cash
Rs 439.10 cr (Consolidated CFS, p.117-118); opening cash Rs 2.27 cr; CFS closing cash Rs 441.37
cr, which the CFS's own Ind AS 7 note reconciles as Cash on hand Rs 0.48 cr + Bank current a/c
Rs 402.29 cr + Bank fixed deposit a/c Rs 38.60 cr (p.118). The Balance Sheet's "Cash and Cash
Equivalents" (Note 14, Rs 87.99 cr) plus "Other Bank Balances" (Note 14A, Rs 432.95 cr) sum to
Rs 520.94 cr exactly, and Note 14A's own split shows the Rs 520.94 cr less Rs 441.37 cr
difference (Rs 79.57 cr) is precisely its 3-12 month fixed-deposit tranche, correctly excluded
from cash equivalents under Ind AS 7. There is no gap. Downstream stages should carry forward
Rs 520.94 cr (Balance Sheet total cash and bank) and Rs 441.37 cr (CFS closing cash equivalents)
as the anchored figures, and should NOT carry forward Stage 1's ~Rs 753.03 cr / ~Rs 232 cr
figures, which do not match any combination of the audited AR's own numbers. Separately: Rs
353.38 cr of the Rs 520.94 cr sits in earmarked current accounts (likely project escrow/DSRA,
consistent with the IREDA and other project financing), not freely available cash — a liquidity
read that treats the full Rs 520.94 cr as discretionary would overstate flexibility.

### D. FIVE QUESTIONS FOR MANAGEMENT

1. What is the legal or contractual basis on which the Group consolidates roughly 70 or more
   step-down SPVs and recognises near-100% economic interest, given several subsidiaries are
   held at only 49% voting interest and Non-Controlling Interest remains immaterial (Rs 1.33cr,
   Note 17, p.124)?
2. Note 4 (p.120) states all title deeds of immovable property are held in the Company's name.
   Note 18A (p.126/169) describes the factory land at Village Bagwara and the Jatawali
   industrial plot as mortgaged in the personal names of Sh. Manish Gupta and Sh. Vikas Jain.
   Which statement reflects actual legal title, and what is the plan, if any, to transfer title
   to the Company?
3. What is the business rationale, expected return, and exit plan for the Group's roughly
   Rs 32.5-34.5cr investment (10% partnership interest) in Happy Buildmart LLP and Harmony
   Buildestate LLP, where the remaining 90% is held personally by the two promoter-director
   families (Note 8, p.121)?
4. What ECL methodology supports a Rs NIL provision against a consolidated receivables book
   that is now 39.5% (Rs 111.35cr) aged beyond 6 months, up from 5.7% two years earlier, and
   what is the ageing status of the new Rs 265.19cr Solarworld Energy Solutions receivable
   (Note 13 p.122-123; Note 46 p.144)?
5. Why are Basic and Diluted EPS identical in both FY25 and FY26 despite an outstanding ESOP
   pool priced deeply in the money (Rs 3.80 exercise price against grant-date prices of
   Rs 238-258), and has any tranche now vested and become exercisable (Note 44/43, p.141-143)?

### E. NOTES-BASED RED FLAGS

- Zero ECL provision against a receivables book where the >6-month-aged share nearly tripled
  in one year (5.7% to 39.5% since transition date), with no disclosed impairment methodology
  — provisioning-adequacy red flag.
- Rs 1,654.01cr corporate guarantee exposure (16.6x YoY growth), 3.0x the parent's own total
  assets, with no covenant terms disclosed for the underlying facilities — undisclosed
  risk-amplifier.
- Internal contradiction on core-asset title (Note 4 vs Note 18A) — an unresolved,
  well-anchored factual conflict about who legally owns the company's factory land.
- Rs 32.5-34.5cr of capital deployed into LLPs 90%-owned by the two promoter-director families,
  with no arm's-length pricing statement anywhere in the RPT note and no stated business
  rationale — related-party capital-allocation red flag.
- Zero Key Audit Matters reported by an auditor being replaced the same AGM, in the year the
  group's inventory grew 4.9x, receivables 2.56x, and guarantee exposure 16.6x — audit-depth
  red flag.
- Note 53's self-disclosed ratio table shows a parenthetical-sign anomaly isolated to the two
  ratios (DSCR, ROE) that improved, while every genuinely worsening ratio is shown unsigned —
  a presentation inconsistency that, whether or not intentional, understates the credibility
  gap between accrual-based DSCR (9.35x) and the actual Rs (73.13)cr operating cash outflow.
- Roughly 70 of ~76 step-down SPVs are consolidated with no Note 59 business-combination
  disclosure at all, and NCI stays immaterial despite several 49%-voting-interest holdings —
  consolidation-mechanics red flag, mechanism undisclosed.

### F. ONE-LINE NOTES VERDICT

The notes reveal moderate to concerning accounting practices. Key concern: zero ECL
provisioning against a fast-aging receivables book that sits entirely inside a thinly
capitalised, heavily guaranteed subsidiary structure. Key strength: capex, tax and actuarial
disclosures are clean and internally consistent, and no primary-statement number was found
restated or fabricated. Overall accounting quality: 4/10.
